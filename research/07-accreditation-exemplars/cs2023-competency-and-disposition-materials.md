# Cliff Notes — CS2023 Competency and Disposition Materials

## Citation and access
- **Authors:** ACM / IEEE-CS / AAAI Computer Science Curricula 2023 Steering Committee and Task Force
- **Title:** CS2023: ACM/IEEE-CS/AAAI Computer Science Curricula
- **Venue and year:** Final Report endorsed by ACM on 18 January 2024, by IEEE-CS on 22 January 2024, and by AAAI on 22 February 2024
- **Source consulted:** `https://csed.acm.org/` (vision statement, knowledge areas, final report index); single-volume edition at `https://dl.acm.org/doi/book/10.1145/3664191`
- **Access status:** the project website and the sectioned PDFs are openly published; the consolidated volume is distributed through the ACM Digital Library. This note synthesises the openly readable web material; the sectioned PDFs did not yield extractable text in this pass and are flagged below.

## What this source is
CS2023 is the decennial revision of the international curricular guidelines for undergraduate computer science, jointly produced by ACM, the IEEE Computer Society and — for the first time in this line of documents — AAAI. It is not an accreditation standard and has no enforcement mechanism. It is a reference model: a body of knowledge organised into knowledge areas and units, a competency framework layered on top of it, a set of characteristics expected of graduates, and a companion volume of curricular practice articles describing how material should be taught rather than merely what should be covered. Its practical role is to be the document a curriculum committee reaches for when asking whether a proposed syllabus is recognisably computer science.

## The argument in full
The task force's published vision statement makes the intellectual commitment of CS2023 unusually explicit, and it is worth reading in its own words because it explains why this document is cited in arguments about behaviour rather than only about content. The first vision item states that the task force "will attempt to incorporate an appropriate competency model for computer science in CS2023." The rationale given is a claim about the discipline itself: "Computer Science is a professional discipline where skills and professional dispositions are just as important as the underlying knowledge." The statement then diagnoses the state of the field: "While the computing education community has accepted the importance of skills and incorporated various pedagogical techniques to promote the development of skills in computing education, it has not yet widely accepted the benefits of professional dispositions to the same extent." The stated goal of building a competency model is therefore rhetorical as much as structural — "We hope to make the importance of Computer Science students developing professional dispositions more salient by attempting to build a competency model of the curriculum."

The second vision item is a hedge, and an honest one. The task force commits to producing an updated knowledge model as well, and explains why: "The value of curricular guidelines is dependent on buy-in by the community. Since convincing the computing education community of the importance of professional dispositions is still a work in progress, we wish CS2023 to be a bridge between the tried and true knowledge model and the yet-to-be-designed competency model of the curriculum." This is the central tension of the whole document. CS2023 argues for competencies and dispositions but delivers a knowledge model as the primary, operational artefact, precisely because it knows adoption depends on continuity with CS2013. A third vision item commits to maintaining consistency between the two models so that adopters of either can compare curricula.

The remaining two vision items concern form rather than substance but shape how the document is used. CS2023 was to be published both as a hardcopy document and as a live online version, on the reasoning that an online form "will facilitate easier navigation among knowledge areas, offer the opportunity to associate supplementary materials (e.g., exemplar assignments, courses) with each knowledge unit and facilitate ongoing improvement of the curriculum long after it has been initially published." And the task force committed to producing "richly-researched articles by experts on curricular practices", drawing an explicit line between the two genres: "Whereas Curricular Guidelines list what should be covered in the curriculum, Curricular Practices describe how and why they should be covered, including challenges, best practices, etc." That distinction is useful to borrow. A syllabus document and a teaching-practice document answer different questions and should not be merged.

The knowledge model itself is organised into sixteen knowledge areas, each with a two- or three-letter code: Algorithmic Foundations (AL), Architecture and Organization (AR), Artificial Intelligence (AI), Data Management (DM), Foundations of Programming Languages (FPL), Graphics and Interactive Techniques (GIT), Human-Computer Interaction (HCI), Mathematical and Statistical Foundations (MSF), Networking and Communication (NC), Operating Systems (OS), Parallel and Distributed Computing (PDC), Security (SEC), Society, Ethics, and the Profession (SEP), Software Development Fundamentals (SDF), Software Engineering (SE), and Specialized Platform Development (SPD). Systems Fundamentals (SF) is also listed, though its entry points to a knowledge-area subcommittee page rather than to a published area page in the same pattern as the others — the precise count therefore needs checking against the final report before being asserted. Several of these codes recur almost verbatim in the proposed 2026-27 ABET computer science Program Criteria, which is not a coincidence; the two documents are read against each other by the same community.

The final report is published in four sections. Section 1 covers the executive summary, an introduction to CS2023, an introduction to the knowledge model and an introduction to the competency framework. Section 2 covers design: principles and processes, characteristics of graduates, and institutional challenges and opportunities. Section 3 carries the detail: the body of knowledge itself, and competency framework examples. Section 4 addresses pedagogy and practices, with articles on pedagogical considerations, curricular practices, and — notably for a document finalised in early 2024 — generative AI and the curriculum. The presence of a dedicated generative-AI section in the flagship curricular guideline is itself a datum about how quickly the field moved between CS2013 and CS2023.

## Key concepts and specifics
- Endorsement dates: ACM 18 January 2024; IEEE-CS 22 January 2024; AAAI 22 February 2024. The AAAI endorsement is new relative to prior revisions and reflects the inclusion of AI as a first-class knowledge area.
- The consolidated report is published with DOI `10.1145/3664191`; a Spanish edition is offered from the site's book downloads.
- Knowledge areas carry stable codes (AL, AR, AI, DM, FPL, GIT, HCI, MSF, NC, OS, PDC, SEC, SEP, SDF, SE, SPD, and SF), which makes them usable as mapping keys in a coverage matrix.
- The vision statement asserts parity: skills and professional dispositions are "just as important as the underlying knowledge."
- The task force explicitly acknowledges that the competency model was "yet-to-be-designed" for computer science at the time of writing and that an existing model would be evaluated, selected and adapted rather than invented.
- Curricular Guidelines answer *what*; Curricular Practices answer *how and why*. These are deliberately separate publications.
- A dedicated treatment of generative AI in the curriculum appears in Section 4.

## Method and evidence base
CS2023 was produced by a steering committee and a large task force operating through a documented process — the site publishes a vision statement, a principles-and-processes section, knowledge-area subcommittees, and survey results gathered from the community. The knowledge model is derived by revision from CS2013 rather than built from scratch. The competency framework is adapted from prior work in the CC2020 line rather than freshly constructed. Endorsement by three professional societies is the legitimation mechanism; there is no empirical validation of the model against graduate outcomes, and none is claimed.

## Vocabulary the source introduces
- **Competency model** — a framing of curriculum in which what a graduate can do is expressed as more than accumulated knowledge.
- **Professional dispositions** — habitual professional behaviours the task force argues are as important as knowledge, and whose under-acceptance in computing education it names as the problem CS2023 is trying to address.
- **Knowledge area / knowledge unit** — the hierarchical units of the body of knowledge, each carrying a stable code.
- **Curricular guidelines vs curricular practices** — what should be covered vs how and why it should be covered.
- **Characteristics of graduates** — a design-section construct describing the intended graduate profile, distinct from both knowledge units and competencies.

## What to remember
- CS2023 gives an internationally endorsed, cite-able warrant for treating dispositions and skills as curricular objects rather than as by-products.
- It concedes openly that the discipline has not yet accepted this, which means the argument still has to be made locally — but it can be made with a flagship document behind it.
- The knowledge model, not the competency model, is the operational artefact. Plan coverage mapping against knowledge areas and use the competency language for justification.
- Sixteen-plus knowledge areas with stable codes make a clean basis for a syllabus coverage matrix that a committee can audit.
- AI is a first-class knowledge area with AAAI as a co-endorsing society, and generative AI has its own pedagogy section.
- The guidelines/practices split is a useful separation of concerns for the course's own documentation.

## Why it matters for the course
CS2023 supplies the disciplinary legitimacy for the half of this course that is not about algorithms — the judgement, communication, collaboration and professional-behaviour material that is otherwise hard to defend as "real" computer science. Read alongside the proposed ABET Criterion 5 language on professional dispositions, it forms a pair: CS2023 supplies the disciplinary argument, ABET supplies the compliance requirement, and the two use compatible vocabulary. It is also the document that establishes that AI content belongs in the core body of knowledge rather than in a specialist elective, and the presence of Software Engineering, Security, Society Ethics and the Profession, and Data Management as peer knowledge areas is a direct warrant for an AI-systems course that spans all four.

## How to use it in course design
- Build a coverage matrix keyed on CS2023 knowledge-area codes; for an AI systems course expect substantive hits on AI, SE, SEC, DM, SEP, PDC and MSF, and be honest where coverage is thin.
- Borrow the guidelines/practices distinction in the course's own documents: one artefact stating what is covered and assessed, a separate one stating how and why it is taught.
- Use the vision statement's parity claim verbatim when defending disposition-oriented assessment to a curriculum committee; it is short, quotable and carries three societies' endorsement.
- Cross-reference the Section 4 generative-AI material when writing the course's own AI-tool policy, so the policy is anchored in curricular guidance rather than in local preference.
- Do not attempt to grade against the competency framework directly. Use it to justify the shape of assessment, and grade against locally defined, observable behaviours.

## Limits and cautions
- CS2023 is guidance, not a standard. It confers no compliance and cannot be pointed to as a requirement; ABET does that job.
- The competency framework is explicitly adapted and, by the task force's own admission, was not previously designed for computer science. Its constructs are less settled than the knowledge model's.
- Disposition language is broad by nature. It licenses assessing behaviour but does not tell you which behaviours, at what standard, or with what evidence — that translation is entirely local work.
- Because the document is a decennial revision, it lags fast-moving areas; a report finalised in early 2024 predates most of the agentic-systems practice this course covers.
- Knowledge-area coverage mapping is easy to game. A single lecture mentioning a topic is not coverage, and committees increasingly ask for the assessment evidence behind a mapping claim.
