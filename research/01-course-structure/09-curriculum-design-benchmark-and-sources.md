# Cliff Notes — 09 Curriculum Design Benchmark and Sources

> Internal working document, not an external source. It benchmarks the existing material package against the accreditation standards and exemplar courses now read in full, and states the design requirement that follows. Every external claim cites the note holding the verified source reading.

## What this document is
The benchmark. It answers one question: what is the distance between the material package that currently exists in this repository and a defensible degree-level curriculum? The answer is not "more content". The gap is structural, and the benchmark exists to say precisely which structures are missing and what evidence establishes that they are required rather than merely desirable.

## The argument in full
The existing package is a strong enablement track. It curates good technical material across transformer internals, retrieval, alignment, evaluation, agent systems and platform specifics, and it is organised well enough that a motivated reader can work through it. That is a real achievement and it should not be discounted. But an enablement track and a curriculum differ in kind, not in degree, and the difference is that a curriculum makes claims about what its graduates can do. Once that claim is made, everything about the required structure changes, because a claim about capability has to be supported by evidence of capability.

The accreditation standards make this concrete rather than philosophical. ABET's Criterion 3 lists five baccalaureate student outcomes and every one is a verb applied to a situation: analyse a complex computing problem and identify solutions, design and implement and evaluate a computing-based solution, communicate effectively in a variety of professional contexts, recognise professional responsibilities and make informed judgements based on legal and ethical principles, and function effectively as a member or leader of a team. The computer science programme criteria add a sixth about applying theory and development fundamentals to produce solutions. Nothing in that list can be demonstrated by a reading list, and nothing in it can be demonstrated by an assessment that only checks recall. Three of the six are not even technical. See [abet-cac-and-cs2023.md](/research/01-course-structure/abet-cac-and-cs2023.md).

The forthcoming changes sharpen the requirement further. ABET's proposed Criterion 5 addition requires "a comprehensive project or experience appropriate to the discipline which: 1) builds on technical knowledge and skills acquired in prior advanced course work, and 2) enables the application of appropriate professional dispositions", supported by a new definition stating that "professional dispositions are behaviors desired in the workplace". Approved by the Computing Area Delegation on 25 October 2024 with comments open to 15 June 2025 and first applied in the 2026-27 review cycle, this is the standard a course being designed now should be designed against. It requires an integrative experience, positions it after the coursework it builds on, and requires it to occasion observable non-technical behaviour. A reading package satisfies none of the three.

CS2023 supplies the disciplinary rather than regulatory version of the same argument, and states it more bluntly than the accreditor does: "Computer Science is a professional discipline where skills and professional dispositions are just as important as the underlying knowledge", followed immediately by the concession that the field "has not yet widely accepted the benefits of professional dispositions to the same extent" as other professional disciplines. Its vision statement also names the distinction this whole benchmark turns on — "Whereas Curricular Guidelines list what should be covered in the curriculum, Curricular Practices describe how and why they should be covered" — and treats the second as a separate publication stream rather than an implicit consequence of the first. The current package is entirely on the guidelines side of that line.

If the standards establish that a spine is required, the exemplars establish that one is achievable, and comparing three of them shows how much the choice of spine determines. **CMU 17-645** builds its spine from a live movie recommendation service serving one million active users, running for several weeks and "graded on how it holds up under real load and changing environment conditions", with lectures sequenced against that system's lifecycle, weekly technology-specific labs, four milestones, oral verification as an ordinary rubric item, specification grading, a sixteen-token extension and resubmission economy, and no final exam. **Stanford CS336** builds its spine from implementation volume instead, taking students through building a language model from scratch on the explicit premise that "the amount of code you will write will be at least an order of magnitude greater than for other classes", with five assignments, public repositories, leaderboards on three of them, and a restrictive AI policy that even asks students to disable autocomplete. **The Berkeley LLM Agents MOOC** builds no spine at all in the assessment sense — twelve guest lectures with completion-graded quizzes, an effort-based written article, three lab assignments, and a five-tier certificate structure, with the course stating plainly that "completing the assignments… is completely optional." All three are legitimate designs and all three are worth reading. Only CMU's produces evidence of the kind ABET's outcomes require, and that is the relevant comparison. Notes at [cmu-17-645-ml-in-production-ai-engineering.md](/research/01-course-structure/cmu-17-645-ml-in-production-ai-engineering.md), [research/07-accreditation-exemplars/stanford-cs336-language-modeling-materials.md](/research/07-accreditation-exemplars/stanford-cs336-language-modeling-materials.md) and [research/07-accreditation-exemplars/berkeley-advanced-llm-agent-course-materials.md](/research/07-accreditation-exemplars/berkeley-advanced-llm-agent-course-materials.md).

Two further observations from the exemplar comparison are worth recording because they cut against intuition. First, CMU has **no formal prerequisites and explicitly does not require a software engineering background**, handling a heterogeneous intake through an anonymous ungraded ten-question diagnostic that returns targeted readings. Rigour is enforced at the exit through evidence, not at the entrance through gatekeeping. Second, CMU's individual assignments are weighted at 35% against the group project's 30%, so the project spine does not swallow individual accountability. Both are structural answers to objections that are usually treated as reasons not to attempt this kind of design.

The benchmark's conclusion is therefore a design requirement, not a critique. The course needs an assessment spine capable of certifying judgement, a project spine capable of generating the evidence that assessment needs, and a documented separation between what is covered and how and why it is covered. The reading lists are not thereby devalued — they become the material that serves those spines rather than the thing that substitutes for them.

## What the benchmark establishes
- **Required, with external warrant:** occasions where the five or six ABET outcomes are performed and observed; an integrative experience positioned after the coursework it builds on; defined and observable professional dispositions; a documented separation of assessment from evaluation in ABET's sense.
- **Demonstrated achievable by exemplar:** a live-system spine with lifecycle-ordered content; oral verification run as an ordinary rubric item at office hours; specification grading; open tool policy made safe by oral evidence; heterogeneous intake handled by diagnosis rather than prerequisites.
- **Present in the current package:** technical content coverage; source curation; a coherent topic map.
- **Absent from the current package:** any evidence-producing mechanism; any project spine; any assessment design; any disposition definition; any statement of what a graduate of this material can do.

## Key specifics carried from the sources
- ABET Criterion 5: "The curriculum requirements specify topics, but do not prescribe specific courses." Architecture is free; the envelope is not.
- Credit envelopes: 30 semester credit hours of computing minimum at baccalaureate level; 40 for computer science plus 15 of mathematics and statistics.
- Proposed comprehensive-project clause and the professional-dispositions definition, first applied in the 2026-27 cycle.
- CMU: twelve units; 35/30/25/10 weighting with no final exam; one million users; four milestones; twelve stated hours per week; $20-100 per month stated AI subscription cost.
- Stanford CS336: five units; five assignments; leaderboards on A1, A2 and A4; six late days with a maximum of three per assignment; AI permitted for conceptual questions but not for solving, with autocomplete discouraged.
- Berkeley: twelve guest lectures; five certificate tiers from Trailblazer to Honorary; quizzes graded on completion; assignments optional.

## What to remember
- Strong content is not a curriculum. The difference is the claim about capability and the evidence supporting it.
- ABET's outcomes are behavioural verbs; three of six are non-technical. Reading cannot demonstrate them.
- The proposed 2026-27 changes require an integrative experience that occasions observable dispositions.
- CS2023 says dispositions matter as much as knowledge, and says the field has not accepted this.
- Three credible exemplars pick three different spines; only the live-system spine produces the evidence the standards require.
- Rigour can sit at the exit rather than the entrance — CMU has no prerequisites and still runs a demanding course.
- Individual weighting can exceed group weighting even when a group project is the spine.
- The output of this benchmark is a design requirement, not a critique of the existing material.

## Why it matters for the course
This document is why the redesign exists. Without it, the obvious response to any perceived shortfall is to add more material, which would make the package longer without making it a curriculum. The benchmark redirects the effort towards the structures that are actually missing, and it does so with external warrant rather than preference, which matters when the design has to be defended to someone who did not participate in making it.

## Limits and cautions
- ABET criteria are programme-level. A single course contributes to them and cannot satisfy them alone; claiming otherwise misrepresents the standard.
- The proposed ABET changes were in the comment period as consulted and may change before the 2026-27 cycle.
- No exemplar publishes effectiveness data. Longevity and openness establish workability, not superiority.
- CMU's structure assumes graduate-level students, substantial TA staffing and infrastructure for a service with real users. None of these transfers automatically.
- This is a planning synthesis. Every claim in it should be traced back to the source note cited before it is used in an external argument.
