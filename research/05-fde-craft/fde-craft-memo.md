# FDE / Engagement Craft Memo

Date: 2026-07-25

## Summary
The FDE role is not software engineering with a customer on the side. It is discovery, negotiation, alignment, and generalization work with code as the instrument.

## End-to-end summary
This memo reframes the FDE role as a field-facing discipline rather than a slight variation on software engineering. The job is to discover what is actually happening in the customer environment, negotiate access and scope, align stakeholders with different incentives, decide what should generalize, and use code as the instrument rather than the definition of the role. That makes engagement craft a serious professional skill set, not a soft supplement to implementation.

## Essential skills
- Contextual inquiry and discovery interviews
- Data-access negotiation
- Stakeholder mapping and organizational politics
- Domain-language acquisition
- Productization versus custom-solution judgment
- Change management and handover
- Demo craft and workshop facilitation

## Main gaps in the current notes
- Discovery methodology is undernamed
- Political decision-making is not explicit
- Data access is treated too technically
- Handover and adoption are missing as first-class outcomes
- The course does not teach when not to build

## What to remember
- Discovery and negotiation are core FDE work.
- Organizational politics are part of the job.
- Adoption and handover are outcomes, not afterthoughts.
- Knowing when not to build is part of competence.

## Why it matters for the course
This memo sets the FDE strand’s scope and helps prevent the course from collapsing into pure implementation.

## Limits
It is a scope memo, so the detailed craft sources should be read as the practical methods behind it.
