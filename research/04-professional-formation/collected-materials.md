# Collected materials — professional formation

Index of the source notes in this folder. Each entry links to full Cliff Notes with citation, verified findings, teaching guidance and verification status. Numbers below are the verified headline figures only; see each note for provenance and caveats.

## Working synthesis

Assess formation through observable behaviours, not self-report: help-seeking latency; error disclosure quality; calibration before and after AI use; ability to justify restraint; ability to recognise limits and escalate.

The evidence in this folder supports four positions. First, that self-efficacy is built by mastery experience rather than encouragement, which argues against motivational messaging and for structured exposure to difficulty. Second, that error-encouraging and failure-first designs outperform error-avoidant and instruction-first designs on transfer, with moderate to large effects and well-defined boundary conditions. Third, that practice volume explains far less of professional performance than the deliberate-practice literature claimed, which undercuts hours-based models of expertise. Fourth, that self-assessment of AI-assisted productivity is unreliable in a specific and measurable direction, which makes calibration a first-class teaching objective rather than a soft skill.

The assessment consequence is that judgement and calibration must be assessed in situ, through narrative entrustment decisions supported by accumulated observation, and that doing so requires importing qualitative rigour criteria rather than abandoning rigour.

## Source-by-source Cliff Notes

**[bandura-self-efficacy.md](/research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md)** — Bandura 1977, *Psychological Review*. Four sources of efficacy expectations: performance accomplishments, vicarious experience, verbal persuasion, emotional arousal. Performance accomplishments are the most dependable. **The 1977 paper is still paywalled and unread.** Bandura's 1995 edited volume *Self-efficacy in Changing Societies* (Cambridge University Press) was read in full on 2026-07-30 and is now the verified source for the four sources, the primacy of mastery experience, guided mastery and collective efficacy. It does not rank the other three sources against each other, and a restatement eighteen years later by the same author is a different text, so the volume does not verify the paper.

**[keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md)** — Keith & Frese 2008, *Journal of Applied Psychology*. Meta-analysis, 24 studies, N = 2,183. Overall d = 0.44; post-training transfer d = 0.56; adaptive transfer d = 0.80. Active exploration and error encouragement each independently effective, though the authors offer that as an interpretation and ask for further study. **Read in full on 2026-07-30 from a locally held PDF**; the magnitudes are verified and the corrections below are not.

**[sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)** — Sinha & Kapur 2021, *Review of Educational Research*. 53 studies, 166 comparisons. Problem-solving-before-instruction beats the reverse at Hedges' g = 0.36 [0.20, 0.51]; g = 0.37–0.58 under high-fidelity Productive Failure. **Reverses to favour instruction-first for grades 2–5 and domain-general skills.** Open access, but **verified at abstract level only — both journal articles remain unread.** Kapur's 2025 book *Productive Failure* (Jossey-Bass) was read in full on 2026-07-30; it states the design features to be domain general and does not mention the young-learner reversal at all.

**[macnamara-deliberate-practice-limits.md](/research/04-professional-formation/macnamara-deliberate-practice-limits.md)** — Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald 2014, *Psychological Science*. Deliberate practice explains 26% of variance in games, 21% music, 18% sports, 4% education, **less than 1% in professions**. Corrigendum 2018, no substantive change. Paywalled; abstract via Europe PMC.

**[edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)** — Edmondson 1999, *Administrative Science Quarterly*. 51 teams. Psychological safety predicts learning behaviour; team efficacy does not; learning behaviour mediates performance. Antecedents: context support and team leader coaching. Green OA via Harvard DASH, but **the paper was never retrieved and remains unread**, so all four of those findings still rest on its abstract. Edmondson's 2019 book *The Fearless Organization* (Wiley) was read in full on 2026-07-30 and restates neither the efficacy discriminant nor the mediation chain.

**[frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)** — Frazier et al. 2017, *Personnel Psychology*. 136 independent samples, 22,000+ individuals, ~5,000 groups. Establishes incremental validity of psychological safety over positive leader relations and work engagement; tests homology across levels. Repository OA via ODU Digital Commons.

**[metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md](/research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md)** — METR 2025, arXiv 2507.09089. 16 experienced developers, 246 real tasks. Forecast 24% faster, self-estimated 20% faster after the fact, **actually 19% slower**. Experts predicted 38–39% speedup. Open access, CC BY 4.0.

**[dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md](/research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md)** — Dell'Acqua et al. 2023, HBS Working Paper 24-013. 758 BCG consultants. Inside the frontier: 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, higher quality. Outside it: **19% less likely to produce correct solutions**. Abstract open; working paper PDF available.

**[govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/04-professional-formation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)** — Govaerts & van der Vleuten 2013, *Medical Education*. Work-based assessment is a socially situated interpretive act, not measurement. Constructivist-interpretivist approaches require qualitative rigour criteria to generate trustworthy evidence. Bronze OA; full structured abstract verified.

**[ten-cate-epas.md](/research/04-professional-formation/ten-cate-epas.md)** — ten Cate 2013, *JGME* ("Nuts and bolts of EPAs") — **no abstract exists and full text was unretrievable**. Content grounded on AMEE Guide No. 99 (ten Cate et al. 2015, *Medical Teacher*, paywalled, abstract verified): EPA identification, elaboration and validation; matrix mapping against competencies; entrustment decision-making across levels of permitted autonomy ranging from full supervision to supervising a junior.

## Corrections and open items

Four of this folder's sources were obtained and read in full on 2026-07-30, and each reading corrected the synthesis it replaced. The corrections are recorded here because they are the most valuable output of that pass, and because three of them had already reached teaching guidance.

- **Keith and Frese: the within-training advantage is absent, not reversed.** The previous note said that error-avoidant instruction looks better during training itself, and used that as fact in three sections. The measured within-training effect is d = −0.15, non-significant, with a 90 per cent interval spanning zero; the authors claim only that immediate performance may not differ or may be depressed. Two omissions bound what the source can be used for: **no study used a no-training control**, so every effect is relative to another training design, and **no study measured on-the-job performance**, so nothing here reaches professional practice — the authors flag that themselves and label their own reasoning about it speculation. A third tested moderator, clarity of task-generated feedback, was missing from the previous note entirely, and it is the boundary condition most relevant to this curriculum, because the method depends on the task revealing its own failures and the systems taught here frequently do not.
- **Edmondson: the 2019 definition drops "shared" and "team".** The author's current formulation is the belief that the work environment is safe for interpersonal risk taking. The narrower 1999 wording that this folder had been treating as definitional, and instructing readers to hold as precise, is twenty years old and belongs to the unread paper. The team-efficacy discriminant and the mediation of performance by learning behaviour are that paper's two principal contributions, the book restates neither, and any page leaning on them is leaning on an unread abstract.
- **Kapur: the design principles are domain-general, and a prohibition is withdrawn.** The previous note converted the meta-analysis's moderator on domain-general *skills as an outcome* into a prohibition on using productive failure for domain-general *subject areas*, and then issued teaching guidance on the strength of the conversion — that the course should not use the method for structured communication or interview technique. The book states that the design features "remain domain general" and names writing and business communication among its applications. **The guidance is withdrawn.** What can honestly be said is that the two sources emphasise different things, that the meta-analysis has not been read past its abstract, and that the question is open.
- **Bandura: the productive zone is not "subjectively threatening and objectively safe".** The previous note stated that principle as a rule of curriculum design and it had reached the teaching guidance. Bandura writes that only-easy successes produce brittle efficacy and that a resilient sense of efficacy requires overcoming obstacles through perseverant effort. Two consequences follow. He treats overestimation of one's own capability as adaptive rather than as a fault, confining the hazard to activities where mistakes are costly or injurious, so the previous recommendation to grade students on calibration removes an adaptive resource outside that carve-out. And collective efficacy — the 1995 volume's principal contribution, and the part of the theory most applicable to team-delivered work — was absent from this repository entirely, because the 1977 paper predates the construct.

**Open.** The 1977 Bandura paper, both Kapur journal articles and the 1999 Edmondson paper are still unread. Where a claim exists only in one of them, the note that carries it says so, and any page using it must declare it unverified.
