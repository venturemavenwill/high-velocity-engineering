# Professional Formation Memo

Date: 2026-07-25

## Summary
Generic growth-mindset training is not a strong foundation for the course. The evidence supports training designs and observable behaviors instead: error management, productive failure, psychological safety, self-efficacy, calibration, and EPA-style entrustment.

## End-to-end summary
This memo rejects generic growth-mindset framing as the center of the professional-formation strand and replaces it with observable learning and work behaviors. The strand should teach how people actually develop professional competence: through controlled error, productive failure, psychologically safe participation, real mastery experiences, calibration against objective performance, and eventual entrustment. That makes the formation strand something the course can assess, not just something it can encourage.

## What to teach instead
- Error management training
- Productive failure sequencing
- Observable psychological safety behaviors
- Self-efficacy through real shipping
- Calibration on AI-assisted work
- Entrustable Professional Activities with a supervision scale

## What to assess
- Help-seeking latency
- Error disclosure quality
- Ability to justify when not to use AI
- Ability to recognize personal limits and escalate
- Ability to defend decisions in oral review

## What not to center
- A standalone growth-mindset module
- Deliberate practice as a generic spine
- Self-report surveys as the primary evidence of formation

## What to remember
- Formation must be observable.
- Real work matters more than abstract motivation.
- AI use should be calibrated, not celebrated uncritically.
- Entrustment is the right end state for professional growth.

## Why it matters for the course
This memo gives the course a formation model that can actually be assessed.

## Limits
It is a directional synthesis, so the source notes provide the evidentiary depth.
