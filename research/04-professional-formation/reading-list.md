# Reading list — professional formation

## Core references

- **Bandura, A. (1977).** Self-efficacy: Toward a unifying theory of behavioral change. *Psychological Review*, 84(2), 191–215. DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.84.2.191 — the four sources of efficacy expectations, and why mastery experience outranks persuasion. **Still paywalled and unread.**
- **Bandura, A. (ed.) (1995).** *Self-efficacy in Changing Societies.* Cambridge University Press — **read in full on 2026-07-30 from a locally held PDF**, and now the verified source for the four sources, the primacy of mastery experience, guided mastery and collective efficacy. It enumerates the other three sources without ranking them against each other, restates the 1977 paper in the author's later words but does not verify it, and contradicts the design principle the earlier synthesis drew from the 1977 abstract.
- **Keith, N., & Frese, M. (2008).** Effectiveness of error management training: A meta-analysis. *Journal of Applied Psychology*, 93(1), 59–69. DOI 10.1037/0021-9010.93.1.59 — d = 0.44 overall, d = 0.80 for adaptive transfer. **Read in full on 2026-07-30 from a locally held PDF**, and the magnitudes are verified. The within-training effect is d = −0.15 and non-significant; no study used a no-training control and none measured on-the-job performance.
- **Sinha, T., & Kapur, M. (2021).** When problem solving followed by instruction works: Evidence for productive failure. *Review of Educational Research*. DOI 10.3102/00346543211019105 — g = 0.36, and the conditions under which it reverses. **Still unread; verified against the open-access abstract only.**
- **Kapur, M. (2008).** Productive failure. *Cognition and Instruction*, 26(3), 379–424. DOI 10.1080/07370000802212669 — the founding classroom experiment. **Still unread and closed.** The 2025 book does not cite it, and its designation as *the* founding study is not corroborated by the author's own origin narrative.
- **Kapur, M. (2025).** *Productive Failure: Unlocking Deeper Learning Through the Science of Failing.* Jossey-Bass — **read in full on 2026-07-30 from a locally held EPUB.** The 4As, the design layers and the two boundary conditions the author actually states; and the claim that the design features are domain-general, which withdraws a prohibition the earlier synthesis had issued.
- **Macnamara, B. N., Hambrick, D. Z., & Oswald, F. L. (2014).** Deliberate practice and performance in music, games, sports, education, and professions: A meta-analysis. *Psychological Science*, 25(8), 1608–1618. DOI 10.1177/0956797614535810 — practice explains less than 1% of variance in professions.
- **Edmondson, A. (1999).** Psychological safety and learning behavior in work teams. *Administrative Science Quarterly*, 44(2), 350–383. DOI 10.2307/2666999 — the founding study; learning behaviour as the mediator. **Still unread**; the green open-access copy at Harvard DASH was never retrieved, so both the efficacy discriminant and the mediation chain rest on the abstract alone.
- **Edmondson, A. (2019).** *The Fearless Organization: Creating Psychological Safety in the Workplace for Learning, Innovation, and Growth.* John Wiley & Sons — **read in full on 2026-07-30 from a locally held PDF.** It supplies the author's current definition, which drops *shared* and *team*, the three failure archetypes and the leader's tool kit, but restates neither of the 1999 paper's two theoretical contributions.
- **Frazier, M. L., Fainshmidt, S., Klinger, R. L., Pezeshkan, A., & Vracheva, V. (2017).** Psychological safety: A meta-analytic review and extension. *Personnel Psychology*, 70(1), 113–165. DOI 10.1111/peps.12183 — 136 samples; incremental validity established.
- **METR (2025).** Measuring the impact of early-2025 AI on experienced open-source developer productivity. arXiv:2507.09089 — the 19% slowdown that participants experienced as a 20% speedup.
- **Dell'Acqua, F., et al. (2023).** Navigating the jagged technological frontier. Harvard Business School Working Paper 24-013 — 758 consultants; gains inside the frontier, 19% accuracy loss outside it.
- **Govaerts, M., & van der Vleuten, C. P. M. (2013).** Validity in work-based assessment: Expanding our horizons. *Medical Education*, 47(12), 1164–1174. DOI 10.1111/medu.12289 — assessment as a socially situated interpretive act, with qualitative rigour criteria attached.
- **ten Cate, O. (2013).** Nuts and bolts of entrustable professional activities. *Journal of Graduate Medical Education*, 5(1), 157–158. DOI 10.4300/jgme-d-12-00380.1 — no abstract; see AMEE Guide No. 99 below for the substantive treatment.
- **ten Cate, O., et al. (2015).** Curriculum development for the workplace using Entrustable Professional Activities: AMEE Guide No. 99. *Medical Teacher*, 37(11), 983–1002. DOI 10.3109/0142159X.2015.1060308 — identification, elaboration, validation, matrix mapping, and entrustment decision-making.

## What this folder should answer

- **What formation evidence is actually supported.** Mastery experience over persuasion; error-encouraging and failure-first training over error-avoidant and instruction-first, within stated boundaries; psychological safety as a precondition for learning behaviour. Weakly supported: practice volume as the driver of professional expertise; generic mindset messaging as an intervention.
- **What should replace generic growth-mindset messaging.** Structured mastery experiences with graduated difficulty; explicit error-management framing during training rather than error avoidance; productive-failure sequencing for domain-specific technical content; team conditions that make disclosure safe.
- **How to assess judgement and calibration in context.** Entrustable Professional Activities assessed through narrative entrustment decisions at designated supervision levels, evidenced by accumulated observation across assessors, and validated against qualitative rigour criteria rather than psychometric reliability.
