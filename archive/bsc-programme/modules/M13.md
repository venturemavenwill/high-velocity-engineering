# M13 · The Economics of Inference

**Quarter:** [Q7](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q7.md) · **Seminar days:** [S061](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S061.md)–[S065](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S065.md) · **Complexity class:** 5
**Strands:** IS · SY · ME
**Platform anchor:** Provisioned throughput for Foundry Models · the API Management AI gateway · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (write) · Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## The central difficulty

Students arrive holding cost as an afterthought — a number somebody in finance produces after the engineering is done, from inputs the engineer does not control.

That belief is not lazy. It is an accurate description of most of the organisations they have seen, and it survives because in most software the belief is nearly harmless: the marginal cost of serving one more request is small enough that nobody has to reason about it. Inference breaks the exemption. The marginal cost of one more request is large, it varies by more than an order of magnitude with the *shape* of the request, and it is set by decisions the engineer makes and nobody else can make.

**The module's stance: latency, throughput and cost are the same decision viewed from three positions, and a promise about one is a constraint on the others.** An engineer who has promised a ninety-ninth-percentile latency has chosen an operating point, which has chosen a batching policy, which has chosen a memory allocation, which has chosen a monthly bill. They usually do not know they have done this, and the bill arrives as a surprise attributed to somebody else.

The difficulty is not that this chain is hard to follow. It is short and every link is arithmetic. The difficulty is that **the links point in the direction students never traverse.** They will happily reason from a cost target to a latency consequence when asked. They do not spontaneously reason from a latency promise to a cost commitment, because in their experience latency is engineering and cost is not.

The module's second difficulty is temporal and is the reason it carries the programme's advance to class 5. **Every decision in this module is checked later.** A cost model built at S061 is billed at S065. A commitment shape chosen at S064 has a break-even that is knowable at week 68. No day in M13 lets the student find out inside the day whether they were right, and that is a different kind of discomfort from anything the programme has previously asked for.

## What the platform makes exact

This is the most naturally practical module in the programme, and the reason is that its four central claims are not analogies for something the vendor documents — they *are* what the vendor documents, in the vendor's own units, with a consequence attached that arrives on a date.

| Module claim | Where it is a documented platform fact |
|---|---|
| Cost per request is a function of shape, not a number | The published PTU sizing method takes request shape, an output-to-input ratio and a cache rate, and states that output tokens require more processing capacity than input tokens and that cached input tokens consume none |
| Every scheduling policy chooses who waits | The gateway's token limit policy is enforced against a `counter-key` you choose; whoever is on the wrong side of that key is who waits |
| Reserving against the worst case costs you concurrency | A managed deployment estimates an in-flight request's cost from its prompt tokens plus the declared `max_tokens`, and the documentation states that leaving `max_tokens` unspecified can produce lower concurrency than expected |
| Capacity, quota and budget are three numbers with three owners | Quota is documented as a policy limit with no cost; capacity as what is actually deployable; and the documentation states that having quota does not guarantee capacity and that a reservation does not guarantee it either |

The fourth line is the module's spine and it is now literal. [S064](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S064.md) does not assert that quota is not capacity. **A student is refused, reads the refusal, and finds out which of the two refused them** — and then reads a second sentence saying that buying a reservation would not have helped.

**Every commercial figure in this module is perishable and is dated at the point of use.** No price, per-model ratio, minimum deployment size, throughput divisor or discount appears anywhere in M13 as a taught fact. Where a student needs one they retrieve it through the Learn MCP Server and record the retrieval date in the same field as the figure, per specification rule 2. **A figure without its retrieval date fails the specification on every day of this module.**

## What the cohort operates, and what it is only shown

**Operated.** Standard deployments and their token accounting, including cached-token reporting. The gateway end to end: `llm-token-limit`, `llm-emit-token-metric` with dimensions, backend pools with priority routing, circuit breaker rules, semantic caching. Azure Monitor metrics and their splitting dimensions. Cost Management. The model-capacities read API and the portal capacity calculator. A deployment attempt that is refused.

**Demonstrated rather than operated.** Creating a provisioned deployment. Spillover and its response headers. The provisioned utilisation metric under real load. Purchasing a reservation. Each requires quota, capacity and money the programme does not hold. **None of the four is assessed on any day in this module**, and each day states which of its material falls on which side of the line before the learning task begins.

What that costs the module is argued rather than minimised: [WP-064](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 records that the quarter's most important refusal is the one the cohort cannot personally receive.

## Why this module sits here

**It requires M11 entirely, and S053 specifically.** Where the time goes is a question about the assembly. A student holding the architecture as a black box can be told that generating a token is memory-bandwidth-bound and cannot derive it, and what cannot be derived cannot be re-derived when the architecture changes — which it will.

**It requires M04 in full.** [S016](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S016.md)'s hierarchy, [S018](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S018.md)'s allocation cliff and [S019](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S019.md)'s saturation curve are not analogies for the serving problem; they are the serving problem, in different units. M04 was placed in year one specifically so that this module could retrieve rather than teach it, and the [Module Map](/archive/bsc-programme/program/07-Module-Map.md) says so.

**It requires M12's system in production.** Every day here profiles, batches, allocates or budgets a real service under real traffic, and the service must be the students' own or the consequence is simulated.

**It requires M06's measurement discipline and S029 in particular.** A cost model is an instrument. It has a construct, an error budget, a sampling scheme and a set of things it cannot detect, and a student who builds one without those has built a spreadsheet.

**It is not placed later** because M14's release decisions assume a cost position exists, and because Q8's exhaustion material at [S077](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S077.md) is an adversary moving an operating point the student must already have chosen.

## Every day produces a build

Four of the five days end with something running that did not exist that morning, built with Copilot in agent mode through a named MCP server against a named service, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

| Day | What ships |
|---|---|
| S061 | A per-class cost model that reads its own inputs from the substrate's telemetry, with a committed check date |
| S062 | A gateway policy — a token limit with a chosen counter key, a routing rule, a refusal and a client signal — in front of the substrate's serving path |
| S063 | An admission configuration with a measured concurrency ceiling and a declared reservation discipline |
| S064 | A capacity commitment, in a deployable form, with its four quantities separated and its break-even computed from a dated rate |
| S065 | Nothing new. The entrustment artefact carries the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement from the four preceding builds into the defence |

**Every artefact in this module carries the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.** The intervention record's fail condition is the standard one: **no entries fails, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.** In a cost module the escape is usually cheap and usually correct — a model that asserts its own inputs against the platform's reported token counts has built the detection rather than performed it, and is accepted.

The frontier statements accumulate into the module's own finding, which is that **the agent is good at the arithmetic and bad at the ownership.** It will produce a correct break-even and will not ask whose money it is. It will size a deployment and will not check whether capacity exists. Whether that pattern holds is measured rather than asserted, and the instrument is the S065 phase 8 tally.

## Task-class variability plan

At class 5 the student decides whether to proceed. Variability is therefore in **what makes proceeding a live question** — a different constraint binds each day, and the student cannot reuse a stance.

- **S061** varies the request shape: four shapes over one deployment, four different binding resources, and a cost per unit output that varies by more than an order of magnitude across them.
- **S062** varies the admission and routing policy: four gateway configurations over one trace, four throughput-latency positions, and four different answers to who waits.
- **S063** varies the reservation discipline: four `max_tokens` and token-estimation policies over one budget, four concurrency ceilings, and one cliff whose position must be inferred rather than read off.
- **S064** varies the *kind* of exhaustion: four situations that all present as running out of capacity, of which only one is a capacity event.
- **S065** varies the payer: the same cost argument to two parties with incompatible interests, **one of whom loses the money if the student succeeds.**

The plan's constraint is that no two days may be resolved by the same move. A student who learns at S062 to buy headroom finds at S063 that the headroom is unreachable under their reservation discipline, and at S064 that they are not permitted to buy it — and then that permission would not have got it for them either.

## Load profile

**High throughout, and unusually flat.** The module has no easy day. S061 and S063 are technically heavy; S062 is arithmetically heavy; S064 is conceptually heavy and technically light; S065 is an assessment occasion carrying four days of prerequisites.

The distinctive load is **cross-quarter retrieval at high density.** Four of the five days retrieve from M04, which is five quarters old. This is the longest-gap retrieval the programme performs at load-bearing depth, and it is deliberate: the [Seminar Day Index](/archive/bsc-programme/program/08-Seminar-Day-Index.md) records M04 as placed early precisely so that M13 can retrieve it. If the retrieval fails, the module must teach queueing and paging from scratch inside a class-5 module, which it cannot do.

The standing load reduction is that **the service, the traffic and the measurement harness are the same across all five days.** Nothing new is introduced except the constraint.

A second reduction is that **every day supplies its measurements already taken.** Students choose, predict, model and defend; they do not spend phase time waiting for a load test to finish, except at S063 step 4 where the waiting is the point.

## Module-level predicted failure modes

**Cost reasoned forward and never backward.** The module's defining failure. Students will compute the cost of a chosen configuration correctly and will not derive the cost commitment implied by a latency promise they have already made. Countermeasure: every day's phase 5 requires the student to state a promise first and its cost consequence second, in that order, and **S062's specification fails an artefact that states a latency target without its throughput and cost consequence.**

**The aggregate cost per request.** The single number most likely to be produced and most likely to be wrong, because cost per request varies by more than an order of magnitude with request shape and the mix is not stable. Countermeasure: **every cost figure in this module must be reported per request class**, and a figure reported only in aggregate fails. This is [S053](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S053.md) point 6 applied to money, and the module states the inheritance explicitly.

**Perishable technique memorised as durable content.** The module's largest teaching risk, and the platform anchor sharpens it rather than softening it. Serving-system memory management, batching strategy, quota mechanics, provisioning modes and every commercial term are listed as **perishable or semi-durable** in the [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) and will change within one cohort's passage. Countermeasure: **the module's durable content is written as measurement obligations rather than as facts about named systems.** A student is never assessed on which technique is current, on what a PTU costs, or on how many of them a model needs; they are assessed on having measured which resource binds, having located the cliff, having dated every figure they used, and having stated what their model cannot detect. Every named product, policy and parameter in every day is marked perishable inline.

**Capacity, quota and budget collapsed into one number.** S064's risk and the module's most consequential professional error, because it sends the student to the wrong party with the wrong request. Countermeasure: **the S065 artefact fails if the three appear as one number**, and the failure condition is published before S061.

**Accepting an agent's capacity arithmetic.** New to this module and specific to it. Asked to size a deployment, an agent will produce a plausible PTU count from a sizing formula, and it will produce it whether or not the quota exists, whether or not the capacity exists, and whether or not anybody has agreed to pay. **Each of those three is a different party's decision and none of them is in the request the student made.** Countermeasure: every day's phase 6 carries an agent case of exactly this shape, and S064's phase 5 requires the student to state, for each figure the agent produced, which of the four quantities it is a claim about. Confident arithmetic about somebody else's decision is the module's characteristic agent failure and it is assessed as such.

**Fatalism about cost.** The affective risk, and it is the mirror of M06's. A student who learns that cost varies by request shape, that the mix is unstable, that quota is somebody else's decision, that capacity may not exist even when quota does, and that the model will be wrong may conclude that budgeting inference is not possible. Countermeasure: **S065's phase 5 reports the realised bill against the cohort's models**, and the design's expectation is that the models are wrong in stated directions by stated amounts, which is what a working model looks like. The check is not assessed, for the reason given at S050.

## Assessment contribution

**One entrustment judgement**, at [S065](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S065.md): *present a cost argument to someone who pays for it.* Expected **2b**. Precursor to EA-1, and the closest the programme comes to EA-3 before [S083](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S083.md).

Specification items:

- **S061** — a cost model for one service, per request class, with its binding resource identified and its stated limits.
- **S062** — a latency promise with its throughput and cost consequences, a named class of requests that waits, and a deployed gateway policy that implements the refusal.
- **S063** — an admission and reservation policy with its measured concurrency ceiling, its distance to the cliff, and the capacity reserved and not used.
- **S064** — a capacity commitment stating capacity, quota and budget separately, with a commitment shape, a break-even computed from a dated rate, and a review date.
- **S065** — the cost argument, delivered, with its expiry condition and the number that will show it was wrong.

Every one of the five additionally checks the **prompt record**, the **intervention record** and the **frontier statement**, and every one fails a figure whose confidence, provenance and retrieval date are not in the same field as the figure.

Dispositions observed: **reliability** at S064, where the commitment has a date; **integrity** at S065, where the student must report the cheapest capacity they did not take before asking for money; **humility** at S065's phase 5, where the bill arrives.
