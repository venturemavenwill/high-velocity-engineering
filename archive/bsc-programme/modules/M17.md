# M17 · Deployment as Engagement

**Quarter:** [Q9](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q9.md) · **Seminar days:** [S081](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S081.md)–[S085](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S085.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** PE · all
**Platform anchor:** the whole stack, handed over — Foundry quota and cost surfaces, Purview data risk assessments, AI Search document-level access control and the AI gateway's per-consumer token metrics, each appearing as an organisational fact rather than a build target · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (quota, pricing, monitor) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## The central difficulty

The technical work is finished, and the engagement is not.

That sentence is the module and it is harder to accept than it looks, because everything in it is a compliment. The system exists. It was built from primitives the students assembled ([M11](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M11.md)), evaluated against criteria they traced and validated ([M10](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M10.md)), served under a cost model they were billed against ([M13](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M13.md)), released through a gate they designed and a governance arrangement they negotiated ([M14](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M14.md)), defended against an adversary and reviewed after a real incident ([M16](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M16.md)). By every standard the programme has taught, the work is done well.

And almost nothing that happens next is decided by any of it.

**A deployed system lives inside an organisation, and the organisation is part of the system.** The outcome the organisation is buying is produced jointly by the software and by work the software does not do: a rota, a check somebody performs, a record somebody keeps, an approval somebody grants, a manager's tolerance for being told bad news. None of that is in the repository. None of it has a maintainer anyone has written down. All of it fails, and when it fails the system stops producing the outcome while every technical indicator remains green.

The students' defence against this is the boundary. Every module since [M02](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M02.md) has taught them to draw one — the seam at [S007](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S007.md), the contract at [S034](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S034.md), the trust boundary at [S073](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S073.md) — and the boundary is what makes engineering tractable. The module's stance is that **the boundary is a decision the student makes, not a fact they discover, and it determines which failures are theirs.** A boundary drawn at the API is defensible, is what everyone does, and makes an entire class of failure invisible: the class in which the deployment succeeds on every measure its team defined and the organisation's cost rises.

From here the available failures are three, and none of them is a failure of correctness.

**Adoption.** The system is correct and people do not use it, or use it in a way that destroys its benefit. The engineer's reflex is training and communication; both are cheap and both are usually the wrong intervention, because the obstacle is rarely that people do not understand.

**Power.** Some parties can refuse and some parties can be harmed without recourse, and the two sets barely overlap. Consultation samples the reachable, so the parties who most need representing are systematically the ones a good stakeholder process omits. And the escalation path a student was taught at [S069](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S069.md) frequently terminates in the person whose decision the finding contradicts, at which point it is not a procedural problem.

**Scope.** What you have undertaken to deliver, to whom, with what warranty — agreed with parties whose interests differ, under constraints you do not control, against somebody entitled to say no. A scope agreed in a conversation and not written survives as long as the two people who agreed it.

The module's difficulty is not that any of this is hard to believe. Every student will assent to all of it inside ten minutes. It is that assent changes nothing until the student has named a person their system depends on, been overruled by somebody with the authority to overrule them, conceded something on behalf of a party who was not in the room, and had a scope refused by the second party after they agreed it with the first.

## Why this module sits here

**It requires the whole programme behind it, and it is the first module for which that is literally true rather than rhetorically true.** The [Module Map](/archive/bsc-programme/program/07-Module-Map.md) records M17's strands as `PE all`, one of only two such entries. The reason is not breadth of coverage. It is that the module's object — a scope negotiation over a running service — is an object composed of every strand's output at once: the four quantities from [S064](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S064.md), the decision records from [S070](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S070.md), the drift record from [S079](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S079.md), the authorisation model from [S078](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S078.md), the domain vocabulary from [S038](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S038.md). A student missing any one of them negotiates about something else.

**It requires PE301 to be one quarter behind rather than concurrent.** [Q8](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q8.md)'s PE301 teaches change management, stakeholder analysis, risk communication to non-technical decision-makers and negotiating scope under organisational constraint — which is this module's content, delivered as coursework, immediately before the module that exercises it. That inverts [M14](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M14.md)'s problem, where the PE strand was recruited with no course at all and [WP-069](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §8 recorded the shortfall as absorbed by the seminar. Here the course exists and has completed, which permits the **prior-course** form of the cross-course join — an artefact element naming which PE301 material the student applied. **WP-069 §8 named that form as the cheap fix it was declining. This module takes it**, at [S081](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S081.md) step 7 and [S084](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S084.md) step 7, and [WP-081](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-081.md) §7 records it as a proposed remedy under instrumentation rule 5 as amended, with S084's use contingent on S081's reported outcome.

**It requires a system with a history the students cannot dispute.** The organisation's part in the system is only visible against a record of the system behaving differently in the organisation than in the evaluation. [S060](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S060.md) shipped it, [S070](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S070.md) decided about it, [S079](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S079.md) reported what drifted, [S080](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S080.md) reviewed an incident on it. That is four quarters of divergence between what was measured and what happened, and it is the module's evidence base.

**It is not placed later** because [M18](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M18.md) assumes all of it. A handover artefact names a receiving organisation, warrants something to it, and states what it must be able to do without you; every one of those three is unanswerable from an org chart and requires S081's boundary, S082's parties and S085's scope to already exist.

**It is not placed earlier** because there is nothing to negotiate about. Scope negotiation with no running service, no cost position, no incident record and no absent party is [S039](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S039.md), which this module treats as its rehearsal and which [WP-039](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-039.md) §6 names as such at a two-year gap.

## The platform, as the thing negotiated over

Every other module in this programme anchors to a service the student operates. **This one anchors to services the student already operates and now has to argue about**, and the difference is the module's whole practical content.

The [Microsoft AI Platform Map](/archive/bsc-programme/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md) gives Q9 the anchor *the whole stack, handed over*. Read as an instruction to M17 it means: no new service, no new layer, no new mechanism. What is new is that each layer now has a **grantor, a payer, a gatekeeper or a bearer** attached to it — a person who can refuse, delay, or be inconvenienced. Four appearances carry the module, and all four are documented behaviours rather than illustrations.

| Appearance | What the documentation actually says | Which day |
|---|---|---|
| **A quota request** | Increase requests are processed in the order received, and **priority goes to customers who actively use their existing allocation**; requests not meeting that condition may be denied. Models from partners and community, Anthropic excepted, do not support increases at all. | [S085](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S085.md) |
| **A cost ceiling** | Budgets alert. **Azure OpenAI does not currently provide the hard limit that would prevent going over a budget**; enforcement is something an engineer builds. Cost visibility itself requires two role assignments at the right scope. | [S083](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S083.md) · [S085](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S085.md) |
| **An access-control action** | Purview's *Remove sharing link* remediation is documented as **to be used sparingly because it could prevent people from accessing the item legitimately**, after which the site owner must set a less permissive link for authorised users. The owner notification email is **not customisable**. | [S082](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S082.md) |
| **An adoption measurement** | The AI gateway can emit token metrics **per consumer** with custom dimensions; the deployment you shipped emits an aggregate, because nobody configured a dimension. | [S084](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S084.md) |

**What follows for the builds.** The [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) requires phase 5 to produce a build. In this module the build is never the engagement and never the deliverable. It is **an instrument whose output is an input to a conversation with a person**, it is bounded to roughly a third of phase 5, and its content is frequently *what it cannot contain*: a component inventory with no people in it, a party set with no unindexed party in it, a cost figure with no decision attached. **A build in M17 that does not end up in front of somebody has failed at its only job**, and the specifications are written so that this is checkable.

The assessment-bearing day is the exception and states it. **[S085](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S085.md) builds nothing.** The prompt record, intervention record and frontier statement from the module's four preceding builds travel into its defence instead, which is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s provision for assessment-bearing days and is compatible with [WP-050](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §3.1's rule that a judgement about conduct under pressure is invalid if the student is simultaneously constructing the evidence being contested.

**What follows for the agent.** The honest prediction on most of these days is that **Copilot cannot help at all with the part that matters**, and the module measures that rather than hiding it. The agent will read a quota, list the regions where a model can be deployed, price a template and query a usage series — all correctly, all through the Azure MCP Server, all faster than a student can. It cannot tell you whether the grantor will say yes, which team the excluded item now belongs to, or what the workaround was for. **The frontier in this module is unusually sharp and unusually well placed for teaching**, because on the technical side it is high and on the organisational side there is nothing there, and a student who locates that boundary empirically has learned the jagged-frontier lesson in the one place where its shape is obvious.

| SY · IS · CF · MS | Supply the object and nothing else. **None has a Q9 course**, and that is the module's stance expressed as a timetable: the technical strands are complete, and what remains is not another of their problems. |

**The all-strand recruitment is real and is also the module's principal risk.** A module that draws on everything can be delivered as a module that draws on nothing, because no single strand's staff owns its content. The mitigation is structural rather than exhortative: **one engagement runs through all five days**, and every day's artefact is an input to [S085](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S085.md) rather than a deliverable in itself, so a day delivered without its strand's object produces a scope statement that cannot be completed.

## Task-class variability plan

At class 6 the student decides **what to hand over, to whom, with what warranty.** Variability across the five days is therefore in **who the counterparty is and what they are able to refuse**, since holding the class constant while varying the counterparty is what prevents the engagement collapsing into one rehearsed conversation with one kind of person.

- **S081** varies the organisation: four deployments of one component into four organisations, matched on the software and divergent on the outcome, **one of which succeeded on every measure its team defined while the organisation's net cost rose.**
- **S082** varies the author of the analysis: four stakeholder maps of one deployment, produced by four parties, **of which the longest and most inclusive is systematically blind and the shortest predicted the failure.**
- **S083** varies the communication: four risk communications about one risk to one decision-maker, with the decisions that followed, **one of which was the best of the four and produced the worse outcome.**
- **S084** varies the adoption outcome: four changes matched on technical quality and evaluation, **one of which was adopted, retained and used exactly as designed by the wrong people.**
- **S085** varies nothing supplied. The scope, the constraints, the parties and the risks are the student's own, produced across the preceding four days, and **the two counterparties are real, disagree with each other, and are each entitled to refuse.**

## Load profile

**Near-zero technical, extreme interpersonal and evidential.** Nothing in this module is computed. There is no model, no threshold, no interval to construct, and no code. A student who finds this module easy on those grounds has not started it.

The module's distinctive load is **holding two parties' constraints simultaneously while representing a third who is absent.** That is the class-6 acquisition and it is different in kind from class 5's. Class 5 required answerability across time: a commitment made in week 64 met in week 70. Class 6 requires answerability to somebody who is not you, does not report to you, and cannot be persuaded by being shown the evidence again — and at S085 it requires it of two such parties at once, whose constraints are incompatible and neither of whom can evaluate the other's. **The student is the only person in the engagement who can see both**, which is the position the module exists to put them in, and it is why the day's central instruction is reachable from its phase 2 bridge rather than needing to be taught.

The standing load reduction is that **one engagement runs through all five days.** The cohort's own deployed service, and the organisation it lives inside, are the object at S081 and are still the object at S085. Every day adds one thing to the same engagement — a boundary, a party set, a risk record, an adoption position — and S085 assembles them into a scope. Nothing is introduced except the next question.

The second reduction is that **S085 builds nothing.** The scope statement is committed before the entrusted phase begins, for the reason [WP-050](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §3.1 gave and this module accepts unchanged: a judgement about conduct under pressure is invalid if the student is simultaneously constructing the evidence being contested.

The third is that **four of the five days are ordinary days at ordinary budgets.** A module whose content is almost entirely dispositional is at permanent risk of becoming a seminar in the pejorative sense, and the eight-phase structure with a pretest, a payload case and a whole task is what keeps it from doing so.

## Module-level predicted failure modes

**Cynicism.** The module's characteristic and worst failure, and the one with the longest half-life: a cohort taught power analysis produces engineers who see interests everywhere, attribute every constraint to somebody's protected position, and trust nobody. That is a worse professional than the naive one, because it is unfalsifiable. Countermeasures: **[S082](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S082.md)'s phase 7 requires the partner to construct the strongest good-faith account of the interest the student has just hypothesised**, and the pair records whether the hypothesis survived; [S083](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S083.md) point 8 states that the decision-maker is competent and acting in good faith and that the student is one input among several; [S084](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S084.md) point 2 makes resistance information before it is obstruction; and every payload case in the module contains at least one party acting correctly.

**Organisational analysis read as an excuse.** "It failed for organisational reasons" is available from S081 onward as a way of not owning a technical failure, and it is more comfortable than the alternative. Countermeasure: **[S081](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S081.md)'s boundary point puts the organisation inside the system the student is responsible for**, and step 6 requires the student to redraw the boundary and state what the redrawing makes them responsible for that they were not responsible for before. An organisational failure inside the boundary you drew is your failure.

**The absent party as a rhetorical move.** A student who names an absent party, records the naming and concedes nothing on their behalf has performed a gesture and will be praised for it. Countermeasure: **[S085](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S085.md) element 2 requires what was conceded on the absent party's behalf and a named person who will tell them, and its absence is a named failure condition** rather than a quality judgement.

**Risk communication read as advocacy.** The most consequential single error in the module, because it is what produces an engineer who quietly makes decisions that were not theirs. Countermeasure: **[S083](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S083.md)'s payload is a communication that was the best of its four and produced a decision against the engineer's recommendation and a worse outcome**, supplied in phase 3 before phase 4 says anything, so that the claim is reached rather than asserted.

**Scope read as a document.** A student who produces an excellent written scope and does not hold it in the room has produced a document. Countermeasure: **the floor is written before the negotiation, at S085 element 7, and the assessors know what it said**; and phase 4's final published question asks the student to name the point at which they would have walked away and whether they reached it.

**Not being overruled, and reading that as success.** The module's quietest failure. Countermeasure: S083 point 7 states that a student who has never been overruled has probably been making the decisions, and the cohort's overrule rate is reported at phase 8 without names.

**The build displacing the person.** New with the practical turn and specific to this module. Every phase 5 in the programme now produces a build, and four of this module's five phase 5s contain thirty minutes with a real maintainer, user, decision-owner or counterparty — the scarcest and least repeatable resource the module has. **A build that overruns takes those minutes and nothing else, because the person leaves at the hour.** Countermeasures, and they are structural rather than exhortative: each build is scoped to roughly thirty minutes of the ninety and its scope is stated in the day; **the person's thirty minutes are timetabled first and the build is what compresses**; each build's output is required to be *used* in the encounter, so a build that is not finished is visibly not finished by the person rather than by the marker; and the module reports at [S090](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S090.md) whether any build displaced any encounter minute. **This countermeasure has not been run and the design does not know whether it holds.** [WP-084](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-084.md) §8 argues the objection at full strength and concedes it.

## Assessment contribution

**One entrustment judgement, at [S085](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S085.md)** — the quarter's first, and the first of the programme's last two. Activity: *negotiate scope under constraint with a party who can say no.* Expected **3a**. It is a precursor to **EA-3** and to **EA-1** and is neither: EA-3 is assessed at PE301, CAP390 and SG390, and EA-1 at ME390 across at least four occasions with at least three distinct assessors.

**The level awarded at S085 is not treated as evidence about either entrusted activity**, and the entrustment statement says so before phase 1. The reason is stated rather than assumed: the programme's only measurement of whether conduct on a designed occasion predicts conduct on a real one is the [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md)-against-[S070](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S070.md) comparison field, and [WP-070](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §8 conceded that field is under-dosed in the only form capable of answering the question. [WP-085](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-085.md) §7 reports what S085 does under each branch of that result.

Specification items:

- **S081** — a system boundary with its contents; three non-software components classified as absorbing, routing or licensing, each with a named person identified from observation; the two-week unavailability consequence for each; the outcome in the organisation's unit with one moved-work instance or an explicit do-not-know; the redrawn boundary with what it makes the student responsible for; the named PE301 material applied; and the agent-produced component inventory with its prompt record, intervention record and frontier statement.
- **S082** — the party set on both axes with placement evidence; the sampling frame and who it cannot reach; one party absent from the substrate's corpus with what the system does to them; what would be told to one party who can be harmed without recourse, in their words, with who will tell them; one hypothesised unstated interest in a form the student would say to its holder; the escalation encounter with the move the student would take if refused and its cost; the outcome in three-part form; and the machine-produced party set with its three build records.
- **S083** — the risk and the decision it bears on with the decision-maker and the date; the four decision units; the interval in the decision-maker's units with its provenance in the same field; the direction the student would rather be wrong in; what was selected out and why; the applicability statement; the outcome recorded in the decision-maker's own words with the condition for raising it again; and the cost-and-exposure query that produced the figure, with its three build records.
- **S084** — the change and its population; the current practice including the workaround and the problem it solves; the four-condition classification with evidence and do-not-know marks; where cost and benefit fall by named party; the two-week and six-week predictions with the falsifying number and check dates; the tested prediction with the contradiction recorded; the intervention with the condition it addresses; the named PE301 material applied; and the shipped per-consumer usage measurement with its three build records.
- **S085** — the scope statement's seven elements, the two negotiation records including a refusal, the phase 5 carry-forward paragraph, and the module's four build records carried into the defence.

**The three build records — prompt record, intervention record, frontier statement — are assessed exactly as the [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) specifies and not more.** They are the weakest evidence class in the architecture, they are never used alone to form a level, and **an intervention record with no entries fails unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.** In this module the escape clause does real work: a student whose inventory script asserts its own coverage against the service's resource list, or whose usage query fails closed when a dimension is missing, has built the detection the record would otherwise have to evidence.

Dispositions observed: **integrity** at S082, where naming an interest you cannot prove is the uncomfortable answer and silence is available, and at S085, in whether what was agreed matches what can be warranted; **humility** at S083, where the decision goes against the student, and at S084, where the workaround being replaced was somebody's solution to a problem the student has not identified; **reliability** at S081, in whether the named maintainer recognises the description of what they do, and at S085, in whether the floor written before the room was the floor held in it.

## Perishability

**This module's durable content is the most durable in the programme, and it is worth stating plainly rather than leaving to the register.**

Nothing M17 assesses depends on a service existing. The boundary as a chosen commitment; the undocumented maintainer; the two power axes; the three ways to be absent; completeness without decidability; the direction-of-error sentence; adoption as a decaying rate; the workaround as somebody's solution; the out-of-scope list as a set of assignments; the floor written before the room. Ten claims, none of them about software, none of them younger than the trades they come from. The [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) will find less to carry here than anywhere else in the wiki.

Three things follow, and the third is the uncomfortable one.

**Re-anchoring is free.** Every platform instance in these five days is a vehicle. If the quota request form moves, if budgets acquire a hard cap, if Purview's remediation actions are renamed or its classic page finishes being retired, the day is re-anchored and not one durable claim changes. That is the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](/archive/bsc-programme/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md)'s stated response working exactly as designed, and this is the module where it costs least.

**The perishable layer is therefore doing less work here and must be held to a lower standard of load-bearing, not a higher one.** A platform detail in [M04](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M04.md) illustrates a mechanism with a physical cause. A platform detail in M17 illustrates an organisational fact with a commercial cause, and the correspondence is looser. **The specific danger is that a documented vendor policy \u2014 "priority goes to customers who actively use their existing allocation" \u2014 reads like a general truth about how grantors behave.** It is not. It is one grantor's published rule, at one date, and a student who generalises it has learned a fact about Microsoft and mistaken it for a fact about scarcity. Every day in this module that uses it says so.

**What the module cannot survive is not supersession.** It is the loss of the people. Four of the five days require somebody from outside the cohort to attend and to be entitled to refuse: two maintainers, a contradicted decision-owner, a decision-maker with authority, a target user, two counterparties. **A vendor change costs an afternoon of rewriting; a withdrawal of the institution's willingness costs the module**, and there is no substitution that preserves the content. [WP-082](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-082.md) §8 objection 2 records the cumulative exposure and states that the design made a resourcing judgement without asking. That remains the module's largest operational risk, and it is a risk about people, which is what the module is about.
