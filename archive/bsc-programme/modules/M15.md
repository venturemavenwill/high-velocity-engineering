## The central difficulty

For eight quarters this programme has been building an apparatus for evaluating an answer.

It is a good apparatus and the students own all of it. A construct and the gap between it and its measure. An error decomposition. An interval and the dependency structure it assumed. A criterion, traceable to named people who would recognise it. A judge with a systematic component that does not average out. An offline number, an online number, and five differences that could have produced the gap. A gate with two error rates, one of which leaves no record. A detection design with four parts and an explicit list of what it will not see. A limits statement with a kind and an expiry condition, published to people who cannot check it.

Every element of that apparatus takes an **answer** as its object.

**An action is not an answer, and the difference is not one of degree.** A wrong answer costs the reader their attention and can be declined; the reader is the last thing in the loop and they have a veto. A wrong action changes state. The change does not reverse when somebody notices, the reader was not consulted, and in the common case there was no reader at all. **The module's stance is that this single difference obliges the programme to re-derive its whole apparatus, and that the re-derivation is the module** — not the orchestration vocabulary, not the protocol surface, not the framework.

The difficulty is not that students will disagree. Stated aloud, the difference between answering and acting is obvious, and a room will grant it in under a minute. The difficulty is that **granting it changes nothing, because the apparatus does not announce which of its parts stopped working.** A criterion still looks like a criterion when its object has become a trajectory. A judge still produces scores. A dashboard still shows a tool error rate near zero. The gate still passes. Nothing fails loudly; the apparatus continues to run and quietly changes what it is about.

Four specific re-derivations carry the module, and each is a day.

**The loop, not the component, decides whether a system acts.** A component that only produces text has already acted if what consumes the text cannot decline. This is why [S071](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S071.md) is about the boundary rather than about capability, and why the limits statement written in Q6 is the artefact it starts from.

**Orchestration is control flow.** This is the module's cheapest and most durable framing and it is deliberately unglamorous. Sequence, branch, loop, fan-out, recursion — with the branch conditions computed by a component that has no obligation to be consistent between two runs on the same input. Once a student can name the construct, they can state the path set, and **the path set is the failure surface.** A student who cannot bound it cannot enumerate what the system might do, and every downstream artefact in the module depends on that enumeration.

**A tool boundary is a caller contract whose caller does not read documentation.** [S034](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S034.md) established that the anomaly catalogue is a contract's operative half because a caller who knows only the model's name cannot handle what they see. The caller here will not read the catalogue either, and will take the strongest available reading of the tool's name. What survives is the part the boundary enforces: the schema it validates, the permission it holds, the precondition the callee checks, the rate it limits. Everything else in the contract is a suggestion. **The boundary of trust is the point at which a value the system did not author becomes input to a decision it will act on**, and it is a place in a path rather than a policy in a document.

**Tool failure decomposes four ways, and two of the four ordinarily report success — with the count of silent shapes a consequence of observational position rather than part of the claim.** The decomposition — **selection, input, execution, output utilisation** — is the only content in this module the [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) marks durable, and it earns that by being a claim about observational position rather than about any implementation. **A tool can report on what happens inside it and on nothing else.** It validates its own arguments and reports its own execution status; it cannot observe that it was the wrong tool to call, and it cannot observe what the system did with its answer. Selection and output utilisation happen outside the tool, no party in the loop is positioned to report them, and that is why they produce runs that look clean. **An input failure whose arguments are valid and wrong joins them**, because the schema accepted the call and the tool has nothing to report — which is why *two of four are silent* is a summary and not the durable claim, and why [S074](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S074.md) point 4 bounds it in the material. This is [S068](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S068.md)'s third detection failure with a cause attached.

The four terms are IS302's and the [Course Catalogue](/archive/bsc-programme/program/06-Course-Catalogue.md) names them in those words. **They are also, exactly, four of the platform's five tool-usage evaluators** — `tool_selection`, `tool_input_accuracy`, `tool_call_success` and `tool_output_utilization`, with `tool_call_accuracy` spanning the first two — which is the module's cleanest instance of a decomposition arriving from two directions at once. **The correspondence is used as an instance and never as evidence.** No agreement with human judgement is published for any of these evaluators, and the module teaches students to say so.

And then the module has to evaluate the result, which is where its worst artefact lives. **A trajectory is a routing record that reads as an explanation.** [S052](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S052.md) established the shape on attention maps and [WP-052](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-052.md) §6 recorded that the trajectory case was the same error in a domain where the artefact is more convincing, that it was the most valuable target the day had, and that S052 did not name it — which it called a gap. [S075](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S075.md) closes it. The seduction is stronger here for a reason that is not about the computation at all: **a trajectory is prose, in the first person, giving reasons.** It reads as an explanation because it is written in the form of one.

## The module's practical turn, and its recursion

**The students have been operating an agent for two years and this is the module in which they build one.** Every day produces a build, on Foundry Agent Service, driven from GitHub Copilot in agent mode through the Foundry MCP Server, and the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s three artefact elements — prompt record, intervention record, frontier statement — attach to every one of the five specifications.

That produces a recursion the module names rather than hides, because it is load-bearing in three separate places.

**The intervention record is a human-in-the-loop control, and this module is about human-in-the-loop controls.** [S071](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S071.md) point 8 says a confirmation step is a control only if the person's decline rate is distinguishable from zero. Applied to the students' own practice, the same test says that an intervention record with no entries is either a trivial task or a reviewer who stopped reading. The Copilot Practice Standard's fail condition already says this; **what this module adds is the reason**, and the reason is the module's own content turned on the room.

**The four shapes occur in the instrument the students are using to build.** Copilot in agent mode selects tools, supplies arguments, executes them and uses what comes back, and every student has watched all four go wrong without having a name for any of them. [S074](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S074.md) supplies the names, and the day's cheapest legitimate example is the one in front of the student.

**And the recursion has a boundary, stated at [S074](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S074.md) phase 4.** A student who can classify their agent's failures has not thereby characterised the agent; they have characterised five days of tasks chosen by staff to have known failures available. The frontier statements pooled across the module are a sample and the module says so, per instrumentation rule 3.

**One further consequence is structural.** Because each day ships something, the module's five-link artefact chain now runs on a running capability rather than on a description of one — which strengthens [S073](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S073.md) and [S074](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S074.md), whose adjudicators are real traces, and which changes nothing about [WP-071](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-071.md) §8's concession that the shadow path still never refuses anything.

## Why this module sits here

**It requires [M14](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M14.md) in full, and the gate specifically.** An agent is a component that can reach the substrate's users, and the release gate is the only thing between them. [Q7](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q7.md) required the gate to exist and to have refused something before this quarter began, because a gate designed while an agent is being built is a gate designed by the person who wants the agent shipped.

**It requires [S055](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S055.md), at sixteen weeks, and this is the longest load-bearing retrieval in the programme.** [WP-055](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-055.md) §6 named it: *a limits statement written for a system that answers is a routing aid; the same statement for a system that acts is a boundary, and the two are not the same document. The day does not make that argument and S071 must.* [S071](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S071.md) makes it, and it makes it against each student's own published statement rather than against a supplied one.

**It requires [M07](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M07.md) entirely.** [S031](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S031.md)'s executions-as-the-object is the module's epistemic foundation and [WP-031](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-031.md) §6 named [S072](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S072.md) as its principal long-range target. [S033](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S033.md)'s third outcome becomes a fourth here. [S034](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S034.md)'s contract acquires a caller who cannot read it. A student without M07 receives this module as a tour of frameworks.

**It requires [S044](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S044.md)'s accept list**, which becomes an allow list without changing shape. Naming the threats you are not handling and naming the actions you are permitting are the same decision recorded from two directions, and [S073](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S073.md) step 5 is where the student discovers that.

**It is not placed later** because [M16](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M16.md) attacks it. [S076](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S076.md)'s adversary reads a prompt that arrives across a boundary [S073](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S073.md) marked; [S078](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S078.md)'s authorisation-at-retrieval-time is [S073](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S073.md) point 2's permission clause with an attacker present; and [S079](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S079.md)'s drift work consumes [S075](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S075.md)'s trajectory evaluation as its unit of observation. **M16 has no separate agent module to build on and does not want one.**

**It is not placed earlier** because it needs a cohort that has already been billed. [S072](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S072.md)'s loop cap is a cost decision as much as a control-flow one, and a student who has not held a cost model and been measured against it will read the cap as a safety setting only.

**The SY gap is real and is named rather than smoothed.** [Q8](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q8.md)'s four courses are IS302, SG302, ME302 and PE301; there is no concurrent systems course. The platform material this module depends on — the gateway, the shadow path, the trace collection — sits in SY301, one quarter behind. The consequence is that **the seminar days may not use the cross-course deferral form for platform mechanics**, because the course is not concurrent and the [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s stricter form requires a concurrent course to defer to. Where a day needs platform mechanics it supplies them itself, at seminar length and marked perishable inline — [S073](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S073.md) step 5's enforcement points are the largest instance. This is recorded as a structural cost of the quarter's course allocation and is argued in [WP-073](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-073.md) §8.

## Task-class variability plan

At class 5 the student decides **whether to proceed at all, under uncertainty and cost.** M13 varied what makes proceeding costly and M14 varied what makes it uncertain. **M15 varies where the consequence of proceeding is located**, because the module's whole claim is that consequence has moved out of the answer and into the loop — and a student who meets it in the same place five times will conclude it lives there.

- **S071** varies who acts: four systems over one request, differing in what each is permitted to do, **one of which takes no action at all and has the largest realised consequence** because something downstream cannot decline its answer.
- **S072** varies the control-flow construct: four orchestration patterns over one task, differing in path-set cardinality, **one of which is the least sophisticated and wins on the substrate's own record.**
- **S073** varies the boundary: four tool boundaries over one capability, differing in what is enforced and what is described, **one of which is not a tool at all but a caller holding its own permissions.**
- **S074** varies the position of the failure relative to the boundary: four failures over one capability, all reporting success, **one of which occurs entirely downstream of the boundary the previous day taught them to watch.**
- **S075** varies the unit of judgement: four evaluations over one set of runs, differing in what each licenses, **one of which agrees best with human raters and is blind to the two failure shapes that report success.**

The plan's constraint is that no two days may be resolved by the same move. A student who learns at S071 to write a boundary finds at S072 that their path set includes routes the boundary does not mention, at S073 that the boundary has no enforcement point, at S074 that the breach it permitted produced a clean-looking run, and at S075 that the evaluation which would have caught it is the one that scored worst.

## Load profile

**Moderate technical, high enumerative, and the module's real load is a chain.**

Nothing here is difficult to compute. A control-flow drawing is five box types. A schema reduction is deletion. A discrimination is a comparison between two records that should agree. A unit of judgement is a choice among three. The technical peak is [S075](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S075.md) step 5's replay from a recorded state, and it is worked with the room.

The distinctive load is that **each day's artefact is the next day's input, and the chain is five links long.** S071 produces a boundary; S072 produces a path set and tests it against the boundary; S073 marks the crossings on that path set and assigns the boundary an enforcement point; S074 classifies the failures those crossings admit; S075 evaluates the runs that result. **This is the longest single-artefact chain in the programme** — M14's was four — and it carries a real dependency risk, which is stated in the failure modes below.

The standing load reduction is that **one agent capability, on one substrate component, on one build path, runs through all five days.** The student picks it at S071 and does not pick again. Nothing is introduced except the next question.

The second reduction is that **every day's contrasting cases are pre-provisioned and every day's historical adjudicator is already run.** Phase 3's four configurations exist before the phase begins and the students execute them rather than build them; the shadow-path results, the substrate's boundary inventory, the existing trace log and the held-back rater panel are all supplied. Students choose, predict, run, mark, classify and defend; they do not wait for infrastructure.

The third is that **one framework is used throughout and none is assessed.** The five constructs, the four enforceable things, the four shapes and the three units are all framework-independent by construction. **Microsoft Agent Framework and Foundry Agent Service are the vehicle, and every named product, protocol, orchestration name, evaluator and split strategy in the module is marked perishable at the point of use.** A student is never assessed on which of them is current.

**The build adds load and the module accounts for it rather than denying it.** Phase 5 ships something on every day, which is more work than describing something, and the compensations are that the capability is chosen once, that the deployment path is worked with the room at S071 and not revisited, that Copilot in agent mode through the Foundry MCP Server is the same instrument the cohort has used for two years, and that the artefact's three added elements — prompt record, intervention record, frontier statement — are records of work already done rather than new work. **The one place this is not enough is [S073](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S073.md)**, whose phase 5 is the module's longest, and [WP-073](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-073.md) §3.1 states it.

## Module-level predicted failure modes

**Agency read as a property of the component.** The module's defining failure and the one most likely to survive it. A student who believes that a system which only produces text has not acted will build a boundary around the wrong object, and every subsequent artefact inherits the error. Countermeasure: **S071's phase 3 payload is a system that takes no action and produces the largest consequence**, and the S071 specification fails a boundary that does not name what consumes the system's output.

**The path set stated as a number when it is unbounded.** Students will count the branches they drew and stop. A loop with a model-computed continuation condition has as many paths as its cap allows, and the cap is usually a configuration value nobody read as a control-flow bound. Countermeasure: **S072's specification requires either a path count or the construct that makes it unbounded together with what bounds it in practice**, and a stated number for a system containing a loop fails.

**The boundary written and not enforced.** The commonest professional error in this area: a policy document listing prohibited actions, with nothing between the system and the actions but the document. Countermeasure: **S073 step 5 requires every prohibited action from S071 to be assigned an enforcement point at a named boundary or re-declared as accepted with its probability, consequence and confidence in the same field**, and an entry with neither fails.

**The four shapes learned as four examples.** A student who memorises selection–input–execution–output utilisation as a list has acquired a vocabulary and not a method — and the risk is sharper here than usual, because the platform supplies an evaluator named after each shape and running five evaluators feels like having applied the decomposition. Countermeasure: **S074's criterion is observational position rather than class label** — a tool reports on what happens inside it and nothing else — and the day's assessed step is naming which party is positioned to observe each shape in the student's own system, with *none* as the frequently correct answer. **An evaluator is not an observing party**, because it is a judge reading the same record, and S074 phase 4 states that. The day also states the one boundary at which its own criterion fails.

**The trajectory read as an explanation.** [WP-052](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-052.md) §6 predicted this retrieval would be the module's least likely to fire and the most valuable if it did. Countermeasure: **S075's phase 1 item 2 strips the tool results from a trajectory and leaves only the system's stated reasoning, and asks the same question about the run** — administered before the day names the case, and reported at phase 8 as the cohort's own movement.

**Dependency collapse across the chain.** A student who loses a link — a boundary not written, a path set not bounded — arrives at S074 with nothing to classify. Countermeasure: **each day publishes a fallback input drawn from the substrate's own configuration**, and the assessor narrative records which inputs were the student's own. This is the same construction [WP-070](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §2 used for M14's four-day chain and it is imported deliberately rather than reinvented.

**Framework fluency mistaken for understanding.** The module's largest teaching risk and the one the [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) exists for, and the practical turn raises it rather than lowering it: a student who has shipped five builds on one framework has five weeks of fluency in a vocabulary that is semi-durable at best. Orchestration patterns are semi-durable and protocol-mediated interoperability is per-delivery. Countermeasure: **the module's durable content is written as measurement obligations rather than as facts about named systems.** A student is never assessed on which framework, orchestration name, protocol or evaluator is current; they are assessed on having named the construct, bounded the path set, marked the crossings, named the observing party and stated what the evaluation cannot see. For any protocol, the assessed question is which of the four enforceable things it carries and which it leaves to the implementer. **Each day's perishability register separates the durable claim from the platform instance in a table, and the specification assesses the left-hand column.**

**Fatalism about agency.** The affective risk, and it is this module's version of [M11](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M11.md)'s. A student who learns that two failure shapes are structurally silent, that the best-agreeing evaluation is blind to both, and that a trajectory is not an explanation may conclude that agent systems cannot be engineered responsibly at all. Countermeasure: **every day produces something that works.** A boundary with an enforcement point refuses actions. A bounded path set is enumerable. A discrimination between intent and tool called catches selection failures that no signal catches. The module's argument is for building this carefully, not for declining to build it, and the instructor states that once, at S074 phase 4, where the risk peaks.

## Assessment contribution

**No entrustment judgement.** [Q8](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q8.md)'s single occasion is at [S080](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S080.md), and the reason M15 carries none is argued on the quarter page: the activity that would be certified is running a system that acts, and [M16](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M16.md) is where its threat model is taught. **Certifying agency before naming its adversary would be this design committing, in its own schedule, the error [S044](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S044.md) exists to correct.** It is stated to students at S071 phase 1, together with the fact that specification items continue at every day.

Specification items:

- **S071** — a revised limits statement carrying the substrate readers' recorded response; a deployed capability on a chosen build path with the choice defended; an action classified by reversibility; a boundary listing prohibited actions with the thing other than the sentence that prevents each; a named decline point with its rate and whether a person can read at it; and the shadow-run result against the pre-committed prediction.
- **S072** — a control-flow drawing in the five constructs; a bounded path set or the construct that makes it unbounded with what bounds it; the model-computed branch conditions named; the deprioritised request class with who submits it; a join that distinguishes a failed contributor from a silent one; and the realised path distribution against the prediction and against the S071 boundary.
- **S073** — every crossing marked on the S072 drawing with its author; a three-part contract for the highest-consequence tool with the enforced and prose parts separated; a reduced schema with its cost; an enforcement point or an explicit accepted re-declaration for every S071 prohibition and every S072 breach path; and the substrate boundary inventory against the student's own marking.
- **S074** — one instance of each of the four shapes drawn from the substrate's trace log; the observing party or an explicit none for each; one discrimination for a silent shape with its false-alarm rate and its independence argued; the shape not being detected with its cost, provenance and confidence in the same field; and the discrimination's results over the existing log.
- **S075** — a traceable criterion naming one party who would not recognise it; a unit of judgement with the question it answers, the question a reader will believe it answers, and the shapes it cannot see; a judge characterisation stating which part of the S048 method does not extend and what was used instead; one intervention result; one class constructed from residuals; and a statement of what the evaluation does not license.

**Every one of the five specifications additionally carries the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s three artefact elements** — the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement — **with the intervention record's fail condition and its sensitivity escape unchanged**: no entries fails unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error. That escape is the discipline [S052](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S052.md) established and [WP-048](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 corrected, and it is load-bearing in this module because the artefacts students build are themselves detection mechanisms: a student whose boundary refuses an action their agent proposed has built the detection the record would otherwise have to evidence.

Dispositions observed: **integrity** at S073 and S074, in whether the enforcement points and the observing parties named are ones that exist; **humility** at S075, in the step 7 statement and in the willingness to report an intervention that dissolved the student's own account; **reliability** at S071, where the boundary is a commitment about what a running system will not be permitted to do.
