## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

Attention is a lookup table with the exactness taken out. You already own the exact version from year one — key in, match, one value out. Loosen the match so that the query is compared against every key and the answer is a blend of all the values weighted by how well each one matched, and you have the mechanism. The room can build it in eight minutes, which is why the day does not spend its time there.

What the day spends its time on is the picture the mechanism produces on the way past. A map of what was attended to is legible, publishable and thoroughly convincing, and **it does not tell you what the output depended on.** A large weight says a value entered the sum. It does not say the sum would have come out differently without it, because the value may be uninformative, may repeat what another value already said, or may be cancelled further down.

You cannot be talked out of reading that map as an explanation. You would agree, you would be able to write the correct sentence in an exam, and you would read the next map you saw as an explanation anyway — because agreeing is free and the picture is persuasive. **So the day makes you do it first.** You are invited to explain an output from the map. Then the token the map says mattered most is swapped for something unrelated and you are shown what happened to the output. What gets falsified is your explanation, not a strawman handed to you.

Then the argument moves to the artefact you will actually meet. Nobody in this programme will ever produce an attention map for a system they ship, because the models they ship against are hosted and their internals are not exposed. **What they will read several times a day for the rest of their careers is an agent's account of why it did what it did** — the same class of object, and one nobody has ever tested. So the day turns the method on it: ask the same thing twice, change one input, measure the output.

**What this paper concedes, and it is a real concession.** The day's most emphasised sentence is that the agent's stated reasoning is a routing record and not a cause. The evidence supports three narrower claims — that the summary is not the process, that it is not guaranteed to be produced at all, and that it cannot be checked. **None of those establishes that it is unrelated to the process**, which is what the memorable sentence will be heard to say. The design kept the stronger wording because it lands harder, and §8 records that as a decision rather than hoping nobody notices.

## Abstract

This paper defends deriving attention from a hash table in the retrieval phase rather than presenting it in the instruction phase, on the ground that a student who reaches the mechanism from a structure they already own holds an anchor that survives architectural change while a student handed a formula holds a formula. It defends the day's opening sequence — invite the reading the day exists to defeat, then defeat it with an intervention before any instruction occurs — against the alternative of stating the correction first, which produces agreement without displacement. It defends splitting phase 3 into an attention-map arm that is demonstrated rather than operated, because serverless deployments do not expose model internals, and a stated-reasoning arm that is operated against a real deployment, on the ground that the second arm carries the same three attribution failures plus one the first cannot show at all: **an artefact that is sometimes not generated cannot be the cause of an output that always is.** This is the day on which the programme discharges its commitment that the agent's stated reasoning is established as a routing record rather than a cause, and the paper defends siting that discharge in a numbered consolidation point rather than an aside. The day is the first to use the corrected sensitivity clause that WP-048 §8 conceded. It concedes that the day's single hard assessment requirement measures compliance rather than belief; it concedes that the practical turn has moved the day's least warranted claim from the periphery to the centre; and it identifies a **second distinct failure mode of rule 5** — the laundering of an untested design choice into the design's repertoire by declaring it a remedy — which is fixed by amendment in the same chain.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The mechanism is easy. That is the problem.

Attention is a hash table with the exactness removed. [S013](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S013.md) built the hash table forty-one days ago: key in, exact match, one value out. Attention compares a query against every key, turns the comparisons into weights, and returns a blend of all the values. Every component is already held. A day spent teaching the mechanism would be a day spent teaching something the room can derive in eight minutes, and phase 2 asks them to.

The real difficulty is that the mechanism produces a by-product that looks like an explanation. An attention map is a picture of what the model "attended to", it is legible, it is publishable, and it is **a routing record with no causal content**. A high weight says a value entered the sum. It does not say the output depended on it, because the value may be uninformative, redundant with other values, or cancelled downstream.

Students arrive already committed to reading maps as explanations, because that is how maps are used in almost everything they have read. **The commitment cannot be dislodged by being told.** A student informed at the start of the day that attention is not explanation will agree, will be able to state it in an exam, and will read the next map they see as an explanation — because agreement is cheap and the map is compelling.

The day's design therefore inverts the order: item 2 explicitly invites the student to explain the output using the map, and item 3 replaces the highest-weighted token and shows the output unchanged. **The commitment must be expressed before it can be defeated.**

**The second problem is that almost none of this is operable, and the thing that is operable matters more.** No student in this programme will ever produce an attention map for a system they ship, because the models they ship against are hosted and their internals are not exposed. What they will read, several times a day, for the rest of their careers, is an agent's stated account of why it did what it did. That artefact is the same class of object and the students have never once tested it.

The platform is unusually direct about what that artefact is. A reasoning deployment's `reasoning_tokens` are counted in `completion_tokens_details` and documented as *hidden tokens that aren't returned as part of the message response content*. What a caller can obtain is a **summary** of them, at a requested level of detail, and the documentation records that *even when enabled, reasoning summaries are not guaranteed to be generated for every step/request* and that this is expected behaviour. For the newest models a planning object may precede a tool call, and its generation is likewise not guaranteed — though, the documentation notes, **you can encourage it by instructing the model to output its plan.** That last clause is the whole argument in one sentence: *the quantity of stated reasoning is a function of what you asked for, not of what the computation did.*

The third problem is the overcorrection. A day that establishes only that these artefacts are not explanations produces students who discard two genuinely useful diagnostic instruments. Point 11 exists for that reason and is not decoration.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Low-to-moderate on mechanism, high on discipline.** The derivation at phase 2 costs almost nothing because every component is retrieved rather than introduced. The load is at phase 5, where a hypothesis, an intervention and a sensitivity statement must be produced together — none of the three is meaningful without the others.

Management:

- **The mechanism is derived, not taught,** which removes the largest possible load source.
- **All four phase 3 inputs, maps and ablation results are supplied.**
- **Step 3's intervention design is worked with the room**, since intervention design is the technical skill and is new.
- **The model, corpus and substrate are yesterday's.**

**Step 2's hypothesis is unsupported.** It is the one place the student must commit before evidence, and it is cheap in working memory.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Being invited to make the error, then shown it,* items 2 and 3.
- *Deriving the mechanism rather than receiving it,* phase 2's bridge.
- *Attributing from maps alone, and committing, before ablation,* phase 3.
- *Reading the map before running the intervention,* steps 4 and 5, which creates the anchor deliberately.
- *Stating what a divergence the intervention could not have detected would look like,* step 6.
- *Producing an alternative explanation of your partner's own intervention result,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Multi-head architecture in detail.* [S053](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S053.md)'s.
- *Positional encoding schemes.* Named at point 6 as a source of structural weight and not developed. S053's.
- *Efficient attention variants.* [S061](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S061.md)'s, where the cost is the subject.
- *Mechanistic interpretability beyond intervention.* Circuit-level analysis is named as existing and excluded; the programme does not have the time and the day's standard does not require it.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** Point 6's structural-weight content — that some positions attract large weight for reasons unrelated to content — is stated as a class of phenomenon to check for, not as a description of where it occurs. **Safe** as a pre-condition on interpretation: check before reading. **Unsafe** as a fact about which positions behave this way, which is a property of particular architectures and training regimes and is among the fastest-moving content in the module. A student who learns *which* positions rather than *that the check is required* has acquired something with a shelf life shorter than their degree.

**The platform instance, scoped in the same form.** Point 8 teaches the reasoning summary as an instance of the routing-record argument, and the correspondence must carry its safe and unsafe conditions because students will otherwise carry it into work this day does not supervise.

*Safe.* Reasoning that a stated rationale is not evidence of a cause; that a causal claim about a deployment requires an input intervention and a measured output change; that an artefact whose generation is not guaranteed cannot be a necessary part of the process it describes; and that increasing the quantity of stated reasoning by instruction has not changed the computation. In each of those the correspondence is a correspondence of *epistemic status*, and it holds for any system that emits an account of itself, on any platform, including ones that do not exist yet.

*Unsafe.* Reasoning that the summary is **inaccurate**, which is a stronger claim than the day makes and one the day generates no evidence for. The documentation does not state that summaries misdescribe the process; it states that they are summaries, that the underlying tokens are hidden, and that generation is not guaranteed. A student who leaves saying *the model's reasoning is a lie* has overshot, has made an unfalsifiable claim, and will be correctly contradicted by the first colleague who reads the documentation. It is also unsafe as a claim about **any particular deployment's** effort levels, summary levels or planning behaviour, all of which are per-family and moving. And it is unsafe as a claim about GitHub Copilot specifically, whose model routing and rendering are not the same object as a direct deployment's API response — **the day teaches the class of artefact, and the class is what transfers.**

The boundary is stated to students at points 8, 9 and 11 rather than left to this paper, per the widening recorded on the [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), and step 7's *what was the account good for* is where a student applying it outside that range would fail: a student who answers "nothing, it lies" has failed the step.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **item 3**.

The student has just explained an output using a map. The top-attended token is replaced with something unrelated. The output does not change. There is no framework needed, no statistics, and nothing to argue with — and critically, **the student's own explanation from item 2 is the thing that has been falsified**, not a strawman supplied by the instructor.

Phase 3's mechanism is the discovery that there is more than one way to be wrong. Input 2 fails because the weight is structural. Input 3 fails because a low-weight token was decisive. Input 4 fails because the aggregate describes no head. A student who has met only one of these learns a special case and generalises it incorrectly; a student who meets three learns that map-reading has no single repair.

Phase 6 supplies the affective content, and its construction is the point: **the map was correct.** Nothing was broken, nobody was careless, and the change was made on an accurate artefact read the wrong way. That is the failure the students will produce for the rest of their careers with dashboards, flame graphs and traces, and the phase says so.

The competence risk is at step 2. A student whose hypothesis is wrong may read the day as evidence that they cannot reason about the model. The instructor states before phase 5 that a falsified hypothesis with a sound intervention is the best available outcome and is what the specification rewards, and that a hypothesis confirmed by an insensitive intervention is the worst.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Invite, then defeat. Item 4's quantitative prediction is collected because it is checked at phase 8 and because a badly-wrong prediction is the cheapest motivation for phase 4 point 3. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction** and is the day's own subject turned on the students' own tool; it is administered here per instrumentation rule 8.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Three retrievals and a bridge that derives the mechanism. This is the highest-value twenty minutes in the module: the anchor it produces — *attention is a hash table with soft matching* — is the retained content, and it was constructed by the room.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Two arms, disclosed. Arm A is four inputs, three failure modes, one working case, intervention as adjudicator, **demonstrated rather than operated and not assessed** because serverless deployments do not expose model internals. Input 1 is not filler; without it the day teaches that maps are worthless, which point 11 then has to undo against the evidence of the phase. **Arm B is four cases run against a real reasoning deployment**, reproducing the same three failures in the register the students work in, plus R4 — one input, repeated, at one setting, producing different accounts and sometimes none. R4 is the case arm A structurally cannot supply, because an attention map is deterministic given the input and a generated summary is not.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Eleven points. Point 1 is the anchor. Point 4 sets the standard. Point 5 is S049 in a new domain and is included partly to demonstrate that the unit-of-analysis rule was not a statistics rule. **Point 8 is the day's discharge of the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) commitment** and is a numbered point rather than an aside because a commitment delivered as a remark is not delivered. Points 9 and 10 are the required perishable point and the required defaults. Point 11 prevents the overcorrection.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Eight steps, **shipping an attribution harness against a real deployment**, with the account read before the intervention runs so that the anchoring pull is experienced rather than described. **Step 6 uses the corrected sensitivity form** rather than requiring a divergence to be found — the first application of the fix [WP-048](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 conceded, and instrumentation rule 5 working as designed. **Step 8 is new**: two requests, nothing varied, and the result is the day's cheapest hard finding. The artefact carries the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A correct map, read wrongly, acted on — then a coherent agent rationale, read as a derivation, acted on. The two are deliberately the same failure, because point 8's whole argument is that they are one.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Alternative explanation of the partner's own intervention result, then the distinguishing intervention. The phase turns the day's method on itself.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 4 against measured rates; the cohort divergence rate; hypothesis survival; **step 8's two stability numbers**; the alternative-explanation rate; **item 5 opened on both halves separately.**

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has read interpretability work will know that attention is not explanation and will say so at item 2, refusing the invitation. Predicted failure: they skip the experience the day is built on and spend phase 3 confirming what they already hold. Countermeasure: phase 3's inputs 3 and 4 are failures that a general scepticism about maps does not predict — a low-weight decisive token and a head-disagreement artefact are specific, and the student must still attribute them wrongly to learn them. Phase 7 then attacks their intervention rather than their map.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is step 3's intervention design. Countermeasures: worked with the room; four supplied ablation examples immediately preceding; and the instructor's statement that a falsified hypothesis is the rewarded outcome.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Input 1 is trivially readable and input 2 becomes easy once the pattern is seen. The difficulty is input 4, where the aggregate map is internally coherent and describes nothing real. Fallback: ask the group to predict what the per-head maps look like before they are shown, which is a harder question than the attribution.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **nihilism about interpretation** — if maps do not explain, and interventions can be insensitive, and single interventions admit alternative explanations, a student may conclude that nothing can be known about why the model did what it did. Phase 7 makes this risk worse, and it is placed knowingly.

Countermeasures: point 8's three legitimate uses; input 1's demonstration that map-reading sometimes works; phase 7's requirement to name the *distinguishing* intervention, which is constructive rather than sceptical; and the instructor's statement that the day's standard — intervene and measure — is achievable, is what the field's better work does, and is not a counsel of despair.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**Item 5's two-part hit rate** — whether students predicted the agent's stated reason, and whether they predicted that it would not be causal — joins the running frontier map per instrumentation rule 9, read by the following year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md). ## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day's hard requirement measures compliance, not belief.**

At its strongest: the specification fails a causal claim supported only by an attention map. Every student will therefore run an intervention, because the artefact demands one, and no student will fail the requirement. What the day wants to change is what a student does in six months with a map, a deadline, and nobody checking — and the artefact is silent on that. The requirement is satisfiable by doing the assigned work, which is not evidence of the disposition it was written to install.

The partial rebuttal is that the day does generate one piece of belief-relevant evidence: the step 4-before-step 5 ordering measures whether the map captured the student's reading even when they knew the intervention was coming, and the phase 8 divergence rate reports it. That is a real measurement of the pull the day is fighting.

But it is a measurement of the pull, not of the response to it, and **the objection is conceded.** The design has one nearly-free opportunity it has not taken. The day-3 between-day item currently supplies a map, a claim, and asks for an intervention that would test it — which prompts the exact behaviour it would be interesting to observe unprompted. **Supplying only the map and the claim, and recording whether the student asks for an intervention without being told to, would be a genuine measurement of the disposition at a cost of nothing.** It is not in the design.

**Objection 2: rule 5 is being used to enter an untested design choice into the repertoire.**

At its strongest: phase 7's warrant states that no prior remedy fits this failure, that the structure is new, and that it is recorded so rule 5 can apply it elsewhere. [Instrumentation rule 5](/archive/bsc-programme/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) says that *a remedy found for one day applies wherever the same failure recurs.* The word doing the work is **found** — it implies the remedy was found to work. Here nothing has been found. A structure has been invented this morning, has never been run, and is being pre-registered as part of the design's repertoire on the strength of its author's confidence. If it is unsound, rule 5 will now propagate it.

This is a second and distinct failure mode of rule 5. [WP-048](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 recorded the first: a clause structure imported from S041 without checking that its factual premise transferred. This one runs the other way — not importing carelessly but **exporting prematurely**. In five days the rule has produced one clear success (this day's step 6, which correctly carries WP-048's own correction forward) and two distinct costs.

**The rule is amended rather than merely recorded**, per the design's standing requirement that an amendment to a documented process include its enforcement or be flagged as documentation-only. Rule 5 now distinguishes a **validated** remedy — one that has run at least once and whose whitepaper reported the outcome — from a **proposed** one, and only validated remedies enter the repertoire. A proposed remedy is recorded, is used at the day that proposes it, and enters the repertoire only after the proposing day's outcome is reported. Enforcement is the whitepaper's own §7 instrumentation, which must state which of the two a structure is. Phase 7 of this day is therefore **proposed, not validated**, and this whitepaper's §9 prediction 4 is its validation test.

**Objection 3: the practical turn has moved the day's least warranted claim from the periphery to the centre.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn and it is the one the design finds hardest.

At its strongest: the day's warranted content is about attention. The mechanism is documented in the literature, the three attribution failures are established there, and arm A rests on that base. **Point 8 rests on nothing of the kind.** It takes a claim about attention weights — that a routing record is not a cause — and asserts that the same holds of a generated natural-language summary, on the strength of an analogy plus three facts from a vendor's documentation: that the underlying tokens are hidden, that the summary is a summary, and that it is not always produced.

Those three facts establish that a reasoning summary is **not the process**. They do not establish that it is **uncorrelated with the process**, which is what "routing record, not a cause" is heard as. A summary generated from hidden tokens by the same model might track the computation closely, loosely, or not at all, and **the day generates no evidence on that question and cannot, because the raw trace is unavailable and the documentation states that attempting to obtain it by other means may violate the acceptable use policy.** The design has built a day whose most emphasised claim is one whose evidence is structurally out of reach, and has then given that claim the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s billing as this day's commitment. R4 is the strongest thing the day has, and R4 establishes non-necessity and instability, not non-causality.

The rebuttal available is that the *operational* claim survives intact and is the one that matters. Whether or not a summary tracks the computation, **a causal claim about a deployment must be established by intervention**, because the summary cannot be checked and the intervention can. That is what the specification assesses and it is unaffected by the objection.

**But the day says more than the specification assesses, and the objection is conceded.** "The agent's stated reasoning is a routing record, not a cause" is a stronger sentence than the evidence supports, it is the sentence the students will carry, and the design chose it for its force.

**The cheap fix is one clause and it is made.** §3.2's scope statement now marks the *inaccuracy* reading as unsafe, and step 7 fails a student who answers that the account was good for nothing. **The remaining fix is not made and is named.** The honest form of point 8's headline is *not the process, not guaranteed, and not checkable* — three claims the evidence supports — rather than *not a cause*, which is one claim it does not. Changing the sentence would cost nothing and would weaken the day, and the design has kept the stronger sentence with its scope statement attached rather than the weaker sentence unqualified. **That is a defensible choice and it is a choice, and it is recorded as one so that a reader who disagrees can say where.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At item 4, the cohort's median predicted top-token-replacement effect rate will be far above the measured rate. *Instrument:* item 4 predictions against phase 3's ablation results.

**Prediction 2.** Groups will attribute input 3 wrongly more often than input 2. *Instrument:* committed phase 3 attributions against ablation. **The claim is that a decisive low-weight token is harder to anticipate than a structural sink**, because the sink has a visible signature and the decisive token does not.

**Prediction 3.** More than half of students' step 4 map readings will bias their step 6 interpretation of the divergence — describing the intervention result in the map's terms rather than the reverse. *Instrument:* step 6 texts coded for which artefact frames the account.

**Prediction 4.** Phase 7 partners will produce a viable alternative explanation in more than half of cases, and in more than a quarter the pair will fail to name a distinguishing intervention. **This is the validation test for the phase 7 structure under the amended rule 5**: if the distinguishing-intervention step fails more often than it succeeds, the structure is not a remedy and does not enter the repertoire. *Instrument:* phase 7 outcomes coded for alternative produced and distinguishing intervention named.

**Prediction 5.** At item 5, a large majority will predict the agent's stated reason accurately and **a majority of those will also record it as the cause**. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded on the two halves separately, against the phase 5 intervention results. **The gap between the two rates is the day's central frontier measurement**, and the claim is that predicting what the agent will say is easy and disbelieving it is not. If the second rate is low — if students routinely predict the reason and decline to treat it as causal — the cohort already holds point 8 and the day's emphasis is misplaced.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That attention is a hash table with the exactness removed, derivable in minutes from what you already hold, and that its by-product — a map of what was attended to — is a routing record with no causal content. The same argument is then turned on an agent's account of its own work.

**What the evidence licenses.** The soft-lookup mechanism, the query-key-value projection and the quadratic cost are **class 1**, grounded in [research/02-technical-foundations/vaswani-attention-is-all-you-need.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/vaswani-attention-is-all-you-need.md). The four attribution claims the day leans on — weight is not influence, intervention as the standard, structural attractors, head-aggregated incoherence — are **class 2**, reclassified in this paper's own evidence status, with no magnitude attached. The reasoning-token behaviour is **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026; the central silence is that the documentation says what a summary is not, and nothing about how faithfully it describes the computation. **That a stated rationale is not a cause is class 3.**

**What to do with it.**

- A causal claim needs an intervention and a measured output change — a standard this design sets rather than a finding it inherits.
- Run the same request twice: an artefact that is sometimes not generated cannot be a necessary part of the process it describes.
- Do not overshoot into *the model's reasoning is a lie*: that is unfalsifiable and unsupported.
- Place the account in the five classes on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md): not an observed result about the computation, and reading it as one is an unsupported inference.

**The concession worth remembering.** The day's most emphasised sentence outruns its evidence. The documentation establishes that a reasoning summary is not the process, not guaranteed and not checkable; none of that establishes it is uncorrelated with the process. The design kept the stronger wording for its force and recorded the choice as a choice.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** Attention as a soft content-addressed lookup, the query-key-value projection that makes the lookup learnable, multi-head attention over separate learned projections, the injected and non-inherent character of positional information, and the quadratic cost in sequence length with the parallelism that motivates it are grounded in [research/02-technical-foundations/](/research/02-technical-foundations/collected-materials.md) and specifically in [research/02-technical-foundations/vaswani-attention-is-all-you-need.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/vaswani-attention-is-all-you-need.md). The measurement standard invoked at point 4 follows [research/03-measurement-evaluation/zheng-mt-bench-chatbot-arena.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/zheng-mt-bench-chatbot-arena.md) and [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md) and the [S030](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S030.md) design.

**Correction to this section, recorded rather than applied silently.** An earlier version of this paragraph also placed here **the divergence between attention weight and causal influence, intervention-based attribution, structural weight attractors, and the incoherence of head-aggregated attention maps**, on the same citation. **That citation does not support any of the four.** `vaswani-attention-is-all-you-need.md` is an architecture paper: its own note records that its ablation table is over *model variations*, that its evidence is translation BLEU, and — in its own words — that students must not be allowed to over-read it. Only the soft-lookup reading resolves to it. **There is no attention-interpretability source in `research/` at all**, so no note in this repository verifies any of the four, and the misplacement meant the paper's most emphasised technical claims carried the strongest evidence label the standard offers on the strength of a paper that does not make them. The four are reclassified below, **with each claim's wording left exactly as it stood**, so that what moved is legible and the day's own scope statements at §3.2 can be checked against it. Nothing in §1, §4 or the day changes; what changes is what this paper says it has behind them.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The reasoning-model behaviour used at phase 3 arm B and at points 8 to 10 was read from Microsoft Learn on 2026-07-26.

*What the documentation says.* Reasoning models return `reasoning_tokens` within `completion_tokens_details`, described as **hidden tokens that aren't returned as part of the message response content but are used by the model to help generate a final answer**. `reasoning_effort` accepts three named levels on the reasoning models that support it, with one older model excluded, and a higher setting is documented as producing a larger number of reasoning tokens. A reasoning summary is requested through a summary parameter on the responses API at one of several named levels, one of which is unsupported on one model series. **Attempting to extract raw reasoning through methods other than the summary parameter is documented as unsupported, as possibly violating the acceptable use policy, and as possibly resulting in throttling or suspension.** **Even when enabled, reasoning summaries are documented as not guaranteed to be generated for every step or request, and this is stated to be expected behaviour.** For one model series a planning object may precede a tool call; **its generation is likewise documented as not guaranteed, and the documentation states that the model can be encouraged to produce it through the instructions parameter.**

*What version.* No page carries one. The planning object is specific to one model generation and the summary levels differ by family; both are expected to be superseded.

*What it does not report that a reader would need.* **The central silence is the one §8 objection 3 turns on: the documentation states what a reasoning summary is not — not the raw tokens, not guaranteed — and states nothing whatever about how faithfully it describes the computation.** No fidelity figure, no agreement measure, no characterisation of when a summary diverges. That silence is what makes the day's headline claim stronger than its evidence, and it is recorded here rather than papered over. A second silence: the documentation states that reasoning tokens are counted in the completion-token details and **states no expected rate at which an output budget is exhausted by them**, which is point 10's third default and is the confusion [S055](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S055.md) spends a day untangling. And consistent with the folder-wide asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](/research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), the material states limits, levels and guarantees-not-given throughout and **states no accuracy anywhere.**

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** Eliciting a misconception before correcting it, deriving a new structure from a retrieved one, contrasting cases with intervention adjudication, anchoring effects from prior exposure, and error management training are cited for **direction and mechanism only**. No effect size is asserted for any of them, and none is asserted for AI-assisted productivity anywhere in this paper.

**The day's four attribution claims sit here, and this is where they belong.** *The divergence between attention weight and causal influence*, *intervention-based attribution* as a method, *structural weight attractors*, and *the incoherence of head-aggregated attention maps* are established in the interpretability literature and are **not verified in this repository**. They are cited for **direction and mechanism only**, and the day is already written that way: point 3 says a weight does not establish that the output depended on the value, point 4 says influence is established by intervening and measuring, point 6 says some positions attract weight for reasons unrelated to content and must be checked for rather than assumed, and point 5 says an aggregate over disagreeing heads describes no head. **None of the four is asserted with a magnitude, a frequency, or an attribution to a named model or architecture**, and §3.2's scope statement independently forbids the form a student would want point 6 in. The consequence a reader should draw is narrow and real: **phase 3 arm A rests on this class and not on class 1**, and §8 objection 3's finding that the day's most emphasised claim outruns its evidence now has a second and separate instance in the arm that objection treats as the day's secure base.

**No ablation rate, weight magnitude or positional behaviour is asserted for any named model**; all such figures in the day are measured on the day's own materials and are marked perishable. **No claim is made anywhere that a reasoning summary is inaccurate**, and §3.2 marks that reading unsafe.

*Flagged 2026-07-30, not yet re-verified:* the header's platform anchor names **Azure AI Search vector weighting** beside the reasoning-model surface, and nothing in this section grounds it — no page, no reading date, no recorded silence — so half of the day's declared anchor carries none of the four things this section requires of a vendor claim, and a reader cannot tell whether it was read, skimmed or inherited from a neighbouring day; the correction is not supplied here.

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The invite-then-defeat opening; the derivation of attention from a hash table in the retrieval phase; **the adoption of intervention-and-measurement as the only attribution this programme accepts, which is a standard this design sets rather than a finding it inherits**; the four-input construction with three failure modes and one working case; the two-arm split between demonstrated and operated; the R4 construction and the claim that a non-guaranteed artefact cannot be a necessary cause; point 11's anti-overcorrection role; the account-before-intervention ordering; step 8's repeated run; phase 7's alternative-explanation structure; and all five §9 predictions are this design's own reasoning, untested. The §8 objection-1 concession is unqualified and names a free measurement the design declined to take. The §8 objection-2 concession identifies **a second distinct failure mode of instrumentation rule 5 — premature export rather than careless import — and amends the rule in the same chain**, distinguishing validated from proposed remedies; phase 7 of this day is classified as proposed, and §9 prediction 4 is its validation test. **The §8 objection-3 concession is the practical turn's cost stated in full**: the day's most emphasised claim outruns its evidence, the evidence is structurally unobtainable, the design has taken the one-clause scope fix and has declined the sentence-level one, and it records the decision as a decision.
