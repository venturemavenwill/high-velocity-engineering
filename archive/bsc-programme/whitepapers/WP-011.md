## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

A data structures course leaves you able to say that a balanced tree searches in logarithmic time and a hash table in constant. **That answers a question almost nobody asks you.** The question you actually get is: this caller does these five things, this often, on data this big, under these constraints — what should we use? The complexity table cannot settle it, because it deliberately throws away the terms that decide it at the size you actually have. So people fall back on habit, which is usually a hash table, and usually not wrong enough for anyone to correct.

The replacement is arithmetic rather than insight: work out the mix of operations from the logs, weight the costs by how often each happens, and add them up. Sometimes the winner is not the structure that is best at the most frequent operation, and that is the whole point — the day's opening item is built so you get it wrong.

This also finishes something started in week three. That day said a representation *throws things away*. On its own that is a warning and it makes people cautious. The other half is that it throws things away **in order to make something cheap**, and the purchase is the reason it exists. Ask only the first question and you become timid; ask only the second and you become careless. The programme needs both, permanently.

The fourth thing on the table is a vector produced by a real service, and it is there because it is the representation you are least likely to interrogate later — by then somebody else will have made all the decisions. Here you cannot avoid them. You must name a model, you must decide about vector length, and you must choose a function to compare two vectors with. **The platform will make all three for you if you say nothing, and then the number it hands back is not the thing you thought you were measuring.** *Similar* is a decision somebody made, not a property of the text.

**What this paper concedes, in the same plain terms.** The day settles your predictions with a measurement, weeks before the programme teaches you what a measurement is or how much of it is noise. That ordering is forced by the timetable rather than chosen, the paper knows the correction arrives sixteen days later, and **it admits that the provisional status of the arbiter you are being asked to trust is not currently signposted to you on the day.** It concedes separately that it cannot tell you whether the service behaved that afternoon the way it behaves on any other.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching data structure selection as frequency-weighted cost reasoning against a measured operation mix, rather than as asymptotic comparison; defends admitting a called embedding model as the fourth representation on the table; and defends siting the programme's longest-interval calibration event inside this day rather than in a dedicated occasion. The first argument is that asymptotic analysis answers a question students are rarely asked — how does this scale — while leaving unanswered the question they are always asked, which is what to give a particular caller; and that the mismatch produces graduates who can recite complexity tables and choose structures by habit. The second is that an embedding is a representation with a purchase and a loss like any other, that students will otherwise meet it first as a capability rather than as a choice, and that the platform's own defaults — a `dimensions` value nobody set, a similarity metric nobody chose, a published score that is not the similarity it appears to be — make the day's thesis operable in a way no in-memory structure can. The third argument is that a retention finding presented ceremonially is received as a claim about the programme, while the same finding delivered mid-content is received as evidence about oneself. The paper further defends measuring on real hardware with real data against a real endpoint, accepting the perishability cost. It concedes that the day may teach students to distrust asymptotic reasoning in settings where it is the correct tool, that its central adjudicating measurement is not reproducible in any principled sense, and that adding a service call to a class-1 day imports an entire failure surface the students cannot yet diagnose.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Students finish a data structures course able to state that a balanced tree has logarithmic search and a hash table has expected constant search. They cannot answer the question an engineer is actually asked, which is: given this caller, doing these operations at these frequencies on data of this size under these constraints, what should we use?

The gap is not ignorance of the table. It is that the table answers a different question. Asymptotic cost describes behaviour as size grows without bound; selection requires cost at the size you have, weighted by the frequencies you observe, including terms the asymptotic account deliberately discards. A student equipped only with the table has no procedure for the actual decision and falls back on habit — which is usually a hash table, and is usually not wrong enough to be corrected.

The day's second problem is the completion of S003. That day established that representations discard, and it carries a risk WP-003 named: the cynical reading, in which everything leaks and nothing can be trusted. The corrective is not reassurance but the other half of the account. A representation discards *in order to buy something*, and the purchase is the reason it exists. Joined, the two halves make a decision procedure; separated, the first is a warning and the second is salesmanship.

The third problem belongs to the programme rather than the topic. S001 asked students to predict their own six-week retention and sealed the prediction. Opening it requires a day at a sufficient interval, and week eleven is the first available. The design's decision — to open it inside a content day, in twelve minutes, between a retrieval set and a contrasting-cases phase — is a deliberate refusal of ceremony. A finding about one's own metacognitive accuracy, delivered as a special event, is read as the programme making a point. The same finding delivered as a routine item is read as data.

The fourth problem is the one the platform layer exists to solve, and it is the reason this day is the most-retrieved in the programme. Students will meet embeddings later as a capability — a thing that finds similar documents — and by then the decisions will be invisible because somebody else will have made them. Here they are unavoidable. You cannot obtain a vector without naming a deployment; you cannot compare two vectors without choosing a function; you cannot set a cut-off without knowing what the number you are cutting off actually is. The platform makes each of these a default rather than a prompt, which means the student who does not ask will get an answer and never learn that a question was asked. **Similar is a decision somebody made, not a property of text** is the sentence [S051](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S051.md), [S056](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S056.md), [S057](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S057.md) and [S058](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S058.md) all assume has landed, and this is the only day in the programme that can install it before the capability makes it feel unnecessary.

- **A ten-week-old sealed prediction** that they have not thought about.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Moderate, with one deliberate concentration.** Individual costs are low-interactivity facts. The interactivity is in the frequency-weighted sum, where changing one operation's frequency can reverse the ranking — and that interaction is the day's content.

Load management:

- **One dataset for the whole module.** Students meet it here and are still using it at S015. Comprehension load is paid once across five days.
- **The measurement apparatus is supplied and pre-verified.** Students run it; they do not build it. Building measurement instruments is S029's work and doing it here would displace the day entirely.
- **The endpoint, credential and deployment name are supplied,** and the phase 3 vectors are generated and cached before the day. Students call the service; they do not provision it. Provisioning is Q4's work.
- **Four representations, not a catalogue.** Enough for a ranking to reverse, few enough to hold. The fourth is the platform one and it is added at the cost of the fourth in-memory candidate, not on top of three.
- **Phase 3 supplies the mixes.** Groups predict and reconcile; they do not construct callers.
- **The P2 opening is time-boxed to twelve minutes** inside phase 2, deliberately, so that it does not become the day.

The design accepts one unmanaged load: step 4's measurement will produce anomalies — a run that disagrees with its neighbours — and students have no framework for measurement variability until S027. The instructor is briefed to supply the answer directly rather than to let it become a phase.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Committing predictions before measurement,* phases 3 and 5. The module's standing structure.
- *Naming the mis-modelled term,* step 5. Substantially harder than noting that a prediction was wrong.
- *Item 3's non-obvious winner* in the pretest, where the best-at-the-most-frequent-operation heuristic fails.
- *The realism demand at phase 7,* which forbids a hypothetical disqualified caller.
- *A ten-week retrieval interval* at the P2 opening, with two minutes of silence before interpretation.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Implementing the structures.* Refused. Implementation is CF102's conventional work and would consume the day.
- *Building the measurement harness.* Refused, per above.
- *Withholding the frequency-weighted framing.* Phase 4 point 2 states it. It is a method, and a withheld method is guessed at badly.
- *Explaining constant factors.* Named at point 4, explained at S016. The deferral is deliberate: the day needs students to accept that constants have causes without yet knowing them, which creates the open loop M04 closes.
- *More than four representations.*
- *Explaining what an embedding model is.* Refused. The day treats the endpoint as something that turns text into a vector for a price. How the vector is produced is IS202's work and a version of it lands at [S051](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S051.md); attempting it here would displace the cost reasoning entirely.

**The scope statement on the platform instance, in strong form.**

The day teaches a simplification — *an embedding deployment is a representation you can call, and a similarity function is a choice you make* — and the simplification has a safe range and an unsafe one, both of which are stated to students at phase 4 point 7 and both of which appear in the day's artefact.

It is **safe** for reasoning about representation choice under a known operation mix: what the vector makes cheap, what it discards, what it costs per record to build and per query to search, and what it cannot tell you. It is safe for setting and defending a similarity threshold, provided the student states which number they are thresholding on.

It is **unsafe** in four conditions and they are named. It is unsafe against **any model, version or region other than the one in front of you** — the deployed catalogue is three models today, the defaults are today's defaults, and a vector generated under one model is not comparable to a vector generated under another, which the documentation states plainly by saying you cannot upgrade between embedding models. It is unsafe as a claim about **retrieval quality**, because the day measures cost and latency and never measures whether the returned neighbours are the right ones — that is [S057](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S057.md)'s work and the day says so. It is unsafe as a claim about **failure behaviour**, because the day's calls succeed and a class-1 student has no framework for throttling, retry or partial failure; [S013](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S013.md) supplies the first and [S019](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S019.md) the rest. And it is unsafe as a claim about **what the platform's score means**, outside the one transform the day actually inspects.

The artefact element enforcing the boundary is phase 5 step 6, which requires the student to name what the winning representation discarded and a caller for whom the loss is disqualifying. A student who selects the embedding and cannot name what it cannot do fails the specification, which is the fail condition the scope rule's most recent widening requires.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **measured disagreement with confident prediction**, which is the same engine as S003 and S007 and is used here for the third time deliberately. By week eleven the structure is familiar, and familiarity is the point: the design wants the *content* to surprise, not the format.

Two things distinguish this day's version. The adjudicator is a measurement rather than an argument or a supplied change, which is a new kind of authority and is the first time the programme lets an instrument settle a dispute. And the data is real — the operation mix in phase 5 comes from the substrate's actual logs, so the caller is not a device.

A third source arrives with the platform layer and is worth naming separately. Phase 3 case D produces the day's only genuine indignation: a room that has just written down a similarity threshold discovers that the number they thresholded is a transform of the quantity they meant, published as such, in documentation they had not read. That reaction is the most reliably motivating event in the quarter, and it is motivating precisely because the platform is not hiding anything — the transform and its inverse are both documented. **What the students discover is not that they were deceived but that they did not look**, which is the disposition the whole programme is trying to build and is much harder to install by exhortation.

The P2 opening carries a distinct and heavier affective load. Most students predicted their retention would be better than it was, and the finding is delivered ten weeks after the prediction, alongside their actual retrieval performance. The two minutes of silence is the design's most important decision here: an instructor who interprets the result immediately converts a personal finding into a programme claim, and the personal version is what changes behaviour.

Competence is protected by phase 3's structure — three of five predictions are usually right, which establishes that the reasoning works before it is shown to be incomplete.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Four items on one dataset and four representations, plus the sealed Copilot prediction. Item 3's non-obvious winner is the construction that matters: the representation that wins is not best at the most frequent operation, which defeats the heuristic students actually use and does so before it is named. The Copilot item asks for four specific elements — model, `dimensions`, similarity function, error — rather than a general forecast, because a general forecast cannot be scored and a scored prediction is the only kind the frontier map can use.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** A short retrieval set with a synthesis item joining S003 and S007, then the P2 opening. The synthesis item is placed immediately before P2 deliberately — it is a retrieval success for most students, which is a better state to be in when receiving a retention finding than a retrieval failure would be.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Five mixes, committed predictions, measured adjudication, four executed configurations of the embedding representation. Five mixes rather than three because the design needs two distinct failure modes — frequency-weighting arithmetic and unmodelled constants — and one or two mixes cannot reliably produce both; this departs from the pattern's two-to-four and the departure is the whole of the justification. Cases A to D vary one service's configuration rather than presenting four services, which is what "executed cases" can mean on a day whose subject is representation rather than architecture. Case D is the payload and it is the design's answer to instrumentation rule 7: the claim that a platform score is not the quantity it is named after is a claim the room will resist, so it is reached from an artefact the room produced rather than asserted from the front. The closing task asks for the rule the group was using, which is the phase's abstraction and is generated rather than supplied.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Eight points. Point 1 completes S003's pair and is stated as a pair, in one sentence, because that is the retained form. Point 3 is the honest scope statement for asymptotic reasoning and is the day's protection against the objection in §8. Point 4 opens the loop M04 closes. Point 6 is the day's defaults-are-opinions point and names four; the Azure MCP Server one is chosen deliberately because the quarter's anchor is the read-only server and the option that makes it read-only is off by default, which is the cheapest available demonstration that a name is not a guarantee. Point 7 is the perishable point and carries the strong-form scope statement.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Six steps on a real logged access pattern, ending in a shipped component rather than an analysis. Step 1's derivation of a mix from logs is the day's only genuinely new analytical skill; step 3 is the day's only genuinely new operational one. Step 5's mis-modelled-term requirement is the reconciliation discipline. Step 6 joins the day to S003 and is the module's recurring close. The three artefact elements are the Copilot Practice Standard's and are not modified here.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** Two disclosures. The instructor's structure choice that outlived its mix, with attention to whether the choice was *recorded as a bet*, which makes S007's framing operational rather than remembered. Then the agent case: a normalisation error inside a generated similarity function, passed by two reviewers. It is chosen over more spectacular agent failures precisely because it is boring — the code was readable, the tests compared floats to floats, and the only thing that would have caught it was a case whose answer was known independently. The transferable claim is that review catches what looks wrong and this did not.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** The realism demand. Without it, the disqualified caller at step 5 is a rhetorical flourish; with it, students must locate a real or plausible person, which is the first small instance of the stakeholder reasoning M17 develops.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Prediction rank distribution, then the Copilot prediction opened against what the agent did. A brief P2 revisit — not a re-interpretation, but a question about what students now believe about their own retention estimates, which is the calibration outcome the programme actually wants.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with performance-engineering experience will predict phase 3's mixes accurately, including the constant-factor case, and will find phase 5 routine. Predicted failure: they treat the day as confirmation and skip step 5's model articulation, which is where their intuition would become transferable. Countermeasure: their phase 3 group is asked to produce the *rule*, and an accurate predictor who cannot state their rule is exposed by the task rather than by the instructor. In phase 7 they are assigned the hardest disqualified-caller cases.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has never measured anything faces an unfamiliar apparatus, unfamiliar data and an unfamiliar failure mode all at once. Countermeasures: the apparatus is supplied and pre-verified; step 1 is worked with the room; step 2's model is supplied for the first representation; and the dataset persists across the module. Residual risk: measurement anomalies will confuse them more than others, and the design's answer is that the instructor supplies the explanation directly rather than treating it as a teachable moment, because it is not one until S027.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Three of five predictions are expected to be right, which is by construction. If a group predicts all five, the fallback is to hand them a sixth mix chosen so that the two representations they ranked adjacent swap under a small frequency change, and ask them to locate the crossover — which previews S012 and is hard.

**The endpoint fails.** The day now depends on a service, which is a failure mode the previous version did not have. A throttled, misconfigured or unavailable deployment stops phase 3 for everyone at once. Countermeasures: the vectors for the phase 3 dataset are generated once before the day and cached, so that a group whose calls fail can proceed on cached vectors and record the failure as an observation; the phase 5 build's dataset is small enough to stay well inside a single deployment's per-minute quota; and the instructor holds a second deployment. Residual risk: a student whose calls fail will conclude something about the platform's reliability from a sample of one, and the day has no framework for that until S027. The instructor states the sample size out loud.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is concentrated in the P2 opening rather than in the content. A student whose prediction was badly wrong, delivered alongside evidence of their actual retention, may experience it as a judgement about their capability rather than about human metacognition generally. Countermeasures: the cohort distribution is shown, and it is uniformly poor, which makes the finding structural; the two-minute silence prevents an instructor framing from arriving before the student's own; and the S001 whitepaper's commitment — that the programme reports its own instrument results honestly whichever way they fall — is honoured here, including in the case where the cohort's predictions were *accurate*, which would be a finding against the design.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence.

One specification item in CF102, requiring the logged mix, committed predictions, measurements taken from the built component, mis-modelled-term reconciliation, the discarded-property statement with a named disqualified caller, and the three artefact elements — prompt record, intervention record and frontier statement.

The distinctive clause: **a prediction matching the measurement exactly, with no model stated, fails.** This looks perverse and is not. An unmodelled correct prediction is either a lucky guess or a post-hoc entry, and neither is the competence being certified. The clause is the same discipline as S009's requirement that survival be explained.

The second distinctive clause is the Standard's: **an intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the build demonstrates that an intervention-worthy error would have been detectable. On this day the demonstration is cheap and specific — a similarity assertion whose correct answer is known independently of the code that produces it — which is why the exemption is real rather than decorative.

Programme instrumentation: **the P2 outcome is the programme's first long-interval calibration measurement** and is the most important instrument in Q2; phase 1 item 4's prediction-rank distribution extends the calibration series; step 5's mis-modelled-term specification failure rate measures whether reconciliation is producing model correction or narration; and **the phase 1 Copilot prediction, scored at phase 8, is the programme's first frontier-calibration datum.** Its reader is next year's cohort, through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), which satisfies instrumentation rule 9. Its branch, per rule 4, is that a cohort whose frontier predictions are systematically over-confident receives an expanded [S045](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S045.md) unassisted segment; a cohort that is systematically under-confident does not.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day teaches students to distrust asymptotic reasoning, which is the more general and more durable tool.**

At its strongest: the whole apparatus — measured constants, frequency weighting, hardware-specific results — trains students to believe that analysis is unreliable and measurement is truth. That belief is wrong and expensive. Asymptotic reasoning is the only tool that answers questions about inputs you have not seen and sizes you have not reached, which is exactly the situation in which choices are hardest to reverse. A student who has learned to measure will measure at the size they have and choose a structure that fails at the size they will have, and the failure will arrive in production.

The rebuttal is that the day's phase 4 point 3 states the scope condition explicitly and in the asymptotic account's favour: it is sufficient when one operation dominates and sizes are large, which is the growth case the objection describes. The day's claim is narrower than the objection reads it — that asymptotic analysis is insufficient *for selection under a known mix at a known size*, which is a different question from scaling.

The concession is about emphasis rather than content. Point 3 occupies a few minutes; the measured-disagreement machinery occupies four hours. Students take away emphasis, not proportion, and the day is very likely to leave a stronger impression of "measure it" than of "and here is when you must not." The structurally correct fix is a phase 3 mix at a size where the measured winner is the asymptotic loser at ten times the size, which would put the scope condition inside the adjudicated experience rather than in a spoken caveat. That mix is not currently in the design.

**Objection 2: the adjudicating measurements are not reproducible, and the day treats them as though they settle things.**

At its strongest: the phase 3 and phase 5 measurements are taken on particular hardware, with a particular runtime, particular library implementations, particular cache state and particular background load. Run them next term and some rankings will differ. The design marks this as perishable content, which handles the *reporting* problem, but it does not handle the epistemic one: the day's entire adjudication mechanism — commit a prediction, measure, reconcile — treats the measurement as the arbiter of the prediction's correctness, when the measurement is itself a noisy observation of a specific configuration. Students are being taught to update their models against an authority the design knows is unstable, sixteen days before ME101 teaches them that measurements have error structure.

This objection the design does not fully answer, and the ordering problem it identifies is real: S011 uses measurement as an arbiter and S027 teaches what a measurement is. That is backwards, and it is a consequence of the timetable rather than a choice — CF102 must run in Q2 and ME101 cannot.

Two partial mitigations exist and neither is sufficient. Phase 3's rankings are constructed so that the *reversals* are robust — the mixes are chosen so that the winner changes by a wide margin rather than a narrow one — which means the day's conclusions survive configuration change even where the numbers do not. And the instructor supplies the variability explanation directly when anomalies appear, rather than letting students infer that measurement is exact. But the design is knowingly asking students to treat as authoritative a class of evidence it will spend all of M06 complicating, and the honest characterisation is that S011's epistemics are provisional and are corrected sixteen days later. The correction is not currently signposted to students at S011, and it should be.

**Objection 3: putting a service call into a class-1 day imports a whole failure surface the students cannot diagnose, and the day will teach the platform badly in the course of teaching representation well.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection that bites hardest on the practical turn: the previous version of this day was closed. Everything in it could be explained from the machine on the desk. The revised version makes phase 3 depend on authentication, a deployment name, a region, a quota, a network and an error taxonomy, none of which is taught here and all of which can fail. When a student's call returns 401 they will learn that the platform is unreliable; when it returns 403 they will learn that permissions are annoying; when it returns 400 because the batch exceeded an aggregate token limit they will learn nothing at all, because the limit is not visible in anything they wrote. The day will spend minutes on incidents whose explanation is deferred to Q4, and those minutes come out of the frequency-weighting work that is the actual content. Worse, the failure modes are *interesting*, and interesting irrelevant material displaces boring essential material reliably.

**This objection is conceded, and the cheap fix is named.** The design does not attempt to argue that a class-1 day can absorb an error taxonomy. What it does instead is bound the surface: the deployment, endpoint, credential and deployment name are supplied and pre-verified; the phase 3 vectors are generated and cached before the day so that a failing call costs a group its own observation and not the phase; and the phase 5 dataset is sized to stay inside one deployment's per-minute quota. The cheap fix, which is in the design, is a **one-page supplied error card** listing the four responses a student may see — 401, 403, 404 and 400 — with the one-line cause the documentation gives for each and an explicit statement that the mechanism behind each is taught later and named. It costs one page, it converts an unexplained incident into a labelled one, and it is the difference between a student who has met an error and a student who has met a mystery. What is *not* fixed, and is recorded as unfixed, is that the day still has no way to tell a student whether the endpoint's behaviour that afternoon was representative, which is the same objection-2 problem arriving through a second door.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A majority of students will fail phase 1 item 3 by selecting the representation best at the most frequent operation. *Instrument:* item 3 responses coded against the best-at-most-frequent heuristic.

**Prediction 2.** The S001 P2 predictions will exceed measured retention for a large majority of the cohort, and the overestimate will be larger for students whose S001 phase 1 confidence was higher. *Instrument:* P2 predictions against S007 and S009 retrieval performance, split by S001 confidence. **A cohort whose P2 predictions were accurate would be a finding against the metacognitive-illusion premise the programme is built on, and would be reported as such.**

**Prediction 3.** In phase 3, groups will fail the constant-factor mix at a higher rate than the frequency-weighting mix, and their stated rule will not mention constants. *Instrument:* per-mix prediction accuracy and the phase 3 stated rules coded for constant-factor mention.

**Prediction 4.** Step 5 mis-modelled-term statements will be rated higher in specificity than the equivalent reconciliation statements at S007, without additional instruction. *Instrument:* blind-rated specificity at S007 and S011. This is the reconciliation-discipline transfer claim; a flat result means the S007 step was learned as an S007 requirement.

**Prediction 5.** A large majority of phase 1 Copilot predictions will name a model and a similarity function and will not mention `dimensions` at all. *Instrument:* sealed predictions coded for whether each of the four requested elements is present. **The `dimensions` omission is the specific claim: students predict what the agent will write and not what it will silently accept, which is the frontier's characteristic blind spot.** If `dimensions` is mentioned by most of the cohort, the day's platform layer is redundant and should be shortened.

**Prediction 6.** At [S051](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S051.md), students will state unprompted that a similarity score is a chosen function rather than a property of the text, at a higher rate than a cohort taught embeddings first as a capability. *Instrument:* an S051 protocol coded for unprompted metric-as-choice framing. Untestable until a variant delivery exists; recorded so that the day's central inherited claim has a named check rather than an assumption.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That a complexity table answers a question you are rarely asked, and that the question you are always asked — what should this caller use — is a frequency-weighted cost at the size you actually have. An embedding deployment is admitted as a fourth representation on the same terms, because *similar* is a decision somebody made and not a property of the text.

**What the evidence licenses.** The cost-model and representation-selection framing is **class 1**, grounded in notes cited below. Metacognitive illusions, spacing and contrasting cases are **class 2**, direction and mechanism only — the P2 opening is about a direction, not a size. The platform layer is **class 4**, read at a stated date, and its silences are the day's material: the vector length is not documented, what a reduced `dimensions` costs is not documented, and **no retrieval-accuracy figure of the vendor's own is published**, so none is quoted here. That this arrangement teaches the pair is **class 3**.

**What to do with it.**

- **Derive the mix from logs, then weight it.** The representation best at the most frequent operation is not automatically the winner, and item 3 exists to prove that on you.
- **Name what the winner discarded and a caller for whom the loss is disqualifying.** Selecting the embedding and being unable to say what it cannot do fails the specification.
- **Say which number you are thresholding on.** A published score can be a documented transform of the quantity it is named after, with the inverse published alongside it — and you will not know unless you look.
- **A default is an opinion somebody else recorded.** A server called read-only whose read-only option defaults to false is a configured control that has not been configured; **pin the revision, host and model** and check the option rather than the name. See the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md).

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes that the day uses measurement as the arbiter of your predictions weeks before the programme teaches what a measurement is, that the ordering is forced by the timetable rather than chosen, and that **the provisional status of that arbiter is not currently signposted to you on the day.** It concedes further that it cannot tell you whether the endpoint's behaviour on the afternoon you measured it was representative of anything.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.**

*What the documentation says.* Microsoft Learn documents three embedding models sold directly by Azure in Foundry — `text-embedding-3-large`, `text-embedding-3-small` and `text-embedding-ada-002` — and states that **you cannot upgrade between embedding models**, so moving between them requires generating new embeddings ([Foundry Models sold by Azure](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/foundry/foundry-models/concepts/models-sold-directly-by-azure#embeddings)). The v1 REST reference for `POST {endpoint}/openai/v1/embeddings` documents `dimensions` as optional with a minimum of 1 and **supported only in `text-embedding-3` and later models**, and `encoding_format` as optional with a default of `float` ([Azure OpenAI Embeddings, API version v1](https://learn.microsoft.com/rest/api/microsoft-foundry/azureopenai/embeddings)). The how-to guide states that an embedding is a vector of floating-point numbers representing semantic meaning, that the `model` value in a request is the *deployment* name, that maximum input is 8,192 tokens per input with a maximum array size of 2,048 and a 300,000-token aggregate per request, and that Entra ID authentication requires the `Cognitive Services OpenAI User` role ([Generate embeddings with Azure OpenAI](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/foundry/openai/how-to/embeddings)). Azure AI Search documents its vector `@search.score` as `1 / (1 + cosine_distance)`, bounded 0.333 to 1.00 for the cosine metric, states explicitly that it **is not the cosine value**, and publishes an inverse function for callers who need the real one ([Relevance in vector search](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/search/vector-search-ranking#scores-in-a-vector-search-results)). The Azure MCP Server documents a **Read only** server start option — "If true, no write operations are allowed" — with a **default of `false`**, and per-tool annotation hints including Read Only and Idempotent ([Azure MCP Server tools](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/developer/azure-mcp-server/tools/)).

*At what version and date.* All pages retrieved 2026-07-26. The embeddings REST reference is at API version `v1`; the Azure MCP Server pages are undated in their own text and carry no version stamp, which is itself a finding.

*What the documentation does not report that a reader of this day would need.* Four silences, and students are shown all four. **It does not state the vector length any model returns** — the how-to guide's own sample output prints `Embedding dimensions: <number>`, redacted, so the length must be obtained by calling. **It does not state what is lost when `dimensions` is reduced**; the parameter is documented as available, with a minimum of 1, and nothing anywhere says what a 1-dimensional embedding is good for. **It reports no retrieval accuracy figure of its own**; the only quality statement is a direction attributed to OpenAI's testing on the MIRACL and MTEB benchmarks, with no interval, no baseline system and no Microsoft reproduction, and this whitepaper therefore asserts no magnitude and no comparison between the models. **And it states no latency or throughput expectation for the embeddings endpoint**, which is the one number the day's cost model most needs and the reason the day measures rather than quotes. The asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](/research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md) — that vendor sources state bounds and never accuracies — holds exactly here, and inverts in one respect worth teaching: the sampling and size bounds are stated precisely, and the quantity the engineer would use them to predict is not stated at all.

*Flagged 2026-07-30, not yet re-verified:* every page above is given a retrieval date and no page-edit stamp, on the day that teaches an irreversible representation decision as its own perishability example; the pair is what makes drift detectable and only one half is recorded.

*Flagged 2026-07-30, not yet re-verified:* three product surfaces are presented here as one platform layer — the models catalogue, the embeddings REST endpoint and Azure AI Search's relevance scoring — and phase 3 case D applies the AI Search score transform to vectors produced by a Foundry embedding deployment; whether that transform is a property of the index or of the vectors is not established in this paper.

The §8 objection-1 concession identifies a missing phase 3 mix that would put the asymptotic scope condition inside the adjudicated experience; the objection-2 concession states that the day's use of measurement as an arbiter precedes the instruction that would justify it, that this ordering is forced by the timetable, and that the provisional status is not currently signposted to students; the objection-3 concession accepts that a class-1 day cannot absorb a service error taxonomy, names the error card as the cheap fix, and records as unfixed that the day cannot tell a student whether the endpoint's behaviour on the day was representative.
