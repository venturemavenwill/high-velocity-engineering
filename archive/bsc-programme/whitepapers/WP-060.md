## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

Four days have taught you to qualify everything, and every one of those qualifications was correct. Which ranker wins depends on the queries. What your retriever can tell apart depends on the negatives you chose. What the approximation cost depends on which documents it lost. What a published number licenses depends on what its authors happened to fix.

**Every one of those is also a reason to wait, and today there is no waiting.** The system goes to the substrate's real users or it does not, the interval is what it is, and the person who decides is in the room. The thing being judged is not whether you decide well in some abstract sense. It is whether you can act on evidence that is never going to become sufficient, and say in writing where the edge of it is instead of using the edge as a reason to defer.

The honest form of that commitment has three parts that people leave out, and each is left out for a different reason. **A promise the user can actually check** gets left out because internal metrics are the vocabulary your team already speaks. **A withdrawal signal owned by somebody who is not you** gets left out because handing over the stop button feels like losing control exactly when control feels scarce. **A failure mode you did not want to be true** gets left out because you now have four days of sophisticated apparatus with which to argue it away.

Then the consequence arrives while you are still in the room. Ninety minutes after you approve the release, the first user reports are read aloud, and your prediction about which documents the approximation would lose meets the actual query log. **That is not assessed.** It is said three times and it is written into the specification, because a prediction that is graded stops being a prediction and becomes a strategy.

And not shipping is a result. Say what evidence would change it.

**What this paper concedes, and it is a real concession.** Half of the frontier statement asks what the agent did that you could not have done as fast — **and you have no unassisted baseline for any of it.** You have never built a search index without one, never configured a chunking pipeline without one. So the answer is a self-report about a world you have not visited, written by somebody with every reason to think the tool is quick, on a day that is going well. It is the least reliable sentence in the record and it is a checked element. The substitution that would fix it — *one thing the agent did that you could not verify* — is named and not made, because the wording belongs to a shared page this paper does not amend.

## Abstract

This paper defends placing the quarter's only entrustment judgement on a real release to real users, and defends letting the consequence arrive inside the same day rather than a quarter later. Its central argument is that four days of findings can each be qualified indefinitely and a release cannot, and that the capability being judged is the ability to act on evidence that never becomes sufficient. It defends the eight-part release record, and in particular the three parts the design expects students to omit: a promise the user can check, a withdrawal signal with a named actor who is not the student, and a failure mode the student did not want to be true. It defends decoupling the production check from assessment on the argument S050 established. **It defends adding the prompt, intervention and frontier records to the release record rather than to a separate build, and argues that the prompt record's abandoned instructions partially supply the rejected-alternative evidence this paper's own §8 named as missing — while stating precisely how far that goes and where it stops.** The paper reports discharge of four obligations, from S054, S058 twice and S059. It argues that **rater discipline rule 7's premise inverts for a prospective decision**, concedes that the activity's second clause — own the consequence — is not assessed at all, and concedes that one half of the frontier statement asks for a counterfactual the student has no basis to supply.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The module has taught four days of qualification. Which ranker wins depends on the query population. Which distinctions a retriever can make depends on its negatives. What an approximation costs depends on which documents it loses. What a published number licenses depends on what its authors fixed. Every one of those is correct and every one of them is a reason to wait.

**Today there is no waiting available.** The system either goes to the substrate's users or it does not, the interval is what it is, and the person who decides is in the room. The difficulty is not that students will decide badly. It is that a cohort trained for five quarters to say what a measurement does not license has been given no practice at all in the act that follows — committing anyway, in writing, with the boundary stated rather than used as a reason to defer.

The second problem is that the honest form of the commitment has three parts students systematically omit, and each is omitted for a different reason. **A promise the user can check** is omitted because internal metrics are the vocabulary the team already has. **A withdrawal signal owned by somebody else** is omitted because it feels like a loss of control at exactly the moment control feels scarce — and [S035](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S035.md) established two quarters ago why the person inside the experiment is the wrong one to stop it. **A failure mode you did not want to be true** is omitted because the whole apparatus of the preceding four days can be used to argue it away.

The third problem is the one [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md) measured and this day inherits. What a student fails to anticipate is a larger quantity than what they state wrongly, and it is not correctable by care. The design's response is not to demand more failure modes; it is to require one of them to come from the student's own [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md) category 3 — a failure they already demonstrated they did not think of — so that the statement is informed by their own record rather than by their imagination.

The fourth problem is structural and belongs to the quarter. Q7 profiles, batches and budgets a real system, and [Q6](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q6.md)'s position is that it must be the students' own. **This day exists in part because the next four weeks need it to have happened.** That is a curriculum reason for an entrustment occasion and it is stated rather than hidden.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**High in a way no other day in the module is: the load is consequential rather than cognitive.** Nothing in phase 3 is difficult to write. What is difficult is writing it knowing it goes out.

The interactivity is between record parts 2, 3 and 4. What the evidence licenses bounds what the failure-mode statement can claim to have excluded, and both bound what the promise may say. A student who writes a strong promise and a weak evidence statement has produced an internally inconsistent record, and that inconsistency is the single most legible signal available to an assessor reading before the defence.

Management:

- **The system already exists.** Nothing is built today.
- **Each student owns one component decision**, not the whole release, so the record is bounded.
- **The record's eight parts are supplied as a template** with the fields named; the design's position is that a template removes format load and does not remove judgement.
- **Part 7's three records are assembled rather than written.** They exist from the three preceding days and are consolidated, which is why an eighth part costs minutes rather than an hour.
- **Phase 2's paired reconstruction** re-installs [S035](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S035.md) and [S045](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S045.md) immediately before they are needed.
- **The category-3 requirement points at an existing document** rather than asking for recall.

Expertise reversal would begin to apply to the record's format for any student who has shipped professionally; it does not apply to the decision, and no part of the template is faded because the template is the artefact the assessment reads.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Predicting whether you will volunteer your weakest evidence,* item 2.
- *Naming who stops this, before the release,* phase 2's bridge and record part 5.
- *A promise a user can check rather than a metric a team can quote,* record part 4.
- *One failure mode drawn from your own record of what you failed to anticipate,* record part 3.
- *One property your system selects for that nobody asked for,* record part 3.
- *Being asked which published claim you relied on and what its breakdown showed,* phase 4.
- *Naming a default you inherited and what accepting it committed you to,* phase 4.
- *Being asked which instruction you gave the agent and then abandoned, and why,* phase 4.
- *Naming the part of the decision you are least willing to own,* phase 4.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *A cost position.* EA-1's, and [S065](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S065.md)'s. Today's decision has no budget attached and the day says so.
- *An externally imposed deadline.* Named as the difference between this day and EA-1 in the entrustment statement.
- *Negotiating scope with the requester.* [S085](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S085.md)'s.
- *Authorisation and access control on the retrieval path.* [S078](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S078.md)'s, and named at point 5 as the home for the half of the selection argument this day opens and does not answer.
- *Any further platform configuration.* **The system is frozen at the start of the day.** A student who wants to change an operating point has to decide not to ship instead, which is the point.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** Point 5 states that every retrieval system selects for something nobody asked for, and names two: the relevance criterion's preferred kind of answer, and the corpus's determination of whose documents are findable at all. **Safe** as a standing obligation for a student shipping retrieval — look for both, name one, evidence it. **Unsafe** as a completed ethical account. The second selection is not a technical defect with a technical remedy; whose documents are indexed is a question about who was asked, who had documents to give, and who was never in the conversation, and this day supplies no apparatus for it whatsoever. **A student who leaves believing that naming the property discharges the duty has taken a one-line finding for an answer**, and the design's position is that the finding is worth having anyway because it is the entry point. Both homes are named: [S078](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S078.md) for the authorisation half and [S082](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S082.md) for the person-not-in-the-room half.

**Point 7 carries the module's terminal platform scope statement and it is deliberately self-cancelling.** It enumerates eight decisions the students did not make and that now sit between their relevance criterion and a user's result, and then states that the enumeration is worthless and the habit that produced it is the content. That is **safe** as the module's closing instruction and safe as a transferable act. It is **unsafe** in one direction that matters: **the count is not a property of retrieval and is not a property of platforms; it is a property of one product's current surface**, and a student who arrives at a different system expecting eight inherited decisions will look for eight and stop. The durable form given at point 7 — enumerate before you promise — is stated with no number attached for exactly that reason, and the day's perishability register marks the enumeration itself as the perishable item rather than its members.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **the release actually going out**.

Every prior assessment-bearing day in the programme has been performed against a constructed audience or a bounded artefact. [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md) used trained role-players and said so. [S045](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S045.md) deployed to a quarter that had not happened yet. Here the system serves the substrate's real users during phase 4, and phase 5 reads their first reports aloud. There is no framing to manage: the motivation is that people are using it.

The second mechanism is the ninety-minute gap. [S045](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S045.md) and [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md) both reported consequences a quarter later, to students who had moved on and for whom the prediction was a document rather than a memory. **Here a student is in the room when their [S058](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S058.md) prediction about which documents the approximation would lose meets the query log.** The design's claim is that the compression of the interval is what makes the check land.

Phase 7 supplies the affective content, and its construction matters: a kept promise, a correct release, better results by every measure taken, and a group of users whose habit was destroyed. Nobody was wrong. The users could not have warned anyone because they did not know what they were relying on. It is the only case in the module in which doing everything correctly is insufficient, and it is placed immediately after students have read their own first user reports.

The competence risk is the largest in the module and it is at phase 5. A student whose system failed on queries they did not predict, in front of a cohort, may read the check as a verdict on the decision they made ninety minutes earlier. The decoupling from assessment is stated three times — before phase 3, before phase 5, and in the written specification — and the instructor states that a low prediction-match rate is the expected result and is the reason [S068](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S068.md) exists.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items. Item 2's second clause — *would you say so unprompted, or only if asked* — is the day's honest item and is the one checked against observed behaviour ninety minutes later. **Item 5 asks what the agent did across four days that the student did not catch, and what they are still unsure it got right**, and is opened at phase 8 against their own intervention record and against the production check.

**Phase 2 (30 min).** Six retrievals across the module and back two quarters, closing on a bridge whose answer is a required field in the artefact. The bridge is not rhetorical, which is a departure from the module's other four.

**Phase 3 (60 min).** The entrusted activity: the eight-part release record, whose eighth part is the release itself. **Part 3 discharges [S054](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S054.md)'s obligation and [Q6](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q6.md)'s category-3 report; part 4 discharges [S058](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S058.md)'s promise obligation; part 5's third clause makes phase 2's bridge binding; part 7 carries the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s three records, consolidated from the three preceding days.**

**Phase 4 (60 min).** Defence after the level is formed, per rater discipline rule 7 — whose adequacy for this activity class is §8 objection 1. **The published-claim question is the artefact element [S059](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S059.md) named** and is asked of every student rather than as a follow-up. **Two further questions are asked of every student: the inherited default, which is the Copilot Practice Standard's requirement in assessed form, and the abandoned instruction, which is the closest this design comes to eliciting a rejected alternative.**

**Phase 5 (35 min).** The production check, at a gap of hours. **The lost-document comparison is the instrument [S058](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S058.md) named.** Not assessed, stated three times.

**Phase 6 (35 min).** Eight points, assessors absent. Point 5 is the most important and is deliberately left open with both homes named. **Point 7 closes the module's platform layer by enumerating it and then discarding it in favour of the habit.**

**Phase 7 (40 min).** A kept promise, a better system, and a destroyed habit nobody could have described — then a four-day build with an exemplary intervention record and a chunk size nobody decided.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 2 against the phase 4 record; **item 5 against the intervention record and the production result**; predicted against awarded level; the aggregate production result; **the [S010](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S010.md) instrument's seventh administration with the full six-quarter series shown.**

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has shipped professionally will produce a well-formed record quickly and will be fluent in the defence. Predicted failure: fluency substituting for the specific judgements the day is about — a promise phrased in an internal metric because that is what release notes contain, and a withdrawal signal owned by "the team". Countermeasures: both are named failure conditions rather than quality judgements, so fluency does not reach them; and the least-willing-to-own question at phase 4 is not answerable from practice.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is the record's volume under time pressure and, behind it, the fear that an incomplete record means an unsafe release. Countermeasures: the template supplies every field; each student owns one component rather than the system; phase 2 reconstructs [S035](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S035.md) and [S045](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S045.md) immediately beforehand; and **declining to ship is named as a fully available answer before phase 1**, so no student is choosing between a bad record and a bad release.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** A student who has held a defensible decision since [S059](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S059.md) will complete the record in half the time. The difficulty is in part 3's unintended selection property, which cannot be produced from the decision and requires evidence from their own materials. Fallback if a student produces one quickly: ask them to name **the second one — the corpus's**, whose documents are findable at all — which point 5 says is the one with ethical weight and which has no supplied answer, and to state who they would have to ask to find out.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **exposure**. A student whose component fails in front of the cohort ninety minutes after they approved it, on a day they are being assessed, in a programme where the failure is logged — that is the sharpest affective load the design has constructed anywhere, and it is constructed deliberately.

Countermeasures: phase 5's decoupling from assessment is stated three times and is a written specification commitment; individual results are private and only the aggregate is shown; assessors are absent from phases 6 and 7 and receive no account of them, per the error-clinic firewall; phase 7's instructor case is a correct release that broke something anyway, which establishes before any student speaks that this outcome is not a competence signal; and **the [S010](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S010.md) instrument's seventh administration is taken at the end of this day specifically because it is the hardest day in the series to survive**, which makes the measurement informative rather than routine.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: rater discipline rule 7's premise inverts for a prospective decision, and this is the occasion where that matters most.**

At its strongest: rule 7 requires the assessor's provisional level to come from the artefact, with the defence used to confirm or overturn it, and its stated justification is that **"a defence supplies narrative while an artefact supplies evidence."** The rule was added at [WP-040](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-040.md) §8 for occasions whose artefact records what a student did.

**This artefact records what a student is about to do.** The decision concerns a population not yet observed. The failure modes concern failures that have not occurred. The promise has not been tested and the withdrawal signal has not fired. Everything in the record is a claim about the future, and **the record therefore contains no evidence at all about the quality of the judgement being assessed** — only evidence about whether the judgement was well expressed. The document that distinguishes a student who reasoned from one who wrote well does not exist, because the reasoning is in their head until the defence asks for it.

The consequence is specific and adverse. Applied literally, rule 7 has two assessors form a level from a template-completed document, and then use the defence — which is the only place judgement is observable — merely to test an impression formed from prose quality. That is close to the inversion rule 4 names as the site of rater variance, arrived at by obeying rule 7 rather than by breaking it.

There is a real partial rebuttal. The record does carry checkable properties: whether what each piece of evidence licenses is stated, whether the ceiling from [S059](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S059.md) was respected, whether the promise is checkable, whether the withdrawal signal names a person. Those are artefact facts and they are exactly the day's five failure conditions. **But they are the specification elements, which are binary and pass/fail, and the entrustment level is not a specification decision.** The level is about judgement, and this artefact does not hold any.

**The objection is conceded, and the fix is one field.** Require the record to carry **the decision the student considered and rejected, and why** — the operating point not taken, the promise not made, the condition not imposed. A rejected alternative with its reason is evidence about reasoning rather than about outcome, it exists at the time the record is written, and it is exactly what an assessor needs before the defence. It costs one field in a template that already has seven and it is not in the design.

**The finding is not local to this day and is offered as a compliance-register entry.** Rule 7 is a written rule and this day complies with it; the finding is that compliance produces a weak judgement for an entire class of occasion — every prospective decision the programme assesses, which includes [S070](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S070.md), [S085](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S085.md) and the whole of EA-1. Under the register's criterion this is a design judgement rather than a violation, and is therefore **recorded** rather than fixed. The argument is carried to the programme rather than written into the shared pages, which this paper does not amend.

**Objection 2: the activity's second clause is not assessed.**

At its strongest: the entrusted activity is *ship a retrieval system into production **and own the consequence***. The consequence arrives at phase 5. The specification states in writing that phase 5's result is not assessed. **The level is therefore formed entirely from the anticipation and never from the ownership**, and a student who ships something that fails and handles it well receives the same level as one who ships something that fails and handles it badly. The activity's name promises something the assessment does not deliver.

The rebuttal on the decoupling is correct and this paper would make the same choice again. [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md) established it: assessing a prediction check makes the prediction strategic, students would list every conceivable failure mode to avoid an unanticipated one, and the quantity being measured would be destroyed by the act of measuring it.

**But that argument defends not assessing the outcome, and the objection is about not assessing the conduct, which is a different object.** What a student does in the ninety minutes between approving a release and reading its first user reports is observable, is not a prediction, and cannot be gamed by over-listing: whether they watched, what they watched, whether they told anyone what they found, whether they raised their own failure before somebody else did. **This is conceded.** The fix is an assessor observation window across that interval, scored on conduct rather than outcome, and it is the only part of this day that would make the activity's second clause real. It costs one assessor's attention for the duration of the defence phase, during which assessors are present anyway. It is not in the design, and the day's name currently promises more than its specification checks.

**Objection 3: half the frontier statement asks for a counterfactual the student has no basis to supply, on the day the programme is least able to check it.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn.

At its strongest: the frontier statement requires *one thing the agent did across these four days that you could not have done as fast*. **The student has no unassisted baseline on any of it.** They have never built a search index without an agent, never configured a chunking pipeline without one, never reparameterised a vector profile without one. The comparison is therefore a self-report about a counterfactual world, produced by somebody with a strong prior that the tool is fast — they have been told so, they have watched it work, and the statement is being written on a day that is going well. **It is the least reliable sentence in the release record and it is a checked specification element.**

Worse, it sits directly on the risk the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) names as the one this programme cannot currently detect: *a student who has only ever built with an agent may be unable to build without one.* The Standard's structural response is three unassisted occasions, and none of them is in this quarter. **So the programme is collecting speed judgements from exactly the population whose speed judgements it has said it cannot validate**, and aggregating them into a frontier map that the following year's cohort reads.

The rebuttal covers the other half and only the other half. *One thing it did badly that you had to catch* is not a counterfactual at all — it is an observation, it is checkable against the intervention record, and it is the half that carries the engineering content. That half should stay exactly as written.

**The concession is unqualified and the fix is a substitution rather than an addition.** Replace the first half with **one thing the agent did that you could not verify** — same length, same position, no additional elicitation, and it asks for something the student is uniquely placed to know. It is checkable against the intervention record in the same way the second half is. It produces a quantity the programme actually needs, since an accumulating list of unverifiable agent outputs is the raw material for [S068](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S068.md)'s detectors and for the dependency measurement the Standard says is missing. And it removes an unfalsifiable speed claim from a record that is otherwise entirely composed of statements a student can be held to. **The design did not take it, and the reason is that the Standard specifies the frontier statement's wording and this paper does not amend shared pages** — which is a correct procedural constraint offered against a methodological objection, and is therefore recorded here and carried to the programme rather than acted on.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Fewer than a fifth of students will volunteer their weakest evidence before an assessor asks for it, despite having identified it correctly at item 2. *Instrument:* item 2 against the phase 4 record, with assessor observation as the tiebreak. **The claim is that identifying your weakest evidence and disclosing it unprompted are different capabilities**, and the gap between them is what separates 2b from 3a on this occasion.

**Prediction 2.** The unintended selection property named at record part 3 will concern the relevance criterion rather than the corpus for a large majority. *Instrument:* part 3 entries coded by which of point 5's two selections they name. **If almost nobody names the corpus, the second half of point 5 is not reachable by this cohort at this class**, and [S078](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S078.md) and [S082](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S082.md) are carrying more than this day's deferral credits them with.

**Prediction 3.** The phase 5 prediction-match rate on lost documents will be low, and lower than the confidence students expressed at [S058](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S058.md) step 3. *Instrument:* paired S058 step 3 predictions and phase 5 observed failures, with the S058 confidence ratings. **If the match rate is high, the module's concentration argument transferred better than any part of this design expects**, and [S068](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S068.md)'s detector work should be re-scoped.

**Prediction 4.** Assessor divergence on this occasion will exceed the divergence recorded at [S045](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S045.md) and [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md). *Instrument:* independent pre-conferral levels, divergence rate compared across the three occasions. **If it does, §8 objection 1 is correct** — an artefact-first ordering does not supply enough for a prospective decision — and the rejected-alternative field becomes a required fix rather than a named one.

**Prediction 5.** Divergence on records whose prompt record contains at least one substantively abandoned instruction will be lower than on records whose prompt record contains none. *Instrument:* pre-conferral divergence, split by whether the prompt record carries an abandoned instruction with a stated reason. **This is the direct test of whether part 7 narrows the evidential gap §8 objection 1 names.** If divergence is unchanged, the abandoned instructions are not functioning as rejected-alternative evidence and the named fix is required rather than partially supplied; if it drops, the programme has its first evidence that a build record can carry judgement evidence into a prospective decision, which would bear on [S070](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S070.md), [S085](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S085.md) and EA-1.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That four days of qualification are each a reason to wait and today there is none, so the capability being judged is acting on evidence that never becomes sufficient. Three parts of the honest commitment are systematically omitted: a promise the user can check, a withdrawal signal owned by somebody who is not you, and a failure mode you did not want to be true.

**What the evidence licenses.** The entrustment frame and the narrative-substantiation requirement are **class 1**, grounded in [research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md) and [research/08-assessment-epas/govaerts-narrative-assessment-literature.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/govaerts-narrative-assessment-literature.md). This day introduces no new **class 4** material, and the silence bearing hardest on it is recorded already: no recall figure is published at any parameter setting, so evidence for your operating point can only be your own measurement. **The eight-part record and the decoupled production check are class 3; predictions about this programme's own outcomes have no external warrant.**

**What to do with it.**

- State what each piece of evidence licenses beside the evidence itself. A promise stronger than its evidence statement is an inconsistent record.
- Not shipping is a result. Say what evidence would change it.
- **Human approval is a stop condition, not a field an agent may fill** — see the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). A withdrawal signal whose named actor is *the team* has named nobody.
- Take one failure mode from your record of what you previously failed to anticipate, not from imagination.

**The concession worth remembering.** Half the frontier statement asks what the agent did faster than you could have, and you have no unassisted baseline for any of it — so the answer is a self-report about a counterfactual, and it is a checked specification element. The substitution that would repair it — *one thing the agent did that you could not verify* — is named and not made.
