## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

An engineer works out that something cannot be done. What happens next is almost never a cover-up. It is a delay — until the analysis is tidier, until the next planning meeting, until there is something to offer instead, until somebody asks. **Every one of those postponements is reasonable on its own and the sequence is not**, because for the whole of it other people are planning, hiring and committing on a premise that is known to be false.

So the failure this day trains is not cowardice and it is not dishonesty. It is timing. That matters because timing is trainable: it has identifiable moments where a decision is made, and each of them can be named out loud. A character failure has none of that, and telling students to be braver would be the sort of instruction that changes nothing.

The day's second argument is about why an impossibility is the right thing to be honest about first. **An impossibility has a property no other engineering finding has: its certainty is not in question.** When you defer a finding about something that merely looks bad, you can always tell yourself you might be wrong. When you defer a finding about something that cannot be done, that excuse is not available — which strips out the epistemic difficulty and leaves only the social one. That separation is not available later, when every finding is contestable.

The third argument is the one the day would not have been able to make three years ago. The request goes to your tool before it goes to you, and **your tool does not decline.** It reads the definition, produces something correct and well-formed, explains itself, and lets the service do the refusing at execution time. That is not a criticism of the tool; a tool that refused everything it could not fully verify would be useless. It is a structural observation with a single consequence: **the loop contains exactly one party capable of holding a finding, and it is you.** Nobody has previously told you that you hold that job.

**What this paper concedes, and it is a real concession.** Point 4 states the duty in its strong form and never says when it does not apply — and there are cases where it does not, where an analysis is genuinely incomplete or where premature disclosure would trigger something irreversible. On the day after this design named unbounded claims as a habit of its own, it made an unbounded one. **The repair was nearly free at the last revision and was refused on purpose**, so that the failure which produced the rule stays visible. That is a decision the paper defends and it is also a real defect left standing. A second concession sits alongside it: the day now teaches a product limitation and a proved impossibility under one heading, and its assessment cannot tell which kind of finding a student is holding.

## Abstract

This paper defends assessing, for the first time in the programme, a student's ability to tell a person something they do not want to hear, and defends doing so on a day whose technical content is an impossibility the student can execute against. The first argument is that the disclosure failure this day targets is not a failure of courage but of timing — findings are almost never concealed and are routinely deferred through a sequence of individually reasonable postponements — and that a deferral failure is trainable in a way a character failure is not. The second argument is that an impossibility whose certainty is not in question is the ideal vehicle, because it isolates the communicative difficulty from the epistemic one that will complicate every later instance. The paper additionally defends the four pre-conclusion checks as the module's guard against a refusal reflex; defends testing the reply by reader comprehension rather than by argument; and defends the day's practical turn, which is that the students' primary instrument, put to the same impossible request, produces a confident and complete answer and does not say so. It concedes that the day's assessment of a communicative act rests on a partner's cold reading, which is a weak instrument; that its central professional claim is normative in a way the programme has not otherwise had to defend, and that the widening prompted by that concession is deliberately not applied here; and that the day now teaches a product limitation alongside a proved one and risks conflating them.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

An engineer determines that a request cannot be satisfied. The question is what happens next, and the observed answer is usually: not much, for a while. The finding is not concealed. It is deferred — until the analysis is more complete, until the next planning meeting, until there is an alternative to offer, until someone asks. Each deferral is defensible in isolation and the sequence is not, because during it people continue to plan, staff and commit on a premise that is known to be false.

This is the day's actual target and it is important to state it precisely, because the obvious framing is wrong. The failure is not dishonesty and it is not cowardice. It is a timing failure produced by a chain of reasonable-seeming postponements, and phase 6's disclosure is chosen specifically to expose the chain — the instructor's attention is on *what they told themselves at the time*, not on what they failed to do.

A timing failure is trainable. It has identifiable decision points, each of which can be made explicit, and the intervention is a norm about when a finding becomes owed — point 4's claim that you owe it at the moment you hold it, not at the moment it becomes undeniable.

The day's second problem is the module's own creation. Four days of hardness material produce a refusal reflex: students who have learned that some problems are provably intractable begin classifying merely difficult problems as impossible. Phase 3's requests 3 and 4 exist entirely to counter this, and point 2's four checks are the procedural guard. This is a case of the module having to protect against its own effect, and the design treats it as such.

The third problem is why this material sits on a complexity day rather than in a professional-skills course. An impossibility has a property no other engineering finding has: **its certainty is not in question**. When a student defers a finding about something that cannot be done, they cannot tell themselves they might be wrong. That isolates the communicative difficulty from the epistemic one, which is exactly what is needed for a first exposure, and it is not available at S050, S065 or S083, where the finding is always contestable.

**The fourth problem is the one the platform anchor exists to create, and it is the day's practical turn.** Until this revision, the day's impossibility was an argument the student constructed and then had to deliver. That is realistic and it omits the step that now precedes it in every real engineering week, which is that the request goes to an agent first.

An existing field in a search index cannot have its type or its filterable, sortable or facetable attributes changed — the documentation says the field has a physical representation and is not modifiable, not even in code, and that the change requires dropping and rebuilding the index. A reranking stage cannot promote a document that never entered the candidate set it reranks. Both are documented, both are stable, and both are the kind of request that arrives in a sentence.

Put either to GitHub Copilot in agent mode and it does not decline. It reads the index definition, modifies the field, assembles a correct and well-formed update, and explains what it did. The request is refused by the service, at execution, and not by the instrument. **So the duty to say so is now owed twice: once by the student to the requester, and once by the student to themselves about the output in front of them** — and the second is the one nobody has ever told them they hold.

This is not a criticism of the tool and the day is careful about that. An agent that refused every request it could not fully verify would be useless. The point is structural: **the loop contains exactly one party capable of holding a finding, and it is the student.** Phase 1's sealed prediction, phase 3's agent pass and point 8 are three ways of arriving at that sentence, and none of them requires the instructor to assert it.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Moderate technically, high socially.** The technical work is one day old — the reduction from S023, applied — and the four checks at point 2 are individually simple. What is demanding is step 4's distinction between what was asked and what is wanted, which requires holding the request, the requester's underlying purpose, and the space of alternatives simultaneously; and step 6's reply, which must be correct, complete, short and readable by a non-specialist at once.

Management:

- **The reduction is not new.** Step 3 reuses S023's technique with its template, and permits a cited documented limitation in place of a constructed argument where the impossibility is a product fact.
- **The four checks are enumerated and worked** on a sixth request before the independent one.
- **The reply's structure is supplied as headings,** so no capacity goes to organisation.
- **One page,** bounded.
- **Phase 3 supplies both the requests and their analyses,** so groups classify rather than analyse. **The agent pass is a fifteen-minute addition and adds no analytical load** — groups paste and record; they do not debug.
- **The step 1 pre-flight is a read-only script** against an index the cohort has used for three days, and the agent writes most of it.

The load the design does not manage is phase 7's revision, which frequently requires rewriting rather than editing. Thirty minutes is tight and the design accepts an incomplete second draft.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Writing an untutored reply at item 3* and comparing it against the tutored one at phase 8.
- *Requests 3 and 4,* which punish the refusal reflex, and which look exactly like request 1.
- *Identifying the skipped check,* phase 3's closing task.
- *The asked-versus-wanted distinction,* step 4.
- *Building the alternative rather than proposing it,* step 5.
- *The response to insistence,* step 7.
- *Reader comprehension as the reply's test,* phase 7, rather than argument.
- *The sealed Copilot prediction at item 5,* which asks a student to predict whether their own instrument will hold a finding.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Formal complexity class theory.* Point 1 states the minimum that licenses the vocabulary. Class relationships, completeness proofs and the hierarchy are omitted.
- *Constructing the reduction from scratch.* S023's work, reused.
- *Index internals, migration tooling and zero-downtime patterns.* Request 5's alias mechanism is named and demonstrated; it is not built.
- *Role-played confrontation.* Phase 7 is a comprehension test, not a negotiation. Negotiation is S039's and S085's material.
- *Withholding the reply form.* Point 5 states it.

**Enumeration scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md).** Point 2's four checks are a deliberate selection, not a complete list. They omit at least two further routes to tractability — input structure permitting parameterised algorithms, and hardness that is an artefact of a representation the engineer could change. Both are real and both were excluded because they require machinery the day does not have and because neither is among the errors students actually make at this stage. The seminar page states the omission; this paragraph records it as a decision.

**The platform instance, scoped in the strong form.** The day teaches two documented limitations as instances of *a request that cannot be satisfied*, and the correspondence must carry its conditions because it will otherwise be applied to the next service the student meets.

*Safe.* Reasoning about how to recognise an impossibility before promising anything; about why a downstream stage cannot repair an upstream omission; about why an alternative must serve the want rather than the words; and about why an instrument's confident output is not a finding. In all four the correspondence is sufficient and correct.

*Unsafe.* Treating either limitation as **a claim about search systems, about indexes in general, or about any other version of this one**. Both are product decisions of a particular service at a particular version; the permitted-change list is documentation that changes, and a candidate window is a number somebody set. **The unsafe condition here is, as it nearly always is, any version other than the one in front of you** — and a student who writes a reply on the strength of a remembered list rather than a read one has committed the day's own failure with the day's own material. Also unsafe: carrying either limitation into an argument as a *hardness* result. S023 point 10 established that a service's refusal is not a theorem, and point 1 of this day repeats it, because a student who tells a requester that something is *provably impossible* when it is *not currently implemented* has committed S023's overreach in a register where it will be checked.

The boundary is stated to students at point 10 rather than left to this paper, and **step 1's shipped pre-flight is the artefact element under which a student applying the simplification outside its safe range would fail** — a pre-flight that hard-codes the permitted-change list instead of reading the live definition is exactly the failure the unsafe condition names, and it is visible in the code.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **a reply the student writes twice**.

Item 3 asks for a draft before any instruction. It is collected, and at phase 8 it is returned alongside the student's step 6 reply, and the student writes the comparison themselves. That construction — the same person, the same request, six hours apart, both artefacts in hand — is the strongest available demonstration that the day taught something, and it requires no instructor claim.

The second engagement is the request's reality. The phase 5 request is a real unanswered one from the substrate, and the reply is addressed to a named person who exists. Students treat this differently from a constructed exercise, and the design relies on it.

**The third engagement is new and the design expects it to be the day's most-discussed fifteen minutes.** A room that has just classified five requests, two of them impossible, hands all five to the agent and watches it answer every one with equal confidence. There is no instructor claim to resist and no argument to have. The design's observation is that the correct inference — *the finding is mine to hold* — is uncomfortable in a way that an inference about a colleague's behaviour is not, because the instrument is the student's own and they have been trusting it since week one.

Phase 6's disclosure supplies the day's most uncomfortable and most useful moment. An instructor describing the reasoning by which they deferred a finding for weeks is recognisable to almost everyone in the room, which is what makes the norm at point 4 land as a discipline rather than as a moral instruction. **The agent case pairs with it deliberately**: an instrument that met an impossibility, did not say so, worked around it, reported success truthfully, and was merged — which is the instructor's own failure with no person in it.

The competence risk is specific: a student may conclude the day is about being blunt. Point 6's bad-reply list includes refusal-without-alternative as its first item precisely to forestall this, and the specification fails such a reply.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** The professional act pretested before the technical instruction. Item 3's collected draft is the day's principal instrument. Item 4's delay prediction is compared at phase 8 against the substrate's actual history, which is usually longer than students predict. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction** and is the day's second instrument: it asks whether the student's own tool will hold a finding, it is administered before the tool is run per instrumentation rule 8, and its expected result is a large and one-directional error.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** A one-day bridge asking which prohibition is most likely violated in a reply to a requester. The answer is usually the claim that no algorithm works, and naming it before step 6 is written is a targeted intervention.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Five requests, three classifications, two traps, all executable. Requests 3 and 4 are the module's self-correction and are stronger than their paper predecessors: **both are changes to an existing field, which is exactly what request 1 is, and the difference is a documented list nobody reads.** Request 5 concedes the impossibility and removes its cost, which previews S025 within the phase. The closing task — which check did you skip — converts a classification exercise into a procedural one, and its new third clause — which of the five did the agent flag — converts it into an observation about the room's own instrument.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points, equally split between technical and professional. Point 1 is deliberately minimal, carries the day's smallest technical footprint, **and now distinguishes a proved hardness result from a product limitation**, which S023's point 10 requires and which the day would otherwise conflate. Point 2 carries the enumeration-scope statement. **Point 4 does not carry the normative scope statement the widened rule requires, and the omission is deliberate** — see §8 objection 1. Point 4 names the three reasons the duty is hard, which is what distinguishes it from an exhortation. Point 7's question — when does a finding stop being yours to hold — is the escalation question and is answered informally here and properly at S069. **Point 8 is the day's practical turn** and is stated as an observation about normal tool behaviour rather than as a warning. Point 9 names three defaults and point 10 carries the strong-form scope statement.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps on a genuine unanswered request. Step 4 is the pivot. Step 6 is the assessed artefact. Step 7 anticipates S085. **Two steps are new and both are the platform anchor's contribution.** Step 1's shipped pre-flight is the artefact a working engineer should have built before promising anything, and it is also the intervention record's intended escape and the artefact element enforcing §3.2's scope boundary. **Step 5's requirement that the alternative be built and demonstrated rather than described** repairs a defect [WP-025](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-025.md) §9 predicted for the following day: a student who must run their alternative cannot offer one they cannot deliver, and S025's step 6 consistency check consequently tests a stronger claim.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** The deferral chain, with the self-justifications named, then the agent case. This is the design's most important error clinic in the module and it is now two cases of one shape. **The agent case is chosen because every statement in it is true**: the field was made filterable, in an index, and the report said so. The defect was which index the running system queried, which appears in no diff and in no test.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Cold reading and comprehension check. The partner is not asked whether the reply is good; they are asked what they now believe, what decision they think they face, and whether they would accept the alternative. Three factual questions about their own understanding, which is a far more reliable test of a communicative artefact than an evaluative judgement. **The partner may now run the step 5 build**, which moves question three from an imagined judgement to an observed one — and which does nothing for questions one and two, as §8 objection 2 records.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** The two drafts compared by the student. The delay distribution against reality. Item 5's sealed predictions against what the agent produced. The commitment names an undelivered finding the student currently holds, which is the day's only action item and is deliberately unverifiable.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with workplace experience will write a competent, diplomatic item 3 draft and may find the day's professional content obvious. Predicted failure: their reply is well-formed and hedged — it states the difficulty, offers to investigate further, and leaves the requester unsure whether to proceed, which is point 6's third bad-reply mode and is the characteristic professional-fluency failure. Countermeasure: phase 7's second question — what decision do you think you are being asked to make — catches exactly this, and a partner who cannot answer it has falsified a reply that reads well.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** A student with no workplace experience may write a technically accurate reply that is unreadable, or may be unable to imagine the requester at all. Countermeasures: the reply's headings are supplied; the requester is a real named person with a described role; and phase 7 supplies immediate reader feedback. Residual risk: step 4's asked-versus-wanted distinction requires inferring a purpose from a request, which is genuinely hard without experience, and the fallback is that the student may ask a staff member what the requester wanted — which is available and is a weaker version of the exercise.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Requests 1, 2 and 5 are usually classified correctly. Requests 3 and 4 are not. Fallback if a group classifies all five: ask them to construct a sixth request that would be permitted if one word were changed, which is a hard construction, is checkable against the live index, and demonstrates whether the checks are understood.

**Affective failure.** Two, and they pull in opposite directions.

The first is **the day read as licence to refuse**. Countermeasures: requests 3 and 4; the four checks; the specification's failing clause for refusal-without-alternative; and step 5's build requirement, which makes the alternative something the student has to produce rather than gesture at.

The second is **discomfort producing over-softening**. A student who finds the duty uncomfortable will write a reply that is technically complete and communicatively evasive, satisfying every specification element while failing the purpose. This is the harder failure because the artefact passes inspection. Phase 7's comprehension test is the only countermeasure and it depends on the partner reading genuinely cold, which the design cannot guarantee once students know what the test is looking for.

**The finding delegated to the tool.** New with the platform anchor and predicted to be common on first exposure. A student whose agent produced a confident schema update will treat the execution failure as a bug to be worked around rather than as a finding to be delivered — which is exactly what the phase 6 agent did. Countermeasure: step 1's pre-flight runs *before* any change is proposed, so the classification exists before the failure does, and item 5's sealed prediction has already asked the student to commit a view about the tool's behaviour.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by S025 (stated guarantees, one day later, where the alternative built at step 5 becomes a promise and where step 6's consistency check now tests a demonstrated alternative rather than a described one), S039 (requirements as negotiated constraint), S050 (saying what the measurement does not license — the same duty with a contestable finding), S065 (the cost argument you present to someone who pays), S069 (governing the release decision, which answers point 7's escalation question) and S085 (negotiating scope under constraint, which is step 7 at complexity class 6).

S050 at roughly two quarters is the significant one. S024's finding is certain and S050's is not, and the design's claim is that a student who has delivered a certain unwelcome finding has one fewer difficulty to manage when the finding becomes uncertain.

**S055 and S087 also retrieve this day**, and the design records what each takes. S055 takes the frame — recognising what a system cannot do and saying so — applied to claims about language models, where point 1's distinction between a proved impossibility and a product limitation is the load-bearing part. S087 takes the timing rule, applied to a safety case, where a finding held is a finding that has not entered the argument. **Neither takes the platform instance and the day says so**, which is what makes the re-anchoring the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](/archive/bsc-programme/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md) anticipates survivable at two years' distance.

Retained content: the four checks, the duty's timing rule, the reply form, the asked-versus-wanted distinction, and the observation that an instrument does not hold findings. Five items, no product facts.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement — the quarter's is at S030.

One specification item, jointly in CF103 and PE101's successor, **plus the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement**. The joint assessment is deliberate: the artefact is a piece of technical reasoning and a piece of professional communication, and assessing it in only one course would tell students which half mattered.

**A reply stating what cannot be done and offering no alternative fails**, unless the student demonstrates that no alternative exists — in which case the demonstration is the alternative. **An alternative that was never run fails**, which is a strengthening of the same clause and is new. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — the step 1 pre-flight being the accepted and intended form, per the escape [S052](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S052.md) established and [WP-048](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 corrected.

**The prompt record carries a specific requirement on this day**: the instruction under which the agent attempted the impossible change is kept, with its output. That is evidence about the instrument rather than about the student, it is the cohort's only systematic record of what the tool does with an impossible request, and it is the input to the frontier map this quarter assembles.

Programme instrumentation: **the item 3 and step 6 draft pair is the day's principal instrument** and the cohort's only within-day before-and-after on a communicative act; item 4's predicted delay against the substrate's actual delay is the cohort's estimate of institutional disclosure latency; the phase 7 comprehension mismatch rate is the communication baseline carried to S065 and S083; the phase 3 most-skipped check is tracked across cohorts. **Item 5's rate — how many predicted the agent would flag the impossibility, against how many found that it did — is this quarter's sharpest frontier datum**, its reader is the following year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) per instrumentation rule 9, and **a cohort whose prediction rate is already accurate triggers the removal of point 8's framing as instruction, since it would then be confirmation** — which is the branch instrumentation rule 4 requires.

The day's phase 3 evidence is a sample and the day says so, per instrumentation rule 3: five requests selected by staff from a substrate backlog, chosen because their classifications were known and because two of them look identical to a third. The agent pass is one agent, one configuration, one day.

Dispositions: **integrity**, in phase 8's undelivered-finding commitment, which is recorded and never followed up — deliberately, since following it up would make it a compliance item.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day's central claim is normative and the programme has no standing to assert it.**

At its strongest: point 4 states that an engineer owes a finding at the moment they hold it. That is a moral claim about professional obligation, and reasonable people disagree with it. There are settings in which withholding a preliminary finding is correct — where the analysis is genuinely incomplete, where premature disclosure would trigger irreversible action, where the finding is another party's to deliver. The day states a strong version of the duty, assesses against it, and offers no account of when the duty does not apply. A programme that spends its whole length teaching students to state the boundary of a claim has here made an unbounded one.

The rebuttal is that point 7 exists precisely to bound it — it asks who else needs to know and when a finding stops being yours to hold — and that step 2 permits "undetermined with a stated next step" as a legitimate conclusion, which is the incompleteness case the objection names.

The concession is that these are thin. Point 7 is a question raised and deferred to S069, and step 2's undetermined option is available but nothing in the day teaches when to use it. The day therefore does assert a strong duty and does not adequately bound it — which, on the day after S023 identified unbounded enumeration as a design habit, is the same failure in a normative rather than a taxonomic register.

**The concession stands and, at this revision, standing is a decision.** The [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s scope rule *was* widened to normative claims, and the widening cites this paragraph as one of the two findings that prompted it. The same page states that the widening applies from the following seminar day and that **the days listed are not retrospectively amended, so that the failure remains legible.** The platform revision reopened every phase 4 on this day and made adding two sentences to point 4 nearly free, and the design declined — not because the sentences would be wrong, but because a rule whose provenance is a recorded failure loses its provenance the moment the failure is tidied away. **A programme that repairs its own conceded defects whenever it happens to have a file open has a clean wiki and no record**, and the record is the more valuable artefact. What is added instead is one clause in the phase 4 warrant on the seminar page stating that the omission is deliberate and why, which is legible to a student and changes nothing about what is taught.

**Objection 2: the reply's assessment rests on a partner's cold reading, which will not stay cold.**

At its strongest: phase 7 works the first time a cohort meets it. By the second or third occasion, students know that partners will be asked what decision they think they face, and will write replies that make the decision conspicuous — which is the right behaviour, but it means the comprehension test is no longer measuring comprehension. It is measuring whether the student anticipated the test. And since the same protocol recurs at S065 and S083, the instrument degrades across the programme exactly where the design most wants to track improvement.

This objection the design does not fully answer. The degradation is real and predictable. Two partial responses exist. Writing a reply that makes the requester's decision conspicuous is the competence being taught, so a student who games the test by doing this has learned the thing — the instrument degrades while the intervention still works. And the mismatch rate is measured on *first* drafts, before revision, which is less susceptible than the post-revision version.

Neither addresses the tracking problem. If the design wants S024, S065 and S083 comprehension rates to be comparable, it needs a reader who is not a fellow student — a staff member, or a rotating external reader, or the actual requester. The last is available at S024 and S065 and is not used, which is the more interesting omission: the substrate's real requester could read the reply and answer the three questions, and the design has a real person in reach and chose a peer. There is no recorded reason for that choice, and it appears to be a convenience.

**The platform revision does not repair this and the design notes exactly what it does instead**, because the distinction matters. The partner may now run the step 5 build, which converts question three — would you accept the alternative — from an imagined judgement into an observed one. Questions one and two, which are the ones the day is actually about, are untouched. **A third reader has been added and it is a machine that can only answer the easiest of the three questions**, and instrumentation rule 1's finding — that an external observer was present and unused — is unchanged and is now in its second recorded offering.

**Objection 3: the day now teaches a product limitation as though it were the impossibility the module spent four days earning.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn.

At its strongest: S021 through S023 build toward hardness as a property of a *problem* — something no amount of engineering effort removes, established by argument, true independently of any implementation. S024's two impossibilities are of an entirely different kind. A field's attributes are immutable because a vendor built the index that way; a reranker sees fifty candidates because somebody chose fifty. **Both could change next year and neither has anything to do with computational hardness.** The day nonetheless treats them as the module's culminating instance, assesses a reduction-shaped argument alongside a cited documentation page, and asks students to deliver both under the same duty. A student who leaves believing that *cannot be done* is one category is a student who will tell a requester that a product limitation is a proved impossibility — which is S023's first prohibition, violated by the day that follows it, using the material the day supplied.

The objection is conceded, and it is the sharpest cost of the platform anchor on this day. The rebuttal available — that the professional duty is identical in both cases, which is true and is the day's actual subject — does not answer the categorical confusion; it explains why the design was willing to risk it.

**The cheap fix is two clauses and both are made.** Point 1 now states that the day's two impossibilities are not *no efficient algorithm is known* but *the operation does not exist*, and that conflating a proved hardness result with a product limitation is the first overreach this material invites. Point 3 restates S023's prohibitions in exactly that register: *this cannot be done* is a claim about an operation on a version of a service, and is not a claim that no system could do it. Step 3 permits a cited documented limitation in place of a constructed reduction and **requires the version it was read at**, which is the distinction made into an artefact element.

What remains unfixed is recorded. The day's *assessment* does not distinguish the two: a student who argues a genuine hardness result and a student who cites a permitted-change list are assessed against the same checked-element list, and nothing in the specification rewards knowing which kind of finding they hold. The obvious repair — a checked element requiring the student to name the finding's category and what would change it — is one line, costs nothing, and is **not made, because it would be a normative-scope repair in a different costume**, and this paper has just declined one of those on the same page for reasons it believes are good. That is an uncomfortable position and it is the honest one: the non-retroactivity boundary buys legibility at the price of leaving real defects in place, and this is the first day in the quarter where the price is visible.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Item 4's predicted deferral delays will be shorter than the substrate's recorded actual delay for the same request. *Instrument:* predicted distribution against the recorded history.

**Prediction 2.** A majority of item 3 untutored drafts will contain no alternative, and a large majority of step 6 replies will. *Instrument:* paired drafts coded for the presence of a substantive alternative.

**Prediction 3.** In phase 7, the most common comprehension mismatch will be on the second question — what decision the reader thinks they face — rather than on the first or third. *Instrument:* mismatch rates by question. **The platform revision makes the third question cheaper to answer correctly, so a drop in third-question mismatches with no movement on the second is the predicted signature and is the confirmation that the added reader reached only the easy question.**

**Prediction 4.** Students with workplace experience will produce item 3 drafts rated more diplomatic and less decisive than those without, and will show a larger phase 7 mismatch on the second question. *Instrument:* blind-rated drafts and mismatch rates, split by the admission background survey. **This is the professional-fluency failure stated as a prediction; a null result would mean the hedging pattern is not experience-linked.**

**Prediction 5.** A substantial minority of item 5 sealed predictions will state that the agent will flag one or both impossible requests, and the agent will flag neither. *Instrument:* item 5 responses against the recorded agent output. **This is the day's frontier datum and the design expects the error to be one-directional. A cohort that predicts correctly at a high rate has a frontier model the programme has not yet given them, in which case point 8 is confirmation rather than instruction and its framing changes** — which is the branch instrumentation rule 4 requires and instrumentation rule 6 requires to be homed, and its home is this day's own next offering.

**Prediction 6.** In at least a third of artefacts, the step 1 pre-flight will hard-code the permitted-change list rather than read the live index definition. *Instrument:* inspection of the shipped pre-flight. **This is §3.2's unsafe condition committed in the artefact and it is the day's own test of whether the strong-form scope statement at point 10 was heard. A low rate would mean the scope statement is doing work, which no day in this programme has yet demonstrated.**

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That the failure being trained is one of timing rather than of courage: findings are rarely concealed and are routinely deferred through a chain of individually reasonable postponements. An impossibility is the right first vehicle because its certainty is not in question, which isolates the difficulty of saying a thing from the difficulty of knowing it.

**What the evidence licenses.** The disclosure-timing failure and the obligation to deliver an unwelcome finding with an alternative sit in **class 1**, grounded in notes recorded here. The two limitations the day executes against sit in **class 4**, read on a stated date, and carry two silences the day depends on: no rate for how often a relevant document misses the first-stage candidate set, and a permitted-change list with no version history. **The duty's timing rule, the four checks and the claim that an agent's non-refusal shows where a finding lives are class 3.** You may say these two operations do not exist on this service at this version. You may not say no system could perform them.

**What to do with it.**

- **Read the live definition, never a remembered list.** A pre-flight that hard-codes the permitted changes commits this day's own failure using this day's own material.
- **Never refuse without an alternative, and build the alternative.** One you cannot deliver is not an alternative, and the specification now says so.
- **Name which kind of impossibility you are holding.** "No efficient algorithm is known" and "the operation does not exist" are different findings with different futures.
- **Human approval is a stop condition, not a field an agent may fill.** Under the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md), this loop contains exactly one party capable of holding a finding; your instrument will answer an impossible request confidently rather than decline it.

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes that point 4 asserts a strong duty — you owe a finding at the moment you hold it — and does not bound it, on the day after this design named unbounded claims as a habit of its own. **Hold the design to that.** The repair was nearly free at this revision and was declined deliberately, so that the failure which prompted the scope rule's widening stays legible.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** Phase 3's five requests and points 1, 2, 9 and 10 are taken from Microsoft Learn's Azure AI Search reindexing, index-update and semantic-ranking pages, read 2026-07-26. What the documentation states: **"Existing fields have a physical representation in the search index, so they aren't modifiable, not even in code"**; "revisions to a field name, data type, or specific index attributes (searchable, filterable, sortable, facetable) require a full rebuild"; a rebuild is get-definition, drop, post-revised, reload, and "deleting an index is irreversible"; the changes that require **no** rebuild are an index description, a new field, setting retrievability on an existing field, updating the query-time analyzer where an index-time analyzer exists, adding an analyzer definition, and adding, updating or deleting scoring profiles, synonym maps, semantic configurations and CORS settings; a new field leaves existing documents null until they are reindexed; an index alias allows a replacement index to be swapped in without changing application code; and for the reranking stage, **"only the top 50 results progress to semantic ranking"** and the stage "can't rerun the query over the entire corpus to find semantically relevant results" — it "reranks the existing result set." The reranked score is documented on a nought-to-four scale with a published rubric, and the result-count default of fifty is taken from the vector-query page read the same day.

**Two silences are recorded and one is taught.** The documentation states no rate, frequency or bound for **how often a relevant document fails to enter the first-stage candidate set** — which is the quantity request 2's requester actually needs, is the reason the request cannot be answered with a number, and is the silence the day's phase 3 is built on. And the permitted-change list is **published without a version history**: it differs between the current reindexing guidance and the legacy update-index reference read the same day, and nothing states when an entry was added or removed, so a reader cannot tell whether a remembered list is current. That is the silence point 9 names to students and it is the direct warrant for step 1's pre-flight reading the live definition rather than a documented list. Consistent with the folder-wide asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](/research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), the pages state sizes, limits and lists throughout and state no accuracy anywhere. The permitted-change list, the candidate window, the score scale and the result-count default are marked perishable at point 10, and one of them is expected to be wrong within the life of a cohort.
