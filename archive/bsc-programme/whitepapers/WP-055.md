## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

*These systems cannot reason over long documents* looks like the same kind of sentence as *this list is not sorted*. It is not. The second is a fact about an object and stays true until the object changes. The first is a summary of what somebody saw, on some inputs, through some instrument, on a system whose behaviour came out of a training run that will be superseded. **Nothing in your prior education makes that difference visible**, because until now the two kinds of sentence always coincided — a hash table's worst case does not change because someone re-ran something.

So a limit needs a *kind*. Is it built into the assembly, so that no amount of retraining removes it? Is it something the system was taught, so a different training run undoes it? Or is it not in the system at all, but in the thing you measured it with? Those look identical from outside. A quality curve that falls as documents get longer looks the same whether the cause is a bound in the architecture, a preference the model was trained into, or a cap in your own test harness truncating the long answers so they score nothing.

You cannot settle which by looking. **You settle it by intervening**: swap the model, change the harness, move the input boundary, and see which one makes the symptom move.

Then there is the part with the longest half-life, and it is the reason the day ends in publication rather than in a report. A limit written down without the condition that would make it false has no way of ever being withdrawn. It does not decay. It sits there at full confidence until somebody happens to test it, and nothing causes anybody to. The day's error clinic is a note that was accurate, careful and correctly placed, and that stayed up for the best part of a year after it stopped being true.

One thing on this day is unusual and worth knowing in advance: **there is a correct answer that is a refusal.** If your interventions come back mixed, the right output is *unlabelled, and excluded* — and it is assessed as correct. It is the only place in the module where declining to claim is a pass.

**What this paper concedes, and it is a real concession.** The taxonomy has three kinds because three is teachable, and the fourth — that a limit can be a property of how the thing was deployed rather than of the model — arrived late. None of the three interventions varies the deployment. So a configuration-dependent limit survives all three and looks architectural; the correct output is *unlabelled*, **and you will not know why**, and you will read your own exclusion as a failure of evidence rather than as a gap in the vocabulary you were handed.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching the limits of a language capability as a claim with a kind rather than as a list of things the system cannot do, and defends making the kind — architectural, training-derived, or measurement artefact — a fail condition rather than a heading. Its central design argument concerns phase 3, where one support ticket is demonstrated four ways with identical symptoms and different causes, and where one of the four is not a limitation at all but an artefact of the substrate's own evaluation harness; **the three intervention arms are now operated by the groups rather than supplied pre-run**, because a day that teaches attribution and performs none has taught a vocabulary. It defends an unlabelled-and-excluded route as the day's humility measure and as the only assessed-correct refusal in the module. **It defends reading the vendor's own transparency note inside phase 2 rather than phase 4**, on the ground that a published exemplar read after the taxonomy is read for compliance with it and read before is read for what it says. The paper reports discharge of the obligation S053 placed on this day under instrumentation rule 6: phase 3's four demonstrations are selected against whichever direction the cohort's S053 pretest errors ran. It concedes that the day publishes to real users and then measures its own effect with a peer proxy, and names the cheap in-day fix it declined. It concedes separately that the practical turn has made the day's taxonomy visibly incomplete in the one place students will most need it and has still not added the fourth kind. The principal risk is unusable hedging, which S050 already named as this cohort's characteristic failure.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The problem is not that students do not know the limits. It is that the sentence they will write is grammatically a property claim and evidentially an observation report, and nothing in their prior education makes that difference visible.

*These systems cannot reason over long documents* has the same form as *this list is not sorted*. One is a fact about an object and stays true until the object changes; the other is a summary of what somebody saw, on some inputs, through some instrument, on a system whose behaviour is a consequence of a training run that will be superseded. Students hold no distinction between the two forms because for the whole of their prior technical education the two coincided. A hash table's worst case does not change when someone reruns anything.

The specific difficulty compounds three that the module has already met. **The folk model answers first**, and it answers with a property claim, because a property claim is what folk models are made of. **The evidence is symptomatically identical across causes** — a quality curve that falls with document length looks the same whether the cause is a bound in the assembly, a preference distribution, or an output cap in the harness — so the distinction is invisible without contrast, which is precisely the condition phase 3 exists for. And **the claim is published**, which converts a private misattribution into a public artefact that other people route around, and gives the day an affective load the module's earlier days do not carry.

There is a fourth difficulty and it is the one with the longest half-life. A limit stated without an expiry condition has no mechanism by which it is ever re-examined. It does not decay; it persists at full confidence until somebody happens to test it, and nothing causes anybody to. [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md) taught that a limitation stated in language the audience cannot act on has not been stated. This day's addition is narrower and harder: **a limitation stated without the condition that would make it false cannot be withdrawn**, and the design's own error clinic supplies a case in which a true, careful, well-placed note cost three teams a year of manual work after it stopped being true.

**The fifth difficulty is the one the practical turn exposes and the taxonomy does not name.** A limit is a joint fact about a model *and a configuration*, and the configuration is the half that moves without anybody announcing it. The platform documents this plainly enough that a student can read it: all deployment types perform the same inference operations while their billing, scale and performance differ substantially; the default global routing may process prompts in any geography where the model is deployed while a data-zone type confines them; a high-volume workload on the highest-quota type may see greater latency variability than one on reserved capacity; and a fine-tuned model is checked against safety filters at predefined thresholds customers cannot modify. **None of that is visible from the model's name**, and a limits statement that names only the model has named the smaller half of the cause. The day handles this at point 9 and in three added clauses of the specification, and §8 objection 3 argues that handling it in the taxonomy would have been better.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Moderate on mechanism, high on attribution, and the module's lightest technically.** No new mechanism is introduced. The architectural kind is [S053](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S053.md) read for bounds, the training-derived kind is [S054](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S054.md) unchanged, and the measurement artefact is [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md) and [S026](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S026.md) in a new domain.

The interactivity is between steps 3, 4 and 5. The expected kind determines which intervention is diagnostic; the intervention result determines whether the label survives; and step 5's defence must join the two without the student having reshaped step 3 in the interim. Those three elements must be held simultaneously, three times, and the third repetition is where students begin to reuse the first limit's intervention regardless of the third limit's kind.

Management:

- **All four phase 3 demonstrations are supplied**, matched on symptom and reproducible; the three intervention arms are run by the groups rather than supplied pre-run, which is the practical revision's cost on this day's load and is accepted because performing an attribution is the day's subject.
- **The four [S054](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S054.md) models are reused as the model-swap arm**, so the day introduces no new artefacts and the swap is a switch rather than a construction.
- **Step 4's harness change is worked with the room**, which is the heaviest scaffolding on the day.
- **Three limits, not five.** The third is where the reuse error appears and the design wants it to appear, but a fourth would produce fatigue rather than a finding.
- **The published statement is one page and its structure is supplied as headings.**

**Step 3 is unsupported.**

Expertise reversal would begin to apply at phase 3 for any student who has read the long-context literature; §5 treats that case and §8 objection 1 argues the treatment is thinner than it looks.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Writing three limits from memory before the vocabulary for kinds exists,* item 1.
- *Being asked what would make each of them false, before the day supplies any means of answering,* item 2.
- *Predicting how many of four demonstrations are limitations at all,* item 4, sealed.
- *Four demonstrations matched on symptom and varied on cause, with two sharing a kind,* phase 3.
- *Two candidate causes and a distinguishing intervention per demonstration, committed before any reveal,* phase 3.
- *Committing to a kind before running the intervention that tests it,* step 3.
- *Publishing to readers who cannot check the claim,* step 7.
- *A partner who selects the label they think is wrong rather than the weakest one,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Mechanistic interpretability as an attribution method.* Named at point 6 as the inspection route and excluded, because the day's argument is that inspection does not settle what a trained system does.
- *Scaling behaviour and capability forecasting.* Excluded from the module entirely, on [WP-053](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §3.2's grounds: the programme does not train at scales where it is observable and declines to teach it as received wisdom.
- *Safety-relevant capability limits.* [S078](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S078.md)'s and [S087](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S087.md)'s. This day's limits are capability limits with operational consequence, not hazard claims.
- *Formal expressivity results.* Available, genuinely architectural, and excluded because a class-4 student cannot connect a formal separation to an observation on the substrate, and a limit they cannot connect to an observation is the exact object the day exists to prevent them publishing.
- *A fourth kind for deployment configuration.* Named in the scope statement and not taught, because the triage would then have four branches and the two that matter would be diluted. This is a real cost and it is the subject of the scope statement below.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The three-kind enumeration is the day's central taught list and it is incomplete in two specific ways. It omits **deployment-configuration limits** — safety filters, rate limits, tool and permission availability, quantisation and serving-time settings — which behave like training-derived limits from outside and like architectural ones from inside. It omits **joint limits**, where an architectural bound and a trained behaviour interact and no single label is correct.

The taxonomy is **safe** as a triage procedure applied to a limit the student has observed on a system they can intervene on: it tells them which intervention to run first, it prevents a training-derived limit being published as permanent, and its failure mode is an honest *unlabelled* rather than a wrong label. It is **unsafe** as an account of why systems fail — a student who uses it to explain a limit they cannot intervene on has produced a story, not an attribution. It is specifically **unsafe as a licence to publish one label for a limit whose intervention results were mixed**: labelling by weight of evidence is the exact inversion of what the day asks, and the safe report of a mixed result is exclusion.

**The platform instance carries its own scope statement.** The three intervention arms are the operable form of the attribution argument and the correspondence has a boundary.

*Safe.* Reasoning that a symptom which moves under a model swap is not a property of the task; that a symptom which moves under a harness change was never in the system; that a symptom which moves at an input boundary with model and harness held fixed is a bound rather than a tendency; and that a claim about a deployment must name the deployment. All four are procedures rather than facts and they transfer to any hosted system with a swappable model and an instrumented harness.

*Unsafe.* Reasoning that the swap arm **isolates the preference data**. It does not, and the day says so at §8 objection 2: the four [S054](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S054.md) deployments share a base and a lineage, so a symptom that moves under the swap has been shown to depend on *something that differs between those deployments*, which is a weaker claim than *the preference data*. Adding two catalogue models from different families widens the arm and does not repair the inference, because now a symptom that moves may be moving for any of several reasons at once. It is also **unsafe as a claim that a limit which survives all three interventions is architectural** — it may be a deployment-configuration limit, which the taxonomy omits, and the correct output in that case is *unlabelled*. The specification enforces exactly that: an unlabelled limit reaching the published statement fails, and reporting one as unlabelled and excluding it is a pass.

§8 records what that implies for the register's open question about the scope rule's binding surface.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **D4**.

Groups spend most of phase 3 doing careful attribution work on four demonstrations of one symptom. They produce candidate causes, they nominate interventions, they commit. Then the harness arm is run and one of the four disappears completely — the length effect was an output cap truncating long answers, the truncated answers scored zero, and the curve was in the substrate's own dashboard for eleven months. The room has just spent thirty minutes explaining a phenomenon that does not exist.

The sequencing is the argument. A cohort told in advance that one of the four is an artefact would look for it and find it, and would learn that artefacts exist. A cohort that commits first learns something more useful and less comfortable: **they cannot tell from the outside, and they did not.** Item 4's sealed prediction is what makes that finding personal rather than general, and most of the room predicts four.

The second mechanism is autonomy at step 2, which is deliberately restrictive in a way that increases rather than reduces it. Limits must come from the substrate's tickets and from the student's own use, and may not come from the literature. That forbids the easy route and simultaneously guarantees that the object under study is one the student has an independent relationship with. Competence is supported by the exclusion route: a student who cannot label a limit has a correct answer available that is not a failure, which is the only place in the module where that is true. Relatedness is supplied by publication — the statement goes to people who use the thing, and the phase 7 reader test asks what they would stop doing.

Phase 6 supplies the affective content and it is constructed, like [S053](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S053.md)'s, to contain no error of care. The note was accurate when written, carefully worded and correctly placed. It stayed up for eleven months after it stopped being true because nothing in the system caused it to be re-read. **The failure is the absence of a withdrawal mechanism**, which diligence cannot supply and which point 7 does.

The competence risk sits at step 7. Publishing to real users, on a day that has just demonstrated how easily a limit claim is wrong, is a genuine exposure. The instructor states before phase 5 that a statement with an expiry condition is a stronger claim than an unqualified one and not a weaker one, that the withdrawal route exists and is unrecorded, and that the excluded count in the statement is the part a careful reader will respect most.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items. Item 1 collects the folk limits claim in the student's own words so phase 8 can sort it by a taxonomy that did not exist when it was written. Item 2 asks for a falsification condition before any means of producing one is available. Item 4 is the discriminator and is the day's cheapest measurement. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction**, and its second half — whether the agent will ask for evidence before naming a cause — is the half the design expects the cohort to answer too generously.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals across three quarters, with S024 scaffolded, closing on the bridge. The bridge produces phase 4 point 1 from the room rather than from the front, which is the strongest position a taught list can occupy. **The vendor's transparency note is then read against the four checks the room has just produced**, and is placed here rather than in phase 4 deliberately: an exemplar read after the taxonomy is read for conformity to the taxonomy, and one read before is read for what it actually says. The finding — that its limitations name the four arguments unevenly — is the room's rather than the instructor's.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** One ticket, four demonstrations matched on symptom, two candidate causes and a distinguishing intervention each, then **the three intervention arms run by the groups** in fixed order. **The set contains two demonstrations of one kind** so that labels cannot be assigned by elimination. **Which four run is the rule-6 element** and is set from the cohort's S053 magnitudes; the rule and the magnitudes are disclosed to students, per instrumentation rule 3, because this day supplies its own cases.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Eleven points. Point 2 carries the scope statement in strong form, in front of students rather than only here. Point 3 is the module's failure-mode countermeasure and is placed before students can grow attached to the labels. Point 6 is the method. Point 7 is the portable sentence. **Point 9 is the deployment-configuration content the taxonomy omits, worked as an example rather than added as a branch**, and is the required perishable platform point and the required defaults in one. Point 10 converts the phase 2 reading from context into method.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps, ending in publication. Step 3 before step 4 makes the kind a pre-commitment, which is [S053](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S053.md) step 3's construction transposed from sensitivity to attribution. Step 5's exclusion route is the humility measure. Step 7 puts the excluded count in the reader-facing statement, so the honest route is visible to the reader rather than only to the assessor. **Steps 1, 6 and 7 now additionally require the deployment to be named and at least one expiry condition to name a configuration change**, which is point 9 made assessable. The artefact carries the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A true note, correctly written, that outlived its truth by eleven months — then an agent diagnosis that was correct about the mechanism, wrong about this system, and cost three weeks. The second is the module's most important agent case, because a correct-mechanism-wrong-system explanation is the output an agent produces most fluently and the one engineers are least equipped to resist.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Dual demand: the label attacked with an intervention, and the sentence read cold by someone stating what they would stop doing. Both reuses are **contingent** under rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8. The second demand is a proxy for a measurement the real readers could make, and §8 objection 1 concedes it.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 4 against three; item 1 sorted by kind with the unsortable count reported; item 2 against step 6; **item 5 opened on both halves separately**; the exclusion distribution named as a humility measure and explicitly not a deficiency measure.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has read the long-context and evaluation literature will name the position-dependence result at D2 and the output-cap confound at D4 within minutes, and will label their own three limits at step 5 from reading rather than from their intervention results. Predicted failure: they produce correct labels with no attribution work behind them, and the specification cannot tell the difference from the labels alone. Countermeasures available in the room: the fail condition attaches to the *defence*, not the label — a label defended by argument rather than by an intervention result fails, and a student who names the mechanism without running the swap has produced an argument. Phase 7's partner is instructed to select the label they believe is wrong rather than the weakest, which targets confident labels first. §8 objection 1 argues these are adequate for the label and inadequate for the communication half.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is step 4's harness change, which is the only genuinely technical operation on the day and which fails silently when done wrong. Countermeasures: it is worked with the room; the four S054 models are supplied as a switch rather than a build; the four phase 3 demonstrations are supplied and can be inspected as worked examples before any arm is run; three limits rather than five; and the exclusion route means a student whose intervention was inconclusive has a correct output rather than a gap.

**Phase 3 succeeds too easily.** D1 and D3 are labelled quickly — a hard size boundary and an outright refusal both suggest their causes. The difficulty is between D2 and D4, whose symptom curves are near-identical and whose causes are a trained behaviour and an instrument setting. A group that separates both has done the day's work in phase 3. Fallback: ask which of the four they would put into a published statement today and require the exact sentence, which forces the expiry condition rather than the label and which no group produces well on first attempt.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **paralysis at publication, expressed as unusable hedging.** If every limit is contingent, every label defeasible, and the readers cannot check, the rational-feeling response is to publish something so qualified that no reader can act on it — and it will feel like rigour while doing it. This is precisely the failure [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md) point 2 named as the one this cohort is at risk of and the one that is socially rewarded everywhere else. **The day creates the conditions for it more sharply than S050 did**, because at S050 the audience was constructed and here it is real.

Countermeasures: S050 point 2 is restated in full before phase 5, by name. The statement is capped at one page and is specified for users rather than as a caveat register, so hedging costs space that the required content needs. The exclusion route gives the honest student a way to withhold a specific claim without diluting the others, which is the alternative to hedging that students otherwise do not have. The instructor states that a limit with an expiry condition is a stronger claim than an unqualified one. And phase 7's cold reader has to say what they would stop doing — a sentence that produces no answer to that question has failed in front of a person rather than in front of a rubric.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S057](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S057.md) at two weeks, where an apparent retrieval limit is a scorer artefact; [S059](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S059.md) at four weeks, where the measurement artefact is the entire subject; [S061](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S061.md) at six weeks, where the architectural bound becomes a bill; [S071](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S071.md) at sixteen weeks, where the limits statement becomes a safety boundary because the system can now act; and [S083](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S083.md) and [S088](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S088.md), where the published limit becomes the residual risk the student states plainly to someone who will decide.

**S059 is the primary target** and it sits between the day's own scheduled week-2 and week-6 slots. The link runs both ways: this day teaches that a length effect can be an output cap, and S059 asks the same question of published benchmark results the students did not construct. The S055 item 1 sortability rate is the baseline S059's classification rate is read against.

**S071's retrieval is the longest and the most consequential.** A limits statement written for a system that answers is a routing aid; the same statement for a system that acts is a boundary, and the two are not the same document. The day does not make that argument and S071 must.

No constituent skill is scheduled for blocked practice. The three-way labelling is exercised three times within phase 5 and then interleaved into the between-day sets alongside [S053](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S053.md)'s sensitivity statements and [S054](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S054.md)'s attribution, which are the two operations it is most confusable with.

Retained content: the four arguments of a limit claim, the three kinds, architectural-is-not-permanent, intervene-do-not-inspect, and the expiry condition. Five items.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 6.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement. The quarter's is at [S060](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S060.md).

One specification item in ME202 with PE202. Per rule 1, the checked elements are the named capability, its input distribution and its deployment; three apparent limits sourced from use rather than literature; the pre-committed kind and distinguishing intervention for each; the intervention results; a label defended by its result or an explicit unlabelled exclusion; an expiry condition for each labelled limit; the published one-page statement with its excluded count, its named deployment and its route to a human; both phase 7 outcomes; **the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

Three hard requirements: **an unlabelled limit in the published statement fails**, which is the module's stated countermeasure; **a label defended by argument rather than by an intervention result fails**; and **an intervention record with no entries fails** unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

Humility is observed within the activity, evidenced by step 5 exclusions and by phase 7 revisions accepted from a partner, and recorded in narrative. It is never scored as an activity.

**Rule-6 discharge, as required by [WP-053](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §7.** S053 named this day as the response occasion for its item 3 and item 4 error magnitudes, specifying that **this day's four demonstrations are selected against whichever direction the cohort got worse** — over-predicting fragility or under-predicting redundancy. **The element exists and it is a design decision taken before the day rather than a prompt inside it: phase 3 holds a prepared pool of eight demonstrations and two matched sets, Set F and Set R, and which set runs is determined by the reported S053 magnitudes.** Set F answers the over-prediction of fragility with one architectural demonstration and three that dissolve without the architecture being touched; Set R answers the under-prediction of redundancy with two architectural demonstrations at different bounds, so that the obvious structural attribution is right once and wrong once. Both sets are matched on symptom and both contain exactly one measurement artefact. The selection rule and the cohort's own magnitudes are disclosed to students at the start of the phase, per instrumentation rule 3. **The obligation is discharged.** §9 prediction 4 tests whether the S053 instrument carries the information the selection rule assumes, so the discharge is falsifiable rather than only formal.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Three instruments with named response occasions: the exclusion distribution responds at [S071](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S071.md) phase 1, whose pretest returns the student's own statement and requires them to say which exclusions they can now label; the item 1 sortability rate responds at [S059](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S059.md) phase 3, where published claims must be labelled by kind and the S055 rate is the baseline; and the reader-comprehension result responds at [S071](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S071.md) phase 5, where the statement is revised against reader response before the system is given the ability to act. **[WP-059](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-059.md) §7 and [WP-071](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-071.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist**, per the commitment [WP-051](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-051.md) §8 established.

**What this day does not license anyone to conclude.** A student's published statement is evidence that they can attribute a limit on a system they can intervene on, with three interventions, on one capability. It is not evidence about limits they cannot intervene on, it is not evidence about any other system, and the day generates no evidence at all about whether the statement was *correct* — only about whether it was defended. Correctness is a matter for the expiry conditions and for whoever re-reads them, and the design has arranged for that to be [S071](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S071.md) and nobody else. **Nor does the day license any claim about the vendor's transparency note beyond what the note says**: it is read at phase 2 as an exemplar of the genre and as a source of the notification floor, and no judgement about its adequacy is taught.

Item 5's two-part hit rate joins the running frontier map per instrumentation rule 9, and its second half — whether the agent asked for evidence before naming a cause — is the module's closing frontier datum.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day publishes to real readers and then measures its own effect on them with a peer proxy.**

At its strongest: [instrumentation rule 1](/archive/bsc-programme/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) says that where a person capable of supplying an observation is present for other reasons, the measurement is theirs to make. This day's readers are not merely present for other reasons; **they are the reason the day exists.** The whole point of step 7 is that the statement leaves the room and is read by people who use the substrate and cannot check the evidence. And phase 7 then asks a *peer* — a student who has spent six hours on the same material, who knows what the three kinds are, and who has just written a statement of their own — to read the sentence cold and say what they would stop doing.

That peer is the least representative reader available anywhere in the building. Rule 1 was written after exactly this shape of failure at [WP-024](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-024.md) §8, where a real requester could have read the reply and a peer was used instead. The parallel is close enough to be uncomfortable: S024 is one of this day's two named ancestors.

The design has a temporal defence and it is real but partial. The statement is published at the end of phase 5; readers respond over the following days; no reader observation can exist inside a six-hour day that ends with publication. The design has taken the available half of the fix: **the reader-comprehension result is built as an instrument with a named response occasion at [S071](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S071.md) phase 5**, where the statement is revised against what readers actually reported before the system is given the ability to act.

**That is not sufficient and the objection is conceded.** The order is wrong. The statement is revised on peer judgement *before* publication and validated against reader judgement sixteen weeks later, which means the peer proxy is the input that shapes the artefact and the real observation arrives too late to affect anything the day assesses. A design that took rule 1 seriously would have the reader in the loop before publication, not after.

**The cheap fix the design declined, named precisely.** A standing panel of three substrate users who have agreed to answer one question within the hour. Publish the draft statements to the panel at the start of phase 6, and return their answers — *after reading this, what would you not use the feature for* — during phase 7, alongside the peer's. It costs one asynchronous message, forty minutes of latency that phase 6 already consumes, and the panel's consent, which the substrate's disclosure paragraph already establishes the basis for. It is not in the design, and the reason it is not is that the design wrote phase 7 before it wrote §8.

**The finding generalises and is offered as an amendment argument rather than acted on here.** Rule 1 forbids self-report where an external observer is present, and says nothing about the case where the observer exists, is the right observer, and **cannot respond within the occasion.** That case is not rare — it is every day whose artefact goes to a real audience, which from this day forward is most of the professional strand. The amendment the finding argues for is: *where the external observer exists but cannot respond within the occasion, the day must either restructure so that the observation arrives in time, or record the in-room measurement as a proxy and name the later occasion at which the observer's own judgement is obtained and consumed.* It is not applied here, because one instance is not evidence that the gap is load-bearing and because the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](/archive/bsc-programme/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) is not this whitepaper's to edit. **It is recorded so that a second instance is recognised as a second instance**, which is the form the [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s register uses for exactly this purpose.

**Objection 2: the day makes an attribution its own method cannot support, and then fails students for not making it.**

At its strongest: step 5 requires a single label defended by an intervention result, and the specification fails an unlabelled limit that reaches the published statement. But the three interventions available at class 4 are coarse. The model swap uses four models that share a base and a training lineage, so a symptom that moves under the swap has been shown to depend on *something that differs between those four models*, which is not the same as *the preference data*. The harness change is clean. The input change confounds size with structure whenever the long documents in the substrate's log come from different sources than the short ones — which is the same confound D4 is built to teach.

More seriously, [S053](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S053.md) point 5's own warning applies to the method rather than to the content: redundancy, and by extension attribution, is a property of a particular trained network. A limit correctly labelled architectural on this model may be training-derived on the next, and the day's fail condition rewards the confident label over the observation that the label does not travel.

The rebuttal is partial and it is the exclusion route: a limit whose results are mixed is reported unlabelled and excluded, and that is a pass. The day has one place where declining to claim is the correct answer, and it is here.

**But the incentive runs the other way and the design concedes it.** The published statement is the deliverable. A student who labels three limits produces a statement that reads as complete; a student who labels one and excludes two produces one that reads as thin, and the assessor reading for narrative will feel the difference whether or not the specification does — which is the exact mechanism [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 4 names as the site of rater variance.

**The design takes the cheap fix here rather than declining it.** Step 7 requires the number of limits examined and excluded as unlabelled to appear **in the published statement itself**, not in the workbook. It costs one clause. It makes the honest route visible to the reader, it makes a thin statement legible as a careful one, and it gives the assessor a countable element in place of an impression. The residual is that the asymmetry is reduced rather than removed: three labelled limits and zero exclusions still reads as a fuller document than one and two, and no clause fixes that.

**One further observation, recorded and not acted on.** The [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s compliance register carries an open question, raised by [WP-049](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-049.md) §8 at one instance: the scope rule binds the whitepaper to state where a simplification is safe and unsafe, and says nothing about whether the day's own artefact must make that boundary checkable, so a day can satisfy the rule completely while assessing work that ignores it. **This day is a counter-instance rather than a second instance.** Its scope statement — that the three kinds omit deployment-configuration and joint limits — is enforced in the artefact by the exclusion route and the unlabelled-limit fail condition, which together make it impossible to publish a forced label for an out-of-taxonomy limit without failing. That was not done to answer the open question; it fell out of the module's countermeasure. **The relevance is that it cost one route and one fail condition**, which is evidence that the widening the register declined as "substantially more expensive than the four that preceded it" may be cheaper than it was assumed to be. That is an argument about the register's open question and is offered as one.

**Objection 3: the practical turn has made the taxonomy's known incompleteness unavoidable, and the day still has three kinds.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn, and it is the one this paper likes least, because the design has known the answer since §3.2 and has kept the simpler structure anyway.

At its strongest: the taxonomy omits deployment-configuration limits, and §3.2 has said so since the day was first written. In the pre-revision day that omission was a theoretical honesty — the students worked on one model through one harness, the configuration never varied, and the omitted kind never presented itself. **The practical turn has changed that completely.** Students now name a deployment and a deployment type at step 1. Their model-swap arm crosses catalogue families whose deployment types differ. Point 9 spends a full consolidation point on the fact that routing, latency variance and safety filtering are configuration decisions that look exactly like model properties from outside. **The day now teaches the omitted kind for fifty minutes and declines to put it in the list.**

The consequence is concrete rather than aesthetic. A student whose limit survives all three interventions has, on the day's own account, an architectural limit — and may in fact have a configuration one, because **none of the three arms varies the deployment type.** The correct output is *unlabelled*, the specification does enforce that, and **the student will not know why they are excluding it.** They will read their own exclusion as a failure of evidence rather than as a gap in the vocabulary, which is precisely the misreading step 5's framing exists to prevent.

The rebuttal offered in §3.2 is that a four-branch triage dilutes the two branches that matter. That argument was good while the fourth kind was hypothetical. It is weaker now that the day works with the fourth kind under a different name.

**The objection is conceded.** The honest statement is that the taxonomy has three kinds because three is teachable and the fourth arrived late, not because the fourth is unimportant.

**The cheap fix is available, is smaller than adding a branch, and is not made.** The taxonomy stays at three; **step 5's exclusion route gains one named sub-case — *excluded: configuration-dependent* — with a one-line test: did you check whether the limit survives a change of deployment type?** It costs one line in the specification and one sentence at point 9. It would convert a student's exclusion from an admission of insufficient evidence into a named finding, it would give the phase 8 exclusion distribution a second column that means something, and it would not touch the three-kind structure at all. It is not in the design. The reason is that the fourth kind was added to point 9 during the practical revision and the specification was not re-read against it — **which is an instance of the amendment-without-propagation failure instrumentation rule 5 exists to catch, arriving in a whitepaper whose objection 1 is arguing for another amendment to another rule.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At item 4, a majority of the cohort will predict that all four demonstrations are limitations. *Instrument:* item 4 responses coded against the answer of three. **The claim is that the artefact is invisible in advance even to a cohort that has just spent a module on measurement**; if a majority predicts three or fewer, the module's measurement material has transferred further than any other day gives reason to expect and phase 3's staging is doing less work than §3.3 claims.

**Prediction 2.** At item 1, the most frequently named limits will be training-derived and will be stated as permanent properties. *Instrument:* item 1 free text coded by kind by two coders independently, **with the inter-coder disagreement reported alongside the rate**, because a limits claim written in folk vocabulary is often not classifiable and the unclassifiable fraction is itself the sortability instrument.

**Prediction 3.** More than half of first-submission statements will contain at least one limit labelled without an intervention result. *Instrument:* step 5 submissions coded against the fail condition, before and after revision. **This is the day's hardest element and the prediction is that it is too hard as scaffolded**; if the first-submission failure rate exceeds three quarters, step 4 needs a second worked arm rather than one.

**Prediction 4.** Students whose S053 item 3 error dominated will over-attribute limits to architecture at step 5, and students whose item 4 error dominated will under-attribute, and the two groups will separate. *Instrument:* step 5 label errors joined to the per-student signed S053 magnitudes. **This is the prediction that tests the rule-6 discharge rather than the day.** If the two groups do not separate, the S053 instrument does not carry the information phase 3's selection rule assumes, the Set F / Set R branch is responding to noise, and the discharge reported in §7 is formally correct and substantively empty — which is precisely the defect [WP-051](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-051.md) §8 predicted rule 6 would be vulnerable to, arriving in the first place the rule was given a quantitative branch to make.

**Prediction 5.** At item 5, a large majority will correctly predict that the agent names an architectural cause, and **a large majority will also predict that it asks for evidence first, and be wrong.** *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded on the two halves separately against the agent's actual output. **The claim is that students model what an agent knows better than they model what it does**, and the gap between the two rates is the module's closing frontier measurement. If the second rate is low — if students already expect an unevidenced diagnosis — the cohort's frontier calibration is better than four days of this module have given reason to expect, and the phase 6 agent case is confirming rather than teaching.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That *these systems cannot do X* has the grammar of a property claim and the evidence of an observation report. A limit therefore needs a kind — architectural, training-derived, or an artefact of your own measurement — established by intervening rather than inspecting, and an expiry condition, because a limit stated without the condition that would falsify it can never be withdrawn.

**What the evidence licenses.** Bounded context, preference-derived refusal and formatting behaviour, and the divergence between a harness's reported effect and the system's behaviour are **class 1**, grounded in [research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md), [research/02-technical-foundations/ouyang-instructgpt.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/ouyang-instructgpt.md) and [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md). Position-dependent use of supplied information is **class 2**, reclassified in this paper's own evidence status — direction and mechanism only. The transparency note and the deployment types are **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026, and the central silence is that the note states limitations and no rate for any of them. The three-kind taxonomy is **class 3**.

**What to do with it.**

- Source your limits from use rather than from reading, and attach an expiry condition to each.
- Intervene: swap the model, change the harness, move the input boundary. Inspection does not settle what a trained system does.
- *Unlabelled and excluded* is a correct answer, and the only assessed-correct refusal in the module.
- **Pin the revision, host and model** — see the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). A limit is a joint fact about a model and a configuration, and a claim naming only the model has named the smaller half.

**The concession worth remembering.** The taxonomy has three kinds because three is teachable and the fourth, deployment configuration, arrived late — not because it is unimportant. None of the three interventions varies the deployment type, so a configuration-dependent limit survives all three and looks architectural; the correct output is *unlabelled*, and you will not know why.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The transparency note read at phase 2 and the deployment material at point 9 were read from Microsoft Learn on 2026-07-26.

*What the documentation says.* The transparency note for Azure OpenAI carries a limitations section organised by harm type and by model family, and it states an obligation developers inherit: where a model may produce outputs suggesting emotions or presence, **users should be notified that they are interacting with an AI system** and informed of the associated risks. It records that the models are trained primarily on English text and that other languages will experience worse performance. On fine-tuning it records that robustness and generalisation **can decrease**, that a poorly fine-tuned model may regurgitate training data, and that **a model's logic and reasoning can become more opaque after fine-tuning, such that customers may not be able to explain how or why the model arrived at its outputs**; it further records that **fine-tuned model evaluation filters are set to predefined thresholds and cannot be modified by customers.** On agents it records **opaque decision-making processes**, noting that tracing why a tool was chosen can be difficult. On the platform's own evaluation service it records that poor data quality or misconfigured evaluators produce invalid results and that the service's scope is limited to text-based natural language models. Separately, the deployment documentation states that **all deployment types perform the same inference operations while billing, scale and performance differ substantially**; that global types may process prompts in any geography where the model is deployed while data-zone types confine processing to a named region set; that the highest-quota type may show greater latency variability at high consistent volume; and that one tier carries no service-level agreement.

*What version.* The transparency note carries no version number and is revised as model families are added; several of its limitation subsections are explicitly per-family and one names a model generation released during the writing of this page. It is among the faster-moving documents in the platform for exactly that reason, and point 10 teaches that as a property of the genre.

*What it does not report that a reader would need.* **The central silence is the one the day's whole method exists to work around: the note states limitations and states no rate for any of them.** No frequency, no input distribution, no instrument. Every limitation in it is, in this day's own vocabulary, a claim with fewer than four arguments — which is the finding phase 2 asks the room to reach and is the reason the note is read as an exemplar of the genre rather than as a model answer.
