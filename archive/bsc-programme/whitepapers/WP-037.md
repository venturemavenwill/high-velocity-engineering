## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

Ask somebody to describe their job and they will describe the job they believe they do. Not because they are hiding anything — because the parts you need are not in the story anyone tells about their work. The workaround they stopped noticing years ago. The step they abandoned. The two minutes every morning spent correcting something. The bit they are slightly embarrassed by. **All of it is absent from the account and all of it is what you came for.**

So you watch instead. And watching has a cost that scales with how often you interrupt: every question turns the person into an explainer and the work into a demonstration, and the automatic parts — the ones worth having — stop surfacing. You will predict how many times you will interrupt in a hundred minutes. You will predict something like fifteen or twenty. The target is under five, and the gap between those two numbers is the day's cleanest measurement of you.

The third idea is smaller and easier to get wrong. You write that the user *checked the value before submitting*. You did not see that. You saw a gaze and a pause and you inferred the rest, and the inference is probably right. It is still an inference, and it matters enormously later, when somebody builds a model on it. So the record has three columns and the third one is for what you worked out rather than what you saw. **An empty third column does not mean you made no inferences. It means you did not mark them.**

The day adds one more instrument and puts it deliberately out of reach: a machine record of the same session, deployed before the person arrives and read after they leave. It is not the correction to the human record. It is a fourth instrument with its own blind spots, and on this platform the vendor has written them down — what the availability figure excludes, how fine the sampling goes, what the response time omits. The interesting result is not disagreement. It is silence.

**What this paper concedes, and it is the largest concession in it.** The thing that most determines what an inquiry finds is not how well you behaved in the room. It is which session you watched, whose work it was, and which task they were doing — and that decision is invisible from inside the room, is made here by staff, and **is addressed nowhere in this programme.** The day spends a hundred minutes and its entire assessed apparatus on the smaller effect. As the paper puts it: the gap is not that it is hard, the gap is that nobody put it anywhere. Ask whose work you did not watch.

## Abstract

This paper defends spending a hundred minutes of a six-hour day on observation with a target intervention rate below five, and defends assessing the day on a three-column record separating what was said, what was done and what was inferred. The first argument is that self-report is a systematically biased instrument whose bias is not dishonesty — people describe the version of their work they believe they perform, omitting the automatic, the embarrassing and the abandoned — and that the omitted material is precisely what an engineer needs; the only access is observation, and observation is degraded by intervention in proportion to its frequency. The second argument is that inference is unavoidable in any observational record and is invisible unless separated, so a record without an inferred column is not a cleaner record but an unmarked mixture. The paper additionally defends asking the same question at the start and the end of the session, and defends the day's practical turn — that students deploy a telemetry policy before the session and reconcile its record against their own afterwards — on the ground that a machine record is a fourth instrument rather than a fourth truth, and that a vendor which documents its own instrument's blind spots supplies the cheapest available demonstration of that claim. It concedes that the day trains a practice whose most consequential bias it names and does not address, and that adding an instrument to a day about watching a person is a risk it has ordered around rather than removed.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Students have interviewed people. S015 put a caller in the room and had students derive an operation mix from what she said, and the day worked. Its method was extractive: a question in, an answer out, the answer treated as data.

That method is adequate when the informant knows the answer and the answer is stable. It fails for the question this module asks, because the thing an engineer needs to know — what actually happens when this person does their work — is not something the person can report. Not through unwillingness. A user describing their work describes the intended version: the workarounds they no longer notice, the steps they abandoned, the small accommodations they made years ago and the two minutes they spend every morning correcting something are all absent, because none of them is part of the story the person tells about their job.

Phase 3 establishes this before any inquiry occurs, using four records of one twenty-minute episode. The divergence between what the user said afterwards and what an observer recorded is specific and has three identifiable causes — omission of the automatic, omission of the embarrassing, reconstruction of the sequence — and once named, those three are the day's taxonomy.

The day's second problem is that observation degrades under intervention. Every question changes what the observer will subsequently see: the user becomes an explainer, the work becomes a demonstration, and the automatic parts — the ones worth having — surface less. Item 3 makes the intervention rate numeric before the session, students predict fifteen to twenty, and the target is under five. Point 6 states the trade honestly: interruption is permitted and it has a cost.

The third problem is inference. An observer records that the user checked the value before submitting, and has recorded an inference — what was seen was a gaze direction and a pause. The inference is probably right and is not an observation, and the difference matters when a downstream model is built on it. Point 5's three-column separation is the day's assessed discipline, and the specification's clause is deliberately counter-intuitive: **an empty inferred column fails**, because a record with no marked inference is a record where inference went unmarked rather than one where it was absent.

**The fourth problem arrived with the platform and it is the one the day is now best equipped to solve.** A student who has been taught that self-report is biased will reach for the machine record, and will treat it as the correction. It is not. It is another instrument with a construct it does not fully cover, and on this platform the vendor says so in writing: the metric named availability excludes the response code the user was actually receiving; the finest available sampling interval is one minute, so anything faster is not absent but invisible; the response-time metric is documented as an approximation that omits client-side latency, with a recommendation to use your own logging instead; and the token counts a team may be relying on are absent entirely for some models unless a parameter is set. **S026's apparatus applies to a dashboard exactly as it applies to a questionnaire, and this is the first day in the programme where a student can check that by reading a page rather than by being told.**

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Low cognitive, high behavioural.** Nothing on the day is difficult to understand. The three-column record is mechanically simple, the four rules are memorable, and the taxonomy has three entries.

What is demanding is sustaining a behaviour for a hundred minutes against a strong contrary impulse. Students want to help, to clarify, to demonstrate engagement, and to fill silences — all of which are steering. The day's real load is inhibitory.

**The instrument adds load in exactly the place the day can least afford it**, and the design's response is temporal rather than substantive: step 0 happens before the user arrives and step 6 happens after the user leaves, so that no part of the hundred minutes of observation is shared with any part of the telemetry work. That ordering is the single most important decision in this phase and it is not a convenience.

Other management:

- **Phase 4 is deliberately short at thirty-five minutes,** the shortest consolidation in the programme, so that phase 5 can run at a hundred.
- **Students observe in pairs and swap at the midpoint,** which halves the sustained-inhibition period and supplies a second record.
- **The three-column form is supplied,** so recording requires no decisions about structure.
- **The dimension budget is stated, not discovered.** Five custom dimensions, and the active-time-series cost of each, are given at point 9 rather than left to be found in an error message.
- **Step 5 is written in isolation,** immediately, before any discussion — and before the machine record is opened.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *A numeric intervention target,* item 3 and step 3, predicted and then measured twice.
- *Asking the same question at the start and the end,* steps 1 and 4, where the change is the evidence.
- *Writing three unmentioned observations in isolation,* step 5.
- *Separating inferred from observed,* point 5 and the specification.
- *Reconciling a human record against a machine one,* step 6, and naming what the machine could not have seen.
- *Predicting an instrument's blind spot before deploying it,* item 5.
- *Comparing two independent records of the same session,* phase 7.
- *Naming something you recorded as observed that was inferred,* the specification's final element.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Interview technique.* This is not an interview and the day does not teach one. Questioning technique is S039's and S083's material.
- *Video or audio recording.* Refused: it changes the session, requires consent machinery the day cannot carry, and would let students defer observation to review.
- *Affinity diagramming or any synthesis method.* S038's work.
- *Dashboard construction, alerting, or any monitoring practice.* The policy is deployed and queried; nothing is built on top of it. That is ME302's material and it would consume the day.
- *Selecting the session or the task.* Supplied by staff, which is the day's most consequential omission and is named at point 4's scope statement.

**Scope on the enumeration, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** Point 4's four steering behaviours are those visible inside the session. They omit **selection steering** — choosing which session to observe, which user, and which task — which shapes the finding more powerfully than any in-session behaviour and is invisible from within. **Safe** as a checklist for conducting an observation someone else has scoped. **Unsafe** as an account of how an inquiry's conclusions are determined, because the scoping decision usually dominates. In this day the scoping is done by staff, so students never make the decision that matters most, and **the programme does not address it anywhere** — S081's organisational material touches whose account gets heard and does not cover sampling of observation sessions. This is a gap, not a deferral.

**Scope on the platform instance, strong form.** The day teaches that a machine record is an instrument, using this gateway's telemetry as the case.

*Safe.* Reasoning about what a specific metric counts and excludes, before using it in an argument; treating a record's sampling interval as a floor beneath which behaviour is invisible rather than absent; and expecting a vendor to have documented both, and looking. Those three transfer to any telemetry a graduate will meet.

*Unsafe* in two directions. **It is unsafe as a claim that vendors document their instruments' limits.** This one does, in these places, and the day's most persuasive fact — that the availability metric's exclusion is published — is a property of this documentation rather than a general property of documentation. A student who expects the next platform to tell them and stops looking when it does not has learned the wrong half. **And it is unsafe as a hierarchy.** The day is not teaching that the human record beats the machine record or the reverse; it is teaching that both are instruments and that the reconciliation is where the information is. A student who leaves ranking them has replaced one unexamined trust with another. Step 6 is where a student applying this outside its range fails, and point 10 states the boundary to the room.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **a person's real work, watched**.

The engagement is not manufactured. A hundred minutes with someone doing their actual job, in their actual setting, is absorbing in a way no constructed exercise is, and students consistently report it as the most memorable day in the quarter. The design's contribution is mainly restraint: it stays out of the way, and the practical turn was designed under that constraint rather than in spite of it — which is why the instrument is deployed before and read after, and touches none of the session.

Step 4's repeated question supplies the day's sharpest single moment. The same question, asked at the start and at the end, frequently receives a different answer — because the user has spent ninety minutes attending to their own work with someone watching, and has noticed something. That change is evidence and it is produced by the observation rather than by the questioning.

The intervention count creates a mild and productive self-consciousness. Students who have predicted twenty and are trying for five monitor themselves, and the monitoring is the training. **Their partner is now also counting**, which makes the number external without making it adversarial, since each is counted by the person they are also counting.

Step 6 supplies the day's second surprise and it is a different kind. A student who has spent ninety minutes producing a careful human record opens the machine record expecting either corroboration or contradiction, and gets something stranger: a record that agrees about everything it can see and is silent about most of what mattered. **The silence is more instructive than a disagreement would have been**, and it is the reason the reconciliation asks specifically what the instrument could not have seen.

The competence risk is inverted from the module's usual shape: the day is easy to do adequately and hard to do well, and a student who intervenes fifteen times still produces a usable record. Phase 7's comparison is where quality differences surface, because a heavily-steered session produces a record that diverges from the partner's in a characteristic direction.

Phase 6's disclosure is chosen for the same reason as S036's: the steering moment is never experienced as steering. It feels like clarifying, and an instructor who can identify the exact question where their own inquiry turned is supplying something no rule can.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five items before the user arrives. Item 1's expected answer is recorded so that it can be compared, which makes the day's inquiry a test of a prediction rather than an open exploration. Item 3 is the behavioural target and its prediction-to-actual gap is the day's cleanest measure. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction**, administered here per instrumentation rule 8, and it is the only item on the day whose answer is checkable against a published document rather than against the room — which is the point: an instrument's blind spot can be looked up before it is discovered.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** The bridge asks which S027 error source self-report introduces. The answer is systematic bias, and framing it that way is what lets students discount self-report without treating the user as unreliable — a distinction the module needs and that students otherwise collapse.

**Phase 3 (40 min).** Four records of one episode. The A-versus-C divergence and its three causes are the phase's original content. **Record D is operated rather than supplied**: groups query the retained metrics for that episode themselves, choosing metric, aggregation and interval, which is what makes its limits a discovery instead of a handout. The three human records are read because they are records of a past episode and cannot be re-run; the design states which is which rather than presenting the whole set as executed. The closing questions — which single record would you keep, and where do C and D disagree — are deliberately hard and the second has a correct answer the first does not.

**Phase 4 (35 min).** Eleven points — ten when this paper was written, plus point 11 added 2026-07-30 and **handed over as a document rather than read in the room**, so the phase's deliberate brevity is preserved and no minute moves. Still the programme's shortest consolidation by density. Point 4 carries the enumeration scope statement including the unaddressed selection-steering gap. Point 7 — what to do when they ask you what they should do — is included because it happens in nearly every session and students have no answer prepared. **Point 8 imports S026's apparatus onto a metric**, which is the day's platform-specific reasoning, and **point 9 names three defaults**, all chosen because all three fail quietly: a namespace you did not pick, token counts that are absent unless a parameter is set, and counts documented as inaccurate when a stream is interrupted. Point 10 carries the platform scope statement in its strong form.

**Phase 5 (100 min).** The inquiry, with an instrument at either end. Step 0 deploys before the user arrives; steps 1 to 5 are the session and are untouched by the platform; step 6 reconciles after the user has left. **That ordering is the phase's most important procedural detail after step 5's isolation**, and both exist for the same reason: a record written in the presence of another record is not an independent record. Step 3's paired count is new and is discussed in §8.

**Phase 6 (35 min).** The steering moment, named precisely, then the agent case. The instructor's ability to identify the exact question that turned their inquiry is the first disclosure's value. The agent case is chosen because **nothing in it was done incorrectly**: the query was right, the metric was real, the chart matched both, and the answer was wrong because the metric's name was the requester's word and its definition was not the requester's meaning.

**Phase 7 (25 min).** Independent records compared, and now four intervention counts compared. Divergence between two people who watched the same ninety minutes is the day's most direct demonstration that observation is an instrument, which is S026's claim arriving in a setting where students would not have expected it.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Expected against actual answers, predicted against both recorded intervention counts, the step 4 answer-change rate, the column ratios, and **item 5 opened**. The empty-inferred-column discussion is where the specification's counter-intuitive clause is justified to the room.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with user-research or support experience will conduct a fluent session. Predicted failure: they interview. Their intervention count will be high, their record will be rich in said and thin in done, and they will produce a good account of what the user believes — which is exactly the artefact the day exists to displace. Countermeasure: the intervention count is now also their partner's, so a high count is externally recorded; their partner's independent record will contain observations theirs lacks; and phase 7 makes the difference visible without an instructor judgement.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** A student uncomfortable with silence will either over-intervene from anxiety or under-record from paralysis. Countermeasures: the paired structure means someone is always recording; the swap at the midpoint bounds the exposure; and the three-column form gives an anxious observer something to do with their hands. Residual risk: their inferred column may be empty because they recorded nothing they were unsure of, which fails the specification for the wrong reason. The instructor is briefed to check column ratios at the midpoint swap.

**Students who substitute the instrument for the observation.** New, and the practical turn's characteristic failure. A student who finds the telemetry more tractable than the person will attend to it, and the day's hundred minutes are the one thing it cannot afford to lose. Countermeasures: the instrument is deployed before the user arrives and read after they leave, so there is no moment at which attending to it is available; the specification does not assess the instrument's completeness; and step 6 asks what the instrument could **not** see, which is a question that cannot be answered from the instrument. Residual risk: a student may write a thin human record in the expectation that the machine record will cover for it, and the design's only detector is phase 7's comparison against a partner who was working under the same expectation.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Identifying that A and C diverge is straightforward. Identifying the three *causes* is not, and groups typically find two. **The C-versus-D question is harder than the design originally expected** and its most common wrong answer is to prefer D on the ground that it is mechanical, which is the exact error point 8 exists to correct and is therefore useful. Fallback if a group finds all three causes and reconciles C and D: ask which of the three would be reduced by the user knowing they were being observed, and which would be increased — a genuine and unresolved question about observation effects.

**Affective failure.** Two, and the second is unusual.

The first is **the user feeling evaluated**. A person doing their work while two students watch and write may become self-conscious, perform, or apologise for their methods. Countermeasures: the master-apprentice framing at point 2 is explained to the user as well as to the students; students are briefed to say what they are learning rather than what they are studying; the pair does not sit behind the user; and **the user is told that the gateway's own record of the session is being collected, what it contains, and who will read it** — which is a duty-of-care requirement under the [Substrate and Infrastructure](/archive/bsc-programme/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page's disclosure position, and is not optional.

The second is **the students' discomfort at extracting value from someone's time without giving anything back**. This is felt more often than the design initially expected and it is a healthy response. Point 7's guidance — what to do when they ask you what they should do — partly addresses it. The design's honest position is that the user is giving ninety minutes and receiving nothing on the day, that this is a genuine imbalance, and that the module's later output going back to them is a deferred and partial repayment.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement.

One specification item in PE201. Per the [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s rule 1, the checked elements are: presence of both answers to the S036 question; a three-column record with all three columns non-empty; **both intervention counts, self and partner**; three unmentioned observations; one identified misclassification of inference as observation; the deployed telemetry policy and its dimension choices; the step 6 reconciliation including what the instrument could not have seen; **and the prompt record, intervention record and frontier statement required by the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)**. **Nothing else is assessed** — not the record's length, not the quality of the observations, not the intervention count's value, and not the instrument's completeness.

**An empty inferred column fails.**

**An artefact whose instrument changed what the user experienced fails.** The module's build rule, which is what permits a day about not disturbing a person to end in a deployment.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the student's own design would have caught an intervention-worthy error. A dimension count asserted against the five-dimension limit, or a pre-session smoke test confirming that token counts are arriving, both satisfy it — and both are the checks that would have prevented a silently empty instrument, which is the failure point 9 names.

Programme instrumentation: **the predicted-to-actual intervention gap is the day's principal measure** and is the cohort's non-intervention baseline, re-probed at S083; the step 4 answer-change rate is the observation-effect measure; the phase 7 record-divergence rate is the observation-as-instrument measure carried to S046; the said/done/inferred ratios are compared across cohorts; **item 5's accuracy is the day's frontier-calibration datum** and is read by the following year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), per instrumentation rule 9.

Dispositions: **humility**, in the specification's inference element, where identifying one's own inference as having been recorded as fact is the day's most uncomfortable requirement.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day trains a practice whose dominant bias it names and does not address.**

At its strongest: §3.2 concedes that selection steering — which session, which user, which task — shapes an inquiry's conclusions more powerfully than any in-session behaviour, that it is invisible from within, and that the programme addresses it nowhere. Meanwhile the day spends a hundred minutes and its entire assessed apparatus on in-session discipline, which is the smaller effect. A student who leaves believing that a low intervention count makes an inquiry sound has been trained to attend to the wrong variable, and the scope statement makes the design's awareness of this explicit rather than excusing it.

The rebuttal is partial. In-session discipline is trainable in one day and selection reasoning is not; and a student who cannot observe without steering will produce a poor record regardless of how well the session was scoped, so the ordering is defensible even if the emphasis is not.

The concession is that teaching the tractable half is a reason for the day's content and not a reason for the programme's silence. Selection of observation sessions is a sampling problem, the students hold S027's and S028's apparatus, and a single phase somewhere in M08 or M17 asking *whose work did you not watch, and would it have looked different* would close it. **The gap is not that it is hard; the gap is that nobody put it anywhere.** This is recorded as a curriculum omission rather than as a day-level limitation, and it is the second such omission this design has found in M08 after [WP-036](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-036.md) §8's curation — both concerning who and what gets sampled. **The re-anchoring has made it worse rather than better in one specific respect**: the machine record is also scoped by staff, since staff choose which traffic the policy is applied to, so the day now has two instruments and one undisclosed sampling decision behind both.

**Objection 2: the intervention count is self-reported and the day's central measure therefore is not one.**

At its strongest, as previously written: the design names the predicted-to-actual intervention gap as the day's principal instrument. The actual count is recorded by the student who is doing the intervening, while concentrating on something else, under a stated target they know they are expected to meet. Every pressure on that number pushes it down, and the design has no independent record — video was refused, and the partner is recording the user rather than the observer.

**The repair this paper named is now made.** The partner counts, in a fourth column, and because the pair swaps at the midpoint each student is counted by the person they are also counting. Four numbers are produced and all four are recorded. That satisfies instrumentation rule 1, which forbids self-report where an external observer is already present and which cites this paper as one of its two founding instances; the rule's provenance on the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](/archive/bsc-programme/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) describes the day as it stood before the repair and is left standing.

What remains conceded is smaller and is new. **The partner's count is not independent of the partner's own performance**: a student who is intervening heavily is a student who is not attending closely to their partner's interventions, so the two counts are correlated in the direction that flatters both. The design has no fix for this that does not require a third person, and the honest statement is that four numbers from two people are better than two from two people and are not four independent observations.

**Objection 3: adding an instrument to a day about watching a person is the wrong day to make practical, and the practical turn buys less here than anywhere else in the module.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn and it is the one the design finds hardest.

At its strongest: this day's whole subject is that the interesting material is not in any record — it is in the automatic, the abandoned and the unremarked, and it is available only to a person paying attention. A machine record contains, by construction, exactly the subset of behaviour that a machine was positioned to see, which is the least interesting subset available. Introducing it therefore risks two things at once. It risks **attention**, because telemetry is tractable and people are not, and a student who finds the session uncomfortable now has somewhere legitimate to go. And it risks **the day's thesis**, because a student who reconciles a human record against a machine record and finds the machine record silent may conclude that the machine record is inadequate — which is true and is not the lesson. The lesson is that *both* are instruments, and the day's structure gives the human record the entire session and the machine record fifteen minutes, which is a weighting that argues for a hierarchy the day denies.

The rebuttal is that the alternative is worse and is what the day previously did. **Transcript D existed before the re-anchoring — it was the system's log, supplied on paper, uninterrogated.** A record students cannot query is a record they must take on trust, on a day about not taking records on trust, and the previous design had that inconsistency sitting inside its own phase 3. Operating the record is what converts it from a fourth handout into a fourth instrument, and the availability-metric finding is not available in any other form.

**The objection is conceded on the weighting and the fix is named and made.** The design cannot give the machine record more time without taking it from the session, and will not. What it can do is stop pretending the weighting is neutral, and point 5 now states the machine record's status explicitly — it is *done*, at the platform's resolution, for the subset the platform can see, and it is a fourth column rather than a fifth kind of truth. **The fix that is named and not made** is an artefact element requiring the student to state which of their three unmentioned observations the instrument could in principle have detected, which would put the hierarchy question inside the assessed record and would cost one line. It is not made because step 6 already carries a reconciliation and the design is not confident the phase can carry two demands, which is the same reasoning [WP-016](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-016.md) §8 used to decline a phase 7 addition and is no more satisfying here.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Predicted intervention counts at item 3 will substantially exceed five for a large majority, and recorded counts will be lower than predicted for a majority. *Instrument:* paired predicted and recorded counts, self and partner. **The self-versus-partner discrepancy is now measurable and its direction is the test of objection 2's residual concession: if partner counts systematically exceed self counts, the self-report bias the rule anticipated is real and the earlier design was measuring compliance rather than behaviour.**

**Prediction 2.** The step 4 closing answer will differ materially from the step 1 opening answer for at least a third of sessions. *Instrument:* paired answers, blind-rated for material difference.

**Prediction 3.** Students with user-research or support backgrounds will produce records with a higher said-to-done ratio than students without. *Instrument:* column ratios split by the admission background survey.

**Prediction 4.** Paired records will diverge on at least one of the three unmentioned observations for a majority of pairs. *Instrument:* phase 7 comparisons. **A very low divergence rate would suggest students are converging before writing, which step 5's isolation is designed to prevent and which would indicate the isolation is not being maintained.**

**Prediction 5.** A majority of item 5 predictions will name a technical failure of the instrument — a misconfiguration, a missing dimension, a query error — rather than a definitional limit of what it counts. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded for technical-versus-definitional, against the step 6 reconciliations. **This is the same shape as [WP-036](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-036.md) §9 prediction 5 and is the module's second frontier-calibration datum. If both predict a defect rather than an omission, the cohort's model of an agent and its model of an instrument share a single error, which is a more useful finding than either alone.**

**Prediction 6.** In phase 3's C-versus-D question, a majority of groups will prefer D on first attempt. *Instrument:* committed answers before phase 4. **A majority preferring C would indicate the room already treats machine records sceptically and point 8 is less necessary than the design assumes; a majority preferring D confirms the substitution risk objection 3 describes and makes point 5's explicit statement load-bearing rather than precautionary.**

**Prediction 7 — point 11's own, added 2026-07-30.** Among the interventions a partner records at phase 7, **solution endorsement** — inviting the informant to agree that a thing the student intends to build would help — will be the modal steering type, ahead of all four of point 4's named classes. *Instrument:* phase 7 partner-recorded interventions coded into five classes — leading question, suggestion-as-question, filled silence, premature agreement, and solution endorsement — with the fifth class added to the existing coding scheme; no new artefact element is required, since the paired intervention counts are already recorded. **The claim is that the failure point 11 finds as the *template* of a professional question bank is also the one this cohort produces unprompted**, which would explain why a bank written by experienced practitioners contains it in every block. If solution endorsement is rare here, the bank's failure is a property of its authors' commercial position rather than a general hazard of interviewing, and point 11 should say so instead of generalising.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That people cannot report their own work accurately, and not through dishonesty — the automatic, the abandoned and the embarrassing are not part of the story anyone tells about their job. The only access is watching, and watching degrades every time you interrupt. It also claims inference is unavoidable, so a record with no marked inference is an unmarked mixture rather than a clean one.

**What the evidence licenses.** Contextual inquiry, the master-apprentice stance and say-do divergence are **class 1**, grounded in [research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md](/research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md), with self-report treated as a biased instrument per [research/03-measurement-evaluation/](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/collected-materials.md). The telemetry is **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026: the availability metric counts only server errors of 500 and above, the finest time grain is one minute, and token counts are documented as inaccurate on an interrupted stream **with no magnitude or direction given**. **That a hundred minutes of non-intervention builds the competence is class 3.**

**What to do with it.**

- Ask the same question at the start and at the end. A change in the answer is evidence you produced by watching rather than by asking.
- Keep three columns and let the inferred one fill. An empty inferred column means your inferences went unmarked, not that you made none.
- **Provenance is not correctness**, per the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). A figure emitted by the platform's own meter is still not the quantity you meant; read what the metric excludes before you argue from it.
- Ask what the machine record could not have seen. That question cannot be answered from the machine record.

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes that the bias which dominates an inquiry is selection — which session, which user, which task — that it is invisible from inside the room, and that the programme addresses it nowhere while spending this day's entire assessed apparatus on the smaller effect. As the paper puts it, the gap is not that it is hard; the gap is that nobody put it anywhere. Ask whose work you did not watch.
