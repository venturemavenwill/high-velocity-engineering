## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

The memory hierarchy is normally taught as a table: this many levels, these sizes, these speeds. Students learn the table and it does nothing for them, because predicting how fast something runs needs you to reason about *which of your accesses land where*, and the table does not tell you that. **This day throws the table away and keeps two facts**: each level is smaller and faster than the one below it, and data moves between levels in fixed-size blocks rather than one item at a time.

Two facts is enough. From them you can work out why two loops doing identical arithmetic on identical data in a different order can differ enormously in speed — and that is the day's opening surprise, because the gap is not slightly bigger than you expect, it is a different order of thing. **The consequence is the point: how fast your program runs is a property of your access pattern**, which you chose, probably without noticing you were choosing.

The day has a second job that is about the programme rather than about hardware. Fourteen weeks ago you were given a model of a machine in which memory access costs the same everywhere. That model is now wrong in the most expensive way possible. **How a programme handles its own superseded teaching is itself a lesson** — call it a correction and students conclude that earlier material was unreliable; call it a simplification with a stated purchase and a stated loss, and students learn that choosing what to ignore is an engineering act. The day takes the second route, and commits in advance to doing the same thing to the two-property model when a later day supersedes it too.

**What this paper concedes, and it is the interesting one.** Partway through the day you will watch a real, paid, external service behave in a way that matches the two-property model exactly. It is extremely convincing. **The paper concedes that it is the day's least warranted moment.** A hardware block is a fixed width because a bus moves a fixed width — you can reason your way to it from physics. The service's block size is a commercial decision that could change in a release note. The structure matches; the causes have nothing to do with each other, and the danger is that having watched one service obey the model you will assume the next one does too.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching the memory hierarchy as exactly two properties rather than as a described architecture, and defends staging the day as the falsification of a model the programme itself supplied at S002. The first argument is that a hierarchy taught as a table of sizes and latencies is retained as trivia and used for nothing, whereas two properties — each level is smaller and faster, and data moves in fixed-size blocks — are sufficient to derive every result the day needs and are durable across hardware generations that will invalidate every number. The second argument is that a programme which teaches a simplification and later contradicts it without acknowledgement teaches students that models are replaced rather than superseded; making the falsification explicit, and locating it in the students' own retrieval of S002, converts a correction into an instance of the discipline the programme is trying to install. The paper also defends measuring every result twice, idle and under load, as the module's structural defence against benchmark reasoning, and defends running the day's contrasting cases against a metered Foundry deployment whose cache exhibits both properties in a form the vendor documents and bills for. It concedes that the two-property model cannot explain several effects students will measure, that the day's dual-measurement discipline demonstrates a problem it cannot yet analyse, and that the platform instance's block size has a commercial cause where the hardware one has a physical cause.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

M03 asserted four times that constant factors have physical causes and deferred every explanation. That was a deliberate debt and it has to be paid, because an unexplained constant factor is a fudge term: students who measure a large unexplained ratio conclude that measurement is necessary and reasoning is futile, which is the fatalism WP-012 identified as its principal affective risk.

The explanation is that memory access is not one operation with one cost. It is a family of operations whose costs differ by orders of magnitude, selected by where the data is, and where the data is depends on the access pattern. This means performance is a property of the pattern, which is under the programmer's control and almost never considered.

The instructional obstacle is that the hierarchy is easy to describe and hard to reason with. Students readily absorb that there are several cache levels of stated sizes with stated latencies, and this knowledge does not help them predict anything, because prediction requires reasoning about *which accesses hit which level*, which requires the block-transfer structure rather than the size table. Teaching the table and hoping the reasoning follows is the standard approach and it does not work.

The day's second problem is a programme-level one. S002 gave students a machine model with uniform-cost memory. That model was a simplification, it was chosen deliberately, and it is now wrong in the most consequential possible way. How the programme handles its own superseded model is a lesson in itself. Presented as a correction, it teaches that earlier material was provisional and possibly untrustworthy. Presented as a representation choice with a purchase and a loss — S003's pair applied to the programme's own teaching — it teaches that simplification is a design act, which is what the programme wants.

The third problem is the module's standing error, introduced here and enforced for five days. A measurement taken on an idle machine with a warm cache and a small working set is not a prediction about production, and students will otherwise learn to take exactly that measurement. Every measurement on every day of M04 is taken twice.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Very high — the module's joint peak with S019.** Reasoning about the hierarchy requires holding an access pattern, a layout, a block size and a capacity simultaneously, and the four interact multiplicatively: changing the layout changes which accesses share a block, which changes the effective capacity, which changes the pattern's hit behaviour. Nothing decomposes.

The design's principal load decision is **point 1's minimalism**. The hierarchy is presented as two properties, not as an architecture. Level counts, sizes, associativity, replacement policy, prefetching and write behaviour are all omitted from the reasoning model, and most are not mentioned. This is a substantial and deliberate simplification whose cost is discussed in §8.

Other management:

- **The same substrate component across all five module days,** so comprehension load is paid once for the module.
- **One dataset, one computation, four patterns** in phase 3 — the module's cleanest controlled contrast.
- **Step 1 worked with the room; step 3's template supplied.** Fading is retained around method and removed from choice.
- **All numbers marked perishable at point 7,** which removes a large quantity of memorisable and useless material from the retention burden explicitly rather than implicitly.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Quantitative ratio prediction,* item 1 and step 3, where students are wrong by a large factor and find out immediately.
- *Explaining case C,* phase 3's closing task, which cannot be done without the hierarchy and is therefore a proof of model possession.
- *Naming the punished pattern,* step 4. The day's discriminator.
- *Applying S003's question to the programme's own model,* phase 2's final item.
- *Dual measurement,* which produces two numbers where students expect one.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Cache architecture detail.* Associativity, replacement, prefetching, coherence — all omitted. Defended in §8.
- *Withholding the hierarchy.* Point 1 states it. It is a physical fact and generating it is not a task.
- *Cache-oblivious analysis.* Genuinely elegant, genuinely a different course.
- *Assembly or hardware counters.* The measurement apparatus reports time, not events. Event counters would move the difficulty into the tooling.
- *Assessment stakes on the same day.*

**The platform instance, scoped in the strong form.** The day teaches the prompt cache as an instance of the hierarchy, and the simplification must carry its safe and unsafe conditions because students will otherwise carry it into work this day does not supervise.

*Safe.* Reasoning about where to place stable content in a request, about why an identical-looking prompt costs differently on two occasions, about why a request issued after an idle gap is slower, and about why adding a small amount of shared content changes nothing. In every one of those the two properties are sufficient and correct.

*Unsafe.* Reasoning about **why the block size is what it is**, about whether it will hold, or about any other service by analogy. A hardware cache line has a physical cause and can be reasoned to; a 128-token hit increment is a product decision and can only be looked up. **The correspondence is a correspondence of structure and not of cause**, and a student who treats the vendor's numbers as evidence for the durable claim has mistaken an instance for a proof. It is also unsafe for reasoning about correctness: prompt caching is documented as having no effect on output beyond latency and cost, and a student who begins treating cache state as a determinant of behaviour has left the model's range.

The boundary is stated to students at point 8 rather than left to the whitepaper, per the widening recorded on the [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), and the specification's revisit condition at step 7 is where a student applying it outside that range would fail.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **a debt being paid**, which is an unusual motivational structure and is available only because the programme deliberately incurred the debt.

Students have measured unexplained constant factors on four separate days and have been told each time that there is a reason and it is coming. By S016 the deferral is conspicuous. Point 4's resolution of the movement term is therefore not new information arriving; it is an answer to a question the cohort has been carrying, which is a materially different reception.

Item 1 supplies the day's surprise. Two loops, same operations, same data, different order — and a ratio far larger than anyone predicts. The magnitude is what does the work: a factor of two would be interesting and a factor of thirty is a different kind of fact, because it means the students' model of computation is not slightly off but structurally wrong.

Phase 2's final item carries a subtler engagement. Asking students what the S002 model discarded, and what it bought, invites them to analyse their own instruction using the programme's own tool. Students consistently find this satisfying, and it establishes something the programme needs: that its own simplifications are subject to the same scrutiny as anything else.

Competence is protected by phase 3's structure — cases A, B and D are ranked correctly by most groups, and only case C defeats them.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Item 1's ratio prediction is quantitative and badly wrong, which is the day's opening. Item 3 requires mapping the two scenarios onto S012's terms before any instruction, and most students assign both to comparison cost, which is the misconception point 4 corrects. Item 4 is the calibration band. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction** and is the day's contribution to the running frontier map; it is administered here and not later, per instrumentation rule 8, because an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Fourteen-week retrieval of the S002 model, then the S003 question applied to it. The design's decision to have students state what the S002 model discarded *before* the day falsifies it means the falsification arrives as a completion rather than as a correction.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four request layouts, one dimension, dual-condition measurement, **executed against a real deployment rather than supplied as data**. Case C is the payload: it is neither best nor worst, it carries strictly more identical content than case A, and it caches exactly as much. No explanation of it is possible without the block-transfer property. A group that explains case C has the model; a group that does not, does not, and this is legible without a test. The loaded condition is produced by the room issuing the same prefix concurrently, which is documented to reduce cache matching above roughly fifteen requests per minute on one prefix — so the contention is real, attributable and, unusually for this module, caused by the students themselves.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Eight points. Point 1's two properties are the day's central design decision. Point 3 works the case C explanation completely, because it is the model's proof of sufficiency. Point 4 discharges the M03 debt. Point 6 handles the programme's own superseded model. **Point 7 names four defaults the platform chose**, which is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) requirement and is also the day's first encounter with a documentation silence — the prefix-routing hash length is given as typically 256 tokens and stated to vary by model without saying how. Point 8 marks perishability and, since the §3.2 widening, the boundary of the analogy.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps at class 2, **shipping a working embedding pipeline** rather than modifying a component. Step 4's punished pattern is the discriminator and step 7's revisit condition continues S012's expiry discipline. Step 5's dual measurement is the module's standing practice, introduced here. The batch-shape decision at step 2 carries the day's sharpest limit: a request may hold up to 2,048 inputs, each under the model's per-input token cap, and still fail because the sum across inputs exceeds an aggregate cap that no individual input violates. That is working-set-versus-allocation two days before S018 names it, and it is the most common way a correct-looking pipeline fails on real data.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A change that improved a benchmark and not production, then an agent failure. The instructor case targets the module's standing error rather than the day's content, because the standing error is the module's actual purpose. The agent case is a batching routine that respected the limit it cited and not the limit that applied, and it is chosen because **the review that would have caught it is not the review a student knows to perform**: the code was correct against a real documented constraint, and wrong against a different real documented constraint.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Find the punished pattern in the real system. Third instance of the realism demand, deliberately unframed.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Ratio predictions and accuracy bands, then the sealed Copilot predictions opened. The commitment names a layout chosen without considering access, which nearly every student has.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with systems or embedded experience may hold a detailed cache model and will find point 1 crude. Predicted failure: they reason with associativity and prefetching, produce a better prediction than the two-property model supports, and conclude the day's model is inadequate rather than deliberately minimal. Countermeasure: point 1 is stated as a *reasoning* model chosen for sufficiency, not as a description, and these students are asked at phase 3 to identify which of their extra mechanisms the case C result requires — usually none. Their phase 7 assignment is the hardest punished-pattern search.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** A student with no hardware model faces the module's highest interactivity with nothing to attach it to. Countermeasures: two properties rather than an architecture; the persistent component; step 1 worked; the prediction template. Residual risk: step 3's numeric predictions may be arbitrary, in which case step 6's reconciliation has nothing to correct. The template requires a *mechanism* per term, which is the mitigation and is imperfect.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Cases A, B and D are ranked correctly by most groups quickly. This is intended — case C requires a formed expectation to violate. Fallback if a group explains case C correctly: ask them to predict the block size from the measured cached-token counts, which is a genuine inference and is hard.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **disorientation** rather than defeat. A student who has just learned that their model of computation is wrong by an order of magnitude, on the day after the class transition, may lose confidence in everything they have measured. Countermeasures: point 6's framing of S002 as a deliberate representation choice rather than an error, which locates the problem in the model's scope rather than in the student's understanding; the fact that all four M03 days' *conclusions* survive the correction, since the rankings were measured rather than derived; and an explicit statement that the two-property model will also be superseded, at S061, and that this is how models work. The last point is the strongest and it is stated once.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by S017 (scheduling, where cache state becomes a cost of switching), S018 (paging, which is the same hierarchy one level down and is deliberately presented as such), S019 (contention, where cache and memory bandwidth are contended resources), S022 (amortisation and expected cost), S053 (the transformer assembled, where attention's cost is a memory-movement cost) and S061 (where the time and memory actually go, which supersedes the two-property model).

S018 at a two-day gap is the closest and is structurally important: presenting paging as the same two properties at a different scale is the day's strongest transfer test, and it is deliberately not flagged to students in advance.

S061 at roughly five quarters is where the model is superseded, and the design's commitment — stated at S016 — is that this will be announced as such.

Retained content: the two hierarchy properties, locality as a property of a pattern, and layout as a decision. Three items, no numbers.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 6.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence.

One specification item in SY102 requiring the characterised pattern, layout choice, three-term numeric predictions, punished pattern, dual measurements, reconciliation, revisit condition, **prompt record, intervention record and frontier statement**.

**A layout choice with no named punished pattern fails.** This is S015's rejected-alternatives discipline in a different setting and is the module's standing test for whether a class-2 decision was made or a preference expressed.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error. The escape clause is the sensitivity discipline [S052](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S052.md) established and [WP-048](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 corrected, and it is load-bearing here: a student whose pipeline asserts its own batch size against the aggregate limit has built the detection the record would otherwise have to evidence, and penalising them for not having intervened would penalise the better artefact.

Programme instrumentation: item 1's ratio predictions are the cohort's order-of-magnitude calibration baseline, which is a different measure from S012's factor-level one and is carried to S062 and S064; the phase 3 case C explanation rate is the model-possession measure; the idle-versus-loaded measurement gap is recorded per cohort and is the module's standing evidence for the benchmark critique; **item 5's sealed prediction accuracy is the programme's first frontier-calibration datum and is read by the following year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md)**, per instrumentation rule 9.

The frontier statements are a sample and the day says so, per instrumentation rule 3: one cohort, one substrate, one corpus, one model family, on a task chosen by staff to have a known failure available. A frontier map assembled from them describes this day's task and not the agent.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the two-property model cannot explain effects students will measure on the day.**

At its strongest: associativity conflicts produce dramatic slowdowns at particular strides that a size-and-block model predicts nothing about. Hardware prefetching makes strided access far better than the model implies, and its cutoff is invisible. Write-allocate behaviour, non-temporal stores and translation-lookaside coverage all produce effects at the scales the day measures. Students running phase 3 and step 5 will observe results the model does not explain, will attempt to explain them anyway, and will construct wrong mechanisms — which is worse than having no mechanism, because a wrong mechanism is confidently applied.

The rebuttal is that every teaching model has a boundary and the relevant question is whether this one's boundary is in the right place. The two properties are sufficient for the four phase 3 patterns, for the layout decisions at step 2, and for the reasoning the module needs at S018 and S019. The omitted mechanisms are mostly *modifiers* of effects the model predicts directionally, not sources of effects it misses entirely — prefetching makes strided better than predicted, it does not make it worse than random.

The concession is that the day does not tell students where the boundary is, and this is now the third instance of the same omission in this design, after WP-012's three-term decomposition and WP-014's five question types. WP-014 recorded it as a pattern; this is its confirmation. The fix is identical each time — one sentence naming what the model omits and why — and its absence three times in one quarter indicates a systematic tendency in the design to present tractable simplifications as complete. Point 7 marks the *numbers* as perishable while presenting the *model* as sufficient, which is precisely the asymmetry the objection identifies. This is recorded as a design-level finding rather than a day-level fix.

**Objection 2: the dual-measurement discipline demonstrates a problem the day cannot analyse and may teach only that measurement is unreliable.**

At its strongest: students measure everything twice, idle and loaded, and observe that the numbers differ — sometimes substantially and sometimes not, with no available account of when or why. They have no queueing model until S019, no contention model until S017, and no measurement-error framework until S027. So the practice produces a reliable observation with no explanatory apparatus attached, which is the precondition for exactly the fatalism WP-012 warned about: measurement is necessary, measurement is unstable, therefore nothing can be known.

This objection the design does not fully answer. The practice is introduced here because the module needs it established before S020's capacity argument, and the explanations arrive at S017 and S019 — one and three days later. That is a short deferral and it is a deferral, and during it students hold an observed instability they cannot account for.

Two mitigations exist and both are partial. The instructor states explicitly that the difference has a mechanism, that it is the module's subject, and that it will be explained within the week — which is a promise of the same kind the M03 deferrals made and which S016 has just demonstrated the programme keeps. And phase 3's dual measurements are constructed so that the *ranking* is stable across conditions even where the ratios are not, which means the day's conclusions do not depend on the unstable quantity.

Neither addresses the deeper point, which is that the design has now twice — at S011 and here — introduced measurement practice ahead of the instruction that justifies it, and has twice defended the ordering as forced. Two forced orderings in one quarter is a curriculum-sequencing problem rather than two local compromises, and the honest statement is that Q2's measurement content is running ahead of Q3's measurement instruction by design necessity, that the design knows this, and that the accumulated cost is not measured anywhere.

**Objection 3: the platform instance is a coincidence dressed as a confirmation, and the day's structure encourages students to read it as evidence.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn and it is the strongest of the three.

At its strongest: the day teaches two properties, then produces a vendor whose documented behaviour exhibits both, then treats that as the properties being confirmed at a second scale. It is not a confirmation. A hardware cache line is a fixed width because a bus transfers a fixed width, and the number can be reasoned to from the physics. A prompt cache's 1,024-token minimum and 128-token increment are product decisions with no derivable cause; they could be halved in a release note. **The day's most persuasive moment is therefore its least warranted one**, and the persuasion runs in the dangerous direction — a student who has watched a real service obey the model will trust the model further than the evidence supports, and will generalise it to the next service by analogy rather than by checking. The design has, in effect, built a demonstration whose rhetorical force exceeds its evidential force, which is precisely the failure mode the programme's measurement strand exists to teach students to detect.

The objection is conceded. The rebuttal available — that the day never claims a shared mechanism, only a shared reasoning structure — is true of the whitepaper and was not true of the day as first written, where point 3 worked the correspondence at length and nothing marked its limit.

**The cheap fix is one sentence and it is made.** Point 8 now states that the hardware block has a physical cause and the token block a commercial one, that the structure is what is assessed and the number is not, and that the correspondence is safe for deciding where to put stable content and unsafe for reasoning about why the boundary sits where it does or for extending to another service by analogy. That is the §3.2 strong-form scope statement delivered to students rather than to this paper, and step 7's revisit condition is where a student applying it outside its range would fail.

What remains unfixed is smaller and is recorded rather than repaired: the design has no way to tell whether a student who states the boundary correctly at step 7 has understood it or has copied it from point 8, because the boundary is now taught before it is required. That is instrumentation rule 8's problem in a new place — an element administered after its answer has been given measures retention — and the fix would be to elicit the boundary at phase 7 from the partner rather than from the student. It would cost nothing and is not made, because phase 7 already carries a demand and the design is not confident it can carry two.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A large majority of item 1 ratio predictions will be low by more than a factor of five, and prediction accuracy will not correlate with general programming experience. *Instrument:* item 1 predictions against measured ratios, split by the admission background survey.

**Prediction 2.** In phase 1 item 3, a majority will assign both scenarios to S012's comparison-cost term rather than to movement. *Instrument:* item 3 responses coded by term assignment. **This is the placeholder-term misconception; if students already assign to movement, point 4's resolution is less necessary than the design assumes.**

**Prediction 3.** Groups that explain case C correctly in phase 3 will produce step 4 punished-pattern statements rated substantially higher than groups that do not. *Instrument:* case C explanation outcome against blind-rated step 4 statements. This tests whether case C is the model-possession proxy the design claims.

**Prediction 4.** At S018, students will identify paging as the same two properties at a different scale without being told, at a rate above half. *Instrument:* an S018 phase 1 probe asking what paging resembles, coded for unprompted hierarchy identification.

**Prediction 5.** A large majority of item 5 sealed predictions will state that the agent will get a limit wrong, and a small minority will name the aggregate batch limit specifically. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded for generic-versus-specific failure identification, against what each student's agent actually produced. **The gap between the two rates is the day's frontier-calibration measure. If the specific rate is high, the cohort's frontier model is better than the design assumes and the phase 6 agent case is redundant; if the generic rate is also low, students are not yet predicting agent failure at all and the sealed item is measuring nothing.**

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That the memory hierarchy taught as a table of sizes and latencies is retained as trivia and used for nothing, and that two properties — each level is smaller and faster, and data moves in fixed-size blocks — are enough to derive everything the day needs. Performance is therefore a property of your access pattern, which you control and almost never consider. The S002 model is superseded, not corrected.

**What the evidence licenses.** The cost model, locality, layout decisions and the benchmark-versus-production failure are **class 1**, with notes cited. Contrasting cases, prediction before adjudication and expertise reversal are **class 2**, direction and mechanism only. The prompt-cache and embeddings behaviour is **class 4**, read at a stated date, and its silences are taught: one eviction boundary is a bound and the other a tendency, the routing-hash length varies by model without being quantified, and **the documentation states latencies, sizes and thresholds throughout and states no accuracy anywhere.** That two properties are sufficient is **class 3**.

**What to do with it.**

- **Name the punished pattern.** A layout choice with no named punished pattern fails, because it was a preference rather than a decision.
- **Measure twice, idle and loaded.** A number from an idle machine with a warm cache is not a prediction about production.
- **Read the platform correspondence as structure, never as cause.** A hardware block width has a physical cause you can reason to; a token increment is a product decision you can only look up.
- **Pin the revision, host and model.** The agent's batching routine was correct against a real documented constraint and wrong against a different one, which makes its output an observed result and not outcome evidence — see the five classes on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md).

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes that the day's most persuasive moment is its least warranted one: a real service appearing to obey the model is a coincidence of structure, not a confirmation, and the persuasion runs in the dangerous direction — towards trusting the model further than the evidence supports and extending it to the next service by analogy. It records, as a design-level finding, that this is the third time in one quarter that a tractable simplification was presented without its boundary.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** The memory-hierarchy cost model, locality, layout decisions and the benchmark-versus-production failure in phase 6 are grounded in [research/02-technical-foundations/subramanya-diskann.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/subramanya-diskann.md), [research/02-technical-foundations/kwon-pagedattention-vllm.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/kwon-pagedattention-vllm.md), [research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md), [research/02-technical-foundations/thakur-beir.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/thakur-beir.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md). The perishability treatment at point 8 follows the [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md).

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The prompt-cache behaviour used at phase 3 and point 3 is taken from Microsoft Learn's prompt caching page for Azure OpenAI in Microsoft Foundry Models, read 2026-07-26: a minimum of 1,024 tokens, an identical leading 1,024 tokens, cache hits for every 128 additional identical tokens thereafter, a `cached_tokens` field under `prompt_tokens_details`, caching enabled by default for supported models, and reduced cache matching above roughly fifteen requests per minute on one prefix and cache key. The embeddings limits at phase 5 are taken from the Foundry Models catalogue page and the embeddings how-to page, read the same day: 8,192 tokens per input, 2,048 inputs per array, an aggregate 300,000-token cap per request, `dimensions` on `text-embedding-3` and later, and `encoding_format` defaulting to `float`.

In-memory retention is documented as "typically" cleared within five to ten minutes of inactivity with a hard bound of one hour, so **one end of the eviction policy is a bound and the other is a tendency**, which is the silence point 5 names at S018. And, consistent with the folder-wide asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](/research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), **the documentation states latencies, sizes and thresholds throughout and states no accuracy anywhere**; nothing in it licenses a claim about how often a cache hit occurs in practice, and none is made here. The page is dated by its content rather than by a version number and is expected to be superseded; the day's numbers are marked perishable at point 8 for that reason.
