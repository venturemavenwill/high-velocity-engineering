# S055 · What These Systems Cannot Do

**Module:** [M11](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M11.md) · **Quarter:** [Q6](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q6.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** IS · ME · PE
**Whitepaper:** [WP-055](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-055.md)
**Depends on:** S024, S050, S054
**Platform anchor:** Foundry model catalogue — swapping deployments across families · the vendor's own transparency note as a published limits statement · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

Four days of mechanism produce a temptation, and the temptation is to say what the thing cannot do.

The sentence is easy to write and almost always wrong in the same way. *These systems cannot count.* *They cannot reason over long documents.* *They cannot say when they do not know.* Each of those is a report of an observation, presented as a property. A property is permanent. An observation is a joint fact about a model, a task, an input distribution and an instrument — and any of the four can change, three of them without anybody retraining anything.

[S024](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S024.md) established the duty: when a thing cannot be done, you say so, at the moment you know it. [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md) established the discipline that governs saying it: a limitation stated in language the audience cannot act on has not been stated, and the two failures — overclaiming and unusable hedging — are symmetric.

Today the claim goes out to people who cannot check it.

And today the students read one that somebody else published. **The platform ships a transparency note, and it is a limits statement written under exactly the constraints the students are about to face.** It names limitations by kind, it names them per model family, it distinguishes what the model does from what the deployment configuration does, and it states an obligation the students inherit at the end of this day: that **users should be notified that they are interacting with an AI system.** It is the only worked example of this artefact the programme can supply, it is not a model answer, and reading it critically is phase 2's job.

The target: **a limit claim is architectural, training-derived, or a measurement artefact; the kind is established by intervention rather than by argument; and a limit published without the condition that would make it false is a claim you have no mechanism to withdraw.**

Note also that the day's selection rule and the cohort's own S053 magnitudes are shown to students at the start of phase 3: **the facilitator has to be able to say why this set and not the other one.**

**Platform prerequisites.**

- **GitHub Copilot agent mode**, with the prerequisite chain [WP-001](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-001.md) §2 records: an active subscription, and an administrator who has not disabled agent mode under it.
- **The Foundry MCP Server and the Microsoft Learn MCP Server** connected, with their tools enabled. Phase 2 opens the vendor's transparency note through the Learn server, so that server is load-bearing here rather than incidental.

Losing the publication removes the artefact's audience, and §5's principal affective countermeasure is that the reader is real; a statement written for a rubric is the unusable hedging the day exists to prevent. **Neither loss has a stated fallback anywhere in this wiki.**

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. **Write down three things these systems cannot do.** Your own words, no sources.
2. **For each, what would have to happen for it to become false?**
3. A real substrate support ticket is supplied: *"it can't handle our long documents."* **Rank three candidate causes** and commit to a first choice.
4. **Predict: of the four demonstrations you will see this morning, how many are limitations of the system at all?**
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to explain why the substrate's language feature degrades on long documents. Write down **what kind of cause it will name, and whether it will ask you for any evidence before naming one.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 4 is the day's discriminator and most of the room answers four. Item 1's list is read back anonymously at phase 8 and sorted by kind.

**Warrant.** Pretesting the limits claim before the vocabulary for kinds exists, so that phase 8 compares the student's untutored claim against the taxonomy rather than against a corrected version of itself. Item 2 is the whole day compressed into one question and is unanswerable without the distinction the day supplies. Item 4 admits a plausible-and-wrong answer that a cohort fresh from a measurement module should be least likely to give and will give anyway. **Item 5 is the day's sharpest sealed prediction** and is administered here per instrumentation rule 8: the agent will produce a fluent, mostly-architectural account assembled from public commentary, and the second half of the item — whether it asks for evidence first — is the one students consistently get wrong in the optimistic direction.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative retrieval set (20 min)

Free recall, no notes.

- [S024](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S024.md): the duty to say so — what it required, and to whom. **Scaffolded prompt supplied**, per the [design pattern](/archive/bsc-programme/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md): this material was last seen thirty-one days and three quarters ago, and unscaffolded retrieval at that gap fails completely rather than usefully.
- [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md): the two symmetric failures, and which of them is socially rewarded.
- [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md): selective true reporting — the set you did not say is part of what you communicated.
- [S054](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S054.md): *aligned* is a two-place word, and the criterion nobody wrote down.
- **Bridge:** S024 required four checks before you may conclude a request cannot be satisfied — the instance class, the exact question asked, the acceptable answer quality, and the actual scale. **What are the equivalent checks before you may conclude a system cannot do something?**

The room converges on model, task, input distribution and instrument, without being given them. That convergence is the day's opening and it costs no exposition.

**Then the vendor's transparency note is opened**, through the Learn MCP Server, and the room applies its own four checks to somebody else's published limits statement for ten minutes. The note is a genuinely careful document and the room is asked one question about it: **which of its limitations name the four arguments, and which name fewer?** The answer is mixed, which is the point, and it establishes before phase 3 that the artefact the students will produce this afternoon is a real professional genre with a real published exemplar and not a classroom exercise.

**Warrant.** Expanding-interval retrieval reaching back three quarters, closing with a bridge that makes the day's central procedure a transfer of an existing list rather than new content. A student who sees that they wrote the four checks themselves at class 2 spends the day on the part that is genuinely new, which is the kind labelling. **The transparency-note reading is new and is deliberately placed inside phase 2 rather than phase 4**, because a published exemplar read after the taxonomy is supplied is read for compliance with the taxonomy, and read before it is read for what it actually says.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

Groups receive **one support ticket and four reproducible demonstrations of it.** The ticket is item 3's: *it can't handle our long documents.* Every demonstration produces the same symptom curve — quality falls as the document grows.

- **D1** — a constraint stated at the top of a document is not applied at the bottom, on documents past a particular size.
- **D2** — a fact placed in the middle of a document that fits comfortably is not used, while the same fact at either end is.
- **D3** — the system declines the document, or answers about its first section and stops.
- **D4** — the substrate's own evaluation harness reports accuracy falling monotonically with input length across the full query log.

Groups are **not** told that the four have different causes, and are not told that one of them has no cause in the system at all.

Task: **for each demonstration, name what would make the failure go away, and say what that tells you about what kind of thing it is.** Two candidate causes per demonstration and the intervention that distinguishes them, produced before anything is revealed.

Then the interventions are run — **operated by the groups, not supplied** — in a fixed order: swap the model and hold everything else; change the harness and hold the model; change the input's size and structure and hold both. All three arms are real. The model swap is a deployment change through the Foundry MCP Server across the four [S054](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S054.md) deployments and two catalogue models from different families; the harness change is a setting on the substrate's own evaluation harness; the input change is a re-run over restructured documents from the real query log.

**D4's length effect disappears entirely.** The harness caps output tokens; long inputs draw long answers; long answers are truncated; truncated answers score zero. Nothing about the system changed with length, and the effect was in the substrate's own dashboard for eleven months.

**Selection under instrumentation rule 6, and disclosure under rule 3.** [S053](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S053.md) named this phase as the response occasion for its item 3 and item 4 error magnitudes, which measure whether the cohort over-predicts fragility or under-predicts redundancy. **The four demonstrations are drawn from a prepared pool of eight, and which four run is decided by that result before the day.**

- **If the item 3 error dominated** — the cohort over-predicts fragility, and will therefore accept an apparent limit too readily and attribute it to the mechanism — **Set F runs:** one architectural demonstration and three that dissolve under an intervention that never touches the architecture.
- **If the item 4 error dominated** — the cohort under-predicts redundancy, believes structure is tightly coupled to behaviour, and will therefore attribute a limit to the wrong component and expect a targeted fix — **Set R runs:** two architectural demonstrations with different bounds, one training-derived, and the artefact, so that the obvious structural attribution is correct for one case and wrong for the other.

**Exactly one demonstration in either set is a measurement artefact, and the sets are matched on symptom.** The selection rule and the cohort's own S053 magnitudes are shown to students at the start of the phase, because this day supplies its own cases and a day that conceals how it chose them is running the selection effect it is about to teach.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases matched on symptom and varied on cause, which is the only construction that makes cause visible when the observable is identical — **and now operated rather than supplied**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), because all three intervention arms are things this platform lets a student do in an afternoon and a day that read them from a handout would be teaching attribution without performing one. The set contains **two demonstrations of the same kind**, so a group cannot assign labels by elimination and must defend each one separately. The two-explanations-and-a-distinguishing-test demand reuses [S052](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S052.md)'s structure and **its use here is contingent** under instrumentation rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 — if S052's reported outcome shows the distinguishing-intervention step failing more often than succeeding, this element is retracted along with every other contingent reuse of it.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **A limit claim has four arguments and is not a claim without all of them.** Which system, on which task, over which input distribution, measured by which instrument. Phase 2's bridge produced this list and it is [S024](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S024.md)'s four checks with the objects renamed.

2. **Three kinds.** **Architectural** — it follows from what the mechanism operates over and how much computation it performs per output, which is [S053](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S053.md)'s assembly read for its bounds. **Training-derived** — it follows from the data and the preference signal, which is [S054](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S054.md)'s territory entirely. **Measurement artefact** — the limit is in your instrument and the system does not have it. **This enumeration is not complete.** It omits deployment-configuration limits — safety filters, rate limits, tool and permission availability, serving-time settings — and it omits **joint** limits, where an architectural bound and a trained behaviour interact and no single label is correct. It is **safe** as a triage procedure for a limit you have observed on a system you can intervene on, and it is **unsafe** as an account of every reason a system fails. Specifically, it is unsafe as a licence to publish one label for a limit whose intervention results were mixed: a student who labels by weight of evidence has done the opposite of what this day asks, and the honest report of a mixed result is *unlabelled*.

3. **"Architectural" does not mean permanent.** It means it follows from this assembly. Assemblies change, and this one has changed twice inside the students' own use of it. The durable part of an architectural claim is the derivation; every number in it is perishable. **This is the point the module's failure mode lives on** and it is why the label alone is insufficient.

4. **Training-derived limits have the shortest half-life and are published as permanent most often.** [S054](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S054.md)'s four models differ on nearly every behaviour in this family, and they share a base. A limit that moves when you change the preference set is a fact about a preference set.

5. **The measurement artefact is the most common and the most confidently reported.** Output caps that truncate long answers; scorers with no representation for abstention, which report an abstention rate of zero; answer extraction that fails on one source's formatting; a prompt template the model was not tuned for; length confounded with provenance. [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md) and [S053](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S053.md) point 6 both apply without modification.

6. **Intervene; do not inspect.** [S052](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S052.md) point 4 and [S054](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S054.md) point 8, unchanged and now load-bearing. Swap the model and hold the rest — if the limit moves, it is training-derived. Change the harness and hold the model — if it moves, it was yours. Change neither and vary the input's size and structure — if it moves at a boundary, it is architectural. Inspection of the architecture tells you what is *possible*, never what this trained system *does*.

7. **The duty generalises a third time.** [S024](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S024.md): when it cannot be done, say so. [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md): say what the measurement does not license. **Today: when you publish a limit, publish its kind and its expiry condition** — what would have to change for it to become false, and how a reader would notice. A limit with no expiry condition is a claim with no mechanism for its own withdrawal, and it will outlive its truth by exactly as long as nobody thinks to check.

8. **You are publishing to people who cannot check.** The substrate's users will read your statement and will not read your evidence, and they will make routing decisions on it. [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md) point 1 applies in full: a limitation stated in language the audience cannot act on has not been stated. This is where the quarter's user-disclosure obligation begins, per [Substrate and Infrastructure](/archive/bsc-programme/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md), and every subsequent day in Q6 inherits it.

   The vendor states a floor for this and the students inherit it: **users should be notified that they are interacting with an AI system.** That is a floor, not a standard. The obligation this day adds sits above it — notification is not disclosure, and a user who knows the thing is an AI system and does not know what it does badly has been told the least useful true fact available.

9. **A limit is a joint fact about a model and a configuration, and the configuration is the part that moves silently.** This is the day's platform content and it is why the taxonomy at point 2 names deployment configuration as an omission rather than pretending it is not there.

   The same model behaves differently depending on how it was deployed, and the differences are documented. **All deployment types perform the same inference operations, and their billing, scale and performance are substantially different.** A high-volume workload on the highest-quota deployment type may see greater latency variability than one on reserved capacity — so a latency limit can be a purchasing decision. The default global routing may process prompts in **any geography where the model is deployed**, while a data-zone type confines them to a named region set — so a residency limit is a deployment-type decision, not a model property. And a fine-tuned model is checked before deployment against **safety filters set to predefined thresholds that customers cannot modify** — so a refusal limit may belong to a filter nobody in your organisation configured. **None of that is visible from the model's name**, and a limits statement that names only the model has named the smaller half of the cause.

10. **The published exemplar is a document with a version, and so is yours.** The transparency note the room read at phase 2 is revised as models change and carries limitations that are explicitly per-family. That is the correct shape and it is worth naming: **a limits statement is not a fact, it is a dated artefact.** The students' statements are dated by construction and say so on their face.

11. **Perishable, and marked as such.** Every deployment type name, every routing behaviour, every filter arrangement and the transparency note's own content are this vendor's at this date, and the note is one of the faster-moving documents in the platform because it is revised whenever a model family is added. **None of it is what the specification assesses.** What is assessed is points 1 to 8, which name no vendor, and step 6's expiry conditions, which exist precisely because point 11 is true of everybody's platform and not only this one.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3, with each of the four demonstrations named at the point its kind is defined. Point 2 carries the scope statement in the strong form required by the [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), and it is stated where students can act on it rather than in the whitepaper alone. Point 3 is the module's failure-mode countermeasure and is deliberately placed immediately after the label vocabulary, before students can grow attached to it. Point 7 is the day's most portable sentence. **Point 9 is the required perishable platform content and the required naming of defaults in one**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), and it is placed here rather than in the taxonomy because a fourth branch would dilute the two that matter while an example of the omitted kind, worked, does not. Point 10 is what converts the phase 2 reading from context into method.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 4. The capability, the limits, the interventions and the statement are the student's. It is published.**

1. **Name a capability of the substrate's language feature that users depend on,** and the input distribution it is actually used on. **Name the deployment behind it**: the model, and the deployment type.
2. **Name three apparent limits of it** — from the substrate's support tickets and from your own use of it, **not from the literature.**
3. **For each, state the kind you expect and the intervention that would distinguish it from the other two kinds.** Committed before anything is run.
4. **Run the interventions:** model swap, harness change, input change.
5. **Label each limit and defend the label with the intervention result.** A limit whose results are mixed is **reported as unlabelled and excluded from the published statement**, and that is a pass.
6. **For each labelled limit, state its expiry condition** — what would have to change for it to become false, and how a reader would notice that it had. **At least one expiry condition must name a configuration change rather than a model change**, if any of your limits admits one.
7. **Write the statement.** One page, for the substrate's users: what the feature does, what it is known to do badly, what kind of thing each of those is, when each might stop being true, how many limits you examined and excluded as unlabelled, **which model deployment and deployment type it runs on**, and how to reach a human.

**Fading:** step 4's harness change is worked with the room, since the substrate's evaluation switches are new and unforgiving. **Step 3 is unsupported.**

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a step 4 harness that reports the output-token cap alongside every score, so that a truncation artefact cannot be read as a model limit, is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch.

**Publication.** Statements are reviewed by staff for factual error only and are otherwise published unedited, at the end of the day, to the substrate's live interface. A student may withdraw their statement from publication at any point without consequence, and the withdrawal is not recorded against them.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 4 in which the artefact leaves the room. Step 3 is placed before step 4 so the kind is a pre-commitment rather than a rationalisation of whatever the intervention produced, which is [S053](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S053.md) step 3's construction applied to attribution instead of sensitivity. Step 5's exclusion route is the day's humility measure: it is the only step in the module where declining to make a claim is the assessed-correct output. Step 7's excluded count is in the statement rather than in the workbook, so that the reader sees the honest route was taken. **Steps 1, 6 and 7's added configuration clauses are point 9 made assessable**: a student who names the model and not the deployment type has published a limits statement that will silently stop being true when somebody changes a SKU, and the day's whole argument is that a claim you cannot withdraw is a claim you should not have made.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a limits note published on the substrate fourteen months ago stating that the feature could not summarise tables. It was true. It was training-derived. It stopped being true in a routine model update eleven weeks later. The note stayed up for the remaining eleven months, and three teams built manual table workflows around a capability that existed.

Attention is directed at one fact: **nobody was wrong when they wrote it.** The note was accurate, carefully worded, and correctly placed. It had no expiry condition and no owner, so nothing in the system ever caused it to be re-read.

Then the agent case, and it is the failure the day's own item 5 predicts. A student asked Copilot why the substrate's feature was failing on long documents. **It produced an excellent answer.** It named the architectural bound, explained the mechanism correctly, cited the right property of the assembly, and recommended a chunking strategy that would have been the right recommendation had the diagnosis been right. It asked for nothing. It ran no intervention, requested no evidence, and did not say that it had not.

The cause was the output cap. The chunking work went ahead and cost three weeks.

Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the explanation was correct about the mechanism and wrong about this system.** Everything the agent said about the architecture was true; none of it was evidence that the architecture was what was happening here. This is point 6 failing at its most persuasive — inspection tells you what is possible and never what this configured system does — and the students have their sealed item 5 in front of them while it is discussed.

Then students, in threes: **a limitation you told someone about and never checked again**, or a diagnosis you accepted from an agent that named a mechanism and cited no measurement.

**Warrant.** Error management targeting points 6 and 7, and constructed like [S053](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S053.md)'s clinic so that the case contains no error of care at all. The instructor case is about the absence of a withdrawal mechanism rather than about diligence, which is why diligence cannot prevent it and why the countermeasure has to be structural. **The agent case is the required addition and is the module's most important one**, because a correct-mechanism-wrong-system explanation is the exact output an agent produces most fluently, it is the output engineers are least equipped to resist, and it is the reason this day's hard requirement attaches to the defence rather than to the label.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **their step 5 labels and their step 6 expiry conditions.**

The partner has two obligations and both are required.

- **Choose the label you believe is wrong, and name the intervention that would show it.** Disagreeing with the label without naming the intervention is not a defeat.
- **Read the published sentence cold, and state what it would cause you to stop doing.** Where that does not match the writer's intent, the sentence is revised before publication.

Per rule 3, both outcomes are recorded in the artefact.

The first demand reuses [S052](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S052.md)'s distinguishing-intervention structure and is **contingent** under rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8; the second reuses [S024](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S024.md)'s reader-comprehension test and is carried under the same contingency discipline.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with a dual demand, attacking the attribution and the communication separately because they fail separately. The second demand is a proxy: the real readers are the substrate's users, they are the external observers instrumentation rule 1 exists to prefer, and they cannot respond inside the day. **The proxy is recorded as a proxy** and [WP-055](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-055.md) §8 concedes that the design declined an available fix.

## When it does not work

The predicted failures and their countermeasures are argued in [WP-055](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-055.md) §5. Restated here as instruction to the person in the room. Nothing below is assessed.

| What you see | What to do | What the design accepts |
|---|---|---|

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in ME202 with PE202. Per [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the named capability, its input distribution and its deployment; three apparent limits sourced from use rather than from the literature; the pre-committed kind and distinguishing intervention for each; the intervention results; a label for each limit defended by its result, or an explicit unlabelled exclusion; an expiry condition for each labelled limit; the published one-page statement including the excluded count, the deployment named, and the route to a human; both phase 7 outcomes; **the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

**An unlabelled limit in the published statement fails.** This is the day's single hard requirement and it is the module's stated countermeasure. Reporting a limit as unlabelled and excluding it from the statement is a pass.

**A label defended by argument rather than by an intervention result fails.** The mechanism is not evidence about the trained system, which is point 6.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

No entrustment judgement. The quarter's is at [S060](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S060.md).

**Disposition observed:** humility, evidenced by the step 5 exclusions and by phase 7 revisions accepted from a partner. Per [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md), it is recorded in narrative within the activity and never scored as one.

**Rule-6 discharge.** [S053](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S053.md) named this day as the response occasion for its item 3 and item 4 error magnitudes, specifying that **this day's four demonstrations are selected against whichever direction the cohort got worse.** The element exists in phase 3, it is the selection rule between Set F and Set R, it is executed before the day from the reported S053 magnitudes, and it is disclosed to students at the start of the phase. The obligation is discharged.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Three instruments with named response occasions:

- **The distribution of limits reported unlabelled and excluded** measures whether the cohort will decline to make a claim when the evidence is mixed. **Response occasion: [S071](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S071.md), phase 1**, whose pretest returns each student's own S055 statement and requires them to state which excluded limits they can now label and which they still cannot.
- **The item 1 sortability rate** — the proportion of freely-stated limits that can be assigned a kind at all — measures whether the taxonomy reaches the claims students actually make unprompted. **Response occasion: [S059](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S059.md), phase 3**, where published zero-shot claims are the cases and each must be labelled by kind before the day's material arrives; the S055 rate is the baseline the S059 rate is read against.
- **The reader-comprehension result** — what substrate users report they stopped doing after reading the published statements — is the external-observer measurement phase 7 could only proxy. **Response occasion: [S071](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S071.md), phase 5**, where the statement is revised against reader response before the system is given the ability to act.

**[WP-059](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-059.md) §7 and [WP-071](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-071.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist**, per the commitment [WP-051](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-051.md) §8 established.

## What this day does not establish

**What it demonstrates.** One substrate feature, one symptom curve, four demonstrations of it, and three intervention arms run by the room. In particular that **D4's length effect disappeared under a harness change** — which is a fact about one harness with one output cap over one query log, and is the day's strongest finding precisely because the room produced it rather than read it. **No context bound, no position profile and no failure rate is claimed for any model**, and the two-set selection means a different cohort sees different demonstrations, which the day discloses to students rather than hiding.

**What it asserts, with no external warrant at all.** **The three-kind taxonomy at point 2 is this design's own.** No note under [research/](/research/99-source-register/source-register.md) supplies it, nothing outside this programme adjudicates whether three is the right number, and the page states its own incompleteness — it omits deployment-configuration limits and joint limits, and it is unsafe as an account of every reason a system fails. A facilitator asked where the taxonomy comes from should say: from this design, as a triage procedure, tested nowhere.

**What rests on a note.** Bounded context with injected positional information, and the divergence between a harness's reported effect and a system's behaviour, at [research/02-technical-foundations/vaswani-attention-is-all-you-need.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/vaswani-attention-is-all-you-need.md) and [research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md).

**What it asserts on direction alone.** Position-dependent use of information inside a long input — which is what D2 is built on — is **not verified in this repository** and no profile is asserted for any model. The vendor's transparency note states limitations and no rate for any of them, and the day's use of it is as an exemplar of the genre rather than as evidence about a system.

**What rests on nothing verified here.** The phase warrants lean on retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, pretesting and cognitive load, and **none of the five has a note anywhere under [research/](/research/99-source-register/source-register.md)**. Productive failure and error management do, at [sinha-kapur](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md) and [keith-frese](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md); both supply direction and mechanism and no magnitude.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** A limit claim has four arguments — which system, on which task, over which input distribution, measured by which instrument — and is not a claim without all of them. **Architectural does not mean permanent**, the kind is established by intervention rather than argument, and **a limit published without its expiry condition is a claim you have no mechanism to withdraw.**

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** Bounded context with injected positional information, and the divergence between a harness's reported effect and a system's behaviour, are verified at [research/02-technical-foundations/vaswani-attention-is-all-you-need.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/vaswani-attention-is-all-you-need.md) and [research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md). **Position-dependent use of information inside a long input is not verified here** and is carried for direction and mechanism only, with no profile asserted for any model. The vendor's transparency note states limitations and no rate for any of them.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Intervene; do not inspect.** Inspection tells you what is possible, never what this configured system does.
- **Give every published limit an expiry condition**, and say how a reader would notice it had expired.
- **Name the deployment type, not only the model.** Routing, residency and filter behaviour are configuration, and the model name is the smaller half of the cause.
- **A label chosen by weight of evidence when the interventions were mixed is an unsupported inference** on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md)'s ladder. The honest report is *unlabelled*, and it is a pass.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Accepting a diagnosis that is **correct about the mechanism and wrong about this system.** It asks for no evidence, and does not say that it asked for none.
