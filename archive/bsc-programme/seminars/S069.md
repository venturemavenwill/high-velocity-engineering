# S069 · Governing the Release Decision

**Module:** [M14](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M14.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q7.md) · **Complexity class:** 5
**Strands:** PE · ME · SG
**Whitepaper:** [WP-069](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-069.md)
**Depends on:** S039, S046, S066, S068
**Platform anchor:** Foundry role-based access control as the release authority — the project managed identity, the roles the evaluation and trace paths require, and the tenant, subscription and project rate limits · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server · Foundry MCP Server (preview) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

Three days have produced a gate, a measured gap and a detection design. Every one of them ends in a question the student cannot answer alone.

[S066](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S066.md) point 7 said a threshold is a decision about which of two errors you prefer, and that it does not belong to the engineer holding the number. It did not say whose it is. [S068](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S068.md) step 3 produced a list of failures the student has decided not to detect, and the list currently has no owner — which is how such lists accumulate. [S067](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S067.md)'s scope statement said the five differences cannot be separated when several changes are released at once, and that the remedy is a release schedule, which nobody in this room controls.

[S046](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S046.md) point 3 supplied the adjudicating question for a criterion: **who bears the cost of a wrong output.** [S039](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S039.md) supplied the negotiation apparatus: every constraint has a purpose, the purpose is often served more cheaply, and a trade the other party cannot evaluate is not consent.

[S024](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S024.md) point 7 asked, twenty-two months ago, when a finding stops being yours to hold, and answered it informally. **It is answered properly today.**

The target: **a release decision has an owner, an authority, a record and a reversal path. An arrangement that does not name all four has not distributed the decision; it has delegated it to whoever is most confident in the room.**

---

## Authority is already written down somewhere, and it is not where you think

The students have spent three days building a gate, a measurement and a signal. Every one of those runs under an identity, and **the identity's permissions are the real authority structure**, whatever the wiki says.

The gate the student built at [S066](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S066.md) has a documented pass threshold that the documentation says the user may override — and *which* user is an access-control fact, not a policy fact. The continuous evaluation rule at [S068](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S068.md) requires the project's managed identity to hold a specific Foundry role before it will run at all. The trace evaluation requires a reader role on **two** resources, the Application Insights component and its linked Log Analytics workspace, and a further role again where the trace tables are protected. **A missing role assignment does not produce an error message about governance. It produces a gate that does not run**, which is [S068](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S068.md)'s third outcome wearing an authorisation costume.

Two further facts are worth having in the room before phase 3.

- **Evaluation run creation is rate-limited at the tenant, at the subscription and at the project.** Three levels, three owners, and the student holds none of them. An arrangement that assumes the gate will run when called has assumed something about three parties it has not spoken to.
- **The roles were renamed.** The documentation states that Foundry User, Foundry Owner, Foundry Account Owner and Foundry Project Manager were previously named otherwise, that the identifiers and permissions are unchanged, and that the old names may still appear in places while the rename rolls out. **The authority did not change and every document naming it became wrong.** That is [S039](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S039.md)'s fourth constraint kind arriving in eighteen months rather than in a decade, and it is the cheapest available argument for point 5's requirement that a record be written so somebody can find it in two years.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

A real release decision from the substrate's history is supplied, with everything the deciders had at the time and nothing they did not. Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. **Who should have made this decision?** Name a role, and say why.
2. **Who actually made it?** From the record.
3. You have found a defect two hours before a release you are not the owner of. **At what point does telling one person discharge your duty?**
4. Predict: **of the failures your own component cannot detect, how many have an owner today?**
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to tell you who is permitted to change the threshold on the gate you built at [S066](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S066.md), and to make that arrangement explicit. Write down what it will produce, and **name the one thing it will get wrong.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 3 is [S024](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S024.md) point 7 taken cold, and the answers are collected in the students' own words so that phase 4 point 4 can be read against them.

Item 5's expected shape is the day's subject applied to the agent: asked who is permitted to change something, an agent with read access will enumerate the identities that hold the permission — accurately — **and will present that list as an answer to the question of who is accountable**, which is a different question and is the conflation point 3 exists to break.

**Warrant.** Pretesting a governance judgement on a real case with the real record available for item 2. Items 1 and 2 are constructed to diverge, which is the day's opening finding and needs no framework: the decision was made by the person who was available, which is neither the person with the evidence nor the person with the authority. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8, and it is deliberately the same conflation as item 1 against item 2 in a form the student will be able to check mechanically.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative retrieval set (20 min)

Free recall, no notes.

- [S039](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S039.md): the four kinds of constraint, and what to ask before asking whether one can move.
- [S046](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S046.md): whose view counts, and the question that decides it.
- [S066](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S066.md): the gate's two error rates, and which one leaves a record.
- [S068](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S068.md): the not-detected list, and what it is a decision about.
- **Bridge:** S046 said the view that counts belongs to whoever bears the cost of a wrong output. S066 said the gate has two error rates. **Do the same people bear the cost of both?**

They do not, and the room can say so in one exchange: a wrong pass is paid for by users and by whoever is on call; a wrong block is paid for by the engineer whose change did not ship and by everyone who needed it. **The two costs fall on different parties, and only one of those parties is in the room when the threshold is set.**

**Warrant.** Expanding-interval retrieval producing the day's structural claim by joining two facts from two quarters. The bridge is the highest-value minute in the day and it is where [S066](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S066.md)'s two error rates stop being a measurement property and become a political one.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**One executed opening, then four cases that are demonstrated rather than operated.**

**The executed part, twenty minutes.** Before any ranking, each group answers one question about the gate they built two days ago: **who is permitted to change its threshold, and who is permitted to stop it running?** They answer it by reading the actual role assignments on the Foundry project, on the Application Insights resource and on its linked Log Analytics workspace, through the Azure MCP Server, and by listing the identities that hold each role — including the managed identities and service principals, which are not people and cannot be asked anything.

Two findings come out of this reliably and neither needs an instructor.

- **The set of identities that can change the gate is not the set of people accountable for the service.** It is larger at one end and smaller at the other, it contains at least one identity nobody can name, and it was assembled by whoever was setting things up.
- **Nobody in the group can say who assigned any of it.** The assignment exists; the decision to make it does not appear anywhere the group can reach.

That is point 1's four parts, failing in front of them, on their own artefact.

**Then the four arrangements.** Groups receive **four governance arrangements that have operated on this substrate**, each with the release record of its period: what was released, what was blocked, what was reversed, and what went wrong. **These are demonstrated rather than operated — an authority structure is not a service call — and no element of the specification depends on running them.**

**The four are selected against whichever of [S066](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S066.md)'s two error rates the cohort proved blind to** — if the cohort could not name the false-block error unprompted, the arrangements are chosen so that their differences show up mainly in what was never shipped.

- **A1** — the engineer with the evidence decides. Fastest. The decision-maker bears none of the cost of a wrong pass.
- **A2** — a release board meets weekly and nobody decides alone. No individual is accountable, and the board's latency is itself a cost that appears in no record.
- **A3** — an automated gate decides and humans may override with a written reason. The overrides are recorded and have never been read.
- **A4** — the staff member accountable for the service decides, and **the engineer is required to state the case against their own change.**

Groups rank the four and commit. **Then the release records are supplied.**

The payload is **A4**. It is the slowest, it concentrates authority in one person, and it is the arrangement the room ranks worst on every criterion the programme has taught about traceability and distributed judgement. On the substrate's record it produced the fewest reversed releases and **the only recorded refusals to ship** — and the mechanism is not the seniority. It is the requirement that the person who wants the change argue against it, which is the only element of the four that changes what the decider is told.

Task after: for each arrangement, **name who bears the cost of a wrong pass, who bears the cost of a wrong block, and who has the authority to accept a failure nobody can detect.**

The third question has no answer in three of the four. **Now ask it of the role assignments the group read twenty minutes ago**, and it has no answer there either.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across governance arrangements with a real release record as adjudicator, and with the case selection instrumented to [S066](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S066.md)'s measured blind spot, which is this day's discharge of that obligation. A4 defeats the room's expectation that distributing a decision improves it, and the task-after's third question is the phase's real product: it is unanswerable for A1, A2 and A3, and that is the finding. **The executed opening exists because the four arrangements cannot be run and the day still has to put something real in front of the room**; the role assignments are real, they are readable in minutes, they are the arrangement the students are actually operating under, and their gap from the written one is the same gap items 1 and 2 measure in the substrate's history. Per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), the four arrangements are stated as demonstrated and are not assessed on any element requiring their operation.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **A release decision has four parts:** an owner, an authority, a record, and a reversal path. Name all four or you have not described an arrangement.

2. **The owner is whoever bears the cost of being wrong.** [S046](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S046.md) point 3 applied to a decision rather than to a criterion — and there are two costs, falling on two parties, which is why an arrangement optimised against one of them is not neutral.

3. **Authority is not expertise.** The person with the evidence is usually not the person entitled to decide, and conflating them is the commonest governance failure in engineering organisations. It produces two symmetric errors: an engineer who decides something that was not theirs, and an engineer who withholds a finding because the decision was not theirs to make.

4. **When a finding stops being yours to hold.** [S024](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S024.md) point 7, answered. Three conditions, and all three are required: **someone else has the authority to act on it; they can act in time; and you have stated it in terms they can act on**, which is [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md)'s standard and not a lower one. Until all three hold, it is still yours. **Telling one person does not discharge the duty — reaching someone who can act does. This is safe wherever an escalation path exists and functions. It is unsafe where the path terminates in the person whose decision your finding contradicts, which is common, is not a procedural problem, and is [S082](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S082.md)'s material; there the question is not how to escalate but what you are prepared to do, and this day does not answer it.**

5. **The record exists for the decision that goes wrong,** and its function is to make the reasoning reviewable rather than to allocate blame. It is written at the moment of decision, by the decider, because a record assembled afterwards is a reconstruction and everyone knows it. [S010](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S010.md)'s discipline, applied in advance rather than after.

6. **The reversal path is part of the decision.** A release with no rollback is not a release decision; it is a commitment. The cost of reversal — in time, in user experience, in data that cannot be un-written — is a term in the decision and is usually estimated by nobody.

7. **Retiring a gate.** [S047](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S047.md) point 6 gave three options for a criterion that fails validation: reconstruct, restrict, retire. All three apply to a release gate and each has a cost to the parties from [S046](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S046.md). **A gate nobody has the authority to retire is a constraint of [S039](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S039.md)'s fourth kind — inherited, with no remaining purpose and no living owner — and it will outlive everyone who remembers what it was for.**

8. **Who may accept an undetectable failure.** [S068](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S068.md)'s not-detected lists exist and every entry is a decision somebody made. The default owner is nobody, which is exactly how such lists grow: no entry is ever accepted, so none is ever refused. **An entry with no owner is not an accepted risk. It is an unmade decision with a consequence attached.**

9. **Defaults chose your authority for you, and one of them is a rate limit.** Three, from this morning. **The permission set on your project** was assembled by whoever provisioned it, contains identities that are not people, and has no record of who granted what. **The roles were renamed under their own holders**, with the documentation stating that identifiers and permissions are unchanged and that old names still appear in places — so every arrangement that named the old role is now wrong and nothing broke, which is how a governance document dies. And **evaluation run creation is rate-limited at the tenant, the subscription and the project.** That third one is the sharpest: **a rate limit is an authority.** Somebody you have never met can stop your gate running, the mechanism is a retry header rather than a refusal, and no arrangement in this room has a row for them.

10. **A gate you cannot retire, and a gate you cannot start.** Point 7 gave three options for a criterion that fails validation — reconstruct, restrict, retire — and all three require somebody with the authority to act. On this platform both ends of that have an address. **Retiring** means removing a required check from a pipeline, which is a permission on the repository and not on the service. **Starting** means holding a role on a project, and on two monitoring resources, and possibly a third on protected tables. A student who cannot say which identity holds which of those cannot describe their own arrangement, however carefully they have written it down. **The durable claim is that authority is a property of a system and not of a document**, and the platform's contribution is that here you can go and read it.

11. **What is perishable here and what is not.** The role names, the resource types, the rate-limit levels and the preview labels will change; the roles have changed once already within the lifetime of the documentation the students are reading. **None of it touches the durable claim**, which is that a decision has an owner, an authority, a record and a reversal path, and that an arrangement whose authority is not the arrangement the system enforces is a description of a wish. **This is safe for reasoning about any system whose permissions you can read — who can change this, who can stop it, who granted that. It is unsafe as a model of authority in an organisation whose real decisions are made outside any system you have access to**, which is common, and where the permission map will tell you confidently about the wrong thing. That case is point 4's unsafe condition and is [S082](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S082.md)'s.

12. **Closure re-derived, not asserted.** *Added 2026-07-30, on a reading of a second internal corpus. Taught and warranted, never assessed — the specification is unchanged.* Points 1 to 8 make a release decision an object with an owner, an authority, a record and a reversal path, and **every one of those is something a person states.** Here is the other shape. A customer-embedded engineering organisation within a large technology vendor built a chained agent pipeline that produces a security plan and then, in a later stage, **re-derives the status of every mitigation in it by searching the workspace** — source, pipeline definitions, container files, several infrastructure dialects, configuration and documentation. It is recorded in [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md). **A claim of *implemented* must cite a file and a location.** Re-run the stage after a fix and the evidence is found, the status flips, and the stage that generates work items generates nothing for that mitigation. The instruction governing the whole stage is the right one: **when in doubt, classify as unclear, because a false claim of implementation is strictly worse than a false negative.**

    **The shape is what to take.** Closure becomes a property of the repository rather than of a person's assertion, so it survives the person leaving, it cannot be granted by confidence, and anyone may re-check it at any time. That is point 5's record with the reasoning made mechanical, and it is the strongest available answer to point 8's ownerless entry: **a status nobody has to claim is a status nobody can quietly stop claiming.** Most organisations do not have it.

    **Three caveats travel with it and none is optional.** **Nothing schedules the re-run.** The derivation is correct whenever it happens and it happens when somebody remembers — which is exactly the failure the mechanism was built to remove, and is point 7's dead gate arriving from the opposite direction: an authority with no cadence is a wish. **A real fix that is invisible in the repository never closes.** A portal setting, a tenant policy, a control applied by another team in a system this search cannot see: the mitigation is genuinely in place, the search finds nothing, the item regenerates indefinitely, and a register that is wrong in a predictable direction is one people learn to ignore. **And the acceptance criteria a person would close a ticket against were written by the same model that wrote the mitigation.** Evidence-derived closure removes the human assertion from the middle of the loop and leaves the **standard** the evidence is judged against generated by one of the parties — which is [S046](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S046.md)'s question about whose judgement counts, arriving where nobody thinks to ask it.

    **What this establishes and what it does not.** It establishes that the arrangement exists and is operable in one real system. **It establishes nothing about whether it closes more mitigations, or better ones, than a person ticking a box**: the corpus reports no comparison, no defect-escape figure and no outcome of any kind, and **no figure from it is reproduced here or anywhere in this wiki.** The idea is worth holding because it is arguable, not because it has been shown to work.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Point 4 discharges [S024](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S024.md) point 7's escalation question and carries the strong-form scope statement. Point 8 is the day's retained content and is what [S070](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S070.md) consumes. Point 3's two symmetric errors are stated together deliberately: a room taught only the first produces engineers who escalate everything, which is the same failure wearing better clothes. **Points 9 and 10 are the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s two required platform points**, and they are placed after point 8 rather than before it so that the permission map is read as an instance of the four parts rather than as a substitute for them. Point 11 carries the perishability boundary to the students, and its unsafe condition is deliberately the same one point 4 names, because a student who leaves believing that reading permissions is reading authority has acquired a more confident version of the error the day exists to correct. **Point 12 is a specimen and is not platform content**, and it is placed last because it is the only thing in the day that removes a person from a decision rather than naming one. It is walked by the instructor, is **deliberately unassessable and adds no checked element**, and the reason is stated in the room: the corpus is internal, nothing in it may be linked, and it reports no outcome, so nothing a student says about it could be marked. Its three caveats are given as part of the point rather than as a coda, because an arrangement this attractive is adopted from its first sentence and the caveats are where the engineering is.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 5. This phase produces a build. The arrangement is the student's to design, to make enforceable, and to negotiate.**

Students use **the gate from [S066](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S066.md), the gap from [S067](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S067.md) and the detection design from [S068](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S068.md).**

1. **State the release decision** the three artefacts support.
2. **Name the owner**, and give the evidence that this person bears the cost of being wrong — both costs, separately.
3. **State the authority:** what this person may decide without asking, and what they may not. Where the two costs fall on different parties, say which one the authority is calibrated against.
4. **Write the record's form:** what is written down at the moment of decision, by whom, and where it will be found by someone looking two years later. **Commit it where the gate lives**, versioned with the pipeline, so that the record and the thing it governs move together.
5. **State the reversal path and its cost**, in time, in user experience, and in anything that cannot be undone.
6. **Assign an owner to every entry on your [S068](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S068.md) not-detected list**, or record that an entry has none. **For any entry in the user-visible-only class, the owner is whoever receives reports from affected users, and you must name that person rather than a function.**
7. **Negotiate the arrangement** with the staff member accountable for the substrate, and record the agreement — or the refusal, and why — in [S034](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S034.md)'s three-part form.
8. **Make one part of it enforceable, and say which three parts are not.** Using Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server, take the arrangement you have just written and make a single element of it a property of the system rather than of the document — a review requirement on the file that carries the gate's threshold, a role assignment that matches the owner you named at step 2, an alert routed to the person at step 6. **Students are paired for this step, and the enforced element must be one the partner has to act on** — the partner holds the approval, receives the alert, or occupies the slot — so that the enforcement is a constraint on somebody rather than a note to yourself. **Then state plainly which parts of your arrangement remain enforced by nothing but agreement, and what would make each of them fail silently.** **Where the substrate's tenant does not permit you to make the change, write it as a proposal and say so: that part is demonstrated rather than operated and is not assessed.**

**The accountable staff member is present for the last thirty minutes and is not obliged to agree.** Six minutes each.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md): the **prompt record**, the **intervention record** — **no entries fails**, unless the arrangement demonstrates it could have detected an intervention-worthy error, of which a review requirement that would have blocked an unauthorised threshold change is the obvious example and is accepted — and the **frontier statement**.

**Fading:** step 4's record form is supplied, and step 7's trade preparation reuses [S039](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S039.md)'s form. Step 8's tooling and the read-only permission queries are supplied. **Steps 2 and 8's second half are unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 5 with a live negotiation against a party who may refuse, which is [S039](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S039.md)'s construction two complexity classes higher and with the student's own artefacts at stake. **Step 6 is the artefact element [S068](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S068.md) named under instrumentation rule 6** and its number is fixed by that reference. Step 2 is unsupported because naming an owner is the day's whole judgement, and step 3's calibration clause is the discriminator — an authority stated without saying which error it is tuned against has not been stated. **Step 8 is new with the build and is the day's answer to point 10.** Its second half is the assessed half: making one element enforceable is a small engineering task, and **stating which three remain unenforced is the judgement**, because an arrangement whose author believes it is enforced is worse than one whose author knows it is not.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate release blocked for eleven weeks by a gate whose owner had left the organisation two years earlier. Everyone involved agreed the gate was wrong. Nobody had the authority to retire it, nobody could establish who would have had, and the eventual resolution was that a new gate was added alongside it. Attention is directed at one fact — **the eleven weeks appear in no incident record, no post-mortem and no metric**, because a wrong block leaves nothing behind.

Then the agent case: a Copilot-produced access review that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. Asked to document who could change a service's release configuration, it enumerated the role assignments correctly, resolved every principal, formatted the result cleanly, and **presented it as the list of people accountable for the service.** Two of the entries were service principals and one was a group nobody in the team belonged to. The list was accurate and the heading was false. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the reviewer checked the entries and the entries were right.** Nobody checked the noun. This is point 3's conflation — authority is not expertise, and a permission is not an accountability — produced by a tool that had no way to know the difference and was not asked to.

Then students, in threes: a rule you have followed whose owner you could not name — **or a list you accepted today whose heading you did not check.**

**Warrant.** Error management targeting points 7, 8 and 10, and closing [S066](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S066.md)'s two-error-rate argument. The instructor case contains no error by any individual: every person acted correctly within their authority, and the aggregate was eleven weeks of harm that the organisation has no way to see. It is the false-block error given a duration and a face. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition** and is chosen because its output is the exact artefact phase 3's executed opening produced, so every student in the room has one in front of them and has already been tempted to read it the same way.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **their step 2 owner and step 6 assignments**, withholding step 7's outcome.

The partner takes the role of **the affected party who was not consulted** — [S046](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S046.md)'s group D, the users who bear the cost of a wrong pass and were in nobody's room — and asks **one question**: *who tells me, and when?*

The pair then decides whether the arrangement answers it, and records the answer.

Per rule 3, the outcome is recorded in the artefact, including an arrangement that cannot answer, which is a result about the arrangement rather than an absence of one.

Per rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8, this reuses [S039](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S039.md) phase 7's absent-party structure with its one-question limit. No day has run, so the reuse is **contingent** on S039's reported outcome, and a reported failure there retracts it here.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with an absent-party test. The one-question limit forces the arrangement's record to carry the reasoning rather than the student, which is [S039](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S039.md)'s argument that an agreement surviving only in the room where it was made is not an agreement. The question is fixed rather than chosen because it is the one question a governance arrangement most often cannot answer.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in ME301 with SG301. Per [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the release decision; the named owner with evidence of both costs; the authority with its calibration stated; the record's form, author and location, **committed where the gate lives**; the reversal path with its cost; an owner or an explicit none for every not-detected entry, with a named person for every user-visible-only entry; the negotiated agreement or refusal in three-part form; **the one enforced element and the statement of which parts remain enforced by agreement alone, with what would make each fail silently**; **the prompt record**; **the intervention record**; **the frontier statement**; and the phase 7 outcome. **Nothing else is assessed.**

**An arrangement missing any of the four parts fails.** This is the day's single hard requirement.

**An owner named as a function rather than a person fails** — "the platform team", "product", "on-call" — because a function cannot bear a cost and cannot be asked a question.

> **Corrected after [WP-089](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-089.md) §8, annotated at the point of change.** The requirement as originally written was unscoped, and in that form it contradicts [S089](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S089.md): a person-named owner is precisely what does not survive a handover, so a programme that fails a function-named owner here and requires a transferable owner there had written two specifications that cannot both be satisfied. The requirement is therefore scoped to **slot and occupant**. The artefact must name **a slot**, which is the transferable object and is what S089 hands over, **and the person occupying it on the date of the decision**, which is who bears the cost and who can be asked a question. A slot with no named occupant fails, and an occupant with no slot fails. Neither prior position was wrong; each was stated without the other.

**A refusal at step 7 is a pass.** An arrangement the accountable party declined, recorded with the grounds, is the specification's expected outcome for a substantial share of the cohort and is not a failure. An arrangement recorded as agreed when it was not is.

**An arrangement claiming to be enforced where it is not fails.** Step 8's second half is the check: a student who lists three unenforced parts has described their arrangement, and a student who lists none has either enforced everything, which is not credible, or has not looked.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the arrangement demonstrates it could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**Nothing that depends on operating the four arrangements is assessed**, and nothing that depends on a permission change the substrate's tenant does not allow. Both are demonstrated rather than operated, and both are stated as such to the room.

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S070](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S070.md).

**Rule-6 discharge.** Two obligations land on this day and **both elements exist.**

- [S066](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S066.md) named this day as the response occasion for its two-error-rate instrument, specifying that phase 3's arrangements be selected against whichever error the cohort proved blind to and that the cost-bearer of each be named. **Phase 3's case selection is a function of the S066 result, and the task-after requires all three cost-and-authority questions for each of the four arrangements.**
- [S068](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S068.md) named this day as the response occasion for its pooled not-detected list, specifying that every entry be assigned an owner or recorded as having none. **That is phase 5 step 6, and it is a checked element with a named-person requirement for the user-visible-only class.**

**No cross-course deferral is available on this day.** [Q7](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q7.md)'s four courses are IS301, SY301, ME301 and SG301, and there is no concurrent PE course. The governance and negotiation material this day requires is therefore carried at seminar length rather than deferred, which is why phase 4 is unusually long on professional content for a class-5 technical module. The structural cost is argued in [WP-069](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §8.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions:

- **The unowned-entry count** — how many accepted-blindness entries across the cohort have no owner after step 6. **Response occasion: [S070](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S070.md), phase 3 item 5, where the ship artefact must state who owns each failure the student cannot detect, and the cohort total is reported at phase 8.**
- **The step 7 refusal rate and grounds** — how many arrangements the accountable staff member declined, and why. **Response occasion: [S070](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S070.md), phase 4, where a student whose arrangement was refused is asked in the defence what they changed, and the answer is part of the record the assessors hold.**

**A third instrument, under rule 9.** The **item 5 sealed prediction accuracy and the step 8 unenforced-part counts** are this day's contribution to the running frontier map and to the cohort's record of what its arrangements actually rest on. Per instrumentation rule 9 the reader is named: **the following year's cohort, through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md)**, which carries the re-verification of the role names and rate-limit levels at point 9 before each offering. Per instrumentation rule 3 the sampling is disclosed to the students: one cohort, one tenant, one project's permission map, and four arrangements selected by staff against the [S066](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S066.md) result rather than at random.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **A release decision has an owner, an authority, a record and a reversal path**, and an arrangement that does not name all four has delegated the decision to whoever is most confident in the room. **Authority is a property of the system rather than of the document**, so the identities that can change your gate are the real arrangement, whatever the written one says.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** The negotiation apparatus behind step 7 — a stated constraint has a purpose, and agreement without comprehension is not consent — follows [research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md), cited for **direction and mechanism only.** **No magnitude is asserted from it**, and the role names at point 9 changed once inside the documentation you read this morning.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Name a slot and the person occupying it on the date of the decision.** A function cannot bear a cost or answer a question.
- **Say which of the two errors your authority is calibrated against.** An unstated calibration is a preference wearing a policy's clothes.
- **List the parts enforced by nothing but agreement**, and what would make each fail silently. Listing none is not credible.
- **Human approval is a stop condition and never a field to be filled**, per the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). A workflow that proceeds when a required review is absent has recorded an approval nobody gave.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Reading a permission list as an accountability list. The enumeration can be right in every row and the heading false, and a reviewer checking entries does not check the noun.
