# S019 · Contention, Queueing and Why Latency Is Not Throughput

**Module:** [M04](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M04.md) · **Quarter:** [Q2](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q2.md) · **Complexity class:** 2
**Strands:** SY · MS · CF
**Whitepaper:** [WP-019](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-019.md)
**Depends on:** S018
**Re-tests:** S012, S017
**Platform anchor:** Foundry tokens-per-minute allocation as the operating point; rate-limit enforcement windows; the shared standard pool · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

Two weeks ago, at S013, caller C was the case that defeated the room: an interactive service behind a queue, where an individual slow operation became a systemic effect. The mechanism was named and deferred. This is where it is paid.

The day's target is the single most counter-intuitive relationship in systems work: **as utilisation approaches capacity, latency does not rise proportionally — it rises without bound.** A system at half capacity and a system at ninety per cent capacity are not two points on a line. The second is close to a cliff of the same kind S018 produced, from an entirely different mechanism.

And the professional consequence: **throughput and latency are different objectives, improving one commonly worsens the other, and a system tuned for one will disappoint anyone who wanted the other.**

The platform adds a third fact that the day exists to make unforgettable. **You are not alone on the machine, and the neighbour you are contending with is not in the room.** A standard Foundry deployment draws on a pool shared across customers; when demand across that pool approaches its limits the service may temporarily reduce your deployment's effective rate limit, without your configured quota changing, visible only in a response header. The day therefore teaches contention twice: once as a curve the student produces, and once as a curve produced by people they will never identify, on a resource they are renting.

## Before you run this day

**Materials to prepare.** None of the following exists in this repository. Case C is the item that carries the day, and it is the one that cannot be planned from documentation.

- **A supplied service with a stated service rate** for phase 1, and a known mean response time at fifty per cent utilisation, so that items 1 and 2 are extrapolations from a given point rather than guesses.
- **Sketch sheets for two curves** — mean response time against demand, and ninety-ninth-percentile response time against demand — collected before any data arrives. The sketches are shown against the measured curves at phase 8, so they must be comparable.
- **A load driver capable of seven demand levels from 30 to 97 per cent of a known allocation**, recording the **full response-time distribution and the 429 rate**, not means. A driver that reports only summaries removes phase 3's adjudicator.
- **Four stream configurations carrying the same total work**: constant size and smooth arrival; variable size and smooth arrival; constant size and bursty arrival; variable size and bursty arrival. **Case C must be constructed so that its per-minute rate is identical to case A's and it is refused where A is comfortable.** That depends on the enforcement window, which the documentation gives as a small period and does not publish for a given deployment — so **the case has to be found by measurement before the day, not derived.**
- **Instrumentation that separates first-attempt from retried requests** for phase 5 step 1. Without it, step 1's split is unproducible and the composite the day concedes stays invisible in the students' own data.
- **At least three candidate signals wired up and readable**: the remaining-token and remaining-request headers, the 429 rate split by status code, and the platform metrics through the Azure MCP Server. Step 7's deliverable is the comparison, so all three must be available to compare.
- **The step 2 loading procedure**, supplied.
- **The phase 6 clinic pair**: the substrate capacity decision made on mean utilisation that failed under a burst, **with the actual arrival pattern set beside the planning assumption**; and the agent case — the smooth-ramping capacity probe that reported the last rate at which nothing failed, correct for the traffic it generated and roughly double what real bursty traffic sustained, together with the review that passed it.
- **A reserve task**, per [WP-019](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-019.md) §5: predict the effect of *reducing* variability while raising utilisation so that mean response time is held constant.
- **The module's cumulative retrieval set for [S020](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S020.md)**, which is issued the same day.

**Staff preparation.**

- **Establish by measurement that case C behaves as the design requires on the deployment you actually hold.** The window is not published; a case built from the documentation may not fire.
- **Know whether the deployment in front of the room is standard or provisioned.** Point 9's two currencies have different answers, and the day's closing point and phase 7's briefing both depend on which.
- **Brief the phase 7 partner to press the uncomfortable first answer** — that on a standard deployment the headroom is not costing money at all. Without that briefing the phase can be won by reciting a price, which is the failure the platform introduced and the briefing removes.

**Platform prerequisites.**

- A Foundry deployment with a **known tokens-per-minute allocation and enough headroom to be driven to 97 per cent**, repeatedly, by every group.
- The ability to generate **bursty arrival at sub-minute resolution**. Case C is a property of the arrival pattern inside the enforcement window, and a driver that can only shape per-minute rates cannot produce it.
- Platform metrics through the **Azure MCP Server in read configuration**, the Microsoft Learn MCP Server, Copilot agent mode, and a disposable environment.
- **Driving a shared standard deployment to saturation affects the pool it draws on.** Whether that is permitted is somebody else's decision; settle it before the week the day runs rather than during phase 3.

**If a prerequisite is missing.**

- *The allocation cannot be driven near saturation.* The knee is the day. [WP-019](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-019.md) names no substitute and none is invented here — a curve stopped at seventy per cent teaches that the relationship is gentle, which is the belief the day exists to remove.
- *Bursty arrival cannot be generated at sub-minute resolution.* Case C goes, and with it the day's strongest statement of its own thesis. Run A, B and D, and say plainly that the arrival-side condition was not produced.
- *A group sketches both curves and the variability condition correctly.* Give them the constant-mean-response-time trade. It is a genuine trade and it is hard.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items on a supplied service with a stated service rate.

1. At 50 per cent utilisation, mean response time is 20ms. Predict it at 90 per cent.
2. Predict it at 95 per cent.
3. You double the server's speed. What happens to mean response time at the same request rate?
4. Predict the accuracy band of your item 2 answer.
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will ask the agent to find the highest request rate this deployment can sustain. Write down what it will produce, and **name the one thing it will get wrong**. Opened at phase 8.

Item 2's true answer is far larger than almost anyone predicts. Item 3's answer is not "it halves."

**Warrant.** Pretesting with quantitative extrapolation. The gap between items 1 and 2 is the day's content, and it is produced as an error the student made rather than as a fact they were told. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 per instrumentation rule 8; its expected answer is that the agent will report a sustainable rate measured as a per-minute average, which is the quantity the platform does not enforce on.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups drive a real Foundry deployment with a known allocation and record the **full response-time distribution and the 429 rate** at seven demand levels from 30 to 97 per cent of that allocation — not just the mean.

Before any data arrives they sketch two curves: mean response time against demand, and ninety-ninth-percentile response time against demand. They commit.

Then the runs happen, under **four stream configurations** carrying the same total work.

| Case | Request size | Arrival pattern |
|---|---|---|
| A | Constant | Smooth |
| B | Variable, same mean | Smooth |
| C | Constant | Bursty, same mean rate |
| D | Variable, same mean | Bursty, same mean rate |

B is worse than A everywhere and much worse near saturation. **C is the phase's payload.** Its requests are identical to A's and its per-minute rate is identical to A's, and it is throttled at demand levels where A is comfortable — because the service evaluates request rate over windows of a second or ten seconds, not over the minute the student has been reasoning in. Nothing in any per-minute number predicts it.

Task after: **state which of the two curves you would show to a person deciding whether to add capacity, and why.**

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across two dimensions — service variability and arrival variability — with a distributional adjudicator, run against a metered service. The design's earlier version varied service-side variability and left arrival-side variability as an instructor anecdote; [WP-019](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-019.md) §8 recorded that asymmetry as an unprincipled oversight with a cheap fix, and case C is that fix. It is also the stronger case: service-time variability is an effect students accept readily, and arrival-time variability is the one that transfers least and causes the most incidents.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **The queue,** as an object. Arrivals, service, and the waiting that happens when they do not line up.
2. **Why waiting is non-linear.** As utilisation rises the probability of arriving to a busy server rises, and the queue you join is longer. The two effects compound. Worked completely.
3. **Utilisation, throughput and latency as three different quantities,** and the relationships between them. Throughput saturates; latency diverges; utilisation is the independent variable.
4. **Variability's effect,** from phase 3's cases B, C and D. Variance in service time or arrival time makes everything worse, and this is not visible in any mean. Case C is the harder half and the one that generalises worst: **an average rate is not a rate**, and the platform enforces on a window you did not choose.
5. **The relationship to S018's cliff.** Different mechanism, same shape. Non-linear saturation is a class, and this is its second member.
6. **What "the system is at 80 per cent capacity" does and does not tell you** — S013's claim types applied to a utilisation figure. On a shared standard deployment it tells you less than students expect, because the denominator is not stable: the service may temporarily reduce a deployment's effective rate limit when demand across the shared pool rises, and the reduction appears in a response header rather than in the configured quota.
7. **Little's law**, stated and used, as the one piece of arithmetic that connects the three quantities and that a student can apply without a queueing course.
8. **The default the platform chose for you, and the window you cannot see.** Request rate is evaluated over a small period the documentation gives as **typically one or ten seconds**, and it does not say which applies to your deployment. Meanwhile the finest time grain published for the platform metrics is one minute. **The window in which you are judged is shorter than the finest window in which you can observe yourself**, which means the burst that throttled you is not merely hard to find in the metrics — it is not in them.
9. **Operating point as a decision, and what headroom costs.** Where you choose to sit on the curve is a trade of cost against headroom, and it is the same bet structure as S018's provisioning. What is new is that the currency depends on the deployment type. On a standard deployment, quota is a policy limit with **no associated cost**, so unused headroom is free in money and is paid for by whoever did not get the allocation. On a provisioned deployment it is billed hourly on the units deployed **regardless of the number of tokens consumed**, so headroom is paid in cash from the hour it exists. An engineer who cannot say which currency they are spending cannot defend an operating point.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3's curves. Point 2 is the day's central explanation. Point 5 makes non-linear saturation a class rather than two coincidences, which is the module's most important generalisation. Point 8 is the day's perishable platform-specific point and its named default, and it is also a documentation silence stated as one. Point 9 is the operating point [S058](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S058.md), [S062](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S062.md) and [S064](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S064.md) inherit, and the two-currency form is what makes it survive the move to those days.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 2. The operating point is not given. This phase produces a build.**

Students use Copilot in agent mode, through the Azure MCP Server in read configuration, to ship a client that holds a stated operating point against the substrate's real traffic. They:

1. **Measure its current utilisation, throughput and latency distribution** — reported **separately for requests that succeeded on the first attempt and requests that were retried**. The two are different quantities produced by different mechanisms, and a single distribution over both is a number with no owner.
2. **Locate the knee** — predict, then find it by loading the deployment in a controlled way.
3. **State the target.** Which quantity matters for this service's callers, and which caller said so.
4. **Choose an operating point,** and state the headroom it buys in requests per second and in weeks of growth.
5. **State what the choice costs, and in which currency** — capacity paid for and not used, or allocation denied to another deployment.
6. **Measure the variability of both the request stream and the arrival stream,** and state how much of the knee's position each accounts for.
7. **State the leading signal** for approaching the knee — and, unlike S018, **state a second signal and say which is better and why.**

Step 6's two-sided requirement is the fix [WP-019](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-019.md) §8 named and did not make. Step 7's two-signal comparison is the improvement [WP-018](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-018.md) §8 identified. The platform supplies at least three candidate signals with different lead times — the remaining-token and remaining-request headers, the 429 rate split by status code, and the platform metrics retrieved through the Azure MCP Server — and the comparison is the deliverable, not the choice.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave, in order, including the ones you abandoned.
- **Intervention record.** Every correction you made. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a load generator that reports its own arrival-window distribution rather than only its mean rate is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch.

**Fading:** step 2's loading procedure is supplied; step 7's first signal may be taken from S018's work.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 2, now shipping rather than describing. Step 6 makes variability a measured quantity on both sides rather than a mentioned one on one. Step 7 converts an impossible task into a tractable comparison. Step 5's currency clause is what stops the operating point being a number with no owner.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate capacity decision made on mean utilisation that was correct on average and failed under a burst — with attention to what the arrival pattern actually looked like versus what the planning assumed.

Then the agent case: a Copilot-generated capacity probe that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. Asked to find the highest sustainable request rate, it ramped smoothly, reported the last rate at which no request failed, and produced a number that was correct for the traffic it generated and roughly double what the real bursty traffic could sustain. It reviewed well because the method was sound and the arithmetic was right. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the defect was in the workload the agent chose to generate**, which was not part of the answer anyone read.

Then students: a time you reasoned about a system using an average of something that varies, or a place today where you accepted a number without asking what traffic produced it.

**Warrant.** Error management. The instructor disclosure targets the arrival-variability blind spot specifically, which is the part of phase 3 that transfers least readily. The agent case is the same blind spot in the instrument rather than in the system, and it is chosen because a generated benchmark's workload is the part of an agent's output that reviewers reliably skip.

---

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents their step 4 operating point to their partner, who plays **the person who pays for the capacity** and whose only question is: why not run it closer to saturation and save the money?

The student must answer with the curve, not with a principle.

On a standard deployment the honest first answer is uncomfortable and the partner is briefed to press it: **the headroom is not costing you money.** The student must then find the real currency — the allocation another deployment did not get, the tail the callers experience, the burst the mean concealed — and defend the operating point in that currency instead.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with a role demand. This is the direct rehearsal of S020's assessment occasion, one day early, and the first time a student defends a cost position to someone with an opposing interest. The briefed follow-up is what prevents the phase from being won by reciting a price.

## When it does not work

These are [WP-019](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-019.md) §5's predicted failure modes, restated as instruction to the person in the room. The whitepaper is the authority; where this section and §5 diverge, §5 is right.

**A student who has taken a queueing or performance-modelling course predicts items 1 and 2 correctly.** They are answering from a memorised model rather than from the compounding mechanism, and the model is usually one that assumes exponential service — so it does not distinguish the variability condition from the base case, and phase 3's second condition is where their advantage disappears. Do not pre-empt it. When it happens, ask them at phase 4 which of their model's assumptions the condition violates.

**A student with no relevant background meets the module's hardest explanation on its fourth consecutive high-load day.** Five things already reduce the load: nothing is derived, the curves are measured, one arithmetic relation is supplied, the loading procedure is given, and step 7's first signal may be reused from [S018](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S018.md). *Residual risk the design accepts:* step 6's attribution of the knee's position to variability may be beyond them. The stated fallback is that they report the measured variance without attributing its effect — that is an honest artefact and it passes.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Not a live risk on this day. The mean curve is sketched wrongly by most, the tail curve by nearly all, and the variability condition by essentially everyone. If a group does sketch all three correctly, ask them to predict the effect of reducing variability while raising utilisation so that mean response time stays constant.

**The cohort arrives flat, and this is the fourth consecutive day of being wrong.** Wrong about layouts, wrong about schedules, wrong about the memory curve, and about to be wrong about queues — with an assessment tomorrow. This is a module property rather than a day property and its countermeasures are structural rather than motivational. **The day resolves completely**, which none of the previous three did: point 2 fully accounts for the measured curves and nothing is deferred, so say that it is resolving. Phase 7 hands the student a defensible position to hold rather than another error to absorb. And [S020](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S020.md) is a defence of their own argument, not a further correction — which is worth saying out loud today rather than tomorrow. The design's position is that the module's shape is correct and that the risk is managed by this day resolving, not by lowering its demands.

## Assessment

Specification item in SY102. Checked elements: measured utilisation, throughput and latency distribution **split by first attempt and retry**; predicted and located knee; stated target with the caller who stated it; operating point with headroom in both units; the cost of the headroom **and the currency it is paid in**; measured variability of the request stream and of the arrival stream, each with its contribution to the knee's position; two compared signals; the prompt record; the intervention record; the frontier statement.

**An operating point defended without reference to the measured curve fails the specification.** **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

Nothing else is assessed.

## What this day does not establish

**Demonstrated.** The full response-time distribution and the 429 rate at seven demand levels on one allocation, measured by the room. That a stream whose per-minute rate is identical to another's is refused where the other is comfortable. That variability on either the request side or the arrival side degrades behaviour near saturation and does so invisibly to any mean.

**Read rather than demonstrated.** The enforcement window — documented as a small period, with **which one applies to your deployment not published**, and the day treats that silence as load-bearing rather than as an inconvenience. Also the shared-pool reduction, the finest published time grain of the metrics, and the two billing currencies at point 9.

**Asserted, and conceded.** Point 2's compounding explanation is a correct account of a queue and [WP-019](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-019.md) concedes that **it is not what most of what the room measures actually is.** A request over the limit is not queued, it is turned away, and much of the recorded delay is the pause the student's own client chose before trying again. Step 1's first-attempt-versus-retry split is the design's answer and it works on the student's own data; the third component, the shared pool, remains invisible and is recorded as invisible.

**What rests on a `research/` note.** The utilisation-latency relationship, variability's effect near saturation and tail-latency reporting, at [research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md); the signal-comparison discipline at [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md). The first records that it was verified against an abstract page and that several of its mechanisms were not read heading by heading.

**What rests on nothing.** The claim the day opens with. That intuition extrapolates queues linearly, and is therefore wrong by kind rather than by degree, is an empirical claim about people and **this repository holds no note for it** — nor for contrasting cases across two dimensions, prediction before adjudication, sketching as externalisation, or cognitive load. [WP-019](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-019.md) §8 also concedes that this is the fifth time in one quarter a practice has been required ahead of the instruction justifying it, and argues the ordering is least-bad — an argument about learning, made from recall, in a paper whose own rule is that such claims trace to a note. **One item is worse than the rest:** error management training carries phase 6 and is cited from general knowledge, while [research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md) records a complete reading.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **Latency diverges as utilisation approaches capacity, and an average rate is not a rate.** Throughput, latency and utilisation are three quantities rather than three views of one, and where you choose to sit on the curve is a bet paid in a currency you must be able to name.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** The utilisation-latency relationship, variability's effect near saturation and tail-latency reporting are verified in this repository at [research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md), and the signal-comparison discipline follows [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md). The learning mechanisms are cited for **direction and mechanism only and no magnitude is asserted.** The vendor documentation gives the enforcement window as a small period and **does not say which one applies to your deployment**; that silence is load-bearing, and no frequency of any kind is published.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- Report latency split by first attempt and by retry. One distribution over both is a number with no owner.
- Measure variability on both sides — what you send, and when you send it.
- Say which currency your headroom is paid in: money, or somebody else's allocation.
- **Classify what a capacity probe gives you** into the five classes on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). A sustainable rate found by ramping smoothly is an *observed result* about the traffic you generated, not *outcome evidence* about the traffic you serve.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Treating utilisation as a fraction of a fixed denominator. On a shared deployment the denominator moves without your quota changing, and the burst that throttled you is shorter than the finest window in which you can observe yourself.
