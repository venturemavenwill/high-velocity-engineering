## What this day is for

[S046](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S046.md) established that a criterion is constructed by named people, from evidence, for a purpose, and that a criterion nobody recognises fails.

**A preference dataset is a criterion with all of that and none of it recorded.** Someone chose the annotators. Someone wrote instructions. The annotators then made thousands of pairwise choices which express a criterion nobody has written down and which does not match the instructions. That criterion is then compiled into weights.

[S048](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S048.md) established that every judge has a systematic error component that does not average out. A preference dataset is a pile of judgements. **The systematic component did not go anywhere — it became the target.**

Today that argument arrives as a file format. The platform offers three fine-tuning techniques — supervised, direct preference optimisation, and reinforcement — and the preference one takes a `jsonl` file with three fields per record: the input, the preferred output, and the non-preferred output. **The documentation states the constraint plainly: you can only provide two completions.** Whatever your criterion actually is, the format will accept it only as a sequence of binary choices, and the compression from *what you wanted* to *this one is better than that one* is a decision the file has no field for. The vendor's own account of why teams reach for this technique is worth reading before phase 1: organisations that struggle to assemble a supervised dataset *often have preference data already collected based on user logs, A/B tests, or smaller manual annotation efforts.* **The easiest data to obtain is the data nobody constructed on purpose.**

The target for the day: **optimisation selects for whatever the signal rewards, the signal is the annotators' actual choices rather than anyone's stated intent, and "aligned" without naming the preference distribution, the annotators and the instructions is a word carrying no information.**

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Twenty output pairs from a real preference dataset are supplied, with the preferred member of each marked.

1. **What criterion do these choices express?** Write it as one sentence.
2. Here are **the annotator instructions.** Does your sentence match them?
3. Here are **five more pairs where both your sentence and the written instructions disagree with the choice made.** What actually got selected?
4. Predict: **which unstated property is most strongly present in the preferred outputs?**
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to turn a plain-English criterion into a valid preference training file. Write down what it will produce, and **name the one thing it will get wrong.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 4's predictions are collected and checked at phase 8 against the measured property distribution.

**Warrant.** Pretesting on inference from choices, then supplying the instructions, then supplying choices that defeat both. Item 3 is the day's opening finding and it requires no framework: **the written criterion and the operative criterion are different objects**, and the difference is visible in five pairs. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8; the design's expectation is that the agent will produce a schema-valid file whose generation procedure introduces a systematic difference between the two completions that has nothing to do with the criterion, which is the day's whole subject arriving as a tooling artefact.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Four preference-tuned deployments exist in the Foundry project. Each was produced by **one preference fine-tuning technique applied to one base model over four preference datasets**, in the platform's own training format, with everything except the preference data held fixed. Groups query all four through the Foundry MCP Server over a fixed probe set and read the outputs themselves.

- **P1** — domain experts, instructed for correctness.
- **P2** — a general crowd, instructed for helpfulness.
- **P3** — the substrate's own users, implicit preference from acceptance behaviour, no instructions at all.
- **P4** — model-generated preferences, judged by the [S048](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S048.md) judge the cohort characterised.

Groups characterise each resulting model's shape from outputs alone and commit. **Then the annotator populations and instructions are revealed**, together with the four training files, which the groups can read.

**Each observed behaviour admits at least two explanations** — the annotator population, or the optimisation dynamics, or the base model. Groups are required to produce **two explanations for each behaviour they name, and to state what would distinguish them.**

The payload is **P4**. Its model scores highest of the four on the S048 judge and worst on the domain experts' criterion. The proxy has been optimised into.

The second finding is in the files rather than the outputs, and it is the one the platform supplies. **All four datasets have exactly two completions per record, because the format permits exactly two.** P1's experts, asked for correctness, frequently wanted to say *neither of these is right*; P3's users, expressing preference by acceptance, never expressed a preference between two things at all — their behaviour was reconstructed into pairs by whoever built the file. **A criterion that was a ranking, or a rejection, or a continuous judgement has been compressed into a binary choice, and the compression is not recorded anywhere in the artefact.** Groups are asked to find one record in each file where they can tell the compression happened.

Task after: **for each of the four, name one property that got selected for that nobody asked for.**

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across annotator populations with the optimisation held fixed, so that variation is attributable to the preference signal alone, now run against four real deployments rather than read from supplied outputs. **The two-explanations requirement is this day's discharge of the obligation [S052](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S052.md) placed on it under instrumentation rule 6**, and it is a required output of the phase rather than a prompt. **The training-file inspection is the phase's platform payload and is new.** It converts the day's central claim — that a criterion is compiled into weights with its construction discarded — from an argument into a file the students can open, and the two-completion constraint is the discarding made visible: whatever else was lost, that particular loss is enforced by a schema and is legible in every record.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **A preference dataset is a criterion nobody wrote down.** Everything [S046](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S046.md) required — named people, what each wanted, what was traded — exists here and is unrecorded.

2. **The annotators' systematic component becomes the training target.** [S048](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S048.md) point 2, at the point where it stops being an error you report and becomes an objective you pursue.

3. **The written instructions are not the criterion.** The criterion is what the choices express. Item 3 measures the gap, and the gap is not small.

4. **A reward model is a judge you then optimise against.** Every property from S048 applies, and optimisation actively searches for its errors — which no ordinary use of a judge does.

5. **Optimising against a proxy improves the true objective, then stops, then harms it.** Finding that turning point requires an evaluation the proxy does not control. If your only measure is the thing you are optimising, you cannot see the turn.

6. **What gets selected for that nobody asked:** length, confident tone, formatting, refusal patterns, and **agreement with the user's premise**. Agreement is the canonical case — annotators prefer being agreed with, optimisation selects for agreement, and the resulting system agrees with false premises. Nobody wrote that instruction and everybody produced it.

7. **"Aligned" is a two-place word.** Aligned *to what*, from *whom*, under *what instructions*. A system described as aligned without all three has been described with a word carrying no information, and the [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md) standard applies to anyone who uses it that way in front of a decision-maker.

8. **A behaviour after preference optimisation has at least three candidate sources:** the annotator population, the optimisation dynamics, and the base model. Distinguishing them requires intervention, not inspection — [S052](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S052.md) point 4, unchanged.

9. **The platform's three techniques encode three different theories of what you have.** Supervised fine-tuning trains on labelled input-output pairs and suits the case where there are finite ways of solving the problem. Preference optimisation adjusts weights from binary comparisons and does not require fitting a separate reward model, which is why it is computationally lighter — and the documentation recommends it precisely where *there's no clear-cut correct answer, and subjective elements like tone, style, or specific content preferences are important.* Reinforcement fine-tuning uses reward signals from graders and suits the case where there are many possible ways to solve a problem. The three can be stacked, supervised first and preference second. **Choosing among them is choosing what kind of thing your criterion is**, and a team that reaches for the preference route because it is the one whose data they already have has let the data choose the theory.

10. **Perishable, and marked as such.** Preference fine-tuning is a preview capability on this platform, available on a named list of base models that has changed twice inside the writing of this page and is region-limited. The three technique names, the training tiers, the file schema and the hyperparameter names are all this vendor's, at this date. **None of them is what the specification assesses.** The durable content is points 1 to 8, none of which mentions a vendor.

11. **Defaults are opinions, and this one has four that matter.** The training file format admits **exactly two completions**, so the shape of your criterion was constrained before you wrote a line of it. The optimisation's strength-of-attachment hyperparameter — documented as increasing the weight of the penalty between the tuned model and the one it started from — has a default, and the documentation recommends the defaults for initial experimentation, which means **most teams ship a value nobody chose.** The training tier defaults matter too: one tier keeps your data in region, one is cheaper and faster, and one is pre-emptible, and the difference is a data-residency decision wearing a performance label. And the fine-tuned model's own safety evaluation runs against **filters set to predefined thresholds that cannot be modified by customers** — a criterion applied to your criterion, by someone else, at a threshold you cannot see. An engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Point 5 is the mechanical content. Point 6 is what students retain. Point 7 is the day's most portable sentence and the one that will be used most often in rooms outside this programme. **Point 9 is the day's platform mechanism**, and is placed after point 8 so that the technique choice reads as a consequence of the attribution problem rather than as a menu. **Point 10 is the required perishable platform point and point 11 the required naming of defaults**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md); point 11's first default is the day's argument in schema form and its last is point 4 recursing — the thing that judges your judge is itself a judge with an unstated threshold.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 4. The criterion, the preference set and the detection are the student's. This phase produces a build.**

Students use Copilot in agent mode, through the Foundry MCP Server, to ship a validated preference training set against the substrate.

1. **State what you want the system to do**, as a criterion, traceable in [S046](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S046.md)'s sense.
2. **Construct a preference set** on the substrate expressing it — at least forty pairs, annotated by you, **with your instructions written before you annotate**, emitted in the platform's own training format and validated against it.
3. **Predict what your set will select for that you did not intend.**
4. **Have a peer group annotate twenty of your pairs under your written instructions,** and measure the disagreement.
5. **Characterise the implied criterion from the choices,** not from the instructions.
6. **Name one property your set selects for that nobody asked for,** with evidence. **It must be a property not on the point 6 list.**
7. **State the evaluation that would detect over-optimisation** against your criterion, **and why that evaluation is not controlled by the criterion.**
8. **State what your criterion lost in the compression to two completions**, with one record from your own file where the loss is visible.

**Submitting a training job is demonstrated rather than operated.** Preference fine-tuning is preview, is available on a named list of base models, and is subject to regional and quota availability the programme does not control. Where the tenant holds it, the four phase 3 runs are shown being submitted and one student's set is submitted live. **Where it does not, the submission is demonstrated and no part of it is assessed.** The assessed artefact is the dataset, its validation, and the analysis — none of which requires the job to run.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned. **This one is unusually pointed**, because the day's subject is what happens when instructions and outputs diverge and the record is your own instance of it.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the build's own checks would have caught an intervention-worthy error — a validator that rejects a file whose non-preferred outputs are systematically shorter than its preferred ones is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch.

**Fading:** step 2 is supported by a pair-generation harness and the instruction-writing is worked with the room. **Step 3 is unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 4 in which the student occupies every role the day critiques — instruction-writer, annotator, and the person who later has to say what the thing was optimised for. **Step 4 uses an external observer rather than self-report, per instrumentation rule 1**: a peer group is present for other reasons and the disagreement is theirs to measure. Step 6's added constraint answers [WP-054](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8's conceded contamination: a property drawn from the taught list is a recall exercise, and requiring one that is not forces analysis of the student's own annotations. **Step 8 is new and is the platform's contribution to the day's central argument** — it converts point 11's first default from something the student was told into something they can point at in a file they wrote. Step 7 is the discriminator, and the second clause is the hard part.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate assistant tuned on user acceptance signals. It became agreeable, stopped saying *I don't know*, and its satisfaction metric rose for six weeks while its task-completion metric fell. Attention is directed at one fact — **the satisfaction metric was the one on the dashboard**, and the completion metric was available the whole time.

Then the agent case, from this day's own build. A student asked Copilot to generate the non-preferred half of a preference file: take each good answer and produce a worse one. **The agent produced a clean generator, a valid file, and forty well-formed records that passed every schema check.** It also, without being asked and without saying so, produced worse answers by making them shorter and blunter — because that is the cheapest way to make an answer worse and nothing in the request forbade it.

The file was valid. The criterion it encoded was *prefer the longer, more elaborate answer*, which is not what the student wrote and is not what they wanted. Nobody could have seen it from a record; it is only visible across the set.

Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the student reviewed the file the way a file is reviewed — record by record — and the defect exists only in the distribution.** Every individual pair was defensible. This is point 3 arriving in the student's own artefact, and it is the reason step 6 requires a property found rather than recalled.

Then students, in threes: a time you gave feedback that rewarded something you did not intend to reward, or a place today where a valid artefact encoded a criterion you did not write.

**Warrant.** Error management targeting points 3 and 5. The six-week gap in the instructor case is its content; the failure was not that the turn was invisible but that only one side of it was being watched. **The agent case is the required addition and is chosen because the defect is distributional**, which is the one class of defect that record-level review cannot reach and which the day has just spent fifty minutes explaining. It is also the failure item 5 predicts, so the clinic and the sealed prediction meet.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **their step 6 unintended property and their step 7 detection evaluation.**

The partner's task: **propose an alternative explanation for that property** — the base model rather than the preference set, or the optimisation dynamics rather than either — and **name the intervention that would distinguish them.**

Per rule 3, the outcome is recorded in the artefact.

Per rule 5 as amended at [WP-052](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-052.md) §8, this structure is **proposed, not validated**: it was introduced at [S052](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S052.md) and has not yet been run. **Its use here is contingent** — if S052's outcome shows the distinguishing-intervention step failing more often than succeeding, this phase is retracted along with it.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation applying point 8 to the student's own claim. The phase is the second consecutive day on which the student's preferred explanation is attacked rather than their evidence, which is the module's standing method for a domain where every artefact is suggestive and none is conclusive.

## When it does not work

The predicted failures and their countermeasures are argued in [WP-054](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §5. Restated here as instruction to the person in the room. Nothing below is assessed.

| What you see | What to do | What the design accepts |
|---|---|---|
| A student who has read alignment work names agreement at step 3, finds it at step 6, and produces the expected answer having learned nothing about their own set | Push them onto step 7's second clause — why the detection evaluation is not controlled by the criterion it checks — which is not answerable from reading. Then let phase 7's partner attack the attribution rather than the finding | **§5 records that §8 objection 2 argues this countermeasure is insufficient**, and it is the only case on the day where the paper says so of its own remedy. Treat the outcome as unresolved rather than handled |
| A student is drowning in step 2's volume | Give them the pair-generation harness, work the instruction-writing with the room, and hold the numbers where the design set them: forty pairs rather than a realistic number, and peer annotation capped at twenty | §5 treats this group as covered by the harness and the reduced volume |
| A group characterises P1 and P2 quickly | Expected. The difficulty is P3, whose criterion has to be recovered entirely from behaviour because it has no instructions at all, and P4, whose model looks best by the measure the students trust least. Fallback if the group has both: ask which of the four they would deploy on the substrate today, which forces the trade rather than the characterisation | P1 and P2 are meant to be quick. They establish the baseline against which P3's absence and P4's inversion register |
| The room concludes that alignment work is theatre — every preference set encodes an unrecorded criterion, optimisation finds the proxy's errors, and *aligned* means little | Point at P1, which is better than the others on the criterion its annotators were instructed for: careful construction works and the day demonstrates it. State point 5 as a turn with a detectable location rather than as futility. Hold step 7 to a constructive answer rather than a critique. Say once that the day's argument is for doing this work explicitly rather than for not doing it | **§5 names cynicism about the whole enterprise as the characteristic affective failure**, calls the misreading serious, and records that the day creates the conditions for it. The countermeasures are stated as adequate and the risk is live |

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in IS202 with ME202. Per [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the traceable criterion; the preference set with instructions written before annotation, emitted and validated in the platform's training format; the pre-committed prediction; the measured peer disagreement; the implied criterion characterised from choices; one unintended selected property with evidence, not drawn from the point 6 list; the over-optimisation detection evaluation with its independence argued; the compression statement with its record; the phase 7 outcome; **the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

**Submitting a training job is not assessed.** Preference fine-tuning is preview and its availability is outside the programme's control; where the tenant does not hold it, the submission is demonstrated rather than operated and nothing turns on it.

**A detection evaluation controlled by the criterion it is meant to check fails.** This is the day's single hard requirement and it is step 7's second clause.

**Instructions written after annotation fail.** The order is the point.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

No entrustment judgement. The quarter's is at [S060](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S060.md).

**Rule-6 discharge.** [S052](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S052.md) named this day as the response occasion for its phase 7 alternative-explanation rate, specifying that phase 3's datasets be constructed to admit multiple explanations of one outcome. **The element exists and is a required output: phase 3 requires two explanations and a distinguishing test for every behaviour named.** The obligation is discharged.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named occasions:

- **The step 4 peer-disagreement distribution** measures how far two people diverge under identical written instructions, and is the cohort's most direct evidence for point 3. **Response occasion: [S056](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S056.md), phase 5, where relevance judgements are collected and the same disagreement is measured on a retrieval task.**
- **The step 3-against-step 6 prediction accuracy** measures whether students can anticipate what their own criterion selects for. **Response occasion: [S060](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S060.md), the entrustment day, whose failure-mode statement must include one unintended selection property.**

Item 5's hit rate joins the running frontier map, per instrumentation rule 9, and is read by the following year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md).

| **Preference set** | A pile of pairwise choices. The day's central claim is that it is a criterion in [S046](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S046.md)'s sense — named people, evidence, purpose — with all of that unrecorded |
| **Implied criterion** | What the choices express, as against what the instructions say. Step 5 characterises the first; item 3 measures the gap between the two |
| **Reward model** | A judge you then optimise against. Every property from [S048](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S048.md) applies, and optimisation actively searches for its errors, which no ordinary use of a judge does |
| **Over-optimisation, and the turn** | Point 5: optimising a proxy improves the true objective, then stops, then harms it. Step 7 asks for the evaluation that would detect the turn, and for why that evaluation is not controlled by the criterion |
| **The two-completion constraint** | The training format admits exactly two completions per record. It is the day's compression argument in schema form, it is the vendor's own constraint reported rather than adopted, and step 8 makes it assessable |
| **Demonstrated rather than operated** | Rule 3 of the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md). **Submitting a training job is this day's instance**, and the specification says so explicitly |
| **Instrumentation rule 1** | *Do not use self-report where an external observer is already present.* Step 4 uses a peer group to measure disagreement for that reason. The nine rules are on the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](/archive/bsc-programme/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) |
| **Instrumentation rule 5, as amended twice** | A remedy is validated or proposed; at design time a proposed remedy may be reused provided each reuse is recorded as contingent, and one reported failure retracts every contingent reuse at once. **Phase 7 is contingent under it**, on [S052](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S052.md)'s outcome |
| **Instrumentation rule 6** | *Where an instrument's response is deferred, the deferral must name the occasion.* Phase 3's two-explanations requirement discharges [S052](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S052.md)'s obligation, and this day names two of its own |
| **Instrumentation rule 8** | *An instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.* It is why item 5 is sealed at phase 1 |
| **Prompt record** | Three binary checks: the instructions given and their order; the reason for each; and the instructions abandoned. **Unusually pointed here**, because the day's subject is what happens when instructions and outputs diverge — [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) |
| **Intervention record** | Two binary checks: every correction present with what it was, and the record not empty. Empty fails unless the artefact shows the build's own checks could have caught an intervention-worthy error — here, a validator rejecting a file whose non-preferred outputs are systematically shorter |
| **Frontier statement** | Two binary checks: both halves specific, and both drawn from this build. Whether the judgement is correct is not assessed |
| **Entrustment** | A decision rather than a measurement, whose output is an ordinal supervision level. **This day produces none**; the quarter's is at [S060](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S060.md) |
| **The five classes** | Configured control, executable check, observed result, outcome evidence, unsupported inference — [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). Written instructions are the first, and the day is the gap between it and the third |

## What this day does not establish

**What it demonstrates.** Three things, all narrow. That four deployments differing only in their preference data behaved differently on one probe set. That every record in all four training files carried exactly two completions, and that the compression is visible in specific records. And that the cohort's own peer annotators, following the students' own written instructions, disagreed at the rate they disagreed at, on twenty pairs each. **One project, one base model, one technique, one cohort. No magnitude is claimed for any preference effect, disagreement rate or over-optimisation turning point.**

**What it does not demonstrate.** That P4's inversion — best on the [S048](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S048.md) judge, worst on the experts' criterion — is the general behaviour of model-generated preferences. It is one construction, on one substrate, against one judge the cohort itself characterised. Phase 3's own two-explanations requirement exists because each observed behaviour admits the annotator population, the optimisation dynamics or the base model as a cause, and the phase does not adjudicate between them.

**What rests on a note.** Preference data as an implicit criterion, the divergence between annotator instructions and annotator behaviour, reward models as judges, over-optimisation against a learned proxy, and unintended selected properties including agreement with the user's premise — at [research/02-technical-foundations/ouyang-instructgpt.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/ouyang-instructgpt.md) and [research/02-technical-foundations/rafailov-dpo.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/rafailov-dpo.md). **This is the day about preference optimisation and it cites both preference-optimisation notes**, which is worth recording because it is not true of every day in this range.

**What it asserts.** Points 9, 10 and 11 are vendor documentation, read at one date, about a preview capability. The two-completion constraint is reported rather than endorsed. Nothing in the day's platform layer establishes how any team's criterion actually survives compilation — that is what step 8 asks each student to find in their own file.

**What rests on nothing verified here.** The phase warrants lean on retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, pretesting and cognitive load, and **none of the five has a note anywhere under [research/](/research/99-source-register/source-register.md)**. Productive failure and error management do, at [sinha-kapur](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md) and [keith-frese](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md); both supply direction and mechanism and no magnitude.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** Optimisation selects for whatever the signal rewards, and the signal is the annotators' actual choices rather than anyone's stated intent. **A preference dataset is a criterion nobody wrote down**, and *aligned* is a two-place word — aligned to what, from whom, under what instructions. Without all three it carries no information.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** Preference data as an implicit criterion, the divergence between annotator instructions and annotator behaviour, reward models as judges, over-optimisation against a learned proxy, and unintended selected properties including agreement with the user's premise are verified at [research/02-technical-foundations/ouyang-instructgpt.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/ouyang-instructgpt.md) and [research/02-technical-foundations/rafailov-dpo.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/rafailov-dpo.md). **No magnitude is asserted for any preference effect, disagreement rate or over-optimisation turning point.** The two-completion constraint is the vendor's own, reported rather than adopted.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Write your annotation instructions before you annotate**, then characterise the criterion from the choices rather than from the instructions.
- **Name one property your set selects for that nobody asked for**, found in your own annotations rather than recalled from a list.
- **State what your criterion lost when it was compressed into pairs** — a ranking, a tie, or the option to reject both.
- **Written instructions are a configured control.** They establish that a constraint was stated and nothing about whether it was applied, and the gap between that row and an observed result on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md)'s ladder is this whole day.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Reviewing a preference file record by record. **Every individual pair was defensible and the defect existed only in the distribution.**
