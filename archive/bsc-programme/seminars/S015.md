# S015 · Choosing a Representation for a Caller You Have Met

**Module:** [M03](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M03.md) · **Quarter:** [Q2](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q2.md) · **Complexity class:** 1 → 2
**Strands:** CF · PE · SY
**Whitepaper:** [WP-015](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-015.md)
**Depends on:** S006, S011, S014
**Re-tests:** S009

**Platform anchor:** the module's four candidate representations run against the caller's derived mix, one of them a Foundry embedding deployment; a repository custom instructions file as the specification that produces the caller's artefact
**MCP servers:** Microsoft Learn MCP Server (grounding) · Azure MCP Server, Read only = true — used to establish what the caller's component actually runs on

---

## What this day is for

This is the complexity-class boundary. Every prior day gave the method; this day gives the task and withholds the method, and the student must choose and defend.

The module's four preceding days each supplied one input to the choice: an operation mix, a comparison cost, a claim type, a problem shape. None of them required a decision. Today does, and the criterion comes from a person — **a real substrate caller, in the room, who states their own requirements and answers questions.**

The design's position is that a first choice made against a real caller's stated needs is a different act from a first choice made against a written brief, and that the difference is what makes the class transition survivable.

The day's second demand is the one PE102 has been building toward. The artefact the caller receives is produced by an agent, and **the thing the student actually writes is the specification that produces it.** A repository custom instructions file is a specification whose conformance is checked by execution rather than by reading. A student who can write a specification and cannot write an instructions file that produces conforming work has not understood what a specification is for — and on this day they find out within ninety minutes rather than at a review months later.

## Before you run this day

**Materials to prepare.** None of the following exists in this repository. The caller is the day, and everything else on the list is arrangeable at short notice while the caller is not.

- **A real substrate caller, present in the room** — a person whose own work depends on the component in question. Not a role-play and not a member of staff. They attend for phase 3 and **return for the last ten minutes of phase 8**, so secure both windows.
- **The caller's briefing.** They describe their work rather than their requirements; they do not volunteer quantities; they answer honestly including "I don't know" and "that has never come up"; and they are never asked which representation they want, because they will answer and the answer will be worthless.
- **A second workflow the caller has not described**, held in reserve for the case where they volunteer frequencies anyway and trivialise the derivation.
- **The phase 1 brief** — a one-paragraph description of a caller's needs, deliberately insufficient, with the insufficiency undisclosed.
- **An allocation of phase 3 questions.** Every student asks at least one, allocated in advance, and the first five questions are assigned rather than volunteered.
- **The four candidate representations, already built earlier in the module** and re-runnable against a mix supplied on the day. They are re-run at step 4, not re-implemented; if they were not kept from S011 to S014, step 4 does not fit in ninety minutes.
- **The instructions file skeleton**: rules named, content empty.
- **The phase 6 clinic pair**: the substrate choice where the caller's description was accurate and the derived mix was wrong, with the record of *which question was not asked*; and the agent case — the file saying "avoid jargon", the fluent page using the word *embedding* four times, and the peer review that read for tone.
- **The subtractive diagnostic**, for a student whose first generated page conforms: ask them to name a rule they could delete without changing the output.
- **Three anonymised step 8 answers**, chosen and prepared during the day, for the caller to read at phase 8.

**Tooling and access to verify beforehand.**

- The Azure MCP Server with **Read only = true**, and read access to the resource group, deployment or index the caller's component actually uses. Step 3 fails without it, and a choice defended against an assumed estate is defended against nothing.
- The Microsoft Learn MCP Server.
- Copilot agent mode configured to pick up a **repository custom instructions file**, since that file is the deliverable the student authors and its effect must be observable within the phase.
- The Foundry embedding deployment used as the fourth representation, reachable as at [S011](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S011.md).
- An environment in which the four representations can be run against the derived mix inside step 4's budget.

**What is assumed to exist from earlier days.**

- **The four module inputs, unassembled**: the operation mix from [S011](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S011.md), the comparison cost from [S012](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S012.md), the claim type from [S013](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S013.md), the problem shape from [S014](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S014.md). Point 4 is the only place in the module they are presented as one method.
- **S006's specification rules, eight weeks old and never yet checked by execution.** The whole of point 7 and step 8 rest on them; this is the day they stop being read and start being run.
- **The S007 bet framing**, on its fourth application, used at point 6 and step 7 without re-teaching.
- **PE102's contextual-inquiry material, running concurrently.** This is the module's only within-quarter cross-course dependency and it is load-bearing at phase 3. Confirm PE102 has delivered it.
- **Copilot agent mode used for four days on this module's builds, and never steered by a written rule set.** The instructions file is the first, and the day depends on that being new.
- No experience of an underdetermined criterion. Every prior task had a determinate right answer, and this one does not.

**If a prerequisite is missing.**

- *The caller volunteers frequencies unprompted.* Introduce the reserve workflow they have not described, which restores the derivation demand.
- *The generated page conforms on the first attempt.* The specification lesson is otherwise lost, because a rule that is never violated teaches nothing about what it checks. Ask for a rule the student could delete without changing the output. It is the same diagnostic subtractively and it is harder.
- *A student stalls at step 1 with the fading removed.* Your mid-phase check is the main mitigation. [WP-015](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-015.md) §5 names this the least-supported point in the module and accepts it as the transition's cost; no further support is supplied.
- *No real caller can attend.* The design refuses both substitutes explicitly — not a role-play, not a member of staff — and [WP-015](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-015.md) offers no fallback. None is invented here. Move the day.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Before the caller arrives.

1. Here is a one-paragraph description of a caller's needs. Choose a representation and justify it in three sentences.
2. What would you need to ask them to be confident?
3. What will they say that you have not anticipated?
4. Predict: will your item 1 choice survive the conversation?

Item 3 is the day's opening move and item 4 is its calibration. Most students predict their choice will survive. Most choices do not.

**Copilot prediction, sealed.** *In phase 5 you will write an instructions file intended to make the agent produce a one-page answer in the caller's language. Write down now: which of your instructions will it follow, which will it satisfy in letter and not in substance, and what will it put in that you did not ask for?* Opened at phase 8.

**Warrant.** Pretesting under deliberately insufficient information, with the insufficiency undisclosed. Item 2 is the generation demand and is what the conversation is then measured against. The Copilot item is the day's specification-conformance prediction and is sealed before the caller arrives, so that it cannot be revised in the light of the requirements.

## Phase 3 · The caller (45 min)

**A real substrate caller attends** — a person whose work depends on the component in question. Not a role-play and not a staff member.

Structure:

1. **They describe what they do,** for ten minutes, without being asked about technology.
2. **Students ask questions,** for twenty-five minutes. The caller answers honestly, including "I don't know" and "that has never come up."
3. **Students write, individually and in silence for ten minutes,** the operation mix as they now understand it.

The caller does not know what representation options exist and is not asked to choose.

**On executed contrasting cases.** This phase replaces the pattern's contrasting-cases phase with an inquiry, so the day's four cases are **not run here**. They are run at phase 5 step 4, against the caller's derived mix, and the four are the module's four representations — hash index, sorted array, inverted term index, Foundry embedding deployment. The design states this rather than manufacturing a phase 3 that would require the caller to wait while students ran benchmarks.

**Warrant.** This replaces the contrasting-cases phase. The contrast is between the student's item 1 assumptions and what the caller actually said, and it is a contrast the student constructs rather than receives. Contextual inquiry practice from PE102 is used directly.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **What changed.** The room's item 1 choices are shown against the phase 3 mixes, unattributed. The gap is the instruction.
2. **What a caller can and cannot tell you.** They know their work and their pain; they do not know their operation frequencies and should not be asked for them directly.
3. **Deriving a mix from a description** — the technique, worked on one student's phase 3 notes with permission.
4. **The four inputs, assembled:** mix from S011, comparison cost from S012, claim type from S013, shape from S014. This is the module's synthesis and is stated as a procedure.
5. **What you owe the caller in return** — a stated claim, its scope, and what it costs them. S013's two reports, with only one caller.
6. **Choosing under residual uncertainty.** You will not know everything. Record what you assumed as a bet, in S007's sense, and state what would falsify it.
7. **An instructions file is a specification, and it is checked by execution.** S006's specification rules apply without modification. Rule 1: state what is actually checked — wording implying a quality judgement the agent cannot make will produce one. Rule 2: a qualification travels with the quantity it qualifies, in the same field — "use the caller's language" without "and no representation vocabulary" produces a document that does both. **A specification that produces non-conforming work is a defective specification**, and this is the first day in the programme where you find that out the same afternoon.
8. **What the platform chose for you, and what is perishable.** Three defaults decide part of your answer before you do. The Azure MCP Server's **Read only** option is off unless set, so an agent you believed was inspecting your estate could change it. Its **Mode** defaults to `namespace`, which determines which tools the agent can even see — a capability boundary you did not draw. And its **Learn mode**, off by default, would let the agent discover a tool's parameters without executing anything, which is exactly what you want while you are still deciding. **Safe** to reason with against this server today; **unsafe at any version other than the one in front of you**, since these are start-up options whose names and defaults carry no stability guarantee. *Defaults are opinions and thresholds are policy* — and on the day you first choose for somebody else, the point is that a default you did not examine is a choice you made on their behalf.

**Warrant.** Consolidation whose first act is showing the cohort its own pre-conversation assumptions. Point 4 is the module's assembly and the only place the four days are presented as one method. Point 7 makes the day's build a specification exercise rather than a prompting exercise, which is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s equivalence stated where PE102 can carry it. Point 8 is reachable from phase 5 step 3 rather than asserted.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 2. The method is not given. The output is a build.**

Students choose and defend a representation for the caller they have just met, and produce the caller's artefact by writing the specification that generates it.

1. **State the operation mix** you derived, and your confidence in each frequency.
2. **State the claim type** the caller needs, and why.
3. **Establish what the component actually runs on.** Use the Azure MCP Server, read-only, to confirm the resource group, the deployment or index the caller's component uses, and its configuration. A choice defended against an estate you assumed is a choice defended against nothing.
4. **Choose a representation, and run the four.** Any representation, including one not covered in the module — but the module's four are measured against your derived mix, so that the rejected alternatives at step 6 are rejected on evidence.
5. **State what it discards** and what it makes cheap.
6. **State the two alternatives you rejected** and the specific condition under which each would have won.
7. **State your bet** — the assumption you are least sure of — and what would falsify it.
8. **Write the instructions file, then generate the caller's answer.** The file is the deliverable you author. It must make Copilot produce one page, in the caller's language, saying what they are getting, what it guarantees and what it costs them. Run it. If the output does not conform, **the specification is defective and you fix the specification, not the output.** Both versions are kept.

**No fading on the choice.** Fading is retained around it: point 3's derivation is worked, the four candidate representations are supplied as a floor, and the instructions file has a supplied skeleton with its rules named and its content empty.

**Artefact elements.** As at S011 to S014, and assessed.

- **Prompt record** — the instructions given, in order, including abandoned ones. On this day the instructions file *is* part of the record, and every revision of it is an entry.
- **Intervention record** — every correction and what it was, **and for each one, whether you fixed the output or the specification.** **Empty fails**, unless the submitted file demonstrates a rule whose violation the student could have detected — which for step 8 means a rule about the caller's vocabulary that can be checked by reading the generated page.
- **Frontier statement** — one thing the agent did faster than you could have, one thing it did badly that you caught.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 2. Step 6's rejected-alternatives-with-conditions is what distinguishes a choice from a preference, and step 4's measurement is what keeps the conditions from being invented. Step 8 is the module's terminal artefact and is the first time the programme assesses a specification by executing it. The fix-the-specification rule is the day's hardest instruction and the one students most reliably violate.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a representation chosen in the substrate after talking to a caller, where the caller's description was accurate and the derived mix was wrong — with attention to which question was not asked.

**Then the agent case.** An instructions file that said *write for the reader, avoid jargon, be clear about limitations*. The generated page was fluent, warm, correctly structured, and used the word *embedding* four times, because "avoid jargon" is a quality judgement the agent cannot make and it made one — settling on a definition of jargon that excluded the vocabulary of the thing it had just built. The student read the page, found it clear, and shipped it. A peer reviewed it and agreed. **Both of them were reading for tone, because the instruction had been about tone.** Attention on the specification: the rule that would have caught it — *no representation vocabulary; if a term appears in the design and not in the caller's own words, it does not appear* — is checkable by anyone in ten seconds, and it is a different sentence from the one that was written.

Then students: a time you chose based on what someone said they needed.

**Warrant.** Error management targeting the day's specific risk, which is not choosing badly but deriving badly from an honest description. The agent case extends the same risk into the specification register: an instruction that sounds right and cannot be checked produces work that sounds right and is not, which is S006's rule 1 with a consequence attached.

---

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **step 6** — the rejected alternatives — and the partner's task is to argue for one of them, using only what the caller actually said and what step 4 measured.

If the partner wins, the student revises. Revision is expected and is not a failure.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with an adversarial demand constrained to the shared evidence. Constraining the argument to the caller's own words and the run measurements is what prevents the phase from becoming a preference contest.

---

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- Items 3 and 4 resolved: what proportion anticipated something the caller actually said, and what proportion's item 1 choice survived.
- **The Copilot prediction opened.** Which instructions the agent followed, which it satisfied in letter and not in substance, and what it added unasked — against what the room predicted. Recorded as this day's frontier-map entry and as the module's closing one.
- **The caller returns for the final ten minutes** and is read three anonymised step 8 answers, and says which one they would rather receive, and why.
- Each student records the question they wish they had asked.
- Retrieval schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, and the module's cumulative set in the inter-quarter gap.

---

- **Day 3:** the module's cumulative set.

## When it does not work

These are [WP-015](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-015.md) §5's predicted failure modes, restated as instruction to the person in the room. The whitepaper is the authority; where this section and §5 diverge, §5 is right.

**A technically strong student treats phase 3 as requirements-gathering to be got through.** The observable form is closed questions that confirm the choice they already made in phase 1, agreement obtained, and a derived mix that reflects their own assumptions rather than the caller's work. This is exactly the failure phase 6's first disclosure targets. Three countermeasures are already built in: the first ten minutes forbid technology questions entirely, phase 4 point 1 displays the cohort's pre-conversation choices against the derived mixes unattributed, and their phase 7 partner is constrained to the caller's own words and the step 4 measurements, which removes technical fluency as an argumentative advantage.

**A student conducts an excellent inquiry and cannot convert it into a defensible choice.** Point 3's worked derivation, the four familiar candidates as a floor, the instructions-file skeleton and the specification's weighting on step 6 all exist for them — step 6 rewards reasoning about alternatives rather than optimality of selection. *Residual risk the design accepts:* with fading removed from the choice, a student who stalls at step 1 has little to fall back on. Check mid-phase. §5 records this as the least-supported point in the module and accepts it as the cost of the class transition.

**The caller is unusually articulate and volunteers frequencies.** The risk here is the reverse of the usual one: the derivation step is trivialised rather than too hard. Callers are briefed not to volunteer quantities; if one does anyway, introduce a second workflow they have not described.

**The agent produces a conforming page on the first attempt.** The day's specification lesson disappears, because a specification that is never violated teaches nothing about what it checks. Both versions are kept by the step's own rule, so where there is only one, ask the student to name a rule they could delete without changing the output. A rule whose deletion changes nothing was not load-bearing, and a file made entirely of such rules is the failure the day is looking for.

**A student does not speak at all during phase 3.** The group format makes silence survivable and therefore likely, and inquiry anxiety is common in students who have never interviewed anyone. This is handled before the phase rather than during it: every student asks at least one question, allocated in advance, and the first five are assigned rather than volunteered.

**A student treats the caller's stated preferences as requirements and abandons their own judgement.** Obedience presented as a decision is the professional-formation failure this day is most likely to produce, and phase 6's first disclosure names it. *Residual risk the design accepts:* §5 states plainly that the countermeasure here is a story rather than a mechanism.

## Assessment

Specification item in CF102, and the module's terminal artefact.

The checked elements are all eight steps, plus **the prompt record**, **the intervention record** and **the frontier statement**. The weighted elements are **step 6** — a rejected alternative without a specific winning condition fails — and **step 8**, which fails if the generated page uses representation vocabulary.

An intervention record with no entries fails unless the submitted instructions file contains a rule whose violation is checkable by reading the generated page. An intervention record in which every entry fixed the output rather than the specification fails, because the day's claim is that a specification producing non-conforming work is defective.

**Dispositions observed:** humility, in whether the student revised after phase 7.

**Nothing else is assessed.** The agent's output quality is not assessed, the phase 1 Copilot prediction is not assessed, and the caller's phase 8 preference is evidence about the artefacts and not about the students who wrote them.

## What this day does not establish

**Demonstrated.** Four representations run against a mix the student derived from a real person, so that the alternatives rejected at step 6 are rejected on evidence rather than on taste. An instructions file written, executed, and its output inspected against a rule the student wrote — which is the first time in the programme a specification is assessed by running it.

**One run is one observation.** The day's own source paragraph records that **the vendor reports no stability property for agent output**: nothing states that the same instructions file yields the same result twice. A conforming page is evidence that the file produced a conforming page once.

**Read rather than demonstrated.** The three Azure MCP Server start-up options at point 8. The day's own flag records that they are quoted with their defaults and no package version attached, and that the day's platform anchor names the four representations and the instructions file while the defaults point rests entirely on a third product.

**Asserted.** That a first choice made against a real caller's stated needs is a different act from one made against a written brief, and that the difference is what makes the class transition survivable. That is the design's position and it carries no external warrant here.

**Two of the day's failure modes have no mechanism behind them.** [WP-015](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-015.md) §5 names the point at which fading is removed from the choice as **the least-supported point in the module** and accepts it as the transition's cost; no further support is supplied. ## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **A criterion you derived from a person is a different object from one you were handed**, and a choice is a rejection with a condition attached. What you owe the caller is a claim, its scope and what it costs them — and **a specification that produces non-conforming work is defective**, so you fix the specification rather than the output.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** The contextual-inquiry practice underlying phase 3, and the position that inquiry must not lead the informant, are verified here, at [research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md). Cognitive load, guidance fading and expertise reversal are cited for **direction and mechanism only**, with no effect size asserted. **The vendor reports no stability property for agent output** — nothing states that the same instructions file yields the same result twice — which is why one conforming run is one observation.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Ask what they do, not what they need.** A caller knows their work and their pain; they do not know their operation frequencies.
- **Confirm what the thing actually runs on before defending a choice about it.** A defence against an assumed estate is a defence against nothing.
- **Reject alternatives with a condition attached**, taken from a measurement rather than from taste.
- **An unexaminable instruction produces unexaminable work.** *Avoid jargon* asks for a judgement the agent will make on your behalf; a rule naming the checkable condition is a configured control you can inspect, in the sense the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md) gives that term.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Reading for tone because the instruction was about tone. The page was fluent, warm and correctly structured, a peer agreed — and the rule that would have caught it takes ten seconds to check and is a different sentence from the one written.
