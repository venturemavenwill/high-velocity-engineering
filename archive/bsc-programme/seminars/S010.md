# S010 · Being Wrong in Public: The First Error Clinic ⊘

**Module:** [M02](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M02.md) · **Quarter:** [Q1](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q1.md) · **Complexity class:** 1
**Strands:** PE · CF · MS
**Platform anchor:** GitHub Copilot agent mode; the module's accumulated repository customization surface · **MCP servers:** Microsoft Learn MCP Server
**Whitepaper:** [WP-010](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-010.md)
**Depends on:** S006, S007, S008, S009

⊘ **Assessment-bearing day.** Phases 5 to 7 are replaced by an assessment occasion and an extended clinic. The day carries no phase 3 contrasting-cases set and no build of its own; the artefact it assesses is the module's accumulated build from S006 to S009.

---

## What this day is for

The programme runs for three years on a norm: students disclose their own errors accurately, in public, to people who will later assess them. Every error clinic depends on it, the substrate work depends on it, and the entrustment judgements in year three depend on evidence that only accurate self-report can generate.

The norm is not established by stating it. It is established by **demonstrating that it holds when it is most likely not to** — on an assessment-bearing day, in front of an assessor who could use what they hear and does not.

That demonstration is this day's actual content. The module assessment is the occasion; the firewall is the lesson.

---

## The firewall, stated in full

Restated at the start of the day, in these terms:

> Nothing you say in an error clinic — on this day or on any other — may be recorded, referenced, or considered in any assessment decision, entrustment judgement, or reference this institution provides about you. This holds regardless of what you disclose. It holds if you disclose that you did not do the work. It holds if you disclose something that would otherwise be a specification failure. The only exceptions are disclosures indicating harm to a person or a deliberate breach of the substrate's user agreement, and those exceptions are stated here so that you know their boundaries in advance.

**The assessor is physically absent from phases 4 and 5.** This is not a courtesy; it is the mechanism.

**What the firewall does not cover, stated in the same breath.** It covers what you say. It does not cover what your tooling wrote down. The editor keeps a local agent debug panel and a local chat history, and both contain far more about how you worked than any clinic disclosure would. **This programme does not collect, read or request them**, and that commitment is of exactly the same kind as the firewall — costly, checkable only by observing that it holds, and worth stating because a commitment you did not know you were being given is not one you can rely on. What a vendor retains under its own terms is a separate question, it is not governed by this programme, and it is not summarised here because the programme has not verified it.

## The unassisted segment, stated in full

This day is the first of the programme's three unassisted occasions, with [S045](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S045.md) and [S090](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S090.md). It exists because of a risk the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) states plainly: **a student who has only ever built with an agent may be unable to build without one, and this programme cannot currently detect that.**

The provision, in these terms, published before the day:

> For twenty minutes at the start of phase 3, you will work on one element of your own artefact without agent assistance. You will state what that element claims, name what a supplied defect breaks, and write the correction by hand. The same element was produced with assistance and is in your artefact, so the two are comparable.
>
> **The comparison is marked by a member of staff who is not your assessor, returned to you in writing, and reported to the programme only as a cohort aggregate.** Your assessor does not see it. It contributes to no supervision level — there is none in Q1 — and, more importantly, **it contributes to no specification decision on this day or any other.** It cannot cause you to fail anything. It exists so that you can see a trajectory while there is still time to act on it.

**This is a third category and the day says so out loud.** Phase 3 is assessed. Phases 4, 5 and 7 are not recorded at all. The unassisted segment is recorded and not assessed, which is precisely the ambiguous middle the day otherwise tells students does not exist. The design's answer is that the middle is made safe by the same two devices the firewall uses — a bounded scope and a physical separation of the person who sees it from the person who judges you — and that hiding the category would be worse than naming it. [WP-010](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-010.md) §8 records the objection that this weakens the day's central distinction, concedes part of it, and names the cheaper alternative the design declined.

**One incoming commitment is discharged here.** [WP-006](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-006.md) §7 names this segment as the branch for a cohort whose sealed frontier predictions across S006 to S009 come out near chance: in that case the segment runs for **thirty minutes rather than twenty**, and the additional ten are spent on the same element with assistance, so that the comparison the student receives is longer and closer. The default is twenty. Which length applies is decided before the day and published with the provision, not chosen on the morning.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items, none technical.

1. Which of the following would you disclose in a clinic if the assessor were present? (A list of eight disclosures of escalating cost.)
2. Which would you disclose if they were absent?
3. Do you believe the firewall?
4. Predict the room's median answer to item 3.
5. **Sealed prediction, about yourself and the tool.** In the unassisted segment you will work on your own artefact without assistance. Write down now, and seal it: **which of the three parts will be hardest** — stating the claim, naming what the defect breaks, or writing the correction — **and what will make it hard**?

Answers are anonymous. The item 1 and 2 gap is the day's opening measurement and is shown to the room at phase 8. Item 5 is the day's frontier item and is the one occasion in the module where the frontier being probed is the boundary of the student's own unaided competence rather than the agent's.

**Warrant.** Pretesting on the day's actual subject. Anonymity is required for the instrument to mean anything, and the anonymity itself is part of the demonstration. Item 5 is administered before the segment it predicts and before phase 4, per instrumentation rule 8.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative module retrieval (30 min)

Extended, no notes, covering all five days. Interleaved and unblocked.

- The three locations invariants live.
- The cost sentence.
- The three-part correspondence.
- The asymmetry, in one sentence.
- What makes an instructions file defective, and what makes it inert.
- Which of S006's, S007's, S008's or S009's claim types is hardest to attack, and why?
- Name a case where an invariant, a seam and a test are three views of one thing.

The last two items are synthesis demands and are the module's integration check. The fifth is the module's second register, retrieved cold, and it is answered accurately by more students than any other item on this list — which is itself a finding, because it is the only one they were told by a failure rather than by an instructor.

**Warrant.** Cumulative retrieval across the whole module at gaps of two to nine days, unblocked. The final items require the module to have been a module.

## Phase 3 · The assessment occasion (75 min)

**First, the unassisted segment (20 min).** Each student is given one element of their own artefact and one defect introduced into it, on paper and in a plain editor with chat and completion features closed. They state what the element claims, name what the defect breaks, and write the correction. No assistance of any kind. The terms above apply in full: marked by a member of staff who is not the assessor, returned to the student, aggregated for the programme, and excluded from every decision made about them.

**Then the defence (55 min).** Students submit the module artefact and **defend one element of it** in a conversation with an assessor, in groups of four with each student answering individually.

The conversation is not a presentation. The assessor asks:

- What claim does this element make?
- What would falsify it?
- Show me where you were wrong during the module, and what you did.
- What in this artefact are you least confident about?
- **Read me one entry from your intervention record and tell me what you would have shipped if you had not caught it.**

The last three questions are the ones that matter, and **they are answered in front of an assessor who is assessing.** This is not the clinic; the firewall does not apply here, and that is stated explicitly. What is said in phase 3 is assessable.

Per rater discipline rule 7, **the assessor forms a provisional judgement from the artefact before the conversation begins**, and uses the defence to confirm, revise or overturn it. Two assessors form that judgement independently and record it before any discussion. The artefact for this day is a completed build rather than a prospective decision, so rule 7's amended form does not apply; nothing here is carried forward from a previous occasion, and no judgement is formed from a previous cohort's work.

The distinction between phase 3 and phase 5 is the day's structural point: **there is a place where you are assessed on your judgement including your errors, and a separate place where nothing is assessed at all.** Confusing the two is what makes disclosure unsafe everywhere.

**Warrant.** Assessment on defensible judgement rather than on artefact quality alone, with the boundary between assessed and unassessed disclosure made explicit and physical. The fifth question is added because the intervention record is the module's only artefact element that is about the student's own attention rather than about their output, and an unread intervention record is a form satisfied.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (40 min) — assessor absent

1. **Why error disclosure is a professional competence,** not a personality trait.
2. **What psychological safety actually is**, in the author's current words — *the belief that the work environment is safe for interpersonal risk taking* — **and, at greater length, what it is not.** Not *nice*; the author says you could say it is the opposite. Not comfort, not ease, and not a personality factor. **Not trust**, and the distinction is exact: trust is whether *you* give somebody else the benefit of the doubt, and safety is whether others will give it to *you*. And **not lowered standards** — taking it to mean an inability to hold people to account is a misunderstanding of the phenomenon. Safe teams report *more* errors, not fewer. **Two words this programme used to teach as definitional are dropped here:** *shared*, and *team*. They belong to the 1999 paper, which this repository has not read; the 2019 restatement drops both, and the group is where the belief is observed rather than what the definition is about.
3. **Blameless review**: what it is, what it is not, and the specific failure of treating it as an absence of accountability. **Safety and standards are two separate and equally important dimensions, not two ends of one bar** — and the quadrant that should worry this room is high standards with low safety, which is the one a demanding programme drifts into by default. Safety *takes off the brakes that keep people from achieving what's possible*; it is *not the fuel that powers the car*. It buys disclosure and supplies no competence. Three kinds of failure are separated by name and given different responses: **preventable failure, which is never good news; complex failure, which is still not good news; and intelligent failure, which must be celebrated.** And safety is **reinforced rather than harmed by fair, thoughtful responses** — which can extend, for a genuinely blameworthy act, to dismissal. That is why this day states the firewall's exceptions in advance instead of leaving them to be discovered.
4. **The firewall's purpose**, and its cost. Assessors give up information they would find useful. That cost is real and is paid deliberately.
5. **Error management versus error avoidance** as training philosophies, and why this programme chose the first. Error management training is active exploration **together with** explicit encouragement to make errors during training and learn from them, and **the trainer provides no further assistance when an error occurs** — the withholding is part of the method, not a staffing economy, which is worth saying in a room that has watched instructors decline to rescue them four times. **Its advantage is a post-training transfer advantage and it does not appear during training.** The meta-analysis found the method effective only where post-training and not within-training performance was considered, and the within-training difference is not significant: the advantage there is **absent, not reversed**, and nobody in this room should be told that the tidier method looks better today. Two bounds are stated in the same breath — every effect there is measured against **another training method and never against no training**, and **no study in it measured on-the-job performance**, so it reaches this room and not the room you will be working in. One moderator is named because it bites here: the method depends on the task supplying **clearly interpretable feedback**, that hypothesis received only limited support, and the systems this programme works on frequently fail silently. **No magnitude is stated for any of it**, on this day or any other.
6. **The four error classes** the module produced, drawn from the clinics at S006 to S009, presented as a cohort-level anonymised distribution.
7. **The dependency risk, named rather than implied.** The programme's primary instrument is an agent, and a student who has only ever built with one may be unable to build without one. This programme cannot currently detect that, which is why the unassisted segment exists, why there are three of them and not one, and why the comparison is returned to the student rather than filed. Whether three occasions is enough is unknown and is recorded as unknown.
8. **A default the platform chose for you, and one this programme chose back.** Perishable, marked, verified against the Visual Studio Code agent-customization documentation as it stood on 15 July 2026. The editor keeps a local agent debug panel and a local chat history, both openable by you and neither requiring you to turn anything on. That is a reasonable default and nobody in this room chose it. **The programme's counter-decision is that it does not collect, read or request either**, which is the firewall's boundary drawn in the only register where students can check it. The general form of the point is the one the programme repeats for three years: *an engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system*, and this time the system is the one recording you.

**Warrant.** The only phase in Q1 where the psychological-safety and error-management material is taught directly rather than enacted. It is placed after the assessment occasion and with the assessor absent, so that the instruction is not itself a performance. Points 7 and 8 are placed here rather than at phase 3 because both concern what the institution does with information about you, which is this phase's subject and not the assessment's. **Points 2, 3 and 5 were enlarged on 2026-07-30**, when the two sources behind them were read in full for the first time; the material added is the negative definition, the two-dimensions framing and the transfer-versus-within-training result, all of which were available in the sources and none of which this day previously said. The phase's length and the assessment are unchanged.

## Phase 5 · Extended error clinic (60 min) — assessor absent

The module's clinic, run at double length.

Opened by the **programme director**, not the module instructor, with a decision they made about this programme that they now believe was wrong — and what evidence changed their mind.

Then, in order: instructor, teaching assistants, then students who volunteer. No student is called on.

The instructor's disclosure is the module's agent case and is required to be one, this being the module's last clinic. **A case where the agent was confidently wrong and review did not catch it**, from the instructor's own work rather than from a case study, with the attention on the reviewer rather than on the model: what did the output look like that made checking feel unnecessary. The four cases raised at S006 to S009 were all supplied by the design; this one is supplied by a person in the room, which is the difference between a taught failure mode and a disclosed one.

Disclosures are collected on the board by class, not by name, and the board is photographed and destroyed at the end of the phase in front of the room.

**Warrant.** Error management training under maximal safety conditions. The seniority ordering is deliberate: the cost of disclosure falls as the discloser's seniority falls, so the highest-cost disclosure comes first. Requiring the instructor's case to be an agent failure closes the module's agent-failure series with the only instance that carries personal cost to disclose.

## Phase 6 · Reconvening and the demonstration (30 min)

The assessor returns and, in front of the room, states:

- what they assessed in phase 3 and on what evidence;
- that they were absent for phases 4 and 5 and will remain uninformed about them;
- that they have not seen and will not see any student's unassisted segment;
- one thing a student said in **phase 3** that they found persuasive, named and with permission.

Then students are told their phase 3 outcomes will be issued within two days and that any student may request the assessor's written reasoning. The unassisted comparison is returned separately, by the member of staff who marked it, in a separate document, so that the two never travel together.

**Warrant.** The firewall is demonstrated rather than promised. The named phase 3 example establishes that disclosure inside the assessed conversation is rewarded, which is the distinction phase 3 exists to draw. The third statement extends the same demonstration to the third category, which would otherwise be the only commitment on this day made in writing and not in the room.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (20 min)

Pairs, brief. Each student states the single most useful thing they learned in the module from someone else's error — their own or another's.

No artefact.

**Warrant.** Vicarious learning made explicit. The phase's function is to establish that other people's disclosed errors were valuable to you, which is the argument for making your own.

## When it goes wrong

The predicted failures and their countermeasures are argued in [WP-010](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-010.md) §5. Restated here as instruction to the person in the room. Nothing below is assessed, and nothing below alters the checked list at [Assessment](#assessment).

| What you see | What to do | What the design accepts |
|---|---|---|
| A student from a culture with genuine blameless review discloses freely and comfortably, and the room reads it as bravado | Hold the seniority ordering. Staff disclose first, so the modelling comes from people whose standing makes it legible as institutional confidence rather than personal | Their free disclosure is good in itself. The failure is only in how it is read by less experienced students |
| A student from a culture where admitting error has been punished does not disclose, reports low belief at item 3, and experiences phase 5 as a trap being set slowly | Call on nobody. Keep phase 1 anonymous so that non-belief is expressible without cost. Say that the same four items return at S020, S030 and S040, which is the design saying it expects belief to lag | **This is the day's hardest case and it is not solvable in one day.** §5's honest position is that this student is served by the series, not by the occasion |
| The room answers the second synthesis item well | Ask instead for a case where an invariant, a seam and a test *conflict*. It is genuinely open and it previews S034 | The five factual items are straightforward for students who kept up; the synthesis items are not, and the second is answered well by a minority |
| A sceptical student reads the whole apparatus — absent assessor, destroyed board, director's confession — as an institution performing trustworthiness | Do not argue. **Every counter-argument is more performance, and §5 says so.** Say instead that the mechanism is checkable over time: the assessor's ignorance of phase 5 will either hold or be observed to fail, across ten quarters | The reading is not unreasonable. The design's position is that scepticism here is appropriate, that item 3's belief rate should be low, and that **a high rate at S010 would be more worrying than a low one** |
| A student cannot start the unassisted segment and concludes in twenty minutes that they learned nothing all quarter | Say before the segment begins that the design expects this to be hard for most of the room, and that the expectation is why the occasion exists. Then rely on the structure: the task is on work they already understand, it is bounded and it stops, the terms were published in advance and say in plain words that it cannot cause them to fail anything, it precedes the defence rather than following it, and the result comes back days later from somebody who is not assessing them | This is the day's most serious new risk. **What the design cannot do is make it not feel like a verdict**, and §8 objection 3 is where that is taken seriously rather than reassured away |

## Assessment

**This day is assessment-bearing and produces no entrustment judgement.**

Two outputs are assessed. The **module artefact** — the accumulated S006 to S009 specification items — pass/fail against specification, with tokens available. The **phase 3 defence**, a specification decision, not a rating.

The following elements are checked, each as a binary decision:

1. The four module specification items, each already passed or resubmitted with tokens.
2. **The module's prompt records,** present for each of the four builds.
3. **The module's intervention records,** present for each. A record with no entries fails unless that day's artefact shows a check that would have detected an intervention-worthy error.
4. **The module's frontier statements,** four of them, each with both halves and each specific to its own build.
5. In the defence: can the student state what the defended element claims.
6. In the defence: can the student state what would falsify it.
7. In the defence: can the student identify where they were wrong during the module and what they did about it. **A flawless artefact whose author can identify nowhere they were wrong does not pass this element**, and that is published in advance.
8. In the defence: can the student read an intervention-record entry and say what they would have shipped had they not caught it.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Presentation, fluency, the number of prompts used, the size of the artefact, and anything said in phases 4, 5 or 7 are not assessed and are not recorded.

**The unassisted segment is not on this list and is not on any list.** It produces a comparison, returned to the student, aggregated for the programme, seen by no assessor. Because Q1 awards no supervision level, the Copilot Practice Standard's requirement that it not be used to award a level would be satisfied trivially here; the operative commitment is therefore the stronger one — **it contributes to no specification decision either.**

**Dispositions observed:** integrity, in phase 3's last three questions. Nothing from phases 4, 5 or 7 is observed by anyone who assesses.

**Durable — assessed.** The firewall's terms. The distinction between assessed and unassessed disclosure. That safe teams report more errors rather than fewer. That a stated commitment is evidenced by an institution paying a cost, not by its wording.

**Durable — taught and not assessed.** Added 2026-07-30 with phase 4 points 2, 3 and 5, and listed separately so that it is clear nothing here has entered the checked-element list. That psychological safety is **not niceness, not comfort, not a personality factor, not trust and not lowered standards**. That **safety and standards are two separate dimensions**, with high standards and low safety the quadrant this programme is most at risk of occupying. That safety takes the brakes off and is not the fuel. That preventable, complex and intelligent failure are three different things with three different responses, and that fair responses reinforce safety rather than damage it. And that error management training's advantage is a **post-training transfer** advantage which does not appear during training, is measured only against other training methods, and has never been measured on the job. **None of this carries a magnitude and none may acquire one**, which is a rule about the kind of claim rather than about how well the source was read.

**Perishable — the vehicle, not assessed.** The agent debug panel and chat history, their locations and what they contain. The customization surface the module built and the file formats it used. All as documented on 15 July 2026 and re-verified before every offering. **The unassisted segment's mechanics are perishable and its purpose is not:** what a student must be able to do unaided will change as the tooling changes, and the requirement that somebody find out will not.

**Silences recorded.** The tooling documents where its local records live. It publishes nothing about what an institution can or does collect from them, because that is not a question about the tool. The programme's commitment fills that silence with a policy, and a policy is not a mechanism — which is the same concession the firewall carries at [WP-010](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-010.md) §8, arriving a second time in a second register.

| **Entrustment** | A decision rather than a measurement — what a student would be allowed to do unsupervised, and on what evidence. Its output is an ordinal supervision level, never a percentage. **Q1 awards none, and this day produces none** |
| **Specification decision** | A binary pass or fail against a published, closed list of checked elements, with no partial credit and no rating. The phase 3 defence is one — [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) |
| **Tokens** | The resubmission economy that makes revision expected rather than exceptional: a failed specification item is resubmitted at the cost of a token. Adapted from the CMU exemplar and defined in the same page |
| **Rater discipline rule 7** | *Form the level from the artefact; use the defence to test it.* The reverse ordering — forming an impression in conversation and reading the artefact for support — is where the variance lives. Its [WP-060](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-060.md) amendment governs prospective decisions and does not apply here, because this artefact is a completed build |
| **Instrumentation rule 8** | *An instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.* It is why item 5 is administered before the segment it predicts and before phase 4 |
| **Durable / perishable split** | The division the register enforces: what may be assessed, against what may only illustrate. The table above this section is this day's, and it carries a third row — **durable, taught and not assessed** — added so that new material could not silently enter the checked list |
| **Prompt record · intervention record · frontier statement** | The three elements that travel with every phase 5 build. Checked for presence and specificity: the instructions and their order with reasons and abandonments; every correction with what it was; and both halves of the frontier judgement, drawn from that build. **Whether a frontier judgement is correct is not assessed and cannot be, because the programme holds no ground truth for the frontier** |
| **The five classes** | Configured control, executable check, observed result, outcome evidence, unsupported inference — [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). A conclusion in none of the five fails |

## What this day does not establish

The day demonstrates a cost being paid, and that is genuinely all it demonstrates. An assessor leaves the room and gives up information they would find useful. A board is photographed and destroyed in front of the people who wrote on it. The item 1 and item 2 gap is shown to the room that produced it. **These are observations of an institution's conduct on one occasion, and none of them establishes that the firewall will hold** — §5 concedes that the whole apparatus can be read as performance, that every counter-argument is more performance, and that the only available check is whether the assessor's ignorance of phase 5 holds across ten quarters.

The unassisted comparison establishes less again. It is one occasion, observed once, which rater discipline rule 1 forbids as a basis for entrustment; it produces a comparison and no level, and it contributes to no specification decision on this day or any other. Whether three unassisted occasions across three years is enough to detect the dependency risk is unknown, and the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) records it as unknown.

What rests on a note: the psychological-safety construct and the error-management-versus-error-avoidance distinction, at [research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md) and [research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md) — **both read in full on 2026-07-30 and both syntheses written from abstracts before that date.** Two claims this day used to make do not rest on either: the separation from team efficacy and the mediation by learning behaviour both stand on a 1999 paper nobody in this repository has read.

What rests on nothing verified here: vicarious learning, evaluative threat and the contamination of self-report administered by an interested party, all cited for direction and mechanism only — and the phase warrants generally, since no note exists under [research/](/research/99-source-register/source-register.md) for retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, pretesting or cognitive load. **No magnitude is asserted for any of it, including for the two sources read in full, because that prohibition is a rule about the kind of claim and reading a source completely does not lift it.**

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **A commitment is evidenced by a cost the institution pays, not by its wording.** The firewall is real because an assessor leaves the room and gives up information they would find useful. Error disclosure is a professional competence rather than a personality trait, and safe teams report more errors rather than fewer.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** The psychological-safety construct taught at phase 4 and the error-management-versus-error-avoidance distinction are verified here, at [research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md) and [research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md), **both read in full on 2026-07-30 and both syntheses written from abstracts before that date.** This paragraph previously described the construct as *a shared belief that a team is safe for interpersonal risk taking, separated from team efficacy, with learning behaviour as the mediator*, and stated all three as verified. **That is corrected rather than replaced.** The definition dropped *shared* and *team* in the author's 2019 restatement; **the separation from team efficacy and the mediation by learning behaviour both rest on the 1999 paper, which nobody in this repository has read**, and the 2019 book restates neither. If you hear anyone — including this programme — say that psychological safety works *through* learning behaviour, that is a claim standing on an unread source, and you have just been shown how to find that out. Vicarious learning, evaluative threat and the contamination of self-report administered by an interested party are cited for **direction and mechanism only**, with no magnitude asserted — and **no magnitude is asserted for the two full-text sources either**, because that prohibition is a rule about the kind of claim and reading a source in full does not lift it.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Ask what a commitment costs the party making it.** That is the only part of it you can check from outside.
- **Notice when a policy is standing in for a mechanism.** This day's undertaking about your local records is a policy, and it says so.
- **Find out what you can do unaided while there is still time to act on it.**
- **Approval is a stop condition, not a field to be filled.** The [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md) forbids an agent recording a human's approval for the reason this day separates the assessed conversation from the clinic: a judgement about a person must be made by somebody who can be held to it.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Assuming the firewall covers everything. It covers what you say. It does not cover what your tooling wrote down, and those local records contain far more about how you worked than any disclosure you could make.
