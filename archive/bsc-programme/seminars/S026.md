# S026 · What Are You Actually Measuring?

**Module:** [M06](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M06.md) · **Quarter:** [Q3](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q3.md) · **Complexity class:** 2
**Strands:** ME · SY · PE
**Whitepaper:** [WP-026](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-026.md)
**Depends on:** S020
**Re-tests:** S001, S009
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Foundry built-in evaluators (`relevance`, `coherence`, `groundedness`) · **MCP servers:** Microsoft Learn MCP Server · Azure MCP Server (read)

---

## What this day is for

Every measurement has two parts and students see one. There is the **construct** — the thing you want to know about — and the **instrument**, which is what you actually do. They are never the same, and the distance between them is where measurement fails.

Response time is not user-perceived speed. Test pass rate is not correctness. Lines changed is not work done. Accuracy is not usefulness. In each case the instrument is real, the number is right, and the claim being made is about the construct, which was not measured.

And then there is the case this quarter is built on. **An evaluator named `relevance` scores how well a response addresses a query.** It does not score whether the response is true. A fluent, well-organised, entirely fabricated answer passes it — not because the evaluator is broken, but because it is doing exactly what its documentation says it does. The gap between the word on the label and the thing being scored is today's subject, and by the end of the day every student will have watched it open on a service they called themselves.

**The day also opens with an apology.** Q2 required students to measure five times before teaching them what a measurement is. Five whitepapers recorded this as a sequencing error. The day's first act is to revisit those measurements and say what was wrong with how they were used.

## Phase 0 · The Q2 correction (15 min)

Before the pretest.

The instructor states, plainly: in Q2 you took measurements at S011, S012, S016, S018 and S019 and were asked to treat them as arbiters. That was premature. Three things were wrong with it, and each is named:

- the measurements were treated as values rather than as observations with error;
- no construct was ever stated, so what the numbers were evidence *for* was never in question;
- the instruments were supplied and unexamined, so their limitations were invisible.

**This phase is not instruction and is not assessed.** It exists because the programme committed at S001 to instrumenting itself in public, and because a day about construct validity that opened by pretending the prior quarter was sound would be self-refuting.

*This phase is additional to the standard eight and the day's remaining phases are shortened proportionally.*

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items on the module's substrate property.

1. Here is a question a real person asked about this system. What would you measure to answer it?
2. What is the thing they want to know, stated in their words?
3. What is the gap between your answer to 1 and your answer to 2?
4. Predict: is the gap larger or smaller than the room's median?
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent, in these words, to *evaluate whether our assistant's answers are good*. Write down what it will produce, and **name the one thing it will get wrong**. Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 3 is the day's move and most students write "none."

Item 5 is the day's contribution to the running frontier map. The design's expectation is that the agent will produce a working evaluation run — correct code, real service, plausible output — that names no construct, accepts every default, and reports a pass rate as though it answered the question asked. The expectation is that most students will predict some error and few will predict *that* one, because it is not an error in the code.

**Warrant.** Pretesting with a gap-identification demand. The near-universal "none" is the day's opening finding, produced by the students. Item 5 is administered here and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8: an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (40 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups have a Foundry project, a model deployment and **one instrument: the built-in `relevance` evaluator at its default settings**. They are given **five named constructs** the number is used for on the substrate — among them *the answer is correct*, *the answer is useful to the asker*, and *the assistant is getting better*.

For each construct they judge, before running anything: does this instrument measure it, measure something correlated with it, or measure something unrelated that is often confused with it? They commit.

Then they run the evaluator over **four response sets**, all answering the same question, and read `score`, `label`, `threshold` and `reason` for each.

| Case | Response set |
|---|---|
| A | Fluent, on-topic, complete and **correct** |
| B | Fluent, on-topic, complete and **fabricated** — the facts are invented |
| C | **Correct and terse** — under roughly twenty tokens |
| D | A **correct refusal** — the context does not support an answer and the assistant says so |

Task after: **for the construct the instrument does not measure but is used for, state what it would take to measure it properly, and why nobody does.**

Then, second task, and it is the one the day's argument depends on: **take the case you found most convincing and say whether your conclusion survives the assumption that the evaluator's score on that case was simply wrong.** For case B it does, because a fabrication that is fluent and on-topic passes an evaluator defined against the query whatever the judge's reliability — there is no path from the evaluator's inputs to the falsity of the content. For case D it does, because the gap is derivable from the published definition rather than from the reading. **For case C it does not**, and the group must say so. You are holding a reading from an instrument nobody has calibrated, and separating what the reading shows from what the definition shows is the discipline this module exists to install.

Case B is the cheapest surprise: it passes, and it passes for a documented reason. Case C is the vendor's own scope statement made visible — the documentation states that scoring reliability may vary for responses under approximately twenty tokens, and the group watches a correct answer score unstably. **Case D is the payload.** It is the most professionally defensible response in the set and it scores worst, and no group predicts it. The instrument moves in the opposite direction to the construct, and the platform's own later evaluators concede the point: `Quality Grader` adds *abstention* as a separate scored dimension, which is the vendor acknowledging that relevance alone did not capture it.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across constructs with a fixed instrument, which is the inverse of the usual construction and isolates the construct-instrument gap. The divergence evidence is now produced by the students against a metered service rather than supplied on a handout, which makes case D's result theirs. The opposite-direction case is the payload and a correct account of it is a correct account of the gap. **The second task is [WP-026](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-026.md) §8's objection-3 repair**: a demonstration whose persuasive force exceeds its evidential force is dangerous, and the cheapest available correction is to make the separation between the two into the exercise.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (45 min)

1. **Construct and instrument,** defined and separated. The construct is what you want to know; the instrument is what you do. Naming both, separately, is the minimum.
2. **The four ways the gap opens:** the instrument measures a proxy; it measures under conditions unlike the ones that matter; it measures a component rather than the whole; it measures something the construct causes rather than the construct. **This list is not exhaustive — it omits instruments that alter the construct by measuring it, and constructs that are not single quantities at all. Both are real; both are taken up at S029 and S046. These four cover the failures students produce.**
3. **Proxies are not illegitimate.** A proxy with a stated relationship to its construct is a measurement; a proxy silently substituted for its construct is an error. The difference is a sentence.
4. **What happens when a proxy is optimised.** A measure that becomes a target stops measuring, and the mechanism is that the cheapest way to move the instrument is rarely the way that moves the construct.
5. **The five constructs from phase 3,** resolved, including the two that move backwards, and case D worked completely.
6. **The reporting obligation:** name the construct, name the instrument, state the relationship, state the conditions.
7. **Defaults are opinions and thresholds are policy.** Five were chosen for you in phase 3 and none was announced. The evaluation framework's default evaluator set is relevance, coherence and task adherence — and **it adds a tool-accuracy evaluator automatically the moment your items carry tool definitions**, so the shape of your input silently changes what is measured. The evaluation level defaults to **turn**, not conversation, so a multi-turn assistant is scored one reply at a time unless you say otherwise. Quality evaluators return a **1-to-5** score; content-safety evaluators return a **7-point** score; textual-similarity evaluators return a float between 0 and 1; **and all three are collapsed into the same binary `pass`/`fail` label**. The collapse happens at a **default pass threshold of 3 on the 1-to-5 scale**, which is overridable and which nobody in this room chose. And a custom code-based evaluator that never calls a model still requires you to name a model deployment, because the service schema demands one. **An engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system**, and this generalises far beyond this vendor.
8. **The documented silence, and why it is load-bearing.** The documentation states scales, thresholds, token limits, sampling rates and hourly caps with precision. **It states no figure anywhere for how often any of these evaluators agrees with a competent human on the same output.** Microsoft's own AI playbook goes further and says that evaluation by language model *has not yet been systematically studied*, listing position bias, verbosity bias and self-enhancement bias among the known problems — the same failure classes the research literature identifies independently. **Every judged score in this stack is therefore uncalibrated, and the sentence reporting one must say so.** This is not a complaint about the vendor. It is the exact situation the reporting obligation at point 6 exists for.
9. **What is perishable here and what is not.** The threshold of 3, the 1-to-5 scale, the twenty-token caveat, the default evaluator set and the names of every evaluator are perishable and will move. **The durable claim is that an instrument named after a construct scores something narrower, and that a score whose agreement with human judgement is unpublished is uncalibrated whoever publishes it.** If Microsoft publishes agreement data next year the durable claim is unchanged and this day re-anchors. That is the difference between an instance and a proof, and it is why the specification assesses the reasoning rather than the number.
10. **The forward link.** Everything from S041 onward is this. A model's accuracy, a benchmark score, an evaluation rubric and a monitoring signal are all instruments standing in for constructs, and each is stated once here.

11. **Two failures a construct statement will not catch, and both are structural.** *Added 2026-07-30, on a reading of a second internal corpus. Taught and warranted, never assessed — the specification is unchanged.* A customer-embedded engineering organisation within a large technology vendor documents its own accelerated delivery practice across roughly ninety documents, recorded in [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md). **Its organising thesis is excellent and belongs in this course.** A delivery can satisfy every agreed success criterion, use excellent architecture, follow textbook engineering fundamentals, ship to production — **and still produce nothing of value**, because everything short of realised business value is activity rather than impact, including the team, the architecture, the fundamentals and the deployment. That is a deliberately uncomfortable claim to put to an engineering audience, and it is points 1 and 6 stated at engagement scale by people with no incentive to say it.

    **The collection then fails its own test, and the failure is countable without reproducing a single figure.** Of its quantitative assertions across the delivery material, a very small number concern an outcome. **The rest are activity measures** — elapsed time, headcount, proportion of code generated, minutes to add a feature, proportion of a backlog judged ready. A body of work whose central claim is that only measurable benefit counts supports itself almost entirely with measures of activity. **The count and the character of the figures are the lesson; no figure from it is reproduced here or anywhere in this wiki**, and none needs to be.

    **The first structural failure: an evidence posture arranged so that nothing can disconfirm it.** The collection carries a brief of anticipated objections with prepared answers — and **a list of prepared answers is a map of where a model actually meets resistance.** Read one that way and it tells you, in the organisation's own words, where it expects to be challenged and how hard. The objection it pre-arms engineers against most heavily is that **the tooling improved and delivery did not speed up.** The prepared answer **reattributes the shortfall to the customer's own organisational constraints.** Pair that with a readiness rubric composed almost entirely of customer dispositions and the arrangement closes: a good result confirms the thesis, and **a disappointing result confirms it too, because the customer was not ready.** No observation is left that could count against it. **This is not a construct-instrument gap.** It is a construct wired to an instrument that cannot return a disconfirming answer, and **point 6's reporting obligation does not detect it**, because every individual sentence in the arrangement is properly hedged and the failure lives in the join between them. **Reading a prepared-objection list as a map is the transferable skill here**, and it is available to you on any vendor's material, including this programme's own.

    **The second: a number-generating apparatus containing no numbers.** The same collection's value-conversation framework contains no unwarranted magnitudes — **because it contains none at all.** No claimed multiples, no savings, no efficiency figures; a catalogue of metric definitions in which not one entry carries an observed value, a benchmark or a typical range; a case-study section reserved and empty. **It ships spreadsheet models and a simulation workbook anyway.** Figures produced downstream will therefore carry the authority of a named framework while resting entirely on elicited inputs — **and because the framework asserts nothing, those figures cannot be traced back to any claim in it. There is no proposition to check them against.** That is a **worse** failure mode than an unwarranted magnitude, which is at least locatable and refutable: you can find the sentence, ask for the study, and mark the claim as unwarranted. **You cannot refute an apparatus.** Its structural diagnosis is one sentence and it is the one to keep: **the cost side is engineered and the return side is elicited** — consumption forecast, instrumented and refined against telemetry by engineers, return chosen by the customer from a menu, measured with instrumentation the customer may not possess, over a period nobody specifies, with causation assumed and no party assigned responsibility for establishing it. **A ratio whose denominator is well instrumented and whose numerator is not is not a well-instrumented ratio, but it will feel like one.**

    **What this establishes and what it does not.** Both failures are diagnosable from the material's own form, which is why they can be taught with no access to the source. **What none of it establishes is whether the practice works**: the corpus reports no comparison, no evaluation, no cost side, no quality or incident data and no failure — every case study succeeds, and the single failure narrative is a synthetic composite the material itself labels as fictional. **The diagnosis is a claim about an evidence posture and never a claim about the competence of the people who hold it.** The material is thoughtful, experienced and in places genuinely wise, and it is entirely unwarranted, and both readings sit in the same documents in the same register. **A student who can hold both at once has the skill this day exists to build.**

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Point 2 carries the scope statement required by the [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md). Point 4 is the day's most operationally consequential idea. Points 7 and 9 are the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required platform content — a default the platform chose, and a perishable point marked as perishable. Point 8 is the module's central real-world instance and is reachable from phase 3's case D artefact, per instrumentation rule 7. **Point 11 is a specimen and is not platform content**, and it is placed last because it is the only place in the day where the failure is above the instrument rather than inside it — points 1 to 9 all concern a construct and an instrument that are both present, and point 11's two failures are an instrument that cannot return the wrong answer and an apparatus with no claim to check. It is walked by the instructor, it is **deliberately unassessable and adds no checked element**, and the reason is stated in the room: the corpus is internal, nothing in it may be linked, and it reports no outcome, so there is nothing a student could be marked against. **Its organising thesis is given before its failure**, deliberately, because a specimen presented only as a failure is dismissed and this one is a claim the programme itself makes.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (85 min)

**Class 2. The instrument is not given. This phase produces a build.**

Students use Copilot in agent mode, with the Learn MCP Server for grounding and the Azure MCP Server in read configuration to inspect the project's deployments and knowledge indexes, to **ship a working evaluation** of **the module's question** — a real, unanswered, operationally relevant question about the substrate. They:

1. **State the construct** in the asker's words, then in precise terms, and note what the precision added or removed.
2. **Propose an instrument.** A built-in evaluator, a combination of them, a local deterministic check, or something you write. Any, including one not covered.
3. **State the relationship** between instrument and construct: identity, proxy with a stated direction, or component.
4. **Construct the divergence case** — an input or condition under which the instrument moves and the construct does not, or vice versa.
5. **State whether the substrate can produce that case,** and then **produce it and run it.**
6. **State what would happen if someone optimised your instrument,** per point 4.
7. **Write the reporting sentence** from point 6, naming the threshold you used and whether you chose it.

Step 5 is where the day bites. A divergence case that survives contact with a real evaluator is a different object from one written on paper, and roughly half do not: the case is constructed against what the student assumed the evaluator scores, and the run shows it scoring something else again.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned. Not a transcript.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — an assertion that the reported threshold matches the threshold actually applied is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch. Both specific, both from this build.

**Fading:** step 1 is worked with the room; step 4's construction is modelled once. Steps 2, 6 and 7 are unsupported.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 2 on the question the module will answer across five days, now shipping rather than describing. Step 4 is the day's discriminator — a construct-instrument gap you cannot exhibit is a gap you have not identified — and step 5's execution is what converts an assertion into a finding. Step 6 makes point 4 personal. The intervention record distinguishes a student who directed the work from one who accepted it, and its fail condition carries the sensitivity escape [S052](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S052.md) established and [WP-048](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 corrected.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (35 min)

Instructor first: a substrate metric that was reported for a year, was accurate throughout, and was used to make a decision it could not support — with attention to the moment when the construct and the instrument were last distinguished in writing.

Then the agent case, and it is the one from phase 1 item 5. A student asked Copilot to *check whether the answers are grounded in our documents*. The agent produced clean, working code: it configured the `groundedness` evaluator, mapped the fields correctly, ran the batch, and reported a comfortable pass rate. **The code was right and the number meant nothing.** The `context` field had been mapped to the assistant's own prior turn rather than to the retrieved documents, so the evaluator was scoring whether the assistant was consistent with itself. Every response was grounded in something. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: the reviewer checked that the evaluator was named `groundedness`, the construct in the request was groundedness, the two words matched, and **matching the words is what review actually consists of when the reviewer has no construct statement to check against.**

Then students: a number you have quoted as though it meant something else, or a place today where you accepted agent output you had not checked.

**Warrant.** Error management. The disclosure's focus on when the distinction was last written down is the phase's content: these gaps open by attrition, not by error. The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen so the failure is invisible to the review a student would actually perform — the code is correct, the service is real, the output is plausible, and the only defect is a construct one.

## When it does not work

These are [WP-026](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-026.md) §5's predicted failure modes, restated as instruction to the person in the room. The whitepaper is the authority; where this section and §5 diverge, §5 is right.

**A student with research-methods or psychometrics exposure already holds the distinction and will use the vocabulary fluently.** Their predicted failure is at step 4: a divergence case that is theoretical rather than substrate-reachable, because the vocabulary permits abstract discussion and their training rewarded it. Two things resist it — step 5 requires substrate reachability **and an executed run**, and phase 7's partner is asked to construct a *decision*, not a scenario. Hold the partner to that; a scenario can be abstract and a decision cannot.

**A student without that background finds the distinction genuinely difficult and has no procedural check for it.** Four supports exist: the fixed instrument across phase 3, the two-form construct statement at step 1, the divergence evidence produced in the room rather than argued, and one question carried across the whole module. *Residual risk the design accepts:* step 4's construction may be beyond them. **The fallback is to select a divergence case from phase 3 and adapt it**, which is weaker and passes.

**Phase 3 is too easy on the construct the instrument does measure.** Groups usually identify it. The phase bites on the two constructs that move *backwards*, and groups typically classify those as merely uninformative rather than as inverted — which is a different and much smaller claim. If a group gets all five, ask them to construct a sixth construct for which this instrument would be a *good* measure. That requires reasoning from the instrument's mechanism and is hard.

**Generalised suspicion of measurement.** This is the module's fatalism risk in its first form and it is the opposite of the intended lesson: a student who concludes that all instruments are invalid will be worse at measurement, not better. Three countermeasures, and they are all in the day rather than in the facilitator's manner — point 3's rehabilitation of proxies, the constructive output at point 6 and step 7, and phase 3's closing task, which explains why imperfect instruments persist for good reasons. **The design's position is that this risk is highest today and declines across the module as the constructive apparatus accumulates, and that [S029](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S029.md) is where it should resolve.** Three consecutive whitepapers make that claim; if S029 does not resolve it, the module has a structural problem rather than three local ones.

## Assessment

Specification item in ME101. Checked elements: the construct in two forms with the precision difference noted; the instrument; the stated relationship; the constructed divergence case with substrate reachability; **the executed run of that case**; the optimisation consequence; the reporting sentence with its threshold named; **the prompt record**; **the intervention record**; **the frontier statement**.

**An artefact in which the construct and the instrument are the same sentence fails the specification.**

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

Nothing else is assessed.

## Perishable content in this day

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| An instrument named after a construct scores something narrower | `relevance` scores relevance to the query, not truth |
| A proxy with a stated relationship is a measurement; a silent one is an error | The `reason` field, which states the relationship and is usually unread |

| Thresholds are policy, not measurement | The default pass threshold of 3 on a 1-to-5 scale |

All evaluator names, scales, thresholds, token caveats and default evaluator sets are **perishable** and are marked at point 9 explicitly. The construct-instrument distinction, the four gap mechanisms, the proxy discipline, the optimisation failure, the reporting obligation and the uncalibrated-score rule are durable and are what the specification assesses.

## What this day does not establish

**Demonstrated.** That a fluent, on-topic, entirely fabricated answer passes an evaluator defined against the query; that a correct refusal — the most professionally defensible response in the set — scores worst; that a correct answer under roughly twenty tokens scores unstably; and that the evaluation framework adds a tool-accuracy evaluator automatically when the input carries tool definitions, so that the shape of the data silently changes what is measured.

**Read rather than demonstrated.** The twenty-token reliability caveat and the English-only support; the default evaluator set; the default evaluation level; the three score scales and their collapse to one binary label; the default pass threshold; and the requirement to name a model deployment for a code-based evaluator that never calls a model. All documentation, read on the day. **Also read:** Microsoft's own AI playbook statement that evaluation by language model has not yet been systematically studied, and its naming of position, verbosity and self-enhancement bias.

**Not established here, and the day makes the limit itself into an exercise.** The phase 3 second task asks whether a group's conclusion survives the assumption that the evaluator's score was simply wrong — and for the terse case **it does not.** That reading is from an instrument nobody has calibrated, and the day requires the room to say so rather than banking the result. What survives is what is derivable from the published definition, which is the fabrication case and the refusal case.

**Point 11 establishes nothing about the practice it examines.** Both failures are diagnosable from the material's own form, which is why they can be taught with no access to the source. **What none of it establishes is whether the practice works:** the corpus reports no comparison, no evaluation, no cost side, no quality or incident data and no failure, every case study succeeds, and the single failure narrative is a synthetic composite the material labels as fictional. **The diagnosis is a claim about an evidence posture and never a claim about the competence of the people who hold it.**

**Asserted.** That these four gap mechanisms are the ones worth teaching — point 2 says so and names two it omits. That the reporting obligation has exactly four parts. And that a proxy with a stated relationship is a measurement while a silently substituted one is an error: that is a standard this design sets, and the difference is a sentence.

**What rests on a `research/` note.** Construct validity, the proxy-optimisation mechanism and the reporting obligations, at [research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md) and [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md) — **the second is a complete reading of the full text and is among the best-founded citations in the programme.** Point 11's specimen rests on [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md). The identified biases of language-model judges are recorded at **abstract-verification depth only** and are cited for direction and mechanism, with no agreement figure asserted because none is published.

**What rests on nothing.** Pretesting, expanding-interval retrieval, contrasting cases with a fixed instrument, and worked-example fading have **no note anywhere in this repository.** The fixed-instrument construction at phase 3 is the inverse of the usual arrangement and is the day's principal structural invention; the argument for it is [WP-026](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-026.md)'s own.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **An instrument named after a construct scores something narrower than the name.** The construct is what you want to know, the instrument is what you actually do, and the distance between them is where measurement fails — sometimes in the opposite direction, so that the most defensible answer in the set scores worst.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** Construct validity, the proxy-optimisation mechanism and the reporting obligations are verified in this repository at [research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md) and [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md). The identified biases of language-model judges are recorded at abstract-verification depth and are cited for **direction and mechanism only; no agreement figure is asserted, because none is published for any evaluator in this stack.**

**Guidance for your own work.**

- Name the construct and the instrument separately. If they are one sentence, you have identified neither.
- State the relationship — identity, proxy with a stated direction, or component — in the report rather than in your head.
- Build the divergence case and then run it. A gap you cannot exhibit is a gap you have not identified.
- **Sort your conclusions into the five classes** on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). A pass rate is an observed result from a configured check; *the answers are good* is outcome evidence, and it cannot be reached at all without a stated construct.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Reviewing by word match. When the request says groundedness and the evaluator is called `groundedness`, there is nothing left for a reviewer to check unless a construct statement exists to check against.
