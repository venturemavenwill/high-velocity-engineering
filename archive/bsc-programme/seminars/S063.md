## What this day is for

[S018](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S018.md) ended on a sentence: **the same policy can be the best and the worst available, determined by one number you did not choose.** The number was the working set, and the student's position was that of an operator whose workload was handed to them.

Today that sentence is inverted. The working set of a serving system is the retained state of the requests currently in flight, the number of requests in flight is set by an admission policy, and **the admission policy is yours.** The one number S018 said you did not choose is now the one number you do.

**And on this platform it is one field in the request body.**

That is the day's genuine surprise and it is documented rather than derived. A managed deployment decides whether it can accept your request by estimating what the request will cost it, and the estimate is the prompt tokens — less any served from cache — plus **the `max_tokens` value you declared.** Declare a large one and you have reserved capacity you will not use. Declare none and the service estimates a value on your behalf, and the documentation states plainly that this can produce lower concurrency than expected when the actual output is short. **The reservation is made either way. The only question is whether you made it.**

Two consequences follow and the day is about both.

**How many requests you can serve at once is an allocation decision, not a hardware fact.** Two disciplines over identical capacity differ in achieved concurrency by a large factor, and the difference is capacity that was reserved and not used. That capacity is already paid for. It is the cheapest capacity in the system and most operators never measure it, because nothing reports it: the platform tells you your utilisation, and utilisation counts the reservation, not the use.

**And the cliff has moved.** [S018](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S018.md)'s system thrashed when it was admitted past its capacity. This one does not. It refuses — immediately, with a stated interval to wait — and refusing is cheap. **So the collapse is not on the server any more; it is on whatever the client does with the refusal.** A client that honours the interval is fine. A client that retries at once has rebuilt the cliff on its own side of the wire, and it is the same positive feedback [S062](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S062.md) point 6 named a day ago.

The target: **the four numbers that characterise a serving path's capacity position, and the obligation to have measured all four before stating a concurrency figure to anybody.**

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items on the substrate's serving path, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. The path currently holds N requests in flight. **Its capacity is doubled and nothing else changes. Predict the new concurrency.**
2. Output lengths on this path vary by roughly a factor of twenty and the current code declares no output limit at all. **What fraction of what is reserved on your behalf is actually used?**
3. Concurrency is raised past what the deployment will accept, and the client retries immediately on every refusal. **Sketch completed requests per second against offered concurrency.**
4. Predict whether your item 3 curve is closer to the room's median or to the measurement.
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to *"increase the number of requests we can have in flight without buying anything."* Write down what it will change, and **name the one thing it will not think to change.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 1's answer is not "double", because one of the three memory terms does not scale. Item 3 is [S018](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S018.md) item 2's sketch demand at a five-quarter gap, and most of the room again draws a smooth degradation. Item 5's answer is almost always the request body: the agent will reach for concurrency settings, connection pools and retry configuration, and will not reach for the one field that decides what each request reserves.

**Warrant.** Pretesting with a sketch demand and a quantitative estimate, on a system the cohort operates. **Item 2 is the day's second instrument and is sited here, before instruction**, so that it measures what the cohort believes about their own service rather than what phase 4 tells them. The deliberate repeat of S018's sketch item is named to the room at phase 8, because the point is that they have drawn this curve wrong before. Item 5 is the day's frontier-calibration datum, administered here per instrumentation rule 8.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups have **one workload and one deployment**, and run it under four reservation disciplines. Nothing about the model, the hardware or the traffic changes; the only thing that changes is what each request declares it will need.

| Case | Reservation discipline |
|---|---|
| **A** | Declare the model's maximum output length on every request. |
| **B** | Declare a per-class prediction of output length, with a retry at a larger value when the prediction is short. |
| **C** | Declare tightly per request, and place the shared instructions where the platform will serve them from cache. |
| **D** | Declare nothing, and let the service decide what to reserve on your behalf. |

Groups predict achieved concurrency, refusal rate and ninety-ninth-percentile latency for each, and commit.

Then measurements arrive, **at five offered-concurrency levels chosen so that the refusal threshold falls between two adjacent samples.** Its position must be inferred, not read off. This is [S018](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S018.md) phase 3's sampling construction reused, and it is named to the room afterwards.

The payload is **D**, and it is the case every group expects to be neutral. Declaring nothing feels like declining to decide. It is not: the service estimates a value on your behalf, and the documentation states that this can produce **lower** concurrency than expected when the real output is short — which on this substrate it usually is. **The option that looks like abstention is the largest reservation in the room**, and it is the one the substrate's own code has been using since Q6.

**C** is the second finding and it is the day's cheapest money. Content the platform serves from its prompt cache does not count against the deployment's capacity accounting at all, so a layout decision from [S016](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S016.md) shows up here as concurrency.

Then the second run, five minutes, and it is the day's other half. The group drives offered concurrency past the point where refusals begin, twice: once with a client that waits the interval the refusal states, and once with a client that retries at once. **The first is a plateau and the second is a collapse.** The server did not change. [S018](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S018.md)'s cliff is still in the system; it has moved to the caller.

Tasks after: **state the number that determines which discipline you want, and say whether you control it.** This is S018's closing task word for word, and the answer has changed. Then: **name where the cliff is now, and who owns the code that produces it.**

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across four disciplines with workload and deployment held fixed, executed rather than supplied, and a non-linear adjudicator sampled so that inference is required. D is the payload rather than A because the cohort will predict A's waste once prompted and will not predict that declining to declare is itself a declaration — and because D is what their own code currently does. The two client behaviours are what preserve [S019](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S019.md) point 5's saturation class honestly: **the design will not manufacture a server-side collapse the platform does not have**, and the finding that the collapse relocated is worth more than the finding that it persists. The repeated closing task is the day's cheapest instructional device: the same question, five quarters later, with the opposite answer.

**Demonstrated rather than operated.** The deployment's own utilisation accounting — the one-minute utilisation figure, the leaky-bucket behaviour that permits brief bursts above the ceiling, and the correction applied when a request finishes and the real cost is known — is a property of dedicated capacity the programme does not hold. It is **shown from a recorded session, with its documentation open beside it, and it is not assessed.** What the room measures itself is the refusal rate, the achieved concurrency, and the gap between what was declared and what was used.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **The third memory term, precisely.** Retained per-request state grows with sequence length and with the number of requests in flight, and it is the only one of [S061](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S061.md)'s three terms under run-time control. The weights are fixed. The transient working state is bounded by the group size chosen at [S062](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S062.md).

2. **Concurrency is an allocation decision, not a hardware fact.** Doubling total memory does not double concurrency, because the fixed term does not scale with it — item 1's answer, and the reason most capacity intuitions in this area are wrong by a constant factor nobody computes.

3. **Reserving against the worst case is the standard error and its cost is proportional to the variance.** With lengths varying by a large factor, most of what is reserved is never touched. **The capacity you reserved and did not use is the cheapest capacity in the system:** it is already bought, already paid for, and recovering it requires no purchase and no permission. **On this platform recovering it is one field**, and the vendor says so — the guidance for maximum concurrency is to declare an output limit as close as possible to the true generation size.

4. **There are two wastes, not one, and both must be measured.** The first is what you declared and did not use. The second is what you sent again and did not have to: content the platform serves from its prompt cache does not consume the deployment's capacity at all, so a shared prefix that is stable across requests is capacity you get back for free. **Which technique implements either recovery is perishable and is marked as such. The durable content is that there are two wastes and that both must be measured.**

5. **Two forms of over-reservation, and only one is easy to see:** capacity declared per request and not used, and capacity consumed by an accounting rule you did not know about. The second is the one that will surprise you. The gateway, for example, counts an image in a prompt at a documented worst case rather than its true size when it is estimating, and estimation is forced on whenever a response streams — so **a streaming, image-carrying caller is charged against your budget at a rate that has nothing to do with what it used.** A policy tuned against the first waste can be worse on the second, and the second is invisible in a utilisation figure.

6. **The cliff did not go away; it changed owner.** [S018](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S018.md)'s system thrashed past its limit. A managed deployment does not: it refuses, immediately, and tells the caller how long to wait — which is a **fast failure by design and is cheaper than the work**, exactly as [S062](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S062.md) point 6 required. So the non-linear collapse [S019](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S019.md) point 5 named as a class now happens **in the client**, when the refusal is retried instead of honoured. That is still the third member of the class, and it is the first whose position is set by code the serving team may not own. **The refusal is not an error and treating it as one is what produces the outage.**

7. **The four measurement obligations.** Whatever the technique, a serving path is characterised by four numbers: **achieved concurrency; capacity reserved and not used — reported together with its mirror, the fraction of requests that reached the declared limit and were cut short; the offered load at which refusals begin; and per-class tail latency at the chosen operating point.** The second obligation carries two numbers on purpose. **A declaration is a limit, and a limit that is reached is an answer the user did not get.** Recovering reservation is free right up to the point where it starts truncating, and an operator who reports the waste without the truncation has reported half of a trade as if it were a saving. An operator who cannot state all four has not characterised the system, and a concurrency figure quoted without the date of the measurement that produced it is not a figure. This is the durable form of everything above, and it is what the specification checks.

8. **What the platform's own accounting does, stated because you will quote it.** Perishable, verified for this offering on **2026-07-26**, and none of it assessed. Utilisation on a dedicated deployment is the ratio of capacity consumed to capacity deployed over a one-minute window, maintained by a leaky-bucket scheme that permits brief bursts above the ceiling and caps you over time. Each request is charged an estimate on arrival — prompt tokens, less cached, plus the declared output limit — and **corrected when it finishes**, up or down, against what it actually cost. At the ceiling the service refuses and states an interval. **Two things follow that most engineers get wrong.** A utilisation figure is a statement about reservations, so a deployment can read as busy while doing very little. And the correction means your over-declaration is refunded *after* it has already stopped somebody else being admitted — which is why the reservation, not the usage, is the thing to fix.

9. **Defaults are opinions, and the loudest one here is an omission.** Three were chosen for you. *The output limit.* Leaving it unset does not mean unlimited and does not mean unreserved; it means the service picks, and the documentation says the pick can cost you concurrency. **A default that is invoked by writing nothing is the hardest kind to notice and the hardest kind to review.** *The estimation mode at the gateway.* Whether prompt tokens are counted before or after the backend has no default at all — somebody had to choose — and it is silently overridden for streaming callers. *The cache.* Whether your repeated prefix is discounted depends on behaviour you did not configure and on a layout you may not have thought about since [S016](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S016.md). **An engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system**, and here what was chosen for them was chosen by the absence of a line of code.

10. **Scope, in the strong form.** The four numbers and the two-waste account are **safe** for a single model on capacity you control, with a known and stable request-length distribution — which is the substrate's condition and most first deployments. They are **unsafe** in three conditions. Where several models share capacity, the fixed term is no longer fixed and the ceiling belongs to whoever else is resident. Where the length distribution shifts, a prediction-based discipline degrades in a way none of the four numbers reveals until the refusal threshold has already moved. And where capacity is shared with tenants you cannot observe — **which is the ordinary condition of a standard deployment, where throughput is documented as varying with other customers' demand** — the ceiling is not yours to state at all. **A student who characterises a shared deployment with these four numbers will quote a concurrency ceiling they do not control, to somebody who will plan against it.**

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3's four disciplines. Point 3 is the retained content and the sentence [S065](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S065.md) will require. Point 6 completes a class opened four and five quarters ago and is stated as a relocation rather than a completion, which is the honest form. **Point 7 is the day's durable content deliberately written as measurement obligations rather than as technique**, because the technique is semi-durable under the [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) and will not survive this cohort. **Point 8 is the day's perishable platform-specific point and point 9 is the day's defaults point**, both required by the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), and both indexed to measurements the room took at phase 3 rather than asserted.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 5. The discipline, the ceiling and the signal are the student's, the ceiling will be quoted to somebody, and this phase produces a build.**

Students work with Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server and **ship a reservation discipline into the substrate's calling code and its gateway policy.** It is a change to what requests declare and to what the gateway estimates, it is applied, and its effect is measured on real traffic before the phase ends.

1. **State the binding resource for your admission decision, and show the arithmetic that establishes it.** Not an assertion — the ratio, with its terms.
2. **State the concurrency your current discipline achieves and the concurrency the same capacity could hold** under a perfect reservation. The gap between them is the day's number.
3. **Measure the capacity you reserve and do not use**, per request class: what each request declared, what it used, and the difference. **Report the truncation rate beside it** — the fraction of requests in each class that reached the declared limit — because that is the cost of the recovery and the two numbers move in opposite directions. **Report the cache-served fraction beside both**, because that is the second waste and it is recovered differently.
4. **Locate the refusal threshold.** Predict its position, then find it by driving offered concurrency past the current ceiling under controlled load. **Then run both client behaviours** — honour the stated interval, and retry at once — and report where the collapse is.
5. **Choose and deploy a reservation discipline.** State the concurrency ceiling it sets and the headroom to the measured threshold.
6. **Report the tail effect of your discipline per request class** at the chosen operating point.
7. **State what you would have to observe to know the threshold had moved,** and commit the signal.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a check that fails the build when a request is issued with no declared output limit is the obvious example and is accepted, because it is the exact defect the agent will leave in place.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch.

**Fading:** step 4's controlled-load procedure is supplied and step 3's per-class accounting is worked with the room. **Step 7 is unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 5, and the only task in the module where waiting for a measurement is part of the period rather than designed out — the threshold cannot be located without loading the system, and locating it is the day. **Step 1 is the artefact element [S061](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S061.md) named under instrumentation rule 6**, and the arithmetic requirement is what makes it a claim rather than a recollection. **Step 6 is the artefact element [S062](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S062.md) named**, and it is placed after the discipline is chosen so that the tail effect is a consequence the student owns rather than a property they inherited. Step 4's second half is what converts the day's relocated cliff from a phase 4 claim into a thing the student's own client did. Step 7 is unsupported because a leading signal for a boundary that moves is the day's genuinely hard act.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate change that raised the concurrency ceiling, was measured, was reviewed, shipped, and was rolled back after an incident three weeks later. The ceiling had been measured correctly. **The threshold had moved, because the traffic mix had shifted toward longer outputs, and the measurement establishing the ceiling was three weeks old.** Attention is directed at the fact that the number was right when it was taken, and that nobody had said when it was taken.

Then the agent case. Copilot in agent mode, asked to *"make the service handle more concurrent users,"* produced a change that raised the client's connection pool, raised the parallelism of the calling loop, and added a retry with exponential backoff on failure. **It was reviewed, approved and shipped, and it made the service worse under exactly the conditions it was written for.** The reservation per request was untouched, so the deployment admitted no more work than before; the extra parallelism turned into refusals; and the retry converted the refusals into more arrivals. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **every individual change was a recognised good practice, the diff contained no error, and the interaction with the platform's admission accounting was in none of the three.**

Then students, in threes: **a limit you were operating under that you had never measured yourself**, or a change you approved because each part of it was standard.

**Warrant.** Error management targeting point 7's as-of-date requirement and point 6's relocated cliff. The instructor case has no error in it at any point that anyone could have been blamed for, and its lesson is a piece of clerical discipline — which is exactly the kind of lesson that does not survive being asserted. The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen because it is composed entirely of correct moves: **there is nothing in it for a reviewer to object to except the thing that is missing.**

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **their step 5 ceiling and their step 7 signal.**

The partner's task: **construct a change in the traffic mix that moves the refusal threshold below the chosen ceiling, and show that the step 7 signal would not have fired in time.**

Both are required. A mix change that moves the threshold but that the signal would have caught is not a defeat.

Per rule 3, the outcome is recorded in the artefact. Per rule 5, this reuses the dual-defeat structure from [S029](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S029.md), used since at S047, S048, S053 and S061; per the amendment at [WP-054](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8, **the reuse is recorded as contingent on S029's reported outcome.**

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with a dual demand, which makes the phase a test of the signal rather than of the ceiling. The construction is the same one phase 6 describes happening to staff, arriving one phase later and produced by a peer rather than told.

## When it does not work

The predicted failures and their countermeasures are argued in [WP-063](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-063.md) §5. Restated here as instruction to the person in the room. Nothing below is assessed and nothing below changes the specification.

| What you see | What to do | What the design accepts |
|---|---|---|
| A student who has run a capacity-constrained serving path predicts the waste fraction accurately, knows what an output limit does to the accounting, and treats step 7's signal as routine because they already have alerts | **Phase 7's partner is required to defeat the signal with a *mix* change rather than a volume change**, which alerting practice generally does not cover. And point 5's second form of over-reservation — capacity consumed by an accounting rule you did not know about — **is not visible in any utilisation-based alert they will have configured**, because utilisation counts the reservation | §5 states both without qualification |
| A student cannot complete step 1, because it requires composing the [S061](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S061.md) model with a capacity budget | Step 3's accounting is worked with the room and produces most of the terms step 1 needs, and phase 3's four disciplines expose the arithmetic as four worked instances before they attempt their own. **Say the fallback out loud: a student who cannot complete the ratio may report the two terms separately with the ratio recorded as omitted** | §5 names that as the honest fallback and requires that it be stated to them |
| A group predicts D's result and explains it | Expected of a strong group, and [WP-063](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-063.md) §8 objection 2 treats phase 3 being too easy as this day's most likely failure rather than dismissing it. **Give them discipline B's prediction-error distribution and ask which of the two wastes it converts into which, at what point B becomes worse than A, and what the truncation rate would have to be for the answer to reverse** | No supplied answer. It is point 5's territory joined to obligation 2's second half, and it is the payload the day would have used had it been designed the other way round |
| A student freezes at step 4, because it asks them to push a path serving real users past the point where it refuses | **Do not read this as timidity.** A student who has internalised the programme's duty of care is behaving correctly. The replica was offered in advance and is specification-conforming; the controlled-load procedure is bounded and supplied rather than improvised; staff escalation is present and its use is not held against anyone. Phase 6 opens with a staff rollback, which establishes that crossing a boundary and being wrong is survivable and recorded here. **And the strongest reassurance is the platform's own behaviour, which they read before they run the experiment: what lies past the boundary is a refusal, not a damaged service** | §5 names fear of the live system as the characteristic affective failure and says in terms that treating it as timidity would be a serious error |

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in SY301 with IS301. Per [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the binding resource with its arithmetic; the achieved and achievable concurrency and the gap; the reserved-and-unused capacity per request class **with its truncation rate and the cache-served fraction beside it**; the predicted and measured refusal-threshold positions; **the two client behaviours and where the collapse was**; the chosen discipline, its ceiling and its headroom to the threshold; the per-class tail effect at the operating point; the committed threshold-movement signal; the phase 7 outcome; **the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

**A reserved-and-unused figure reported without its truncation rate fails.** This is the day's first hard requirement and it is the one added most recently: the waste and the guarantee it was buying are two halves of one trade, and reporting the first alone converts a decision into a saving.

**An admission discipline reported without its distance to a measured refusal threshold fails.** This is the second.

**A concurrency figure reported without the date of the measurement that established it fails.** This is the second, and it is the [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md)'s as-of-date rule applied to a number rather than to a claim.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**No named technique, policy attribute or accounting rule is assessed.** Serving-system memory management is semi-durable under the register and the platform's accounting is perishable outright; the specification checks the four measurement obligations rather than which discipline the student chose. A student who chooses discipline A and characterises it honestly passes; a student who chooses the currently fashionable one and cannot state its threshold does not.

**Demonstrated rather than operated, and therefore not assessed:** the deployment utilisation figure, the leaky-bucket burst allowance, and the post-completion correction. Students see them, read the documentation that defines them, and are examined on none of them.

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S065](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S065.md).

**Rule-6 discharges.** Two obligations placed on this day are discharged here.

- [S061](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S061.md) named this day as the response occasion for its item 2 binding-resource error rate, requiring an artefact element in which the student states the binding resource for their admission decision and shows the arithmetic. **The element exists as phase 5 step 1, it is assessed, and the cohort's S061 error rate is reported against it at phase 8.** The obligation is discharged.
- [S062](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S062.md) named this day as the response occasion for its item 2 median-and-tail prediction error, requiring an artefact element reporting the tail effect of the student's own admission policy per request class. **The element exists as phase 5 step 6, it is assessed, and the S062 predictions are reported against it at phase 8 under both load conditions.** The obligation is discharged.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions:

- **The item 3 cliff-shape and threshold-position error**, measured against the inferred position from phase 3's five samples, is the cohort's non-linear-saturation calibration at its third exposure. **Response occasion: [S064](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S064.md), phase 5 step 4, where headroom must be stated as distance to the measured threshold in the units of the forthcoming demand change — not as a utilisation percentage — a required artefact element.**
- **The step 3 reserved-and-unused figure and its paired truncation rate**, per class, measure how much already-purchased capacity the cohort is holding unused and what recovering it would cost. **Response occasion: [S065](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S065.md), phase 3, where the cost argument must state the cheapest capacity available before requesting any spend — and must state what taking it would do to the users who would be cut short. Asking for money for capacity the system already holds is a published failure condition of that day's entrustment judgement.**

## Perishable content in this day

**This is the most perishable day in the module.** Serving-system memory management is listed as **semi-durable** in the [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) — re-verified annually, teachable as fact only with an as-of date, and not assessable without one — and the platform's admission accounting is perishable outright. The day is written so that everything assessed is a measurement obligation and everything named is an instance.

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| Concurrency is an allocation decision, not a hardware fact | What each request declares as its output limit |
| Reserving against the worst case costs concurrency in proportion to the variance | An unset output limit, estimated on your behalf, and the guidance to declare it tightly |
| Recovering a reservation converts a guarantee into a probability | A declared output limit that is reached, and an answer that stops mid-sentence |
| There are two wastes and both must be measured | Declared-and-unused capacity, and content the cache serves for free |
| An accounting rule you did not know about is still a reservation | An image counted at a documented worst case whenever estimation is in force |
| A fast refusal is cheaper than a degradation | Refusal at the ceiling with a stated wait interval |
| Non-linear collapse happens wherever a refusal is treated as an error | A client that retries at once instead of honouring the interval |
| A utilisation figure measures reservations, not use | A one-minute ratio of consumed to deployed capacity, corrected after the fact |
| A ceiling on shared capacity is not yours to quote | Standard deployments, whose throughput varies with other customers' demand |

- **Durable:** concurrency as an allocation decision; the fixed term that does not scale; the two wastes and the requirement to measure both; **the paired reporting of waste and truncation as two halves of one trade**; the two forms of over-reservation; the relocation of the non-linear saturation class to the client; and the four measurement obligations at point 7.
- **Perishable:** all four disciplines as instantiated; every measured concurrency, waste fraction and threshold position; every accounting rule, metric name and documented worst case in point 8; and the substrate's own ceiling. **No technique or accounting rule named in phase 4 may appear in an assessment item, and each carries an as-of date in the delivered material.** All were verified for this offering on **2026-07-26**.
- **Local:** the substrate's capacity, its length distribution, its traffic mix, the phase 6 rollback, and the phase 6 agent change.

**Demonstrated rather than operated:** the deployment utilisation accounting in full. What that costs the day is one thing and it is worth naming — **the room never sees a reservation and a use side by side in the platform's own number**, and has to assemble that comparison itself from what requests declared and what they returned. The assembly is more work and is arguably the better lesson, and the design does not pretend the substitution was chosen for that reason.

## What this day does not establish

**What it demonstrates.** Four reservation disciplines, over one workload on one deployment, sampled at five offered-concurrency levels, with everything but the declaration held fixed — and then the same path driven past its refusal threshold twice, under two client behaviours. **The plateau and the collapse are the cleanest demonstration in the module**, because the server is identical in both runs and the difference is entirely in code on the other side of the wire.

**What it cannot demonstrate, by construction.** The platform's own reservation accounting, which is a property of dedicated capacity the programme does not hold. The room sees the one-minute utilisation figure, the burst allowance and the post-completion correction **from a recording, with the documentation open**, and is examined on none of them. The cost is real and the page names it: **the room never sees a reservation and a use side by side in the platform's own number.** Nor does the day establish anything about shared capacity — point 10 says outright that on a standard deployment, whose throughput varies with other customers' demand, **the ceiling is not yours to state at all.**

**What rests on a note, and how deeply.** The account of retained per-request state is cited to [kwon](/research/02-technical-foundations/kwon-pagedattention-vllm.md) and [pope](/research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md). [WP-063](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-063.md)'s Evidence status does something unusual and correct here — it records that the kwon note's account of the system's internals is **reconstructed rather than read**, and declines to claim anything about eviction or preemption on that authority. **It does not say the same about pope, whose note records the identical limitation**: verification against the arXiv abstract page, section outline reconstructed. Both are cited in the same sentence for the same claim and only one carries its depth. **No figure from either enters this wiki.**

**A note that exists, was read in full, and is cited nowhere on this day.** [crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md) states that aggregate performance can conceal failure modes entirely, that the remedy is slicing decided in advance, and that which slices matter is a domain question rather than a statistical one — **which is what per-class reservation accounting is**, and what obligation 2's pairing of waste with truncation depends on. It supplies no magnitude and its limits section forbids carrying any figure out of it. [WP-063](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-063.md) records the omission in its own words: the evidence review stopped at the folders it expected to need.

**What rests on nothing verified here.** Contrasting cases with a single varied factor, pretesting with a sketch demand, pre-commitment before measurement, dual-defeat peer articulation and error management training are all filed as unverified. **Two have full readings here** — [sinha-kapur](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md), whose design guidance names the hold-some-features-invariant rule phase 3's four disciplines obey, and [keith-frese](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md), read in full and page-anchored. **The second records that the vicarious form was excluded from that meta-analysis for failing its operationalisation, and phase 6 here is entirely vicarious**: a supplied staff measurement and a supplied agent change. Neither would lift the no-effect-size rule and the second says so. Retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, pretesting and cognitive load have **no note anywhere** under [research/](/research/99-source-register/source-register.md).

**What rests on no external warrant at all.** The inversion bridge; the four measurement obligations in place of a technique; the five-level sampling with the threshold between adjacent points; the declare-nothing case as payload; the relocation of the saturation class to the client; and the as-of-date fail condition. These are the design's own arguments, stated at [WP-063](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-063.md) §9 as predictions with named instruments, none measured.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **Concurrency is an allocation decision, not a hardware fact.** The capacity you reserved and did not use is the cheapest capacity in the system — already bought, recoverable without a purchase and without anybody's permission — and there are two wastes, not one, both of which must be measured.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** The account of retained per-request state is grounded in [research/02-technical-foundations/kwon-pagedattention-vllm.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/kwon-pagedattention-vllm.md) and [research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md), cited for **direction and mechanism only**: the state grows with sequence length and with requests in flight, and the fixed term does not scale. **No magnitude is asserted from either.** The admission accounting at point 8 is vendor documentation read at a stated date, and every number you measured today belongs to one deployment on one afternoon.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Never state a concurrency figure without the date of the measurement behind it.**
- **Report reserved-and-unused beside the truncation rate.** They move in opposite directions, and the first alone turns a trade into a saving.
- **Find the refusal threshold by driving load at it**, then run both client behaviours — the collapse may be on your side of the wire.
- **Sort what you can honestly say into the five classes** on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md): a declared output limit is a configured control, the achieved concurrency is an observed result, and only the second is evidence.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Treating a refusal as an error. A fast refusal with a stated interval is cheaper than the work it declined, and the outage comes from a client that retries at once instead of waiting.
