# S040 · Staying in the Problem: An Endurance Exercise ⊘

**Module:** [M08](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M08.md) · **Quarter:** [Q4](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q4.md) · **Complexity class:** 3
**Strands:** PE · CF · SY · ME
**Whitepaper:** [WP-040](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-040.md)
**Depends on:** S036
**Re-tests:** S030, S037, S038, S039
**Platform anchor:** the substrate's API Management gateway and the quota constraint behind it, unresolved · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (**read-only, deliberately**) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

⊘ **Assessment-bearing day, and the quarter's entrustment judgement.**

---

## What this day is for

Every instinct a competent engineer has pushes toward resolution. The problem is understood well enough, the ambiguity is tolerable, and there is work to be done — so they converge, and the convergence feels like progress.

Sometimes it is. Sometimes the problem was not understood, the ambiguity was the whole difficulty, and six weeks are spent building the wrong thing well.

**This day trains the opposite capacity: staying in an unresolved problem, deliberately, for six hours, and producing a characterisation rather than a solution.**

The problem is real, staff have not solved it, and nobody in the room will solve it today. That is the exercise.

**And one of the things in the evidence pack is a working implementation.** Before the day, the request was handed to an agent, which built it, deployed it and reported success. It runs. It is in front of you. Nobody thinks it is the answer, and explaining why is the hardest thing the day asks.

## Why the server is read-only today

Every other day this quarter runs the Azure MCP Server in write configuration, because that is its default: the read-only start parameter is `false` unless somebody sets it. **Today it is set.** Nothing on this day changes any infrastructure, and the constraint is stated rather than assumed, because a room holding an unresolved problem for six hours with an agent that can deploy is a room that will deploy.

That is not a claim that students lack discipline. It is the day's own subject applied to its own conditions: **the cheapest way to prevent a convergence is to remove the action it would take**, and every other mechanism on this day is more expensive and less reliable than one parameter.

## The delivery standard on this day

This day carries **principle 4 of the [FDE Delivery Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/14-FDE-Delivery-Standard.md): anchor decisions in working software rather than in presentations**, in that page's wording rather than as a quotation — and it is the one day in the strand that teaches the principle's limit rather than its use. A decision *anchored* in running code is better than a decision anchored in a deck. **A decision *concluded* from running code is a different act**, and the implementation on the table is the evidence for the difference: it runs, it does exactly what was asked, and it establishes nothing about whether the requester is better off.

**What the principle costs here is the day's entire difficulty.** A standard that rewards demonstrable software rewards convergence, and convergence is what this day withholds for six hours. A student who has absorbed principle 4 as *a demo settles it* arrives holding the disposition the entrustment judgement is formed against, and the fifth defence question is where it surfaces.

**The playbook artefact already on the table is the engineering feasibility spike, in its disposal half.** Its standing rule is that **the code written for a spike is not the solution's code**; the phase 3 implementation is spike output, which is why it is evidence rather than a proposal and why adopting it is the one move the day forbids. **No new assessed form is introduced from either source.** This day's checked-element list is closed, an assessment-bearing day cannot take on a new assessed artefact, and what the standard supplies here is a boundary rather than a deliverable. [WP-040](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-040.md)'s evidence status records that **neither source reports any outcome for the practice**.

## The entrustment judgement, stated to students before phase 1

> Today's judgement is on one activity: **characterise a problem from evidence and defend the characterisation to someone who asked for something else.** It is a precursor to the programme's third entrusted activity and it is not that activity.
>
> Two assessors will each form an independent judgement before conferring. **Each forms their level from your written characterisation before the defence begins, and uses the defence to test it** — per rater discipline rule 7, the level is formed from the artefact and not from the conversation. The narrative requirement applies. The expected level for a second-year student is 2a to 2b.
>
> **The judgement at S030 is not carried forward.** It is not averaged with today's and it does not raise or lower your starting point. Each occasion is judged on its own.

**Staff preparation.**

- **Set the read-only start parameter, and confirm it.** Every other day this quarter runs the server in write configuration because that is the default; today it is deliberately changed. The day's own argument is that **the cheapest way to prevent a convergence is to remove the action it would take**, and an unset parameter makes the whole prohibition an appeal to discipline.
- **Build the implementation from the request alone.** One line, verbatim, nothing added. If staff improve the prompt, the artefact stops being evidence about what the request contained.
- **Brief assessors on rater discipline rule 7.** The level is formed from the written characterisation before the defence; the defence tests it and does not create it.
- **Sequence the fifth defence question before the sixth.** The student disposes of the artefact before facing the person, and the order is deliberate.
- **Assessors are absent for phases 5 and 6.** The firewall applies and this is its fourth demonstration under assessment conditions.

**Platform prerequisites.**

- **The Azure MCP Server in read-only configuration** — the one prerequisite on this day that is a setting rather than a resource, and the one the day makes a teaching point of at point 8.
- **The agent's implementation, deployed and reachable**, with telemetry students may query for as long as they find it useful.
- The **Microsoft Learn MCP Server**, for the metric definition behind the phase 6 case.
- **An administrator can gate the read permissions on the running implementation's telemetry.** Note that read-only is what the day wants; a permission set that is too generous is the failure mode here, which inverts every other day this quarter.

**The live system this day depends on.** The substrate as a source of an unsolved problem, three users' observation records and its own data — and one deployed implementation that has to stay up for six hours while nobody is allowed to change it. **The problem is the dependency with no substitute.** It must be real, it must be unresolved, and staff must still be working on it, because point 9's promise — that the characterisations go to people working on it — is the day's principal answer to futility.

**If a prerequisite is missing.** If the implementation cannot be built or cannot be kept running, the fifth defence question has nothing to point at and the day reverts to its pre-platform form, which could not make the working-software argument at all. If no genuinely unsolved problem is available, **the day does not run**: no page here states a substitute, and a problem with a known answer turns an endurance exercise into a guessing game with assessors who know the answer. Neither fallback is written down anywhere in this repository.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items, sealed and opened at phase 8.

1. What do you already believe the answer is?
2. How confident are you, and on what evidence?
3. At what point today do you expect to want to stop analysing and start proposing?
4. Predict your supervision level, and say why.
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** One of today's evidence items is an implementation the agent produced from the request alone. Write down **what you expect it to have built, and the one thing about the problem it will have had no way to know.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 1 is the day's most important item. **Every student has an answer**, and writing it down is what makes the rest of the day honest.

Item 5 is not a question about the agent's competence. It is a question about what the request contained, asked in a form where the answer is checkable an hour later.

**Warrant.** Pretesting the held solution so that the day's characterisation can be compared against it. Item 3 asks the student to predict their own convergence point, which is the behaviour under training. Item 5 is administered at phase 1 and may not migrate, per instrumentation rule 8.

## Phase 3 · The problem (60 min)

**A real unsolved substrate problem** is presented — one staff have worked on and not resolved, with the evidence they gathered and the approaches they abandoned.

Students receive:

- the original request and who made it;
- three observation records from different users;
- the substrate's relevant data, including the gateway's own record of what it did;
- a summary of what staff tried and why each attempt was abandoned;
- **a working implementation of the request, built by an agent from the request alone, deployed and running.**

The sixth item is operated rather than read. It has an endpoint, it has telemetry, and students may drive traffic through it and measure it for as long as they find it useful. **It is evidence, not a proposal.** Nobody in the room authored it, nobody is being asked to endorse it, and adopting it as an answer is the one move the day forbids.

Its prompt record is supplied with it and is one line long: the request, verbatim, with nothing added. That brevity is the point. **The agent was given exactly what the requester gave the engineer, and it produced something that works** — which is either evidence that the request was right or evidence that working is not the same as right, and deciding which is most of the day.

Students work individually. **No proposals are permitted**, and the read-only configuration means none can be deployed.

The required output at the end of the hour is a **written characterisation**: what the problem is, what evidence supports that, and what would change it.

**Warrant.** The day's substance. Supplying staff's abandoned approaches is deliberate — it removes the easy moves and forces the student past the point where they would otherwise converge. **Supplying the agent's implementation removes a different easy move**: without it, a student who privately holds the obvious solution can spend the hour believing it would have worked. With it, the obvious solution has been built, it is running, and its inadequacy has to be argued rather than assumed. The day has no contrasting-case set and never had one; the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](/archive/bsc-programme/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) permits the departure for assessment-bearing days, and [WP-040](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-040.md) §4 argues it.

## Phase 4 · The defence (60 min)

Groups of four. Each student defends for fifteen minutes to **two assessors and the person who made the original request**.

**Assessors have read the written characterisation and formed a level and a narrative before the defence begins**, independently, per rater discipline rule 7. The defence tests that level; it does not create it.

Questions published in advance:

- What is the problem?
- What is your evidence, and what does it not establish?
- What did you believe before today, and what changed it?
- What would you need to know that you do not?
- **There is a working implementation of what I asked for, running, in front of us. What is wrong with it?**
- *(From the requester)* I asked for something specific. Why have you not given me an answer?

The fifth question is the day's platform-anchored demand and the sixth is its human one, and they are asked in that order deliberately: the student must dispose of the artefact before facing the person.

**Warrant.** Assessment on defensible characterisation under questioning from an interested party. The requester's presence is what makes the final question real. The fifth question is the addition the practical turn requires and it is the day's sharpest discriminator: a student who cannot say what is wrong with a working implementation of the request has not characterised the problem, however well they describe it.

## Phase 5 · Consolidating instruction (35 min) — assessors absent

1. **Why convergence feels like progress,** and why the feeling is uninformative.
2. **The cost of premature convergence,** stated with the substrate's own history: three cases, with what each cost.
3. **The cost of endless analysis,** stated equally plainly. This day is not an argument for never deciding.
4. **How to tell them apart:** you may stop analysing when further evidence would not change what you do. Not when you feel confident.
5. **The cohort's characterisations,** shown unattributed. They differ, and the differences trace to which evidence each student weighted.
6. **What the assessors were listening for:** whether the student could say what their evidence did not establish.
7. **What a working implementation is evidence of.** It establishes that the request is implementable, what implementing it costs, and what it does to the system. It establishes **nothing** about whether the requester is better off, because that is a claim about a person and the artefact never met them. **The speed is the trap.** An implementation that took an afternoon feels provisional and an implementation that took six weeks feels committed, and neither feeling is about whether it is right — which means the agent has removed the one signal engineers were unconsciously using.
8. **A default the platform chose for you, overridden today.** The Azure MCP Server's read-only start parameter defaults to `false`; write access is the condition unless somebody changes it. Today somebody did. **You should know that the setting exists, that it was not on by accident, and that on every other day this quarter it was off.** The wider point is the one M08 has been making for five days: a default is a decision somebody made about your work, and the ones that matter most are the ones you never see because they never produced an error.
9. **What happens to your characterisations** — they go to staff, who are still working on this.

**Warrant.** Consolidation after assessment. Point 3 is the day's honest counterweight and is given equal weight to point 2, because a day arguing only for delay would produce a worse engineer than the one it is correcting. **Points 7 and 8 carry the day's platform content.** Point 7 is placed here rather than at phase 3 because it is the answer to the question phase 4 asked, and delivering it earlier would supply the defence rather than test it. Point 8 satisfies the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s default requirement; the day's phase 4 is a defence rather than a consolidation, so both platform points sit here, and [WP-040](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-040.md) §4 records the departure.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min) — assessors absent

Opened by the **staff member who has been working on this problem**, describing where they converged too early and what it cost.

Then the agent case, from the substrate's own history: a Copilot-authored change that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. A user reported that the service kept refusing them. An engineer asked the agent to check, the agent produced a correct query against the metric named availability, and the answer was 100%. The metric excludes 429s by definition. The user's report was recorded as unreproducible and closed.

Attention goes to why review did not catch it, and the answer is uncomfortable: **there was nothing to catch.** The query was right, the chart was right, the metric was real, and the reviewer's job as they understood it was to check those things. What failed was upstream of the review — the question "is the service available to this user" was answered by an instrument that defines availability without reference to this user. Two engineers and an agent all did their work correctly and a person was told they were wrong.

Then students: **the moment today when you wanted to propose something, and what it was.**

**Firewall applies. Assessors are absent and remain uninformed. Fourth demonstration under assessment conditions.**

**Warrant.** Error management on the day's specific discipline, opened by someone who failed at it on this exact problem. The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition; the room has met this failure twice already, at S037 in a low-stakes setting and at S039 with a person on the other side of it, and it arrives here a third time as the closing argument — **the module's whole subject is what happens when everybody does their job correctly and the wrong question was asked.**

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (25 min)

Pairs. Each student states **their item 1 answer** — what they believed before the day started — and their final characterisation.

The partner's task: **do these describe the same problem?**

Where they do, the pair examines whether the day's evidence was used or was assembled around a conclusion already held.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with a prior-belief comparison. Per the [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s rule 3, the outcome is recorded in the artefact.

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (25 min)

- **Sealed items opened.** Item 3's predicted convergence point against when the student actually wanted to stop; item 4's predicted level against assigned; **item 5's prediction against what the agent actually built and what it had no way to know.** The cohort's item 5 accuracy is recorded in two parts — identifying what would be built, and naming the missing input — and the design's expectation is that the first is high and the second is low.
- **Assessor narratives returned in writing,** with both shown where they diverged and the resolution stated.
- The S010 psychological-safety instrument re-administered. Fourth administration.
- Each student writes what they would need to do differently for the next level.
- **The long-horizon prediction check is sealed.** Each student records what they expect this problem to turn out to be, and what evidence they expect will settle it. The envelope is opened twice: at [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md), where the student states what their own measurement did and did not license about it, and at [S090](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S090.md), where the final defence asks what they got wrong and why. Per instrumentation rule 6, the deferral names both occasions; **whether those days carry an artefact element that consumes it is a commitment they must discharge and is recorded in [WP-040](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-040.md) §6 as unverified from here.**
- **The quarter closes.** The module's solution prohibition ends, and students are told they may now propose.

## When it does not work

These are [WP-040](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-040.md) §5's predicted failure modes, restated as instruction to the person in the room. The whitepaper is the authority; where this section and §5 diverge, §5 is right. Nothing here is assessed and nothing here changes what is.

**A student with product or consulting experience produces a fluent characterisation and will have converged earliest**, because experience supplies plausible answers faster. Their item 1 and their final characterisation will match, and their evidence section will be an assembled justification for a position they held before the day began. Phase 7's comparison is the detector, and the specification already requires an account of why the day's evidence did not change a prior belief where it did not.

**A student with no ready answer may find the hour genuinely difficult and produce a thin characterisation.** All the evidence is supplied, the four required parts are stated, and the specification assesses parts rather than depth. **§5's residual risk is worth knowing before you read the artefacts**: their statement of what the evidence does not establish may be the strongest part of the submission and their problem statement the weakest, which passes — and is arguably the correct outcome for this day.

**A student adopts the implementation.** This is the platform decision's characteristic failure. Someone who has spent the week building will read a running system as an answer and shape a characterisation around it. Three things are already in place: the specification's fail clause on endorsement, the fifth defence question asked in front of two assessors, and the read-only server, which means an endorsement can never become an action. **§5's residual risk is the one to watch for: a characterisation whose problem statement is the implementation's premise restated, which is endorsement in a form the fail clause does not catch** — and phase 7's prior-belief comparison is the only detector, **weaker here than anywhere else, because the implementation was in the room before item 1 was written about it.**

**Phase 3 has no adjudicator.** Unlike every other phase 3 in the programme, nothing resolves. That is the design and it is stated to students rather than discovered by them. For a student who genuinely stalls, **the instructor supplies a fourth observation record rather than a hint** — which restarts the evidence work without supplying a direction.

**The day is read as an argument against deciding.** Point 3's cost-of-endless-analysis is given equal weight to point 2 for exactly this reason; point 4's stopping criterion is a permission as much as a restraint; phase 8 lifts the prohibition explicitly; and the scope statement at point 7 says that building to learn is legitimate and that what the day prohibits is building as an answer. Deliver all four.

**Futility.** A day producing no solution, on a problem nobody will solve, assessed by people who also do not know the answer, can read as performance. **The running implementation makes this worse rather than better** — a student can now point at a working system and ask what six hours added. Four answers are available: the characterisations go to staff who are working on it, point 9 says so in the room, Q5 reports whether any of them changed the approach, and the long-horizon check at S050 and S090 gives the day a resolution it cannot supply itself. **§5 is candid that the last is the strongest available answer and that it arrives a quarter and two years late respectively.**

## Assessment

**Assessment-bearing. The quarter's entrustment judgement.**

Three outputs:

1. **The module artefact** — S036 to S039 specification items — pass/fail, tokens available.
2. **The characterisation and its defence** — a specification decision.
3. **The entrustment judgement** on *characterise a problem from evidence and defend the characterisation to someone who asked for something else*, with mandatory narrative substantiation, formed independently by two assessors **from the written characterisation before the defence begins**.

Per rule 1, the checked elements of the characterisation are: a stated problem; the evidence supporting it; an explicit statement of what the evidence does not establish; a statement of what would change it; **an account of what the agent's implementation does and does not establish**; the phase 7 prior-belief comparison outcome; the absence of any proposed solution; **the prompt record; the intervention record; the frontier statement.**

**On the three Copilot Practice Standard elements at an assessment-bearing day.** The prompt record is supplied rather than produced: it is the one line the agent was given, and the student reproduces it and says what it did not contain. **The intervention record is empty by construction** — correcting the agent would be proposing, and the day forbids it — so the standard's escape clause is not a formality here but the sharpest element on the page: **the artefact must demonstrate that the student's own characterisation would have detected the error the agent made.** A characterisation that could not have caught it fails, whether or not it is otherwise well written. The frontier statement names one thing the agent did that no student could have done as fast, and one thing it did badly — and the second is always the same thing, which is why the day asks for it.

**A characterisation identical in substance to the student's item 1 prior belief, with no account of why the day's evidence did not change it, fails.**

**A characterisation that endorses the agent's implementation as the answer fails.** The module's standing rule, in the form the practical turn makes possible.

Nothing else is assessed — including whether the characterisation is correct, which nobody knows.

**Dispositions observed:** integrity, in the item 1 disclosure and its phase 7 comparison; humility, in the response to the fourth and fifth defence questions.

## What this day does not establish

**Demonstrated.** That a cohort given one unsolved problem and six hours produced characterisations that differed, and that the differences traced to which evidence each student weighted; the cohort's item 5 accuracy, in two parts; and what the fourth administration of the psychological-safety instrument returned. One problem, one room, one day.

**Read rather than demonstrated.** The read-only start parameter's default, and the definition of the availability metric behind the phase 6 case. Vendor documentation.

**Not established, and the page says so.** **Whether the characterisation is correct — nobody knows**, which is the whole design and is stated in the specification. Whether any student's characterisation changes what staff do, which is reported in Q5 rather than today. And the long-horizon prediction, which is sealed and opened at S050 and S090 — with [WP-040](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-040.md) §6 recording that **whether those days carry an artefact element consuming it is a commitment they must discharge, and is unverified from here.**

**Asserted.** That convergence feels like progress and that the feeling is uninformative. That the stopping criterion at point 4 — stop when further evidence would not change what you do — is the right one. And that six hours of withheld resolution trains a capacity rather than only testing patience. All three are the design's own reasoning and none is a finding.

**What rests on a `research/` note.** The entrustment apparatus, in full: narrative substantiation, independent judgement before conferring, the supervision levels, the non-accumulation of levels, and the what-if requirement that shapes the published defence questions — at [research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md) and [research/04-professional-formation/ten-cate-epas.md](/research/04-professional-formation/ten-cate-epas.md), both full readings. **Direction and mechanism only, and no claim is made about entrustment reliability at any number of observations.** The negotiation framing behind the sixth defence question, at [research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md). The spike's disposal rule, at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md).

**What rests on nothing.** The day's own psychology. **Premature convergence — the observation the entire exercise is built on — has no note anywhere under [research/](/research/99-source-register/source-register.md)**, and neither does the stopping criterion at point 4. The learning mechanisms are in the same position: **retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, pretesting and cognitive load have no note anywhere**, which matters more here than on an ordinary day because phase 2 is an extended cumulative retrieval across five days and phase 1 is a sealed pretest that a judgement is partly read against. Error management, which carries phase 6, does have one, at [research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md), for direction and mechanism only. And **no note covers Azure Monitor metrics or their definitions**, so the metric behind the phase 6 case rests on documentation this repository has not read.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **Convergence feels like progress, and the feeling is uninformative.** You may stop analysing when further evidence would not change what you do — not when you feel confident. And a working implementation establishes that the request is implementable and what implementing it costs; it establishes nothing about whether the requester is better off, because that is a claim about a person the artefact never met.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** Premature convergence, problem characterisation as a distinct deliverable, and the entrustment apparatus — narrative substantiation, independent judgement before conferring, non-accumulation of levels — are grounded in [research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md), [research/04-professional-formation/ten-cate-epas.md](/research/04-professional-formation/ten-cate-epas.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md). **Direction and mechanism only; no claim is made about entrustment reliability at any number of observations.**

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Write down the answer you already hold, first.** Everyone has one, and recording it is what makes the rest of the analysis honest.
- **State what your evidence does not establish** in the same document as what it does.
- **Prefer a control you have verified to one you have inferred.** The read-only parameter was set rather than assumed; on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md), a protection you inferred from adjacent documentation is *unsupported inference* and licenses nothing.
- **Remove the action rather than resolving not to take it.** It is the cheapest way to prevent a convergence and the most reliable.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Reading speed as provisionality. An implementation that took an afternoon feels tentative and one that took six weeks feels committed, and neither feeling is about whether it is right — so the signal engineers were quietly using has gone.
