## What this day is for

S024 established that you must say when something cannot be done. This day is about what you ship instead.

The module's problem is now inverted. Having accepted that optimality is unavailable, the engineer must choose what to promise — and the temptation is to promise nothing, or to promise informally, or to promise something that sounds like optimality without being it. All three are common and all three are failures of the same kind: **the caller cannot tell what they are getting.**

The target: **every heuristic ships with a statement of what it guarantees, expressed in terms the caller can check.** An approximation ratio, a restricted input class on which it is exact, an anytime property, or — if none of these is available — a measured distribution with its horizon. Something. The one unacceptable answer is a heuristic with no stated promise, because a caller who cannot check has to trust, and trust is not a guarantee.

**Today the module's own service supplies both the best case and the worst case for that argument.**

The best case is that the approximation has an adjudicator you already own. The same field the approximate search runs on will answer an exhaustive query, and the documentation names that exhaustive query as the way to build a ground-truth set against which an approximation's recall is measured. **A promise the students can prove is a command away** — which is rare in this programme and is why the day is placed here.

The worst case arrives with the evaluators. Foundry offers two ways to score the same retrieval. One computes its metrics arithmetically against human relevance labels and needs no model at all. The other asks a language model to judge, returns a score from one to five, applies a default pass threshold of three, and attaches a written reason. The second looks like the stronger evidence and is the weaker: **no agreement with human judgement is published for it, or for any other evaluator in this stack.** Every judged score the students produce today is uncalibrated, they are taught to write that word, and it is the sentence the measurement strand spends the rest of the quarter earning.

This is the module's terminal day and the quarter's bridge into measurement.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items. Three heuristics for a substrate problem are supplied, each with a description written the way such things are usually written.

1. For each, state what it guarantees.
2. For each, state what a caller could check to know whether the guarantee held.
3. Which of the three would you deploy, and what would you tell the caller?
4. Predict: are any of the three descriptions actually making a guarantee?
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to *tell me how good this retrieval configuration is.* Write down what it will report — and **name what its number will not be a measure of.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 4's answer is that none of them is, and most students believe at least one is. Item 5's expectation is that a majority will predict a number and a pass, and that very few will name the thing that is missing, because nothing in their education so far has suggested that a scoring instrument might itself be unvalidated.

**Warrant.** Pretesting on the reading of real guarantee language. The descriptions are drawn from genuine documentation and their vagueness is not exaggerated. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8, and it is the day's bridge into M06: a student who cannot yet name what a judged score lacks is a student for whom S026 through S029 have work to do.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups receive **five approaches to one intractable substrate problem** — returning the right documents for the substrate's real queries — each with a genuinely different guarantee form, and each runnable against the module's index.

| | Approach | What it can promise |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Exhaustive scan | Exact. The global nearest neighbours, provably, at a cost the day measures |
| **B** | Approximate search with the filter applied during traversal | Exact in one respect — the requested count is returned if it exists — and approximate in the other |
| **C** | Approximate search with reranking, scored by `document_retrieval` against human relevance labels | A measured distribution, with ranking metrics **and a count of how many retrieved documents the labels do not cover** |
| **D** | The same configuration scored by the LLM-judged `retrieval` evaluator | A score from one to five, a default pass threshold of three, a pass label and a written reason |
| **E** | A configuration with no proof and no measurement | Nothing. **It performs best on the supplied queries** |

Groups run all five, rank them for deployment, and commit. **E usually wins on the supplied queries, and D usually ranks second**, because D is the only approach in the room that produces a sentence explaining itself.

Then a caller's requirement is revealed: they need to be able to tell their own customer what the system promises.

Task after: **re-rank, and state what changed.** Then a second task, harder: **E and D both fall. State why they fall for different reasons.**

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across guarantee forms with a late-revealed criterion, now executed against a real index and real evaluators. Approach E remains the phase's payload: it is genuinely the best performer and the worst thing to ship, and the reason is not performance. **Approach D is the phase's second payload and is new with the platform anchor.** E has no evidence; D has evidence produced by an instrument nobody has validated, and the two are not the same defect. Groups reliably collapse them — both are excluded, so both must be excluded for the same reason — and separating them is the hardest thing the room does all week. It is also the distinction the whole measurement strand rests on, and a group that reaches it here meets S026 with the question already formed.

**Approach D's score is uncalibrated and the day says so in those words.** No agreement between any Foundry evaluator and human judgement is published. That is not a criticism of the evaluator; it is a fact about what is known, and the correct professional response is to report the score with the sentence attached, which is what the specification requires.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **What you can promise when you cannot promise optimality** — the four forms: proved ratio, restricted exactness, anytime quality, measured distribution with horizon. **This list is not exhaustive. It omits guarantees conditional on input parameters, probabilistic approximation schemes, and guarantees that hold only in the limit — all real, all requiring machinery this day does not have. It is the list of promises a student can construct and a caller can check.**
2. **The checkability requirement.** A guarantee the caller cannot verify is a claim about your competence, not about the system.
3. **Approximation ratios,** worked on approach A: what a ratio bounds, and what it does not bound about any individual instance — S022's population-versus-individual sentence, directly.
4. **Restricted exactness** and the obligation that comes with it: you must be able to detect when an instance falls outside the class. Approach B's promise is of this kind and its condition is written into the vendor's own sentence — *if they exist in the index* — which is a restriction the caller must be told about and usually is not.
5. **Anytime algorithms** and why the quality signal matters more than the quality.
6. **The measured distribution** as a legitimate last resort, and what makes it legitimate: sample provenance, horizon, and the conditions under which it was taken. **Approach C makes provenance mechanical.** Its metric set includes a count of how many retrieved documents carry no relevance judgement at all, which is the provenance question asked by the tool rather than by a conscientious engineer. A high count does not mean the configuration is bad; it means **your measurement covers less of what you measured than you thought**, and reporting the metrics without it is the sample-provenance failure this point exists to prevent.
7. **A judged score is a measurement whose instrument has not been characterised.** Approach D produces a number, a threshold, a pass label and a reason. It has the form of evidence completely. **No agreement between this evaluator and human judgement is published, so nothing is known about what the number tracks**, and the only honest way to report it is with that stated. This is not a reason to avoid it — an uncalibrated instrument used knowingly is a legitimate tool, and used unknowingly it is worse than none. S029 designs an instrument; S048 characterises a judge; today the job is only to say the word.
8. **Approach E, and why performance is not the criterion.** The module's closing argument. **And approach D, and why it fails for a different reason:** E offers no evidence and D offers evidence of unknown quality, and a professional register that cannot separate those two will accept either.
9. **Defaults are opinions and thresholds are policy.** Three were chosen for you. **The pass threshold of three on a scale of one to five is a decision nobody made** — it is a default, it decides which of your configurations ships, and it will be quoted in a meeting as though it meant something. One evaluator requires you to name a model deployment and the other requires none, **so one of your two quality numbers depends on which judge you happened to deploy and the other does not.** And a minimum-score threshold on the retrieval side is available only for a single-method query, because the fused score's range is smaller and less stable — which means the natural place to put a quality floor is the one place the platform will not let you.
10. **Writing the promise:** one sentence, checkable, addressed to the caller, with what happens when it is not met.
11. **What is perishable and what is not.** The metric names, the score ranges, the threshold's value, the evaluator catalogue and the preview boundaries are perishable, and the whole evaluator set may be renamed within the life of this cohort. **The durable claims are that a promise must be checkable by its recipient, that a measured distribution is only as good as its stated provenance, and that a score from an uncharacterised instrument must be reported as such.** The correspondence is safe for reasoning about what a retrieval promise can be and how to state it, and unsafe for reasoning about how good any of today's configurations actually is — today's measurements are one corpus, one label set and one judge, and step 7 is where a student who forgets that would fail.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3's re-ranking. Point 1 carries the enumeration-scope statement. **Point 2 deliberately does not carry the criterion scope statement the rule now also requires**, because the rule was widened to criteria *because of* this day's omission, and the [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) states that each widening applies from the following seminar day and that the earlier days are not retrospectively amended so that the failure remains legible; [WP-025](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-025.md) §8 records why the platform turn's opportunity to repair it was declined. Point 7 is the day's principal gain from the platform anchor and is the sentence the quarter is built to install; point 9 satisfies the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s default-naming requirement, and point 11 carries the strong-form scope statement, which binds every platform-specific phase 4 point and is new content rather than an amendment.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 2. The approach and the promise are both the student's. This phase produces a build.**

Students take the problem they argued was intractable at S024 and, using Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server in read configuration and the Learn MCP Server for grounding:

1. **Choose an approach,** from any of the forms at point 1 or another.
2. **Establish the strongest guarantee you can** — prove the ratio, characterise the exact class, demonstrate the anytime property, or measure the distribution with its horizon. **Where the guarantee is measured, the exhaustive configuration is the adjudicator and the measurement is run, not described.**
3. **State the detection method** for the guarantee's failure: how the caller, not you, would know.
4. **Write the promise,** one sentence, checkable.
5. **State what happens when it is not met** — the fallback, the notification, or the accepted loss.
6. **Test the promise against the S024 requester's reply,** which you wrote yesterday: is it the alternative you offered, and does the thing you demonstrated yesterday deliver the promise you are making today?
7. **State the instance class** on which your approach performs worst, whether the substrate produces it, and **what your measurement does not cover** — in the shape point 6 supplies.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned.
- **Intervention record.** Every correction, and what it was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that your own design would have caught an intervention-worthy error — a coverage assertion that fails the run when the uncovered-document count exceeds a stated level is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch.

**Where a judged score is reported it carries the word *uncalibrated*, and one sentence saying what evidence would remove the word and who would have to produce it.** A promise resting on an uncharacterised instrument without both fails the specification.

**The minimum-score threshold parameter is preview and is demonstrated rather than operated.** It is shown once, it is not available for step 2, and it is not assessed.

**Fading:** step 2 supplies templates for the ratio proof and the measurement protocol; step 4's sentence form is supplied.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 2 continuing directly from S024's artefact, which is the module's only two-day task and makes the module's argument concrete: yesterday you said it could not be done, today you say what you will do instead. Step 2's *run, not described* clause and step 6's second question are new with the platform anchor and are the pair that closes the module — the alternative demonstrated yesterday and the promise made today must be the same object, and until this revision nothing forced them to be.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate heuristic deployed with an informal promise that was understood differently by the team and by the caller — with attention to the exact words used and how each side read them.

Then the agent case: a Copilot evaluation that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. Asked how good the retrieval configuration was, the agent wired up the LLM-judged evaluator, ran it across the query set, and reported a mean score above the threshold with a high pass rate and a paragraph of sensible commentary. Everything it did was correct. The evaluator was the right one for a dataset with no ground truth, the data mapping was right, the code was clean, and the summary was accurate about what the tool had returned. **It was merged as evidence that retrieval quality was good.** Attention goes to why review did not catch it: the output had a number, a threshold, a label and a reason — it had every visible feature of a measurement — and the missing feature, that nobody has published what the instrument agrees with, is not visible anywhere in the output and is not a thing the reviewers knew to ask for.

Then students: a promise you made about something you had not measured, or a number you accepted today because it had a threshold next to it.

**Warrant.** Error management on the day's professional content. The disclosure's focus on the exact words is deliberate: this failure is almost always a wording failure rather than a competence failure. The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is the module's last and most consequential one, because it is the failure mode the next two years of this programme are most likely to reproduce.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student's partner takes their step 4 promise and must **construct the instance on which it is violated — and run it** — then say whether the step 3 detection method would have caught it.

Two outcomes are acceptable: the promise holds, or the promise fails and detection catches it. The unacceptable outcome is a failure that detection misses.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation combining the S006 breaking-caller move with S018's detectability pair. This is the module's terminal integration and the students are told so. Requiring the breaking instance to be executed rather than argued is the platform anchor's contribution and removes the phase's weakest outcome, which was an attack nobody tested. **The design defect [WP-025](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-025.md) §8 recorded is not repaired here and the concession stands unchanged:** nothing in the phase calibrates what counts as a fair break, the repair it names — requiring the partner to state whether the substrate could produce their instance before detection is tested — is one sentence and is still not made, and the module still ends on its least-specified thirty minutes.

## When it does not work

These are [WP-025](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-025.md) §5's predicted failure modes, restated as instruction to the person in the room. The whitepaper is the authority; where this section and §5 diverge, §5 is right.

**A student with an optimisation or operations-research background selects and establishes a guarantee fluently, and writes a promise nobody can check.** Their step 4 sentence will be precise and unreadable: the ratio, the conditions and the qualifications, and no non-specialist able to use it. **Phase 8's judgement is made by the whole class and a technically impeccable unreadable promise fails it** — which is the countermeasure, and it only works if the class actually judges rather than defers. §5 notes this is the second consecutive day on which technical fluency makes the assessed element harder.

**A student who cannot prove a ratio has three other promise forms available**, and the measured-distribution route is open to anyone who completed [S022](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S022.md) — and is cheaper still now that the adjudicator is a query parameter rather than an apparatus. Templates exist for both and the day carries the module's lowest intrinsic load. *Residual risk the design accepts:* they may default to the measured distribution when a stronger guarantee was available. That is a real loss, step 2's *strongest you can establish* framing catches it only partially, and it is not worth converting into a penalty.

**Phase 3's first ranking is straightforward and is meant to be.** The difficulty is the re-ranking, and it is in *articulating* the reason rather than in reaching the conclusion — most groups know E is out and cannot say what principle excluded it. Ask whether approach C, with labelled measurement and no proof, is excluded by the same principle. It is not, and the distinction is the day's content. **If a group separates D from E without prompting, ask what evidence would move D into C's position and who would have to produce it.** That is [S048](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S048.md)'s question and it is genuinely hard.

**The checkability requirement read as bureaucratic.** A student who has built a heuristic that works, and is told it cannot ship because the promise is not verifiable by a non-specialist, may conclude the programme prefers paperwork to results. Three things push back: phase 6's incident, where the wording failure had a real cost; phase 3's construction, where the room excludes E rather than being told to; and point 8's framing of the exclusion as a consequence rather than a rule. **§5's honest position is that this risk is highest for the strongest students and that all three countermeasures are argumentative rather than structural.**

**The word without the understanding**, which §5 predicts will be the day's most common shallow pass. The specification requires a judged score to be reported as *uncalibrated*, and the cheapest route to that is to type the word. The countermeasure is the second sentence — what evidence would remove the word, and who would have to produce it — which cannot be typed without a model of what calibration is. **The design does not claim that is sufficient.**

**And the phase 7 defect, which is not repaired.** Nothing in the phase calibrates what counts as a fair break: a partner may construct an instance the substrate could never produce, defeat the promise with it, and the student has learned nothing about their own design. The repair [WP-025](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-025.md) §8 names is one sentence — require the partner to state whether the substrate could produce the instance *before* detection is tested — and it is still not made. The module ends on its least-specified thirty minutes and the facilitator should know that going in.

## Assessment

Specification item in CF103 and MS103 jointly, and the module's terminal artefact.

Checked elements: the approach; the established guarantee with its evidence, run rather than described; the caller-side detection method; the one-sentence promise; the not-met procedure; the S024 consistency check in both directions; the worst-case instance class with substrate reachability and the measurement's stated coverage; the prompt record; the intervention record; the frontier statement.

**A promise the class cannot judge checkable at phase 8 fails the specification.** **A promise resting on a judged score that is not reported as uncalibrated, with the evidence that would remove the word and the party who would have to produce it, fails the specification.** **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

Nothing else is assessed. **The preview threshold parameter demonstrated at phase 5 is not assessed.**

Each widening applies from the following seminar day and earlier days are not retrospectively amended, which is why point 2 carries no criterion scope statement and says so — [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) |
| **The five classes** | Configured control, executable check, observed result, outcome evidence, unsupported inference — [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md) |

## What this day does not establish

**Demonstrated.** That the configuration with no proof and no measurement performs best on the supplied query set; that an exhaustive scan on the same field can serve as the ground truth against which an approximation's recall is measured; that the label-based evaluator reports how many retrieved documents no label covers; and that the judged evaluator returns a score, a threshold, a pass label and a written reason — which is the complete visible form of a measurement and is why phase 6's reviewers had nothing to object to.

**Read rather than demonstrated.** The evaluator names and metric names; the score ranges; the default pass threshold; which of the two evaluators requires a model deployment; and the preview status of the minimum-score threshold. All documentation, read on the day.

**Not established here, and not establishable anywhere at present.** **What a judged score tracks.** No agreement between any evaluator in this stack and competent human judgement is published, on any task, so the number's relationship to the construct is unknown rather than weak. **This is a fact about what has been published and not a finding about the instrument**, and no agreement figure is asserted in this day, this module or this programme. Also not established: how good any of today's five configurations actually is. One corpus, one label set, one judge.

**Asserted.** That every heuristic must ship with a promise its caller can check, and that performance is not the criterion. Both are standards this design sets; point 8's argument for them is the design's own and rests on no source. **Point 2's checkability requirement deliberately carries no criterion scope statement**, the scope rule was widened to criteria *because of* that omission, and the platform turn's opportunity to repair it quietly was declined so that the failure remains legible.

**What rests on a `research/` note.** The checkability position and the provenance conditions on measured claims, at [research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/collected-materials.md](/research/05-fde-craft/collected-materials.md). That no agreement with human judgement is published for these evaluators is itself recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md) — **the day's most load-bearing claim is a documented silence, and the silence is on file.** Error management, which carries phase 6, has a complete reading at [research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md).

**What rests on nothing.** Pretesting, cumulative and expanding-interval retrieval, contrasting cases with a late-revealed criterion, and worked-example fading have **no note anywhere in this repository.** The late-revealed criterion is the whole architecture of phase 3 and is reused deliberately at [S029](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S029.md) so that students recognise the move; that reuse is a design decision resting on the design's own judgement.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **Every heuristic ships with a promise its caller can check, and a score from an uncharacterised instrument must be reported as such.** Performance is not the criterion. A configuration that wins on the supplied queries and promises nothing is worse to ship than a slower one whose promise you can state.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** The checkability position and the provenance conditions on measured claims are verified in this repository at [research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/collected-materials.md](/research/05-fde-craft/collected-materials.md). That no agreement with human judgement is published for these evaluators is recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md): **nothing is known about what a judged score tracks, and no agreement figure is asserted anywhere in this programme.** The instructional mechanisms are cited for **direction and mechanism only.**

**Guidance for your own work.**

- Write the promise as one sentence its recipient can check, and say what happens when it is not met.
- Name who detects the failure. If the answer is you, you have not finished.
- Report a judged score with the word *uncalibrated*, what evidence would remove it, and who would have to produce that evidence.
- **A control is not evidence.** Wiring up an evaluator is a configured control; its output is an observed result, and it becomes outcome evidence only when something has characterised the instrument — see the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md).

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Collapsing *no evidence* into *evidence of unknown quality*. Both are excluded and they are excluded for different reasons, and a register that cannot separate them will eventually accept either.
