# S037 · Contextual Inquiry with a Real User

**Module:** [M08](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M08.md) · **Quarter:** [Q4](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q4.md) · **Complexity class:** 3
**Strands:** PE
**Whitepaper:** [WP-037](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-037.md)
**Depends on:** S036
**Platform anchor:** Azure API Management `llm-emit-token-metric` policy and Azure Monitor metrics for the Foundry deployment · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (write, for the telemetry policy; monitor namespace for queries) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

S036 exhausted the request as evidence, and built it. What remains cannot be obtained from a document or from a deployment: **what actually happens when this person does their work.**

Students have interviewed people. At S015 they questioned a caller and derived an operation mix, and that was a good day whose method was extractive — they went in with a question and came out with an answer. This day inverts it. The method is **observation with minimal intervention**, and its central discipline is that you do not steer.

The reason is that what people say and what people do diverge, systematically and without deception. A user describing their work describes the version they believe they perform, cleaned of the workarounds, the abandoned attempts and the small accommodations they have stopped noticing. Those are the interesting parts, and they are only available by watching.

**And there is a fourth record available today that was not available at S015.** The gateway the user is working through can be made to write down what it did, request by request, while you watch them. That record is not the truth either. It is another instrument, with its own resolution, its own blind spots, and a vendor who documents both — and the most useful hour of the day is the one where the observer's record and the machine's record disagree about the same ninety minutes.

---

## The delivery standard on this day

This day carries **principle 1 of the [FDE Delivery Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/14-FDE-Delivery-Standard.md): build for real operational use, not for demonstration** — that page's wording rather than a quotation. The front line is not a figure of speech today. It is one person, doing their own work, for ninety minutes that cannot be re-run.

**What the principle costs here is everything a competent engineer would rather do.** It forbids helping. It forbids repairing the thing you can see is broken. It forbids the clarifying question that would resolve your own uncertainty fastest, because each of those alters the work you came to observe. It costs the instrument as well: what you deploy at step 0 records and changes nothing, which is a build whose entire value is that it does not act — and a student who finds that unsatisfying has located the principle's price rather than a defect in the exercise.

**No playbook artefact belongs on this day, and that is worth stating rather than working around.** The playbook specifies records a team produces about its own decisions — decision records, trade studies, spikes, team agreements, a checklist of its own conditions — and it specifies nothing for finding out how somebody else works. **The gap is real, it is not filled by the delivery standard built on top of it either, and the three-column record this day assesses is this programme's own.** [WP-037](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-037.md)'s evidence status records the absence as the citation.

**Staff preparation.**

- **Brief the user, and give them the disclosure.** They are told what the gateway's own record of the session contains and who will read it. [WP-037](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-037.md) §5 records this as a duty-of-care requirement under [Substrate and Infrastructure](/archive/bsc-programme/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) and states that **it is not optional.**
- **Brief the students on how to sit and what to say.** The master-apprentice framing is explained to the user as well as to the room; students say what they are learning rather than what they are studying; and the pair does not sit behind the user.
- **Check the column ratios at the midpoint swap.** §5 makes this the instructor's job, because an empty inferred column fails the specification for the wrong reason — not because the student conflated inference with observation, but because they recorded nothing they were unsure of.
- **Point 11 is handed over as a document and is not read in the room.** Phase 4 is deliberately short so that phase 5 gets its minutes, and the point was added against that brevity rule on the condition that it costs none of them.

**Platform prerequisites.**

- **The gateway the user's traffic actually passes through**, so that the step 0 policy is scoped to them and records the session they are in.
- The **Azure MCP Server**, in write configuration for the telemetry policy and with the monitor namespace available for the phase 3 and step 6 queries. **Both are needed and they are not the same permission.**
- **Azure Monitor metrics for the Foundry deployment, and retained data going back far enough to cover phase 3's past episode.**
- The **Microsoft Learn MCP Server**. Item 5 is the only sealed item in the module whose answer is checkable against a vendor's documentation rather than against the room, and that is the point of it.
- **An administrator can gate the monitor read permission, the policy write permission and the metric retention period** — and retention is the one nobody checks until the query returns nothing.

**The live system this day depends on.** A running gateway carrying one person's real work, plus a metric store holding a past session. **The person is the harder dependency and the one with no substitute.** Note also that step 0's build is an instrument that records and changes nothing: per [M08](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M08.md)'s build rule, an instrument that alters what the user experiences fails the specification, so the deployment must be verified as passive before the user arrives.

**If a prerequisite is missing.** If the telemetry is unavailable, phase 3 loses record D and step 6 loses its reconciliation, and the day reverts to the three human records — which is the design that ran before the practical turn, and which cannot make the day's second argument that a machine record is an instrument with a documented gap. **If the user does not come, the day does not run.** No page in this repository states a fallback for that, and a simulated user would defeat the day's own premise, which is that what people say and what people do diverge.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items, before the user arrives.

1. Ask your step 9 question from S036. Write the answer you expect.
2. What will they do during their work that they will not mention?
3. How many times do you expect to speak in the next ninety minutes?
4. Predict: will your expected answer to item 1 be close?
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to instrument this session — to emit a token metric per request, with the dimensions you would want, and to give you the queries that show what happened. Write down what it will produce, and **name the one thing about this user's session it will silently fail to record**. Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 3 is the day's discipline made numeric. Most students predict fifteen to twenty; the target is under five.

**Warrant.** Pretesting the expected answer so that it can be compared against the actual, and pretesting the student's own intervention rate, which is the behaviour the day is training. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 per instrumentation rule 8, and it is the only one of the five items whose answer is checkable against a vendor's documentation rather than against the room — which is deliberate, because it is the day's first evidence that an instrument's blind spots can be looked up rather than discovered.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (40 min)

Groups receive **four records of the same twenty minutes**: a substrate user performing a task through the gateway, recorded four ways.

- **Transcript A** — what the user said afterwards when asked to describe the task.
- **Transcript B** — what the user said during the task, thinking aloud.
- **Record C** — an observer's record of what the user did.
- **Record D** — **the platform's own record of that episode**, retained and queryable.

A, B and C are read, because they are records of a past episode and cannot be re-run. **D is operated.** Groups query it themselves through the Azure MCP Server's monitor tooling, choosing the metric, the aggregation and the interval, and they find out what it holds by asking it rather than by being handed a printout.

The four disagree. They disagree in specific and characteristic ways, and D disagrees with C for reasons nobody in the room predicts:

- **The finest interval available is one minute.** Every platform metric on this resource is sampled at `PT1M`. The user's four rapid retries inside eight seconds are one point.
- **The metric named availability did not move.** `AzureOpenAIAvailabilityRate` is computed as total calls minus server errors over total calls, where server errors are HTTP responses of 500 and above. **The refusals the user experienced were 429s, and 429s do not reduce it.** The episode in which the user was blocked repeatedly reports availability at 100%.
- **The refusals are visible somewhere else.** The request-count metric carries a status-code dimension, so a group that splits by it sees them — and a group that does not, does not.
- **The vendor says its latency figure is not the user's latency.** The response-time metric is documented as an approximation that does not account for client-side latency between the client and the endpoint, with the recommendation that you refer to your own logging instead.

Task: **identify the three places where A and C diverge, and say what caused each.** Then: **identify one place where C and D disagree, and say which you would believe.** Then: which single record would you keep if you could keep only one?

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across evidence types with a fixed episode. The A-versus-C divergence is the phase's original payload and its causes — omission of the automatic, omission of the embarrassing, and reconstruction of the sequence — are the day's taxonomy. **D is the phase's new payload and it does something the human records cannot: it establishes that a machine record is an instrument with a construct-instrument gap of its own, and that the gap is documented rather than discovered.** The availability result is the sharpest single fact in the module — a metric whose name is the user's word for what they wanted, computing something that excludes the thing that happened to them.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (35 min)

Deliberately short. The day's substance is phase 5.

1. **Say and do diverge,** and the divergence is not deception. Three mechanisms, from phase 3.
2. **The master-apprentice stance.** You are there to learn how the work is done, from the person who does it, in the place where it happens.
3. **The four rules:** go where the work is, watch it happen, ask about what you see rather than about what they think, and do not steer.
4. **What steering looks like** — a leading question, a suggestion offered as a question, a silence you filled, and agreeing too quickly. **This list omits the steering that occurs by selecting which session to observe and which task to ask for, which is real, is invisible from inside the session, and is not addressed anywhere in this programme.**
5. **Separating said, done and inferred** in the record. The single most important discipline and the day's assessed artefact. **The machine record is a fourth column and it is not a fifth kind of truth** — it is *done*, at the resolution the platform samples at, for the subset of behaviour the platform is positioned to see.
6. **Interruption as a cost with a benefit.** You may interrupt; each interruption changes what you will subsequently observe.
7. **What to do when they ask you what they should do.** They will.
8. **A machine record has a construct, an instrument and a gap, exactly like any other.** S026's apparatus applies without modification. The construct is *what happened to this user*; the instrument is a metric with a name, a computation, a sampling interval and a set of dimensions somebody chose. **Reading the metric's definition is not optional diligence; it is the only way to know what you are looking at**, and today the definition is one Learn MCP lookup away.
9. **Defaults are opinions, and today three were chosen for you — all of them silently.** The metric namespace defaults to **"API Management"**, so a team that emits without naming one finds their custom metrics in a namespace they did not pick and cannot easily tell apart from anyone else's. **Certain models, especially when streaming, do not include token counts in the response at all unless `include_usage` is set to true** — which means the instrument you deployed can be correct, running, and empty, and nothing will tell you. And when a stream is interrupted or terminated unexpectedly, **the captured token counts are documented as inaccurate**, without any statement of by how much or in which direction. **A silently empty instrument is worse than no instrument**, because it produces a record that looks like evidence.
10. **Perishable and durable, separated.** The metric names, the definitions, the sampling interval, the dimension limits and the default namespace are **perishable**, and the ten legacy metrics on this resource that the documentation instructs you not to use for this service will not carry those instructions forever. What is durable is that every record is an instrument, that a record's silence is a property of the instrument rather than of the world, and that the way to find out is to read the definition. **Safe** for reasoning about what today's records do and do not contain. **Unsafe** as a general claim about telemetry, and unsafe as a claim about what the platform will report next year.

11. **A real question bank, read against this day's rules.** *Added 2026-07-30, on a reading of a second internal corpus. Taught and warranted, never assessed. **Handed over as a document rather than worked in the room**, because this phase is deliberately short and phase 5 needs its minutes.* A customer-embedded engineering organisation within a large technology vendor maintains a large practitioner question bank for customer discovery, recorded in [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md).

    **Parts of it are very good, and the good parts are named first because a specimen taught only as a failure is not read.** It enforces a **breadth-first block before any deep dive**, structurally rather than by advice — an independent rediscovery of the rule against investigating detail inside a category before establishing that the category matters. It carries textbook prior-attempt questions, threshold questions asking what would be good enough, and a closing pair in every block asking what good would look like and how improvement would be measured. And it carries several genuinely **behavioural** items: a recent shipped feature and where it stalled, how often tests are disabled under deadline, whether security has ever actually blocked a release, what happens when the key person is away. Read those against [research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md](/research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md) and they hold.

    **Four structural failures, against the same literature.** **It asks the customer to endorse the interviewer's solution, systematically, in every block** — each section asks where the interviewer's own solution category would help, asked by the person who has come to deliver it. That is the central prohibition in the question-quality literature, present as the **template** rather than as an occasional slip. **The specimen answers lead the witness:** nearly every question ships with example responses, which is defensible as preparation for a novice and lethal in the room, because appending examples narrows the response set — and the specimens disproportionately name the frictions the method addresses. **There is no observation of any kind.** It is entirely self-report about an engineering system; nobody is asked to demonstrate a deployment, share a screen during a pipeline run, or set up an environment while being watched. **That is point 1 of this day, absent.** **And there is no consolidation across informants**, with an artefact that actively prevents it — a lookup table converting a single utterance from a single person into a prescribed intervention, which is [research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md)'s consolidation step deleted and replaced with a routing rule.

    **Three absences are worth more than the four failures**, because a failure is visible in a question and an absence is invisible to the person holding the instrument. **Nothing about data** — where it comes from, who may see it, whether it is correct or labelled, or whether anyone consented. **Nothing asking what result would show it had *not* worked**, in a bank whose entire purpose is to establish what should be built. **And nothing asking who loses if it succeeds**, alongside commercial material carrying an explicit headcount claim.

    **What this establishes and what it does not.** The failures and the absences are **diagnosable from the questions' own form**, which is exactly why they can be taught without any access to the source: you can look at *where would this help* and say what it does to an answer. **What none of it establishes is what the bank actually elicited.** The corpus records no outcome for it — nothing about what any of these questions produced, from anybody, ever — so **the diagnosis is a claim about an instrument and never a claim about the people who wrote it or about what they found with it.** That distinction is the same one point 8 makes about a metric, and it is the reason this is handed over rather than marked.

**Warrant.** Consolidation kept short so that phase 5 can run at length. Point 4 carries the scope statement including the unaddressed selection-steering gap. Point 5 is the assessed discipline. Points 8 and 9 are the day's platform-specific content; point 9 satisfies the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s default requirement and its three items are chosen because all three fail *quietly*, which is the property the day is about. **Point 11 is a specimen and is the phase's only addition, made against its own brevity rule, so it is resolved rather than absorbed: it is handed over as a document and is not read in the room, and no minute of this phase or any other moves.** It is placed here rather than in the error clinic because it is a reading exercise on an artefact and not an error to be diagnosed — nobody in the room made it, and phase 6's disclosure mechanism depends on the case being one a student could have produced. It is **deliberately unassessable and adds no checked element**: the corpus behind it is internal, nothing in it may be linked, and it reports no outcome, so nothing a student says about it could be marked. What it supplies that this day cannot otherwise obtain is **a large, competent, real instrument built by practitioners for practitioners in which points 1 and 3 are simply missing** — which is more persuasive than any constructed example that the rules of this day are not obvious.

## Phase 5 · The inquiry, and its instrument (100 min)

**Class 3. The inquiry is the student's. This phase produces a build, and the build is an instrument rather than a remedy.**

A **real substrate user** performs their actual work, in their actual environment, for ninety minutes. Students observe in pairs — one observing, one recording — and swap at the midpoint.

Structure:

0. **Before the user arrives, ship the instrument.** Using Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server, deploy an `llm-emit-token-metric` policy scoped to this user's traffic, with the dimensions you have decided you need. **You may configure at most five custom dimensions**, and the number of active time series the policy generates is the product of each dimension's distinct values — so this is a design decision with a cost, not a checklist. The policy observes; it changes nothing the user experiences. Per the module's build rule, an instrument that alters the service fails.
1. **Ask the S036 step 9 question** at the start. Once. Record the answer.
2. **Observe.** Record continuously, in three columns: said, done, inferred.
3. **Ask about what you see,** not about what they think. **Your partner counts your interventions**, in a fourth column, while you count your own. Two counts, independently produced.
4. **At the end**, ask the same question from step 1 again, and record the answer.
5. **Immediately afterwards**, and before discussing with anyone, write the three things you observed that the user did not mention.
6. **Then, and only then, query your instrument** for the same ninety minutes. Reconcile it against your three-column record: what it confirms, what it contradicts, and **what it could not have seen**.

Step 4's repetition is the day's instrument on the user. Step 3's paired count is the day's instrument on the observer. Step 6's reconciliation is the day's instrument on the instrument.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** What you asked the agent for at step 0, in order, including what you abandoned.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that your own design would have caught an intervention-worthy error — a dimension count checked against the five-dimension limit, or a pre-session smoke test confirming that token counts are actually arriving, are both accepted and are both the right check to have built.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 3 with a real user in a real setting. The three-column record is the discipline; step 5's isolation prevents the observation from being reshaped by discussion; step 6's ordering is deliberate and load-bearing — a student who reads the machine record first will write the human record to agree with it. **Step 3's paired count is the repair [WP-037](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-037.md) §8 identified and did not make**, and it is now made: instrumentation rule 1 forbids self-report where an external observer is already present, the partner was always present, and the fourth column costs nothing.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (35 min)

Instructor first: an inquiry they conducted that produced a confident and wrong conclusion, with attention to **the moment they started steering** and why it felt like clarifying.

Then the agent case: a Copilot-built session dashboard that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. Asked to show whether the service had been available to this user during the session, the agent selected the metric named availability, wrote a correct query, produced a correct chart, and reported 100%. The user had been refused throughout. The metric excludes 429s by definition and the definition is published.

Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the reviewer checked that the query was right, and it was.** The chart matched the metric, the metric matched its own definition, and nobody read the definition, because the metric's name was the same word the requester had used. This is S026's construct-instrument gap arriving from the direction nobody watches — not a badly built instrument, but a correctly built one measuring a different construct.

Then students: something you asked today that you should not have, or a number you accepted today without reading what it counted.

**Warrant.** Error management on the day's specific discipline. The steering moment is almost never experienced as steering, which is what makes the first disclosure useful. The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen because the agent did nothing wrong: it answered the question it was asked, with a real metric, correctly — which is precisely the failure the module exists to teach.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (25 min)

Pairs — the same pairs who observed together. Each states **their three unmentioned observations** from step 5, independently written.

Where they differ, the pair determines whether one observed something the other missed, or whether one inferred something and recorded it as observed.

They then compare **their two intervention counts of each other** against each other's self-counts, and record all four numbers.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with an independent-record comparison. Two people watching the same ninety minutes producing different records is the day's most direct evidence that observation is itself an instrument. The four-number comparison is what makes the day's principal measure external rather than self-reported.

## When it does not work

These are [WP-037](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-037.md) §5's predicted failure modes, restated as instruction to the person in the room. The whitepaper is the authority; where this section and §5 diverge, §5 is right. Nothing here is assessed.

**A student with user-research or support experience conducts a fluent session, and interviews.** Their intervention count will be high, their record rich in *said* and thin in *done*, and their output a good account of what the user believes — which is exactly the artefact this day exists to displace. You do not have to say any of that. The count is now their partner's as well as their own, so a high count is externally recorded; their partner's independent record will contain observations theirs lacks; and phase 7 makes the difference visible without an instructor judgement.

**A student uncomfortable with silence will either over-intervene from anxiety or under-record from paralysis.** The paired structure means somebody is always recording, the midpoint swap bounds the exposure, and the three-column form gives an anxious observer something to do. **The residual risk is an inferred column that is empty because they wrote down nothing they were unsure of**, which fails the specification for the wrong reason — so check the column ratios at the swap rather than at the end.

**A student substitutes the instrument for the observation.** This is the practical turn's characteristic failure and the day cannot afford it, because the hundred minutes are the one thing it cannot lose. Three things are already in place: the instrument is deployed before the user arrives and read after they leave, so there is no moment at which attending to it is available; the specification does not assess the instrument's completeness; and step 6 asks what the instrument could *not* see, which cannot be answered from the instrument. **§5's residual risk is a student who writes a thin human record expecting the machine record to cover for it, and the only detector is phase 7's comparison against a partner working under the same expectation.**

**Phase 3 goes too smoothly, or not smoothly enough.** Identifying that A and C diverge is straightforward; identifying the three *causes* is not, and groups typically find two. **The C-versus-D question is harder than the design originally expected**, and its most common wrong answer is to prefer D because it is mechanical — which is the exact error point 8 exists to correct and is therefore useful. If a group finds all three causes and reconciles C with D, ask which of the three would be reduced by the user knowing they were observed and which increased. That is a genuine and unresolved question about observation effects.

**The user feels evaluated.** A person doing their work while two students watch and write may become self-conscious, perform, or apologise for their methods. Explain the master-apprentice framing to them as well as to the room; brief students to say what they are learning rather than what they are studying; do not let the pair sit behind them; and **tell them what the gateway's record of the session contains and who will read it — which is a duty-of-care requirement under [Substrate and Infrastructure](/archive/bsc-programme/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md), not a courtesy.**

**The students are uncomfortable at taking somebody's time and giving nothing back.** This is felt more often than the design initially expected and **it is a healthy response.** Point 7 — what to do when they ask you what they should do — partly addresses it. **The design's honest position is that the user gives ninety minutes and receives nothing on the day, that this is a genuine imbalance, and that the module's later output going back to them is a deferred and partial repayment.** Say that rather than reassuring them.

## Assessment

Specification item in PE201. Checked elements: both answers to the S036 question; the three-column record with all three columns non-empty; **both intervention counts, self and partner**; the three unmentioned observations; one identified misclassification of inference as observation; **the deployed telemetry policy and its dimension choices**; the step 6 reconciliation including what the instrument could not have seen; **the prompt record; the intervention record; the frontier statement.**

**A record with an empty inferred column fails the specification.** Everything an observer records is partly inference; an empty column means the distinction was not being made.

**An artefact whose instrument changed what the user experienced fails the specification.** The module's build rule.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the student's own design would have caught an intervention-worthy error.

Nothing else is assessed — not the record's length, not the quality of the observations, not the intervention count's value, and not the instrument's completeness.

Say-do divergence and its three mechanisms, the master-apprentice stance, the four rules, the said/done/inferred separation, interruption's cost, and the treatment of a machine record as an instrument are durable and are what the specification assesses.

## What this day does not establish

**Demonstrated.** What one person did in ninety minutes, once, in a session that cannot be re-run; that four records of the same twenty minutes diverged, and in which places; that two observers of the same session produced different records; and the four intervention counts each pair generated. One user, one day.

**Read rather than demonstrated.** Every metric fact at points 8 and 9 — the one-minute time grain, the availability metric's computation from server errors, the status-code dimension on the request count, the response-time metric's documented exclusion of client-side latency, the default metric namespace, token counts being omitted unless usage reporting is switched on, the inaccuracy of counts when a stream is interrupted, and the five-dimension limit with its active-time-series consequence. Vendor documentation, read on the day — and the day's own argument is that reading it is the only way to know what you are looking at.

**Not established, deliberately.** By how much, or in which direction, token counts are inaccurate when a stream is interrupted: **the vendor states the inaccuracy and gives neither size nor direction, and none is asserted here.** Two further things are not established and both are named on the page rather than hidden. **The steering that happens in choosing which session and which task to observe is real, is invisible from inside the session, and is not addressed anywhere in this programme.** And **the question bank at point 11 establishes nothing about what it actually elicited** — the corpus records no outcome for it, from anybody, ever — so the diagnosis is a claim about an instrument's form and never a claim about the people who wrote it or about what they found with it.

**Asserted.** That the said/done/inferred separation is the right discipline and the right assessed artefact. That step 6's ordering is load-bearing — that a student who reads the machine record first will write the human record to agree with it. Both are the design's reasoning.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** Say and do diverge, systematically and without deception, so what a person tells you about their work is a biased instrument rather than a dishonest one. **And the machine's record is an instrument too** — a metric has a name somebody chose, a computation, a resolution and a set of dimensions, and reading the definition is the only way to know what you are looking at.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** Contextual inquiry, the master-apprentice stance, say-do divergence and non-intervention discipline are grounded in [research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md), [research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md](/research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md) and [research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md](/research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md). **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.** The vendor states that token counts are inaccurate when a stream is interrupted and gives neither size nor direction — a documented silence rather than an oversight.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Keep three columns — said, done, inferred.** An empty inferred column means the distinction was not being made.
- **Write your own record before you read the machine's**, or you will write yours to agree with it.
- **Read the metric's definition before you use its name**, and have somebody else count your interventions.
- **Do not treat a deployed instrument as a reading.** Per the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md) it is a *configured control* until you have queried it — and an instrument can be correct, running and silently empty, which produces something that looks like evidence.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Believing a number because its name is the word the requester used. The availability figure was correctly defined, correctly queried and correctly charted, and it excluded the refusals the user actually experienced.
