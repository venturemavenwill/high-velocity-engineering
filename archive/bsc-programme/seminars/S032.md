# S032 · Synchronisation and Errors You Cannot Reproduce

**Module:** [M07](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M07.md) · **Quarter:** [Q4](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q4.md) · **Complexity class:** 3
**Strands:** SY · ME · PE
**Whitepaper:** [WP-032](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-032.md)
**Depends on:** S027, S031
**Re-tests:** S010
**Platform anchor:** API Management semantic caching — `llm-semantic-cache-lookup` and `llm-semantic-cache-store` · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (write) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

The defect is real. Users have seen it. It has occurred four times in six weeks. You cannot reproduce it, and you are being asked what to do.

This is the ordinary condition of concurrency work and almost nothing in a student's training prepares them for it, because every defect they have ever fixed was one they could make happen. Without reproduction, the standard loop — change something, observe, conclude — is unavailable, and students who lack a substitute will either do nothing or change something and declare victory when the symptom does not recur.

The target: **build an evidence argument for a defect you cannot reproduce, state what would falsify it, and decide what to do on that basis.** The day is a measurement day disguised as a systems day, and it retrieves from S027 for that reason.

The defect is on the gateway the room deployed yesterday. Semantic caching was switched on to reduce cost, and a user has received an answer to a question they did not ask. **Whether it happens depends on what else was in the cache at that moment, and the cache empties itself on a timer**, so the same request that failed on Tuesday succeeds on Wednesday and nobody has touched anything. This is not a constructed teaching case. It is the documented behaviour of the feature: the vendor states, in the policy reference, that because responses are returned on similarity rather than exact match, semantic caching can surface responses that are incorrect, outdated or unsafe for the current request.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (20 min)

Five items. A real defect report from the room's own gateway is supplied: four occurrences, six weeks, no reproduction.

1. What would you do first?
2. What evidence would convince you that you had found the cause?
3. What evidence would convince you that you had *not*?
4. Predict: how many of the room's item 1 answers are attempts to reproduce it?
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to "add semantic caching to this API to reduce cost." **Write down the `score-threshold` you expect it to choose, and say whether that value makes matching stricter or looser.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 3 is the day's move. Item 4's answer is nearly all of them. Item 5 has a correct answer that almost nobody in the room holds, and the design's expectation is that the cohort's error and the agent's error will point the same way.

**Warrant.** Pretesting on the situation rather than the technique. Item 4 makes the reproduction reflex a cohort-level observation rather than an individual correction. Item 5 is administered here and not later, per instrumentation rule 8, and is the day's contribution to the frontier map; it is constructed so that a student cannot score it by predicting generic agent unreliability.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (40 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups have the gateway with semantic caching enabled and a corpus of prior prompts already stored. They receive **one defect and four investigative situations**, differing in what evidence is available, and in each they work with the evidence that situation actually permits.

- **Situation A** — a gateway trace is available for one occurrence; the cache lookup and its outcome are visible in it.
- **Situation B** — aggregate metrics only. Cache hit ratio and token spend are available by consumer; the hit ratio rose in the complaint window.
- **Situation C** — user reports only, with timestamps and no system-side evidence, because tracing was not enabled at the time.
- **Situation D** — the same as C, but the defect has stopped occurring and nobody changed anything.

For each, groups state what can be concluded and commit. Groups may send traffic in situations A and B and may not in C and D, which is the constraint the situations exist to impose.

Then ground truth is supplied. The cause is the same in all four: a prompt was answered from a cache entry stored for a different, semantically nearby prompt, at a threshold set loosely enough to admit it.

Task after: **for situation D, state what you would tell the person asking whether it is fixed.**

Situation D defeats most groups and is the module's hardest single question — and on this platform it has a mechanism, which most groups do not find: **the cached entry expired.** The `llm-semantic-cache-store` policy takes a required time-to-live in seconds, the entry aged out, and the defect will return when a similar prompt is next stored. Nothing was fixed. Nothing was even changed.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across evidential situations with a fixed cause. Situation D is the payload: a defect that stopped without intervention is the case where the reproduction reflex is most tempting and most useless. Running the cases rather than reading them adds one thing the previous design could not supply — **groups in situations C and D can see the other groups obtaining evidence they are forbidden**, which makes the evidential asymmetry a felt constraint rather than a stipulation.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (45 min)

1. **Reproduction is a sampling problem.** A defect occurring in a small fraction of executions is not hiding; you have not sampled enough, or you have not sampled the right conditions. On this platform there is a third case, which is worse: **you cannot sample the right conditions, because the condition is the contents of a cache you do not control and cannot inspect directly.**
2. **What synchronisation primitives actually promise** — mutual exclusion, ordering, visibility — and which of the three students usually forget. **This list omits the memory-model guarantees that make visibility precise, which SY201 covers formally and this day treats as a black box. Treating visibility as a black box is safe for reasoning about specifications and unsafe for writing lock-free code, which the day does not do.**
3. **Where synchronisation defects come from:** missing exclusion, insufficient ordering, assumed visibility, and correct primitives composed incorrectly. The fourth is the one that survives review, and today's defect is an instance of it — every component behaved as documented.
4. **Evidence without reproduction.** What a trace, a metric, a log and a user report each license — S028's licensing discipline applied to defect diagnosis. Phase 3's four situations are the four evidence classes, and situation B is the trap: a cache hit ratio that rose in the complaint window licenses a hypothesis and confirms nothing.
5. **The evidence argument** as an artefact: hypothesis, supporting observations, what would falsify it, and the confidence you are reporting.
6. **Deciding under an unfalsified hypothesis.** You may act on an unconfirmed diagnosis; you may not describe it as confirmed. The two are different and the difference is a sentence.
7. **Situation D, resolved, and its mechanism named.** A defect that stopped occurring is not evidence that it is fixed, and saying so to someone who wants to close the ticket is the day's professional demand. Here the mechanism is available: the cached entry expired at the time-to-live set on the store policy. **A disappearance with a known mechanism is still not a fix**, and having the mechanism makes the conversation harder rather than easier, because you must now explain that you know why it stopped and that this is bad news.
8. **Instrumenting for next time,** which is the only reliable output when the current evidence is insufficient. Gateway tracing was not enabled at the time; that is the finding, and it is [S068](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S068.md)'s subject in embryo.
9. **The threshold, worked completely, and marked perishable.** Semantic caching selects a stored response by vector proximity of the incoming prompt to previous ones, filtered by a `score-threshold` between 0.0 and 1.0. **The direction is the opposite of what its name suggests: lower values require *higher* semantic similarity for a match.** So a threshold raised to loosen nothing loosens everything. The documentation states that a value above 0.2 may lead to cache mismatch and advises starting near 0.05 and tuning by the ratio of hits to misses. **The number is perishable and the structure is not: a similarity threshold is a correctness knob, and a wrong setting produces a plausible answer to a question nobody asked.**
10. **Defaults are opinions and thresholds are policy.** Three today, and one of them is a security boundary. `score-threshold` has **no default and is required**, which is the platform declining to choose — itself a decision, and one that means every deployment's correctness rests on a number somebody picked in a hurry. `ignore-system-messages` **defaults to `false` while the documentation recommends `true`**, so the shipped default contradicts the shipped advice. And `vary-by` is **optional**: omit it and the cache is not partitioned, so one consumer's stored answer can be returned to another. The documentation names this as the mechanism for controlling cross-user access to cache entries; it is a one-line element, it is not on by default, and the deployment succeeds without it.
11. **What the documentation does not say.** It states that a threshold above 0.2 *may* lead to mismatch. It does not say how often, on what data, against what baseline, or with what consequence — and there is **no published accuracy, hit-precision or false-match rate for this feature at any threshold**. None is asserted here. Consistent with the folder-wide asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](/research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), the page states latencies, bounds and thresholds throughout and states no accuracy anywhere. **A correctness knob shipped without a characterisation of what it does to correctness is exactly [S029](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S029.md)'s unvalidated instrument**, and the only party who can validate it is you, on your own traffic.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3's four situations. Point 2 carries the scope statement in the strong form. Point 6 is the day's most important professional distinction and is the one students collapse. Points 9 to 11 are the day's platform content and satisfy the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md); point 10's third default is included because a caching parameter that is also an access-control boundary is the clearest available demonstration that **a default is a decision with a blast radius**.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (85 min)

**Class 3. The evidence standard is the student's. This phase produces a build.**

Students take the unreproduced defect on their own gateway and, using Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server:

1. **State the hypothesis** — what you believe is happening, precisely enough to be wrong.
2. **List the observations** that support it, and what each individually licenses.
3. **State what would falsify it,** and whether that observation is obtainable.
4. **State the alternative hypothesis** that best explains the same observations.
5. **Design and deploy the instrumentation** that would distinguish them — S029's design sequence, applied, and now shipped. Tracing, a token metric with a dimension that separates cache hits from misses, or a `vary-by` partition that would make cross-consumer service visible. It goes on the gateway and it runs.
6. **State what you would do now,** before the instrumentation returns anything, and why that is defensible.
7. **Write the report** to the person who raised the defect, per point 6's distinction.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the Copilot Practice Standard.

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the instrumentation you deployed would itself have detected an intervention-worthy error — a dimension that distinguishes served-from-cache from served-from-model is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch.

**Fading:** step 2's licensing form is supplied; step 5 reuses S029's sequence without re-teaching. Policy syntax is agent-authored and is not a difficulty.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 3. Step 4's alternative hypothesis is the day's discriminator — a diagnosis with no rival is a diagnosis that has not been tested. Step 6 is the professional act: deciding without confirmation. Step 5's promotion from design to deployment is the practical turn and it changes what the step can be assessed against: an instrument that was designed and not deployed could be judged only on its description, and one that is running can be checked for whether it emits the distinguishing observation.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate defect declared fixed, that recurred four months later, where the original change was correct and did not address the cause — with attention to what the team believed at the time and what they had written down.

Then the agent case, and it is the day's sharpest because **the reviewer made the agent's mistake independently.** A team asked Copilot to enable semantic caching and to "be conservative — only return a cached answer when the prompts are very similar." It produced a correct, well-formed policy pair with `score-threshold="0.85"`, and explained in its own summary that a high threshold demands high similarity. The reviewer read the explanation, agreed with it, and approved. It is backwards: lower values require higher similarity, so 0.85 is close to accepting anything. Cost fell dramatically, which everyone read as the feature working, and for five weeks a fraction of users received confident answers to questions they had not asked.

Attention goes to why review did not catch it. **The agent's error and the reviewer's error had the same cause** — both inferred a parameter's direction from its name — and a review conducted by someone who shares the author's misconception is not a review. It is a second vote. The available defence was not more care; it was one lookup, or one test with two prompts that mean different things.

Then students: something you concluded was fixed because it stopped happening, or a place today where you accepted the agent's explanation rather than its output.

**Firewall applies as always.**

**Warrant.** Error management targeting situation D's failure mode in its consequential form. The instructor disclosure's detail that the change was *correct* is the phase's content: a defensible action taken on a wrong diagnosis produces a wrong conclusion that is invisible for months. The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen because it defeats the countermeasure students believe in — human review — by targeting the misconception the human already holds.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents their step 1 hypothesis and step 2 observations, and the partner's task is to **construct a different hypothesis that explains all the same observations** — and to say what single observation would separate them.

If the partner cannot find a separating observation, both hypotheses are currently unfalsifiable and the pair says so.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with a competing-explanation demand, escalated from S027's version: here the partner must also supply the discriminating observation, which is the experimental-design move S029 taught.

## When it does not work

These are [WP-032](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-032.md) §5's predicted failure modes, restated as instruction to the person in the room. The whitepaper is the authority; where this section and §5 diverge, §5 is right. Nothing here is assessed.

**A student with production on-call experience produces a plausible hypothesis in minutes and a weak alternative.** Operational practice rewards one actionable theory and penalises deliberation, so their step 1 will be fluent and their step 4 will be thin. Do not correct it directly. Their partner constructs the rival at phase 7, and an experienced student's hypothesis is usually the easiest rival to find precisely because it was reached quickly.

**A student without the background is applying M06's apparatus in an unfamiliar domain with one bridge — phase 2's final item.** Everything available has already been given: the licensing form, the instrument sequence reused verbatim from S029, the defect and its evidence supplied, and policy syntax agent-authored. If step 5's instrumentation design is generic, let them state which observation would distinguish the hypotheses without designing the instrument that emits it. **§5 is explicit that this preserves the reasoning and loses the deployment**, so it is a reduced artefact rather than an equivalent one.

**Situations A and B go too smoothly.** Expected. C is harder and D is the difficulty, and the phase is designed to bite there. If a group resolves D, ask what they would say to someone who has budget for exactly one more week of investigation. That forces a decision under a resource constraint, has no clean answer, and previews S065.

**The platform fails on the day.** Two separate dependencies can take it out — the external cache and the embeddings backend — and either failing removes situations A and B. Stand both up and verify them before the room arrives. Where you fall back to recorded traces, say so: the cases were **demonstrated rather than operated**, and step 5's deployment is not assessed. **Situations C and D are unaffected**, because absence of evidence is what defines them, so the day survives in reduced form rather than collapsing.

**The day experienced as an argument for inaction.** This is the characteristic affective risk and it is the inverse of the argument. A student who absorbs that they cannot confirm the diagnosis may conclude that the responsible move is to do nothing until they can. Point 6's first half is the countermeasure and it is stated as a permission: **you may act on an unconfirmed diagnosis; you may not describe it as confirmed.** Hold the step ordering — step 6 before step 7 — because a student who writes the report first describes a situation, and one who decides first describes a decision.

## Assessment

Specification item in SY201 and ME101's successor jointly.

Per the [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s rule 1, the checked elements are: presence of the hypothesis; the observations with individual licensing; the falsifier and a statement of its obtainability; the alternative hypothesis; **the deployed instrumentation and the distinguishing observation it emits**; the act-now decision with its justification; the report; **the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.**

**A report describing an unconfirmed diagnosis as confirmed fails.** So does a step 1 hypothesis that no observation could falsify. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the deployed instrumentation demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

Nothing else is assessed. Whether the hypothesis turns out to be right is explicitly not assessed and cannot be, which is the situation's defining feature.

The substrate's specific defect, every policy attribute, the threshold guidance and the tier availability are **perishable** and are marked at points 9 to 11. Reproduction-as-sampling, the four defect sources, evidence licensing without reproduction, the evidence-argument artefact, and the act-without-confirming distinction are durable and are what the specification assesses.

**Scope, strong form.** Treating the semantic cache as an instance of an unreproducible concurrency defect is **safe** for reasoning about evidence, licensing, rival hypotheses and what to instrument. It is **unsafe** as a model of synchronisation mechanism — there is no lock here, no memory model, and no interleaving of instructions; the shared mutable state is a cache and the concurrency is between requests, not threads. A student who carries today's evidential discipline into a lock-free setting has transferred the right thing; one who carries the cache as a mental model of a mutex has not. Step 4's rival hypothesis is where the difference shows.

## What this day does not establish

**Demonstrated.** That four evidential situations over one seeded cache supported different conclusions; that a prompt was answered from an entry stored for a semantically nearby prompt at a threshold loose enough to admit it; and the room's own reproduction-reflex rate at item 4. All of it is one room, one cache, one afternoon.

That is the situation's defining feature and it cannot be assessed.

**Asserted.** That the act-without-confirming distinction is teachable in a day, and that situation D is the module's hardest single question. Both are the design's own reasoning.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **Reproduction is a sampling problem, and a disappearance is not a fix — even when you know the mechanism.** You may act on an unconfirmed diagnosis; you may not describe it as confirmed. The difference is one sentence, and it is the whole of the professional demand.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** The synchronisation failure classes and the declared-fixed recurrence are verified in this repository at [research/02-technical-foundations/collected-materials.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/collected-materials.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/palantir-fde-practitioner-writing.md](/research/05-fde-craft/palantir-fde-practitioner-writing.md); the evidence-licensing apparatus you reused today follows [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md). The learning mechanisms are cited for **direction and mechanism only.** The documentation says a threshold above a stated value *may* lead to mismatch and **publishes no rate, dataset or baseline for it** — there is no accuracy or false-match figure for this feature at any threshold, and none is asserted here.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- State the rival hypothesis. A diagnosis with no competitor has not been tested.
- Name the single observation that would separate them, then deploy the thing that emits it.
- Look a parameter's direction up rather than inferring it from the parameter's name.
- **Provenance is not correctness.** An agent's account of its own change tells you where a value came from and warrants nothing about whether it is right — see the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). Today the reviewer read the account, agreed with it, and shared the error.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Accepting a review from somebody who holds your misconception. That is a second vote rather than a review, and the defence is not more care — it is one lookup, or one test with two prompts that mean different things.
