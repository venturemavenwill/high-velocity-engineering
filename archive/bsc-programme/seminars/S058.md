# S058 · Approximate Search: Trading Recall for Latency

**Module:** [M12](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M12.md) · **Quarter:** [Q6](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q6.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** SY · IS · ME
**Whitepaper:** [WP-058](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-058.md)
**Depends on:** S014, S019, S025, S057
**Platform anchor:** Azure AI Search approximate search — HNSW parameters, exhaustive KNN, scalar and binary quantization, oversampling and rescoring · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (Search) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

Yesterday's retriever compares a query against every document. That is a cost model, not an algorithm, and at the substrate's corpus size it is already the wrong one.

Every approximate index is a structure imposed to avoid comparisons, and every one of them is a bet: **that the documents you skip were not the answer.** The bet is parameterised, the parameter is an operating point in [S019](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S019.md)'s exact sense, and it is almost always set by whoever wrote the default.

**Today the defaults have names and the students change them.** The service exposes the graph's link count, its construction breadth and its search breadth, each with a published default and a published range; it exposes two compression schemes and an oversampling factor to offset them; and it will run an exhaustive scan on request, which at this corpus size is affordable and is the reference every measurement is taken against. Nothing on this day is a simulation.

The day's real content is what happens when the bet is wrong. An index that returns 98 of every 100 documents an exhaustive search would have returned looks like a small, uniform, acceptable loss. **It is not uniform.** Approximation error concentrates — on isolated documents, on small clusters, on the fine distinctions at short range — and those are exactly the documents the dense retriever was built to find and the keyword baseline could not. The approximation can therefore consume the entire advantage that justified the system, while reporting a recall figure that reads as excellent.

The target: **recall against exact search and accuracy against relevance are two different criteria, the loss between them concentrates rather than spreads, and the operating point is a promise you make to a caller who must be able to check it.**

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Four index configurations are described by their structure only, with no results. Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. **Rank the four by query latency**, and separately by memory.
2. **Predict each one's recall against exhaustive search** on this corpus.
3. Here are **the probe respects you could not predict at [S051](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S051.md) and probed at [S057](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S057.md) step 2.** For each, **predict what compressing the vectors does to it.**
4. **Of the documents an approximate index fails to return, what do they have in common?**
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to reconfigure your index for a stated latency budget. Write down what it will change, what it will leave alone, and **the one quantity it will report to you that does not mean what it appears to mean.**

Item 4 is the day's payload question, asked before anything is shown, and most of the room has no answer beyond *they were further away*.

Item 5 is the day's frontier item and the design's expectation is specific: the agent will produce a defensible parameter set, will justify it fluently, and **will state a recall expectation the documentation does not support** — because no recall figure at any parameter setting is published anywhere, and a fluent generator asked for one will supply it.

**Warrant.** Pretesting with two easy items and three hard ones. Item 1 is answerable from structure and gives every student a correct answer early. **Item 3 is this day's discharge of the obligation [S057](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S057.md) placed on it under instrumentation rule 6**: the cohort's twice-measured blind spots are carried into a third domain, so that phase 8 can report whether a respect a student could not predict about an embedding is one they also cannot predict about its compression. Item 5 is sealed here and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups have **one corpus, one retriever — [S057](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S057.md)'s hard-negative configuration — one query set, and four index configurations built as four vector profiles on one service.** Every figure in this phase is measured by the room.

- **I1** — exhaustive exact search. The reference, and at this corpus size it is affordable.
- **I2** — the navigable-neighbourhood graph at its published defaults. High recall, moderate latency, high memory.
- **I3** — the same graph with its search breadth reduced toward the bottom of the documented range. Lower latency, lower recall, same memory.
- **I4** — compressed vectors with rescoring disabled. Highest throughput, smallest memory, **and the highest recall against exact search of the three approximations.**

Groups predict the ordering on recall and on latency and commit. **Then the configurations are run and aggregate recall and tail latency are read off.** Then, separately, **recall per query class and the identity of the documents each configuration lost** — reported on **the query class where this cohort's [S057](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S057.md) margin over the keyword baseline was thinnest.**

The payload is **I4**. Its aggregate recall is the best of the three. Its losses are almost entirely documents that only the dense retriever found — the ones the [S056](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S056.md) baseline missed and that motivated building the retriever at all. **Compression discards precision at short range, and short range is where those decisions were being made.**

**A fifth configuration is offered and refused, and the refusal is the point.** Rescoring — fetching extra candidates from the compressed index and re-ordering them with the uncompressed vectors — is the service's own answer to I4's problem, and it is available. It is **not** available on the exhaustive path, because there is nothing to oversample. Groups are asked to say, before being told, which of I1 to I4 can be rescued this way and why, which requires them to have understood what the compression discarded rather than that it discarded something.

**Then the construction is disclosed**, per [instrumentation rule 3](/archive/bsc-programme/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md): each configuration's parameterisation, how the corpus's cluster structure was characterised, on what basis this query class was selected, and the fact that a corpus with different cluster structure would produce a weaker concentration. The disclosure is made in the same terms the day demands of students at step 5.

Task after: **for each configuration, write the promise you could honestly make to a caller**, in one of [S025](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S025.md)'s four forms.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases with the corpus, retriever and query set fixed so every difference is attributable to the index, **and measured by the students on a real service rather than supplied.** The two-part reveal is required: supplying the per-class breakdown alongside the aggregate would let a group see the concentration without first committing to a conclusion from the aggregate, which is the conclusion every index benchmark publishes. **The query class is selected from [S057](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S057.md)'s step 5 margin data**, discharging the second obligation placed on this day. The rescoring question is placed before the explanation because it is the phase's only test of whether the room's model of compression is mechanical or verbal.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **Exhaustive search is a cost model.** Comparisons times corpus size, with movement dominating at scale — [S012](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S012.md)'s three terms and [S016](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S016.md)'s hierarchy, unchanged. Knowing when it is adequate is worth more than knowing how to avoid it, and for many real corpora it is adequate.

2. **Every index is a structure imposed to skip comparisons.** A neighbourhood graph is [S014](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S014.md)'s shape traversed greedily; a partition index is [S013](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S013.md)'s bucketing with proximity in place of equality. **Neither is new machinery. Both are structures the cohort met in year one, carrying vectors.** The service you used this morning offers exactly two options — the graph and the exhaustive scan — which is itself a decision somebody made on your behalf, and it is worth noticing that the family you were not offered is one you could implement.

3. **The recall–latency curve, and the parameter as an operating point.** [S019](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S019.md) point 8 unchanged: the position is a trade of cost against completeness, it is a decision, and accepting a default is making it without saying so.

4. **Recall against exact search is not accuracy against relevance.** They are two criteria with two adjudicators. An index with perfect recall against a bad retriever is perfectly faithful to a wrong answer, and a system's users experience only the second criterion.

5. **Approximation loss concentrates; it does not spread.** [S053](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S053.md) point 6 in a new domain. The lost documents are isolated points, members of small clusters, and cases decided by fine distinctions at short range — which is to say, disproportionately the documents a dense retriever was built to find. **A 98 per cent aggregate recall figure is consistent with losing every document that justified the system.**

6. **Compression trades geometric precision for memory and throughput**, and the precision it trades is precision at short distances. Long-range structure survives compression well; the ordering of the top few results does not.

7. **What you can promise**, in [S025](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S025.md)'s four forms: a proved ratio, which is rarely available here; restricted exactness, meaning an exact path retained for a named query class; anytime quality, meaning search until a latency budget and report what was reached; and a measured distribution with its horizon, which is the ordinary case. **And [S025](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S025.md) point 2 applies without softening: a recall promise the caller cannot verify is a claim about your competence rather than about the system.**

8. **The platform instance, and the silence at the centre of it. Marked perishable.** Three parameters govern the graph you used this morning: the number of bidirectional links built per element, the breadth of the candidate list held during construction, and the breadth held during search. Each has a published default and a published range, and the ranges are narrow enough that the whole recall–latency curve this day is about lives inside them. Two compression schemes are offered — one that narrows each number's resolution to a small integer, one that reduces each to a single bit — with an oversampling factor and a rescoring step to offset the loss, **and rescoring is available only on the graph, because the exhaustive path has nothing to oversample from.**

    **Now the part that matters more than any of it.** The documentation states every one of those parameters, every range, every default, the storage reduction each compression achieves, and the conditions under which the vendor recommends testing rather than adopting. **It states no recall figure. Anywhere. At any parameter setting.** It says approximate methods "sacrifice some accuracy" and does not say how much, which is the exact quantity this day is about, and which is the quantity a vendor is least able to publish because it is a property of your corpus rather than of their software. **The silence is correct and it is still a silence**, and the consequence for you is that the number is yours to measure or yours to guess.

9. **Defaults are opinions and thresholds are policy — and four of them decided your operating point before you arrived.**

    - **The graph's link count defaults to the bottom of its own documented range**, with the documentation observing that lower values return less noise. That is a relevance opinion expressed as an integer.
    - **The construction and search breadths default near the top of theirs.** Somebody decided you would rather pay latency than lose recall, and did not ask.
    - **The oversampling factor has a default**, which means a compressed index is quietly fetching more candidates than you asked for and re-ordering them, and your latency includes that.
    - **The similarity metric is a per-field choice with a recommended value that depends on which embedding model produced the vectors** — so the correct value is determined by a decision made on a different day, in a different service, by a different person.

    Each is defensible. None was announced. **An engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system**, and on this day four such choices sit between the student and the recall figure they are about to promise somebody.

10. **Scope, in the strong form.** Everything above treats the index as static and the query distribution as the one you measured on. That is **safe** for a build-and-measure decision on a fixed corpus, which is this day's task and [S060](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S060.md)'s. It is **unsafe** in three named ways, and each has a home. An index accumulating insertions and deletions degrades away from its measured recall with nobody watching, which is [S068](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S068.md)'s detectability problem. A query distribution that shifts moves the operating point without anyone touching the parameter, which is [S067](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S067.md)'s. And every latency figure here assumes the index sits where it sat when you measured it, which stops being true the moment it is co-resident with anything else, and is [S063](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S063.md)'s. **A student who ports today's numbers into a system that grows, drifts or shares memory has carried a measurement past all three of its conditions.** Points 8 and 9 carry a fourth boundary: they are **safe** for reasoning about this service at this version, and **unsafe** as a description of approximate search in general — the parameter names, the defaults and even which index families are offered are one vendor's choices, and the structural material in [research/02-technical-foundations/](/research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md) is what transfers.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Points 4 and 5 are the retained pair and point 5 is the day's whole argument. Point 2 is deliberately deflationary — the day's technical content is two year-one structures — because the alternative framing, in which vector indexes are a specialised subject, is the framing that makes students accept defaults. **Point 8's second half is the module's most important documentation silence and is taught rather than worked around**, per the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](/archive/bsc-programme/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md)'s standing observation that this stack's documentation states latencies and sizes everywhere and accuracy nowhere. Point 9 is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required naming of defaults.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 4. The budget, the operating point and the promise are the student's. This phase produces a build.**

Each student reconfigures **their own index from [S057](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S057.md)** to an operating point they choose, using Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server, and measures what it cost them.

1. **State the latency budget, who set it, and what happens to whom when it is exceeded.** If nobody set it, say so — that is a finding.
2. **Choose an index family and a parameterisation. State the operating point you are choosing and the one you are declining**, and why. Name every parameter you left at its default and say what accepting it committed you to.
3. **Predict the recall loss, and predict which documents you will lose** — not how many, which. Commit in writing.
4. **Build it and measure.** Recall against an exhaustive scan over the same vectors, and **latency at the tail rather than at the mean**, per [S013](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S013.md) point 7. The exhaustive path is your reference and it is one flag.
5. **Report recall per query class, and characterise the documents you lost** — group them and state what they share. **At least one shared property you name must not be among the three supplied at phase 4 point 5.**
6. **State what the approximation costs on the query class that motivated your [S057](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S057.md) retriever**, and whether the dense system is still worth its cost at this operating point.
7. **Write the promise to the caller**: one sentence, in one of [S025](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S025.md)'s forms, checkable by the caller, with what happens when it is not met.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** What you asked the agent for, in order, including what you abandoned.
- **Intervention record.** Every correction and what it was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that its own design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a build that computes its own recall against the exhaustive path and refuses to promise a figure it has not measured is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch.

**Fading:** step 2 is worked with the room, since the parameter semantics are the only genuinely new technical content. The reconfiguration itself is produced by the agent and read by the student. **Step 3 is unsupported.**

**Fix imported under rule 5.** Step 5's final clause is [WP-054](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8's named remedy for an instrument contaminated by the instruction preceding it, applied here at its second structurally identical site. Its reuse is **contingent** under the amended rule 5, since S054 has not run.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 4, **now reconfiguring a running index rather than describing one.** Step 3's second clause is the day's hardest demand and the one phase 3 prepares: predicting *how much* you will lose is arithmetic, predicting *what* you will lose is a claim about the geometry. Step 2's second sentence makes point 9 assessable. Step 6 is the discriminator — a student who concludes at step 6 that the dense retriever is no longer worth its cost has produced the day's best possible artefact and is not penalised for it. Step 7 makes the operating point a commitment to somebody rather than a configuration value.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate index whose probe parameter was lowered to meet a latency budget during a traffic increase. Aggregate recall against exact search fell by a small and correctly-reported amount. The documents lost were a small, isolated cluster belonging to the substrate's newest group of users, whose vocabulary and content resembled nothing else in the corpus. Those users' search results went empty and they did not report it; they stopped searching. Attention is directed at two facts — **the recall number was correct, was reported, and was read by the person who made the change** — and that the concentration was invisible because nobody had asked which documents.

Then the agent case, and it is the day's own material. A student asked Copilot to tune their index for a latency budget. The agent lowered the search breadth, explained the trade correctly in general terms, cited the parameter's documented range accurately, and **stated a recall expectation.** The figure was plausible, was expressed to two significant figures, and **has no source — no recall figure at any parameter setting is published for this service anywhere.** The student put it in a design note. Two people read the note. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **every other number in the agent's answer was correct and citable, the wrong one was the only one that mattered, and it was wrong in the one direction nobody checks — a number that was never measured rather than a number that was measured badly.**

Then students, in threes: a time you accepted a small aggregate loss without asking where it fell, or **a number you passed on today whose provenance you did not check.**

**Warrant.** Error management targeting point 5. The instructor case contains a correct measurement, an informed decision-maker and a real constraint, which is what makes it a lesson about the unit of reporting rather than about diligence. The users who stopped searching rather than complaining are the second half of the case and connect to [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md)'s audience C. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen so that it lands on point 8's silence**: an agent asked for a figure the documentation does not contain will produce one, and the review that catches it is the one nobody performs, which is checking whether the number exists at all.

## When it does not work

The predicted failures and their countermeasures are argued in [WP-058](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-058.md) §5. Restated here as instruction to the person in the room. Nothing below is assessed.

| What you see | What to do | What the design accepts |
|---|---|---|
| A student who has tuned an index ranks phase 1 correctly and writes step 7's promise from familiarity — a recall figure at a parameter setting, which satisfies the form and is checkable by no caller | Send it to phase 7's second demand, delivered by a peer: how would the **caller**, not the student, detect that the promise was false? A promise phrased as an internal metric is defeated undetectably in about a minute | §5 states the countermeasure without qualification. The undetectable defeat is the phase's most useful outcome and is recorded as both a defeat and a confirmation |
| A student cannot choose a parameterisation at step 2, because the parameters have no intuitive meaning to them | It is worked with the room. Four configurations with their parameterisations disclosed sat in front of them minutes earlier, and the exhaustive reference costs nothing to run. Say plainly that **choosing a bad operating point produces a usable artefact for this day**, because steps 5 and 6 measure the consequence rather than the choice | §5 treats this group as covered by the fading and the supplied configurations |
| A group gets the latency ordering immediately and guesses the recall ordering | Expected. The difficulty is the second reveal, and specifically explaining why the configuration with the **highest** aggregate recall has the worst concentrated loss. Fallback if a group explains it quickly: ask what the corpus would have to look like for I4's loss to be spread rather than concentrated | **§5 records that this fallback is, per §8 objection 2, the question the day's own materials are constructed to avoid.** It is the right question to ask and the design does not have an answer to it |
| A student concludes that the parameter is one of dozens, nobody can measure them all, and the honest position is to accept the defaults and say so | Hold the line where the day drew it: **one parameter chosen, not all of them**, and the day says so. Use step 1's *if nobody set it, say so*, which makes an unowned budget a reportable finding rather than a personal failure. Point at phase 6, where the case turns on a single unasked question rather than a missing measurement programme. State once that the difference between accepting a default and choosing it is a sentence in a document | **§5 names defaults fatalism as the characteristic affective failure and calls it more sophisticated than carelessness and harder to argue with, because it is nearly correct.** The countermeasures are stated as adequate and the position is not refuted, only bounded |

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in IS203. Per [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the latency budget with its setter and its consequence; the chosen and declined operating points **with every accepted default named and what accepting it committed you to**; the pre-committed prediction of which documents will be lost; measured recall and tail latency; **per-class recall with the lost documents characterised, including at least one shared property not supplied at phase 4 point 5**; the cost on the [S057](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S057.md) motivating class with the still-worth-it judgement; the caller promise in one of [S025](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S025.md)'s forms with its failure behaviour; the phase 7 outcome; **the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

**A recall figure reported without its per-class breakdown fails.** [S053](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S053.md)'s standard, third application.

**A promise the caller cannot check fails.** [S025](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S025.md) point 2, applied as a fail condition for the first time.

**A recall figure cited rather than measured fails.** No recall figure for this service at any parameter setting is published anywhere; a number in the artefact must be one the student took, and its provenance is checked.

No entrustment judgement. The quarter's is at [S060](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S060.md).

## What this day does not establish

**What it demonstrates.** Four index configurations over one corpus, one retriever and one query set, measured by the room: an ordering on latency and memory, an ordering on recall against the exhaustive reference, and the identity of the documents each configuration lost on one query class. **The concentration finding is a fact about this corpus's cluster structure**, and the day discloses at phase 3 that a differently-clustered corpus would produce a weaker one. No recall figure, latency figure or concentration magnitude may leave the room.

**What rests on a note.** Approximate nearest-neighbour search, the recall–latency trade and its parameterisation, compression as a trade of short-range geometric precision, and the non-uniform distribution of approximation error — at [research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md) and [research/02-technical-foundations/jegou-product-quantization.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/jegou-product-quantization.md).

**And one of those two notes was never read.** [WP-058](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-058.md)'s own release note records that the product-quantization source is paywalled, that the author deposit returned an automated challenge, that **the full text was not retrieved**, and that the note's entire technical account is therefore reconstructed rather than read. No figure is carried from it and none may be. **The disclosure belongs on the one day whose specification fails a student for citing a number nobody measured**, and a facilitator asked where the compression argument comes from should say: from a note whose source nobody in this repository has opened.

**A third note bears on this day and is named by the whitepaper rather than by the page.** [research/02-technical-foundations/subramanya-diskann.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/subramanya-diskann.md) appears in [WP-058](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-058.md)'s evidence status and not in this seminar's own source statement. **What the vendor does not say, which is the day's subject.** **No recall figure at any parameter setting is published for this service anywhere.** The documentation states every parameter, every range, every default and every storage reduction, and says only that approximate methods sacrifice some accuracy. **What rests on nothing verified here.** The phase warrants lean on retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, pretesting and cognitive load, and **none of the five has a note anywhere under [research/](/research/99-source-register/source-register.md)**. ## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **Recall against exact search and accuracy against relevance are two different criteria with two different adjudicators**, and your users experience only the second. Approximation loss concentrates rather than spreads — on isolated documents, on small clusters, and on the fine distinctions at short range — so a high aggregate recall figure is consistent with losing every document that justified building the system.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** Approximate nearest-neighbour search, the recall–latency trade and its parameterisation, compression as a trade of short-range geometric precision, and the non-uniform distribution of approximation error are verified at [research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md) and [research/02-technical-foundations/jegou-product-quantization.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/jegou-product-quantization.md). **No recall figure, latency figure or concentration magnitude is asserted for any named index family.** The vendor publishes no recall at any parameter setting.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Measure against an exhaustive scan over the same vectors.** It is one flag, and it is the only reference your recall figure has.
- **Predict which documents you will lose, not how many.** The count is arithmetic; the identity is a claim about the geometry.
- **Report tail latency rather than the mean, and per query class rather than in aggregate.**
- **Recall against exact search is an observed result; whether your users still find things is outcome evidence.** The fourth row of the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md)'s ladder is the expensive one.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Accepting a recall expectation from an answer in which every other number was correct and citable. **The check that catches it is not whether the number is right but whether it exists at all.**
