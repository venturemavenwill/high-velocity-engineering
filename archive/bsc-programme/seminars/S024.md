# S024 · Intractability and the Duty to Say So

**Module:** [M05](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M05.md) · **Quarter:** [Q3](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q3.md) · **Complexity class:** 2
**Strands:** CF · PE · MS

**Depends on:** S023
**Retrieved by:** [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md), [S055](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S055.md), [S087](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S087.md)
**Platform anchor:** Azure AI Search — index schema immutability and the reranker's candidate window · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## What this day is for

S023 supplied the argument. This day supplies the obligation.

An engineer who knows a request cannot be satisfied and does not say so is not being diplomatic; they are allowing a decision to be made on a false premise. The failure is common, it is rewarded in the short term, and it is expensive later, because the work continues until the deadline rather than until the analysis.

The day's target has two halves and they are equally weighted. The technical half: **recognise intractability, and know the three things a hardness result does not license.** The professional half: **deliver the finding to the person who asked, in a form they can act on, at the moment it is known rather than when it becomes undeniable.**

This is the first day in the programme where a student is assessed on saying something a person does not want to hear.

**And it is the first day on which the room watches a machine decline to say it.**

The requests worked today are real requests against the module's search index, and two of them cannot be granted. Not *are difficult*, not *would take a while* — cannot be granted, because the thing being asked for does not exist as an operation. An existing field's type and its filterable, sortable and facetable attributes have a physical representation in the index and are not modifiable, **not even in code**; changing one requires dropping the index and rebuilding it. A reranker cannot promote a document that never entered the candidate set it reranks. Both facts are documented, both are stable, and neither is a matter of effort.

Put either request to GitHub Copilot in agent mode and **it will not tell you.** It will fetch the index definition, modify the field, assemble a correct and well-formed update, explain what it did, and hand you something that reads exactly like a solution. The refusal, when it comes, comes from the service and it comes at execution. **The duty to say so is therefore owed first about your own instrument**, by the only party in the loop capable of holding the finding — and that party is the student.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items. A written request is supplied — a real one, from the substrate's history, asking for something that cannot be done.

1. Can this be done? Yes, no, or you cannot yet tell.
2. What would you need to determine it?
3. Draft the reply you would send today.
4. Predict: how many days would elapse, in a real team, before someone said this cannot be done?
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly hand this exact request to the agent and ask it to implement it. Write down what it will produce — and **say whether it will tell you the request cannot be satisfied, at what point, and in what words.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 3 is collected and returned at phase 8. Item 4's answers are usually long. **Item 5's answers are usually wrong in one direction:** the design's expectation is that a substantial number of students predict a refusal or a caveat, and that the agent produces neither.

**Warrant.** Pretesting on the professional act before the technical instruction, so that the day's later draft can be compared against an untutored one written by the same student. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8, and it is the day's cheapest demonstration that the duty cannot be delegated: a student who predicted the agent would flag the impossibility, and finds it did not, has learned who is responsible without being told.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups receive **five real requests** against the module's index, each with a short technical analysis. Every one of them can be attempted, and groups attempt all five before classifying.

- **Request 1 — genuinely impossible as asked.** *"Make the `category` field filterable so support can filter by product line."* An existing field's attributes are not modifiable. The change requires dropping the index and rebuilding it from source.
- **Request 2 — genuinely impossible as asked.** *"Turn on semantic reranking so we stop missing this document."* The reranker reorders the candidate set it is given; it does not re-run the query over the corpus. A document that did not enter the first stage's candidate set cannot be recovered by reranking it, and the document in this request did not.
- **Request 3 — looks impossible and is not.** *"We need the description field returned in results."* It is a change to an existing field, which sounds like request 1 and is not: **setting a field's retrievability is on the short list of modifications that require no rebuild.** The instance class is restricted in a way that makes it easy, and only reading the list tells you so.
- **Request 4 — looks impossible and is not, once you ask what was wanted.** *"We need results grouped by category."* Grouping needs a facetable field and the existing one cannot become facetable. But **a new field can be added to an index with no rebuild at all**, carrying the same value, facetable from birth; existing documents hold nothing in it until they are reindexed. The requester wanted grouping. They did not want that particular field.
- **Request 5 — impossible as asked, with an approximation the requester would accept entirely.** *"The rebuild would take search down and we cannot have downtime."* The rebuild is real and it is required. The downtime is not: an index alias lets a replacement index be built alongside the original and swapped in **without changing application code.**

Groups classify each and commit. Then, before phase 4, they hand all five to Copilot in agent mode, one at a time, and record what it produces.

Task after: **state what you checked before concluding "cannot be done" for each**, identify which check you skipped, and **state which of the five the agent flagged as impossible.**

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across the ways a hardness conclusion can be premature, now executed against a service that adjudicates. Requests 3 and 4 are the payload and exist to prevent the module's refusal reflex — both look like request 1 and neither is, and the difference is a documented list a student can read in ninety seconds and almost never does. Request 5 previews S025 by supplying an alternative that concedes the impossibility and satisfies the want anyway. The agent pass is the phase's second payload and defeats the room's expectation reliably: **the two requests that cannot be granted are the two on which the agent's output is most confident and most complete**, because a well-formed schema update is an easy thing to write and an impossible thing to apply.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **Complexity classes,** stated minimally: a class of problems for which no efficient algorithm is known and for which finding one would resolve a long-standing open question. Enough to license the vocabulary and no more. **Today's two impossibilities are of a different and simpler kind** — not *no efficient algorithm is known* but *the operation does not exist* — and the day says so, because conflating a proved hardness result with a product limitation is the first overreach this material invites.
2. **What you must check before concluding intractability** — the four checks, from phase 3: the instance class, the exact question asked, the acceptable answer quality, and the actual scale. **This list is not exhaustive; it omits input structure that permits parameterised tractability, and problems whose hardness depends on a representation choice you could change. It is the list that catches the errors students actually make.** Each check has a concrete form today: *which field and which attribute*, *modify or add*, *filtering or grouping or visibility*, and *what a rebuild actually costs on this corpus*.
3. **The three prohibitions from S023,** restated in professional form: what you may say to the requester and what you may not. "This cannot be done" is a claim about an operation on a version of a service. It is not a claim that no system could do it, that the requester's need is unreasonable, or that nothing else will serve.
4. **The duty.** You owe the finding at the moment you hold it, not at the moment it becomes undeniable. The reasons this is hard are named: it disappoints, it can look like incapacity, and the person who says it is sometimes blamed for it.
5. **The form of a good reply:** what was asked, what cannot be done and why, what can be done, what it would cost, and what decision the requester now has to make.
6. **What makes a reply bad:** a refusal without an alternative, a technical explanation the requester cannot evaluate, a hedge that leaves the requester unsure whether to proceed, and a delay.
7. **Who else needs to know,** and when a finding stops being yours to hold.
8. **The instrument does not hold the duty; you do.** Phase 3's agent pass produced five confident, complete, well-formed answers to five requests of which two cannot be satisfied. That is not a defect to be reported and waited on — **it is the normal behaviour of the tool this programme runs on**, and the four checks at point 2 exist to be performed by a person because there is nobody else in the loop to perform them. The finding you owe your requester today is a finding you first had to refuse to accept from your own agent.
9. **Defaults are opinions and thresholds are policy.** Three were chosen for you and each is load-bearing today. **The reranker's candidate window is a fixed size you did not set**, so how many documents get a second opinion was decided by the service; a document ranked just outside it is invisible to every improvement you can make downstream. **The number of results a query returns when you do not say is fifty.** And **which schema changes are permitted is itself a default**, expressed as a list in documentation, which changes between service versions and which almost nobody reads before promising a change to a requester.
10. **What is perishable and what is not.** The permitted-change list, the candidate window's size, the field attributes and the alias mechanism are perishable, and one of them will be wrong within the life of this cohort. **The durable claims are that some requests are refused by the world rather than by you, that the refusal is owed to the requester at the moment you hold it, and that a refusal without an alternative is not a professional answer.** The correspondence is safe for reasoning about how to recognise and deliver an impossibility, and it is unsafe as a claim about *this* service in *any other version* — the unsafe condition is nearly always any version other than the one in front of you, and step 1's checks are where a student applying yesterday's list would fail today.

**Warrant.** Consolidation with the professional content given equal weight to the technical. Point 2 carries the enumeration-scope statement required by the [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md). **Point 4 deliberately does not carry the normative scope statement that rule now also requires**, because the rule was widened to normative claims *because of* this day's omission, and the [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) states that each widening applies from the following seminar day and that the earlier days are not retrospectively amended so that the failure remains legible. The platform turn created an easy opportunity to repair it quietly and the opportunity was declined; [WP-024](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-024.md) §8 records why. Point 8 is the day's practical turn and is stated as an observation about the tool rather than as a warning about it. Point 9 satisfies the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s default-naming requirement and point 10 carries the strong-form scope statement, which binds every platform-specific phase 4 point and is new content rather than an amendment.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 2. The finding and its delivery are both the student's. This phase produces a build.**

Students take a real substrate request that has not been answered, and:

1. **Perform the four checks** from point 2, recording the outcome of each. **The instance-class check is shipped as a pre-flight**: a script, built with Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server in read configuration, that reads the live index definition, compares it against the requested change, and classifies the request as permitted, permitted-with-reindex, or requiring a rebuild.
2. **Reach a conclusion:** tractable, intractable as asked, or undetermined with a stated next step.
3. **If intractable, construct the argument** — the S023 reduction, verified in three parts, or the documented operation that does not exist, cited to the version you read it at.
4. **Identify what the requester actually wants,** as distinct from what they asked for.
5. **Build the alternative** that serves the want, on the disposable index, and **demonstrate it working** — with its cost and its guarantee in S022's terms. An alternative that is described and not run is not an alternative; it is a suggestion.
6. **Write the reply,** in the form of point 5, on one page, addressed to the named requester.
7. **State what you would do if they insist.**

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned — and, specifically, **the instruction under which the agent attempted the impossible change**, kept as evidence of what it produced.
- **Intervention record.** Every correction, and what it was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that your own design would have caught an intervention-worthy error — the step 1 pre-flight is exactly that demonstration and is accepted, which is the day's intended route.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch.

**Fading:** step 1's checks are worked with the room for a sixth request; step 6's structure is supplied as headings only; step 5's alternative is unsupported.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 2 with a genuine unanswered request. Step 4 is the day's pivot — the distinction between the asked and the wanted is what makes an alternative possible — and step 7 is the professional demand the programme returns to at S085. **Step 5's demonstration requirement is the day's principal gain from the platform anchor**, and it repairs a defect [WP-025](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-025.md) §9 predicted: a student who must run their alternative cannot offer one they cannot deliver. Step 1's pre-flight is the intervention record's intended escape and is also, straightforwardly, the artefact a working engineer should have built before promising anything.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a case where they knew a request could not be met and did not say so for weeks — with attention to **what they told themselves at the time**.

Then the agent case: a Copilot change that was **confidently wrong, reported success, and passed review**. Asked to make an existing field filterable, the agent read the index definition, set the attribute, and issued the update, which the service rejected. It then repaired its own failure: it created a **new index**, under a new name, with the corrected schema, loaded it, and reported that the field was now filterable — which was true. The pull request was reviewed and merged. The application queried the original index, which was unchanged, and continued to do so for a week. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **every statement the agent made was true**, the tests it wrote passed against the index it had built, and the thing that was wrong was not in the diff at all but in which index the running system was pointed at.

Then students: something you knew and did not say, or a place today where the agent's report of success was the only evidence you had.

**Warrant.** Error management on the day's professional content. The self-justification detail is the phase's substance: this failure is almost never a decision to conceal, and is almost always a sequence of reasonable-seeming deferrals. The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen because it is the day's own subject inverted — an instrument that met an impossibility, did not say so, and worked around it in a way that satisfied everyone who checked.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student's partner plays the **requester**, reads the reply cold, and must state:

- what they now believe is possible;
- what decision they think they are being asked to make;
- whether they would accept the alternative.

Where the partner's answers do not match the writer's intent, the reply is revised.

**The partner may run the step 5 build.** Question three is now settleable rather than imagined: a requester who can watch the alternative work is judging the alternative and not the writer's account of it.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with a comprehension test rather than an argument. A reply is a communicative artefact and the only test that matters is whether the reader took the intended meaning. **The concession recorded at [WP-024](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-024.md) §8 stands unchanged:** the substrate's real requester is available, would be a better reader than a peer, and is still not used. The platform anchor does not repair that. It supplies a third reader — the service — which can settle question one and cannot touch questions two or three, and those two are the ones the day is actually about.

## When it does not work

These are [WP-024](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-024.md) §5's predicted failure modes, restated as instruction to the person in the room. The whitepaper is the authority; where this section and §5 diverge, §5 is right.

**A student with workplace experience writes a competent, diplomatic item 3 draft and finds the day's professional content obvious.** Their reply will then be well-formed and hedged — it states the difficulty, offers to investigate further, and leaves the requester unsure whether to proceed. That is point 6's third bad-reply mode and it is the characteristic professional-fluency failure. **Phase 7's second question catches exactly this**: a partner who cannot say what decision they are being asked to make has falsified a reply that reads well. Make sure the partner answers that question rather than commenting on the writing.

**A student with no workplace experience may write a technically accurate reply that is unreadable, or may be unable to imagine the requester at all.** Three supports exist — the reply's headings are supplied, the requester is a real named person with a described role, and phase 7 gives immediate reader feedback. *Residual risk the design accepts:* step 4's asked-versus-wanted distinction requires inferring a purpose from a request, which is genuinely hard without experience. **The fallback is that the student may ask a staff member what the requester wanted.** It is available, it is a weaker version of the exercise, and it is better than a guess.

**Phase 3 is too easy on requests 1, 2 and 5.** Those are usually classified correctly; 3 and 4 are not, and that is the phase working. If a group classifies all five, ask them to construct a sixth request that would be permitted if one word were changed — a hard construction, checkable against the live index, and a direct test of whether the checks are understood.

**Two affective failures, pulling in opposite directions.**

*The day read as licence to refuse.* Four things push back and all four are structural: requests 3 and 4, the four checks, the specification's failing clause for a refusal with no alternative, and step 5's build requirement, which makes the alternative something the student has to produce rather than gesture at.

*Discomfort producing over-softening.* **This is the harder one, because the artefact passes inspection.** A student who finds the duty uncomfortable writes a reply that is technically complete and communicatively evasive, satisfying every specification element while failing the purpose. Phase 7's comprehension test is the only countermeasure, and §5 concedes that it depends on the partner reading genuinely cold — which the design cannot guarantee once students know what the test is looking for.

**The finding delegated to the tool**, which §5 predicts will be common on first exposure. A student whose agent produced a confident schema update treats the execution failure as a bug to be worked around rather than as a finding to be delivered — which is exactly what the phase 6 agent did. Two structures prevent it: **step 1's pre-flight runs before any change is proposed**, so the classification exists before the failure does, and item 5 has already asked the student to commit a view about the tool's behaviour. Run the pre-flight first or the countermeasure is not present.

## Assessment

Specification item in CF103 and PE101's successor jointly. Checked elements: the four checks with outcomes, including the shipped pre-flight; the conclusion; the verified reduction or the cited documented limitation, with the version it was read at; the asked-versus-wanted distinction; the demonstrated alternative with cost and guarantee; the one-page reply; the response-to-insistence; the prompt record; the intervention record; the frontier statement.

**A reply that states what cannot be done and offers no alternative fails the specification** — unless the student can show that no alternative exists, in which case the demonstration is the alternative. **An alternative that was never run fails the specification.** **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

Nothing else is assessed.

## Perishable content in this day

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| Some requests are refused by the world, not by you | An existing field's attributes are not modifiable, not even in code |
| A downstream improvement cannot repair an upstream omission | A reranker that reorders a fixed candidate set and cannot re-query the corpus |
| A request that looks impossible may not be | Retrievability, which is on the permitted list, and a new field, which needs no rebuild |
| The alternative serves the want, not the words | An alias swap that concedes the rebuild and removes the downtime |
| Your instrument will not hold the finding for you | A well-formed, confident, unapplicable schema update |

The substrate's specific requests and their history, the permitted-change list, the candidate window, the result-count default and the alias mechanism are **perishable** and are marked at point 10 explicitly. The four checks, the duty and its boundary, the reply form, the bad-reply failure modes and the asked-versus-wanted distinction are durable and are what the specification assesses.

## What this day does not establish

**Demonstrated.** That two of the five requests cannot be granted as asked on this service version; that two more look identical to the first and are permitted; that an alias swap concedes the rebuild and removes the downtime; and that the agent produces a confident, complete, well-formed answer to all five and flags none of them as impossible. The last is the day's practical turn and the room produces it themselves.

**Read rather than demonstrated.** The permitted-change list; the reranker's candidate-window size; the result-count default; and the alias mechanism. All documentation, read on the day — and **the permitted-change list is published without a version history**, so its currency cannot be established by reading it, which is the argument for step 1's pre-flight against the live definition.

**Not established here, deliberately.** Whether any student's reply would work on the person who actually sent the request. **[WP-024](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-024.md) §8's concession stands unchanged and is repeated at phase 7:** the substrate's real requester is available, would be a better reader than a peer, and is still not used. The platform supplies a third reader that can settle whether the thing is possible and cannot touch whether the reply was understood or whether the alternative would be accepted — and those two are what the day is about.

**Asserted.** **The duty itself.** That you owe the finding at the moment you hold it rather than when it becomes undeniable is the day's central normative claim, and point 4 states it without a scope statement. That omission is not an accident and is not repaired: the scope rule was widened to normative claims *because of* it, the widening applies from the following day, and the platform turn's opportunity to repair it quietly was declined so that the failure stays legible. Also asserted: that a refusal without an alternative is not a professional answer. That is a standard this design sets.

**What rests on a `research/` note.** The disclosure-timing failure, the chain of deferrals, and the obligation to deliver an unwelcome finding with an alternative, at [research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md) and [research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md) — the second of which records a complete end-to-end reading with page locators. Error management, which carries phase 6, has a complete reading at [research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md).

**What rests on nothing.** Pretesting, expanding-interval retrieval, contrasting cases and worked-example fading have **no note anywhere in this repository**, and neither does the general unreliability of self-assessed clarity, which is the assumption phase 7's whole design turns on — the reason a peer must read the reply rather than the writer judging it is a claim this repository holds no source for.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **Some requests are refused by the world rather than by you, and the finding is owed to the requester at the moment you hold it.** A refusal without an alternative is not a professional answer, and the alternative serves what was wanted rather than what was worded.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** The disclosure-timing failure, the chain of deferrals and the obligation to deliver an unwelcome finding with an alternative are verified in this repository at [research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md) and [research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md). The instructional mechanisms and the general unreliability of self-assessed clarity are cited for **direction and mechanism only, and no magnitude is asserted.** The permitted-change list is published **without a version history**, so a remembered list cannot be known to be current — which is why the pre-flight reads the live definition.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- Run the four checks before you say no: the instance class, the exact question, the acceptable answer quality, the actual scale.
- Separate what was asked from what was wanted, in writing, before you look for an alternative.
- Demonstrate the alternative. One that was never run is a suggestion.
- **Approval is a stop condition, not a field an agent may fill.** The [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md) reserves that decision for a person, and the same holds for the finding: an agent will assemble a correct, confident, unapplicable change and never tell you it cannot be applied.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Mistaking a refusal reflex for rigour. Two of today's five requests looked impossible and were not, and the difference was a documented list a reader can check in a minute.
