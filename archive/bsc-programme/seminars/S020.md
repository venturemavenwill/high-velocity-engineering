# S020 · Your First Capacity Argument ⊘

**Module:** [M04](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M04.md) · **Quarter:** [Q2](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q2.md) · **Complexity class:** 2
**Strands:** SY · PE · MS · ME
**Whitepaper:** [WP-020](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-020.md)
**Depends on:** S016, S017, S018, S019
**Re-tests:** S009, S015
**Platform anchor:** Foundry quota and capacity; the quota increase request as the real decision · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

⊘ **Assessment-bearing day.** Phases 5 to 7 are replaced by preparation, defence and reconciliation.

---

## What this day is for

The quarter has taught four cost models and required nineteen predictions. This day requires **one quantitative claim about the future of a real system, made before observation, defended to a person who will act on it.**

That is a different act from every prior prediction, in three ways. The claim is about something that has not happened. Nobody knows the answer, including the assessor. And somebody will decide something on the basis of it.

The substrate has a **real forthcoming load change** — a scheduled event, a new cohort, a feature launch, a seasonal pattern. Students argue about whether the system can take it.

And the decision is now a real one with money and refusal in it. The substrate's Foundry deployments have allocations; the decision-owner's options are to request more quota, to buy provisioned units, to move work into the batch class, or to do nothing; and each has a price and a lead time. **The day's hardest fact is the one the vendor states plainly and every student has to discover anyway: quota is a policy limit enforced by Azure with no associated cost, and having it does not mean the capacity exists.** Provisioned capacity in a region changes through the day; a region can be sold out to a customer who holds unused quota for it; and a deployment that releases capacity has no guarantee of getting it back. A capacity argument that ends at "we will ask for more quota" has not reached the constraint.

## Before you run this day

**Materials to prepare.** None of the following exists in this repository. Two of the items are people rather than documents, and [WP-020](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-020.md) records both as things the design does not control.

- **A real forthcoming load change on the substrate** — a scheduled event, a new cohort, a feature launch, a seasonal pattern — that **has not yet happened** and whose outcome will be measurable in Q3. The assessment's validity rests on it, and the paper concedes that the design does not control its properties.
- **A real decision-owner**: a staff member responsible for the substrate who will act on what they hear, who states at phase 5 point 4 what they actually needed, who opens phase 6 with their own wrong capacity decision including what they had said beforehand about what would make them wrong, and who says afterwards which of the four options they chose. Not a role-play. This is the day's most important structural feature and, in the paper's words, its least reproducible.
- **Two assessors** who will form a level from the artefact independently before the defence begins, who have not seen each other's, and who are **absent from phase 6 and are never told what was said in it**.
- **The four priced options with their current prices and lead times** — a quota request, provisioned units, the batch path, or nothing — read close enough to the day that the fourth defence question is answerable against real numbers.
- **The substrate's full measurement history from [S016](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S016.md) to [S019](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S019.md)**, assembled and available in phase 3, plus current quota and usage readable from the platform.
- **The phase 2 cumulative set**, including the three synthesis items, and **one worked memory-cliff-and-queueing-knee interaction** to supply rather than leave open.
- **The phase 6 agent case**: the capacity argument whose model was sound, whose arithmetic was right, whose units were consistent, and whose identified constraint was a configured tokens-per-minute allocation that could have been raised in an afternoon — with the review that passed it because everything checkable was correct.
- **The [S010](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S010.md) psychological-safety instrument**, to be re-administered identically at phase 8.
- **A record for both long-horizon series and a named Q3 session to resolve them in.** The series are recorded on this day and resolved on no day; without the session they are a literature rather than a measurement.

**Staff preparation.**

- **Rehearse the judgement block and read it to the room before phase 1**, in the terms given. A weighting discovered afterwards is a different assessment, and the design's position is that an assessment whose rules are discoverable only afterwards is not the one it claims to be.
- **Read every origin attribution before the defence**, because the third question is targeted at an output the student attributed to the agent. That targeting costs no time and is what converts ownership from an impression into a checkable element.
- **Brief the decision-owner** to ask the fourth question as a genuine request rather than as a test.
- **Brief the circulating staff** on the line they must hold in phase 3: questions of fact are answered, questions of reasoning are refused.
- **Confirm the tokens position** for resubmission, per the [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md), before anyone needs it.

**Platform prerequisites.**

- Foundry deployments with **real allocations**, and current quota and usage readable through the **Azure MCP Server in read configuration**. Step 3 turns on the difference between an allocation, a resource and a commitment, and a student cannot classify a constraint they cannot see.
- Current regional capacity and current prices for provisioned units. Both are read on the day rather than prepared, because both move.
- The Microsoft Learn MCP Server and Copilot agent mode. **The agent is present by design**; this is not one of the programme's three unassisted occasions.
- **Quota and capacity visibility is subscription-scoped and is not yours to grant unless you own the subscription.** Settle read access well before the week the day runs.

**If a prerequisite is missing.**

- *There is no real forthcoming load change.* The day's premise goes with it. The design offers no substitute, and inventing one would make the answer knowable — which is the single property the day requires the question not to have, because the assessors not knowing the answer is what changes how students prepare.
- *No decision-owner can attend.* [WP-020](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-020.md) supplies no replacement and none is invented here. An argument defended to an assessor is an examination; that is a different act and should be described as one rather than presented as this day.
- *A student cannot identify a binding constraint at all.* That is a specification failure. Tokens are available, and resubmission is a written argument without a second defence, which §5 accepts as a weaker but adequate remedy.

## How the judgement is formed, stated before phase 1

This is read to the room before any item is opened, in these terms.

- **Two assessors form the level from the artefact, independently, before the defence begins**, per rater discipline rule 7. The defence can move the level in either direction and is recorded as having done so. An assessor who has not formed a level from the artefact does not sit the defence.
- **The weighted element is the assumption**, not the number. An argument with a correct number and no identified breaking assumption does not pass. An argument with a wrong number and a well-identified assumption does. This is stated before preparation because a weighting discovered afterwards is a different assessment.
- **No carry-forward.** Nothing from S016 to S019 raises or lowers this judgement, and nothing recorded here is carried into Q3's first entrustment occasion. The module artefact is assessed separately and against its own specification.
- **Named failure conditions.** The argument fails if the claim is not a number with a unit and a condition; if no binding constraint is identified; if no breaking assumption is identified; if the confidence statement misrepresents the evidence; or if the intervention record is empty and the artefact does not demonstrate that its own checks could have caught an intervention-worthy error.
- **The error clinic is firewalled.** Assessors are absent from phase 6 and are not told what was said in it, ever. Nothing surfaced there may appear in any judgement about the student who surfaced it.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Before any preparation, individually and in writing.

1. Can the system take the forthcoming change? Yes, no, or it depends on what.
2. What is the single number you would most want to know?
3. What is the assumption most likely to make your answer wrong?
4. Predict whether your answer will change during the day.
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will prepare this argument with the agent. Write down what it will contribute that you could not have produced as fast, and **name the one thing it will get wrong**. Opened at phase 8 and **recorded for the long-horizon check**.

Sealed. Opened at phase 8.

**Warrant.** Pretesting on the assessed question itself, before preparation, so that the day's movement is measurable. Item 3 is the assumption-identification move the assessment weights most heavily, elicited before it is coached. Item 5 is the module's fifth sealed frontier prediction and the only one that enters a long-horizon series; it is administered at phase 1 and may not migrate, per instrumentation rule 8.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative module retrieval (30 min)

Extended, unblocked, covering all five module days.

- The two hierarchy properties.
- The five conflicting scheduling objectives.
- The working set, and what distinguishes it from allocation.
- The three quantities from S019 and the relation connecting them.
- Which of the four days' models is most likely to be the binding constraint here, and why?
- Name a case where the memory cliff and the queueing knee would interact.
- **State the difference between quota and capacity, and name one thing you can hold without having the other.**

The final three items are the module's integration check.

**Warrant.** Cumulative unblocked retrieval at gaps of one to nineteen days, with synthesis items requiring the module to have been one thing. The quota-and-capacity item is added because it is the distinction the defence turns on and because a student who cannot state it will produce an argument that ends one step early.

## Phase 3 · Preparation (75 min)

Students work individually with Copilot in agent mode and the Azure MCP Server in read configuration. Available: the substrate, its full measurement history, the module's four cost models, current quota and usage read from the platform, and staff who will answer factual questions but will not evaluate reasoning.

Required output, committed before phase 4:

1. **The claim.** A number with a unit and a condition. "The system will serve the additional load with ninety-ninth-percentile latency under X ms" — not "the system will cope."
2. **The model** that produces it, showing which of the four cost models bind and which do not.
3. **The binding constraint** and its margin. Where the constraint is an allocation rather than a machine, say whether it is a quota limit, a capacity limit, or a limit on what the shared pool will sustain — the three have different remedies and different lead times.
4. **The assumption that would most change the answer,** with the direction and rough magnitude of the change.
5. **What you would measure** in the first hour after the change to know whether you were wrong. Name the signal, its lead time, and the window it is observable in.
6. **The confidence statement** — what you are and are not claiming.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), and they are assessed with the rest.

- **Prompt record.** What you asked the agent for, in order, including what you abandoned.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that its own checks could have detected an intervention-worthy error — an arithmetic assertion tying the claim to the measured curve is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did that you could not have done as fast, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch.
- **Origin attribution.** For each of the six required outputs, state whether it originated with you or with the agent. This is a factual record, not a confession; an argument assembled largely from agent output and understood completely is a good argument, and one assembled entirely by hand and understood poorly is not. **The third defence question will be asked about an output you attributed to the agent.**

**Warrant.** Individual preparation with factual support and no reasoning support, which is the closest available approximation to the professional condition and is the assessment's validity requirement. The agent is present because it is present in the professional condition too, and because an argument assembled with an agent and defended without one is exactly the disclosure the programme's dependency risk requires. Step 3's three-way distinction is the platform's contribution to the day and is where most preparations fail.

## Phase 4 · The defence (60 min)

Groups of four. Each student defends for twelve minutes to **two assessors and a person who actually owns the decision** — a staff member responsible for the substrate, who will act on what they hear. Both assessors have already formed a level from the artefact, independently, and neither has seen the other's.

The questions are published in advance:

- What is your claim, precisely?
- Which constraint binds, and how do you know?
- What would have to be true for you to be wrong? **Asked about an output the student attributed to the agent.**
- If I have budget for one change, what should I spend it on?
- What will you tell me if you are wrong?

The fourth question is not in the prepared output and requires the student to convert an analysis into a recommendation on the spot. Its options are real and priced: request additional quota, deploy provisioned units and pay for them by the hour whether or not they are used, move a class of work into the batch path and accept its turnaround, or spend nothing. The decision-owner will say afterwards which they chose.

The third question's targeting costs no additional time and is the day's answer to a specific risk: **the weighted element is the one an agent supplies most fluently.** Asked what assumption would most change an answer, an agent will name one, plausibly, every time, and accepting it is not a correction and so leaves no trace in the intervention record. Directing the question at an attributed output makes ownership checkable rather than inferred.

**Warrant.** Assessment on defensible judgement under questioning, with a real decision-owner present. The published questions remove ambush; the fourth question preserves genuine demand. Levels formed from the artefact before the defence are the rater discipline rule 7 requirement, and their movement during the defence is itself recorded, because a level that never moves means the defence is not doing the work the design claims for it.

## Phase 5 · Consolidating instruction (40 min)

After all defences.

1. **What a capacity argument is.** A model, a binding constraint, a margin, and the assumption that would break it. Four parts, and an argument missing any one is not one.
2. **The cohort's claims,** shown unattributed as a distribution. The spread is the instruction.
3. **Where the spread came from** — traced to specific differing assumptions rather than to differing competence.
4. **What the decision-owner actually needs,** stated by the decision-owner: usually the margin and the assumption, rarely the model. **Added 2026-07-30, from a source this day already cited and had not read in full.** The company that named this kind of role states the measure for its field engineers and states it in one line: "They measure success in terms of impact on the customer's goal." **It states no measure at all for its product engineers**, whom it describes by what they own and decide. That asymmetry is worth holding as you leave this defence: the argument you just made is judged by what the decision-owner does next, not by the quality of the model that produced it — and the same company places the field role in its business-development division rather than its engineering one, so the reporting line follows the measure. **Neither fact is evidence that the arrangement works.** The article is recruiting material, it reports no quantity, and it never states why the model exists.
5. **Quota is not capacity, and neither is a reservation.** The three things a student may have confused: an allocation you are permitted, a resource that exists to be allocated, and a commercial commitment to pay for it. The documentation is explicit that the first has no cost and does not imply the second. This is the module's last durable claim and the one [S064](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S064.md) inherits directly.
6. **Being wrong about the future is normal.** What is not normal is being wrong without having said what would make you wrong.
7. **How this argument will be checked.** The load change happens; the outcome is recorded; the comparison is reported to the cohort in Q3. **The frontier predictions are checked on the same schedule and reported as a separate series.**

**Warrant.** Consolidation after assessment rather than before, so that the instruction lands on an experience rather than preparing for one. Point 2's spread display is the day's central instructional device. Point 5 is placed after the defence because it is the distinction most students will have discovered during it, and consolidation of a discovered distinction is cheaper than instruction in an undiscovered one.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min) — assessors absent

Opened by the decision-owner, not the instructor: a capacity decision they made about this system that was wrong, what it cost, and what they had said beforehand about what would make them wrong.

Then the agent case: a capacity argument prepared with Copilot that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. The model was sound, the arithmetic was right, the units were consistent, and the constraint it identified was the deployment's configured tokens-per-minute allocation — which is a policy limit, not a capacity, and could have been raised in an afternoon. The real constraint was two layers away and the argument never reached it. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **everything checkable was correct**, and the defect was that the model stopped at the first limit it found a number for.

Then students: something you were sure of during phase 3 preparation and revised, or a place where you accepted the agent's framing of the problem rather than its answer.

**Firewall applies. Assessors are absent and remain uninformed.**

**Warrant.** Error management under the firewall demonstrated at S010, now on its second assessment-bearing occasion. The decision-owner opening rather than the instructor is deliberate: the disclosure comes from the person whose judgement was just being emulated. The agent case is chosen because framing errors are the class of agent failure that survives every check a student knows how to run, and because it is the day's own weighted element failing in a form that looks like success.

---

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (25 min)

Pairs, after the clinic. Each student states **the assumption they identified at phase 3 step 4**, and the partner states theirs.

Where they differ, the pair determines whether both are load-bearing or whether one of them dominates.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation focused on the day's weighted element. Comparing assumptions rather than conclusions is what makes the difference between two arguments legible.

---

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- **Phase 1's sealed answers opened.** How many changed, in which direction, and whether item 3's assumption survived preparation.
- **Item 5 opened.** Each student's frontier prediction read against their own frontier statement from phase 3. The two were written five hours apart about the same tool on the same task, and the distance between them is the day's calibration measure for the programme's primary instrument.
- The S010 psychological-safety instrument re-administered, identically.
- The quarter's retrieval schedule issued for the inter-quarter gap.
- Each student records the date the load change occurs and what they expect to be told.

## The long-horizon prediction check

Two series are recorded on this day and neither is resolved on it. This is the programme's first deferred adjudication and the machinery is stated here because later days depend on it.

| Series | Recorded | Resolved | Retrieved at |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Capacity** — the claim, its binding constraint and its breaking assumption | Phase 3 | Q3, when the load change has happened and the outcome is measured | [S040](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S040.md), [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md), [S090](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S090.md) |
| **Frontier** — the phase 1 prediction about the agent, and the phase 3 statement about what it actually did | Phases 1 and 3 | Q3, alongside the capacity comparison | [S040](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S040.md), [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md), [S090](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S090.md) |

The two are kept separate and are not combined. The capacity series asks whether the student's estimate of a system was right. **The frontier series asks whether their estimate of their own principal instrument was right**, which is the question the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) commits the programme to answering and the one it has the weaker claim to. A student may be well calibrated on one and badly calibrated on the other, and the design expects that combination to be common.

The reader of both is named, per instrumentation rule 9: the cohort, at the Q3 report, and the following year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md). **S090 is the series' terminal reading and is also one of the programme's three unassisted occasions**, which means the frontier series can be read there against work produced without the instrument it describes. That is the only place in the programme where that comparison is available, and it is the reason S090 rather than any earlier day carries the terminal reading.

## When it does not work

These are [WP-020](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-020.md) §5's predicted failure modes, restated as instruction to the person in the room. The whitepaper is the authority; where this section and §5 diverge, §5 is right.

**A student with capacity-planning experience produces a well-structured argument quickly, and a narrow confidence statement.** Professional practice rewards decisiveness, so their step 4 assumption will be chosen for defensibility rather than for how much of the answer it carries. It will look competent and it is the failure the weighting exists to catch. The third defence question is asked by an assessor who has read their step 4, which makes a defensible-but-minor assumption visible immediately; phase 7 exposes the same thing when a peer identifies something larger.

**A student who struggled through M04 faces a synthesis of four models they hold shakily.** Four things already help: the enumerated output structure, factual staff support, published defence questions, and the weighting itself — a poorly estimated number is survivable. *Residual risk the design accepts:* they may not identify a binding constraint at all, which is a specification failure rather than a weak pass. Tokens are available and resubmission is a written argument without a second defence, which the design accepts as weaker but adequate.

**Phase 2's synthesis items are too hard.** Expected, and not treated as a failure. The memory-cliff-and-queueing-knee interaction is answered well by very few, and that is the honest measure of a module that ran four consecutive high-load days. Supply one worked interaction rather than leaving the item open.

**Preparation panic in phase 3.** Seventy-five minutes, an unfamiliar genre and an assessment. The enumerated structure is the countermeasure and it works by removing a decision — no student should be spending time deciding what to produce. Circulate, answer factual questions, keep people moving.

**The defence is experienced as an interrogation.** Twelve minutes, five questions, two assessors and a decision-owner is an intense format in the second quarter, and this is the more consequential of the day's two affective risks. Three things work against it: the questions are published, the decision-owner asks the fourth as a genuine request rather than as a test, and the firewalled clinic follows immediately and is opened by the decision-owner's *own* failure — which is the strongest available signal that the defence was not an examination of the student's worth. *Residual risk the design accepts:* §5 records that whether twelve minutes with three people is too much for week twenty is the design's principal open question about this day.

## Assessment

**Assessment-bearing, no entrustment judgement** — per the [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md), the first is made in Q3.

Two outputs:

1. **The module artefact** — the accumulated S016 to S019 specification items — pass/fail against specification, tokens available.
2. **The capacity argument and its defence** — a specification decision on whether the four parts of point 1 are present and whether the student can defend them under questioning.

Checked elements: the claim as a number with a unit and a condition; the model; the binding constraint with its margin and its kind; the breaking assumption with direction and magnitude; the first-hour measurement with its lead time and observable window; the confidence statement; the prompt record; the intervention record; the frontier statement; **the origin attribution across all six required outputs**.

**The weighted element is step 4.** An argument with a correct number and no identified breaking assumption does not pass. An argument with a wrong number and a well-identified assumption does.

**Failure conditions, named:** a claim that is not a number with a unit and a condition; no binding constraint identified; no breaking assumption identified; a confidence statement that misrepresents the evidence; **an intervention record with no entries where the artefact does not demonstrate that its own checks could have detected an intervention-worthy error.**

**No carry-forward.** Levels are formed from the artefact by two assessors before the defence, per rater discipline rule 7, and defence movement is recorded.

**Dispositions observed:** integrity, in whether the confidence statement at step 6 matches the actual state of the evidence.

Nothing else is assessed.

## What this day does not establish

**Demonstrated.** Nothing about systems — the day introduces no content. What it produces is evidence about students: whether a claim is a number with a unit and a condition, whether a constraint has been classified as a quota limit, a capacity limit or a shared-pool limit, whether the named assumption is load-bearing, and whether the student can hold all of it under questioning from someone who will act on it.

**Read rather than demonstrated.** That quota is a policy limit with no associated cost; that provisioned capacity in a region changes through the day; and the prices and lead times of the four options. All are documentation and current pricing, read on the day.

**Not established here, deliberately.** Whether any student's claim was *right*. Both series — the capacity claim and the frontier prediction — are recorded on this day and resolved in Q3, and the day is honest that it is closing neither.

**Asserted.** That weighting the breaking assumption above the number produces a better engineer. The argument for it is given and it is the design's own reasoning, not a finding. And the weighted element is the one an agent supplies most fluently: asked what assumption would most change an answer, an agent names one, plausibly, every time, and **accepting it leaves no trace anywhere**, because the intervention record only records corrections. The origin attribution is the answer, and [WP-020](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-020.md) records immediately that it is self-reported, unverifiable, and that a student who claims everything cannot be contradicted — residue the design accepts rather than a problem it has solved.

**What rests on a `research/` note.** The capacity-argument structure and the binding-constraint concept, at [research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md); the assessment posture students were subject to today, at [research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md). **[WP-020](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-020.md) is candid that the first of these is thin:** the technical notes behind the structure are about index design and inference scaling rather than about capacity arguments, what they support is the existence of binding constraints rather than the shape of an argument about them, and one of them records that its full text was never retrieved. ## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **A capacity argument has four parts — a model, a binding constraint, a margin and the assumption that would break it — and the assumption outlives the number.** Permission to hold a resource is not the resource: you can hold an allocation for capacity that is not there, and every remedy has a lead time.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** The capacity-argument structure and the binding-constraint concept are verified in this repository at [research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md); the assessment posture you were subject to today follows [research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md). **Corrected 2026-07-30.** This sentence previously also cited [research/05-fde-craft/palantir-fde-practitioner-writing.md](/research/05-fde-craft/palantir-fde-practitioner-writing.md) for the capacity-argument structure, and the full reading of that source completed on that date does not support it: there is no capacity argument in it. **What it does support is narrower and is now carried at phase 5 point 4** — that a field engineer's stated success measure is impact on the customer's goal, that no measure is stated for the product-engineering role, and that the field role sits organisationally in business development. All three are the company's own words about its own model in recruiting material that **reports no quantity and measures no outcome**, so they license a framing and nothing else. Optimism bias in capacity estimation is cited for **direction and mechanism only, and no magnitude is asserted.** The documentation states prices, bounds and billing rules and **states no probability that a capacity request succeeds**; none is claimed here.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- Lead with the assumption that would make you wrong, and give it a direction and a rough size.
- Classify the constraint before recommending anything: a quota limit, a capacity limit, or what the shared pool will sustain.
- Name the first-hour signal, its lead time, and the window it is observable in.
- **Provenance is not correctness.** You can say exactly where your breaking assumption came from and still not know that it binds. That is why the artefact attributes each output's origin, in the sense the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md) uses, and why the third question was asked about an output you attributed.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Stopping at the first limit you can find a number for. A configured allocation is the easiest constraint to quote and often the easiest to raise, and an argument that ends there has not reached the constraint.
