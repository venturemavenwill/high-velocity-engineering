# Seminar Day Design Pattern

> The template every one of the ninety seminar days instantiates. Each phase carries a named instructional warrant traceable to [Learning Science Foundations](/archive/bsc-programme/program/02-Learning-Science-Foundations.md). A seminar day that departs from this pattern must say so and justify the departure in its whitepaper.

## Shape

A **six-hour day**, one per teaching week, non-recorded, attendance-required. Four concurrent courses converge here; the seminar day is where the strands interlock rather than run in parallel. Roughly 320 minutes of scheduled activity plus breaks.

The ordering is deliberately unconventional in one respect: **the students attempt before they are taught.** Phases 1, 3 and 4 implement problem-first sequencing on the productive-failure and pretesting evidence. Anyone reading the schedule expecting instruction to precede practice is reading the wrong design.

## The eight phases

| # | Phase | Minutes | Primary warrant |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pretest and prediction | 25 | Pretesting effect; generation; calibration |
| 2 | Cumulative retrieval set | 20 | Testing effect; distributed practice; interleaving |
| 3 | Contrasting cases and generation | 45 | Productive failure; preparation for future learning |
| 4 | Consolidating instruction | 50 | Cognitive load; worked examples with faded guidance |
| 5 | Whole-task learning task | 90 | Four-component design; variability of practice |
| 6 | Error clinic | 40 | Error management training; hypercorrection; psychological safety |
| 7 | Peer articulation | 30 | ICAP interactive tier; self-explanation |
| 8 | Calibration and commitment | 20 | Metacognitive calibration; spacing schedule |

---

### Phase 1 — Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Students attempt six to eight items on material **not yet taught that day**, and attach a confidence rating to each. Scores are never recorded and never contribute to assessment.

- **Function.** Generates the errors that phase 4 will correct, activates and differentiates whatever prior knowledge exists, and produces confidence data that phase 8 will use for calibration.
- **Design constraints.** Items must admit a plausible-but-wrong answer, or the generation mechanism has nothing to work on. At least two items must be drawn from the day's genuinely hard distinction, not its easy definitions.
- **Failure mode.** If students experience this as a test, participation collapses. It is framed explicitly and repeatedly as instrumentation, and staff take the pretest visibly in week one and get items wrong on purpose.

### Phase 2 — Cumulative retrieval set (20 min)

Closed-book retrieval drawn from **earlier seminar days across the whole programme**, not from last week. Feedback is given immediately. Format is varied within the set — recall, application, explanation, critique — rather than standardised.

- **Function.** The primary retention mechanism of the programme. Because gaps expand and sources interleave, this phase is where the spacing schedule actually executes.
- **Design constraints.** Every seminar day plan names its source days explicitly. Items last seen more than two quarters previously carry a scaffolded prompt, because retrieval that fails completely provides little benefit.
- **Failure mode.** Drift toward recency. Any retrieval set drawn predominantly from the preceding fortnight has silently become review and should be rebuilt.

### Phase 3 — Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

Small groups are given two to four cases that differ along the dimension the day is about, and are asked to invent an account, a metric, a procedure or a design that distinguishes them. **They are not told the target concept and they are expected to fail to reach it.**

- **Function.** Productive failure. The struggle differentiates prior knowledge and makes the subsequent instruction interpretable. Contrasting cases make the relevant dimension noticeable without naming it.
- **Design constraints.** Cases must vary on the target dimension and be matched on others, or students will discriminate on the wrong feature. Groups produce an artefact — a written criterion, a sketched mechanism — because unrecorded discussion leaves phase 4 nothing to build on.
- **Failure mode.** This phase fails silently if the instructor rescues the group. Staff answer process questions and refuse content questions.

### Phase 4 — Consolidating instruction (50 min)

The only expository phase. It builds directly on the artefacts produced in phase 3, naming what students groped toward, contrasting their generated accounts with the canonical one, and working through examples with guidance faded across the sequence.

- **Function.** Consolidation. Productive failure without this phase is simply failure.
- **Design constraints.** Worked example first, then completion problem, then unguided problem, for any content with high element interactivity. Representations integrated, not split. Anything students must reason with is available in writing rather than spoken, because transient information cannot be re-inspected. Guidance is withdrawn as competence develops rather than accumulated, per expertise reversal.
- **Failure mode.** Reverting to a prepared lecture that ignores what phase 3 produced. The phase is partly unscriptable by design and this is a real staffing cost.

### Phase 5 — Whole-task learning task (90 min)

One task on the live substrate, whole rather than decomposed, at the complexity class appropriate to the module. Supportive information is provided in advance; procedural information is provided **just in time** at the moment of use.

- **Function.** Integration. Constituent skills taught separately do not integrate spontaneously; the whole task is where the strands are actually exercised together.
- **Design constraints.** Task class complexity increases across the eighteen modules. Variability within a class is mandatory: no two tasks in a class share surface structure. Real system, real data, real consequence. Simplification of early classes must preserve the structure of the whole task, because an over-simplified whole task teaches a schema that must later be unlearned.
- **Failure mode.** The task becomes an exercise with a known answer. If staff know in advance what every group will produce, the complexity class is set too low.

### Phase 6 — Error clinic (40 min)

Errors from phase 5, from the substrate's recent incident log, and from staff's own work are surfaced and analysed. Explicitly blameless by published protocol. Staff bring their own errors and are expected to.

- **Function.** Error management. The evidence that error-encouraging training transfers better than error-avoidant training is concentrated exactly in tasks unlike those practised — which is the condition this programme is designed for.
- **Design constraints.** High-confidence errors from phase 1 are prioritised, because confidently held errors are corrected especially reliably once feedback arrives. The distinction between error in exploration, which is encouraged, and error in verification, which is not, is stated every time.
- **Failure mode.** If any error surfaced here ever appears in an assessment judgement about the student who surfaced it, the phase is dead for the rest of the programme. This is a hard firewall.

### Phase 7 — Peer articulation (30 min)

Structured pairs or triads in which **every participant must produce**, not merely agree. Typical structures: independent commitment, then exchange, then joint reconciliation; or explain-to-a-partner-who-must-find-the-flaw.

- **Function.** Interactive constructive engagement, the highest tier of the ICAP ordering, plus self-explanation. Also supplies the vicarious mastery experience that self-efficacy formation depends on.
- **Design constraints.** Unstructured pair work degrades into one partner explaining and one receiving, which is passive for the receiver. The structure must force bilateral production. Near-peer participation from later cohorts is used where the substrate rota allows.
- **Failure mode.** Consensus-seeking. Reconciliation must require identifying where the two accounts differ, not agreeing that they broadly match.

### Phase 8 — Calibration and commitment (20 min)

Students revisit their phase 1 confidence ratings against their current understanding, predict their performance on this material at a stated future date, and record the prediction. Predictions are confronted when that date arrives in a later phase 2.

- **Function.** Directly attacks the fluency-based metacognitive illusion. Students who do not hold a model of why the design feels hard will optimise their private study toward re-reading.
- **Design constraints.** Predictions must be for a delayed test, not an immediate one, because it is precisely at delay that judgements are miscalibrated. The confrontation must happen, or the phase is theatre.
- **Failure mode.** Confrontation with miscalibration damages self-efficacy if the psychological-safety conditions are not already established. The two mechanisms are designed together and this phase is withheld in week one.

## The between-day protocol

The seminar day is not self-contained. Three things run between days and are part of the design.

- **Expanding-interval retrieval.** Short retrieval sets at expanding gaps, sourced programme-wide, delivered asynchronously. These populate the following week's phase 2.
- **Interleaved problem sets.** Assembled across the four concurrent courses, not blocked by course, so that strategy selection is exercised rather than execution alone.
- **A sleep-spanning gap between phase 5 and its written reflection.** Reflection on the learning task is due no earlier than the following day.

## Complexity classes

The eighteen modules span six complexity classes. A class is defined by what the student must decide, not by what they must implement.

| Class | Student decides | Modules |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nothing; the task is specified and the method is given | M01–M03 |
| 2 | Method, given a specified task | M04–M06 |
| 3 | Method and success criterion | M07–M09 |
| 4 | Problem formulation, from an ambiguous brief | M10–M12 |
| 5 | Whether to proceed at all, under uncertainty and cost | M13–M15 |
| 6 | What to hand over, to whom, with what warranty | M16–M18 |

## Adaptations that are permitted

A whitepaper may justify departure from the pattern in these ways, and must name which:

- **Blocked rather than interleaved practice** for a constituent skill that genuinely requires automaticity.
- **Instruction before generation** where the content has no plausible-but-wrong answer space, or where the cohort's prior knowledge makes phase 3 redundant under expertise reversal.
- **Extended phase 5** to 140 minutes with phases 3 and 4 compressed, for integrative days at the end of a module.
- **Assessment-bearing days**, where phases 5 through 7 are replaced by an entrustment occasion. These are named in the [Seminar Day Index](/archive/bsc-programme/program/08-Seminar-Day-Index.md).

## Every day is executed, not read

This pattern describes the shape of a day. The [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) describes how it is run, and it binds every day in the programme. The [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md) describes how the instrument running it is configured, and adds three further requirements to every day: **the day states its configuration or declares that it runs unconfigured**; **every phase 5 artefact classifies its own conclusions into the five classes**, with a conclusion in no class failing; and **any day using an external configuration corpus pins the revision**, host and model it observed. Three rather than six, deliberately — a day carrying nine additional requirements satisfies none of them well.

In summary: each day names a **platform anchor** and the **MCP servers in use** in its header block; phase 1 carries a sealed prediction about what GitHub Copilot will do and where it will be wrong; phase 3's four contrasting cases are **executed against a real service** wherever the subject permits; phase 4 carries at least one perishable platform-specific point and at least one point naming a **default the platform chose for you**; **phase 5 produces a build**, whose artefact carries a prompt record, an intervention record and a frontier statement; and phase 6's error clinic includes a case where the agent was confidently wrong.

**A day with no platform anchor and no build does not exist in this programme.** The pedagogy below is unchanged by this — the eight phases, the fading, the warrants and the retrieval schedule are all as specified. What changed is that the whole-task learning task is now a whole task.

## What every day hands the student

**Every seminar day closes, before its Related pages, with a section headed `## What to take from this day`.** It is the only part of a day's page written *to the student* rather than to the person evaluating the design, and it exists because the pattern above had a gap that was invisible while the design was being written: **a day could satisfy all eight phases, all nine instrumentation rules and the whole assessment specification, and still never state in one place what a student should walk out holding.** The material was present and distributed across a warrant, a perishability register and a specification, which is the correct place for each of them and the wrong place for a reader who has just spent 320 minutes in the room.

Four parts, fixed, so that a student can compare across days and a designer cannot substitute enthusiasm for content.

1. **The claim to hold.** The day's durable claim in the student's own operating terms — what they should be able to *state and act on*, not what was covered. It is drawn from the left-hand column of the day's perishability register, which already carries it.
2. **What the sources license, and what they do not.** What the cited material actually supports, at the verification depth the research note records. **Direction and mechanism only where the note is a synthesis or an abstract**, and the section says so rather than leaving the student to infer it.
3. **Guidance for your own work.** Concrete, checkable actions that survive the room. **At least one is inferred from the agent configuration corpus** — the five classes of conclusion, control-is-not-evidence, provenance-is-not-correctness, advisory-versus-authoritative, untrusted-content-is-data, approval-as-a-stop-condition, or pin-the-revision — chosen because it fits the day. **Where none fits, the section says none fits.** A guidance item forced onto a day it does not belong to teaches that the frame is decorative.
4. **Where this is easy to get wrong.** The specific failure the day's own material predicts, named so the student can watch for it in themselves.

**It is not assessed and it may not introduce anything.** Every claim in it already appears elsewhere on the day or in a cited note; the section is a projection over the day, in the sense [concepts/projections.md](/concepts/projections.md) uses, and rule 1 there binds it — **a takeaway carrying a fact no phase carries is an invention, and is a defect.** Nothing in it is assessable content, because assessable content is specified in the day's Assessment section and duplicating it in a friendlier register would create two specifications that can disagree.

**Provenance.** Added 2026-07-30, at the same revision that added the [Agent Configuration Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). The two are connected: the corpus that standard is built on is a large, careful control system that never states, in one place, what its user should conclude — and reading ninety seminar days to check whether this design had the same shape found that it did. **The finding is recorded rather than quietly repaired**, because the design's response to noticing a defect in someone else's artefact was to look for it in its own, and that sequence is worth more than the section.

## Instrumentation rules

Nine rules govern how a day measures its own students, all added after the same defects recurred across unrelated days.

**1. Do not use self-report where an external observer is already present.** If a person capable of supplying the observation is in the room for other reasons — a partner, a paired student, a real user, a decision-owner — the measurement is theirs to make. Identified at [WP-024](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-024.md) §8, where a real requester could have read the reply and a peer was used instead, and again at [WP-037](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-037.md) §8, where a student self-counted a behaviour their own partner was positioned to count. In both cases the external observer cost nothing and was not used.

**2. A protocol reused across days stops being a measurement.** Where the same elicitation is run at three or more days, students learn the instrument and their responses measure familiarity with it as much as the quality being probed. Such protocols remain valid as interventions and their rates must not be compared across occasions. Identified at [WP-034](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-034.md) §8, which retracted a comparison [WP-031](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-031.md) §7 had drawn between the S028 and S031 document-only rates.

**3. A day must state how its own evidence was sampled.** Where a day supplies cases, selects sessions, chooses which artefact is examined, or bounds who is observed, the sampling decision must be disclosed to students in the same terms the day would demand of them. Identified at [WP-038](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-038.md) §8 as the third instance in one module: [S036](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S036.md)'s curated request set presented without its construction, [S037](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S037.md)'s staff-scoped observation sessions, and [S038](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S038.md)'s six-minute narration sampling the common case. In each the design ran a selection effect it teaches students to detect, and in each disclosure would have cost nothing.

**4. An instrument must state what its result changes.** Every measurement a day takes of its own cohort must specify, in the day's design, what follows from a bad result — which day carries the response, and what that day does differently. A measurement with no branch is an observation the programme has committed to making and not to acting on. Added after [WP-043](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-043.md) §8 found the module's second instance in three days — [S041](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S041.md)'s unverified cross-course deferral and [S043](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S043.md)'s unbranched transfer test — and named the underlying habit: **this design measures more readily than it responds.** That is a better failing than the reverse and is still a failing.

**5. A remedy found for one day applies wherever the same failure recurs.** Where a whitepaper identifies a countermeasure for a design problem, that countermeasure is part of the design's repertoire and must be applied at every subsequent day exhibiting the same problem — not rediscovered. Added after [WP-046](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-046.md) §8 recorded the fourth observation of a remedy discovered in one place and not carried across: [S038](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S038.md)'s expert-politeness countermeasure was not applied to [S046](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S046.md)'s structurally identical public recognition check.

**Amended at [WP-052](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-052.md) §8, which distinguishes a validated remedy from a proposed one.** A **validated** remedy has run at least once and its outcome has been reported in the proposing day's whitepaper; only validated remedies enter the repertoire and bind subsequent days. A **proposed** remedy is a structure the design has invented and not yet run: it is recorded, it is used at the day proposing it, and it enters the repertoire only once that day's outcome is reported. A whitepaper introducing a new countermeasure must state in §7 which of the two it is. The amendment followed the rule's second distinct failure mode in five days — [WP-048](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 recorded careless import, where a clause shape was carried across without checking that its factual premise transferred; WP-052 §8 recorded **premature export**, where an untested structure was pre-registered for reuse on the strength of its author's confidence. In the same period the rule produced one clear success, [S052](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S052.md)'s step 6, which carried WP-048's own correction forward.

**Amended again at [WP-054](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8, which found the previous amendment unsatisfiable at design time.** No day in this programme has run, so no remedy can move from proposed to validated while the design is being written, and the rule as amended would have forbidden every reuse of every new structure for the remainder of the programme. **At design time a proposed remedy may be reused, provided each reuse is recorded as contingent on the proposing day's reported outcome, and a single reported failure retracts every contingent reuse at once.** The retraction clause is what makes the concession safe: it prevents the design accumulating dependents on an untested structure until removing it becomes expensive, which is the ordinary route by which an unvalidated pattern becomes load-bearing. WP-054 §8 also records, without acting on it, that the amendment being applied was itself an untested design change reused immediately — **the design's rule-making is subject to the same defect its rules address, one level up.**

This rule is distinct from the [compliance register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), which tracks violations of written rules. A remedy is not a rule; it is a solution the design already owns and keeps failing to reuse. **The two findings are the same shape from opposite directions — rules propagate to where they were discovered rather than where they generalise, and remedies do not propagate at all.**

**6. Where an instrument's response is deferred, the deferral must name the occasion.** Rule 4 requires an instrument to state what its result changes. It is satisfiable by naming a change that occurs at the same occasion, and every instrument in the programme did exactly that — which is why the deferred case went uncovered. Where the honest response to a measurement is *later* rather than *now*, the day must name the later day, and that day must carry an artefact element that consumes the result. Added after [WP-050](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §8 found that both of its concessions were the same concession — [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md)'s entrustment level with no comparison against [S070](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S070.md)'s real audience, and its category-3 prediction check with no later occasion requiring the student to have used it. Those were the fourth and fifth instances of the habit rule 4 named, and the first two in which the missing response was specifically a forward link.

The rule applies from [S051](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S051.md) forward. Earlier days are not retrospectively amended; their whitepapers retain the concessions, so that the failure remains legible in the same way the [Whitepaper Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s scope-rule widenings do.

**7. A claim the day expects students to resist must be staged, not asserted.** Where a phase 4 point (a) contradicts a belief the day's own phase 1 or entry-state analysis records students as holding, and (b) has a named engineering consequence, that point must be reachable from an artefact the day's phase 3 or phase 5 produces — or the whitepaper must name the staging that would exercise it and record its absence. **The rule does not require every phase 4 point to be demonstrated;** most consolidate what phase 3 produced, which is what phase 4 is for. It binds only where the design has itself predicted the resistance and then declined to meet it. Opened at [WP-049](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-049.md) §8 at one instance, recorded at two by [WP-053](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §8, and elevated after three whitepapers independently reached the third instance — [WP-057](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-057.md) §8 (single-vector capacity), [WP-064](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 (routing an ask to the right party), [WP-067](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-067.md) §8. In every instance the staging was available and cheap, and in every instance the asserted claim was the one the room did not already hold. **The mechanism is that a designer builds contrasting cases for the claims they expect an argument about, and writes the settled-feeling claims into phase 4 — and the settled-feeling claims are frequently the ones the folk model survives.**

**8. An instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.** Where a day's instrument is administered inside or after phase 4, the day must either administer it before phase 4 as well and report the movement, or state in its whitepaper that the instrument measures retention rather than reasoning. Opened at [WP-054](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 with its rule form already stated, recorded at two by [WP-062](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-062.md) §8 and [WP-066](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-066.md) §8, and elevated at the third instance reached independently by [WP-074](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-074.md) §8 and [WP-086](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-086.md) §8. The pattern is specifically a **phase 5** failure: the pretest structure exists to prevent it and works, but phase 5 elements are written as learning-task steps and are only claimed as measurements later, in the whitepaper's §7.

**9. An instrument must name the party who will read its result.** Every measurement a day takes must name a reader, and the programme must publish, before each offering, the subset of instruments it will actually analyse that year. **A design that instruments more than it can read has produced a literature, not a measurement.** Added at [WP-090](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-090.md) §8, the closing whitepaper's principal argument, whose §9 prediction 2 forecasts that fewer than one in ten of the programme's own §9 predictions will ever be measured. The rule is the design's last and least comfortable finding about itself.

**On the numbering.** Rules 7, 8 and 9 were each proposed as "rule 7" by different whitepapers written in parallel, and two of the three reached their third instance in the same week. The collision is recorded rather than tidied away: it is direct evidence for rule 9's claim that this design generates findings faster than it can integrate them, and it is the reason rule 9 exists.

**A rule-5 checklist is maintained alongside the compliance register**, listing each remedy in the repertoire with the failure it addresses, to be consulted at §5 of every whitepaper. Added after [WP-072](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-072.md) §8 found rule 5 violated on two consecutive days, both times discovered only while writing §8 — on the ground that **rule 5 is the only instrumentation rule with no checkable trigger.**

None of the nine rules constrains what a day teaches. All nine constrain what it may claim to have measured, what it may claim to have demonstrated, what it must disclose, what it must do about what it finds, when it must do it, who must read it, and what it must reuse.
