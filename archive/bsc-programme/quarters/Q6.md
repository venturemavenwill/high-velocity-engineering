## The quarter's theme

Q6 is the quarter most students think they came for, and it is deliberately placed sixth.

The reason is stated plainly in [Q5](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q5.md): a student who cannot evaluate cannot learn anything durable about language models, because almost every claim made about them is an evaluation claim and almost none of them travel. A student arriving here from Q5 knows that a benchmark is a criterion somebody constructed, that a model-as-judge has a systematic error component, and that an interval without its dependency structure is not a result. **Those three facts are what make this quarter teachable.**

M11 builds the mechanism: what a representation is, what attention computes, how a transformer is assembled from parts the student has already met, what preference optimisation actually selects for, and — the module's most important day — what these systems cannot do.

M12 builds the application the substrate needs: retrieval. It begins by requiring students to beat a lexical baseline they will initially assume is obsolete, and ends with a retrieval system in production serving real users.

**The quarter's stance:** these are instruments with characterisable behaviour, not oracles and not toys. The engineering question is never *is it good* but *what does it do, on my distribution, measured how, against what baseline, at what cost.*

## The quarter's platform

Every day in Q6 is executed against two services, and the pairing is the quarter's argument in operational form.

**The Foundry model catalogue** is where a model stops being a name and becomes a deployment with a SKU, a billing model, a region and a set of things it will not do. The catalogue carries hundreds of models across providers including Azure OpenAI, Mistral, Meta, Cohere, NVIDIA and Hugging Face, and it carries a **model leaderboard (preview)** with six benchmark categories — quality, safety, performance, cost, scenario, and quality of embedding models. Students use it from S051 and it is the quarter's standing supply of other people's evaluation claims.

**Those leaderboard figures are vendor-published benchmark results and are not this programme's claims.** They are cited only with the caveat the documentation itself gives: *public benchmarks use standardized datasets and might not reflect performance on your specific data.* The documentation additionally records that benchmark data is not available for all models, that quality datasets can become saturated as models are trained on similar data, that results vary with prompt construction and the number of few-shot examples, and that performance figures come from synthetic workloads on single-region deployments. **A quarter that teaches benchmark scepticism and then quotes a leaderboard uncritically would be teaching the opposite of its content**, so the leaderboard is treated here as a worked example of the thing S059 exists to dismantle.

**Azure AI Search** is where retrieval stops being a similarity function and becomes an index with a schema, an algorithm, a ranker and a set of defaults nobody chose. Vector, hybrid and semantic ranking are all operable from week one of M12, and M11 uses the same index as its measuring apparatus.

The toolchain is the programme's standing one, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md): **GitHub Copilot in agent mode, through named MCP servers, against named services.** Every day in this quarter produces a build.

**This quarter's platform layer is the most perishable in the programme.** M11's anchors in particular — model leaderboards, reasoning summaries, preference fine-tuning — are preview or near-preview capabilities whose surface has moved inside the writing of these pages. Every day separates its durable claim from its platform instance and says which is assessed.

## Why the class does not rise

Q5 reached class 4 and Q6 stays there. This is the only quarter in the programme that does not advance a class, and the decision is deliberate.

Class 5 requires the student to **own a system across time** — to make decisions whose consequences arrive weeks later and to be answerable for them. Q7's serving material supplies that naturally, because a capacity decision made in week 61 is a bill in week 68.

Holding class 4 through Q6 buys something the programme needs: **the entire language-model body of knowledge is met at a class the student already occupies.** A student learning attention, preference optimisation, dense retrieval and approximate search simultaneously with a new autonomy demand would learn neither. The design would rather spend a quarter consolidating a class than advance on schedule.

## Substrate role

Q6 is where the substrate acquires the capability its users have been asking for.

- **S051 to S053** work on representations and models over the substrate's own text, not a public corpus, indexed in a real Azure AI Search index the students build.
- **S054** examines the preference data the substrate's own feedback mechanism has been collecting since Q3, which is the first time students learn what that mechanism was for, and expresses it in the Foundry preference fine-tuning format.
- **S055** produces a written statement of the substrate's language capability's limits, which is published to users.
- **S056 to S059** build and evaluate retrieval against the substrate's real query log, with the S049 dependency structure argued.
- **S060** ships it, to all users, with an entrustment judgement on the decision.

**A disclosure obligation applies from S055 onward,** per [Substrate and Infrastructure](/archive/bsc-programme/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md): users are told what the language capability is, who built it, what it is known to do badly, and how to reach a human. From S055 the statement additionally names the model deployment and deployment type the feature runs on, because a limit is a joint fact about a model and a configuration and the configuration is the part that changes without anybody noticing. **Every subsequent Q6 day inherits the obligation**, and S060's release carries it into production.

## Entrustment occasions

**One**, which is the fewest of any quarter after Q1.

- **S060** — *ship a retrieval system into production and own the consequence.* Precursor to EA-1. Expected 2b–3a.

The reduction is intentional. Q5 carried two occasions and Q7 carries two; Q6 is the quarter in which the largest volume of new technical material arrives, and the design declines to assess entrustment while students are at their least secure. **Specification items continue at every day.**

## What Q6 must achieve for Q7 to be possible

1. **A mechanical account of a transformer.** Q7's serving material is about where time and memory go, and that question is meaningless to a student who holds the architecture as a black box.
2. **A characterised retrieval system in production.** S061 to S064 all profile, batch and budget a real system, and it must be the students' own.
3. **A published limits statement.** S066's release material and S070's release decision both assume a student who has written one and had users read it.
4. **The benchmark trap, felt.** S059 is the direct precursor of every claim Q7 and Q8 ask students to evaluate about someone else's system.
5. **A running index and a running deployment they can bill.** Q7 opens on cost and capacity, and the arithmetic is not teachable against a hypothetical. By S060 every student owns an Azure AI Search index and at least one Foundry model deployment whose deployment type they chose and can defend.

## Perishability

**The M11 platform layer is the most perishable content in this programme**, and the quarter says so on every one of its first five days.

Model leaderboards are preview. Reasoning summaries are a documented feature whose generation is explicitly not guaranteed. Preference fine-tuning is preview and is available on a named and moving list of models. Vector search parameters that the days rely on — recall windows, thresholds, filter overrides — include preview parameters on preview API versions. None of that weakens the durable claims, and all of it means the day's instance will need re-verifying before it is next taught.

The durable claims are: that a notion of similarity is constructed and recoverable; that weight is not influence; that which component is load-bearing is a question about a configured system rather than an architecture; that optimisation selects for what the signal rewards; and that a limit claim has a kind. **None of those is a claim about a vendor.** Each day's perishability register separates them from the instance, and the specification assesses the durable half.
