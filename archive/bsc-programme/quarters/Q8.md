## The quarter's theme

[Q7](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q7.md) ended with a release gate that had refused something. Q8 is the quarter in which the thing on the other side of the gate stops answering and starts acting.

That is a single change and it is not a small one. Everything the programme has built for eight quarters was built to evaluate an **answer**: a criterion for it, a judge of it, an interval around it, a gate in front of it, a statement of what it does not license. An answer that is wrong costs the reader their time, and the reader can decline it. **An action that is wrong changes state, and the state does not change back when somebody notices.** Every apparatus the programme owns has to be re-derived against that difference, and M15's five days are the re-derivation.

M15 supplies the loop. Agency is not a property of a component; it is a property of what consumes the component's output, and a system that only answers has already acted if something downstream cannot decline. Orchestration is control flow whose branch conditions are computed by a component with no obligation to be consistent, which makes the path set — not the prompt — the failure surface. A tool boundary is a caller contract whose caller does not read documentation. **Tool failure has four shapes, and which of them are silent follows from who is positioned to observe rather than from the class label.** And a trajectory is a routing record that reads as an explanation, which is the most convincing wrong artefact the programme has taught.

M16 supplies the adversary and the accident, and observes that the detection problem is frequently the same one.

**The quarter's stance:** a limits statement written for a system that answers is a routing aid; the same statement for a system that acts is a boundary, and a boundary whose only enforcement is the sentence is a routing aid with a firmer tone.

## The practical turn, and the recursion nobody should hide

Every previous quarter has asked students to operate an agent. **This one asks them to build one**, and the two activities are the same activity seen from opposite ends of the same interface.

That is not a rhetorical flourish; it has three operational consequences and the quarter states all three to students at [S071](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S071.md).

**The [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s apparatus becomes recursive.** The prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement are, from week 71, artefacts about a system that resembles the one producing them. A student writing *the agent selected the wrong tool and I corrected it* is writing an intervention record and a selection-failure instance in the same sentence, and the design would rather they noticed that than have it arrive as an unremarked coincidence. **The intervention record is the human-in-the-loop control the students are simultaneously being taught to distrust**, and the honest position is that it is subject to exactly the [S071](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S071.md) point 8 test the day applies to everything else: a review whose decline rate is indistinguishable from zero is an audit trail.

**The students' own tool is one of the four cases.** GitHub Copilot in agent mode selects tools, supplies arguments, executes them and uses what comes back. All four shapes occur in it, the students have two years of experience of that, and [S074](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S074.md) is where the experience becomes a decomposition rather than an anecdote. The [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) names this day for exactly that reason.

**And the recursion has a limit, which is stated rather than elided.** Building an agent does not make a student a better judge of the agent that built it, and the programme has no instrument that would show otherwise. The frontier map accumulated across the quarter is a sample of one cohort on one substrate, per instrumentation rule 3, and it describes these tasks rather than the tool.

## What holds at class 5, and what advances to class 6

**M15 holds class 5**, which is the third module to do so after [M13](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M13.md) and [M14](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M14.md). At class 5 the student decides whether to proceed at all, under uncertainty and cost, and M15's version of that decision is **whether to let the system act** — which is the same decision with a new failure surface and the same latency of feedback. A student who decides at S071 that a class of action may be taken finds out at S074, on the substrate's own trace log, what that permission produced.

The class does not advance inside M15 for a reason worth stating plainly. **Class 6 is about what you hand over, to whom, with what warranty, and M15 has nothing to hand over yet.** The agent capabilities built across S071 to S075 are the students' own, run on a shadow path or behind the [M14](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M14.md) gate, and nobody outside the room depends on them. A module that asked for a warranty on a capability nobody has received would be teaching the form of class 6 without its content.

**M16 advances to class 6**, and the mechanism is the adversary.

- A **safety argument** is a claim made to somebody who will rely on it. [S076](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S076.md) to [S078](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S078.md) produce the evidence and [S087](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S087.md) assembles it, but the disposition — that a claim about your own system's security is a warranty and not an opinion — is acquired here.
- An **incident** has a receiving party by construction. [S080](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S080.md)'s forensics are conducted in front of the people who were involved, and the output is an account somebody else must be able to act on without the author present.
- The **detection problem is shared between attack and accident**, and recognising that is what lets a student write one handover that covers both. [S079](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S079.md) is where the two arrive at the same monitor.

Two consequences follow and both are structural.

**The artefact must survive its author's absence.** From S076 onward every assessed artefact is written for a reader who will not be able to ask a question. This is the class-6 acquisition and it is why M16's specifications check comprehensibility to a named non-author rather than completeness against a checklist.

**A prior level cannot be carried forward.** [S080](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S080.md) judges an occasion. A student who was 3a at [S070](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S070.md) has established nothing about conduct in a room containing the people whose system broke.

## Substrate role

Q8 is the quarter in which the substrate acquires the ability to act, and the sequencing of that acquisition is itself taught.

- **The substrate cannot take any action until week 71 ends.** [S071](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S071.md) converts each student's Q6 limits statement into a boundary before any action capability is enabled, and the ordering is the day's argument rather than an administrative convenience. Students are told that the ordering is deliberate and why.
- **A shadow path runs from S071 to S073.** Actions are computed, recorded and not executed. It exists so that the boundary work has an adjudicator that is not a person's judgement and not a live consequence, and its limits are stated at [WP-071](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-071.md) §8: a shadow path never tries, so it tests what a system proposes and not what a boundary refuses.
- **Each student's capability is built on Foundry Agent Service and is theirs.** The build path is chosen at [S071](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S071.md) and held for the module: a **prompt agent**, authored in the portal or through the SDK and run by Foundry with no application code to maintain, or a **hosted agent**, the student's own container image running on Microsoft-managed infrastructure. Both routes are documented; both are used in the cohort; and the choice is itself assessed content, because it determines what the student can enforce and what they must accept. **The platform mechanics are perishable and are marked at the point of use.**
- **Every hosted agent acquires a dedicated Microsoft Entra agent identity and a dedicated endpoint at deploy time, both created automatically.** Nobody in the room asks for either, and the endpoint is documented as available immediately after deployment, with publishing not required for programmatic access. That is a reachability property the boundary written at [S071](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S071.md) has to account for and which no student would have discovered by reading their own configuration.
- **The first executed agent capability passes through the release gate built at [S066](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S066.md)–[S070](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S070.md), in week 74.** The gate is the only thing standing between an agent and the substrate's users, and [Q7](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q7.md) required it to exist and to have refused something before this quarter began.
- **The substrate's users are told, before week 74, that a system may now take action on their behalf**, what it may and may not do, and how to reach a human — under the disclosure obligation running since [S055](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S055.md), per [Substrate and Infrastructure](/archive/bsc-programme/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md).
- **S076 to S078 attack the students' own capabilities**, on an isolated copy with the real permission model, and the findings are disclosed to the substrate's operators under the responsible-disclosure protocol SG390 later assesses.
- **[S080](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S080.md)'s incident is real and is not scheduled.** The day runs on whatever the substrate has actually produced by week 80, and the design's contingency is stated at [M16](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M16.md): if no incident of adequate severity has occurred, the day runs on an incident from a prior cohort's record with its participants present.

**The withdrawal commitment is live in this quarter.** [Substrate and Infrastructure](/archive/bsc-programme/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) names [S068](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S068.md) as the first test of it. Q8 is the second and harder one: a capability that acts, on real users, whose failures include two shapes that report success.

**What cannot be operated is demonstrated and is not assessed.** Three capabilities this quarter would otherwise use carry a licensing or tenant-administration prerequisite the programme does not hold, and each day states which of its material falls under this. Private MCP connectivity is documented as requiring the standard agent setup with a customer-supplied virtual network, which a course tenant does not have; Purview's data-security policies are documented as requiring Microsoft Entra ID user-context tokens; and continuous evaluation and the agent monitoring dashboard are in preview. **Where a capability is demonstrated rather than operated, the day says so and the specification does not assess it**, per the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](/archive/bsc-programme/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md)'s open question about what a course lab can actually run.

## Entrustment occasions

**One**, at [S080](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S080.md). This returns the quarter to [Q6](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q6.md)'s rate after Q7's two.

- **[S080](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S080.md)** — *conduct a blameless forensic review of a real incident, in front of the people involved, and produce an account a non-participant can act on.* Precursor to EA-2 and touching EA-3. Expected 2b–3a.

**[M15](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M15.md) carries no entrustment occasion, and the reason is stated here rather than left as an omission**, in the form [Q6](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q6.md) used for its own reduction.

An entrustment judgement answers *what would we allow this student to do unsupervised.* The activity M15 would be certifying is **running a system that acts against the substrate**, and M15 ends in week 75. [M16](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M16.md) is where the cohort is shown that the capability they have just built has an attack surface none of them has modelled — a prompt an adversary authored, a poisoned retrieval path, an authorisation check that is not on the retrieval path, and a drift nobody saw. **Certifying agency in week 75 and teaching its threat model in week 77 would be the design committing, in its own assessment schedule, the error the programme spends [S044](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S044.md) and [S087](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S087.md) correcting: a claim about a system made before its adversary was named.**

Two further reasons support the same decision and neither would carry it alone.

- **M15's content is the least durable in the programme.** Orchestration patterns and protocol-mediated interoperability are recorded as semi-durable and per-delivery in the [Durable and Perishable Register](/archive/bsc-programme/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md). An entrustment level is a claim about a person that outlives the material it was formed on, and the design prefers to form it where the material does not move under it. The one M15 item the register marks durable — the four shapes of tool failure — is a decomposition, and a decomposition is assessed by specification, which M15 does at every day.
- **[S080](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S080.md) requires an incident, and an incident requires a system that has been running.** Placing an occasion at S075 would put two entrustment judgements five weeks apart in a quarter whose second one cannot be scheduled at all. The design would rather have one occasion that is real than two of which one is timed for symmetry.

**Specification items continue at every day**, including all five of M15. The absence of an entrustment occasion is not an absence of assessment and is stated to students as such at S071.

## What Q8 must achieve for Q9 to be possible

1. **A capability the students own that acts, with a boundary that has refused something.** [S085](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S085.md) negotiates scope under constraint with a counterparty, and the thing being negotiated is what the system may do. A student whose boundary has never bound has nothing to concede and nothing to defend.
2. **A trajectory evaluation the student built and knows the blind spot of.** [S087](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S087.md)'s safety case is an argument assembled from evidence, and [S088](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S088.md)'s residual-risk statement is what the evidence does not cover. [S075](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S075.md) point 8 — that the evaluation which agrees best with human raters is blind to the two failure shapes that report success — is the single most load-bearing sentence Q9 inherits, and a student who cannot state it will write a safety case out of the wrong evidence.
3. **An incident reviewed blamelessly, with the people involved in the room.** [S089](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S089.md)'s handover warranty and [S090](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S090.md)'s final defence both require a student who has been answerable in front of people who were harmed by a decision and has neither defended nor collapsed. That composure is acquired at [S080](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S080.md) or it is not acquired.
4. **Class 6 entered rather than announced.** [M17](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M17.md) opens at class 6 and does not teach it. The transition happens inside M16, at the point where an artefact stops being written for an assessor and starts being written for somebody who will rely on it without the author present.
