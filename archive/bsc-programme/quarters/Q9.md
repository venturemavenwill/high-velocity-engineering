## The quarter's theme

Q8 ended with a real incident reviewed blamelessly on a system that acts. Q9 is the quarter in which the engineering stops being the subject.

That is a structural claim and the timetable states it before any seminar day does. **Q9 has no `CF`, `SY`, `MS` or `IS` course.** Those four strands complete at Q8 and do not appear again. What remains is a capstone, a decision practicum, a handover course and a safety-case course — four terminal courses, none of which introduces new technical material. A student reading the catalogue can see, without being told, that the programme has stopped teaching them how to build things.

M17 supplies the reason. A deployed system lives inside an organisation, and **the organisation is part of the system**: the rota, the approval step, the person who re-keys a record at seven in the morning, the manager's tolerance for being told bad news. None of it is in the repository and all of it determines the outcome. The failures available from here are failures of adoption, power and scope, and none of them is repaired by being more right.

M18 supplies the exit. What transfers, to whom, with what warranty — and the oral defences that discharge the three entrusted activities.

**The quarter's stance:** the technical work is finished and the engagement is not, and a graduate who cannot tell those two apart will keep improving a system nobody is using.

**This is the only quarter whose four courses are all terminal.** Every other quarter's courses teach something and assess it; these four assess activities the programme has been rehearsing since Q3. **The professional-formation course sits one quarter behind the module that needs it, and this time that is an advantage rather than a defect.** PE301 — change management, stakeholder analysis, risk communication to non-technical decision-makers, negotiating scope under organisational constraint — is [Q8](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q8.md)'s, and it is M17's material exactly. The seminar days of M17 therefore exercise a course the students have completed rather than one running alongside them, which permits the **prior-course** form of the cross-course join: an artefact element requiring the student to name which PE301 material they applied. **SG390 is the fourth and last security course**, and Q9 is the only quarter in which the security strand carries a terminal assessment of its own. The [Module Map](/archive/bsc-programme/program/07-Module-Map.md) names security as the design's thinnest area. That judgement is not repaired here; it is only that the thinness is least visible in the quarter where every remaining course is about consequence.

## The platform, in the only register left

Every quarter before this one put a service in front of the student and asked them to operate it. Q9 does something the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](/archive/bsc-programme/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md) does not have a row for: **it puts the platform in front of somebody else and makes the student answerable for what that person then does about it.**

The distinction is not rhetorical, and [M17](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M17.md) is where it is sharpest. In M17 the platform stops being the thing being built and becomes **the thing being negotiated over**. A quota request, a cost ceiling, an access-control decision that inconveniences a team, a rollout somebody resists — each of those is a service the cohort already knows how to operate, appearing as an organisational fact with a person attached to it.

Four of them are load-bearing in this quarter and all four are documented rather than invented.

- **A quota request is a request to a party with a published priority rule.** Microsoft's own documentation states that quota increase requests are processed in the order received and that **priority goes to customers who actively use their existing allocation, with requests that do not meet the condition liable to be denied**. That is not a technical constraint. It is a grantor's policy, it is readable in advance, and it means the scope a student agrees with one party determines whether a second party is able to say yes.
- **A cost ceiling is an alert and not a cap.** Azure Cost Management budgets notify; the documentation states plainly that **Azure OpenAI does not currently provide the hard-limit functionality that would prevent going over a budget**. The substrate's own monthly ceiling is therefore enforced at the gateway by per-consumer token policies, per [Substrate and Infrastructure](/archive/bsc-programme/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) — which is an engineering decision somebody made because the platform did not make it for them.
- **An access-control action has a named victim.** Purview's data risk assessment offers *Remove sharing link* as a remediation and its own documentation says the action is **to be used sparingly because it could prevent people from accessing the item legitimately**. That is a governance control whose documented cost is somebody else's inconvenience.
- **A governance instrument has a sampling frame.** The default data risk assessment runs weekly over **the top 100 SharePoint sites by usage**, with item-level scanning restricted to ten sites and unsupported on OneDrive. It samples the used. [S082](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S082.md)'s claim that a consultation samples the reachable has a machine instance in this quarter, and the machine's frame is legible where the human one is not.

**M18 inherits the same register and changes its tense.** What transfers, with what warranty, is a claim about an estate the receiving organisation will operate without the student — and a warranty about a platform whose posture tooling publishes no detection rate and whose judge evaluators publish no agreement figure has to say so, which is [SG390](/archive/bsc-programme/program/06-Course-Catalogue.md)'s stated position and [S087](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S087.md)'s and [S088](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S088.md)'s content.

**What this implies for perishability is the reverse of every other quarter.** Q9's platform detail is the most disposable in the programme and its durable content the least. Re-anchoring these ten days to a different vendor would change the examples and nothing else, because the claim is never about the service. What the quarter cannot survive is losing its counterparties: **the scarce resource in Q9 is people who are entitled to refuse**, and no re-anchoring supplies one.

## What completes at class 6

There is no class 7. Q8 entered class 6 at [M16](/archive/bsc-programme/modules/M16.md) and Q9 does not advance; it completes.

Class 6 asks the student to decide **what to hand over, to whom, with what warranty.** [Q8](/archive/bsc-programme/quarters/Q8.md) supplied the first term. M16's adversary and incident work established what a system is actually like when somebody is trying to break it and when nobody is — which is what makes a warranty a claim rather than a formality. Q9 supplies the other two, and it supplies them in the only order that works.

- **To whom** is M17's. It is not answerable from the org chart, because the receiving party is not the party who commissioned the work, is frequently not in the room, and in at least one case has no channel to the room at all.
- **With what warranty** is M18's. A warranty is a statement about a system operated by people who did not build it, under conditions the builder will not observe, and it is defensible only if the preceding four days established who those people are.

**The distinguishing feature of class 6 is not difficulty and it is not latency of feedback.** Class 5's acquisition was answerability across time: a decision made in week 64 met in week 70. Class 6's is different and harder to instrument — **answerability to a party who is not you and does not report to you.** Every earlier class ended with the student holding the consequence. This one ends with the student handing it to somebody else and being answerable for whether they can carry it.

Two consequences follow.

**Scope is an artefact, not an understanding.** A scope agreed in a conversation and not written survives exactly as long as the two people who agreed it. [S085](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S085.md)'s assessed object is a written scope statement with a warranty and a floor, and [WP-050](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §6 named this day as the point where the written form arrives: a statement that outlives the conversation cannot be repaired by the next sentence.

**A prior level cannot be carried forward, and neither can this one.** [S085](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S085.md) and [S090](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S090.md) judge occasions. A student who was 3a at S070 has established nothing about a negotiation with a counterparty who can refuse. The stronger version of the same rule is stated at S085 and is new: **the level awarded at S085 is not evidence about EA-1 or EA-3 either**, because the programme's only measurement of whether constructed-occasion conduct predicts real-occasion conduct is the [S050](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S050.md)-against-[S070](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S070.md) comparison field, and [WP-070](/archive/bsc-programme/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §8 conceded that instrument is too weak to support the inference.

## Substrate role

Q9 is the quarter in which the substrate stops being the cohort's and becomes somebody else's.

- **The organisation around the substrate becomes a taught object.** [S081](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S081.md) requires students to identify, by name and from observation, the people who perform the non-software work the service depends on. Two of those people are present for the last thirty minutes of the learning task and may be asked what they actually do. Per the [Substrate and Infrastructure](/archive/bsc-programme/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) commitment, S081 and S084 are both days on the real-users list and neither degrades to a case study.
- **[S082](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S082.md) puts a finding in front of the staff member whose decision it contradicts**, who is briefed to decline where the merits warrant it. This is the case [S069](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S069.md) point 4 named as unanswered.
- **[S083](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S083.md) puts a live, unresolved risk from the substrate's own drift record in front of a real decision-maker with authority and no technical background, who makes a real decision on the day.**
- **[S084](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S084.md)** measures adoption of a change the cohort shipped, against a real user present for thirty minutes.
- **[S085](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S085.md)** negotiates the scope of the next term's engagement against two real counterparties whose interests differ and who are each entitled to refuse.
- **S086 to S090** transfer the running service to the incoming cohort and to the staff who will hold it between cohorts, and defend the transfer.

**The substrate's own instrumentation is the material of four of those five days**, and the [Copilot Practice Standard](/archive/bsc-programme/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s build requirement is met against it rather than against a disposable environment. The cohort's Cost Management scope, its gateway token metrics, its search index permission model and its Purview assessment results are all live, all readable through the Azure MCP Server, and all belong to somebody. **Every build in M17 is small by design and none of it is the day's deliverable** — the deliverable is a delivered engagement, and the build exists to put a number in front of the person the engagement is with.

**The substrate does not stop at week 90, and that is the quarter's most important property.** Every prior quarter's work ended when the timetable did. This one ends with a service that continues to run for people who are not students, operated by people who were not in the room when it was built — which is the only condition under which a handover can be assessed at all.

## Entrustment occasions

**Two seminar occasions, plus the course-level assessments that are the entrusted activities themselves.**

- **[S085](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S085.md)** — *negotiate scope under constraint with a party who can say no.* Precursor to **EA-3** and to **EA-1**. Expected **3a**.
- **[S090](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S090.md)** — the final defence, programme-wide.
- **ME390** assesses **EA-1** across at least four occasions with at least three distinct assessors. **PE390** assesses **EA-2**. **CAP390** and **SG390** assess **EA-3**.

The seminar occasions and the course occasions are deliberately not the same thing, and the [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s exit targets apply only to the second set: **3b for EA-1 and EA-2, 3a–3b for EA-3.** No seminar day awards an exit level, and no seminar level is an input to one.

**Neither seminar occasion carries a level forward, from Q8 or from each other.** Specification items continue at every day.

## What the programme finishes with

There is no Q10, so this section states what exists at week 90 rather than what the next quarter requires. It is stated in the same form, because the obligations are the same shape: things that must be true, or the award does not mean what the [Programme Charter](/archive/bsc-programme/program/00-Program-Charter.md) says it means.

1. **Three entrustment levels with narratives, and a published statement of what they do not license.** EA-1 from ME390, EA-2 from PE390, EA-3 from PE301, CAP390 and SG390 — each an ordinal level with narrative substantiation from multiple assessors across multiple occasions, and each accompanied by the [Assessment Architecture](/archive/bsc-programme/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s published reliability position. A transcript that reports the levels and omits the reliability position has converted a decision back into a measurement, which is the failure the whole architecture exists to prevent.
2. **A running system with a receiving organisation.** Not a portfolio. The substrate serves real users on week 91 and the students are not operating it, and the handover artefact defended at [S089](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S089.md) and [S090](/archive/bsc-programme/seminars/S090.md) is what makes that true. This is also the [Programme Charter](/archive/bsc-programme/program/00-Program-Charter.md)'s largest single risk arriving at the point where it can no longer be deferred: if the substrate cannot carry a real receiving organisation, EA-2 has been rehearsed and not assessed.
3. **A record of having been wrong in public, with dates.** The calibration confrontations at every phase 8, the long-horizon prediction checks, and the error-clinic series. The record is the student's and is not part of the transcript, by design. **Its function is that a graduate who meets their first professional surprise has a category for it and evidence that they have survived the category before.**
4. **The programme's own instrumentation results, returned to the students who generated them.** Every §9 prediction in ninety whitepapers was tested on this cohort. Commitment 7 of the charter requires the design to be falsifiable; returning the results is what makes the commitment something the cohort can check rather than something the design says about itself.

**What the programme does not finish with**, and says so: a ratified set of entrusted activities, an answer to whether assessor staffing supports the occasion counts the architecture assumes, or evidence that any of this works. All three are open questions in the charter and none is closed by having run.
