# WP-056 — Can This Be Solved Without AI At All

**Seminar day:** [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md) · **Module:** [M12](/wiki/modules/M12.md) · **Quarter:** [Q6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 4
**Platform anchor:** The build-path comparison extended with the option the vendor does not sell — a deterministic system, evaluated on the same terms

## In plain terms

The governance regime this module is built on opens with the right question. **Can this be solved without AI at all?**

It belongs in a responsible-AI module rather than in a procurement one for a reason the regime does not state and this paper does. **Every harm the rest of this module assesses is a harm of inference** — a fabricated citation, a leaked instruction, a drifted behaviour, a confident wrong answer acted on by somebody with no way to tell. A system that does not infer removes the entire class. It is the only intervention in the stage that does, and it is the one nobody costs.

It is asked here, in an assurance stage, for two further reasons. **It needs the system model** — a comparison against an alternative is not writable until you can say what the thing is made of. And this is where the programme's requirements about records converge, because the artefact this session produces is a record rather than a judgement.

**The judgement was made much earlier, at [S008](/wiki/seminars/S008.md).** This session is not the first asking and does not pretend to be. It exists because a judgement with no findable record cannot be revisited, and this particular decision will be revisited: **the alternative you rejected is the first thing anybody reaches for when the system disappoints.**

Four elements do the real work.

**The alternative must be designed from scratch and actually built.** The practice material behind this programme has a rule worth borrowing and inverting — do not automate the existing workflow, ask how you would do this from scratch given the tooling. **The same discipline applies to the thing you are comparing against**, and a non-AI option that is the current process with better forms is a straw figure the author built themselves. In the commonest applied case the alternative is not hypothetical: lexical retrieval is a derived method with decades of evidence behind it, and it can be standing up in an afternoon.

**The comparison set is frozen before either output is seen, and one instrument scores both.** A set written after one system's behaviour is known is a set that system passes. This is the least emotive requirement in the session and the one that most often changes the answer.

**The rejection must name evidence that could have appeared while the decision was still open.** *If the model had not worked* is available only after the money is spent. A rejection no evidence could have reversed is not a rejection, which is [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s claim about engagements applied to technology choice.

**And the comparison the learner was handed does not contain the row they need.** The vendor's build-path guidance is candid, well-structured and explicit about its axes — and every option in it contains a model, because it is a guide to choosing among products rather than a guide to deciding whether to buy one. **Adding the missing row is the session's platform work**, and the durable observation is definitional: a comparison whose every option contains the thing under question cannot answer whether the thing is needed.

One structural fact frames all of it and the paper states it rather than implying it. **The organisation asking this question is usually the organisation selling the answer.**

## Abstract

This paper defends placing the standing first question of a responsible-AI regime inside an assurance stage rather than at the start of an engagement, and defends requiring a fully designed, actually built non-AI alternative evaluated against the same frozen set on the same instrument. The argument runs in four steps. **First**, the judgement is made early and the record is made here, because a record is what makes a decision revisitable when the system disappoints and a comparison is not writable before the system model exists. **Second**, the question is a capability question before it is an ethical one: the planning literature's claim that auto-regressive models cannot plan and cannot self-verify rules out a class of proposals on architectural grounds, which is a cheaper and better-evidenced conversation than a harm argument. **Third**, the alternative is a real competitor rather than a rhetorical one — the derivation of lexical retrieval makes every component of the baseline arguable, and heterogeneous zero-shot evaluation shows the baseline does not collapse on domains it was not built for, which is the property a governance argument actually needs. **Fourth**, most comparisons of two systems are run in a way that could not have produced a different answer, so the session imports the discipline of a frozen set, a stated unit of analysis, and an explicit statement of what difference the comparison could have detected.

The session installs a technology-free problem statement, a from-scratch alternative, the missing row in the vendor's build-path comparison, a cost comparison carrying the categories the source corpus omits entirely, a statement of evidence that could have changed the decision while it was open, and a disclosure of the delivery organisation's own commercial interest.

The paper concedes that an artefact produced at this point in an engagement will usually be written to defend a decision already taken; that the disclosure element may be unacceptable to a real employer; that the claim about inference being the harm class is design reasoning with no external warrant; and that no source available establishes that asking the question changes anything.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A delivery organisation arrives at an engagement holding a solution category, and every structure around it converts that into a requirement.

**The practice corpus behind this programme demonstrates the mechanism unusually clearly.** Its organising thesis is excellent — a delivery can meet every agreed criterion, use good architecture and ship to production **and still produce nothing of value** — and everything short of realised benefit is reclassified as activity. But its readiness criteria are composed almost entirely of customer dispositions, and the objection it prepares its engineers against most heavily is that the tooling improved and delivery did not speed up. **Its prepared answer reattributes the shortfall to the customer's own organisational constraints.**

**A model whose failure explanation is always the customer's readiness has arranged its evidence so that a disappointing result confirms rather than challenges the thesis.** That arrangement is not dishonest and nobody built it deliberately; it is what happens when the people who assess fit are the people who deliver.

The second problem is that the question, asked casually, is unanswerable. *Could this be done without AI?* invites *yes, but worse*, and there the matter rests. **The question only bites when the alternative has been designed to the same fidelity**, because only then can it be priced, argued with, and preferred.

The third problem is the one the platform anchor exists to expose. The material a team actually reaches for when making this decision is a vendor comparison, and a vendor comparison is a good instrument pointed at a different question. It answers *which of these should we use*, competently and with unusual candour about its own products' limits. **It cannot answer *should we use any of these*, and nothing in it pretends otherwise.** The gap is structural rather than a defect, and a team that does not notice it will mistake a completed comparison for a completed decision.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session requires the system model from [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md), because a comparison needs something to compare, and it retrieves [S003](/wiki/seminars/S003.md)'s problem behind the request, [S008](/wiki/seminars/S008.md)'s judgement about simpler technology and [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md)'s discipline of a feasibility question that has an answer.

It also assumes the retrieval material from earlier in the programme at the level of *what a lexical index is and how to stand one up*. A learner who has never built one will spend the session's practical time on that rather than on the comparison, which is a worse use of it but not a wasted one.

**The prior it works against is that the decision is settled.** It is settled. The session is not trying to reopen it; it is producing the artefact that lets somebody else reopen it later, and a cohort that misreads this will treat the exercise as pointless because they cannot change the outcome.

**A second prior is more damaging and less visible: that a non-inferring system is obviously worse.** Most learners hold this without ever having tested it, and the evidence runs the other way more often than they expect — not that the baseline wins, but that it does not collapse, which is a different and more useful property.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Low intrinsic load, high interpersonal load, and one genuine technical demand. The difficulty is writing a credible case against the work your team is being paid for, in a document your team will read. The technical demand — building the alternative rather than describing it — is deliberately placed alongside the affective one, because a learner with something running has an easier conversation than one with an argument.

**3.2 The opening items are a Productive Failure generation phase, and item 2 is the instrument.**

Learners are asked to design a non-AI alternative before being taught what one is. Most will offer the status quo, be asked for its components and its operator, and discover the distinction themselves. That is the **failed generation effect** — attempting an answer before instruction improves later learning even when the attempt is wrong — and the design condition attached to it is binding rather than advisory: **the generation phase must not be scaffolded**, because guidance that reduces the failure rate reduces the learning ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). This is why §§4, 5 and 9 are gated.

The same source imposes a second condition the session must satisfy and which is easy to omit: **the consolidation phase must explicitly prompt comparison.** Presenting a correct answer beside a learner's incorrect one achieves nothing on its own; only the condition instructed to compare them learned more. Exercise 3 is that instruction, and it is written as a comparison prompt rather than a rewrite prompt for exactly this reason.

**That warrant's limits are substantial and this paper states them rather than absorbing them.** The evidence is school, university and laboratory evidence. The claim that the design works in corporate training and adult education is asserted in the author's voice and supported by nothing in his reference list. The source is a trade book in which anecdote and evidence sit in adjacent paragraphs. And the author's own model contains a failure quadrant — **Unproductive Failure**, low performance *and* low learning — which is what a generation phase produces if nothing assembles the attempts afterwards.

**3.3 Why the question is a capability question before it is an ethical one.**

There is a class of proposals that can be declined without any argument about harm. The position paper this rests on states that auto-regressive language models **cannot, by themselves, do planning**, and closes the obvious escape route in the same breath: they cannot self-verify either, **because self-verification is itself a form of reasoning** ([research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md)). Its constructive half assigns the model a bounded role — a **universal approximate knowledge source**, broad in coverage and carrying no correctness guarantee — with correctness supplied by an **external verifier** and flexibility by the generator, and neither trusted with the other's job.

**The design consequence is that a necessity argument has two independent grounds and the architectural one is stronger.** *This should not be built because it could harm somebody* is a contestable judgement about values. *This should not be built this way because the component cannot supply the guarantee the design assumes* is a contestable judgement about engineering, and engineers are much better at having that argument. The session teaches learners to reach for the second first.

**Limits.** A position paper; its claims are arguments and have been contested; scoped to auto-regressive models as of early 2024, with reasoning-trained models postdating it. **The architectural discipline transfers and remains correct whether or not model reasoning improves**, which is the half the session uses; the empirical claim is not presented as settled anywhere in the session or this paper.

**3.4 Why the alternative is a real competitor, and why that is an evidence claim rather than a preference.**

A designed alternative that nobody believes in is a straw figure with better paperwork. The session's answer is to make the alternative one with a derivation and a published record.

Lexical retrieval is **derived, not invented**: the probability ranking principle gives the objective, inverse document frequency falls out of the derivation rather than being assumed, the saturating response to repeated terms is justified by an eliteness argument about evidence for a latent property, and length normalisation is exposed as a parameter precisely because a long document may be verbose or may genuinely cover more ground ([research/02-technical-foundations/robertson-zaragoza-bm25-and-beyond.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/robertson-zaragoza-bm25-and-beyond.md)). **Every component answers a stated modelling question, and can therefore be argued with** — which is a governance property, not only an engineering one.

The deployment evidence comes from heterogeneous zero-shot evaluation across lexical, sparse, dense, late-interaction and re-ranking families on domains they were not trained for ([research/02-technical-foundations/thakur-beir.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/thakur-beir.md)). **The lexical baseline is not best and does not collapse anywhere.** The most expressive families lead on average at high computational cost; the efficient neural families often underperform, which the authors characterise as considerable room for improvement in generalisation. **A method whose worst case is acceptable is operationally different from a method whose average is higher and whose worst case is unknown**, and a governance artefact is a document about worst cases.

**Limits on both.** The monograph is paywalled, its full text was not retrieved here, and the derivation is recorded from the wider literature rather than read from the source; it predates dense retrieval and retrieval-augmented generation entirely; its independence assumption is known false; its conventional parameter values are conventions rather than optima. The benchmark is a snapshot of its own period whose rankings have moved; its datasets are a sample of retrieval tasks rather than the population, with relevance judgements differing in density; and it has since become an optimisation target in its own right, which weakens its zero-shot premise over time. **Neither licenses a claim that the baseline will be adequate for any particular problem.**

**3.5 Why the cost categories are named explicitly.**

The corpus behind this programme contains **no cost side anywhere** — every claim in it is a gross benefit, with staffing, compute, the cost of the discovery activity itself and opportunity cost all absent ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). A learner who inherits that habit will produce the same asymmetry. Naming the categories converts an omission into a visible blank.

The same corpus supplies a sharper diagnosis in its value-conversation material: **the cost side is engineered and the return side is elicited.** Consumption is forecast by engineers against their own telemetry; return is chosen by the customer from a menu, measured with instrumentation the customer may not possess, over a period nobody specifies, with causation assumed and **no party assigned responsibility for establishing it.** The session's `causation_owner` field exists to make that omission impossible to reproduce silently.

**3.6 Why the comparison set is frozen and the instrument stated once.**

An eval is an experiment, and most two-system comparisons are run in a way that could not have produced a different answer ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). Three of that source's recommendations bind this session. **Compare on paired item-level differences rather than on two headline summaries**, because the two systems find the same items easy and hard and summaries discard that information. **Do the power analysis before the run**, so the team knows what the comparison is capable of seeing. And treat the **Minimum Detectable Effect** as the honest reading of a null: an eval too small to detect a difference produces the same output as a system with no difference, and only one of those is a finding.

Its worked example carries the lesson better than the principles do — a superficial reading of three benchmark comparisons hands the win to one model, and a correct analysis reverses it. **Ordinary practice produced the wrong answer, not merely an imprecise one.**

**Limits.** An analytical paper rather than an empirical one; its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions and are worked examples rather than constants; **statistical validity says nothing about construct validity**; and its power analysis needs prior variance estimates a first-time team does not have. The session accordingly asks for a stated Minimum Detectable position and accepts *unknown*, rather than requiring a computed one.

**3.7 Why the vendor comparison is the anchor rather than a target.**

The build-path guidance splits the options by service model, states the selection criteria as capability, timeline and control requirements, admits that its fast setup lacks network isolation, publishes a department-scale envelope for one of its own products, and states that the options compose rather than exclude ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). **That is better documentation than most, and the session says so.**

It is used here because the omission it contains is honest and structural rather than evasive. Extracting the axes and discarding the products is the durable-versus-perishable exercise this programme is built around, and the missing row is the cheapest available demonstration that **a decision framework inherits the boundary of the question it was written to answer.**

**Limits.** First-party, single-vendor, read through search-returned excerpts with several comparison tables truncated, **highly perishable**, and carrying no adoption, cost, failure-rate or outcome data. It compares the vendor's options against each other and against nothing else.

**3.8 The claim this paper cannot warrant, stated where a reader will find it.**

The session's opening move — that the least harmful system is often the one that does not infer, because the harms this module assesses are harms of inference — **is design reasoning with no external warrant.** It is an argument from the structure of the module's own harm taxonomy, it is checkable by reading that taxonomy, and it has never been tested against outcomes. No source available to this programme reports whether teams that build a deterministic alternative end up with safer systems, or with any systems at all.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The movements run situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**4.1 Opening item 1 is a filter.** A problem statement that does not survive having every product name removed was a solution statement, and a learner who discovers this in Stage 6 has discovered it late but not too late. The session strengthens the filter by requiring a second deletion — every verb that presupposes inference — because *summarise these reports* survives the first pass and is still a solution.

**4.2 Opening item 2 is asked before instruction because the failure is instructive.** Most learners will offer the status quo, notice on being asked for components and an operator that it has neither, and thereby learn what a designed alternative is without being told. **This is the generation phase and it is deliberately unscaffolded** (§3.2). A session that helped learners toward a good alternative during the opening would not be a gentler design; it would be a worse one.

**4.3 Opening item 5 asks who benefits and whether they were in the room**, which is [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md)'s question aimed at the delivery side rather than the customer side. It is the only place in the programme where it is turned around.

**4.4 The alternative must be built, not described, and the requirement is new to this revision.** A described alternative cannot be priced, cannot be shown to fail, and cannot be preferred; designing it to the same fidelity is the only way to make the comparison symmetric, because **asymmetric comparisons always favour the option that was described in detail.** Requiring it to run adds a second property the description cannot supply: it removes the author's ability to imagine its failures. **A learner who has watched their own baseline handle a query set is arguing from an observation; one who has written a paragraph about it is arguing from a belief**, and the room can tell the difference.

**4.5 The comparison set is frozen and committed before either output is seen.** This is the session's only mechanically checkable element and it is placed there on purpose, because §8's first objection is that everything else is self-reported. A commit timestamp earlier than the first result is evidence; a claim that the set was chosen fairly is not.

**4.6 The unit of analysis is stated once and applied to both options.** A metric without its unit of analysis is not a metric, and two systems scored under different units are not being compared. The session asks for the statement in the artefact rather than in the learner's head, because the failure is not that teams choose badly but that they never choose at all and therefore cannot say what their number answers.

**4.7 The learner states what their comparison could have detected, and *unknown* is an acceptable answer.** The design deliberately does not require a computed Minimum Detectable Effect, because the variance estimates needed for one are not available to a team running a comparison for the first time (§3.6). What it requires is that the learner cannot write *no meaningful difference* without having considered whether the instrument could have seen one. **A confident null from an instrument of unknown resolution is the most common and most expensive error in this genre**, and it is the one a governance reader is least equipped to catch.

**4.8 The platform work is additive rather than critical.** The learner records the vendor comparison's axes and the date, then adds the row it does not have. **The design does not ask them to find fault with the documentation**, and the session says explicitly that the documentation is answering the question it was asked. The lesson is about the boundary of a decision framework, and it is much easier to see when the framework in front of the learner is a good one.

**4.9 The agent is used to attack the record rather than the system.** Two prompts: argue the deterministic option should have won using only what is written; and identify every claim that would still be written exactly this way if the opposite were true. The second is the harder and the more useful, and it is a direct test of whether the artefact is an analysis or an advocacy document with sections.

**4.10 The commercial interest is disclosed.** An assessment of whether a technology is necessary, written by the party that sells it, with no disclosure, is structurally the same artefact as a readiness rubric composed of customer dispositions. **Naming the relationship does not neutralise it; it lets a reader discount appropriately**, which is all any disclosure ever does.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The status quo as alternative.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* the components-operator-failure-mode requirement in the standard, the requirement that the alternative be built, and the warning sign — your alternative has no components and no operator.

**The straw alternative.** *Countermeasure:* the frozen set and the single instrument, which constrain the comparison after the design is fixed. **This is a weaker countermeasure than it looks**, because a learner can still build a deliberately poor baseline; what the frozen set prevents is the commoner and quieter version, in which the baseline is fine and the queries were chosen after its weaknesses were known.

**The set written afterwards.** *Countermeasure:* the commit requirement, which is the session's only mechanical check.

**The undetectable difference reported as a null.** *Countermeasure:* the stated-detectability field and exercise 5. **§9's prediction 4 predicts this countermeasure failing**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**The unpriced comparison.** *Countermeasure:* the named cost categories, checked column by column, plus the `causation_owner` field that makes the source corpus's central omission impossible to reproduce silently.

**The retrofitted question.** *Countermeasure:* the requirement that disconfirming evidence be of a kind that could have appeared during the engagement. **This is a detection rather than a prevention**, and §8 concedes that the session cannot prevent it.

**The capability answer.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's portability test — a justification that fits a different problem unchanged is not a justification.

**The unowned interest.** *Countermeasure:* the disclosure element. §8 concedes that a real employer may not accept it.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. Four constituents are assessed at [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md): that the problem statement contains no technology; that the alternative is a design with components and an operator rather than a description of the present; that the comparison set was committed before the first result; and that the recorded rejection names disconfirming evidence which was available while the decision was open.

**Whether the decision was correct is not assessed**, here or at [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md), and nothing in this programme can assess it. The distinction matters and follows the same reasoning the programme applies to refusal elsewhere: correctness depends on facts about the engagement that the assessment cannot hold constant, whereas the reasoning is under the learner's control and is the transferable part.

**Nor is the cost comparison assessed for accuracy**, only for the presence of both columns in the same units and a named causation owner. Assessing accuracy would require the programme to hold a view about compute prices and staffing rates, which it does not and should not.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: asking this in Stage 6 produces a justification rather than a decision, and the artefact will be cited as evidence that the alternative was considered when it was not.**

At its strongest: the money is spent, the team is assembled, the customer is committed. **Every artefact produced at this point is written by somebody who knows the answer**, and this one will be filed and later quoted — *we assessed the non-AI option* — as though a genuine comparison had occurred. That is worse than not asking, because it manufactures the appearance of due diligence and inoculates the decision against future challenge.

**Substantially conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to this session.** Four partial answers. The judgement is not made here — it was made at [S008](/wiki/seminars/S008.md), in Stage 1, when it was cheap — and this session produces the record rather than the decision, which is a smaller and more honest claim. The disconfirming-evidence element makes retrofitting detectable, because a rejection resting on evidence only available after commitment is visibly post hoc to any second reader. The frozen set with its commit is a mechanical constraint that a purely defensive document cannot satisfy retrospectively without falsifying a timestamp, which is a different and more serious act than writing a flattering paragraph. And a built alternative is expensive to fake.

**The declined fix is to delete this session as duplicative of [S008](/wiki/seminars/S008.md).** It is declined because a judgement with no findable record cannot be revisited, and this decision is revisited by default: the rejected alternative is the first thing anybody reaches for when the system disappoints, and at that moment the existence of a dated, priced, honestly disconfirmable rejection is worth more than the original judgement was. **The residual risk — that the record is written to defend rather than to inform — is real, is not eliminated, and is what §9's prediction 2 measures.**

**Objection 2: requiring an engineer to disclose their own organisation's commercial interest is naive, and the element trains a behaviour no employer will accept.**

At its strongest: a delivery organisation will not sign out an artefact stating that it recommended a technology it sells. The element therefore guarantees one of two outcomes — the artefact is not produced, or the sentence is removed before it leaves the building — and a curriculum requirement that cannot survive contact with an employer has taught nothing except how to be uncomfortable.

**Conceded, and the design's answer is narrow.** The element requires the relationship to be *named*, not characterised as corrupting: *this organisation supplies the recommended technology* is a statement of fact, is standard in several professional disclosure regimes, and is not an accusation. **The declined fix is to drop the element.** It is declined because an assessment of whether a technology is necessary, produced by the party selling it with no disclosure, is structurally the artefact this session exists to critique — and a programme that teaches learners to detect that arrangement cannot require them to construct one. **The friction is real and this programme cannot resolve it.** A learner who finds the sentence removed by their organisation has learned something the session could not otherwise teach, and [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) accepts a record of that removal in its place.

**Objection 3: the retrieval evidence is being used to license a claim it does not support, and the session's deterministic alternative is a special case dressed as a general one.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the design finds hardest technically. The benchmark result — that a lexical baseline generalises robustly across domains it was not trained on — is a claim about *retrieval*. The session generalises it into a stance about *systems*, and most of the systems learners are building are not retrieval systems. A team building a classification workflow, a summarisation pipeline or an agent that takes actions has no lexical baseline available, and telling them that a strong deterministic competitor exists is telling them something that happens to be true in one subfield. **The session then compounds the problem by choosing the one domain where its argument is easiest**, which is the oldest rhetorical move in engineering education and the one this repository is normally hostile to.

**Partly conceded and the concession is specific.** The benchmark licenses a claim about retrieval and nothing wider, its authors say so, and the session's limits section repeats it. What generalises is not the finding but the *method*: build the alternative, freeze the set, score both on one instrument, and price both. That method is available for a classification workflow — the deterministic competitor is a rule set, and rule sets have the same arguable-component property as a derived ranking function. It is genuinely weaker for open-ended generation, where the honest answer is that no deterministic competitor exists and the necessity argument has to be made on the capability grounds of §3.3 instead. **The declined fix is to restrict the session to retrieval problems and say so.** It is declined because the necessity question is not a retrieval question, and a version of this session that only fired for teams building search would exempt exactly the teams whose systems infer most. **The cost of declining is that a learner on an open-ended generation problem will do the weakest version of this exercise**, and the session does not currently detect that; §9's prediction 5 attaches an instrument to it.

**Objection 4: the claim that a non-inferring system is less harmful is asserted rather than shown, and it is the sentence the whole session rests on.**

At its strongest: the session opens by telling learners that the harms of the module are harms of inference and that declining to infer removes the class. That is an argument about a taxonomy this programme wrote. **A deterministic system that denies somebody a benefit through a rule nobody has reviewed for years causes real harm, is much harder to challenge than a model output, and is invisible to every instrument this module installs.** Automated decision-making caused injustice for decades before anything inferred. Presenting the deterministic option as the safe one is a category error that could produce worse systems than the ones it displaces.

**Conceded without qualification, and it is the most serious substantive objection to the session's framing.** The rebuttal is narrower than the claim the session makes: what removing inference removes is the harm class *this module is instrumented to assess* — fabrication, drift, injection, confident false completion — and it substitutes a different class that this module does not assess at all. **That is a redistribution of harm, not a reduction of it**, and stating it as a reduction is the session's own strongest failure. **The declined fix is to soften the opening to a neutral comparison** — both options carry harms, assess both. It is declined on generation-phase grounds: a neutral opening produces a neutral answer, and the cohort's default is already to assume the deterministic option is worse, so a design that opens neutrally opens on the side it is trying to test. **The cost is recorded here rather than hidden: the session overstates its case at the opening and corrects it in the limits section, which is a worse structure than correcting it up front, and a reader who weighs the risk of the overstatement more heavily than this paper does is making a defensible call.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first alternatives will describe the current process rather than a from-scratch design. *Instrument:* the submitted alternative, coded *designed* / *partially designed* / *status quo* by a second reader, using the presence of components, an operator and a named failure mode.

**Prediction 2.** Most disconfirming-evidence statements will name evidence available only after commitment. *Instrument:* each statement coded *available during the engagement* / *available only afterwards* / *absent*.

**Prediction 3.** Cost comparisons will price the non-AI alternative more completely than the option being built, and the `causation_owner` field will be the most frequently empty field in the record. *Instrument:* a category-by-category presence check across both columns by a second reader, using the named categories, plus a completeness count per field across the cohort.

**Prediction 4.** Despite the stated-detectability field, a majority of records will report a comparative conclusion without stating what difference the comparison could have detected, or will state it as *unknown* and then draw a confident conclusion anyway. *Instrument:* the records, coded for whether the detectability statement and the conclusion are consistent. **This predicts the failure of §5's newest countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 5.** Learners whose systems are retrieval problems will produce substantially more complete alternatives than learners whose systems are open-ended generation problems, and the gap will not be visible in the assessed elements. *Instrument:* submissions stratified by problem type and coded on the same *designed / partially designed / status quo* scale. **This is the instrument attached to §8's third objection**, and a confirmation means the session is exempting the teams whose systems infer most.

**Prediction 6.** A minority of learners will attempt to build the deterministic alternative at all, and among those who do, a majority will report being surprised by how well it performed on their own frozen set. *Instrument:* a built/not-built count, plus a pre-recorded expectation compared against the observed result per learner. **If the surprise does not occur, the session's central empirical wager is wrong for this cohort's problem set**, and the material's use of the retrieval evidence should be demoted from argument to illustration.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the alternative must be built to be comparable; that a comparison whose set was chosen after the results were seen is advocacy; that a rejection no evidence could have reversed is not a rejection; and that a record, unlike a judgement, can be revisited by somebody who was not there.

**What the evidence licenses.** Much less than the session's confidence suggests. The retrieval evidence is good and is about retrieval. The capability argument is a contested position paper. **No source available to this programme establishes that asking this question changes any outcome**, and the regime that asks it publishes no evidence that any assessment ever changed a system. **The claim that a non-inferring system is less harmful is this design's own argument and §8's fourth objection concedes it is at best a redistribution of harm.**

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper.** That is a rule of this repository rather than an accident of what was read, and it holds even where a source states a number in a full text.

**The concession worth remembering.** You will write this artefact knowing the answer. The design's only real defence is that somebody will read it later under different pressure, and that what you leave them determines whether they can act.

**The silences that matter, and the first is the session's subject.** **The comparison publishes no option that does not contain a model**, and it does not state that limitation anywhere, because it is a guide to choosing among the vendor's products rather than a guide to deciding whether to use one. A reader who takes it as a complete decision framework will complete it and still not have made the decision. **It publishes no adoption data, no failure-rate data and no cost comparison for any build path**, so the selection guidance is a reasoned framework rather than a validated finding; the session accordingly assesses the axes and the reasoning and never the recommendation. **It compares the vendor's options only against each other** — not against any other supplier's, and not against no supplier at all. **And it states no scale envelope for most of the tools it compares**, with the department-scale statement the exception rather than the pattern, which is why the session instructs learners to record an absence as a finding rather than to infer a limit from one instance.
