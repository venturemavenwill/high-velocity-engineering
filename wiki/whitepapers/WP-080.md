## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

Everyone in this room can write a blameless post-incident report. They have sat in blameless error clinics for seventy weeks and the protocol is published. **So blamelessness is not what is being judged.** What is being judged is the discipline of an account: which parts are sourced, which are inference, and what the record simply cannot settle.

And the record is not a record. It is an alert history with gaps, a deploy log in the wrong time zone, a channel transcript in which somebody typed a hypothesis at two in the morning that everyone afterwards treated as a fact, notes written by a tired person, and traces containing exactly what somebody configured them to keep for exactly as long as somebody paid to keep them. Every previous occasion in the programme handed students evidence that had been assembled for them. **This evidence was produced as a by-product of people trying to fix something.**

Two things follow, and they are the day.

**The first is that the account will default to people.** Under sixty minutes of pressure the shortest way to say what happened is a sequence of things somebody did — not because anyone stopped believing in blameless review, but because that is the grammar of a story. *The on-call engineer restarted the wrong service* is four words and the alternative is a sentence. So the remedy is stated as a mechanical rewriting rather than as a value, because a value cannot be applied at speed and a rewriting can. **And the second half of the rewriting is the half that does the work** — not *the system permitted a restart*, but *and here is what made it reasonable* — which is trivially skippable in a document and impossible to skip when the person concerned is sitting opposite you asking what you wrote about them.

**The second is that the attribution may not be available**, and *undecided, with the evidence that would decide it* passes at the same levels as a decided attribution. That is published before the day starts so nobody reads it as a way out.

An agent is allowed inside the assessed hour, because a sixty-minute window against a large trace record with the tool withheld measures reading speed. And the day did not need a new rule for it: **an entry an agent produced and you did not check against the record is an inference**, which was already a published failure condition.

**What this paper concedes, and it is a real concession.** The programme's error-clinic firewall is unconditional and it covers the error clinic. **Phase 3 is not an error clinic.** It is an assessed artefact read by two assessors forming a level — and for any student who was on the rota during this incident it contains their own decisions, reconstructed by themselves, in a module whose own content is that an honest account of error requires certainty that the disclosure cannot be used. The obvious fix is to exclude students from analysing incidents they were in. **It is declined**, because those are the incidents with a complete record, a reachable narrator and a real stake, and excluding them leaves an exercise. The paper says plainly that recording this is not a remedy.

This paper defends an entrustment judgement on an account of something that has already happened, and defends assessing the account's discipline rather than its conclusion. It defends five failure conditions published before the day begins, of which the sharpest is that naming a person as the cause places a student below 2b regardless of everything else in the artefact, and it defends stating that rule as a mechanical rewriting rather than as a value. It defends *undecided, with the evidence that would decide it* as a pass at the same levels as a decided attribution, on the ground established three weeks earlier that an attack and an accident produce the same record. It defends permitting an agent inside the assessed hour, and defends the one construction under which that is assessable: **an entry produced by an agent and not verified against the record is an inference**, which places it under a condition the day already published. **Section 7 reports the discharge of the three obligations [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md), [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md) and [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) placed on this day.** Section 8 concedes that the programme's error-clinic firewall covers phase 7 and not phase 3, so the day requires an honest account of error to be written for assessors — in a module whose content is that this cannot be done.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Every student in this room can write a blameless post-incident report. They have been in blameless error clinics for seventy weeks, the protocol is published, and if asked to describe one they will describe it correctly. **The competence the day must produce is not the description and it is not the intention.**

Four difficulties operate and only the second is about blamelessness.

**The record is not a record.** It is an alert history with gaps, a deploy log whose timestamps are in a different zone, a channel transcript in which somebody typed a hypothesis at 02:14 that everybody afterwards treated as a fact, a set of contemporaneous notes written by a person who was tired, and a body of traces that contains exactly what somebody configured it to keep for exactly as long as somebody paid to keep it. Every prior occasion in the programme gave students evidence that had been assembled for them, including the ones that taught them to interrogate evidence. Here the evidence was produced as a by-product of people trying to fix something, and the first thing the account has to do is say which parts of itself are inference.

**And there is now a fifth difficulty the design created by making the programme practical.** Students will use an agent to read the record, because the record is large and the window is sixty minutes and refusing the tool would measure typing speed. **An agent asked what happened will produce a causal narrative from an incomplete record, fluently, without marking which parts are inference** — not because it is unreliable but because that is what the request asks for and the gaps are where fluency is cheapest. The day's first failure condition and the agent's characteristic output are the same object, which is either the design's luckiest coincidence or the clearest available illustration that the discipline being assessed is not a discipline about tools.

**The mechanism defaults to people.** Under sixty minutes of pressure, the shortest way to say what happened is a sequence of things somebody did, and it is not a failure of intent — it is the natural grammar of a narrative. A student who believes in blameless review completely will still write *the on-call engineer restarted the wrong service* because it is four words and the alternative is a sentence. The day's answer is a mechanical transformation stated as a rule, because a value cannot be applied at speed and a rewriting can.

**The attribution may not be available and the student will supply one anyway.** [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) three weeks earlier established that attack and accident produce the same record and that what separates them is a recording decision made in advance. Today they find out whether anybody made it, for a real incident, and the honest answer may be no. The difficulty is not epistemic — the room reached this conclusion at [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) and can restate it — it is that *undecided* feels like a failure to produce a deliverable, and the artefact is being assessed.

**And the account has a reader who will act on it.** This is what makes the occasion class 6 rather than a repeat of [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s communication demand. The recommendations at item 8 go to somebody who was not in the incident, has no ability to ask a follow-up question, and will change a running system on the strength of what is written. **What transfers is not the analysis but three sentences with owners on them**, and the discrimination the day is built to make is between a student who can analyse and a student who can hand over.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Nothing is built and the load is entirely evidential.** This follows [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §3.1 and [WP-070](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §3.1, both of which established that a judgement about reasoning under pressure is invalid if the student is simultaneously constructing the evidence. Here the evidence was constructed by other people, badly, in the middle of the night, which is the point.

The interactivity is concentrated between items 1, 2 and 3 and the order is load-bearing. The timeline's sourcing determines which entries can support the latency attribution; the latency attribution determines what the detection design could have shown; and what the detection design could have shown is a large part of what decides the attack-or-accident question, because a condition that was invisible to every signal is a condition nobody chose to make visible — which is [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) point 7 arriving as a constraint on today's own conclusion. **A student who writes item 3 before item 2 will decide the attribution from the narrative**, which is the failure the ordering exists to prevent.

Item 8 has almost no interactivity with the rest and that is deliberate. Recommendations are not derived from the account by a procedure; they are a judgement about what should change, and the account's function is to motivate rather than to entail them. Placing item 8 last and requiring each recommendation to cite the evidence in the account that motivates it keeps the two connected without pretending the second follows from the first.

Management:

- **Nothing is built on the day.**
- **The four module artefacts are in front of the student**, as at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md).
- **The account's form is supplied**, so no time is spent on structure.
- **The agent is available and its records are a by-product rather than an extra task.** A prompt record is what the student did; an intervention record is what they corrected; a frontier statement is two sentences. None of the three requires work that was not already performed, which is the only construction under which they can be added to a sixty-minute assessed window without displacing the assessment.
- **Item 4's transformation is stated as a rewriting** — *every "X did Y" becomes "the system permitted Y, and here is what made Y reasonable"* — which is a syntactic operation and can be applied under pressure. A value cannot.
- **The sampling is disclosed before the phase**, so students are not simultaneously reconstructing an incident and wondering why this one.
- **Consolidation comes after the assessed work**, so nobody performs while holding a framework.

Phase 4's fifteen minutes per student, in groups of four, with two assessors, the on-call member and the accountable owner present simultaneously, is the binding constraint on the day and is the same constraint [WP-070](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §3.1 identified: the [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s open question about staffing is live here in its sharpest form, and this occasion is worse than [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s because one of the four people is the person who handled the incident and there is only one of them.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Writing a three-sentence account before opening the record*, item 1, and seeing it against the timeline at phase 8.
- *Answering the attack-or-accident question from symptoms alone, with the evidence that would change the answer*, item 2.
- *Naming what the record does not contain, before knowing whether it matters*, item 3.
- *Marking your own inferences in a document that will be assessed*, artefact item 1.
- *Being asked, of an entry you did not write yourself, how you know it is true*, phase 4.
- *Being required to state what made a decision reasonable when you know the outcome*, artefact item 5.
- *Writing "undecided" in an assessed artefact*, artefact item 3.
- *Being asked, by the person concerned, what you wrote about them*, phase 4.
- *Being asked to drop one of your three recommendations by the person who would have implemented it*, phase 4.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Running an incident.* Incident command, escalation practice and on-call simulation are excluded; [WP-069](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §3.2 named them this day's and the substrate's, and the day takes the second half only. Analysing an incident and running one are different competences and the second is the substrate rota's, continuously, all year.
- *Determining the true cause.* Not assessed and not assessable: the specification checks the discipline and the discriminating evidence, not the conclusion.
- *A hostile reviewer.* Everyone in phase 4 acts in good faith, as at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) and [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md). The on-call member's question is uncomfortable and is not adversarial.
- *A written report that outlives the conversation.* Today's account is defended within hours. A record that must survive without its author is [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md)'s.
- *Cross-student comparison of the same incident.* Every student analyses the same incident, and the cohort's attributions are shown unattributed at phase 6 point 3. **They are not ranked**, per the [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s prohibition on ranking assessed work, and the temptation to run this occasion as a comparison of accounts is refused for that reason.
- *Assessing the agent records.* They are checked for presence and defended; **no level attaches to them**, and the assessors are told so in writing. An elegant prompt record is a writing sample, and this occasion assesses a forensic account.
- *Withholding the agent.* Considered and refused. A sixty-minute window against a large trace record, with the tool unavailable, measures reading speed and rewards the student who guesses which twenty per cent of the record matters. **The programme's unassisted-work provision is at [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md), [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md)**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), and this is not one of them.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The eight-item forensic account is **safe as the form of a review of an incident on a system you operate, where the record exists, the participants are reachable, and the organisation has already committed to a blameless protocol.** It is **unsafe in three conditions and each produces a confident-looking account on a false premise.** It is unsafe where the review has a legal or contractual consequence: item 5's reconstruction of what somebody believed at a moment is exactly the material a liability process will use against them, and a student who applies this form inside one has produced a document whose purpose is not the purpose the form assumes. It is unsafe where the participants are not reachable — item 5 requires an information state, item 5 without the person is an attribution of belief, and the form has no marker distinguishing a reconstructed information state from an evidenced one. And it is unsafe where the organisation has not committed to blamelessness: **an account written in this form and read by a management that wants an attribution will be mined for one**, because item 5 supplies the material and item 4's rewriting does not remove it. **In all three the account will pass this day's specification and harm somebody.** The first and third are stated to students at phase 6 point 2; the second is a gap in the form and §8 records it.

**One further condition applies to the tool and it is stated in the entrustment brief.** The account is **safe to produce with agent assistance where every entry is verified against the record and the verification is the student's**. It is **unsafe wherever the record is thin**, because that is precisely where an agent's output is most fluent and least checkable, and it is the condition under which the day's own error clinic supplies a case of a fabricated deployment assembled from a cancelled change request. The day's response is not a warning: it is that **an unverified agent-supplied entry falls under a published failure condition**, so the unsafe condition has a consequence in the artefact rather than a caveat in this paper.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **the on-call member's question at phase 4**: *what did you write about me?*

It is asked of every student, the answer is read from the account as written, and the person asking is the person who was awake at three in the morning doing the thing the account describes. Every artefact in the programme has had a reader; this is the first that has had a subject.

The mechanism is not discomfort for its own sake. It is that item 4's rewriting rule is trivially satisfiable in a document and not satisfiable in a conversation with the person concerned. A student who has performed the transformation mechanically — replacing *the engineer restarted the service* with *the system permitted a restart* and stopping — will discover in that exchange that the second half of the rule, *and here is what made it reasonable*, is the half that does the work, and that they did not write it. **That discovery is the day's instruction and it happens inside the assessed occasion**, which is why phase 6 comes afterwards.

The second mechanism is **the accountable owner's question**: drop one of your three. Item 8 asked for three recommendations and not for a priority among them, deliberately, so that the priority has to be produced live under a constraint the receiver imposes. That is EA-2's actual shape — a handover is negotiated, not delivered — and it is the part of the entrusted activity this rehearsal can reach.

Autonomy is complete: the attribution is the student's, and *undecided* is a pass. Competence is supported by the fact that nothing is built and the four inputs are theirs. Relatedness is supplied by two people in the room who were part of the event.

The affective risk is real and specific: **a student analysing an incident they were themselves on call for**, describing their own decisions to two assessors who are forming an entrustment level. §8 objection 2 treats this as the day's central unresolved problem rather than as a risk to be managed, and the countermeasures — the sealed-level ordering, the published failure conditions, the fact that item 5 requires a charitable reconstruction of every decider including oneself — mitigate it and do not remove it.

A second affective risk is quieter: **a student whose [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) commitment is shown at phase 5 to have missed the incident entirely.** The countermeasure is the sealing of the level before the phase, the decoupling from assessment stated at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) and [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) as well as here, and the cohort-level reporting.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items on the incident's opening symptoms only. Item 2 is administered before the record because [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s finding is that the record does not decide, and asking afterwards would measure reading rather than discrimination. Item 3 is pooled and is the day's most durable output. **Item 4 is the module's closing Copilot prediction and carries no assessment weight.**

**Phase 2 (30 min).** Seven retrievals across the module and three earlier days, two module-boundary items, and a bridge that names the single place where the module's two subjects diverge. The room answers.

**Phase 3 (60 min).** The entrusted activity. **The sampling is disclosed first, per instrumentation rule 3.** Eight items, nothing built, **and the three agent records carried alongside.** Items 2, 3 and 7 are the rule-6 elements [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) and [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md) named. Item 4's rewriting is the day's hard requirement; item 8 is the class-6 element.

**Phase 4 (60 min).** Level formed from the account first, per rule 7. Four questioners, two of whom are not assessors. The on-call member's question is asked of every student; the accountable owner's is the EA-2 negotiation; **the agent question is asked about entries the assessors choose rather than entries the student presents.**

**Phase 5 (35 min).** Levels sealed first. Three parts, none assessed: the commitment against the incident, **with instrumentation rule 2 applied at the third administration of the same latency measurement**; the [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) transition; and the pooled record-gap list handed to the substrate's owner.

**Phase 6 (35 min).** Eight points, assessors absent. Point 2 relocates accountability rather than removing it; **point 6 states the cost of the strand's late start to the cohort rather than to a whitepaper**; **point 7 reports what the agent did to this account and joins it to the room's own item 1 and to the reviewers in the clinic**; point 8 names three forward occasions with their instruments.

**Phase 7 (40 min).** A staff member's own contemporaneous messages from a decision that made an incident worse, read as written. **Then a prior year's agent-assisted account containing three entries that were not in the record, one of which was implemented.** Then the sharpest student prompt in the programme.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 1 against the timeline; item 2 against item 3 with the [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) transition; **item 3's pooled list**; **item 4 opened with the corrected-entry and surfaced-entry counts**; predicted against awarded level; the phase 5 commitment result with three latency readings side by side; assessor narratives with divergences and defence movement; **the [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) instrument's tenth administration.**

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has been on call professionally will produce a fluent timeline and a competent mechanism. Predicted failure: **item 5.** Reconstructing what a decider knew at a moment is the part experienced responders skip, because they know what the decision should have been and the account writes itself backwards. The artefact will contain decisions annotated with their outcomes rather than with their information states. Countermeasure: the on-call member is in the room and is briefed to say whether the reconstruction matches what they actually knew, and a reconstruction the subject does not recognise is treated by the assessors as a failed element rather than as a difference of opinion.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is paralysis at item 1: an incomplete record, sixty minutes, and an assessed artefact. Countermeasures: the form is supplied; **marking an entry as inference is explicitly permitted and is a checked element rather than a concession**, so a sparse well-marked timeline passes and a dense unmarked one does not; staff answer questions of fact throughout; item 3's *undecided* clause is stated in the entrustment brief before phase 1 so that nobody spends thirty minutes deciding whether they are allowed to write it; **and the agent removes the reading bottleneck that would otherwise make the window a test of speed.** The last countermeasure is also this student's largest new risk, and §8 objection 4 treats it as one.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** A student for whom the attribution is obvious — the record does decide, for them — has a real situation and not a defective one, and the day should not manufacture ambiguity. The difficulty is relocated to phase 4's perturbation: *what if the first entry in your timeline is an hour out; what if the person you did not interview has a different account; what if this recurs next week.* The first has no prepared answer and is the strongest, because a one-hour shift moves the deploy either side of the onset and the attribution frequently depends on which. Fallback if a student handles all three: ask what they would have to have recorded, and who would have had to agree to record it, for their conclusion to have been available on the first day of the incident rather than the fifth.

**Affective failure.** Two, and they are not symmetric.

The first is **the student who was in the incident**, treated at §8 objection 2 as an unresolved design problem rather than as a risk.

The second is **a student who reads point 2 as permission**. A room told that blameless review relocates accountability to the owner of a recommendation can hear that nobody is accountable for what happened. Countermeasure: point 2 is stated as a relocation with the mechanism named — a review that names a person changes one person's behaviour and no system's, and the change leaves when they do — and **item 8's owner requirement is a published failure condition**, so the occasion produces named accountability in the artefact rather than removing it from the discussion. The instructor states once that the most accountable document in the room is the one with three names on it.

## 6. Retention architecture

**Retrieves from** [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) at seventy weeks, [S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md) and [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) at four quarters, and the module's four days at one to four weeks. The [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) retrieval is the longest in the programme for a concept a day depends on operationally, and it is scaffolded to the point of quotation: the firewall's exact terms are the prompt rather than the answer, because what the day needs is not that students remember the firewall but that they hold its *reason*, which point 2 then extends from a cohort protocol to a document discipline.

**Retrieved by** [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) at one quarter (what actually transfers, where the item 8 handovers are the material), [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) at one quarter (the safety case, whose claims must be selected against the pooled record-gap list, and which the [Seminar Day Index](/wiki/program/08-Seminar-Day-Index.md) records as retrieving S076–S079 as a block), [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) (residual risk, written against these accounts), [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) (the handover artefact, which must carry the disposition of each recommendation made here) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md).

**[S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) is the consequential retrieval** and it is the only occasion in the programme where a student's recommendation meets the organisation that received it. A recommendation implemented, a recommendation declined with a reason, and a recommendation never answered are three different results about the same handover, and the third is the most common and the least discussed.

Retained content: the eight-item form; the sourced-or-marked rule and its extension to anything a tool supplied; *undecided with the discriminating evidence*; the rewriting rule and its second half; the information state rather than the outcome; three recommendations with owners; and **the record contains what somebody chose to keep for as long as somebody paid to keep it.** Seven items.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 4, and at [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md).

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**The quarter's only entrustment judgement, and the programme's first rehearsal of EA-2.** Expected 2b to 3a; 3b available and rare. It touches EA-3 at item 8 and at the on-call member's question, and it is neither activity.

Level formed from the account before the defence, per rater discipline rule 7. Narrative substantiation mandatory and required to cite specific entries and specific moments.

**Five named failure conditions**, four of them level conditions and one a narrative finding. The sharpest is that **naming a person as the cause is below 2b whatever else the account contains**, and it is stated as a mechanical rewriting rather than as a value for the reason §3.1 gives. **The *undecided* condition is symmetric in the way [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s no-ship condition was**: an undecided attribution with the discriminating evidence named is a pass at the same levels as a decided one, and it is stated before phase 1 so that the option is not read as a concession. **The agent is handled inside the existing second condition rather than by a sixth**: an entry produced by an agent and not verified against the record is an inference, and an unmarked inference is below 2b. Adding a sixth condition would have made the tool the subject; extending the second makes the *evidence* the subject, which is what the day is about.

Per rule 1 the checked elements are enumerated on the day and end with *nothing else is assessed* — **not whether the attribution is correct, and not whether the recommendations are adopted.**

**Phase 5's results are not assessed**, and the specification says so in writing. [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §7's argument is accepted unchanged for the third time in this programme: an assessed prediction check is a strategy.

**The sampling disclosure is an instrumentation rule 3 discharge.** Rule 3 requires that where a day supplies cases, selects sessions or chooses which artefact is examined, the sampling decision is disclosed to students in the same terms the day would demand of them. **This day selects one incident from a quarter's worth, on a criterion — that its attribution is genuinely open — that makes it deliberately unrepresentative.** The disclosure states the candidate count, the exclusions and the criterion, and states that a representative incident would have been less useful and that presenting it as typical would have been the selection effect the programme teaches students to detect. The rule was added after [WP-038](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-038.md) §8 found three instances in one module; this is a case where the disclosure costs one paragraph and the omission would have been the fourth.

### Discharge of the three obligations placed on this day

**These paragraphs are the reports [WP-077](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-077.md) §7, [WP-078](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-078.md) §7 and [WP-079](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-079.md) §7 require, and all three obligations are discharged.**

**[S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md)** named this day's phase 3 item 3 as the response occasion for its T4 misclassification rate, specifying that the incident be selected so that the attack-or-accident question is genuinely open at the start, and that the student's attribution carry the evidence that decides between the two, with *undecided, and here is what would decide it* a pass at the same levels as a decided position. **The element exists as artefact item 3; the selection criterion is not merely honoured but disclosed to students at the top of phase 3 under instrumentation rule 3; the *undecided* clause is one of the day's five published failure conditions rather than a permission buried in a rubric; and the cohort transition from the [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) classification to today's is reported at phase 5 part two.** It is not a mention: it determines which incident the day runs on. The obligation is discharged. **Its one dependency is stated at §2** — if the quarter produces no incident with an open attribution, the discharge fails and this paper says so rather than reporting a discharge it did not make.

**[S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md)** named this day's phase 3 item 7 as the response occasion for its unowned disclosure-position count, specifying that the forensic account state who was entitled to decide what users were told about the incident and whether anybody did. **The element exists as artefact item 7, it is a checked element, and it carries [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s named-person requirement rather than accepting a function** — which matters here more than at [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md), because on the day of an incident the answer *the comms process* is the answer that produces silence. The obligation is discharged.

**[S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md)** named this day's phase 3 item 2 as the response occasion for its signed latency error, specifying a comparison of the incident's detection latency against the detection commitment in force at the time. **The element exists as item 2's final clause, it is a checked element, it is the subject of a published defence question — *whose commitment was it, and did it hold?* — and phase 5 part one reports it against each student's [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) commitment and [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) contract.** The obligation is discharged.

**Instrumentation rule 2 is applied rather than cited.** Phase 5 part one is **the third occasion on which the same latency measurement has been taken** — [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) phase 7, [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) step 4, and here. Rule 2 states that where the same elicitation runs at three or more days, responses measure familiarity with the instrument as much as the quality being probed, that such protocols remain valid as interventions, and that **their rates must not be compared across occasions.** The three figures are therefore shown side by side as separate measurements, the constraint is stated to the cohort in phase 5 and again at phase 8, and no trend is asserted. [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) phase 8 flagged the threshold in advance, at the second administration, which is the only reason it is being honoured rather than discovered afterwards.

**Older forward links.** [WP-068](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-068.md) §6 named this day as one where detection latency is the first question asked and §7 recorded the committed latency as a promise held to here; **that is item 2 and phase 5 part one.** [WP-069](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §3.2 excluded incident command and on-call structure and named them "[S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md)'s and the substrate's". **Only the second half is taken.** The on-call structure is present as the record, the narrator and the phase 7 opener; incident command as a practice is the substrate rota's, continuously, and is not taught on this day. The boundary is stated at phase 3 rather than left implicit, so that the deferral is not silently over-discharged.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments, both responding outside this module.

- **The pooled phase 1 item 3 list** — what this cohort wanted to know and the substrate does not record — responds at [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md), whose safety-case claims must be selected against it, so that the case rests on quantities the substrate can evidence and its gaps are the cohort's own findings rather than the designer's.
- **The item 8 handover** responds at [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md), whose artefact must carry the disposition of each recommendation made here: implemented, declined with a reason, or never answered.

**[WP-087](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-087.md) §7 and [WP-089](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-089.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

Programme instrumentation: the item 1 account-survival rate; the item 2 to item 3 attribution transition, joined to [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md); the pooled record-gap list; **the item 4 frontier pair and the corrected-versus-surfaced agent-entry counts**; assessor divergence; whether the defence moved the provisional level; and the psychological-safety series, **tenth administration**, the ninth having been taken at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) without a number.

ABET and CS2023 contribution: PO4 and PO5, with PO3 at item 8. The [Outcomes and Accreditation Map](/wiki/program/01-Outcomes-and-Accreditation-Map.md) names this day as secondary evidence for PO5, evidenced principally by the substrate rota and incident record. Dispositions: **integrity**, at item 1's inference marking and at item 5, where the honest reconstruction is charitable to somebody the outcome makes look wrong; **humility**, at item 3, where the honest answer may be that the record does not decide; **reliability**, at phase 5, where a promise made in week 70 meets an incident.

**What this day does not license anyone to conclude.** It does not license a claim that the student can respond to an incident — nothing here is performed under incident conditions. It does not license a claim about the incident's cause; the specification checks the discipline and not the conclusion. It licenses a claim that the student can produce a sourced account of an event they did not control, state what it does not establish, decline an attribution the evidence does not support, describe human decisions without attributing fault, and hand three changes with owners to somebody who was not there. **The last of those is EA-2's core act and it is the only part of EA-2 this occasion touches** — what transfers, to whom, with what warranty is assessed at PE390 and CAP390 across a running system, and this is one account of one incident.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: this is a rehearsal of EA-2 that does not hand over a system.**

At its strongest: EA-2 is *hand over a running system to a receiving organisation* — what transfers, to whom, with what warranty, what the receiving organisation must be able to do without you, and what the residual risk is. **This day hands over an account of something that already happened, plus three recommendations.** The system is not transferred, the receiving organisation is the one the student is already in, and the residual risk is not stated anywhere in the artefact. Calling it EA-2's first rehearsal inflates what the occasion is.

The rebuttal is that the day rehearses one component of EA-2 and names it: **producing something a receiving party can act on without you.** Item 8's three recommendations go to an implementer who was not in the incident, each carries an owner and a cost, and the accountable owner's phase 4 question forces a live negotiation of priority under a constraint the receiver imposes — which is the shape of a handover rather than the shape of a report. The [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) lists EA-2's rehearsals as S086, S088 and S089; **this day is not among them**, and the entrustment statement says it is a precursor rather than a rehearsal in that list's sense.

What is conceded is that the *warranty* — the element that makes class 6 what it is — is thinner here than the module's own framing claims. [M16](/wiki/modules/M16.md) says the warranty on this artefact is which parts of the account the student will stand behind, and the specification operationalises that as inference marking and discriminating evidence. **That is a warranty on the account's epistemics and not on the system's behaviour**, which is what EA-2 means by the word. The design accepts the narrower reading and has labelled the occasion accordingly.

**Objection 2: the error-clinic firewall covers phase 7 and not phase 3, and the day requires an honest account of error to be written for assessors.**

At its strongest, and this is the day's central problem. Rater discipline rule 5 is unconditional: nothing surfaced by a student in a seminar day's error clinic may enter an assessment judgement about that student, ever. **It covers the error clinic.** Phase 3 is not an error clinic; it is an assessed artefact, read by two assessors who are forming an entrustment level, and for any student who was on the rota during the incident **it contains their own decisions, reconstructed by themselves, under items 4 and 5.**

The programme's entire position on accurate error disclosure is that it requires the person disclosing to be certain the disclosure cannot be used. [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) established that the norm is created by demonstrating the firewall holds when it is most likely not to, and [WP-010](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-010.md)'s whole argument is that a single breach kills the mechanism for the cohort. **Here the design has built an occasion in which a student's account of their own error is not merely visible to assessors but is the assessed object** — and it has done so in a module whose phase 6 point 2 tells students that a review naming a person changes nothing. The student is being asked to write a blameless account that includes themselves, for people who will grade it.

Three things mitigate and none resolves. **The specification does not assess the conclusion or the conduct**, only the discipline: a student whose own decision was wrong loses nothing by describing it accurately, provided the description meets item 5's form. **Item 4's rewriting applies to the student as much as to anyone**, so the honest account of one's own error is already required to be stated as a system property. And **the level is formed from the account and the defence and from nothing else**, with phases 6 and 7 firewalled as usual.

**The cheap fix is to exclude students from analysing incidents they were involved in.** It is obvious, it is what a cautious design would do, and **it is declined.** The reason is that the incidents the cohort was on call for are the ones with a complete record, a reachable narrator and a genuine stake; excluding them would leave incidents nobody in the room touched, which is an exercise, and it would produce the outcome the [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) withdrawal commitment refuses — a student who can perform forensics.

**This objection is not answered.** The design has created a second, narrower channel through which a student's own error reaches an assessor, has declined the exclusion that would close it, has not extended the firewall to cover it because extending it would make the assessed artefact unreadable, and has recorded the choice. **It is the same shape as the concession [WP-070](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §8 objection 2 made** — a design that has repaired a disclosure problem for the substrate's users and has not repaired the corresponding one for its own students — and this is its second instance. Recording it here is not a remedy.

**Objection 3: item 5 has no marker distinguishing a reconstructed information state from an evidenced one.**

At its strongest: item 5 requires, for each decision made during the incident, what made it reasonable given what the decider knew at that moment. **Sometimes that is evidenced** — the decider wrote it in the channel, or is in the room and said so. **Sometimes it is the student's charitable reconstruction of somebody's mental state**, and the account's form makes the two look identical. Item 1 requires inferences in the *timeline* to be marked; item 5 has no equivalent requirement, and the entries most likely to be wrong are the ones about what a person believed.

That is a genuine gap in the form and it is conceded without qualification. **The fix is one clause — apply item 1's marking rule to item 5 — and it is not in this design.** It is not declined for a reason; it was not noticed until this paper was written, and adding it now would be a change to an assessed specification made without the phase-time analysis the other items received. It is recorded here so that the omission is legible, and §3.2's scope statement names the unreachable-participant case as one where the form is unsafe, which is the same gap seen from the outside.

**Objection 4: an entrustment judgement about reasoning under pressure, performed with a tool that reasons.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the practical turn creates on the one day where it costs most. The judgement is on *conduct a blameless forensic analysis of a real incident and report it*. **A capable agent can produce a timeline, propose an attribution, describe a mechanism and draft recommendations**, and a student who is a competent director of that tool and an incompetent forensic analyst may produce an artefact the assessors cannot distinguish from a good one. The programme has an unassisted-work provision precisely because it knows this, and it did not use it here. **A sceptical reader is entitled to ask what exactly is being entrusted.**

The rebuttal has three parts and the design believes the third is the one that holds. First, **the assessed elements are not producible by a tool that has not read the record with the student.** Item 5 requires a reconstruction of what a decider knew at a moment, verified in the room by the person who was there; item 3 requires the discriminating evidence for a position the record may not support; item 8 requires three recommendations with named owners inside the substrate's own organisation. **An agent can draft all three and cannot own any of them**, and the defence is where ownership is tested. Second, **phase 4's agent question is asked about entries the assessors choose**, so the discrimination is between a student who verified and a student who accepted, which is the discrimination the day exists to make. Third, **the level is formed from the account before the defence and the defence can lower it**, so a fluent artefact that dissolves under questioning is recorded as exactly that.

**What is conceded is that the day has no way to measure how much the agent contributed, and does not want one.** The prompt and intervention records are self-reported and could be written after the fact; the design says so and assesses none of them. **The honest position is that this occasion measures whether a student can stand behind an account, not whether they produced it unaided**, and that those are different claims. The programme's claim is the first, stated in the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md): a graduate can architect and ship a working system principally by prompting, **and can say what the result does badly.** This day assesses the second half of that sentence and the assessors are told so in the brief.

**The cheap fix is to run one item unassisted — the mechanism at item 4, twenty minutes, no tool — and it is declined.** It would produce a clean comparison, it is exactly the structure [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md), [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) already use, and it is declined because **an assessed occasion is the wrong place to take that measurement**: a student writing under a tool restriction inside an entrustment judgement is being measured on two things at once, and the unassisted provision exists specifically as a non-assessed comparison. **[S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) is nine weeks away and is where this belongs.** That is a real answer and it is also a deferral, and this paper is putting [WP-090](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md) on notice that the module's most agent-dependent artefact has no unassisted comparison anywhere.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Fewer than a third of students will write *undecided* at item 3, and the students who do will have a higher mean level than those who decided, because the discriminating-evidence requirement is harder to satisfy for a decided position than for an undecided one. *Instrument:* item 3 positions coded, joined to awarded levels. **The second half is the claim.** If undecided positions cluster at the bottom, the failure condition is being read as a safe option rather than as a demanding one, and the specification's wording has produced the opposite of its intent.

**Prediction 2.** More than half of first-draft mechanisms will contain at least one unrewritten "X did Y", and the on-call member's phase 4 question will surface a further one in a majority of the remainder. *Instrument:* accounts coded for person-as-actor constructions before the defence, and the defence transcripts coded for constructions surfaced there. **The gap between the two counts is the measure of how mechanical the rewriting was**, and a small gap means students are applying the rule and not the reason.

**Prediction 3.** The cohort's [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md)-to-today attribution transition will run toward *undecided* rather than toward the opposite classification. *Instrument:* the phase 5 part two transition table. **If students who misclassified an accident as an attack three weeks ago now classify confidently in the other direction, the module has taught a correction rather than a discrimination**, which is a worse outcome than the original error and would require [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s T4 to be rebuilt as something other than a reversal.

**Prediction 4.** At [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md), the largest category for this day's recommendations will be *never answered* rather than implemented or declined. *Instrument:* the [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) disposition element, coded three ways. **This is a prediction about the organisation and not about the students**, and if it holds it is the most important finding either day produces: a handover whose recommendations are neither taken nor refused is the ordinary fate of a post-incident report, and no part of this programme currently teaches what to do about it.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of students will use an agent for at least the timeline, and **the number of unverified agent-supplied entries surfaced at phase 4 will exceed the number recorded by students in their own intervention records.** *Instrument:* intervention records coded for corrections to agent-supplied timeline entries, against the defence transcripts coded for entries the assessors surfaced. **The gap is the measure of whether the sourcing discipline was applied or performed.** If the two counts are close, the module's four preceding intervention records have installed the habit and the day should say so; if the defence surfaces substantially more, the discipline is being written rather than run, and item 1's marking rule needs an artefact-side check that the day does not currently have.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That the competence being judged is not blamelessness, which you already hold, but the discipline of an account: which parts are sourced, which are inference, and what the record cannot settle. Naming a person as the cause is treated as a failed rewriting rather than as a failure of values, because a value cannot be applied at speed and a rewriting can.

**What the evidence licenses.** Entrustment as a decision, ordinal levels and mandatory narrative are **class 1**, grounded in [research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md) and [research/08-assessment-epas/govaerts-narrative-assessment-literature.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/govaerts-narrative-assessment-literature.md). Blameless review, hindsight effects and the tendency of fluent models to bridge gaps in a source are **class 2** — direction only, and **no rate is asserted at which an agent fabricates.** The record's shape is **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026, with no default retention stated. **The eight-item form, the five failure conditions and the decision to permit an agent inside the assessed hour are class 3.**

**What to do with it.**

- Mark inference. A sparse, well-marked timeline passes and a dense unmarked one does not.
- Treat an agent-supplied entry as an unsupported inference until you have put it against the record yourself. **Provenance is not correctness** — see the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md).
- Write *undecided*, with the evidence that would decide it, when that is the truth. It passes at the same levels as a decided attribution.
- An alert's absence in this record licenses nothing, because nobody has published what it detects.

**The concession worth remembering.** The programme's error-clinic firewall covers phase 7 and not phase 3. If you were on the rota during this incident, your own account of your own error is the assessed object, read by two assessors forming a level, in a module whose content is that this cannot be done honestly. The exclusion that would close it was declined because it would leave only incidents nobody in the room touched. **The paper says plainly that recording this is not a remedy.**
