# WP-080 — A Transfer the Recipient Signs

**Seminar day:** [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) · **Module:** [M16](/wiki/modules/M16.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md)

> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Entrustment decision** | A judgement about the supervision a person requires for a defined activity, recorded as an ordinal level with a mandatory narrative, formed from accumulated observation |
> | **Acceptance record** | What the recipient signs: what is accepted, by whom, with what authority, on what date, and what is explicitly not accepted |

## In plain terms

This occasion asks one question, and it is a question about the learner rather than about their handover: **can they be trusted to decide when a running system may be left with somebody else?**

That is not checkable against a list, which is why this is an entrustment decision and not a specification check. It is formed from what assessors saw across the whole stage — the manifest that recorded an unowned line or hid one, the runbook execution the learner sat silent through or spoke during, the obligation they recorded as unassigned or tidied away, the rehearsal they rescued or did not — and today's defence is used to test that reading rather than to form it.

The session's own content is a distinction the whole industry blurs. **A signature records that somebody was willing to accept something. Only observation records whether they can operate it.** A handover with a signature and no rehearsal has transferred blame, tidily, with a date on it. The acceptance record here therefore carries a section stating what the signatory is *not* accepting: the unowned lines, the dependencies documented rather than disposed, the acts the rehearsal showed they cannot yet perform. That section is the one that makes the signature honest, and it is the one every commercial pressure in the room argues against including.

## Abstract

This paper defends closing the transfer stage with an entrustment decision rather than a specification check, and defends the narrowness of what such a decision may claim. The argument is that the substantive property of a handover — whether the decision to leave was sound — is not checkable from an artefact, is exactly what accumulated observation across a module can support, and is destroyed by being reduced to a list. The occasion produces a transfer decision, an acceptance record with an explicit not-accepting section, and a live defence against a challenger arguing the recipient is not ready. The level is formed by more than one assessor across more than one occasion, with independent written judgements before deliberation and a mandatory narrative naming a specific observed action. The paper concedes that every pressure making this decision hard in practice is absent or simulated here, that no source behind this programme reports whether any delivered system survived its delivery team, and that an entrustment level recorded on this basis is a description of observed reasoning and not a prediction of behaviour.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

The decision to leave is made under a distinctive pressure profile, and none of its components is present in a classroom.

The date is fixed and somebody else's revenue depends on it. The engineer is already assigned to the next engagement. The sponsor wants the matter finished and reads hesitation as a failure of confidence. The recipient will be blamed whether they accept or refuse, so they will accept. The delivery organisation is frequently rewarded for a continued relationship, which corrupts the incentive in the opposite direction and just as effectively. **Every one of those pressures pushes towards a decision made on the calendar rather than on the recipient's state**, and none of them is illegitimate.

The second problem is evidential. The person best placed to judge readiness is the person with the strongest interest in the answer, and their evidence is a set of artefacts they wrote themselves. The only external check available is the rehearsal, and it is uncomfortable, short and easy to run badly.

The occasion's job is to make the reasoning visible: what was observed, what was inferred, what argued against the decision, and whether the learner went looking for it.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why entrustment rather than specification.** A specification checks whether an artefact has stated properties, which is defensible when the properties are the point. Here the property of interest is a judgement, exercised once, about somebody else's readiness. Reducing it to a list would produce a checkable proxy and would certify the proxy. The programme's position is that where the question is *how much supervision would this person need*, the answer should be recorded as a supervision level with a narrative, and never as a score.

**3.2 Why multiple observers and multiple occasions.** Rater variance in judgements of this class is large, and a single observation by a single assessor is not defensible. Independent written judgement precedes deliberation, because assessors reaching the same level frequently reason from incompatible premises, and deliberating first destroys the evidence that this has happened. Divergence beyond one sub-level is investigated rather than averaged. **Direction and mechanism only; no reliability figure is asserted here or anywhere in this wiki.**

**3.3 Why the artefact forms the level and the defence tests it.** An artefact supplies evidence and a conversation supplies narrative, and assessors given both reason from the narrative. Forming the impression in conversation and reading the artefact for support is where the variance lives.

**3.4 Why a prospective decision needs a rejected alternative.** This occasion records an intention rather than a completed act, which weakens §3.3 — the artefact contains what the learner means to do, and the reasoning lives in the conversation. **The element that restores it is a named alternative not taken, with its reason:** the owner not chosen, the condition not imposed, the date not moved. That is why the occasion's final question is what the learner would have needed to see to decide the other way.

**3.5 Why signature and capability are separated explicitly.** A signature is obtainable. In the supplied situation it is obtainable this morning, from a willing party with no authority, and taking it produces a complete-looking handover. **The separation is the session's substantive teaching claim and it is this design's own argument**, resting on the observation that acceptance and ability have different producers: acceptance is produced by a conversation and ability by practice.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**The challenger argues from the learner's own rehearsal record.** This is the design's central device. The learner produced the evidence against themselves at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), and the occasion turns it into the opposing case — so a learner who ran a weak rehearsal is not rewarded with an easy defence but arrives with nothing to reason from, which is visible.

**The easy exit is offered and its acceptance is the observation.** A signature today, from the willing under-authorised team leader, with no conditions attached, closing the engagement on the date. **This is what happens in practice and it is not a trick.** A learner who takes it and can say why — because the alternative was worse, because the condition they wanted was unobtainable, because they judged the gap recoverable — is reasoning. A learner who takes it because it was offered is not, and the difference is audible.

**Both decisions pass and both can fail.** Leave, leave with a named condition, and do not leave yet are treated identically; what is read is the grounds. **The refusal is checked for cost**, because *not yet* with no condition, no observer and no consequence for the date is not a decision, it is a deferral wearing a decision's clothes.

**The final question is fixed and known in advance:** what would you have needed to see to decide the other way? Publishing it does not weaken it. A learner who has prepared an answer has done the thinking the question exists to provoke.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The signature as proof.** *Countermeasure:* the acceptance record's not-accepting section, and the challenger's instruction to offer a signature that would make the section unnecessary.

**The suppressed rehearsal.** The observed incapacity does not reach the acceptance record. *Countermeasure:* assessors hold both records, and the fit between them is what §7 reads for.

**Departure dressed as transfer.** Every reason concerns the delivery side. *Countermeasure:* the defence is listened to for a single sentence about what the recipient can do, and its absence is the finding.

**The costless refusal.** *Countermeasure:* the condition must name an observer and a date, which is the same test applied to a disqualifying condition at [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md), reappearing at the other end of the arc.

**Conceding under pressure.** *Countermeasure:* none available. It is read directly as evidence about the learner, which is what this occasion is for.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**An entrustment decision. No checklist, and nothing in this section is one.**

**The judgement.** Can this learner be trusted to decide when a running system may be left with somebody else?

**How the level is formed.** From accumulated observation across [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md)–[S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), by more than one assessor, on more than one occasion. Each assessor records level and narrative independently before any discussion. The provisional level comes from the artefacts; the defence confirms, revises or overturns it. Divergence of more than one sub-level is investigated by re-observation where possible, never resolved by averaging. The scale and the rater rules are at [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md).

**What assessors read for**, none of which is a threshold:

- Fit between the learner's account of readiness and what was observable in the rehearsal record, **in either direction** — overstating and understating are the same defect, and a learner who declared a capable recipient incapable has misread the evidence exactly as much as one who did the reverse.
- Whether the evidence against the decision was presented by the learner before it was produced by the challenger.
- Whether the line between a signature and a demonstrated capability held under pressure from somebody who wanted it blurred.
- Whether a decision to leave was grounded in the recipient's state rather than in the delivery organisation's calendar.
- Whether the learner could name what would have changed their mind, and whether they had looked for it.

**Narrative is mandatory and must name a specific observed action.** A level recorded without one is rejected by moderation rather than by the assessor. A conforming narrative states what the learner was asked to do, what they did including at least one specific observed action, what the assessor inferred, and what would have to change for the next level.

**The firewall.** Nothing the learner surfaced about their own error in a rehearsal debrief may be used against them here. Ever. A learner who reports intervening outside their written condition has produced the most useful evidence available in [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), and a programme that penalises the report will not receive it again. **If this is breached once, the mechanism is dead for the cohort.**

**What the level does not record.** Whether the handover was good. Whether the recipient was capable. Whether the transfer would have succeeded. **It records that this learner, observed on these occasions, reasoned about leaving in a way that would be defensible to a colleague who was not present.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: an entrustment level formed on simulated occasions is a judgement about how somebody performs in a programme, presented in the vocabulary of professional supervision.**

At its strongest: entrustment scales were developed where a supervisor watches a trainee do the real activity, with a real consequence, repeatedly, over a long period. Here the activity is fictional, the recipient is a role-player, the consequence is a mark, and the occasions number fewer than a working fortnight. Borrowing the vocabulary imports a credibility the setting has not earned, and the level will be read by a future employer as though it meant what it means in the settings the scale came from.

**This is conceded without qualification.** The mitigations are real and insufficient: the level is coarse, the narrative is mandatory and names an observed action, the programme publishes that rater variance in this class of judgement is large, and this paper states the limit in the section a reader is most likely to reach. **The fix that was considered and declined** was to abandon entrustment vocabulary and record a pass against a specification, as [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md) does. It was rejected because the property this occasion is about is not checkable from an artefact, and a specification would certify the proxy while silently discarding the question — which is a worse error and a less visible one. The rejection trades an honest overreach for a dishonest simplification, and the objection is entitled to say that both are bad.

**Objection 2: the separation of signature from capability is unrealistic, because in commercial practice the signature is the transfer.**

At its strongest: acceptance is a contractual event with legal and financial consequences, agreed between organisations, and it discharges obligations regardless of anybody's ability. A programme teaching that the signature is not the point is teaching a moral position that its learners will be unable to act on, since neither party's commercial function will accept a handover document whose principal feature is a section listing what has not been accepted.

The rebuttal is narrow and concedes the frame. The session does not claim the signature is unimportant; it claims that **the signature and the capability are different facts, and that recording only one of them produces a document whose accuracy nobody can check later.** The not-accepting section is unusual and will be resisted, and the session's defence of it is that a recipient who signs without it is signing something untrue, which is a poor foundation for the relationship the delivery organisation says it wants. **Whether any commercial function would accept such a document is untested, and no source behind this programme reports one being attempted.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A substantial share of learners will accept the challenger's easy exit, and most of those will describe the occasion afterwards as having gone well. *Instrument:* the acceptance record's signatory against the post-occasion self-report.

**Prediction 2.** Learners will more often present evidence against their own decision when their rehearsal produced a recorded incapacity than when it did not. *Instrument:* the defence transcript, coded for self-presented counter-evidence, against the rehearsal record's incapacity field.

**Prediction 3.** Decisions of *do not leave yet* will more often lack a named observer and date than decisions to leave will lack a stated ground. *Instrument:* both decision types coded for completeness against their respective standards.

**Prediction 4.** Independent assessor levels will diverge by more than one sub-level on a noticeable share of learners, and divergence will concentrate on learners who refused to leave. *Instrument:* the paired independent judgements before deliberation, with divergence cross-tabulated against decision type. **If divergence concentrates on refusals, the occasion is under-specified for that decision** and the standard for a refusal needs writing, not the scale.

**None of these has been measured.** No cohort has run this occasion, and the programme's own position is that most predictions in this wiki will never be measured at all.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a signature records willingness and only observation records ability; that saying *not yet* is a decision only when it has a cost; and that presenting the evidence against your own decision is worth more than defending it well.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The rater discipline is direction and mechanism from assessment literature. The entrustment scale is borrowed from settings this one does not resemble. Everything about the occasion's design is this design's argument.

**The concession worth remembering.** This occasion records how somebody reasoned about leaving, on a fictional engagement, under simulated pressure, watched by people who could not be harmed by the answer. That is worth having and it is not what it will look like on a transcript.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That rater variance in judgements of this class is large and that a single observation by a single assessor is not defensible; that assessors given both narrative and score reason from the narrative; that declaring a change complete is itself a mechanism of regression, and that people who never agreed with a change wait for that moment. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude, rate or timescale is asserted, and none may be inherited from the sources named above.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The separation of signature from demonstrated capability; the not-accepting section of the acceptance record; the treatment of all three decisions as equally passable with only the grounds assessed; the requirement that a refusal carry a cost, an observer and a date; the requirement for a rejected alternative on a prospective decision; the challenger's brief and the easy exit; the fixed final question; the debrief firewall; the application of an entrustment scale to simulated occasions at this frequency; and all four predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** **This session has no platform anchor and relies on no vendor documentation.** The occasion assesses a judgement, which no product surface bears on. **The silence worth recording, and it is the largest one in this stage:** no vendor documentation known to this programme defines what it means for a customer to be able to operate a delivered system, publishes any criterion for acceptance of an AI-bearing system, or specifies what a delivering organisation must observe before withdrawing. Governance material published across the industry defines what must be assessed before a system is deployed and stops at deployment. **The interval this whole stage is about — between a system going live and the people who built it leaving — is undocumented by every party with an interest in it.** If any vendor publishes a withdrawal or acceptance criterion, this occasion acquires an anchor and this paper records its version and what it still omits.
