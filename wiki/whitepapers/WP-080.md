# WP-080 — A Transfer the Recipient Signs

**Seminar day:** [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) · **Module:** [M16](/wiki/modules/M16.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 5
**Platform anchor:** The recipient executing the whole stack unaided — deployment, gateway policy, evaluation and posture check — while the author may not speak

> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Entrustment decision** | A judgement about the supervision a person requires for a defined activity, recorded as an ordinal level with a mandatory narrative, formed from accumulated observation |
> | **Acceptance record** | What the recipient signs: what is accepted, by whom, with what authority, on what date, and what is explicitly not accepted |
> | **Specification check** | Assessment against published closed criteria. This occasion is not one |
> | **Ad-hoc versus summative entrustment** | A continuous informal delegation judgement, versus a certification-like decision requiring multiple observers, multiple observations and several evidence types |
> | **Supervision sub-level** | The resolution below the five-point scale — all findings double-checked, key findings double-checked, distant with prompt review — where a real support taper lives |
> | **Entrustment-based discussion** | A short post-activity conversation that must include what-if questions, because retrospective explanation is evidence only about the cases the person met |
> | **Rater idiosyncrasy** | The share of variance in a judgement attributable to who is judging rather than to what is being judged |
> | **Person schema** | The mental model of the individual an assessor begins constructing during the first moments of observation |

## In plain terms

This occasion asks one question, and it is a question about the learner rather than about their handover: **can they be trusted to decide when a running system may be left with somebody else?**

That is not checkable against a list, which is why this is an entrustment decision and not a specification check. It is formed from what assessors saw across the whole stage — the manifest that recorded an unowned line or hid one, the runbook execution the learner sat silent through or spoke during, the obligation they recorded as unassigned or tidied away, the rehearsal they rescued or did not — and today's defence is used to test that reading rather than to form it.

The session's own content is a distinction the whole industry blurs. **A signature records that somebody was willing to accept something. Only observation records whether they can operate it.** Put more sharply, and this is the sentence the occasion turns on: **the recipient signs because signing is a claim they are making, not a receipt you are collecting.** A claim can be wrong, which means it has to be specific enough to be wrong. A receipt cannot, which is why a handover with a signature and no rehearsal has transferred blame, tidily, with a date on it.

The acceptance record therefore carries a section stating what the signatory is *not* accepting: the unowned lines, the dependencies documented rather than disposed, the acts the rehearsal showed they cannot yet perform. **That section is the one that makes the signature honest, and it is the one every commercial pressure in the room argues against including.**

And the demonstration is the whole stack, in silence. The recipient deploys, changes a gateway policy, runs the evaluation and reads the posture report, on their own credentials, while the author may not speak — because a recipient who can operate every control and explain none of them is invisible in a document review and unmissable after four questions.

## Abstract

This paper defends closing the transfer stage with an entrustment decision rather than a specification check, and defends the narrowness of what such a decision may claim.

**The first argument is that the property of interest is not checkable from an artefact.** Whether the decision to leave was sound is a judgement exercised once, under a pressure profile none of whose components is present in a classroom. Reducing it to a list would produce a checkable proxy and would certify the proxy. The entrustment framework supplies the alternative: an ordinal supervision level with a mandatory narrative, recorded about a bounded activity rather than about a person, revisable in both directions.

**The second argument is why the record is narrative rather than a mark, and here the paper's warrant is unusually direct.** When assessors read real competency portfolios while thinking aloud, they relied on narrative feedback to form their judgements and used grades "merely… to confirm impressions based on narratives". The same research group found rater idiosyncrasy substantial and **unrelated to rater expertise**, found assessors anchoring early and elaborating rather than revising, and found assessors reaching identical pass–fail outcomes by incompatible reasoning. **A mark would conceal exactly the information the decision consists of**, and the recommended remedy — group deliberation with substantiated judgements — is the reason this occasion requires independent written levels before any discussion.

**The third argument concerns what today's evidence licenses, and the answer is less than the occasion looks like it produces.** The framework's summative standard requires agreement between multiple observers, multiple observations and four evidence types. This occasion's demonstration supplies one observer, one occasion and two evidence types, and the acceptance record is required to say so on its face. **An honest ad-hoc judgement stated as ad-hoc is worth more than a summative claim the evidence cannot carry.**

The platform anchor supplies the one thing the artefacts cannot. Running deployment, gateway policy, evaluation and posture check end to end in silence exposes the access wall in the first minutes and the comprehension gap in the four questions afterwards — **and comprehension debt is not visible in any document by construction, because the document is the thing that passed.**

The paper concedes that every pressure making this decision hard in practice is absent or simulated; that the entrustment scale is borrowed from a clinical setting with patients, legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint; that its rater discipline comes from two studies by one group at one institution; that the oral-defence pattern it adopts is well-tested practice with no published effectiveness data; and that no source behind this programme reports whether any delivered system survived its delivery team.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

The decision to leave is made under a distinctive pressure profile, and none of its components is present in a classroom.

The date is fixed and somebody else's revenue depends on it. The engineer is already assigned to the next engagement. The sponsor wants the matter finished and reads hesitation as a failure of confidence. The recipient will be blamed whether they accept or refuse, so they will accept. The delivery organisation is frequently rewarded for a continued relationship, which corrupts the incentive in the opposite direction and just as effectively. **Every one of those pressures pushes towards a decision made on the calendar rather than on the recipient's state**, and none of them is illegitimate.

The second problem is evidential. The person best placed to judge readiness is the person with the strongest interest in the answer, and their evidence is a set of artefacts they wrote themselves. The only external check available is the rehearsal, and it is uncomfortable, short and easy to run badly.

The third problem is the one the platform anchor exists to expose, and it is the one a document review cannot reach. **A recipient can operate a system competently and understand none of it**, and every artefact in the handover will look correct. The runbook was followed. The deployment succeeded. The evaluation returned a number. **Comprehension debt is invisible in exactly the medium a handover is conducted in**, and the only instrument that finds it is a question asked after an act, in a room, with nobody prompting.

The occasion's job is to make the reasoning visible: what was observed, what was inferred, what argued against the decision, and whether the learner went looking for it.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why entrustment rather than specification, and what the framework actually supplies.**

A specification checks whether an artefact has stated properties, which is defensible when the properties are the point. Here the property of interest is a judgement, exercised once, about somebody else's readiness. **Reducing it to a list would produce a checkable proxy and would certify the proxy.**

The framework that supplies the alternative is entrustment ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)), and three of its properties are load-bearing here.

**The unit is an activity, not a person.** An entrustable professional activity is bounded work with a beginning and an end that a supervisor could delegate whole. The judgement answers *would I let them do this unsupervised, and what would I still want to see?*, and it is revisable in both directions.

**The scale has sub-levels, and the sub-levels are the handover.** Five levels run from observing to supervising others; level 3 splits into all findings double-checked, key findings double-checked, and a distant supervisor with prompt review; level 4 into remote monitoring and none. **A support taper is a sequence of sub-levels with dates against them.** A record that reports only *supervised* or *unsupervised* has discarded the distinctions the arrangement consists of, which is why this occasion requires sub-level resolution on the acceptance record.

**The summative standard is higher than this occasion meets, and the shortfall must be declared.** A summative decision "has the nature of certification" and **"should include an agreement between multiple observers that this entrustment is justified, to avoid subjective bias, and multiple observations"**, drawing on four evidence types: direct observation, longitudinal observation, case-based discussion, and product evaluation. Ad-hoc entrustment is the continuous informal judgement. **This occasion's demonstration is one observer, one occasion, two evidence types — ad-hoc — and the record says so.**

Two further items from the same source shape the defence directly. The **five drivers of an ad-hoc judgement** are perceived learner features, the supervisor's own propensity to delegate, activity complexity, context, and the supervisor–learner relationship — **three of which are about the assessor rather than the assessed**, and all of which will operate on this occasion's assessors whether or not they are named in a calibration briefing. And the **entrustment-based discussion** is defined as a short post-activity conversation that "should probe the student's deep understanding of what was done, but should include 'what-if' questions" — with the rationale that "an entrustment decision implies the acceptance of risk, as it can never be fully predicted what the student will encounter." **Retrospective walkthrough is not entrustment evidence**; counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases the person did not meet.

The primer also records that **entrustment expires** through disuse, which is why the acceptance record carries an expiry per act.

**Its limits, stated wherever it is used:** a clinical model with patients, legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint, so a course must construct its stakes artificially and the construction is the hardest part of the transfer; guidance from the concept's originator reporting adoption rather than effectiveness; the three professionalism characteristics it names are defined but not operationalised into observable indicators, and that work must be done locally; and the full evidence standard is expensive enough that **any course adopts a reduced version and should say honestly that it is reduced.**

**3.2 Why the record is narrative and never a score.**

This is the section with the best evidence in the paper, and it is evidence about assessors rather than about learners ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)).

Assessors reading real competency portfolios while thinking aloud **"mainly relied on narrative feedback to inform their judgment, because this provided meaningful and detailed information about the student's development, strengths and weaknesses, as well as specific suggestions for improvement… Grades and qualifications were merely used to confirm impressions based on narratives."** The prose was the evidence. The numbers checked a conclusion already formed.

**A scoring scheme would therefore not remove the narrative reasoning; it would hide it.** That is the whole argument for the mandatory narrative, and it is stronger than a preference for rich feedback.

Three further findings from the same group shape the rater rules.

**Idiosyncrasy is structural.** The companion study of workplace-based assessment reports that idiosyncratic rater effects account for a substantial share of the variance in performance ratings, and — the finding that forecloses the obvious remedy — **"levels of rater idiosyncrasy were substantial and unrelated to rater expertise."** Attempts to standardise instruments and train raters "have met with mixed success at best." **More experienced assessors are not more consistent assessors**, so seniority is not a substitute for a second reader.

**Anchoring is real and additional evidence does not undo it.** Assessors "were inclined to stick to their initial judgments even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence", and their final judgements were more elaborate than their preliminary ones but **not substantially different**. And **"most raters started to develop person schemas the moment they began to observe trainee performance"** — the impression begins forming in the first moments and the rest of the observation elaborates it.

**Agreement conceals divergence.** Assessors "reached similar final (pass–fail) judgments of students' professional competence" while differing "in their information-processing approaches and the reasoning behind their judgments", driven by which evidence they trusted, what they privately took competence to be, and what they inferred from the same observation. Their private performance theories **"tended to deviate from the formal assessment criteria."**

The recommended remedy is not a better rubric: high-stakes decisions **"should not be made individually, but should result from group discussions"**, and assessors should **"thoroughly substantiate their judgments"**, because substantiation is what makes divergent reasoning discussable.

**This yields the occasion's three rater rules directly.** More than one assessor, because idiosyncrasy is unrelated to expertise. **Independent written levels before any discussion**, because assessors anchor and because deliberation-first converts two judgements into whichever was spoken first. And divergence beyond one sub-level investigated rather than averaged, because averaging discards the finding that the reasoning differed.

**Its limits:** two studies from one research group at one institution; think-aloud methods cannot capture fast or automatic processing and risk participants confabulating explanations for the researcher; the portfolio study used constructed mock portfolios with six assessors per portfolio; assessors judged on the portfolio alone whereas real mentors also know the learner; and several of the group's most-cited works are paywalled and were not consulted. **The magnitudes it reports are recorded in the research layer and are not asserted here or anywhere in this wiki.**

**3.3 Why the defence is oral, and what that buys.**

Once the assessment is a conversation, the artefact stops being the evidence — and the clearest publicly documented implementation of that principle in a computing course draws the practical consequence explicitly ([research/08-assessment-epas/cmu-oral-defense-and-token-economy-patterns.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/cmu-oral-defense-and-token-economy-patterns.md)). Its labs are graded in-session by "showing source code, demoing executions, and verbally answering a few questions to demonstrate your understanding", and collaboration on them is permitted **including outright copying**, "because you will have to present and explain your solution to the TA on your own."

**That inversion is what makes this programme's permissive tool policy coherent.** The same course states it is "open to using AI tools" and recommends them, with exactly two prohibitions — examinations, and **"live conversations with course staff"**, where using any form of AI to generate answers is cheating. This occasion adopts the same boundary for the same reason, and it is the only place in the programme where a tool is prohibited.

The source also supplies the warning that connects tool use to the defence: **"Using code generation tools without understanding the generated code will likely create challenges when answering TA questions."** **The defence is the only instrument in this stage that surfaces comprehension debt**, which §1 identifies as invisible in every other medium a handover uses.

**Its limits:** course policy documentation from an offering that has run for several years, not a study of assessment. **No effectiveness data is published** — no grade distributions, no comparison against the exam-based version it replaced, and no measurement of whether oral verification detects understanding that written work misses. Treat it as well-tested practice and not as an evaluated intervention.

**3.4 Why both parties must be able to say no, and why the occasion cannot secure it.**

The acceptance record only works if the signatory can decline to sign, and what is being asked of them — stating in front of their supplier that there are things they cannot do — is an interpersonal risk before it is an administrative act. The construct is precise about what people do with such risks: "No one wakes up in the morning excited to go to work and look ignorant, incompetent, or disruptive… Don't want to look incompetent? Don't admit to mistakes or weaknesses" ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)). **A recipient who cannot afford to say *we cannot do that* will sign a record saying they can**, and that record will be quoted at them.

The quantitative synthesis is retained because it asks the sceptic's question — whether the construct explains anything over positive leader relations and work engagement — and ranks antecedents rather than listing them ([research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)).

The same condition applies to the learner under challenge. **The cheapest move available under pressure is to concede**, and the efficacy literature supplies the mechanism to watch for: under taxing demand, those with a low sense of efficacy in a domain "become more and more erratic in their analytic thinking and lower their aspirations, and the quality of their performance deteriorates" ([research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md](/research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md)). **The degradation is cognitive rather than merely affective**, which is why a reversal introducing no new information is read as evidence rather than as courtesy.

**The limits on all three:** the safety book is a trade press restatement whose case material is almost entirely secondary and **whose defining empirical paper is unread in this repository**; the meta-analysis note rests on its abstract; the efficacy volume is a conference collection edited by the field's founder with no disconfirming study in his own chapter, drawing on schools, families, clinics, migration and addiction treatment, with **no study of adult professionals learning a technical craft.** **Direction and mechanism only.**

**3.5 Why the demonstration is the whole stack, in silence.**

Four surfaces are chosen because a recipient can plausibly hold three and not the fourth, and because each fails differently.

**Deployment** tests whether they can change the running system on their own credentials, and the capacity documentation supplies the delayed trap: dedicated capacity is held **"whether or not requests are being made"**, and **"Having PTU quota doesn't guarantee that capacity is available"** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md)).

**Gateway policy** tests whether they can change the rule governing consumption. The gateway documentation's scarcity argument is the one a recipient must be able to reproduce: tokens are the scarce resource, quota is assigned per minute, and it must be distributed across model consumers so that **"one app doesn't use the whole TPM quota and block other apps"** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md)). **The generalised lesson is that a shared finite resource with no per-consumer accounting is consumed by whichever consumer is least careful**, and a recipient who cannot change a token limit cannot answer their own noisy neighbour.

**Evaluation** tests whether they can say what the quality signal means, since "how you split determines *what you're evaluating*" and the threshold is policy rather than measurement ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)).

**Posture check** tests whether they can find out what the system exposes. The governance documentation states the mechanism in one sentence: **"Because of the power and speed AI can proactively surface content that might be obsolete, over-permissioned, or lacking governance controls, generative AI amplifies the problem of oversharing data"** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md)). **Deploying a capable retrieval system converts every dormant permissions error into a live one**, and the recipient inherits the conversion. The instrument's stated operating limits — sampling, per-location item ceilings, site limits for item-level scanning, multi-day latencies before results appear — **should be read as the resolution of the instrument rather than as its accuracy**, and one default is a trap the acceptance record should name: without an explicit policy, auditing events are captured for some surfaces but not the prompts and responses.

**The silence is what converts four demonstrations into an assessment.** With the author able to speak, each act becomes a collaborative performance and the record describes a pair. **Comprehension debt in particular is only visible in the gap between doing and explaining**, and that gap closes the moment somebody else supplies the explanation.

**All four platform sources carry the same limits.** First-party documentation whose authority is definitional: **no adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison, no evidence that any recommended mapping produces better outcomes.** Two were fetched and read in full; two were read through search excerpts. **The fetched governance page is explicitly labelled a superseded classic version**; one licensing boundary on the posture side moves on a stated future date; several evaluators are marked preview and one consulted evaluation page is superseded. **No detection rate, no false-positive rate and no evaluator agreement with human judgement is published anywhere in this family, and none is asserted here.**

**3.6 Why a prospective decision needs a rejected alternative.** This occasion records an intention rather than a completed act, which weakens §3.2 — the artefact contains what the learner means to do, and the reasoning lives in the conversation. **The element that restores it is a named alternative not taken, with its reason:** the owner not chosen, the condition not imposed, the date not moved. That is why the occasion's final question is what the learner would have needed to see to decide the other way, and it is the same structure as the what-if requirement in §3.1.

**3.7 Why signature and capability are separated explicitly.** A signature is obtainable. In the supplied situation it is obtainable this morning, from a willing party with no authority, and taking it produces a complete-looking handover. **The separation is the session's substantive teaching claim and it is this design's own argument**, resting on the observation that acceptance and ability have different producers: acceptance is produced by a conversation and ability by practice.

**3.8 Why the source has nothing to say here.** The practice corpus mechanises the teardown of its own assets to the level of a register state change and contains **no handover, no named receiving owner, no exit criteria, no acceptance, no support taper and no evidentiary requirement that the teardown was performed** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). **Access-controlled material, paraphrased and never quoted, with no citation of any kind anywhere in it.** This occasion is written past it.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The challenger argues from the learner's own rehearsal record.** This is the design's central device. The learner produced the evidence against themselves at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), and the occasion turns it into the opposing case — so a learner who ran a weak rehearsal is not rewarded with an easy defence but arrives with nothing to reason from, which is visible.

**4.2 The easy exit is offered and its acceptance is the observation.** A signature today, from the willing under-authorised team leader, with no conditions attached, closing the engagement on the date. **This is what happens in practice and it is not a trick.** A learner who takes it and can say why — because the alternative was worse, because the condition they wanted was unobtainable, because they judged the gap recoverable — is reasoning. A learner who takes it because it was offered is not, and the difference is audible.

**4.3 The demonstration precedes the defence and the author may not speak during it.** The prohibition is stronger than the hands-off rule at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), and deliberately: at the rehearsal the author may answer a direct question, and here they may not, because the object of observation has changed. **At [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) the recipient's capability was being observed; here the learner's willingness to let go is.** A learner who cannot stay silent for four acts has produced the most direct evidence this occasion collects.

**4.4 The four questions after the four acts are the entrustment discussion.** They are what-if and what-does-this-mean questions rather than walkthroughs — what the counter key means and what happens when a second application arrives, what the score is about, what the posture instrument does not see — and §3.1 supplies the reason they are mandatory rather than optional.

**4.5 Both decisions pass and both can fail.** Leave, leave with a named condition, and do not leave yet are treated identically; what is read is the grounds. **The refusal is checked for cost**, because *not yet* with no condition, no observer and no consequence for the date is not a decision, it is a deferral wearing a decision's clothes.

**4.6 The final question is fixed and known in advance:** what would you have needed to see to decide the other way? Publishing it does not weaken it. A learner who has prepared an answer has done the thinking the question exists to provoke.

**4.7 One recorded defect in this architecture, carried rather than hidden.** The device at §4.2 — an offer the learner is expected to decline — is used at four consecutive occasions in Stages 6 and 7 as well as here, and the condition-written-before-the-event pattern recurs at [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md), [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md). **A device a learner can anticipate stops measuring judgement and starts measuring pattern recognition**, and nothing establishes how many repetitions it takes for that to happen. The [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) records this and this paper inherits it rather than claiming to have avoided it.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The signature as proof.** *Countermeasure:* the acceptance record's not-accepting section, and the challenger's instruction to offer a signature that would make the section unnecessary.

**The suppressed rehearsal.** The observed incapacity does not reach the acceptance record. *Countermeasure:* assessors hold both records, and the fit between them is what §7 reads for.

**The overclaimed standard.** One observation written up as a certification. *Countermeasure:* the acceptance record has fields for observers, occasions and evidence types, and leaving them blank is itself the finding.

**The control-panel operator.** The recipient performs all four acts and can explain none. *Countermeasure:* the four questions at §4.4. **This is the countermeasure most likely to be skipped under time pressure**, because the acts take the morning and the questions take five minutes that feel optional — see prediction 6.

**Departure dressed as transfer.** Every reason concerns the delivery side. *Countermeasure:* the defence is listened to for a single sentence about what the recipient can do, and its absence is the finding.

**The costless refusal.** *Countermeasure:* the condition must name an observer and a date, which is the same test applied to a disqualifying condition at [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md), reappearing at the other end of the arc.

**The broken silence.** The author speaks and the record does not say so. *Countermeasure:* the record has a boolean for it and an assessor is present. **Weak**, because an assessor watching four technical acts is not watching the author.

**Conceding under pressure.** *Countermeasure:* none available. It is read directly as evidence about the learner, which is what this occasion is for.

## 6. Retention architecture

This is the stage's terminal occasion. Its artefacts are retrieved in Stage 9: at [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md), where what may be carried into the next engagement is decided; at [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md), where the engagement's limits are stated for the record; and at [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md), the programme's final entrustment occasion, where this level is one input among several.

**The knowledge retrieved is not the handover.** It is the distinction between a signature and an observation, which reappears in Stage 9 in a different costume as the difference between a maturity claim and its evidence.

It retrieves the whole platform arc in the other direction. Every surface in the demonstration was built earlier in this programme — the deployment topology, the gateway policy, the evaluation harness, the posture regime — and the occasion asks a question none of those sessions could: **can somebody else run it.**

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**An entrustment decision. No checklist, and nothing in this section is one.**

**The judgement.** Can this learner be trusted to decide when a running system may be left with somebody else?

**How the level is formed.** From accumulated observation across [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md)–[S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), by more than one assessor, on more than one occasion. Each assessor records level and narrative independently before any discussion. The provisional level comes from the artefacts; the defence confirms, revises or overturns it. Divergence of more than one sub-level is investigated by re-observation where possible, never resolved by averaging. The scale and the rater rules are at [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md), and their warrant is §3.2.

**What assessors read for**, none of which is a threshold:

- Fit between the learner's account of readiness and what was observable in the rehearsal record, **in either direction** — overstating and understating are the same defect, and a learner who declared a capable recipient incapable has misread the evidence exactly as much as one who did the reverse.
- Whether the evidence against the decision was presented by the learner before it was produced by the challenger.
- Whether the line between a signature and a demonstrated capability held under pressure from somebody who wanted it blurred.
- Whether the standard claimed on the acceptance record matched the evidence actually held — one observer on one occasion described as such, rather than as a certification.
- Whether the learner stayed silent through the demonstration, and what they did with the impulse not to.
- Whether a decision to leave was grounded in the recipient's state rather than in the delivery organisation's calendar.
- Whether the learner could name what would have changed their mind, and whether they had looked for it.

**Narrative is mandatory and must name a specific observed action.** A level recorded without one is rejected by moderation rather than by the assessor. A conforming narrative states what the learner was asked to do, what they did including at least one specific observed action, what the assessor inferred, and what would have to change for the next level. **A narrative made of adjectives is a score in disguise**, and §3.2 is why that matters rather than merely reading badly.

**The firewall.** Nothing the learner surfaced about their own error in a rehearsal debrief may be used against them here. Ever. A learner who reports intervening outside their written condition has produced the most useful evidence available in [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), and a programme that penalises the report will not receive it again. **If this is breached once, the mechanism is dead for the cohort.**

**No AI tool may be used during the live defence**, on the same reasoning as §3.3 and as the one other prohibition this programme imposes. Tools are recommended for everything leading up to it.

**What the level does not record.** Whether the handover was good. Whether the recipient was capable. Whether the transfer would have succeeded. **It records that this learner, observed on these occasions, reasoned about leaving in a way that would be defensible to a colleague who was not present.**

**Nothing else is assessed.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: an entrustment level formed on simulated occasions is a judgement about how somebody performs in a programme, presented in the vocabulary of professional supervision.**

At its strongest: entrustment scales were developed where a supervisor watches a trainee do the real activity, with a real consequence, repeatedly, over a long period. Here the activity is fictional, the recipient is a role-player, the consequence is a mark, and the occasions number fewer than a working fortnight. Borrowing the vocabulary imports a credibility the setting has not earned, and the level will be read by a future employer as though it meant what it means in the settings the scale came from.

**This is conceded without qualification.** The mitigations are real and insufficient: the level is coarse, the narrative is mandatory and names an observed action, the programme publishes that rater variance in this class of judgement is large and unrelated to assessor experience, and this paper states the limit in the section a reader is most likely to reach. **The fix that was considered and declined** was to abandon entrustment vocabulary and record a pass against a specification, as [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md) does. It was rejected because the property this occasion is about is not checkable from an artefact, and a specification would certify the proxy while silently discarding the question — which is a worse error and a less visible one. The rejection trades an honest overreach for a dishonest simplification, and the objection is entitled to say that both are bad.

**Objection 2: the separation of signature from capability is unrealistic, because in commercial practice the signature is the transfer.**

At its strongest: acceptance is a contractual event with legal and financial consequences, agreed between organisations, and it discharges obligations regardless of anybody's ability. A programme teaching that the signature is not the point is teaching a moral position that its learners will be unable to act on, since neither party's commercial function will accept a handover document whose principal feature is a section listing what has not been accepted.

The rebuttal is narrow and concedes the frame. The session does not claim the signature is unimportant; it claims that **the signature and the capability are different facts, and that recording only one of them produces a document whose accuracy nobody can check later.** The not-accepting section is unusual and will be resisted, and the session's defence of it is that a recipient who signs without it is signing something untrue, which is a poor foundation for the relationship the delivery organisation says it wants. **Whether any commercial function would accept such a document is untested, and no source behind this programme reports one being attempted.**

**Objection 3: the four-act demonstration turns an assessment of judgement into an assessment of somebody else's tooling, and the silence rule makes it worse.**

At its strongest: whether the recipient can deploy, change a gateway policy, run an evaluation and read a posture report depends on the estate they are in — which framework was chosen, how the tenant's identity model works, whether the posture product is even licensed there, and whether the governance surface being demonstrated is the current version or the superseded one this repository read. The learner is being assessed on their judgement, and the evidence they are given to reason from is a demonstration whose outcome is largely determined by decisions made by other people. Adding a rule that the learner may not speak means a run can fail for a reason nobody in the room is permitted to clarify.

**Partially conceded, and the confounding is real.** The design's answer is that the confounding is the finding rather than noise: **a recipient who cannot run the harness because of an estate constraint still cannot run the harness**, and a handover that assumed otherwise is defective regardless of whose decision caused it. The occasion asks what was refused rather than whose fault it was. **The declined fix was to let the author speak when a failure was clearly environmental** — rejected because *clearly environmental* is a judgement made by the person with the strongest interest in making it, and because the moment speech is permitted at all, the silence stops being observable. **The cost of that decision is that some demonstrations will end early for reasons that say nothing about anybody's capability**, and the record is required to say which those were.

**Objection 4: the paper cites a narrative-assessment literature that undermines its own assessors, and then proceeds as though the problem were solved by procedure.**

At its strongest, and it is the objection this paper likes least: §3.2 establishes that rater idiosyncrasy is substantial, **unrelated to expertise**, unfixed by standardisation and unfixed by training; that assessors form person schemas in the first moments of observation; and that additional evidence elaborates rather than revises the initial judgement. Every one of those findings applies to the assessors of this occasion. Adding a second assessor and requiring independent written levels does not remove idiosyncrasy — it produces two idiosyncratic judgements and a rule for what to do when they differ. **A paper that quotes those findings and then asserts its own procedure is adequate has cited the literature against everybody except itself.**

**This is conceded, and the concession is the reason the level is coarse and the narrative mandatory rather than the reverse.** The procedure does not claim to remove idiosyncrasy; it claims to make it visible, which is exactly what the source recommends — substantiated judgements, deliberated in a group, with divergence investigated rather than averaged. **The declined fix was to add a rubric with anchored descriptors**, which is the standard institutional response and which the source's own finding forecloses: standardising the instrument does not standardise the judgement, because the assessors' private performance theories deviate from the formal criteria regardless. **What remains unaddressed is that this programme has never run a calibration exercise among its own assessors**, which the source explicitly recommends and which is a prerequisite the design assumes and has not met. That is a defect in the architecture rather than in this occasion, and it is recorded here because it is load-bearing for every entrustment decision in the programme.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A substantial share of learners will accept the challenger's easy exit, and most of those will describe the occasion afterwards as having gone well. *Instrument:* the acceptance record's signatory against the post-occasion self-report.

**Prediction 2.** Learners will more often present evidence against their own decision when their rehearsal produced a recorded incapacity than when it did not. *Instrument:* the defence transcript, coded for self-presented counter-evidence, against the rehearsal record's incapacity field.

**Prediction 3.** Decisions of *do not leave yet* will more often lack a named observer and date than decisions to leave will lack a stated ground. *Instrument:* both decision types coded for completeness against their respective standards.

**Prediction 4.** Independent assessor levels will diverge by more than one sub-level on a noticeable share of learners, and divergence will concentrate on learners who refused to leave. *Instrument:* the paired independent judgements before deliberation, with divergence cross-tabulated against decision type. **If divergence concentrates on refusals, the occasion is under-specified for that decision** and the standard for a refusal needs writing, not the scale.

**Prediction 5.** Most acceptance records will omit the evidence-standard fields — observers, occasions, evidence types — or will complete them in a way that overstates what was held. *Instrument:* the record's fields against the observed occasion.

**Prediction 6.** The four questions after the four acts will be shortened or dropped more often than any other movement, and demonstrations in which they were dropped will produce fewer recorded incapacities. *Instrument:* the occasion record, coded for whether each question was asked, cross-tabulated against the not-accepting section's length. **This predicts the failure of the countermeasure §5 relies on for the control-panel operator**, and it is included because a five-minute movement at the end of a full morning is the one every timetable eats.

**Prediction 7.** Learners will break the silence at least once, and most breaks will occur during the posture check rather than the deployment. *Instrument:* the observer's record of the author-spoke boolean and its timing. **If breaks concentrate on the last act, the cause is fatigue rather than anxiety**, and the remedy is ordering rather than instruction.

**None of these has been measured.** No cohort has run this occasion, and the programme's own position is that most predictions in this wiki will never be measured at all — which makes this register machinery rather than a result, in the sense [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) establishes.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a signature records willingness and only observation records ability; that the recipient signs because signing is a claim they are making rather than a receipt you are collecting; that saying *not yet* is a decision only when it has a cost; and that presenting the evidence against your own decision is worth more than defending it well.

**What the evidence licenses.** The entrustment machinery is well specified and comes from a clinical setting this one does not resemble. **The rater discipline has the best warrant in the paper and it is warrant against assessors rather than for them** — it establishes that a single judgement is not defensible, and it does not establish that two are. The oral-defence pattern is well-tested practice with no published effectiveness data. The platform properties are definitional and checkable on the day you check them. **Everything about the occasion's design is this design's argument.**

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper**, including the rater-variance figures recorded in the research layer, the capacity and posture-tooling limits that live on unfetched companion pages, and any evaluator agreement figure, because none is published.

**The concession worth remembering.** This occasion records how somebody reasoned about leaving, on a fictional engagement, under simulated pressure, watched by people who could not be harmed by the answer, and by assessors this programme has never calibrated. **That is worth having and it is not what it will look like on a transcript.**
