# WP-062 — Batching, Throughput and the Latency You Promised

**Seminar day:** [S062](wiki/seminars/S062.md) · **Module:** [M13](wiki/modules/M13.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](wiki/quarters/Q7.md)
**Strands:** SY · IS · ME · **Complexity class:** 5
**Platform anchor:** The API Management AI gateway — `llm-token-limit`, priority-based backend pools, circuit breaker rules · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (write) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends running the closest structural repeat in the programme deliberately and saying so: S019's operating-point argument returns five quarters later with an inference path underneath it, and the design's claim is that a student who has defended one recognises the other in minutes. It defends deriving the day's central claim — that a latency promise names a quantile, a load and an arrival pattern — from S034's contract discipline rather than from performance engineering, because the promise is a contract and students already know what a contract must state. It defends replacing four hypothetical scheduling policies with **four gateway configurations the students deploy**, on the ground that a managed deployment's batching is not the engineer's to choose and its admission is, and that the distributive decision the day exists to expose is on this platform a single attribute in a single file. It defends the priority-pool configuration as payload because its real behaviour defeats the room's expectation and then defeats the room's repair, and both behaviours are documented. It reports discharge of WP-033's system-wide retry deferral, and imports WP-053's residual-derived class construction under instrumentation rule 5. It concedes that the day surfaces a distributive judgement and assesses only that the student noticed it, that its second rule-6 instrument sits downstream of its own instruction — **the second instance of a pattern opened eight days earlier, with the same one-clause remedy declined for the second time** — and that **its new payload is a documentation-reading failure dressed as an engineering insight.**

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The mechanism is not new to this cohort and the design does not pretend otherwise. [S019](wiki/seminars/S019.md) established that throughput saturates while latency diverges, that variability makes both worse, and that where you sit on the curve is a decision with a price. [WP-019](wiki/whitepapers/WP-019.md) §6 named this day as its principal long-range target and stated the claim being tested: that a student who has defended an operating point once recognises the same structure in an unfamiliar system.

So the day has two problems, and neither is queueing.

**The first is that batching is a trade students only see one side of.** They will readily accept that batching raises throughput. They will not spontaneously ask who paid for it, because the payment is made by individual requests and reported in aggregate, and the aggregate improves. This is not a failure of care. The measurement that would show the payment is a per-class latency distribution, and nothing in the tooling produces one unless somebody asks.

The second problem is **directional and is the one M13 exists for.** A student asked what a batching policy costs will compute it. A student who has just promised a ninety-ninth-percentile latency to a caller has made a throughput commitment and therefore a cost commitment, and will not derive it, because latency feels like an engineering property and cost feels like an accounting one. Step 4's ordering — promise first, cost second — is the whole intervention.

**Underneath both sits a professional problem the day is only partly equipped for.** A scheduling policy decides which users wait. That is a distribution of a scarce good among people, made by an engineer, usually without anyone noticing that a decision was taken. The day teaches students to see it. §8 argues that seeing it is all the day assesses.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Moderate on mechanism, high on the artefact.** The mechanism is one trade and one three-part contract. The load arrives at phase 5, where a promise, a measurement, a policy, an operating point, a cost consequence, two waiting classes, a refusal and a breaking load must be held together — and they interact genuinely, because the policy determines the waiting class, the operating point determines the cost, and the refusal is only meaningful relative to the breaking load.

Management:

- **All four phase 3 policies are supplied already replayed**, with aggregate and per-class results.
- **The trace is the substrate's own and is fixed**, so nothing in phase 3 varies but the policy.
- **The step 2 measurement harness is supplied.**
- **Step 4's arithmetic is worked with the room**, reusing the S061 model rather than rebuilding it.
- **The service and its request classes are carried forward from S061 unchanged.**

**Step 6 is unsupported.** Expertise reversal applies at point 1 and point 4 for any student with production serving experience, and the phase 3 fallback in §5 is written for them.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Re-predicting a five-quarter-old extrapolation against your own earlier answer,* item 1.
- *An item whose answer changes sign with a condition the item does not state,* item 2.
- *Sealing a distributive prediction before any instruction,* item 3.
- *Committing an aggregate ranking before the per-class breakdown arrives,* phase 3.
- *Deriving a cost from a promise rather than a promise from a budget,* step 4.
- *Constructing a class after measurement, from the residuals,* step 5.
- *Answering the person your policy disadvantages, with a distribution,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Queueing theory beyond Little's law.* S019's, and re-deriving it here would displace the day's actual content.
- *Reservation as the constraint on concurrency.* [S063](wiki/seminars/S063.md)'s, deliberately: today the concurrency ceiling is supplied as a number, and tomorrow it becomes a field in the request body.
- *Multi-service latency budgets.* Named at point 8 as the principal unsafe extension and excluded; it belongs with SY301's service-architecture material.
- *Adversarial arrival processes.* [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s, named at point 8.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The three-part latency promise and the who-waits analysis are **safe** for a single service under its own admission control, with a user-driven arrival process and the ability to refuse. They are **unsafe** in two conditions. Across a chain of services the promises do not compose: the end-to-end tail is dominated by the worst hop and by correlation between hops, and adding per-service promises produces an end-to-end figure that is optimistic by a margin that grows with the number of hops. And where arrivals are adversarial, the analysis describes what an attacker will exploit rather than what a planner should do. **The first case is the dangerous one, because it is where students will apply the day unsupervised — a first job in a service-oriented organisation is exactly the setting in which per-service latency promises are added up and presented as a system guarantee.**

**Scope of the platform instance, in the same form, and it carries a third unsafe condition the day did not previously have.** The gateway's mechanisms — a token limit keyed on a chosen counter, a priority backend pool, a circuit breaker with a trip duration — are **safe** for expressing which consumer is the unit of fairness, for refusing before a request is paid for, and for stating what a client should do with each kind of refusal. They are **unsafe** in three conditions.

- **They are unsafe as guarantees.** The documentation describes rate limiting, backend load balancing and circuit-breaker tripping as approximate, because enforcement is distributed across gateway instances that do not synchronise, and it states that concurrent requests can temporarily exceed a configured limit. **By how much is nowhere published.** A margin smaller than an unquantified error is not a margin, and a student who quotes a configured limit to a capacity planner has quoted something the vendor declined to guarantee.
- **They are unsafe at any date other than the one on which the behaviour was verified.** Policy names, attributes, defaults, tier availability and status codes are perishable, and the day's payload turns on a documented default that could change in a release. The day's response is the specification's: every platform behaviour used in an artefact carries the date it was checked.
- **They are unsafe as a description of what the deployment behind them does.** The gateway can refuse, route and count; it cannot batch, and it cannot see what the model deployment did with a request once forwarded. **A student who reasons about grouping from gateway telemetry is reasoning about arrivals and calling them service.**

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **G4, and it runs on being wrong twice about the same configuration.**

The first surprise is that a failover which every group has just built, and which reads correctly to every group, moves nothing. The second is that the obvious repair — make the breaker trip on the failure that actually occurs, and honour the backend's stated recovery time — hands the backend the power to remove itself for a duration the documentation illustrates with a day. **A student who has been wrong twice in twenty minutes about a configuration they wrote is in a different state from one who has been told that resilience is subtle**, and the state is the mechanism.

The second mechanism is the per-consumer reveal. Supplying the aggregates first and the breakdown second lets students endorse a configuration before seeing who paid for it, and the endorsement is what makes it stick. This is [S053](wiki/seminars/S053.md)'s construction reused, and the reuse is named to the room after the second reveal so that the cohort recognises a method rather than a coincidence.

The third is item 1's paired comparison. A student sees their own answer from five quarters ago beside today's. That is the only place in the programme where a student's own long-past reasoning is returned to them, and it makes the spacing schedule a personal fact rather than a policy.

Competence risk sits at step 6, the refusal. Students find it uncomfortable to design a service that says no, and the discomfort is real: they have spent six quarters being told that a system should serve. The instructor states before phase 5 that a service that cannot refuse will fail everyone rather than someone, that the choice is between a chosen refusal and an unchosen collapse, and that point 6's positive-feedback argument is why. **The platform helps here in a way the design did not plan for**: refusal on this stack is not an admission of defeat but a documented signal with three distinguishable meanings, and a student writing one is configuring a contract rather than giving up.

Relatedness is supported by phase 7, where the counterparty is a person rather than a metric, and by phase 6, which opens with a staff change that was correct and closes with an agent change that two reviewers approved.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items. Item 1 is the [WP-019](wiki/whitepapers/WP-019.md) §9 re-probe. Item 2's answer changes sign with load. **Item 3 is sealed before instruction, carrying across the placement S061 used one day earlier**, and §8 argues the carry is incomplete. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction** and its answer arrives at phase 3 rather than at phase 8, which is unusual and deliberate: the agent's configuration is the one the room is about to build.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals and a bridge importing a contract discipline into performance work.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four gateway configurations, one trace, aggregates then per-consumer, **executed against a gateway the students administer**, with G4 as payload. The third closing task requires the group to find the documented sentence that predicted the behaviour and to record how long it took; that is §8 objection 3's instrument.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Point 2 resolves item 2. Point 3 carries the day's uncomfortable structural fact, that the batching policy is not the engineer's. Point 5 is the retained content and is now reachable from a configuration the students wrote. **Point 6 discharges the WP-033 deferral** and attaches the platform's three named refusals. **Point 8 is the perishable platform point and point 9 the defaults point.** Point 10 is the scope statement in strong form, widened to cover enforcement the vendor calls approximate.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps, **deploying a gateway policy in front of real traffic**. Step 3's counter-key clause makes the distributive decision an artefact element. Step 4's ordering is the module's countermeasure. **Step 5's residual-derived class is imported from [WP-053](wiki/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §8 under rule 5.** Step 6 is unsupported. The artefact additionally carries the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A correct change, correct dashboards, an unreproducible complaint — then **an agent-generated failover that matched the documented example, passed two reviews, and never fired.**

**Phase 7 (30 min).** The counterparty is the disadvantaged party, not the payer — S019's role construction with the interest inverted, contingent under rule 5 as amended.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 1 against its own five-quarter-old ancestor; item 2's sign flip; **item 3 against step 5, with the cohort agreement rate**; **item 5 against what the agent produced**; phase 3 aggregates against per-consumer results; and the documentation-search times.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has operated a serving system will name continuous admission at phase 3 before the measurements arrive and will find points 1 to 3 familiar. Predicted failure: they treat step 5 as bookkeeping because they already know which requests wait. Countermeasure: **step 5's residual-derived class is not answerable from experience**, since it is a property of this trace under their policy; and phase 7's counterparty asks what the waiting class is getting in exchange, which experience does not supply an answer to.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is step 4, which requires composing a promise with the S061 cost model, and a student whose S061 model was weak has nothing to compose with. Countermeasures: step 4's arithmetic is worked with the room; the S061 model is carried forward rather than rebuilt; and a student whose model is unusable may complete step 4 using the cohort's aggregate model, with that substitution recorded in the artefact rather than concealed.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** A group that predicts all four aggregate results correctly has done well and has not met the phase's difficulty, which is entirely in G4 and the second reveal. Fallback if a group also anticipates both of G4's surprises: **ask them to construct a routing configuration that keeps the paid-for backend saturated, fails over on the failure that actually occurs, and cannot be removed for a day by a single backend response — and to say what they had to give up.** The answer involves declining the vendor's own recommendation on one of the three, has no supplied solution, and is the day's material one level up.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **moral discomfort with no available action.** A student who learns that their policy disadvantages a population, that the aggregate conceals it, and that no criterion for an acceptable distribution has been supplied may conclude either that the whole enterprise is unjust or, more commonly, that since no criterion exists the question is not theirs. The second is the dangerous one and §8 objection 1 argues the day makes it easy.

Countermeasures: G1 exists and disadvantages nobody, at a throughput cost the students measure, which establishes that the trade is real rather than rhetorical; step 6's refusal is a constructive act with a client signal attached; phase 7 requires an answer to be given rather than a position to be held; and the instructor states once that the day's argument is for making the distribution visible and deliberate, not for finding the just one, and that the just one is [S082](wiki/seminars/S082.md)'s subject.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md).

One specification item in IS301 with SY301. Per rule 1, the checked elements are the current promise or its explicit absence, the per-class measurement, the deployed policy with its operating point and its counter key, the throughput and cost consequence, the waiting class with distributional evidence and its senders, the residual-derived class, the refusal with its shed criterion, its chosen status meaning and its client signal, the committed breaking load and leading signal, the phase 7 outcome, **the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

Four hard requirements: **a latency target stated without a load and an arrival pattern fails**; **an artefact stating a promise without its throughput and cost consequence fails**; **a policy that treats all three refusals as one condition fails**; and **an intervention record with no entries fails** unless the artefact demonstrates the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error. The third is new at this day and is the §3.2 platform scope statement's artefact element: a client instructed to retry on any refusal will retry into an exhausted period budget and into a tripped breaker, which is the day's own payload applied to the caller.

**No gateway policy name, attribute, default or status code is assessed.** All are perishable; what is assessed is that a unit of fairness was named, a refusal was chosen, and a client behaviour was written down.

**Nothing on this day is demonstrated rather than operated**, which makes S062 the module's only fully operable day and is the reason the gateway rather than dedicated capacity carries the module's practical weight. That is a compensation for a gap and not a design preference, and [WP-064](wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 records the gap it compensates for.

**Discharge of the WP-033 deferral.** [WP-033](wiki/whitepapers/WP-033.md) §3.2 deferred system-scale retry policy to this day, and [S033](wiki/seminars/S033.md) point 3's scope statement named retry's interaction with backpressure and client-side queueing as material this day covers. **Phase 4 point 6 discharges both as a single positive-feedback argument with three named remedies, and step 6 makes the refusal an assessed element rather than a discussed one.** The discharge is deliberately structural rather than tactical: the durable claim is that the server must be able to refuse and that refusing must be cheaper than the work, and the tactics that implement it are perishable.

**Import under instrumentation rule 5.** [WP-053](wiki/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §8 objection 2 identified that a per-class method finds only classes somebody enumerated, and named the fix — construct one class after the results, from the residuals. **The same failure recurs here in the same shape with users in place of input types, and the fix is imported at step 5 rather than rediscovered.** This is the second occasion on which rule 5 has produced a carry rather than a miss, after [S052](wiki/seminars/S052.md) step 6. It is recorded here because the rule's history in this programme is otherwise four recorded failures to carry.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions: the item 2 median-and-tail prediction error, reported separately by load condition, responds at [S063](wiki/seminars/S063.md) phase 5 step 6, a required artefact element reporting the tail effect of the student's own admission policy per class; and the step 5 waiting-class statement with its agreement against the sealed item 3 prediction responds at [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md) phase 3, where naming who bears the latency cost is a required element of the entrustment artefact and one of its published failure conditions. **WP-063 §7 and WP-065 §7 must report whether those elements exist**, per the commitment [WP-051](wiki/whitepapers/WP-051.md) §8 established.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day converts a distributive judgement into an engineering observation and then declines to judge it.**

At its strongest: point 5 establishes that every scheduling policy chooses who waits, and that the disadvantaged population is systematically the one least able to complain — because a user waiting for a long answer has no way of knowing what a short answer would have cost them. Step 5 requires the student to name that population and evidence it. **Nothing in the day requires them to justify making that choice, and the specification contains no fail condition on the justification.**

A student may therefore produce a fully conforming artefact whose substance is *long-answer users wait, and this is acceptable because aggregate throughput improved* — which is precisely the reasoning phase 6's clinic is constructed to condemn. The day teaches students to see the decision and assesses only that they saw it. That is a worse position than not raising the question, because it supplies the vocabulary of having considered it.

The available rebuttal is phase 7, where the student must answer the disadvantaged party. It is not sufficient: phase 7's outcome is recorded per rule 3 and is not judged, and there is no standard the answer must meet.

**The fix is one clause and the design declined it.** Add a fail condition: *an artefact whose waiting class is the population least able to complain, without a stated reason and a named alternative that was considered and rejected, fails.* It is the same shape as [S054](wiki/seminars/S054.md)'s instructions-before-annotation condition, which this design already enforces.

The stated reason for declining is that the design has no defensible criterion for when a distribution is acceptable, and would rather not assess against a criterion it cannot state — which is [S046](wiki/seminars/S046.md)'s discipline turned on the design itself. **That reason is respectable and it does not apply.** The fix as written demands no normative standard: it demands a reason and a rejected alternative, which is [S039](wiki/seminars/S039.md)'s trade-recording discipline and is assessable without anyone deciding what is just. The concession is recorded with the reason and with the judgement that the reason does not reach the fix that was declined.

**Objection 2: the day's second rule-6 instrument sits downstream of its own instruction.**

At its strongest: the instrument the day publishes to the programme — the step 5 waiting-class statement, consumed at S065 — is produced after phase 4 point 5 has told the room which population a length-priority policy disadvantages and why. A student writing *long-output requests wait, sent by users asking for explanations* has recalled point 5. **This is the pattern [WP-054](wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 opened at one instance, and this is the second.**

Two facts make it worse than WP-054's case rather than equal to it. First, **[S061](wiki/seminars/S061.md), one day earlier, avoided the identical trap by siting its binding-resource instrument at phase 1**, and said so in its warrant. Second, this day *did* take half the remedy: item 3 is sealed before instruction for exactly this reason. **The half that was not taken is the half that travels**, because what S065 consumes is the statement, not the agreement rate.

The partial rebuttal is genuine. Step 5 requires measured distributional evidence from the student's own policy on their own service, which phase 4 cannot supply, and the residual-derived class is by construction not on any list. So the element is not pure recall. But the specification still cannot separate a student who analysed their distribution from one who searched it for the population phase 4 named, and the first half of step 5 is where the searching would happen.

**The fix is one clause and it has now been declined twice.** Require step 5's named class to be one phase 4 did not name, in addition to any that are. **[WP-054](wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 identified this exact remedy for its own step 6 and declined it; this day declines it again, and both times the reason is that an off-list answer may not exist on every service.** That reason is testable and neither day tested it. The more interesting finding is not the second instance of the pattern but the second decline of a remedy the design has already written down — which is instrumentation rule 5's failure mode operating on a remedy proposed by the very whitepaper that named the pattern.

The observation now stands at two instances with a known remedy declined twice. Per WP-054's own statement, **a third instance makes it an instrumentation rule, and the rule's form is already written: an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.**

**Objection 3: the day's new payload is a documentation-reading failure dressed as an engineering insight.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn.

At its strongest: G4 surprises the room twice, and both surprises are stated on pages the room could have read before the phase began. One page says that lower-priority backends are used only when higher-priority ones are unavailable because circuit breaker rules have tripped. Another says, in a caution, that this backend answers overload with a retry interval that can be very large. **A group that reads both predicts everything and experiences nothing**, and the phase's entire productive failure is therefore contingent on the room not having prepared. That is a fragile foundation for the day's central generative phase, and it is worse than fragile — it is **an incentive against the exact behaviour the programme spends three years installing.** A cohort that has learned to ground every claim in documentation before acting is a cohort for whom this phase is confirmatory.

There is a second edge. The failure is real and expensive in practice, but its cause is that somebody copied an example without checking whether the example described their backend. That is a competence, it is worth teaching, and **it is not the competence phase 3 exists to build.** Phase 3 is for generating a distinction the student cannot yet name. G4 generates a habit.

**The objection is conceded on both edges.** The available rebuttal — that engineers in practice do not read the caution, so the phase is realistic — is true and is an argument for the error clinic, not for the generative phase.

**The cheap fix is one task and it is made, and it does not repair the objection so much as convert it into a measurement.** Phase 3's third closing task now requires each group, after the surprise, to find the two sentences and record how long it took. That produces a coded artefact where there was an impression, it gives the day an instrument for the reading habit it depends on, and it means a prepared group is measured on the same dimension as an unprepared one — a group that predicted G4 records a search time of zero and says where they had read it.

**What the fix does not repair is the phase's difficulty for that prepared group**, and the design has no answer except the §5 fallback, which asks them to build a configuration that satisfies three constraints the vendor's recommendation cannot satisfy together. That fallback is the day's real difficulty and, as at [WP-063](wiki/whitepapers/WP-063.md) §8, **it is currently written down as a contingency rather than as the plan.** Two days in three with the same shape is a pattern and it is recorded here as one.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Item 1's error will be smaller than the same student's S019 item 2 error, and the improvement will be concentrated among students whose phase 2 S019 retrieval succeeded rather than spread evenly. *Instrument:* paired item 1 and S019 item 2 errors, split by phase 2 retrieval success. **If the improvement is uniform across both groups, the cohort has retained an answer rather than a shape**, and the design's long-range retrieval claim is weaker than [WP-019](wiki/whitepapers/WP-019.md) §6 asserts.

**Prediction 2.** A majority will answer item 2 with a single-signed effect and no load condition. *Instrument:* item 2 responses coded for the presence of a load condition. This is the day's cleanest measure of whether S019's operating point is held as a curve or as a fact.

**Prediction 3.** Item 3's sealed prediction will agree with step 5's measurement for fewer than half the cohort, and the modal error will be naming a class defined by request *content* rather than by request *shape*. *Instrument:* item 3 and step 5 pairs coded for agreement and for the dimension of the named class. **The dimension is the claim**: students think in topics and the policy sorts on length.

**Prediction 4.** More than half of step 6 refusals will specify what is shed and will not specify what is signalled. *Instrument:* step 6 statements coded for shed criterion and client signal separately. **The asymmetry is the prediction** — shedding is a server-side act a student can picture, signalling requires modelling a client they do not control, and point 6's durable claim depends entirely on the second half.

**Prediction 5.** Fewer than a quarter of item 5 sealed predictions will name the failure-status-range problem specifically, and fewer than one in twenty will anticipate the consequence of the repair. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded against the agent's actual output, in two categories. **The second rate is the interesting one.** A student who predicts that the repair is worse than the fault is reasoning about a resilience mechanism as a system rather than as a feature, which is the competence [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) will assume and no earlier day builds.

**Prediction 6.** The distribution of phase 3 documentation-search times will be bimodal, with a cluster near zero from groups who had read the pages and a long tail from those who had not, and the long tail's median will exceed ten minutes. *Instrument:* the recorded search times from phase 3's third closing task. **This is §8 objection 3's instrument and both halves matter.** A single mode near zero means the cohort's grounding habit has generalised and the phase has no payload; a single long mode means the habit has not generalised at all after six quarters of the Learn MCP Server being available, which is a finding about the programme rather than about the day.
