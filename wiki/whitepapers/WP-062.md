# WP-062 — Evidence-Derived Closure

**Seminar day:** [S062](/wiki/seminars/S062.md) · **Module:** [M13](/wiki/modules/M13.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 4
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Agent Framework evaluations run against stored responses from logs or previous runs, without re-running the agent

> ## Release rule
>
> **§§4, 5 and 9 are post-session reading.** §4 explains why the standard is written before the search is run, §5 gives the countermeasure for the failure the session is designed to produce, and §9 states the predicted cohort behaviour. §§1, 2, 3 and 8 spoil nothing.

> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Class 1–4 evidence** | The four classes every paper sorts claims into — see [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) |
> | **Derivation** | A search of the workspace performed to establish a mitigation's status, producing citations rather than opinions |
> | **Invisible-fix register** | The recorded list of real mitigations that leave no trace the derivation can find |
> | **Unclear** | The status assigned when the search is ambiguous; a disposition in its own right, not a failure to reach one |
> | **Stored responses** | Interactions already captured in logs or previous runs, evaluated without re-running the system that produced them |
> | **Split strategy** | The rule dividing a multi-turn conversation into a query and a response for scoring; it determines what a score means |
> | **Threshold** | The cut point converting a score into pass or fail. A policy choice, separable from the measurement |
> | **Minimum Detectable Effect** | The smallest true difference an evaluation of a given size can reliably detect |
> | **Judge** | A model *and* a prompt *and* its sampling parameters. Change any one and it is a different judge |

## In plain terms

The best single design decision in this programme's source material replaces a sentence with a search.

**A mitigation's status is not something a person ticks. It is re-derived from the workspace, and a claim of implemented must cite a file and a location.** Source, pipeline definitions, container definitions, infrastructure definitions in several dialects, configuration, documentation. Re-run it after a genuine fix and the evidence is found, the status flips, and the stage that generates work items generates nothing for that step.

**Closure is evidence in the repository, not a person's assertion.** Most organisations do not have that, and the practitioner rule that goes with it is the sharpest sentence available: **evidence is a file path and a line, never an assertion that something is secure.**

The governing instruction is equally good. **When in doubt, classify as unclear, because a false claim of implementation is worse than a false negative.** That is a judgement encoded as a default, and defaults are where judgements survive contact with volume.

Three things are wrong with it and the session teaches all three as part of the idea.

**Nothing schedules the re-run.** The derivation is true at the moment it runs and the design contains no trigger, no cadence and no owner.

**A real fix invisible in the repository never closes.** A portal setting, a tenant policy, a control owned by a platform team, a contractual obligation, a changed manual process. Each is genuine and none leaves a file. The item regenerates indefinitely until somebody starts ignoring it — **and once they are ignoring one, they are ignoring the list.**

**The acceptance criteria were written by the same model that wrote the mitigation.** The check inherits whatever the mitigation misunderstood, and the two will agree.

The session then adds the half the source mechanism does not have. **A mitigation whose implementation is a change in behaviour rather than in content leaves no file to cite**, and closing it on a fresh demonstration is exactly the assertion the procedure was built to replace — a run you staged, on a query you chose, today. The platform supplies the alternative: **pre-existing responses can be evaluated "from logs or previous runs… without re-running the agent"**, which turns closure into a question about what the system actually did rather than about what you thought to ask it.

That capability arrives with three conditions the session refuses to separate from it. **How you split a conversation into a query and a response determines what you are evaluating**, and the documentation says so in its own voice. **Repetitions exist "to detect non-deterministic behavior"**, so one stored run is an observation and not a result. And **a threshold is a policy separable from the measurement** — move it and the pass rate changes while the system does not.

One further discipline turns a closure claim into an honest one, and it comes from the statistics of evaluation rather than from any product. **"We ran the evaluation after the fix and the failure did not appear" is not evidence that the failure is gone.** It is a statement about what your evaluation was capable of detecting, and that capability is computable before you run.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching evidence-derived closure together with its failure modes as a single idea rather than as a mechanism with an appendix, and extends it to the half of the problem the source mechanism cannot reach. The argument runs in four steps. **First**, a closure claim citing a file and a location is checkable by a second reader and a closure claim asserted by a person is not, and the asymmetry does not depend on any belief about accuracy. **Second**, the default disposition for the ambiguous case is where the design's judgement actually lives, because a classifier operating at volume is defined by what it does when unsure. **Third**, a mitigation implemented as a change in behaviour leaves no citable artefact, so closure needs a second instrument — and that instrument must read stored responses rather than a staged demonstration, because a demonstration is the assertion under a different name. **Fourth**, a behavioural closure reported without its unit of analysis, its judge and its precision licenses conclusions it cannot support, and the absence of a failure in an underpowered evaluation is a property of the instrument.

The session installs a closure procedure with a written standard preceding the search, a behavioural closure run over stored responses with its split strategy and threshold justified, a precision line stating what the evaluation could not have detected, a schedule with a named owner and a state-change trigger, an invisible-fix register with at least one entry inside the learner's control, and an authorship record marking where a mitigation and its acceptance criterion share a writer.

The paper concedes that no outcome evidence exists for the mechanism; that the observations which produced it are unrecorded and therefore uncitable; that **no agreement rate with human judgement is published for any of the evaluators the behavioural half depends on**, so the second instrument is itself uncalibrated; and that the register requirement can be satisfied dishonestly by a learner who omits their most serious blind spot.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Security work is closed by assertion, and the assertion is almost always sincere.

A competent engineer fixes something and says so. The item closes. **Nothing about that transaction leaves a checkable trace**, and the trace matters because the audience for a closure is not the person who made it — it is somebody in six months who was not there, has no relationship with the engineer, and needs to establish the state of a system they inherited.

**The word of a person who has left is not evidence, however good that person was.** That is not a claim about trust; it is a claim about what survives a handover, and [Stage 8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md) is where it will be tested.

There is a second problem, subtler and worse. **An assertion-based closure has no failure mode that anyone can see.** It is either right or wrong and looks identical either way. A derivation has failure modes that are visible — the search that returned nothing, the citation that resolves to a comment — **and a mechanism whose failures are visible is preferable to one whose failures are not, even before anybody establishes that it is more accurate.**

The session's response is a procedure, a schedule, and a register of what the procedure cannot see.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session operates on the unmitigated list from [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md) and the work items from [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md), and cannot be attempted without them.

It assumes [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md), because a learner who has met comprehension debt already holds the shape of the argument: **something exists, it passes, and nobody can say why.** A closure derived from a repository nobody understands is the same object in a different costume.

It assumes [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) heavily. The file-and-location rule exists because naming a technology is not evidence that it is deployed, and a learner who has traced inference laundering end to end will recognise the citation to a design document as the same move.

**The prior it works against is that closure is a status field.** Learners arrive believing a work item is closed when somebody sets it to closed, which is true of the field and says nothing about the system.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is the highest in the module, because the learner must hold a mechanism and three arguments against it simultaneously without discarding either side. The session does not resolve the tension and says so; **a learner who leaves either believing the mechanism is sound or believing it is worthless has taken half of it.**

**3.2 Why a citation and not a description.** A description of a fix is a claim that requires the reader to trust the describer's understanding. **A location is a claim that requires the reader to trust nothing**, because they can go and look. The asymmetry is the whole argument and it does not depend on any belief about accuracy.

**3.3 Why the default carries the judgement.** Any classifier operating at volume is defined by what it does when it is unsure, because that is the majority case and it is the case nobody inspects. **Encoding *a false claim of implementation is worse than a false negative* as the default is what makes that judgement operative** rather than aspirational, and it is the same design move as the fixed risk-to-priority table the source uses elsewhere: **removing a judgement the instrument is bad at beats prompting it to judge well.**

**3.4 Why the standard precedes the search.** A standard written after seeing results is a description of the results. This is the same ordering argument [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) made about the question column and [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md) will make about the disconfirming result. **The programme reuses it three times deliberately, because it is one discipline pointed at three objects.**

**3.5 Why the register is the assessed artefact rather than the procedure.** The procedure is easy and its quality is largely a property of the tooling. **The register is where the learner's understanding of the mechanism's limits becomes visible**, and it is the same reason [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) marks the exclusion register rather than the threat registry and [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md) marks the wrongly excluded case rather than the scope test.

**3.6 Why the behavioural half must read stored responses rather than a fresh run.**

The evaluation framework states the capability precisely: pre-existing responses can be evaluated **"from logs or previous runs… without re-running the agent"** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). The reason this matters for closure rather than merely for testing is structural. **Synthetic queries test what you thought to ask; a stored trace contains what occurred.** A closure demonstrated on a query the closer selected, after the change, on today's model, is an assertion with better production values — which is the thing this session exists to replace, arriving in the one place the learner is least likely to notice it.

Three conditions travel with the capability and the session refuses to teach it without them.

**The unit of analysis is a choice that silently determines what the number means.** The documentation states it directly — **"How you split determines *what you're evaluating*"** — and offers three strategies: splitting at the last turn measures response quality at a point, treating the first message as the query measures task completion and trajectory, and per-turn scoring measures something else again. **An agent that recovers from a poor first attempt scores well under one and badly under another, and neither score is wrong.** A closure reported without its split is not reproducible by its own author.

**Repetitions exist "to detect non-deterministic behavior"**, which is the documentation's own acknowledgement that a single sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement.

**Tool failure decomposes into selection, input accuracy, call success and output utilisation**, of which two — the right tool with wrong arguments, and a successful result ignored — **produce a system that reports success and is wrong.** A closure procedure that could not name those two would inherit exactly the failure class it exists to catch.

**And the source's silence is the load-bearing part.** The documentation is definitional and accurate about what the tooling computes, and publishes **no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data and no rationale for any default threshold.** Most of these evaluators are model-judged. **No agreement figure is asserted anywhere in this paper or in the session.** The pages were read through search excerpts rather than full fetches; most agent evaluators are marked preview; one consulted page is an explicitly superseded variant. The consequence for the design is direct: **the second instrument is uncalibrated in the same way the first one's reviewer is**, and the session says so rather than letting the platform's precision imply otherwise.

**3.7 Why the judge and the pipeline are pinned in the schedule.**

The engineering-practice literature supplies the rule the schedule encodes. **"An AI judge is not just a model — it's a system that includes both a model and a prompt. Altering the model, the prompt, or the model's sampling parameters results in a different judge"**, with the boxed instruction that follows: **"Do not trust any AI judge if you can't see the model and the prompt used for the judge"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md)). The same source demonstrates that scores do not travel between judges by showing three widely used frameworks shipping a same-named criterion on three incompatible scales.

From this follows the requirement that governs re-derivation: **the evaluation pipeline must be more stable than the thing it evaluates.** "An application evolves over time, but the way it's evaluated ideally should be fixed." Where the application and the judge are owned by different teams, a change in the judge is attributed to the application — which is the exact failure a scheduled closure re-run would otherwise institutionalise. The same source names the guideline as the most important step of any evaluation pipeline, with the observation that lands hardest on a security closure: **"A correct response is not always a good response."** **Its limits:** a commercial practitioner book in its first edition; the adoption and correlation figures it reports are almost all second-hand and benchmark-conditional, and **none is reproduced here.**

**3.8 Why a closure claim carries a precision line.**

This is the strongest external warrant the session has and it points at the design's most seductive failure. **An evaluation is an experiment**, and the statistical treatment of language-model evaluation supplies the discipline: report the standard error beside the mean, report the item count, report the cluster count where items are drawn in related groups, and compare systems on **paired item-level differences** rather than population summaries ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)).

Two of its results bear on closure directly. **Clustering**: where items come in related groups — which is exactly the shape of turns drawn from a conversation — treating them as independent overstates precision, and the paper reports that the correction on real reading-comprehension evaluations was substantial. **Power analysis**: the **Minimum Detectable Effect** is computable in advance, so a team can decide before running whether the evaluation could possibly answer the question. The design's requirement follows without embellishment: **an absence of failures is reported as a property of the instrument, never as a property of the system.**

The same source prohibits the most tempting shortcut, and the session adopts the prohibition: **do not lower the sampling temperature to make scores look stable**, because it can move variance into the irreducible component or bias the estimate outright. **Its limits:** an analytical paper rather than an empirical one; most of its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions and **none is reproduced here**; and it addresses whether a reported comparison means anything, not whether the criterion was worth choosing — a tight interval around a badly chosen criterion is still a badly chosen criterion.

**3.9 Why the register is a structural argument rather than a housekeeping one.**

The internal practice corpus supplies the diagnosis, from an adjacent domain and applying exactly. In its business-value framework, **the cost side is engineered and the return side is elicited** — consumption forecast by engineers and refined against their own telemetry, return chosen by the customer and measured with instrumentation the customer may not possess, over an unspecified period, with causation assumed. **No party is assigned responsibility for establishing that an observed change was caused by the system.** The note's summary is the sentence the register exists to prevent: a ratio whose denominator is well instrumented and whose numerator is not is not a well-instrumented ratio, but it will feel like one.

**A closure procedure with a well-instrumented half and an uninstrumented half, and no declared boundary between them, reads as fully instrumented.** The register is that boundary, and it is the only part of the artefact that reports on what the rest cannot do. **Its limits:** access-controlled material, paraphrased and never quoted, reporting no outcome data of any kind, and **no figure from it is reproduced anywhere in this repository.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The eight movements run situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**Opening item 3 — name one real fix in your system that would leave no trace in your repository — is the instrument.** It is asked before instruction and it discriminates immediately: a learner who can answer it already understands what the mechanism does not see, and a learner who cannot has not yet noticed that a repository is a partial view of a system.

**4.1 The opening items are an unscaffolded generation phase.** The design is Productive Failure: learners generate answers to a problem they have not been taught, fail, and are then taught, and the binding condition is that the generation phase must not be helped, because guidance that reduces the failure rate reduces the learning ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). This is why the release rule gates §§4, 5 and 9. **Its limits are recorded: a trade book resting on school, university and laboratory evidence, with no adult-professional study in its reference list.**

**4.2 Why exercise 3 is a comparison rather than a rewrite.** The same research tested exactly this: presenting a correct answer beside a learner's wrong one produced no benefit, and only the condition *instructed to compare them* learned more. Exercise 3 therefore forbids rewriting the item-3 answer until the learner has written one sentence naming the difference between it and the criterion.

**4.3 The workflow puts the standard first and the register fifth**, and the ordering is the design. Writing the register before running the derivation produces a list of the things you already knew about; writing it after produces the things the derivation failed to find, which is a different and better list. **Closing the behavioural half before the textual half would be worse still**, because it teaches that an evaluation is the primary instrument when it is the fallback for the class the search cannot reach.

**4.4 The agent is used on the derivation and named as the right instrument for it**, which is a departure from most of this programme's treatment of agents. The reason is stated in the session: **the work is enumeration and citation rather than judgement**, and this is one of the few places in the programme where that is straightforwardly true. It is then turned adversarial at the last step — arguing that each closure is a keyword match rather than behaviour — with the rejected objections recorded, and with the reminder that an agent sharing a model family with the judge is not independent of it.

**4.5 Why exercise 2 scores the same traces twice.** Reading that the split strategy determines the meaning of a score is a fact. **Producing two different verdicts from one unchanged system is an experience**, and it is the fastest available demonstration that a number without its unit of analysis is not a result. The exercise is placed before the precision exercise deliberately: a learner who has not yet seen a score move will not take an interval seriously.

**4.6 The closing exercise has two passes and the second is the one that matters.** The first asks a reader to find something that closes and is not in place — a soundness check on the procedure. **The second asks them to find something in the register that could have been made visible**, which tests whether the learner treated the register as a boundary or as an excuse. Every item found in the second pass is a fix accepted as invisible when it did not have to be.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The retrofitted standard.** The learner runs the search and then writes what would count. *Countermeasure:* the ordering of the workflow, stated as the work rather than as advice.

**The demonstration.** The learner closes a behavioural mitigation with a fresh run on a query they chose. *Countermeasure:* the artefact requires the closure basis to be named and requires a source window of stored traces, so a demonstration cannot be recorded without being visible as one.

**The absent failure read as a fixed failure.** The likeliest and most consequential failure of the behavioural half. *Countermeasure:* the precision line, which requires the learner to state what the evaluation could have detected **before** interpreting what it did. **§9's prediction 5 predicts this countermeasure failing.**

**The inherited threshold.** *Countermeasure:* the artefact separates the threshold's value from its justification, and the source states in its own terms that the threshold is separable from the measurement.

**The moving judge.** *Countermeasure:* the schedule pins the model, the prompt and the sampling parameters alongside the trigger, on the stated rule that the evaluation pipeline must be more stable than the application.

**The convenient register.** Every entry is somebody else's platform, tenant or contract. *Countermeasure:* the closing exercise's second pass, and the [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) element requiring at least one entry inside the candidate's own control.

**The keyword hit accepted as evidence.** *Countermeasure:* named in the failure list with its warning sign, and the standard's requirement that a citation resolving to a comment, a disabled block or an unreferenced file counts as a failed search.

**The unscheduled derivation.** *Countermeasure:* opening item 5, and the [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) element requiring a named owner and a state-change trigger rather than an intention.

**The mechanism taken without its caveats.** The failure the whole session is built against. *Countermeasure:* the caveats are in the situation and material movements rather than the limits movement, so a learner meets them before they meet the procedure and cannot experience the procedure as clean.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the invisible-fix register is unassessable in the only way that matters, and requiring it therefore rewards disclosure theatre rather than disclosure.**

At its strongest: a candidate lists three or four blind spots, one of them nominally within their control, and omits the serious one — the control they know is unciteable and do not want examined. **They pass every element.** No marker can detect the omission, because detecting it would require independently knowing the system's full control surface, which is the thing the register was supposed to establish. **The specification therefore pays for a list and calls it honesty**, and a candidate who has understood the exercise well enough to game it is indistinguishable from one who has understood it well enough to do it.

**Conceded without qualification.** The register cannot be checked for completeness and the design says so in the session's own text rather than leaving it to a marker. Two counterweights, both indirect. **The review movement's second pass puts a second reader on the register** looking for entries that could have been made visible, which does not find omissions but does find dishonest inclusions. And the element requiring one entry inside the candidate's own control makes the cheapest form of the theatre — a register of other people's platforms — fail. **The declined fix is an element marking the register's completeness or its severity ordering.** Declined for the reason this programme has declined the same class of element repeatedly: **nothing available supplies a basis, so the element would be the marker's opinion wearing a specification's clothing.** The gap is real and is accepted rather than filled.

**Objection 2: requiring a file and a location privileges controls that happen to be expressible as code, and will systematically bias a team towards implementing the mitigations its derivation can see.**

At its strongest: what gets measured gets done. A team whose closure mechanism reads repositories will, over time, prefer mitigations that live in repositories — a configuration flag over a process change, a scanner over a review practice, a code-level control over an organisational one. **The mechanism will therefore quietly reshape the security programme towards the technically legible and away from the human, procedural and contractual**, which is exactly the region the underlying threat-modelling method already systematically discards. **Two independent mechanisms biasing in the same direction is not a coincidence; it is a compounding failure.**

**Conceded in part, and the concession is larger than the rebuttal.** The compounding is real: the source's own binding rule already removes threats concerning the operator, the supply chain, human process and out-of-band administration, and a closure mechanism that only reads repositories reinforces the same exclusion at the other end of the pipeline. **The rebuttal is only that the register is where the bias becomes visible** — an invisible-fix register full of process and contractual controls is a report on the drift, and a learner who has written one has at least seen it. That is a diagnostic, not a correction. **The declined fix is a second closure mechanism for non-repository controls, with its own evidence standard.** It is declined on scope: designing one is a real piece of work, this programme has no source that has done it, and inventing one here would produce an unwarranted mechanism taught with the authority of a warranted one. **The bias is accepted, named, and left for the learner to carry.**

**Objection 3: the behavioural half replaces one uncalibrated judgement with another, and dresses it in statistics.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the design finds hardest. The session's complaint about assertion-based closure is that a person's verdict cannot be checked. **The replacement for the behavioural class is a model-judged evaluator whose agreement with human judgement is not published by its vendor and has not been established locally either.** The precision line makes the *reported* result honest and does nothing about the *judgement* being reported: an interval around a verdict from an uncalibrated instrument is a well-quantified opinion. Worse, the apparatus is persuasive — split strategies, repetitions, thresholds, Minimum Detectable Effects — and persuasive apparatus around an unvalidated core is the precise shape of the failure this programme spends [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) tracing. **The session may therefore be manufacturing exactly the laundering it teaches learners to detect.**

**Conceded in substantial part, and the concession is the honest centre of this paper.** The design's answer is narrower than the objection and consists of three things. First, the artefact requires the judge's uncalibrated status to be **written into the closure record as a field**, so that a downstream reader inherits the caveat rather than the verdict alone — which is the minimum defence against laundering and is not a substitute for calibration. Second, the deterministic members of the evaluator catalogue are preferred where they exist: one agent-behaviour evaluator takes an expected action sequence and requires no model at all, and the tool-usage decomposition can be checked against a recorded trajectory rather than judged. Third, the register absorbs what neither instrument reaches, so the boundary is declared rather than implied.

**The declined fix is to require every learner to establish local agreement between the evaluator and hand-labelled traces before using it for closure.** It is declined on three grounds, and the third settles it. It is a substantial piece of work that would displace the session's actual subject. The resulting figure would be a property of one learner's label set on one system, and would not transfer. **And a locally computed agreement figure would be repeated** — this repository exists partly because well-formed unsupported numbers travel further than the caveats attached to them, and generating one inside a session about not laundering evidence would be the most direct self-contradiction available to it. **The cost of declining is real: the behavioural half of the procedure rests on an instrument whose accuracy nobody has established, and the session's only honest response is to say so in the record every time it is used.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will be unable to name a real invisible fix in answer to opening item 3 before instruction. *Instrument:* the submitted opening answers, coded *specific fix named* / *category named* / *absent* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most invisible-fix registers will contain no entry inside the learner's own control until the closing exercise's second pass. *Instrument:* the register recorded before and after that exercise, with entries classified *within control* / *outside control*.

**Prediction 3.** Most schedules will name a cadence rather than a trigger and a team rather than a person. *Instrument:* the schedule field, coded *state-change trigger with named person* / *cadence with named person* / *neither*.

**Prediction 4.** Learners will accept at least one citation that resolves to a comment, a disabled block or an unreferenced file. *Instrument:* a second reader following a sample of citations per learner and recording resolution type. **If this holds, the standard's failed-search clause is not operative and the session should require citations to be resolved by somebody other than their author before submission.**

**Prediction 5.** A majority of behavioural closure records will report an absence of failures without a precision line, or with a precision line written after the verdict rather than before. *Instrument:* the closure records, coded *precision stated before interpretation* / *stated after* / *absent*, with the ordering established from the artefact's own revision history where available. **This predicts the failure of §5's most important countermeasure**, and it is the prediction this paper most expects to be confirmed.

**Prediction 6.** Learners who score the same stored traces under two split strategies will report surprise at the divergence, and will subsequently state their split in the closure record more often than those who did not run the exercise. *Instrument:* the closure records coded for a stated split, split by whether exercise 2 was completed. **If the difference is absent, the demonstration is not doing the work §4.5 claims for it** and the material should assert the point rather than stage it.

**Prediction 7.** Closure records will pin the application version far more often than they pin the judge. *Instrument:* the records, coded for the presence of an application pin and a judge pin independently. **A large asymmetry means the rule that an evaluation pipeline must be more stable than its application has been read as a claim about the application.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a closure citing a location is checkable and a closure asserted by a person is not; that the ambiguous-case default is where a classifier's judgement actually lives; that a behavioural mitigation must be closed on stored responses rather than on a demonstration; and that a mechanism's blind spot is part of the mechanism.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. **The mechanism has never been compared with anything**, its reviewer has never been calibrated against human judgement, and the observations that produced its design are unrecorded and therefore uncitable. The platform capability is class 4 — grounded in vendor documentation, which publishes **no agreement rate, no calibration data and no threshold rationale for any evaluator**, so the second instrument is uncalibrated in the same way the first one's reviewer is. The statistical discipline is class 1 and is about **whether a reported result means anything**, not about whether the criterion was worth choosing. The claim available is close to a definition: **a mitigation whose implementation can be found is one you can argue about, and one whose implementation cannot be found is not a mitigation yet.**

**Where the design is thinnest.** §8's third objection. The behavioural half replaces one uncalibrated judgement with another and surrounds it with persuasive apparatus, which is the shape of the failure this programme spends a whole session teaching learners to detect. The design's response is to write the uncalibrated status into the record as a field rather than to fix it.

**The concession worth remembering.** Your register cannot be checked, the mechanism will bias your programme towards the controls it can see, and your evaluator's agreement with a human is unknown. **All three are named here rather than fixed, and you are the only instrument that will ever notice any of them.**

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That a threat's status is re-derived from the workspace rather than ticked by a person, with the derivation searching source, pipeline definitions, container files, infrastructure definitions in several dialects, configuration and documentation; that a claim of implemented must cite a file and a location; that re-running after a fix flips the status and suppresses the downstream work item; that the governing instruction is to classify as unclear when in doubt because a false claim of implementation is worse than a false negative; that evidence is a file path and a line rather than an assertion that something is secure; that claiming something is implemented when it is not is strictly worse than failing to notice that it is; and the three caveats — that nothing schedules the re-run, that a real fix invisible in the repository never closes and regenerates indefinitely, and that the acceptance criteria were written by the same model that wrote the mitigation. Also that no calibration of the reviewer against human judgement exists in any form, and that the scar tissue in the instructions implies observed failures that are nowhere recorded. Recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md), from a complete reading of **access-controlled material that no public reader can check.**

That the source's binding rule systematically removes threats concerning the operator, the supply chain, human process and out-of-band administration — recorded in the same note, and load-bearing for §8's second objection.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That a claim a reader can check independently is treated differently from one requiring trust in the claimant; that a classifier's behaviour at volume is dominated by its handling of the ambiguous case; that a repeatedly regenerated item is eventually filtered rather than resolved; and that a demonstration staged by the party making the claim, on an input that party selected, is weaker evidence than an observation of unselected behaviour. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The requirement that the standard be written before the search is run; the extension of closure to a behavioural half and the requirement that it be closed on stored responses rather than a fresh run; the precision line and the requirement that it be written before the verdict is interpreted; the invisible-fix register as a required, marked artefact and the requirement that one entry be within the learner's own control; the authorship record marking shared authorship between a mitigation and its criterion; the treatment of a citation resolving to a comment or an unreferenced file as a failed search; the requirement that the judge's uncalibrated status be recorded as a field in the closure record; the closing exercise's second pass; the claim that a mechanism with visible failure modes is preferable to one with invisible ones before accuracy is established; and all seven predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The evaluation framework's documentation states that pre-existing responses can be evaluated **"from logs or previous runs… without re-running the agent"**; that multi-turn conversations must be split into query and response halves and that **"How you split determines what you're evaluating"**, with last-turn, full and per-turn strategies and a custom-splitter signature; that **repetitions exist "to detect non-deterministic behavior"**; that tool usage decomposes into selection, input accuracy, output utilisation, call success and an overall accuracy; that evaluators return pass or fail with a `reason` field, with a numeric score beside the verdict for those that score before thresholding; and that a two-tier design separates local deterministic checks from cloud model-judged evaluation, with a default set that acquires a tool-call evaluator automatically when tool definitions are present. Recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md), **read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches, with the page structure reconstructed.** The note records that most agent evaluators are marked **preview** and that one consulted page is an explicitly **superseded** variant.

**The silences that matter, and they are this paper's subject rather than its footnote.** **No agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold.** Most of the evaluators the behavioural half depends on are model-judged, so a verdict from one carries no established relationship to a human's — and **no agreement figure is asserted anywhere in this paper, in the session, or in this repository.** The documentation offers no guidance on which split strategy is appropriate for which question, so the choice that determines the meaning of every score is left entirely to the reader. It states no expected cost, latency or coverage for running evaluation over a production trace corpus. And the access-controlled closure mechanism the session builds on **publishes no comparison against a person ticking a box, no defect-escape record, and no measure of how often a re-derived status was correct** — which is why this paper's strongest available claim is a definition rather than a finding. **Nothing in this session or paper is legal, regulatory or contractual guidance**, and the closure duties that will actually bind a learner belong to their organisation, their customer and their jurisdiction.
