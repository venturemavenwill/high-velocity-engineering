# WP-050 — Saying What the Measurement Does Not License

**Seminar day:** [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md) · **Module:** [M10](wiki/modules/M10.md) · **Quarter:** [Q5](wiki/quarters/Q5.md)
**Strands:** ME · PE · **Complexity class:** 4 · **Assessment-bearing**
**Platform anchor:** the student's own Foundry evaluation report, and the uncalibrated clause every judged score in it must carry · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server (preview) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends an entrustment judgement whose target is not honesty but usable honesty, and defends naming its two failure conditions as symmetric before the day begins. The central argument is that overclaiming and unusable hedging are the same failure of duty, that only the first is socially punished, and that a cohort trained for four days in the limits of its own evidence is therefore at risk of the second while believing itself rigorous — so the design must name the second failure explicitly or it will reward it. The paper defends constructing four audiences with different wants over one finding, defends including an audience whose want no honest formulation can satisfy, and defends placing consolidation after the performance so that every point indexes an encounter the student has just had. It further defends giving the first audience a documented artefact to argue with rather than only a want, because the sentence this cohort will find hardest to hold is that an authoritative score is not a calibrated one. It defends explicitly decoupling the day's long-horizon prediction check from assessment, on the ground that an assessed prediction stops being a prediction. It concedes that the design has no evidence that behaviour under constructed pressure predicts behaviour under real pressure, that the decoupled prediction check has no consequence anywhere, and that the day's added reconfiguration exercise shows students their defended finding was one of several without giving them anything to do about it. The first two concessions converge on one missing structure, and that convergence is used to elevate a standing pattern-level observation into a programme rule.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Four days have produced a finding. [S046](wiki/seminars/S046.md) built the criterion from named people. [S047](wiki/seminars/S047.md) validated it against drift. [S048](wiki/seminars/S048.md) characterised the judge. [S049](wiki/seminars/S049.md) bounded the estimate with its dependency structure argued.

The problem this day faces is that none of that work is self-executing. A finding with an honest interval, delivered to a person with an interest in a different answer, is not a communication — it is the start of a negotiation the student did not agree to enter.

**The students are honest.** That is not the difficulty and the day says so in its opening. Two years of programme have selected for it and four days of module have reinforced it. The difficulty is that honesty has three distinct failure modes at the point of delivery, and the cohort is protected against only one.

The first is overclaiming, which they will not do.

The second is **hedging into unusability**. A student who states the finding with every qualification the evidence requires, in the vocabulary the evidence is expressed in, has produced a statement the audience cannot act on. The duty is not discharged. And this failure is rewarded everywhere the student has been — it looks like rigour, it survives peer review, and no one has ever told them it is a failure. **A cohort four days deep in the limits of its own evidence is at maximum risk of it.** The day names it in advance and calls it symmetric with overclaiming, which is the design's strongest single move.

The third is **selection**. Every true statement is chosen from a set of true statements, and what the student did not say is part of what the audience heard. [S013](wiki/seminars/S013.md) phase 7 raised this thirty-seven days ago and left it open. Its epistemic half is answerable now; its ethical half is not, and goes to S088.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Low cognitive, extreme dispositional.** The finding is already built; nothing is computed today. This is deliberate and is the reason the module's load peak is placed at S049 rather than here: an entrustment judgement on communication under pressure is invalid if the student is simultaneously constructing the thing being communicated.

The interactivity that remains is between the four encounters. What the student says to A constrains what they can say to C without contradiction, and D's surviving sentence may be drawn from any of the three.

Management:

- **Nothing is built on the day.**
- **The finding is fixed** across all four encounters, by design, so that variation is entirely in the response.
- **Three minutes of reset** between encounters, which is short deliberately — the pressure should accumulate.
- **Audiences are trained role-players from scripts,** not peers, so that the pressure is consistent across students and the assessment is comparable.
- **Consolidation comes after,** which removes the load of holding seven points while performing.
- **The four reconfigurations are prepared in advance** against each student's S049 artefact and are executed rather than described, which is the only way phase 6 can carry them inside thirty-five minutes alongside seven points.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *A self-prediction of one's own softening,* item 3, checked against observed behaviour four hours later.
- *A sealed prediction about the agent's quotable sentence,* item 5, opened against what it actually produces.
- *Four audiences over one fixed finding,* phase 3.
- *An audience whose want cannot be met,* audience C.
- *An audience who will quote one sentence,* audience D, placed last.
- *An audience who contradicts you with a document,* audience A, placed first.
- *Naming the encounter in which you were least honest,* phase 4.
- *Consolidation after performance,* phase 6.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *A real audience.* Not available four times in one day with a fixed finding. This is §8 objection 1.
- *The ethical analysis of selection.* Point 3 answers the epistemic half only. S088's.
- *Written communication.* Every encounter is spoken. The written form is S085's, where the artefact outlives the conversation.
- *Hostile or bad-faith audiences.* All four want something in good faith. A bad-faith audience is a different skill and is not in the programme.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The day's four audiences are a construction, not a taxonomy of the audiences an engineer meets. **Safe** as a set of pressures to have felt before meeting them unrehearsed — the builder's pressure, the schedule's pressure, the affected party's pressure, and the quotation's pressure are distinct and are each common. **Unsafe** as a claim that these four exhaust the space, and specifically unsafe as preparation for the two cases the day excludes: an audience acting in bad faith, and an audience with authority over the student's employment. Neither is rare in practice, and the day's repertoire is not adequate to either. This is stated to students at phase 6.

**The platform instance, scoped in the strong form.** Two pieces of platform content enter this day and each needs its boundary.

*The uncalibrated clause, under audience A's pressure.* **Safe** as preparation for any conversation in which the authority of a source is offered as evidence about the quality of a number — which is most conversations about vendor tooling, and many about instruments the student's own organisation built. **Unsafe** as a rule about vendors, or as a licence to treat any unmeasured quantity as worthless. The clause says that a number whose agreement with human judgement is unmeasured cannot be reported as though it were measured; it does not say the number is wrong, that the evaluator is bad, or that the evaluation should not have been run. **A student who deploys the clause as a way of declining to give an answer has committed the day's second named failure using the day's own vocabulary**, which is the specific misuse the assessors are told to look for.

*Phase 6's four reconfigurations.* **Safe** as a demonstration that a judged finding is a claim about a system under a configuration, and that some of the configuration was chosen by a vendor. **Unsafe** as a demonstration that the finding was arbitrary. Three of the four runs change what is being measured and one changes only where the pass line sits; conflating them teaches that evaluation is unstable rather than that it is conditional, which is the module's standing affective risk in its most corrosive form. The distinction is stated at the table and again at point 7.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **audience C**.

Every communication exercise the students have met has had a solution: a formulation that satisfies the other party. Audience C wants to know whether the system is safe for them, which the evidence cannot establish, and there is no wording that satisfies them. Students discover this in real time, with a person in front of them, and the discovery is the day's emotional centre.

That it is placed third and not last matters. A student who meets C last leaves the day with an unresolved failure. A student who meets C third has fifteen minutes and audience D to work out that saying *nothing I have can tell you that* is an answer rather than a defeat.

Audience D is the second mechanism and is placed last because it converts the day's lesson into something operational. The student cannot control the surviving sentence. They can control the set of sentences from which it is drawn. That is a strategy, it is available immediately, and it is the sentence most students report keeping.

Phase 5 supplies the day's other affective load, and it is why the phase is placed after the assessed work rather than before. Category 3 — failures that occurred and were not stated — is a measurement of what a student did not think of, delivered five weeks late, about a system real people used. It is uncomfortable in a way nothing else in the programme is, and it is deliberately unassessed.

The competence risk is at phase 4's last question. A student asked to name the encounter in which they were least honest may hear an accusation. The framing is set before phase 1: **this is a calibration question, and declining to answer is itself recorded as an answer rather than as a refusal.**

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items. Item 3 is unusual in the programme: a prediction about one's own conduct, checked against observation within the same day. Most self-predictions in the programme concern outcomes; this one concerns integrity, and the four-hour gap is short enough that the student remembers what they wrote. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction** and is placed here rather than after the encounters, per instrumentation rule 8, so that it is written before the student knows how hard audience D is.

**Phase 2 (30 min).** Seven retrievals and a bridge. The bridge names the day's lineage to S028 step 7 explicitly, which is worth the minute: a student who understands that the epistemic work is already done spends the day on the part that is new. **The uncalibrated clause is retrieved rather than taught**, because it is the sentence four audiences will contest and reconstructing it under time pressure is the state the student will be in during the encounters.

**Phase 3 (60 min).** Four encounters, twelve minutes each. The scripts are the day's most expensive artefact and the reason role-players are trained rather than drawn from the cohort — a peer playing a product owner produces a different and weaker pressure, and produces it inconsistently across students, which makes the assessment incomparable. **Audience A's script now presses on the uncalibrated clause specifically**, and it is first rather than later by design: it is the only encounter in which the student is contradicted by an artefact rather than by a want, and a student who concedes calibration to a vendor's authority in the opening twelve minutes has surrendered the strongest thing they hold before the harder audiences arrive.

**Phase 4 (60 min).** Defence after the level is formed, per rater discipline rule 7, **with the three records carried in from S046 to S049 read as part of the artefact** — this day has no build, and they are the only evidence available of how the finding was made. The A-versus-C question is the diagnostic one: a student who adjusted vocabulary between them has done the day's work, and a student who adjusted the claim has not. Two questions are added: one testing whether the student can distinguish an authoritative source from a calibrated one under contradiction, and one testing whether a logged intervention was understood.

**Phase 5 (35 min).** S045's commitment discharged. Three categories, individual results private, aggregate to the cohort, and no assessment attached.

**Phase 6 (35 min).** The four reconfigurations first, then seven points, assessors absent. **The reconfigurations discharge the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s executed-cases requirement on a day whose phase 3 is an assessment**, following the precedent [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md) set, and they are placed after the judgement and behind the error-clinic firewall so that a student whose finding moves is not penalised for having discovered it. Point 2 is the day's most-needed correction. Point 3 discharges half of S013's open question and names S088 for the rest. Point 7 gains a sixth thing owed to a reader for a judged finding, and marks it perishable in content and durable in existence.

**Phase 7 (40 min).** A limitation that was stated, in writing, and ignored — in a section the decision-maker did not read — then the agent case. The instructor case is chosen precisely because nobody in it was dishonest. **The agent case is an omission rather than an error**, which is the only shape of failure in this programme that an agent produces by default and that a line-by-line review provably cannot see, and it is therefore the only agent case that indexes point 3 rather than point 1.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 3 against phase 4's named encounter; item 5 opened against the sentence the agent actually produces, set beside the sentence the student gave audience D; predicted against awarded level; category 3's cohort total stated plainly; the S010 instrument's sixth administration with the full five-quarter series shown.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with communication training will handle A, B and D fluently. Predicted failure: fluency at C, where fluency is the wrong response and a smooth reassurance is the specific error. Countermeasure: the named failure condition — a student who satisfies audience C is either mistaken or has changed the finding, and the assessors check which. This is the only assessment rule in the programme that treats a successful-looking outcome as evidence of failure.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is freezing at C or D. Countermeasures: the two failures are named before phase 1, so a student who says *I cannot tell you that* knows in advance it is an allowed answer; C is placed third rather than last; and phase 6 comes after, so nobody has to hold the framework while performing.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Unlikely, but a student who handles all four cleanly is asked at phase 4 what they would have said to an audience with authority over their employment — which is outside the day's scope and is named as such, and is used diagnostically rather than assessed.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **cynicism**: four audiences all wanting the finding to say something else may produce a student who concludes that communication is management and that everyone is trying to extract a claim. This is the module's dispositional load in its most corrosive form and it would damage every remaining quarter.

Countermeasures: all four audiences act in good faith and this is stated; audience C's want is described as legitimate rather than as pressure; point 5 frames the unmeetable want as a case with an answer rather than as a trap; phase 7's case contains no dishonest party; and phase 6 is run with assessors absent so that the discussion can be candid. The instructor states once that every person in the four encounters wanted the same thing the student wanted, which is to make a good decision, and that this is why the encounters are hard rather than why they are hostile.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by S053, S062, S070, S085 and S088.

**S070 at roughly eight weeks is the real test.** The release-decision day has a real audience and a real consequence, and it is where this day's capability either transfers or does not.

**S085 is the written form.** A statement that outlives the conversation cannot be repaired by the next sentence, and the surviving-sentence strategy from audience D becomes the whole problem rather than a part of it.

**S088 receives the deferral.** The ethical half of selective true reporting — when selection becomes deception — is named at phase 6 and carried there. This is the second half of a question opened at S013 and it will then have been outstanding for two years, which is the longest deferral in the programme and is recorded as such.

Retained content: the two symmetric failures, the unmeetable want, the surviving sentence and its set, selection as communication, and the placement duty. Five items.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 3, week 8.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**The quarter's second entrustment judgement**, and the closest precursor to EA-3. Expected 2b.

Level formed from the artefact, **the three records carried in from S046 to S049**, and the four encounters, before the defence, per rule 7. Narrative substantiation mandatory and required to cite specific moments. **This day produces no build**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s provision for assessment-bearing days, and the records are read rather than re-produced.

Three named failure conditions: a claim allowed to stand stronger than the interval supports in any encounter; four encounters that are the same words four times; and a satisfied audience C. **Withdrawing the uncalibrated clause under audience A's pressure falls under the first** and is not a fourth condition — the clause is part of the finding, not a caveat attached to it, and treating it as a separate rule would invite the reading that it is optional.

One specification item in ME201 with PE201. Per rule 1, the checked elements are the delivered finding carrying its uncalibrated clause, the four audience wants and the four responses, the self-identified least-honest encounter or an explicit declining, **the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement carried in from S046 to S049**, and one sentence the student will not say again. **Nothing else is assessed.**

**Phase 5's result is not assessed, and the specification says so in writing.** The argument is short: an assessed prediction is a strategy. A student marked down for category 3 will, at the next S045-equivalent, state every conceivable failure mode, which drives category 3 to zero while destroying the quantity the check was built to measure. The design would rather have an honest measurement of what students fail to anticipate than a manipulated one, and this is the trade §8 objection 2 examines.

**Phase 6's four reconfigurations are not assessed and the assessors do not receive them.** A finding that moves under reconfiguration is a fact about evaluation, not a fault of the student who defended it, and an assessor who saw the movement could not unsee it when reading a defence formed an hour earlier.

Programme instrumentation: the item 3 self-prediction against phase 4's named encounter is the day's headline measure and the programme's only direct measurement of integrity calibration; **item 5's sealed prediction against the sentence the agent produces is the module's closing frontier datum**, read by the following year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) per instrumentation rule 9; category 3's cohort total is the third long-horizon prediction check after S020 and S040; and the S010 instrument's sixth administration completes its five-quarter series.

Dispositions: **integrity**, at every phase.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: constructed pressure is not pressure, and the day assesses a capability it cannot observe.**

At its strongest: the student knows audience B's launch date is fictional, that audience C is a role-player who will not use anything, and that audience D will not publish. Nobody's job is at risk and nobody's product ships. Whatever the four encounters measure, it is performance in a simulation — and the day's own entrustment statement calls it a precursor to EA-3, which is a real communication with a real decision-maker. A student who is fluent under constructed pressure and folds under real pressure will be awarded 2b and will be wrong.

The rebuttal has two parts. First, the alternative is unavailable: four real audiences, in one day, over one fixed finding, with comparable pressure across a cohort, does not exist. Second, the day is honest about what it is — the entrustment statement says explicitly that this is not EA-3, and no level awarded here carries forward.

What is conceded is that **the design has no evidence that constructed-pressure behaviour predicts real-pressure behaviour, and it has the means to get some.** S070 puts each student in front of a real release decision with a real audience eight weeks later. Comparing each student's S050 level against their S070 conduct would test the day's central assumption directly, and it is the kind of check the programme performs elsewhere. **The design does not specify that comparison.** It is not expensive; it requires one field in the S070 record.

**Objection 2: the prediction check has been made honest by making it consequence-free, and a measurement with no consequence changes nothing.**

At its strongest: category 3 measures what a student did not think to say about a system real people used. It is the most valuable single datum the programme collects about engineering judgement. The design responds to it by telling the student the number and doing nothing else — no assessment, no remediation, no requirement that the student act on it, and no later occasion at which it is revisited. A student with a large category 3 receives an uncomfortable afternoon and no mechanism.

The rebuttal on the assessment point stands and is defended in §7: assessing the check destroys it. That argument is correct and the design would make the same choice again.

But it is a defence of *not assessing*, and the objection is about *not responding*, which is a different thing. **This the design concedes.** Category 3 is measured, reported, and dropped. The obvious response — carry each student's category 3 forward to their next failure-mode statement, at S066 or S070, and require them to say what they have changed — is available, costs one artefact element, and preserves the decoupling entirely, since the requirement is to have used the information rather than to have scored well on it.

**The elevation.** These two concessions are the same concession. Objection 1 identifies evidence produced at S050 with no later occasion that consumes it. Objection 2 identifies evidence produced at S050 with no later occasion that consumes it. They are the fourth and fifth instances of the standing pattern-level observation that **this design measures more readily than it responds**, and the first two in which the missing response is specifically a *forward link to a named later occasion* rather than an absent action at the point of measurement.

Per the programme's convention, three instances elevate an observation to a rule. This is five, with a mechanism now visible: [instrumentation rule 4](wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) requires an instrument to state what its result changes, and a designer can satisfy it by naming a change that occurs at the same occasion — which every instrument in the programme does, and which is exactly why the deferred case was never covered. **The rule is therefore widened, in the same form as the four widenings recorded in the Whitepaper Standard's scope rule.** The widening is made at the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) as instrumentation rule 6, applies from S051 forward, and this day is not retrospectively amended — its two concessions stand so that the failure remains legible.

**Objection 3: phase 6's reconfigurations tell a student their defended finding was one of several and give them nothing to do about it.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn, and it is separate from the pair the elevation is built on rather than a third instance of it.

At its strongest: the student spends an hour defending a number to four people. An hour later the same number is recomputed four ways, three of which change what was being measured, and every one of the four changes was available to them before they started. **The order is deliberate and it is also cruel.** The design's defence is that a student who reconfigured before the encounters would have defended a better-understood number and learned less, which is true and is an argument for the design's convenience as much as for the student's development. And the exercise's honest content — that a judged finding is a claim about a system under a configuration — arrives at the point in the day when the student has least capacity to act on it, is not assessed, is not carried into any later artefact, and is deliberately hidden from the assessors. **A student who concludes from it that their 2b was awarded for defending an arbitrary number has drawn a reasonable inference from what the day did**, and nothing in the day contradicts them.

**The objection is conceded.** The design has built a demonstration whose emotional force is at its maximum precisely where its usefulness is at its minimum.

**Two fixes are named and one is made.** The made one is a distinction, stated at the table and again at point 7: three of the four runs change what is measured and one changes only where the pass line sits, and conflating them teaches that evaluation is unstable rather than that it is conditional. That is the difference between the day's lesson and the inference the objection describes, and it costs a sentence. **The unmade one is the forward link**, and it is the same shape as objections 1 and 2 above: the natural response is to require the student's next evaluation report — at S066 or S070 — to state the configuration its numbers were produced under, which would consume phase 6's finding at the cost of one artefact element. **It is not specified here, and per instrumentation rule 6 that is a defect this day would be required to remedy if it fell after S051.** It does not. The concession therefore stands with the other two, and it is the third instance in one day of the design generating evidence it has arranged no occasion to use.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Fewer than half of students will correctly predict, at item 3, the encounter in which they in fact softened. *Instrument:* item 3 against phase 4's named encounter, with assessor observation as the tiebreak where the two disagree.

**Prediction 2.** The modal failure will be unusable hedging rather than overclaiming, and the ratio will be at least three to one. *Instrument:* assessor substantiations coded against the two named failure conditions. **This is the prediction the design most wants to be wrong about**, since the day names both failures in advance specifically to prevent it.

**Prediction 3.** Audience C will produce the lowest-rated encounter for a majority of students, and audience D the highest variance. *Instrument:* per-encounter assessor notes.

**Prediction 4.** Category 3 will be non-empty for a large majority of students, and its most common content will be operational rather than statistical — failures of integration, load and human use rather than of the model. *Instrument:* category 3 entries coded by kind. **If this holds, it is an argument that M09 and M10 taught the wrong half of the problem**, and it is carried to Q7's design.

**Prediction 5.** The sentence Copilot produces at phase 8 will be judged more quotable than the student's own by a majority of the room, and will carry less of the finding's uncertainty. *Instrument:* a show of hands on quotability, against the two sentences coded for retained qualification. **The prediction the design wants is that both hold at once**, because that is the day's whole argument about audience D in one comparison — the most quotable sentence available is not the one you would choose to have quoted, and the tool optimises for the first.

**Prediction 6.** In the phase 3 encounters, audience A's pressure on the uncalibrated clause will produce more softening than any other single scripted move, and softening there will predict softening at B. *Instrument:* per-encounter assessor notes coded for clause retention, against the phase 4 named encounter. **If the clause survives A and the finding softens at B anyway, the day's placement argument is wrong** and A should be moved later.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** Risk communication under audience pressure, the distinction between stating a limitation and communicating one, selective reporting, entrustment judgement construction, ordinal supervision scales and narrative substantiation are grounded in [research/04-professional-formation/](research/04-professional-formation/collected-materials.md), [research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md) and [research/08-assessment-epas/govaerts-narrative-assessment-literature.md](research/08-assessment-epas/govaerts-narrative-assessment-literature.md). The criterion, judge and interval machinery carried into the day follows [research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md), [research/03-measurement-evaluation/zheng-mt-bench-chatbot-arena.md](research/03-measurement-evaluation/zheng-mt-bench-chatbot-arena.md) and [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md).

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The four reconfigurations at phase 6 are all documented behaviour, read on Microsoft Learn 2026-07-26. The conversation split strategies — last turn as the default, full, per-turn, and custom splitters — are from the Agent Framework evaluation page, which states in its own words that how you split determines what you are evaluating. The evaluation level, `turn` or `conversation` with `turn` as the omitted default, is from the cloud-evaluation page, where conversation-level evaluation is **marked preview**. The default pass threshold of 3 on a 1-to-5 Likert scale, overridable by the user, is from the general-purpose and RAG evaluator pages. The judge model is supplied as an initialisation parameter, and the rubric-evaluator page publishes a ranked list of candidate judge models with no measurement attached to any row.

**The silence is the day's subject and it is singular.** **No agreement with human judgement is published for any Foundry evaluator**, and the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](wiki/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md) prohibits any page in this wiki from filling that gap. It follows that every judged score in every student's finding is uncalibrated, that the clause saying so is part of the finding rather than a caveat on it, and that **audience A's strongest argument — that the score came back from a documented service operated by a large company — is true and is not evidence about agreement.** Two further silences are recorded and not taught here: no minimum detectable effect appears in the comparison output that produced the interval, and no cadence is published for when a criterion should be re-validated. Consistent with the folder-wide asymmetry in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), every page cited across this module states scales, defaults, limits and cadences and states no accuracy anywhere.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** Role-play with trained standardised audiences, consolidation placed after performance, self-prediction checked against observation, expanding-interval retrieval and error management training are cited for **direction and mechanism only**. No effect size is asserted for any of them, and none is asserted for AI-assisted productivity anywhere in this paper. **No claim is made about the magnitude of transfer from constructed to real pressure**; §8 objection 1 states that the design has no evidence on the point.

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The symmetric-failures framing and its statement in advance; the four-audience construction and their ordering; audience A's scripted pressure on the uncalibrated clause and the argument for placing it first; audience C's unmeetable want and the failure condition attached to satisfying it; audience D's surviving-sentence strategy; the item 3 self-prediction; the item 5 sealed prediction and the phase 8 comparison of two sentences; the phase 4 least-honest question and the treatment of declining as an answer; the four reconfigurations at phase 6 and their placement behind the error-clinic firewall; the explicit decoupling of phase 5 from assessment; and all six §9 predictions are this design's own reasoning, untested. The §8 concessions are three. The first two are conceded without qualification, identify evidence generated with no later occasion that consumes it, and their convergence is used to **elevate a standing pattern-level observation to a programme rule** — instrumentation rule 6, widening rule 4 to require that a deferred response name its occasion — which applies from S051 and does not retrospectively amend this day. The third concedes that phase 6's reconfiguration exercise lands where the student can least act on it, records the one-sentence distinction as made, and records the forward link as unmade — **which is the same defect as the first two, found for a third time in the same paper, in content that did not exist when the rule was written.**
