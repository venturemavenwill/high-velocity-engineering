## In plain terms

A runbook is a document written by somebody with full context, for somebody with none, to be read at an hour when the author cannot be asked.

Authors cannot audit their own context. Every step they write is checked against a model of the system they carry in their head, and the model silently supplies what the document omits. The result is a document that is completely correct and partly unperformable, and no amount of careful re-reading by its author will reveal which parts.

There is one cheap instrument that reveals it: give the document to somebody else, watch them run it, and say nothing. The stops are the defects. **The author's silence is not politeness — it is the measurement**, because a spoken sentence substitutes the author's context for the document's and destroys exactly the reading the exercise exists to take.

The session then asks for the revision, and watches for the characteristic bad repair: adding a paragraph of background that would have prevented that one executor from asking that one question, rather than fixing the step that could not be performed.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching runbooks through a single cold execution by a non-author rather than through a documentation standard. The argument is that the defects that matter in operational documentation are invisible to the author by construction, and that observation is the only instrument available at a session's scale. The session produces one runbook for one failure that actually occurred, an execution record with at least one recorded stop, and a revision. It leans on a distinction between having information and being able to act on it, taken from a commercially interested practitioner model with no independent evaluation, and uses it for direction and mechanism only. The paper concedes that a single execution by a single peer on a prepared morning is a demonstration and not a measurement, and that nothing behind this programme establishes that systems with executed runbooks survive longer than systems without.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Operational documentation fails in a specific and repetitive way, and the failure is structural rather than a matter of effort.

The author knows which dashboard the entry condition is read from, what "normal" looks like on it, which of two similar-looking failures is the common one, and who actually answers the escalation channel at night. None of that is in the document, and none of it is missing by choice — it is missing because it never presented itself as information. It presented itself as the situation.

Three consequences follow, and each has a countermeasure in the session's standard. Entry conditions become comparatives with no reference value, so the operator cannot tell whether they are in the runbook. Branches lose the evidence that selects them, so the operator picks the wrong one confidently. And escalation routes end at people rather than roles, so the document expires when the author's telephone number does.

**The session's job is to make one author see one of these in their own document**, in a way that is not arguable, within a single session.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session assumes an incident history from Stage 7 and a manifest line from [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md) that named runbooks as a transfer object with an owner.

The requirement that the runbook address a failure that **actually occurred** is load-bearing and depends on that inheritance. An invented failure produces an invented runbook, whose defects are also invented, and the execution then tests the learner's imagination rather than their document.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 The knowledge / ability distinction, and its warrant.** The session rests on the claim that giving a person the information needed to perform an act and that person being able to perform it are different states, that the second requires practice, time and a tolerant environment, and that some people, for some changes, do not reach it.

That distinction comes from a **practitioner change model published by the consultancy that owns its trademark and sells training and certification in it.** Its supporting evidence is that firm's own benchmarking survey work — practitioner self-report, no methodology reproduced, no independent replication — and **no independent evaluation of the model appears anywhere in it.** It licenses **direction and mechanism only**: no ordering strength, no rate, no proportion, no magnitude, no claim that using the model improves outcomes.

**One correction travels with it.** The model is widely summarised as holding that effort past the blocking element is wasted. **The book does not say that**, and this repository withdrew the claim on reading it; the author's own formulations are conditional, with a single absolute confined to instruction in the absence of willingness. The conditional form is the one used here.

**3.2 Why observation rather than review.** A document review by a second author produces opinions about wording. An execution produces a location: the executor stopped at step four. The second is a fact about the document and the first is a fact about the reviewer, and only one of them can be acted on.

**3.3 Why the author is silent.** The instrument measures what the document supplies. Anything the author says is not in the document, and speaking therefore does not help the executor — it destroys the reading and leaves the defect in place for the person who will be alone with it later.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**The silence is the design and it is the part most likely to be broken.** Watching somebody fail at something you can fix in a sentence is unpleasant, the social pressure in the room is entirely towards speaking, and the learner has a plausible justification available — that the executor is a peer rather than the real recipient, so the exercise is artificial anyway. The session pre-empts that by making the executor's stop the deliverable: a run with no stops is reported as a failed instrument, not as a good runbook.

**One safety exception is written in rather than left to judgement.** If the executor is about to do something with a consequence outside the exercise, the author stops the run rather than coaching it, and records that they did. The distinction between stopping and coaching is the thing being taught, and it recurs in a sharper form at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), where the operator is the actual recipient and the intervention condition must be written before the observation begins.

**The revision is the deliverable and the first draft is the instrument.** This is stated to learners in advance, because a learner who believes the first draft is the assessed object will write defensively and the execution will produce nothing.

**The review movement watches for the local repair.** Adding context that answers one executor's one question is the commonest fix and it does not generalise; fixing the unperformable step does. Naming this as the thing the room listens for converts it from a trap into a standard.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The rescue.** The author speaks. *Countermeasure:* the pairing is with a learner from a different engagement, so the author has no context to add cheaply, and the execution record has an explicit field for interventions.

**The happy-path runbook.** The learner documents the procedure rather than the response, and the executor completes it without difficulty. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires at least one branch with its selecting evidence, and a run with no stops is reported rather than praised.

**The unstartable runbook.** The entry condition is a comparative with no reference — the drafts look worse than usual. *Countermeasure:* the executor cannot begin, which makes this the one defect the instrument detects immediately and unarguably.

**The local repair.** *Countermeasure:* the review movement, which asks what changed rather than whether it is better.

**The single-execution generalisation.** A learner concludes from one peer's difficulty that the recipient will have the same difficulty. *Countermeasure:* stated in the session's limits and tested properly at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) with the actual recipient. **This countermeasure is a caveat rather than a mechanism**, and caveats lose.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. One specification item at [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md): at least one runbook with an execution record produced by a non-author, containing at least one recorded stop.

**The runbook's quality is not assessed and the executor's competence is not assessed.** What is checked is that the execution happened and left a record. An assessor marking runbook quality is marking their own model of the system, which is the defect the session exists to expose.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: one execution by one peer proves nothing, and the session presents it as though it did.**

At its strongest: the executor is a single person with idiosyncratic gaps, drawn from the same cohort with the same training, running the document once in a supportive room on a prepared morning with no time pressure and no consequence. Whatever they stop at may be a fact about them. Two executors would disagree; twenty would produce a distribution; one produces an anecdote, and an anecdote in this shape is exactly the evidence standard this programme criticises its source corpus for accepting.

**This is conceded without qualification.** The rebuttal does not rescue the inference — it changes the claim. The session does not assert that the observed stop is the defect the recipient will hit; it asserts that a document whose author has never watched anyone else run it contains defects the author cannot enumerate, and that one execution moves the count of known defects from zero. **The fix that was considered and declined** was multiple executors per runbook, which was rejected on session time, since it costs one execution per learner per peer and the session already runs both directions. The rejection buys coverage of the cohort at the price of the inference, and the objection stands against the price.

**Objection 2: real operational documentation is written for people with organisational context, and stripping it to what a stranger can perform makes it worse — longer, more literal, and unreadable by the people who will actually use it.**

At its strongest: an operations team that has run a platform for years does not want to be told which dashboard to open, and a document that tells them will not be read at all, so the session optimises for a reader who does not exist.

The rebuttal is that the reader does exist in this stage specifically: the transfer case is precisely the case where the operator has no context, and the supplied situation puts the credentials with a managed-service supplier who attended none of the engagement. **The objection is correct about steady-state documentation and wrong about handover documentation**, and the session's scope is handover. A team that writes every runbook this way is over-applying it, and the paper says so here rather than leaving it implied.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first-draft runbooks will contain an entry condition that a non-author cannot evaluate. *Instrument:* the executor's start time and their first question, coded *could start* / *could not start*.

**Prediction 2.** Authors will speak during the run more often than they predict they will. *Instrument:* a pre-run written commitment from each author on whether they expect to speak, compared with the observed intervention count in the execution record.

**Prediction 3.** The most common revision will be an addition of background rather than a repair of an unperformable step. *Instrument:* the diff between draft and revision, coded *context added* / *step repaired* / *branch added* / *escalation changed* by a second reader.

**Prediction 4.** Runbooks written for a failure that actually occurred will produce more recorded stops than runbooks written for a hypothetical failure. *Instrument:* stop counts, split by whether the learner's chosen failure appears in the inherited incident history. **This is the prediction that matters**, because if it fails the session's requirement for a real failure is an unnecessary constraint and should be removed.

**None of these has been measured.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That an author cannot find the defects in their own operational documentation, that observation is the cheapest instrument that can, and that silence is what makes the instrument work.

**What the evidence licenses.** That having information and being able to act are distinct states is direction and mechanism from a commercially interested practitioner source. Everything about the session's method is this design's argument.

**The concession worth remembering.** One executor, once, is an anecdote. The session's claim is narrower than the exercise feels: not that you have found the defect, but that you now know your document has some.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That the practice corpus describes no transfer of runbooks or of any other operating artefact to the customer, and that its discovery method contains no observation of any kind — nobody is asked to demonstrate a deployment, share a screen during a pipeline run, or set up an environment while being watched. Recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md). Also verified: the warrant, structure and limits of the change model cited in §3, recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md) on a complete reading, including that no independent evaluation of it is cited anywhere in it and that the claim about effort past the blocking element was withdrawn by this repository as unsupported by the text.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That an author's own knowledge of a system substitutes silently for what their documentation omits, and that a person's demonstrated performance is a different observable from their stated understanding. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The cold-execution instrument; the requirement of author silence and its framing as measurement rather than courtesy; the written safety exception and the stop-rather-than-coach rule; the requirement that the failure be one that actually occurred; the standard's four elements — observable entry condition, evidence-selected branches, role-based escalation, stated termination; the claim that a run with no stops indicates a failed instrument; and all four predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** **This session has no platform anchor and relies on no vendor documentation.** Runbook content is platform-specific and perishable; the session's subject is the relationship between an author and a reader, which is not. **The silence worth recording:** operational documentation guidance published by platform vendors describes what a runbook should contain and does not describe how to find out whether anybody other than its author can execute it. No vendor documentation known to this programme publishes an execution-observation practice, a defect rate for author-written procedures, or any measure of whether an operating procedure was performable by its recipient. If such material appears, this session acquires an anchor and this paper records its version and its omissions.
