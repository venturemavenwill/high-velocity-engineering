## In plain terms

The corpus behind this stage contains a catalogue of difficult situations in a facilitated session, each with a way of handling it — and, unusually, with a list of things not to do.

**The prohibitions are the valuable part**, and the session is built entirely around them. The reason is that a room going badly is precisely when a facilitator's judgement is worst: they are behind, they are being watched, the thing they prepared is not landing, and every instinct is pointing at the intervention that makes the discomfort stop. Advice about what to do is not much use in that state. A short list of things you promised yourself you would not do is.

So the rehearsal is marked against the prohibitions and deliberately not against whether the room recovered. **A learner who handled a situation clumsily while keeping the prohibition passes.** A learner who rescued the room by breaking one does not, however well it went, because outcome marking teaches that the intervention which happened to work was correct — and the whole point of the catalogue is that some interventions work in the room and cost you the engagement.

The session then does the same argument once more in a form the learner can inspect. An escalation design answers three questions a facilitator answers by instinct — when does this stop, what happens next, and who receives it — and written as configuration they come apart. **The one that matters is the third case nobody designs for: not understanding, not working, and not agreeing are three different failures**, and a design that routes a sceptic to the same place as a parse failure has decided the customer is malfunctioning. Which is exactly what a tired facilitator does at forty minutes, only slower and without a record.

## Abstract

This paper defends a rehearsal assessed against prohibitions rather than outcomes, and defends closing it with a configuration exercise that commits the same error at machine speed.

The argument runs in four parts. **First**, difficult-room handling is selected under degraded judgement, so the useful unit is a pre-committed prohibition rather than a repertoire. **Second**, outcome-based evaluation systematically endorses the interventions that relieve the facilitator's discomfort, which is why the corpus's own prohibition lists are its most useful and least justifiable content. **Third**, most of the catalogue is about conditions with a person standing in front of them, so the intervention with any chance of working changes what speaking costs rather than appealing to the individual. **Fourth**, an escalation design makes the stopping decision separable into a condition, a recipient and a payload — three things a facilitator fuses under pressure — and makes visible the specific collapse this session most wants a learner to notice.

The paper records an amendment made while writing it: the session as first designed rehearsed the room and produced no artefact anybody could inspect afterwards, so its central claim about near-misses rested entirely on self-report in a session whose thesis is that self-assessment under pressure is exactly what fails. The correction adds the escalation design and is documented at §4.5 rather than applied silently.

The paper concedes that marking against restraint can produce paralysis and that the protection against it is untested; that no prescribed handling in the corpus has been evaluated against any alternative; that the prohibitions have less support than the prescriptions; that one claim attached to the catalogue — that heard sceptics become strong advocates — is a convenient belief taught as a specimen; and that the error-management literature offering the strongest reason to rehearse this way **measured no on-the-job performance anywhere.**

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A facilitator in a deteriorating room has one dominant motive, and it is not learning about the customer. It is to make the deterioration stop.

Almost every intervention that stops it costs something that is invisible at the time. Answering the sceptic ends the objection and removes the person best placed to tell you what will block the work. Filling the silence ends the discomfort and narrows the answer. Guessing at an unanswerable question restores your standing and puts an unrecorded fiction into somebody's notes. Cutting the participant exercise because you are behind protects the schedule and removes the only part where the customer speaks.

**Each of those is locally successful and globally expensive**, and the local success is the only signal available in the room.

There is a second problem the platform work exists to reach. A facilitator who has kept every prohibition still has no artefact anybody can examine, so the practice cannot be reviewed, argued with or improved by anyone other than the person who lived it. **A design decision that leaves no artefact is not a decision anybody can be wrong about.**

The session's response is to move the evaluation off the outcome entirely, to rehearse under assignment rather than choice so that learners meet the situation they are worst at, and to require one artefact in which the same decision is written down and therefore checkable.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) supplies the forbidden list, the silence discipline and the authority-transfer mechanism, and without them the rehearsal degenerates into improvisation practice.

The prior it works against is that difficulty is caused by difficult people. Learners arrive with a person-centred model — the dominator, the sceptic, the quiet one — and the session's move is that most of the catalogue is about conditions with a person standing in front of them.

The platform work has a mixed entry profile. Learners who have built conversational systems will already have a fallback and will assume it covers everything, because a fallback that catches unparsed input feels complete. Learners who have not will design the handover and forget the condition. **Neither group arrives holding the distinction that decides the exercise: that disagreement is not a failure of comprehension, and that a design which cannot tell them apart will treat a customer's objection as a defect.**

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Low intrinsic, high affective, and the affective load is the design's intent rather than a side effect. Performing badly in front of peers while being told that performing badly is acceptable is uncomfortable in a specific and useful way: it separates the learner's self-assessment from the assessment being applied.

**3.2 Why assignment rather than choice.** A learner choosing their situation chooses the one they can already handle. This is design reasoning and no source tests it.

**3.3 Why the response to pressure is redirection rather than firmness.** The negotiation literature supplies the move and the vocabulary. **Negotiation jujitsu** treats a stated position as one possible option and asks about the interests and principles behind it; invites criticism of one's own proposal rather than defending it; recasts a personal attack as a description of the problem; and lets silence stand after a question rather than rescuing the other party from answering ([research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md)).

The same source supplies three further things this session uses. **Separate the people from the problem**, because criticism of a proposal is heard as criticism of a person, and because inferring intentions from one's own fears is the commonest error in a room that has turned. **Negotiate the process when the process is the problem** — its taxonomy of tactics gives a facilitator a vocabulary for the difficult room, and its instruction is to recognise the tactic, raise it explicitly and negotiate the rules, while **not diagnosing every difficult behaviour as manipulation.** And **the alternative to agreement is the standard** against which any offer is judged: a facilitator who has not thought about what happens if the session produces nothing will accept anything rather than let that happen, which is the mechanism behind every concession in this catalogue.

**Its limits.** A practitioner framework developed through analytic distinction and worked cases; its historical examples illustrate mechanism without isolating causes; **it reports no controlled comparison of anything**, and no effectiveness claim for teaching it is warranted by it. Its own later material concedes that positional bargaining is economical in narrow cases, that fair standards conflict, and that cultural and gender patterns are clues for inquiry rather than predictions about an individual.

**3.4 Why silence is reframed as conditions.** A moment where somebody could have spoken and did not leaves no trace except in the person who held back, and the reasons are usually a rapid calculation about interpersonal risk rather than a disposition ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)). The same source records taken-for-granted rules about speaking upward — do not criticise something the boss may have helped create, do not speak without solid data, do not speak up when the boss's boss is present, do not say anything negative in a group — which read against an attendance sheet explain most of what a facilitator experiences as reticence.

It follows that the intervention with any chance of working is a change to what speaking costs — a factual question they own, asked of them by name and by expertise, or removing the manager from the segment — rather than an appeal. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted, and the construct is being applied outside the setting it was developed in.**

**Its limits.** The 2019 trade book was read in full for this repository; **the 1999 empirical paper it restates has not been read here.** Its literature review is narrative rather than systematic and **reports no disconfirming study**; its case studies are secondary and outcome-selected; its leadership framework rests partly on consulting experience with **no trial reported.**

The interviewing literature supplies the operational discipline and one boundary. A reticent participant may have a different rhythm, need more structure or more silence, be reacting to the topic, connect better with another interviewer, or be inhibited by observers or a manager — and the team must **diagnose rather than label the person difficult** ([research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md](/research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md)). It also separates discomfort from danger and is explicit that persisting through discomfort is a judgement rather than an obligation. **Its limits: no controlled comparison, no measured effect, no validated threshold; first edition only.**

**3.5 Why the sceptic entry is treated as the strongest and its attached claim as the weakest.** The handling is good: validate the history, ask what was different last time, ask what would need to be true now, recruit them as the person who can explain the obstacles. The claim attached to it — that sceptics who feel heard often become the strongest advocates — is unevidenced and **converts a person who is telling you something inconvenient into a future asset**, which is exactly the shape of belief this programme trains learners to distrust. The session teaches the handling and quarantines the claim.

**3.6 Why a rehearsal that looks bad is not a failing rehearsal.** This is the section a learner should read before performing.

The error-management training literature defines its intervention as active exploration **plus** explicit instruction that errors are expected and informative, with **no further assistance when an error occurs** ([research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md)). Two findings shape this design. The advantage of such training is **absent when measured during training and present on later transfer** — so a rehearsal that looks bad in the room is not evidence the design is failing, and a programme evaluating itself inside the session will see nothing and drift toward scaffolding. And the advantage is largest on **adaptive transfer**, meaning structurally novel problems rather than harder versions of the practised one, which is what an unrehearsed difficult room is.

**Its limits, and they bound the whole section.** Every effect is relative to another training method; **no study in the pool used a no-training control**, so nothing speaks to absolute effectiveness. **No study measured on-the-job performance.** The pool is laboratory training, almost entirely software skills, mostly with students. And the variant closest to this rehearsal — in which learners examine others' errors rather than making their own — was **excluded from that literature by definition**, with its effectiveness raised as an open question and not answered. **Applying any of it to facilitation is this session's inference and not the paper's claim.**

**3.7 Why the escalation design belongs in this session.**

A facilitator's stopping decision has three parts that are fused in the moment: the condition under which this stops being the right conversation, what happens next, and who receives it. Written as configuration they separate, and the separation is the transferable content.

The platform also forces the distinction the session most wants: **failing to understand and failing to work are different cases** needing different next moves, because the evidence each carries is different — the utterance in one, the attempted action and its error in the other. A design that routes both to one place has thrown away the more useful signal.

The vendor's own axes bound what a handover can be. Declarative agents are "limited to Copilot's orchestrator and models" and "rely on user-initiated interactions", while custom engine agents own their orchestration and can "trigger actions automatically, even without direct user input" ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). **An agent that cannot act unprompted cannot escalate on its own initiative**, which decides the fallback before it is designed. The low-code path's stated limits — less fine-grained control over actions and response formatting, a rich-card schema ceiling, department-scale positioning — bound what the handover can carry.

**Its limits.** First-party vendor selection guidance, commercially framed, read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches, with several comparison tables truncated. **No adoption data, no failure-rate data and no evidence that any recommended mapping produces better outcomes.** Product names are in active flux. **The three-way distinction between not understanding, not working and not agreeing is this repository's framing and is not the vendor's.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The rehearsal record's third field is the session.** *Which prohibition were you closest to breaking* is answerable only by the learner, cannot be inferred from the transcript, and is the only place where the near-miss is recorded. A catalogue of handled situations tells you nothing; a record of what you nearly did tells you what to prepare for.

**4.2 The catalogue is not reproduced in this programme's structure.** The situations are taught in this design's own grouping and in original prose, and the corpus's ordering and labels are not carried. This is partly the confidentiality rule and partly pedagogy: a catalogue delivered as a list is memorised as a list, and the transferable skill is building your own from your own near-misses.

**4.3 The review movement asks for the worst record rather than the best.** A cohort in which nobody volunteers a bad one has not understood the marking scheme, and that is itself the diagnostic.

**4.4 What the platform workflow is for.** Not tooling competence. It makes the stopping decision decomposable: **a condition, a recipient and a payload are three separable things that a facilitator fuses under pressure and calls judgement.**

Its sharper purpose is to stage, in miniature and in a form that leaves a record, the exact collapse the session is about. A learner feeds their design a well-formed disagreement and watches it routed to the same place as a parse failure. **That is the sceptic answered rather than recruited, executed by a machine, consistently, at scale, and visible in a log** — which is the only setting in which a learner can watch themselves make the error without also having to manage a room.

The attack step's third question — *what does this design throw away at the moment it hands over* — is the one that carries into the room, because it is the question a facilitator never asks about their own interventions.

**4.5 A defect found in this session's own design, and the amendment.**

The session as first written produced one artefact: a rehearsal record, written by the learner, about their own near-misses, reviewed by peers who had watched a few minutes of role-play.

**Every field of it was self-report, in a session whose thesis is that judgement under pressure is exactly what fails.** The near-miss field is the design's best idea and it is also, by construction, unverifiable: nobody else can see what you nearly did. That is defensible for the near-miss itself. It was not defensible as the session's *only* output, because it left the learner with no object anybody could disagree with and nothing to review between the rehearsal and the assessment at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md).

Worse, the module had by this point twice established that guidance which leaves no artefact does not bind — at [S012](/wiki/seminars/S012.md) about waivers, at [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) about wellness — and this session's own product was an unverifiable narrative.

The amendment adds produce-items 5 to 7 and the workflow. **It does not assess the escalation design**, because assessing it would produce learners optimising a configuration rather than examining a reflex.

**The amendment is recorded rather than silently applied.** Assume there are others this paper has not caught.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Outcome self-marking.** The learner narrates the situation as a recovery. *Countermeasure:* the record's required near-miss field, and the review's single question.

**The converted sceptic.** The learner answers the objection well and reports it as success. *Countermeasure:* the standard's explicit statement that the room getting easier while nothing new was learned is the warning sign.

**Silence read as consent.** The learner records agreement they cannot attribute. *Countermeasure:* the requirement at [S018](/wiki/seminars/S018.md) that a finding name its cases, which makes unattributed consensus visible one session later. **Nothing catches it inside this session.**

**Prohibition inflation.** The learner writes many prohibitions so that some survive. *Countermeasure:* none available. The forbidden list is capped by [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md)'s plan, which requires the list to be usable under pressure, but the cap is not enforced.

**Disagreement routed as malfunction.** The learner's escalation design treats a well-formed objection as a comprehension failure and retries. *Countermeasure:* workflow step 4 supplies the sceptic input deliberately and step 5 requires the learner to write down what happened. **This catches the error in the artefact and not in the room**, which is the most this session can do.

**The configuration read as the lesson.** The learner produces a tidy escalation design and concludes the session was about conversational architecture. *Countermeasure:* the attack step's third question, which is about what a handover discards rather than about what it carries. Prediction 5 in §9 predicts this countermeasure failing.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md), [S018](/wiki/seminars/S018.md) and [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md).

The dominating-participant situation is deliberately left with a visible loose end here and closed at [S018](/wiki/seminars/S018.md): the room-management problem becomes an evidence problem when the loudest informant turns out to be the population. **The learner should feel the connection arrive rather than be told it.**

The escalation design exports one distinction at long range. **Not understanding, not working and not agreeing** is the same three-way split the evaluation stages need when a system's output is judged, and a learner who has met it as a routing decision meets it later as a measurement decision.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence.** One specification item at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md), shared with [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md): prohibitions honoured during the assigned difficulty, or the breach self-recorded.

**Never assessed:** whether the rehearsed room recovered; whether the learner appeared competent; and the quality, completeness or correctness of the escalation design.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: marking against prohibitions rewards inaction and produces facilitators who freeze.**

At its strongest: a scheme in which the safest submission is to do nothing and keep every prohibition selects for passivity. Real rooms need somebody to act. A learner who has been rewarded for restraint in rehearsal will, in a real session with a customer relationship at stake, do nothing while the session fails — and the design will have caused it.

**This is a serious objection and it is partly conceded.** The design's answer is that the record's fourth field requires the learner to say what the situation was evidence of about the customer, which cannot be filled by a learner who did nothing; and that the rehearsal is immediately followed by [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md), where a session that produces no finding fails on other grounds. **The declined fix is to mark handling on a combined scale of restraint and effectiveness.** It is declined because a combined scale collapses to the effectiveness term whenever the two conflict, which is the only case that matters. **Whether the two-session arrangement actually prevents passivity is untested.**

**Objection 2: prohibitions written by one organisation, from unrecorded incidents, are not a defensible curriculum.**

At its strongest: the catalogue has no provenance. No handling in it was compared with an alternative, no situation is reported with its outcome, and the prohibitions are the least justified part of an already unjustified artefact. Teaching them as binding — and marking against them — gives an unevidenced list the authority of an assessment scheme.

**Conceded without qualification.** The catalogue is codified experience with no evaluation attached and the paper says so in every place it appears. The design's position is that a prohibition list is the honest shape for knowledge of this kind: it records what somebody learned by getting it wrong, and it does not claim to have measured anything. **The declined fix is to teach the situations without marking against the prohibitions.** It is declined because an unmarked prohibition is advice, and this stage has already established at [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) what happens to advice inside a system that marks other things.

**Objection 3: staging the failure in a configuration teaches a routing lesson and calls it a facilitation lesson.**

At its strongest, and it is the objection the §4.5 amendment most deserves: a conversational design routes disagreement badly because intent classification is hard, not because the designer lacked judgement. The learner who watches their fallback mishandle a sceptic has learned something about a classifier's confidence threshold, and the paper then asserts — with nothing behind it — that the same learner will recognise the same reflex in themselves at forty minutes in a real room. That is an analogy presented as a mechanism. Worse, the platform documentation supporting the movement was read through search excerpts, publishes no outcome evidence of any kind, and does not itself draw the three-way distinction the session builds on.

**Partially conceded, and this is the amendment's real cost.** Three defences, none complete. The exercise is placed **after** the rehearsal, so the learner already has a name for the reflex before they see it executed. The required output is the **write-down at step 5** — what your design does with a sceptic, and whether that is right — which is a judgement rather than a configuration. And the design is **not assessed**, so the learner cannot be rewarded for a tidy routing table. **The declined fix was to drop the platform work and extend the rehearsal by one more situation** — keeping the room and dropping the artefact — rejected because a session with no inspectable output leaves its own central claim resting on self-report, which is the defect §4.5 records. **The cost of that rejection is exactly the analogy this objection names, and prediction 6 is the only thing that would detect it.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners' first rehearsal record will describe the outcome before it names the prohibition. *Instrument:* the records, coded for which appears first.

**Prediction 2.** The sceptic and the unanswerable question will produce more prohibition breaches than the silent participant. *Instrument:* observer tallies by situation, aggregated across the cohort.

**Prediction 3.** Learners who record a near-miss in rehearsal will self-report a breach at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md) more often than those who record none. *Instrument:* the [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md) submissions, coded for self-recorded breach, against this session's records. **If self-recording does not carry forward, the near-miss field is producing a rehearsal artefact rather than a habit.**

**Prediction 4.** Asked what they would have to observe for the sceptic-becomes-advocate claim to be false, most learners will produce a condition that could not occur. *Instrument:* the written condition, judged for whether it could fire, by the same criterion used at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md).

**Prediction 5.** A majority of first escalation designs will route a well-formed disagreement to the same destination as an unparsed utterance. *Instrument:* the submitted designs, coded *two paths* / *three paths* by a second reader, against the step-5 write-up.

**Prediction 6.** Learners who completed the escalation exercise will name the sceptic or the unanswerable question as their near-miss at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md) more often than learners who did not. *Instrument:* the [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md) records, coded by named prohibition, split within cohort on completion of the platform work. **This is the direct test of §8's third objection, and it is the prediction this paper would least like to lose** — a null means the configuration exercise taught routing and nothing else.

**Prediction 7.** Learners instructed to compare their item-5 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 6) will name a specific prohibition and a later cost more often than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* a second reader's binary judgement, split within cohort on exercise-6 completion. **The same prediction is made at [WP-010](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-010.md) through [WP-013](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-013.md), so a null across all of them would indict the comparison pattern rather than any one session.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That difficult-room handling must be evaluated against prohibitions because outcome evaluation endorses whatever relieved the facilitator; that most of the catalogue is about conditions rather than about people; and that a stopping decision has a condition, a recipient and a payload which a facilitator fuses under pressure.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing. The catalogue is unevaluated and uncheckable by any public reader. The prohibitions are the least supported part of it. The negotiation framework reports no controlled comparison of anything. The psychological-safety mechanism is carried outside its setting at the level of direction only, from a book whose underlying paper has not been read here. The error-management literature **measured no on-the-job performance anywhere** and its closest variant to this rehearsal was excluded from it by definition. The platform documentation evidences no outcome and does not itself draw the distinction the session builds on.

**The concession worth remembering.** A scheme that rewards restraint can produce paralysis, and the design's protection against that is an arrangement of two sessions nobody has tested. And note §4.5: this session established that judgement fails under pressure and then asked the learner to audit their own judgement under pressure, with no artefact. **The failure the session names is one the session committed.**
