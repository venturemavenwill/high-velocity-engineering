# WP-066 — The Baseline Nobody Measured

## In plain terms

You are about to claim the system improved something. The state before is gone, and nobody destroyed it — it was simply never recorded, because at the moment it could have been, everybody was thinking about whether the thing could be built.

**By the time anybody wants a before, the before has become a memory held by people with an interest in the after.**

The framework this module teaches from does the most instructive possible thing about this. **It instructs its reader to establish a baseline, and never exhibits one being established.** It catalogues metric definitions and never reports one measured. Its case-studies section is reserved and empty. **There is no worked example available to this programme**, and the session says so in its own text rather than hiding it in the paper.

So the session teaches four disciplines and one refusal.

The disciplines: define the measure so that two people compute it identically; find a record rather than a person, because a record made for another purpose was not made to come out any particular way; state the period rather than saying "before"; and name the instrument together with whether it will still exist later.

The refusal is the important one. **Where no baseline exists, say so and downgrade the claim.** Do not reconstruct it. A reconstructed baseline is not a weak baseline; it is a different thing wearing a baseline's clothes, and it is produced by exactly the people whose account of it should be trusted least.

And one item is imported from nowhere. **What result would make the customer conclude this had not worked?** The corpus's own question bank asks in every block what good would look like and how improvement would be measured. **It asks nowhere what failure would look like.** That absence is why the item is here.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching baseline establishment as a discipline whose commonest correct outcome is a written statement that there is no baseline and a downgraded claim. The argument is that a before state reconstructed after the result is produced by parties with an interest in it and is not evidence; that a measure whose definition moved between before and after has measured the definition; that an instrument the team will lose access to cannot support a repeatable claim; and that a claim with no disconfirming value is unfalsifiable in the same way an undisqualifiable engagement position was. The session installs a baseline record carrying the measure, the value, the period, the instrument and its taker; a disconfirming result written before measurement and put to the customer; a confounder list; and, where no baseline exists, a written downgrade naming what it gives up. The paper concedes that its own source contains no instance of any of this being done, that the disconfirming-result requirement is written past the source entirely, and that the downgrade it demands is professionally costly in a way the session cannot offset.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

The moment at which a baseline can be taken and the moment at which anybody wants one are separated by the whole engagement.

**At the start, nobody is thinking about measurement.** The question is whether the thing can be built, whether the customer will engage, whether the data exists. Measurement is a later problem and everybody is right that it is a later problem — **which is why the window for solving it closes before anybody notices it was open.**

At the end, the demand arrives suddenly and from somebody senior. **The only available source is the recollection of people who spent the intervening period building the thing**, and their recollection is not dishonest; it is reconstructed under exactly the conditions that make reconstruction unreliable.

There is a second problem which is worse because it is invisible. **A measure whose definition was refined during the engagement will be better defined at the end than at the start**, and the refinement almost always improves the after figure — not through bad faith, but because a definition improved by people building a system improves in the direction the system operates in.

And a third. **Nobody has written down what failure would have looked like.** The corpus's discovery apparatus asks what good looks like, in every block, and never asks the complement — so no engagement in it has a value that would have counted against the work.

The session's response is a record with dates, a record-first search strategy, a disconfirming value written first, and a licensed downgrade.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session operates on the requirement written at [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md) and made falsifiable at [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md), and on the feasibility question posed at [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md).

It assumes [S003](/wiki/seminars/S003.md), because the measure has to attach to the problem the customer experiences rather than to the system that was built, and a learner who has not separated those will baseline the wrong thing precisely.

**It descends directly from [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md).** The disconfirming result is the disqualifying condition in a measurement's clothing, and the same test applies: **a result that could never occur is a decoration.** That inheritance is stated in the session so that a learner recognises the shape rather than meeting it as new.

**The prior it works against is that a baseline is a number you go and get.** It is a record you either have or do not have, and the professional skill is what you do in the second case.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is low. The session's difficulty is that its best outcome is a weaker deliverable, and a learner has to accept that before the work makes sense.

**3.2 Why a record beats a recollection.** A record generated by the work — tickets, logs, invoices, rotas, exports — was made for another purpose by somebody with no stake in this question. **A recollection elicited for this question was made by somebody with one.** That is a difference in provenance rather than in accuracy, and it is the only difference available at this distance.

**3.3 Why the period must be stated.** "Before" is not a period; it is a direction. **Two people using "before" will select different windows, and the selection will be made after the result is known.** Stating the period is what makes the figure a measurement of something rather than of a choice.

**3.4 Why the instrument's future matters.** A baseline is only useful if the after measurement is comparable, and comparability requires the same instrument. **An instrument the team will lose access to at handover produces a before that can never have an after**, which is a defect discoverable now and not later.

**3.5 Why the disconfirming result is written first.** A failure condition written after the result is known is selected from the space of conditions that did not occur. **Writing it first makes it a prediction; writing it afterwards makes it a description.** This is the same ordering argument [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) made about the question column and [S062](/wiki/seminars/S062.md) made about the evidence standard.

**3.6 Why the downgrade is licensed rather than penalised.** If a missing baseline is a failure, learners will produce one. **Making the written statement an acceptable — indeed the expected — outcome removes the incentive to reconstruct**, and the incentive is the whole problem. The cost is that a weaker claim is genuinely weaker, and §8 concedes it.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 3 — what result would make the customer conclude this had not worked — is the instrument**, and its provenance is stated in the session itself: it is absent from the source's entire discovery apparatus, which asks the complement in every block and this nowhere.

**The work movement's third step takes the disconfirming result to the customer**, and its most valuable outcome is a refusal. **A customer who will not name a value that would have meant failure has told you how the result will be read**, and that goes in the record as a finding rather than as an obstacle.

**The second work step is a search strategy rather than an instruction.** "Establish a baseline" is what the source says and it is not actionable. **Look for a record made for another purpose** is actionable, and it names the class of evidence that is available in the situations where a baseline is missing.

**The review movement asks one question of a stranger — could you have taken this measurement yourself, from what is written here — because that question tests the record and not the number.** A record that passes it can produce an after measurement; a record that fails it has produced a figure and no method.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The retrospective baseline.** The likeliest failure and the hardest to detect from the artefact. *Countermeasure:* the date fields, the record-first search strategy, and the [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) element checking that the before and after were not produced together.

**The unfalsifiable improvement.** *Countermeasure:* opening item 3, the customer conversation, and the [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) element requiring the disconfirming result to be dated before the measurement.

**The moved measure.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that before and after share a definition and, where possible, an instrument, with the difference stated where they do not.

**The instrument you do not own.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that the instrument's owner be named and its future stated, which surfaces the problem at baseline time rather than at handover.

**The reconstructed memory.** The failure the licensed downgrade exists against. *Countermeasure:* the downgrade, made explicitly acceptable, plus the warning sign — your source for the before state is a person rather than a record.

**The measure nobody had.** A first-ever measurement presented as an improvement. *Countermeasure:* named in the failure list, and the [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) classification element, which forces the claim to be labelled descriptive.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: licensing the downgrade will produce a cohort that stops looking, because the written statement is cheaper than the search.**

At its strongest: any specification that accepts *we could not establish this* as a passing outcome will be met with that answer by most candidates, most of the time. **Establishing a baseline is genuinely hard work — finding records, negotiating access, agreeing a definition with somebody who does not want to agree one — and writing a paragraph explaining that it was not possible is an afternoon.** The design has removed the incentive to fabricate and replaced it with an incentive to give up, which is a different failure with the same output: no baseline.

**Conceded in part, and the rebuttal is narrower than the objection.** The written statement is not a paragraph; it must name **what the claim was downgraded to and what the downgrade gives up**, and a downgraded claim is a materially worse deliverable that the candidate then has to defend at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) and hand forward at [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md). **The cost is deferred rather than removed.** That is a real counterweight and it is weaker than fabrication pressure would have been in the opposite direction. **The declined fix is an element marking the adequacy of the search that preceded the downgrade.** Declined because search adequacy has no available standard — nothing in any source says how hard one should look — and an element with no standard is a marker's opinion in a specification's clothing. **The design accepts a cohort that under-searches over a cohort that reconstructs**, on the argument that an absent baseline is honest and a fabricated one is not, and that argument is a preference rather than a finding.

**Objection 2: teaching baseline discipline from a source that has never exhibited one is teaching a procedure with no demonstrated feasibility.**

At its strongest: the framework instructs, catalogues and reserves, and never once shows the work being done. **That may not be an oversight. It may be that establishing a baseline in a real commercial engagement is not achievable within the time and access available**, and that the framework's silence is an accurate record of what its authors could actually do. This session then assembles a procedure from first principles, presents it with the confidence of practice, and sends learners to attempt something no documented practitioner has completed.

**Conceded without qualification, and it is the most serious objection in this module.** The session states it in its own limits movement rather than confining it here. **The rebuttal is only that the alternative is worse**: a module that declined to teach baselines because none has been observed would leave learners producing after figures with no before, which is the current condition of the practice. **The declined fix is to reduce the requirement to a discussion rather than an artefact.** It is declined because a discussion produces nothing that can be handed forward, and [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) needs the confounder list as an input. **The feasibility of the procedure is unestablished, the session says so, and a learner should expect the downgrade to be the common outcome rather than the exception.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most candidates will be unable to answer opening item 1 with a value and a source. *Instrument:* the submitted opening answers, coded *value with named source* / *value without source* / *no value* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most candidates will leave opening item 3 blank or generic before instruction. *Instrument:* the answers coded *specific value capable of occurring* / *generic* / *absent*.

**Prediction 3.** Where a customer is asked for a disconfirming result, most will decline to name one. *Instrument:* the record's customer-response field, coded *named* / *declined* / *not asked*.

**Prediction 4.** Most baseline records will rest on a recollection rather than on a record made for another purpose. *Instrument:* the source field, coded *contemporaneous record* / *recollection* / *mixed*. **If recollection dominates after the second work step has been taught, the record-first strategy is not actionable as written and the session should supply a worked search over a specific class of record.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a before state reconstructed after the result is not a baseline; that a definition refined during the engagement improves in the direction the system operates; and that the honest outcome of most baseline work is a written statement and a weaker claim.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes, and less than usual about practice. **The source instructs and never demonstrates**, so the procedure taught here is assembled rather than observed. The disconfirming-result requirement is written past the source entirely and rests on an argument first made at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md).

**The concession worth remembering.** You are being taught something nobody in the source material has been shown to do. **Expect the downgrade**, and notice that it is the correct answer rather than the failed one.
