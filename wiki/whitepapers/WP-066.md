# WP-066 — The Baseline Nobody Measured

**Seminar day:** [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md) · **Module:** [M14](/wiki/modules/M14.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 4
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Agent Framework evaluations beside the published first-party Foundry IQ retrieval benchmark — the baseline run before the change, or not available afterwards at any price, and what happens when the baseline that *was* run gets renamed in the documentation that cites it

> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Class 1–4 evidence** | The four classes every paper sorts claims into — see [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) |
> | **Baseline** | The state of the measure before the intervention, with its period, its instrument, its unit of analysis and its taker recorded |
> | **Deferability** | Whether a measurement's subject survives the act you want to measure. Non-deferrable measurements have one window |
> | **Disconfirming result** | The value that would have meant the work did not succeed, written before any output was examined |
> | **Downgrade** | Restating a causal claim as an association, or an association as a description, when the evidence for the stronger form is absent |
> | **Split strategy** | The rule cutting a multi-turn interaction into a query half and a response half. It is the unit of analysis and it determines what a score means |
> | **Judge** | A model scoring another model's output against a rubric. A judge is a system — a model plus a prompt plus sampling settings — and changing any of the three changes the instrument |
> | **Gold-standard sample** | The small set of observations for which trustworthy labels exist, against which a cheap large-scale estimate can be corrected |

## In plain terms

You are about to claim the system improved something. The state before is gone, and nobody destroyed it — it was simply never recorded, because at the moment it could have been, everybody was thinking about whether the thing could be built.

**By the time anybody wants a before, the before has become a memory held by people with an interest in the after.**

The session opens on a distinction almost nobody draws. **Measurements differ in whether they can be deferred.** Latency, cost, failure rate and output quality can all be captured whenever you decide to look, because their subject persists. The state of the world before your change has no such property: **shipping destroys the subject, and the baseline is therefore the only measurement in the whole programme that cannot be taken later at any price.**

The framework this module teaches from does the most instructive possible thing about this. **It instructs its reader to establish a baseline, and never exhibits one being established.** It catalogues metric definitions and never reports one measured. Its case-studies section is reserved and empty. **There is no worked example available to this programme**, and the session says so in its own text rather than hiding it in the paper.

So the session teaches five disciplines and one refusal.

The disciplines: define the measure so that two people compute it identically; find a record rather than a person, because a record made for another purpose was not made to come out any particular way; state the period rather than saying "before"; name the instrument together with whether it will still exist later; and **state the unit of analysis**, because the same stored interaction produces different scores depending on how it was cut into a query and a response, and nothing in either number announces which question it answered.

The refusal is the important one. **Where no baseline exists, say so and downgrade the claim.** Do not reconstruct it. A reconstructed baseline is not a weak baseline; it is a different thing wearing a baseline's clothes, and it is produced by exactly the people whose account of it should be trusted least.

And one item is imported from nowhere. **What result would make the customer conclude this had not worked?** The corpus's own question bank asks in every block what good would look like and how improvement would be measured. **It asks nowhere what failure would look like.** That absence is why the item is here.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching baseline establishment as a discipline whose commonest correct outcome is a written statement that there is no baseline and a downgraded claim. The argument runs in five steps. **First**, measurements divide into those whose subject survives the intervention and those whose subject is destroyed by it, and the baseline is the canonical member of the second class — which makes its capture window close before anybody notices it opened. **Second**, a before state reconstructed after the result is produced by parties with an interest in it and is not evidence, while a record generated by the work for another purpose was made by nobody trying to make it come out a particular way. **Third**, a comparison requires a stable instrument, and on this platform the instrument includes the judge, the prompt and the sampling settings — so a baseline and a follow-up scored by different judges are two measurements of two things, with the application taking credit for the judge's movement. **Fourth**, the unit of analysis is a choice that silently determines what any score means, and a team that never chose is reporting an answer to a question it did not ask. **Fifth**, a claim with no disconfirming value is unfalsifiable in the same way an undisqualifiable engagement position was.

The session installs a baseline record carrying the measure, the value, the period, the instrument and its survival, the unit of analysis, the taker, a disconfirming result written before any output is examined, a confounder list, and — where no baseline exists — a written downgrade naming what it gives up. The platform workflow makes one property physically observable in an hour: that the same unchanged interaction scores differently under different split strategies.

The paper concedes that its own source contains no instance of any of this being done; that the disconfirming-result requirement is written past the source entirely; that the baseline the session teaches learners to take is scored by instruments whose agreement with human judgement is unpublished, which is a circularity the design mitigates and does not escape; and that the downgrade it demands is professionally costly in a way the session cannot offset.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

The moment at which a baseline can be taken and the moment at which anybody wants one are separated by the whole engagement.

**At the start, nobody is thinking about measurement.** The question is whether the thing can be built, whether the customer will engage, whether the data exists. Measurement is a later problem and everybody is right that it is a later problem — **which is why the window for solving it closes before anybody notices it was open.**

At the end, the demand arrives suddenly and from somebody senior. **The only available source is the recollection of people who spent the intervening period building the thing**, and their recollection is not dishonest; it is reconstructed under exactly the conditions that make reconstruction unreliable.

There is a second problem which is worse because it is invisible. **A measure whose definition was refined during the engagement will be better defined at the end than at the start**, and the refinement almost always improves the after figure — not through bad faith, but because a definition improved by people building a system improves in the direction the system operates in.

A third is newer and belongs to this platform generation. **The instrument can move without anybody touching the application.** Where a score is produced by a model judging an output, the judge is a system rather than a constant: a model, a prompt and a set of sampling parameters. Change any of the three and the instrument has changed. If the application team and the judge are different teams — or, more commonly, if the judge is a managed service whose model version is not under anybody's control — **a movement in the score will be attributed to the application by default**, because that is the only thing the application team knows they changed.

And a fourth. **Nobody has written down what failure would have looked like.** The corpus's discovery apparatus asks what good looks like, in every block, and never asks the complement — so no engagement in it has a value that would have counted against the work.

The session's response is a record with dates and a pin, a record-first search strategy, a declared unit of analysis, a disconfirming value written first, and a licensed downgrade.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session operates on the requirement written at [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md) and made falsifiable at [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md), and on the feasibility question posed at [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md).

It assumes [S003](/wiki/seminars/S003.md), because the measure has to attach to the problem the customer experiences rather than to the system that was built, and a learner who has not separated those will baseline the wrong thing precisely.

It assumes the evaluation vocabulary installed across [M13](/wiki/modules/M13.md) — stored traces, advisory versus authoritative checks, and the standing rule that a passing check is a claim about a check. **The baseline is where that vocabulary is turned outward for the first time**, from the system's own health to the world's state before the system existed.

**It descends directly from [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md).** The disconfirming result is the disqualifying condition in a measurement's clothing, and the same test applies: **a result that could never occur is a decoration.** That inheritance is stated in the session so that a learner recognises the shape rather than meeting it as new.

**The prior it works against is that a baseline is a number you go and get.** It is a record you either have or do not have, and the professional skill is what you do in the second case.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is low and the concepts are few. The session's real difficulty is affective rather than cognitive: its best outcome is a weaker deliverable, and a learner has to accept that before the work makes sense. The platform workflow carries most of the extraneous load and is deliberately short.

**3.2 The deferability asymmetry is definitional, and stating it as such is the design's first honesty.** A measurement whose subject persists can be taken at any time. A measurement whose subject is destroyed by the intervention has one window. That is a property of what a baseline is, not a finding about teams, and the session presents it as class 3 reasoning rather than as evidence. **It is nonetheless the argument that justifies the session's position at the head of the module**, because everything else in M14 is deferrable and this is not.

**3.3 Why a record beats a recollection.** A record generated by the work — tickets, logs, invoices, rotas, exports — was made for another purpose by somebody with no stake in this question. **A recollection elicited for this question was made by somebody with one.** That is a difference in provenance rather than in accuracy, and it is the only difference available at this distance. The interviewing literature this programme opened on reaches the same conclusion from the other side: people will give you the answer they can see would please you, not from dishonesty but because you asked a question whose desired answer was legible, and the repair is to ask about past behaviour and specific past episodes rather than about general states ([research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md)). **That source is a practitioner text reporting no measurement of any kind, and the local copy carries no publisher, year or edition**, so it cannot be cited by edition and is used here for the mechanism only.

**3.4 Why the period must be stated.** "Before" is not a period; it is a direction. **Two people using "before" will select different windows, and the selection will be made after the result is known.** Stating the period is what makes the figure a measurement of something rather than of a choice.

**3.5 Why the instrument's future matters, and why the judge is part of the instrument.**

A baseline is only useful if the after measurement is comparable, and comparability requires the same instrument. **An instrument the team will lose access to at handover produces a before that can never have an after**, which is a defect discoverable now and not later.

The sharper form of this is stated directly in the one book in this repository that treats evaluation as an engineering problem inside a running system: **"An application evolves over time, but the way it's evaluated ideally should be fixed. This way, evaluation metrics can be used to monitor the application's changes. However, AI judges are also AI applications, which means that they also can change over time"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md)). The same source gives the misattribution mechanism concretely — where the application and the judge belong to different teams, "the application team might mistakenly attribute the changes in the evaluation results to changes in the application, rather than the changes in the judges" — and closes with the standing constraint: "If the evaluation process changes constantly, you won't be able to use the evaluation results to guide your application's development." Its boxed rule is the operational form: **do not trust any judge whose model and prompt you cannot see.**

**Its limits travel with it.** It is a practitioner book rather than a study. It is heavily citational and honest about that, but a substantial part of its most useful material rests on unattributed interviews, and where it reports a figure the figure almost always originates elsewhere. **No magnitude from it appears in this paper or in the session.**

**3.6 Why the disconfirming result is written first.** A failure condition written after the result is known is selected from the space of conditions that did not occur. **Writing it first makes it a prediction; writing it afterwards makes it a description.** This is the same ordering argument [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) made about the question column and [S062](/wiki/seminars/S062.md) made about the evidence standard, and it is the ordering commitment that runs through this programme's entire teaching pattern.

**3.7 Why the unit of analysis is part of the baseline rather than an implementation detail.**

This is the platform's contribution to the session's argument and it is the most transferable thing in the workflow. A multi-turn interaction must be cut into a query half and a response half before anything can be scored, and the documentation states the consequence in its own words: **"How you split determines *what you're evaluating*"** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). Three strategies are supplied — split at the last user message for response quality at a point, treat the first message as the query and everything after as the response for task completion and trajectory, or score each exchange independently for fine-grained analysis — with custom splitters permitted.

The consequence generalises far beyond this API. **An agent that recovers well from a bad first attempt scores well under one strategy and badly under another. Neither score is wrong; they answer different questions.** A baseline whose split is unrecorded is therefore not reproducible even by the person who took it, and a before-and-after pair scored under different splits is a comparison of two unrelated quantities.

**3.8 Why one sample is not a measurement, and why an uncertainty-free number overclaims.**

The documentation supplies repetitions **"to detect non-deterministic behavior"**, which is the vendor conceding directly that a single sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement. That is the minimum position, and the measurement literature supplies the maximum.

The statistical treatment this module rests on opens by attacking prevailing practice head-on: evals are run and reported with a highest-number-is-best mentality, in which a leading result is highlighted "but not necessarily to test that result for any kind of statistical significance" ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). Its analytic move is to treat an eval's questions as a draw from a hypothetical larger population, which lets the analyst "jump 'through the looking glass' of the specific questions that appear in an eval in order to study the underlying skill that the eval is attempting to measure." Three of its conclusions bear on a baseline. **An evaluation reported without an interval overclaims**, because the reader cannot distinguish a difference from noise. **Where questions arrive in related groups — several questions about one passage, one question translated into several languages — the independence assumption fails and an uncorrected interval is too narrow**, which makes the measurement look more precise than it is. And **power analysis belongs before the run rather than after it**: knowing the smallest difference an evaluation of a given size can reliably detect tells you in advance whether the evaluation can possibly answer the question you care about, which converts "we ran it and saw no difference" into a statement about what the instrument was capable of seeing.

**Its limits are recorded and matter here.** It is an analytical paper rather than an empirical one, and most of its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions. Its whole apparatus is valid only where the questions genuinely are a sample of the population you care about — if they were hand-selected, curated for difficulty or leaked into training, the inference machinery is valid about the wrong target. And **statistical validity says nothing about construct validity: a tight interval around a meaningless metric is still meaningless.** **No figure from it is reproduced here.** The full treatment is deferred to [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md), where the interval is the assessed object; at S066 it does one job, which is to stop a learner treating a single run as a baseline.

**3.9 Why a baseline from another domain is not your baseline.**

The retrieval benchmark literature is the cleanest available demonstration that a number's transferability is not a property of the number. Neural retrieval systems had been reporting improvements measured overwhelmingly on the collection they were trained on, and that design cannot distinguish a model that has learned retrieval from one that has learned a particular corpus, query style and notion of relevance ([research/02-technical-foundations/thakur-beir.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/thakur-beir.md)). The response was to make heterogeneity the design principle and evaluate zero-shot across eighteen deliberately dissimilar datasets. **A simple lexical method predating the neural literature did not win and did not collapse anywhere**, while **the computationally cheapest families transferred worst** — so efficiency and generalisation were, at that moment, in tension.

The transfer to this session is one sentence: **a baseline established on a domain, a query style or a document shape unlike yours is not a baseline for your domain.** Importing one is a category error dressed as diligence, and it is attractive precisely because a published figure looks more authoritative than a messy local export.

**Its limits:** a snapshot of one year's practice; eighteen datasets are a sample of retrieval tasks and not a map of them; the constituent relevance judgements differ in density and construction, so per-dataset scores are not strictly comparable to one another; and the benchmark has since become an optimisation target, which weakens its own zero-shot premise over time. **The methodology and the trade-off structure transfer. The rankings do not.**

**3.10 Why the learner's own impression is disqualified as a source.**

The strongest available evidence that felt effect and measured effect diverge comes from a randomised trial of experienced open-source maintainers working on their own repositories, who were **slower with AI assistance while believing they had been sped up** ([research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md](/research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md)). Forecasts before the study, from the participants and from independent expert forecasters, all pointed the other way. **The datum this session needs is the post-hoc one: having done the work, participants still believed the assistance had helped.** Direct experience did not correct the misperception, which is precisely why a baseline cannot be replaced by the judgement of the people who did the work.

**The study's limits are large, and its authors published an explicit list of things their result does not show — a practice worth imitating.** The developer sample is very small; the population is expert maintainers on codebases they know intimately, which is close to a worst case for assistance; the outcome is self-reported time. The research group now labels the result out of date and has redesigned the study, because participants increasingly decline to work in the no-assistance arm at all, which compromises randomisation. **That public retraction of currency is itself part of why the source is cited.** No magnitude from it is reproduced here.

**3.11 Why a labelling budget is not a reason to abandon the baseline.**

The practical objection to any baseline discipline is that hand-labelling is unaffordable, and the two obvious responses are both wrong. Treating a model's judgements as labels propagates its systematic error into the estimate and produces an interval that is invalid because it ignores prediction error. Using only the small labelled sample is valid and wasteful.

A third construction exists. A small gold-standard sample is used to estimate and remove the systematic discrepancy between the model's predictions and the trustworthy labels, and the correction is applied to the estimate computed over the large model-labelled set, with the correction's own uncertainty propagated into the final interval ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/angelopoulos-prediction-powered-inference.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/angelopoulos-prediction-powered-inference.md)). The load-bearing property is that validity holds **without any assumptions on the machine-learning algorithm supplying the predictions** — a poor model widens the interval rather than corrupting it, and a better model buys precision rather than permission. **That is the correct incentive structure, and it is the reason this method rather than a judged score is the module's eventual destination.**

**Its conditions are strict.** Gold labels must genuinely be gold, because the method corrects the model against the labels and cannot correct the labels. The labelled subset must sample the same population as the unlabelled data; convenience sampling breaks the guarantee silently. And it says nothing about whether the estimand is the right one. **This note was written from the abstract and published summary; no coverage rate, interval width or sample size is reproduced here.** At S066 the method exists only to remove an excuse; it is used properly at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md).

**3.12 Why the downgrade is licensed rather than penalised.** If a missing baseline is a failure, learners will produce one. **Making the written statement an acceptable — indeed the expected — outcome removes the incentive to reconstruct**, and the incentive is the whole problem. The cost is that a weaker claim is genuinely weaker, and §8 concedes it.

**3.13 Why the opening items are unscaffolded.** They are a generation phase in the sense the productive-failure literature uses: learners attempt a problem they have not been taught, fail, and are then taught, with the failed attempt improving later learning even when it is wrong ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Two of that literature's stated conditions bind this session. **Guidance during the generation phase reduces the failure rate and reduces learning**, which is why the release rule gates §§4, 5 and 9. And **the consolidation phase must explicitly prompt comparison** — presenting a correct answer beside a learner's wrong one achieves nothing on its own, and only the condition instructed to compare them learned more. Exercise 3 is that instruction, and it is a condition rather than a stylistic preference. **The literature's evidence is school, university and laboratory work; there is no adult-professional study in its reference list, and applying it to working engineers is an extrapolation this programme concedes at [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md) §8.**

**3.14 Why a baseline that *was* taken is the better specimen, and why the design added it.**

Every other argument in this session concerns a baseline nobody took. **The design's late addition concerns one that was taken, competently, and then described as something else by the page that cites it** — and the reason for preferring it as a teaching object is that it inverts the learner's expectation about where the failure lives.

The substrate is a first-party engineering evaluation with named authors, named datasets, a named metric and **per-dataset results reported rather than only an average** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-iq-intelligence-layer.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-iq-intelligence-layer.md)). By the standards this session teaches, that is good work. **The baseline it used was the same product at a lower setting of its own reasoning-effort dial**, which is a real, useful and honestly reported comparison: it answers whether turning the dial up is worth the latency and the tokens. **The product's own frequently-asked-questions page restates the result as an improvement over traditional single-shot retrieval-augmented generation — a comparison that was never run**, against a system that does not appear in the experiment.

**The design's judgement is that this is the stronger of the two failure modes to teach, for three reasons.** It is not a story about incompetence, so a learner cannot file it under *other people are careless*; the underlying work is better than most of what they will inherit. **It is asymmetric in visibility: a missing baseline announces itself and a renamed one does not**, and the renamed version is the one that travels because it is the one printed where a customer reads. And **it is checkable in minutes, first-party, on live pages, with no tenant and no privileged access** — which makes it the cheapest genuine exercise in the module.

**The design's response is the read-back step and its record, not a warning.** The learner opens the citation, identifies the baseline the experiment actually used, and writes **one sentence** the experiment supports, then names what would have to be measured to support the printed sentence. **The output is the sentence and the missing arm; the figure is forbidden**, and a submission containing it fails. That prohibition is not squeamishness about the number's accuracy — the measurement is real. It is that **a number detached from its baseline becomes a claim about whatever baseline the reader assumes**, which is exactly the transformation the specimen demonstrates.

**A correction is recorded here rather than made quietly, because this paper's own justification changed.** The repository's prohibition on this claim formerly rested on the absence of any published methodology. **A methodology now exists.** The prohibition stands and its warrant is now the opposite of what it was — not *nobody showed their working* but *the working shows a different comparison from the one being quoted.* **A repository that updated the conclusion and left the reason would be carrying the class of stale claim it exists to prevent**, which is why the change is written down rather than absorbed.

**Two properties of that experiment are carried into the session because the learner will be held to them.** **The judge is a language model with no published agreement with human raters**, and **no interval, variance or run count accompanies any figure**, so nothing states whether the reported difference is distinguishable from noise. Both are the absences [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) requires a learner to fill in their own record, and meeting them first in somebody else's published work is the design's preferred order.

**What this licenses, and the limits that bound it.** It licenses a claim about a discrepancy between two pages on a dated reading. **It does not establish that the product is inferior to the architecture the page names** — the experiment simply does not address it. **It does not establish intent**, and compression by somebody who did not run the experiment is the likeliest explanation. **And it establishes nothing about frequency**; no survey of documentation restatement exists, for this vendor or any other, and none is asserted. **The source must not be pooled**: its product documentation is definitional and contains no measurement, while its engineering evaluation is evidential but weak — **a substantial part of the corpus was author-constructed, the scoring method defers to an earlier post that was not read, and the charts were not retrievable.** It is **the most perishable material in this repository and the only entry whose names are more perishable than its capabilities.** **Every number in it is forbidden downstream and none appears in this paper or in the session.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The movements run situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**Opening item 3 — what result would make the customer conclude this had not worked — is the instrument**, and its provenance is stated in the session itself: it is absent from the source's entire discovery apparatus, which asks the complement in every block and this nowhere. **The gap between the confident answers to item 1 and the empty answers to item 3 is the session's opening measurement**, and items 1, 2, 4 and 5 exist partly to establish the contrast that makes item 3's emptiness legible to the learner who wrote it.

**4.1 Why the disconfirming result goes at the top of the file rather than in the write-up.** It would be easy to require it somewhere in the record. The design instead makes it the first field, because the failure it guards against is not ignorance of the requirement but forgetting it under the pressure of reporting a good number. A record whose first line names the value that would have meant failure is much harder to summarise as a success, and **the summary is where the error is actually committed.**

**4.2 Why the unit of analysis is an artefact field.** §3.7 gives the mechanism; the design consequence is that the split strategy is recorded beside the question it answers, in the same line. A learner who writes *last-turn* and *the claim is about task completion* in adjacent fields has produced a visible contradiction. **The field exists to make that contradiction visible on the page rather than in a later argument.**

**4.3 Why the workflow runs deterministic checks before judged ones.** The evaluation surface offers two tiers — local checks that run without API calls, and cloud judged evaluation — and permits both in a single run. The ordering is not a cost optimisation. **A deterministic check's failure mode is legible and a judged check's is not**, so establishing what can be established cheaply first means the learner knows which part of the result rests on an uncharacterised instrument. The two-tier design also physically instantiates the cost-and-speed ladder the evaluation literature argues for in the abstract, which means the API carries part of the lesson.

**4.4 Why the workflow's observable is two scores from one interaction.** The session could assert that the split strategy matters. Instead the learner runs one stored trace twice and reads two different numbers off an unchanged interaction. **That is the cheapest available demonstration that a metric is meaningless without its unit of analysis**, and it takes minutes. Every later platform session in this stage is a variation on the same move: make the property physically observable rather than argue for it.

**4.5 Why the last workflow step attacks the artefact rather than improving it.** A model asked to improve something adds; a model asked to attack it must locate a weakness, and locating is a harder operation than adding. The step also requires the learner to record what they rejected, which is the only evidence available that the instrument was used adversarially rather than consulted for reassurance.

**4.6 Why the review question is asked of a stranger.** *Could you have taken this measurement yourself, from what is written here?* That question tests the record and not the number. A record that passes it can produce an after measurement; a record that fails it has produced a figure and no method.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The retrospective baseline.** The likeliest failure and the hardest to detect from the artefact. *Countermeasure:* the date fields, the record-first search strategy, and the [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) element checking that the before and after were not produced together.

**The unfalsifiable improvement.** *Countermeasure:* opening item 3, the disconfirming result as the record's first field, and the [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) element requiring it to be dated before the measurement.

**The moved measure.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that before and after share a definition and, where possible, an instrument, with the difference stated where they do not.

**The moved instrument.** The failure §3.5 exists against, and the one this platform generation has made routine. *Countermeasure:* the pin — host, model, dataset hash, date — as a required field, and the standard's warning sign: you cannot say which model version scored the baseline. **This countermeasure is textual and §9's prediction 5 predicts it failing.**

**The unstated unit of analysis.** *Countermeasure:* the split-strategy field paired with the question it answers, and exercise 2, which produces two scores from one interaction.

**The instrument you do not own.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that the instrument's owner be named and its survival stated, which surfaces the problem at baseline time rather than at handover.

**The reconstructed memory.** The failure the licensed downgrade exists against. *Countermeasure:* the downgrade, made explicitly acceptable, plus the warning sign — your source for the before state is a person rather than a record.

**The measure nobody had.** A first-ever measurement presented as an improvement. *Countermeasure:* named in the failure list, and the [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) classification element, which forces the claim to be labelled descriptive.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. **The programme's entrustment decisions begin at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md).**

Three elements of this session's artefact are checked at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md): that a baseline record exists naming the measure, the value, the period, the instrument and its taker — or that a written statement says there is none, names what the claim was downgraded to, and says what the downgrade gives up; that the disconfirming result is present and dated before the measurement; and that the unit of analysis is stated with the question it answers.

**Whether the baseline value is correct is not assessed** and never could be. **Whether a baseline exists at all is not assessed either**, which is the design's most consequential exclusion and the reason the downgrade is a passing outcome.

The elements are written as things a reader can check on the page rather than as judgements about the learner's understanding, which follows the narrative-assessment finding that assessors form their judgement from the prose and use scores only to confirm it — so the prose is what the specification should require ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). **That literature is single-institution medical-education research using mock portfolios and think-aloud methods that cannot capture fast or automatic processing; transfer to engineering assessment is an argument rather than a given.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: licensing the downgrade will produce a cohort that stops looking, because the written statement is cheaper than the search.**

At its strongest: any specification that accepts *we could not establish this* as a passing outcome will be met with that answer by most candidates, most of the time. **Establishing a baseline is genuinely hard work — finding records, negotiating access, agreeing a definition with somebody who does not want to agree one — and writing a paragraph explaining that it was not possible is an afternoon.** The design has removed the incentive to fabricate and replaced it with an incentive to give up, which is a different failure with the same output: no baseline.

**Conceded in part, and the rebuttal is narrower than the objection.** The written statement is not a paragraph; it must name **what the claim was downgraded to and what the downgrade gives up**, and a downgraded claim is a materially worse deliverable that the candidate then has to defend at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) and hand forward at [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md). **The cost is deferred rather than removed.** That is a real counterweight and it is weaker than fabrication pressure would have been in the opposite direction. **The declined fix is an element marking the adequacy of the search that preceded the downgrade.** Declined because search adequacy has no available standard — nothing in any source says how hard one should look — and an element with no standard is a marker's opinion in a specification's clothing. **The design accepts a cohort that under-searches over a cohort that reconstructs**, on the argument that an absent baseline is honest and a fabricated one is not, and that argument is a preference rather than a finding.

**Objection 2: teaching baseline discipline from a source that has never exhibited one is teaching a procedure with no demonstrated feasibility.**

At its strongest: the framework instructs, catalogues and reserves, and never once shows the work being done. **That may not be an oversight. It may be that establishing a baseline in a real commercial engagement is not achievable within the time and access available**, and that the framework's silence is an accurate record of what its authors could actually do. This session then assembles a procedure from first principles, presents it with the confidence of practice, and sends learners to attempt something no documented practitioner has completed.

**Conceded without qualification, and it is the most serious objection in this module.** The session states it in its own limits movement rather than confining it here. **The rebuttal is only that the alternative is worse**: a module that declined to teach baselines because none has been observed would leave learners producing after figures with no before, which is the current condition of the practice. **The declined fix is to reduce the requirement to a discussion rather than an artefact.** It is declined because a discussion produces nothing that can be handed forward, and [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) needs the confounder list as an input. **The feasibility of the procedure is unestablished, the session says so, and a learner should expect the downgrade to be the common outcome rather than the exception.**

**Objection 3: the session teaches learners to take a baseline with instruments whose accuracy is unpublished, which is circular.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the design finds most uncomfortable. The workflow's judged evaluators are model-judged. **No agreement rate with human judgement, no calibration data and no threshold rationale is published for any of them.** So the paper spends §3.5 insisting that the instrument must be stable and §3.8 insisting that an uncertainty-free number overclaims, and then instructs a learner to record a baseline produced by an instrument whose relationship to the thing it purports to measure is entirely unknown. **A stable uncharacterised instrument is not obviously better than an unstable one; it is consistently wrong instead of inconsistently wrong**, and the design has taught the learner to be confident about a number's reproducibility while silent about its meaning.

**Partly conceded, and the concession is real.** Three answers, of which only the third is satisfying. The workflow runs deterministic local checks first, precisely so that some part of the baseline rests on something legible — a queue identifier is present or it is not, and no judge is involved. The record's pin makes the judge's identity part of the artefact, so a later reader can at least ask the question rather than being unable to formulate it. **And exercise 5 sends the learner to look for the published agreement and requires them to record the absence with a date**, which converts the circularity from an invisible assumption into a stated limitation on the learner's own claim. **The declined fix is to require every learner to hand-label a sample and compute agreement with the judge before using it.** It is declined on load grounds at this point in the module and is not declined permanently: it is precisely the work [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) does, using the small-trusted-sample correction of §3.11. **The cost of deferring it is that a learner leaves S066 holding a baseline whose instrument they have not characterised**, and this paper records that as an accepted cost rather than a solved problem.

**Objection 4: everything the workflow observes is a property of one platform revision, so the session is a supervised tour of a snapshot.**

At its strongest: the split strategies, the evaluator names, the repetitions setting and the threshold defaults are all facts about a configuration that will not exist in a year. The durable content — *write down what you measured and when* — could be taught in a fraction of the time with no product attached.

**Conceded on the observations and rejected on the method.** Every claim in the session's perishable table is paired with its version-bound instance, and the record requires a pin for exactly this reason. **What transfers is the act of characterising a measurement before trusting it, and that act cannot be practised on a description.** A learner who has never seen one interaction produce two scores will read a reported metric the way everybody else does. **The declined fix is to teach the principles without the anchor**, and it is declined because a constructed example would teach the same lesson worse: the two-strategy divergence is not a defect of this product but a consequence of what a multi-turn interaction is, and observing it on a real surface is what makes it credible.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most candidates will be unable to answer opening item 1 with a value and a source. *Instrument:* the submitted opening answers, coded *value with named source* / *value without source* / *no value* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most candidates will leave opening item 3 blank or generic before instruction, and their item 1 answers will be markedly more specific than their item 3 answers. *Instrument:* the answers coded *specific value capable of occurring* / *generic* / *absent*, compared across items 1 and 3 within candidate.

**Prediction 3.** Where a customer is asked for a disconfirming result, most will decline to name one. *Instrument:* the record's customer-response field, coded *named* / *declined* / *not asked*.

**Prediction 4.** Most baseline records will rest on a recollection rather than on a record made for another purpose. *Instrument:* the source field, coded *contemporaneous record* / *recollection* / *mixed*. **If recollection dominates after the record-first strategy has been taught, the strategy is not actionable as written** and the session should supply a worked search over a specific class of record.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of baseline records will omit the judge's model version from the pin, despite the pin being a required field and §3.5 being the material's longest subsection. *Instrument:* the submitted records, coded for pin completeness field by field. **This predicts the failure of §5's weakest countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 6.** Learners instructed to compare their item-3 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 3) will produce disconfirming results judged capable of firing more often than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* a reviewer's binary judgement across two cohorts, or a within-cohort split on whether exercise 3 was completed. **This is the session's only direct test of the mechanism §3.13 rests on**, and a null result would mean the comparison requirement is ceremony.

**Prediction 7.** Learners who run exercise 2 will report surprise at the divergence between the two scores, and a majority will not have predicted the direction of the difference beforehand. *Instrument:* a recorded prediction taken before the second run, compared against the observed outcome. **If most learners predict correctly, the observable is not doing the work §4.4 claims for it** and the exercise should be replaced with an assertion.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the baseline is the only non-deferrable measurement in the programme; that a before state reconstructed after the result is not a baseline; that a definition refined during the engagement improves in the direction the system operates; that the instrument includes the judge; that the unit of analysis silently determines what a score means; and that the honest outcome of most baseline work is a written statement and a weaker claim.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes, and less than usual about practice. **The source instructs and never demonstrates**, so the procedure taught here is assembled rather than observed. The disconfirming-result requirement is written past the source entirely and rests on an argument first made at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md). The statistical material is well grounded and is deliberately used at less than full strength today; the platform material is definitional and silent exactly where a learner would want it to speak.

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper.** That is a rule of this repository rather than an accident of what was read, and it holds even where a source states a number in a full text that has been read in full.

**The concession worth remembering.** You are being taught something nobody in the source material has been shown to do, using instruments whose agreement with human judgement nobody has published. **Expect the downgrade**, notice that it is the correct answer rather than the failed one, and read §8's third objection before you quote your own baseline at anybody.
