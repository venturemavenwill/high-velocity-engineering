# WP-027 — Error Has Structure

**Seminar day:** [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md) · **Module:** [M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md) · **Quarter:** [Q3](/wiki/quarters/Q3.md)
**Strands:** ME · MS · SY · **Complexity class:** 2
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Foundry evaluators as an instrument with its own error structure · **MCP servers:** Microsoft Learn MCP Server · Azure MCP Server (read)

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching error as a decomposition with source-specific remedies rather than as variability to be reduced by sampling, and defends building the day's pretest as a sequence in which each item defeats the answer to the previous one. The first argument is that the default student model — variation is noise, more samples fix it — is correct for exactly one of four common error sources and is actively harmful for the other three, since it licenses continued sampling of a biased instrument; and that the remedy for an error is determined by its mechanism, so diagnosis must precede treatment. The second argument is that a misconception held with confidence is displaced more reliably by three successive self-generated revisions than by a single contradiction. The paper additionally defends locating all four error sources inside a single instrument the students operate — a language-model evaluator, which is non-deterministic, biased toward length, versioned underneath its callers, and subject to a documented hourly cap whose overflow policy is silent omission — and defends extending the day's systematic-error argument to the students' own agent. It concedes that the four-source taxonomy is orthogonal to the way error actually presents, that the day's selection-effect teaching cannot be practised on data the programme controls, and that its most transferable claim is asserted in one register and assessed in none.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Students who have accepted that measurements vary hold a single model of why: noise. The model has a remedy attached — take more samples — and the remedy is satisfying because it is always available and requires no diagnosis.

It is correct for one error class and wrong for three. A systematic offset is unaffected by sample size; averaging a thousand readings from a mis-calibrated instrument yields a very precise wrong number. Drift makes early and late observations measurements of different things, so aggregating them produces a figure describing no state the system was ever in. A selection effect makes the sample unrepresentative regardless of size, and increasing the sample increases confidence in the wrong value.

The harm is not merely that the remedy fails. It is that the remedy *appears* to work: variance falls, the reported figure stabilises, and confidence rises while accuracy does not. More sampling of a biased instrument is a mechanism for becoming more certain of something false, and students will apply it by default.

The day's second problem is diagnostic. The four sources are distinguishable from data — by grouping, ordering and comparison — with one exception. Selection effects are invisible in the recorded data, because the diagnostic evidence is precisely the observations that were not recorded. Condition D in phase 3 is constructed so that groups have all the data and cannot reach the answer from it, which is the correct experience: the only route is to reason about the recording mechanism rather than about the recordings.

The third problem is a conflation the module must prevent. S026 taught validity; S027 teaches reliability; students merge them, and the merged version loses both. Point 8 keeps them joined by making the relationship explicit: a construct-instrument gap is a systematic error that is invisible in the data because the instrument is working correctly. Phase 2's bridge asks the same question in the other direction.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Moderate to high.** The individual error sources are separable and each is simple. The interactivity is in the budget at step 2, where sources must be estimated relative to one another, and in step 3's dominance judgement, which requires holding all four estimates simultaneously.

Selection effects add a second kind of difficulty that is not load in the usual sense: the reasoning is not complex, it is *counterfactual*, and students have no practice reasoning about data that does not exist.

Management:

- **One measurement across four conditions** in phase 3, so all variation is in the error structure.
- **Full raw data supplied,** with ground truth revealed after commitment.
- **The budget template supplied** at step 2, listing the four sources so that no capacity goes to recalling them.
- **Step 5 worked with the room,** because counterfactual reasoning about absent observations is the day's hardest demand and is the least likely to be reachable unaided.
- **The module's single question and the student's own S026 instrument,** so no new context is introduced.
- **No inferential statistics.** Estimation only.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *The sequential pretest,* where each item defeats the previous answer.
- *Condition D,* whose diagnostic evidence is absent by construction.
- *Estimating the selection effect,* step 5.
- *Falsifying your own diagnosis,* step 7 — what would the measurement look like if you were wrong.
- *The partner's competing explanation from the same data,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Formal error propagation.* Estimation only; the calculus is ME101's.
- *Confidence intervals.* S028's material, deliberately absent so that the day's magnitudes remain explicitly estimates.
- *Instrument calibration procedures.* Named at point 6 and not taught.
- *Constructing the four conditions.* Supplied.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md).** Point 2's four sources are a selection. They omit interactions between sources, error introduced by the act of measuring, and error in recording rather than in observing. The first is deferred to S029 and the second to S068; **the third is not taught anywhere in this programme**, which the seminar page states. That omission is deliberate and thin: recording error is common in practice and the programme has no natural home for it. It is recorded here as a known gap rather than as a justified exclusion.

**The platform instance, scoped in the strong form.** The day treats a language-model evaluator as an instrument with a four-source error structure, and the reading will be carried into work this day does not supervise.

*Safe.* Reasoning about what repetition can and cannot fix in a judged evaluation; concluding that a score obtained under one judge deployment is not comparable to one obtained under another; concluding that a system which drops work under load and reports only what it processed has manufactured a selection effect; and reconciling submitted against returned counts as a general defence. Each of those is derivable from the mechanism rather than from the vendor, and each transfers to any queue, any judge and any sampled pipeline.

*Unsafe.* Two readings. **First, treating the four sources as an exhaustive account of what goes wrong in a language-model judge.** They are not. Prompt sensitivity, position effects within a compared pair, and the interaction between a judge's own training and the outputs it grades are all real, all documented in the research literature, and none decomposes cleanly into the four. The taxonomy is a diagnostic aid for data the student holds, not a theory of the instrument. **Second, and more dangerous, inferring magnitudes.** Condition B demonstrates that a verbosity offset exists and is directional; it establishes nothing about its size, and a student who reports "the judge is biased toward long answers" without a bound has made exactly the unbounded claim [WP-026](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-026.md) §8 identified as this module's characteristic failure. Step 2's budget requires a magnitude for that reason, and S028 supplies the apparatus to bound it.

The boundary is stated to students at point 11 rather than left to this paper, and step 2's requirement that every budget entry carry an estimated magnitude — including the agent entry added at §8 — is where a student applying the taxonomy outside its range would fail.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **being wrong three times in twenty-five minutes about the same question**.

Phase 1's items are the same question with successive groupings disclosed. A student answers "more samples," is shown a time-of-day grouping and revises, is shown a machine grouping and revises again, and is then asked whether a hundred times more samples would give the true value. Three revisions, all self-generated, on one question, before any instruction.

This construction is more effective than a single contradiction for a confidently held belief, because a single contradiction can be absorbed as an exception. Three successive defeats of the same reflex, each by a different mechanism, make the reflex itself the thing in question.

Condition D supplies the day's second and more durable engagement. Groups have every observation and cannot determine the answer, and the reason — the evidence is the observations that are missing — is genuinely arresting. Students consistently report it as the day's memorable moment.

Phase 7's competing-explanation demand is enjoyable in the way S021's violating-input construction was: a bounded adversarial task with a clear win condition.

The competence risk is that the day is entirely negative — four sources, three of which sampling cannot fix, and one that cannot be diagnosed at all. Point 5's error budget and point 6's remedies are the constructive output and are deliberately placed before the practical work.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Sequential disclosure across five items. The design's most important decision here is that items 2 and 3 supply *groupings* rather than answers, so each revision is the student's inference rather than a correction received. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction**, administered here per instrumentation rule 8, and its expected answer is item 1's answer produced by a machine — which is the day's cleanest available demonstration that the agent shares the room's reflexes rather than correcting them.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** The bridge asks which S026 gap mechanism produces an error more sampling cannot fix. All four do, which is the answer, and stating it prevents the validity-reliability conflation from the day's first twenty minutes.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** One evaluation, four conditions, **executed rather than supplied**, committed diagnoses, ground truth after. The change from a constructed dataset to a live instrument does real work in three of the four conditions and changes condition D's character entirely. Previously condition D's missing observations were missing because the design removed them; now they are missing because **the platform's evaluation-run cap was reached and the runs were skipped**, which is a documented default with a documented remedy and no signal in the output. The phase's critical dependency is unchanged — the recorded data must be internally consistent and unremarkable — and it is now satisfied by the platform rather than by construction, which is a stronger position: no amount of scrutiny helps, and the students can read the cap in the documentation and still not see its effect in their results.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Eleven points. Point 2 carries the scope statement including the untaught omission. Point 3 addresses the reflex directly. Point 6's honest entry — nothing; this is a stated limitation — is important and is the entry students omit. Point 8 joins the module's two days. **Point 9 extends the day to the students' own agent** and is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s commitment for S027; it is reachable from condition B rather than asserted, per instrumentation rule 7, though §8 records that reachability is not the same as assessment. Point 10 names the platform's chosen defaults and point 11 separates durable claim from instance.

**Phase 5 (85 min).** Seven steps at class 2 using the student's own S026 instrument, **now shipping a harness rather than producing a document**. Step 5 is the hardest and is worked. Its second half — reconcile submitted against returned — is the day's build and is roughly four lines of code; the design's argument for it is that a countermeasure this cheap, for a failure class this severe, being absent from a production platform is the most instructive fact available about how selection effects survive. Step 7 applies S009's falsification discipline to a diagnosis rather than to a claim, which is a genuine extension and is the day's most sophisticated demand.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** An averaging window that concealed a drift, then an agent failure. The attention to who chose the window and when is the phase's content: aggregation choices are inherited artefacts, nobody owns them, and they silently determine what the measurement can show. The agent case — repetition raised against a verbosity offset — is chosen because it is the room's own item 1 answer, executed competently, reviewed and approved, and because the review had no place in it where anyone was required to name the error source being addressed.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Competing explanation from the same data, then the settling observation. The second half is the programme's first informal experimental design and is the direct precursor of S029.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** The item 1 answer's trajectory across the sequence, shown as a movement rather than as a correction, then the sealed Copilot predictions opened in two bands.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with laboratory or experimental background will hold systematic-versus-random already and will handle conditions A to C. Predicted failure: they diagnose condition D as an unusually large systematic offset, because their training supplies three of the four categories and selection effects belong to a different tradition. Countermeasure: condition D's ground truth is revealed with the missing observations shown, which is unanswerable, and the phase's closing task asks what in the data should have told them — for D the honest answer is nothing, which is the lesson.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The budget at step 2 requires estimating four magnitudes with no statistical apparatus. Countermeasures: the template; ground truth exposure in phase 3 as a calibration reference; and the acceptance that estimates are order-of-magnitude. Residual risk: step 5's counterfactual reasoning may be unreachable, and the fallback is to state the recording mechanism and identify one class of observation it would miss, which is weaker and passes.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Conditions A, B and C are diagnosed correctly by most groups within twenty minutes, which is intended — the pattern must form before D violates it. Fallback if a group diagnoses D: ask them to construct a fifth condition in which two sources are equally dominant and the standard remedies conflict, which is hard and previews the interaction case the day omits.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **the noise reflex replaced by paralysis**. A student who has learned that sampling fixes one source in four, and that one source cannot be diagnosed at all, may conclude that no measurement can be trusted — the module's fatalism risk in its second form. Countermeasures: point 6's remedies, including the honest do-nothing entry; step 6's residual-error statement, which is a constructive output rather than a disclaimer; and phase 3's ground truth, which demonstrates that correct diagnosis is achievable in three of four cases. The design's position is unchanged from S026: this risk peaks in the module's first half and should resolve at S029, where the apparatus becomes constructive.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement.

One specification item in ME101, now carrying a shipped harness and the three [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) elements. **An error budget containing only random variation fails.** Like S026's same-sentence clause, this is checkable without judgement and targets the day's specific reflex. **An error budget with no agent entry fails**, which is the §8 objection-3 repair and is equally checkable.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — step 5's submitted-versus-returned reconciliation is such a demonstration and is accepted.

Programme instrumentation: the phase 1 item 1 answer trajectory is the cohort's reflex-displacement measure; the phase 3 condition D diagnosis distribution is the selection-effect baseline, re-probed at S067; step 5's selection-effect estimates are compared across cohorts against the substrate's actual recording gaps, which staff know and students do not; **item 5's sealed prediction accuracy is the day's frontier-calibration datum**, read by the following year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), per instrumentation rule 9; and **step 2's agent entries are collected and coded across cohorts for whether the named systematic component is one the student observed or one they inferred from reputation**, which is the day's only measure of whether point 9 landed.

The frontier statements are a sample and the day says so, per instrumentation rule 3: one cohort, one substrate, one harness shape, on a task where a specific systematic source was known to staff in advance.

Dispositions: **humility**, at step 4, where declining to reduce an error and saying so is the honest and less impressive option.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the four-source taxonomy is orthogonal to how error actually presents.**

At its strongest: real measurements contain all four sources simultaneously, in proportions that shift with conditions, interacting in ways that make "the dominant source" ill-defined. Phase 3's four conditions each isolate one source, which does not occur in practice and could not — the isolation is a teaching artefact. A student trained to identify the dominant source will, on real data, identify whichever source their diagnostic happens to be sensitive to, and will apply its remedy while three other sources continue operating.

The rebuttal is that decomposition is valuable precisely because the sources are mixed. A student with no taxonomy sees undifferentiated variation and applies the sampling reflex; a student with four categories can at least ask which are present and estimate their relative sizes, which is step 2's budget rather than step 3's single dominant source. The budget is the artefact; the dominance judgement is a summary of it.

The concession is that the day's structure emphasises the summary over the budget. Phase 3 asks for the dominant source, step 3 asks for the dominant source, and phase 7 argues about the dominant source. The budget appears once, at step 2, with a template. That weighting is backwards relative to the design's own defence, and the fix is to make phase 3 ask for the *budget* under each condition — with one source dominant in each, which the conditions already provide — rather than for a single label. That change is not made, and its absence means the day trains a diagnosis the objection correctly identifies as ill-defined on real data.

**Objection 2: selection effects are the day's centrepiece and the programme cannot let students practise on them.**

At its strongest: condition D is supplied with a known ground truth, which requires the design to know which observations went missing. On the substrate, at step 5, no such ground truth exists — the missing observations are missing. So students perform the diagnosis once, on a constructed case with an answer, and then perform an unfalsifiable estimation on real data with no feedback. The competence the day claims to build is exercised exactly once, under conditions that cannot recur.

This objection the design does not fully answer, and it identifies something structural. Selection effects are diagnosable in practice only by reasoning about the recording mechanism — by knowing how observations come to be recorded and inferring what that process excludes — and that reasoning can be assessed even when its conclusion cannot be checked. Step 5's framing partially reflects this: it asks *how would you know*, not *what is the effect*.

What is missing is feedback. Staff know the substrate's actual recording gaps; students do not; and nothing in the day's design closes that. The available repair is direct and is not made: after step 5 is committed, supply the substrate's known recording gaps and let students check their reasoning. This costs nothing, it converts an unfalsifiable estimate into an adjudicated one, and it is exactly the commit-then-adjudicate structure the programme uses everywhere else. Its absence appears to be an oversight, and it is the second consecutive whitepaper to identify a missing adjudication step in this module.

**Objection 3: the day's most transferable claim is asserted in one register and assessed in none.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn.

At its strongest: point 9 tells students that Copilot's systematic component does not average out, that repetition makes them more confident in the same wrong thing, and that the remedy is a changed instrument. That is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s commitment for this day and it is the single point from this module the students will use most often for the rest of their careers. **And the day's evidence for it is an analogy.** Condition B demonstrates a verbosity offset in a *judge*; point 9 asserts that the same structure holds for an *agent*; the two are different systems doing different jobs, and the day supplies no case in which an agent's systematic component is measured, exhibited or diagnosed. The error clinic's agent case is a narrative, not an observation. So the day satisfies instrumentation rule 7's letter — the claim is reachable from an artefact — while the artefact is about something else, and it satisfies rule 4 not at all: nothing in the specification changes depending on whether a student believed point 9 or not.

The objection is conceded, and the concession has a sharper edge than the design would like. **A module whose subject is that instruments must be characterised before their outputs are trusted was about to teach its central claim about the students' own primary instrument on the strength of a resemblance.** That is the failure the module exists to prevent, committed by the module, about the tool the whole programme now runs on.

**The cheap fix is one mandatory budget line and it is made.** Step 2 now requires every error budget to carry **one entry for the agent**: the systematic component of the tool that produced the harness, with an estimated magnitude and a named remedy, and with *more prompts* explicitly excluded as a remedy. The specification fails a budget without it. This costs a line, it converts point 9 from an assertion into a required diagnosis, it gives rule 4 something to change, and it produces — across a cohort — the first collection of observed agent systematic components this programme has ever held.

What remains unfixed is recorded. The entry is a student's estimate with no ground truth, so it is diagnosable and not adjudicable — which is objection 2's structural problem in a second place, on the same day, for the same reason. The design notes without resolving it that both of this day's unadjudicated estimates concern the two things it most wants students to reason about, and that this is probably not a coincidence: **the sources you cannot adjudicate are the ones worth teaching, because the ones you can adjudicate are the ones a tool already handles.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A large majority will answer "more samples" at phase 1 item 1, and a majority will still answer affirmatively at item 4 despite having revised twice. *Instrument:* paired item 1 and item 4 responses. **The second half is the interesting claim: the reflex survives its own defeats.**

**Prediction 2.** Condition D will be diagnosed correctly by a small minority, and the most common wrong answer will be systematic offset. *Instrument:* per-condition diagnosis with error direction.

**Prediction 3.** Step 2 budgets will list fewer than three sources for a majority of students on first submission. *Instrument:* source counts on first and resubmitted artefacts.

**Prediction 4.** At S067, students will identify the offline-online gap as a selection effect without prompting at a higher rate than a comparison cohort. *Instrument:* an S067 protocol coded for unprompted selection-effect framing. Untestable until a variant delivery exists.

**Prediction 5.** A large majority of item 5 sealed predictions will name a coding or configuration failure, and a small minority will predict that the agent will propose repetition against a non-random source. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded by predicted failure register, against what each agent actually proposed. **The design's claim is that students predict agent failure in the register they review in, which is code, and that the agent's actual failure mode on this day is a reasoning failure identical to their own.**

**Prediction 6.** A majority of step 2 agent entries will name a systematic component the student inferred from general reputation rather than one they observed in their own build, on first submission. *Instrument:* agent entries coded observed-versus-inferred, with the prompt record checked for corroboration. **This is the §8 objection-3 repair's own check. If the observed rate is high, point 9 has landed and the analogy was sufficient; if it is near zero, the mandatory entry has produced a ritual rather than a diagnosis, which is the characteristic failure of a required field and would falsify the fix rather than the claim.**

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That error decomposes into sources with source-specific remedies, that the reflex you already hold — take more samples — is correct for exactly one of four, and that applying it to the other three is a mechanism for becoming more certain of something false while the variance obligingly falls.

**What the evidence licenses.** Systematic bias, drift and selection effects in measurement sit in **class 1**, grounded in notes recorded here; that language-model judges favour length is cited for direction only, and condition B establishes direction and nothing else. The instrument's behaviour sits in **class 4**, read on a stated date: repetitions exist, in the vendor's own words, to detect non-deterministic behaviour; runs are skipped at a documented hourly cap; and nothing is emitted when they are. **The four-source taxonomy, the sequential pretest and the mandatory agent budget entry are class 3.** You may say the judge favours length. You may not say by how much, and no agreement or accuracy figure for any evaluator may be asserted, because none is published.

**What to do with it.**

- **Diagnose before you remedy.** The remedy follows from the mechanism, and three of the four mechanisms are untouched by the only remedy you currently reach for.
- **Reconcile what you submitted against what came back.** It is about four lines, and its absence from a production platform is the most instructive fact available on how selection effects survive.
- **Put a magnitude on every budget entry, including the honest do-nothing one.** A direction with no bound is the unbounded claim this module keeps producing.
- **Pin the revision, host and model.** Under the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md), the judge model here is a caller-supplied parameter, so a score's meaning depends on a deployment that the score itself does not record.

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes that the day's most transferable claim — that your agent's systematic component does not average out — rested on a resemblance between a judge and an agent, with no case anywhere on the day in which an agent's systematic component was measured. **Hold the design to that.** A module whose subject is that instruments must be characterised before they are trusted was about to teach its central claim about the students' own primary instrument on the strength of a likeness, and the budget entry that repairs it is diagnosable and not adjudicable.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** Error structure, systematic bias, drift and selection effects in measurement, and the aggregation-window failure at phase 6, are grounded in [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md), [research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/](/research/05-fde-craft/collected-materials.md). That language-model judges exhibit verbosity bias among a small set of identified biases is recorded in the measurement folder's note on Zheng et al. at abstract-verification depth and is cited for **direction and mechanism only**; no magnitude is asserted, and condition B's role in the day is to establish direction, which is all the design claims.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The instrument behaviour used at phase 3 and points 9 to 11 is taken from Microsoft Learn's Agent Framework evaluation page, the agent monitoring dashboard how-to and the continuous evaluation article for Microsoft Foundry, all read 2026-07-26. Specifically: repetitions exist, in the documentation's own words, **"to detect non-deterministic behavior"**, which is the vendor stating that the same input does not reliably produce the same score; continuous evaluation evaluates production interactions **"at a set sampling rate"**; the monitoring dashboard's quality figures are computed over **sampled** agent outputs; **evaluation runs are skipped when an hourly run limit is reached, and the documented default limit is 100 runs per hour**, with the documented remedy being to raise the limit or wait; and judge reasoning is redacted by default, with a configuration flag to retain it. The judge model is a caller-supplied parameter, so a score's meaning depends on a deployment the score does not record.

**Three silences are recorded and one is taught.** The documentation states that skipped runs occur and **does not say that anything is emitted when they do** — the troubleshooting entry is the only route by which an engineer learns their results are incomplete, and it is reached by noticing a symptom. That is the silence condition D exposes and point 10 names. The documentation states that repetitions detect non-determinism and **publishes no figure for how large the non-deterministic component is** for any evaluator, so condition A's magnitude is discoverable only by running it. And, consistent with the cross-source asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](/research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), **nothing in this documentation states any accuracy, agreement or false-positive rate for any evaluator**, and no such claim is made here. The pages are dated by content rather than version, continuous evaluation is marked preview, and the cap value and sampling defaults are marked perishable at point 11.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** Contrasting cases with a fixed measurement, sequential disclosure and successive belief revision, the pretesting effect, retrieval practice at expanding intervals, cognitive load and relational interactivity, worked-example fading, and error management training are cited for **direction and mechanism only**. No effect size is asserted for any of them, and none is asserted for AI-assisted productivity anywhere in this paper.

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The sequential five-item pretest and its grouping disclosures; the four executed conditions and the decision to let the platform's own cap produce condition D; the eleven points of phase 4, the honest do-nothing remedy at point 6 and the agent extension at point 9; the seven-step phase 5 with the submitted-versus-returned reconciliation as its build; the mandatory agent budget entry; phase 7's competing-explanation and settling-observation protocol; the specification's random-only and no-agent-entry failure clauses; and all six §9 predictions are this design's own reasoning, untested. The §8 objection-1 concession accepts that the day weights a single dominance judgement over the error budget, contrary to its own defence, and specifies the phase 3 change that would correct it. The objection-2 concession identifies that the day's centrepiece competence is exercised once under conditions that cannot recur, specifies a costless adjudication step that would fix it, and notes that this is the second consecutive whitepaper in this module to find a missing adjudication. **The objection-3 concession accepts that the day's most transferable claim rested on a resemblance between a judge and an agent, that this is the module's own failure committed about the programme's primary tool, records the mandatory-budget-entry fix as made, and records that the new entry is diagnosable and not adjudicable — the same structural gap, on the same day, for the same reason.**
