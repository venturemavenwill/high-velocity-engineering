## In plain terms

There is almost always something already running. It works badly, people depend on it, and somebody built it.

Three responses are available and **all three are real engineering choices**, which is the session's whole content, because most teams only have two and one of those is usually not considered.

**Adopt its model.** Use the existing system's concepts and vocabulary, and accept its constraints as yours. The domain-modelling literature is unusual in defending this properly: it argues that a mature model has knowledge crunched into it, may be more advanced than your own understanding, and could drag you into a better design — and that the option is refused more often than it should be because it feels like a loss of authorship rather than because it is wrong. **The refusal is emotional and the source says so.**

**Translate at the boundary.** Keep your own model and build a layer that converts between the two. This is genuine engineering and it is expensive, and the expense is a standing one: the layer is maintained for as long as both models exist. The literature costs it with a parable about a wall that bankrupted a dynasty, and the moral is that the benefit of isolation must be balanced against its cost.

**Do not integrate.** Accept the duplication, or the manual step, or the two systems that do not talk. **Integration is always expensive, and two things being related in a story does not mean they must be connected in a system.** This is the option that is almost never written down, and requiring it to appear on the list is the session's structural intervention.

## Abstract

This paper defends requiring an explicit boundary decision — adopt, translate, or do not integrate — for every existing system a design meets, with a cost and a change condition attached to each. It argues that adoption is systematically under-considered for non-technical reasons, that translation is systematically under-costed because its cost is continuous while its build is a single event, and that non-integration is systematically absent from option lists. It requires the boundary map to describe reality including its organisational contours, and forbids redrawing it before reality has changed. The paper concedes that the literature supplying its vocabulary declares two prerequisites which many integration engagements do not satisfy, that the corpus's antipattern claim rests on practitioner judgement with no outcome data, and that requiring non-integration to appear as an option will produce entries written to satisfy the requirement.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

The default response to an existing system is to work around it, and the default is not a decision.

**It is a sequence of small avoidances.** Reasoning about the old system is unpleasant, its maintainers are hard to reach, its documentation is stale, and the fastest way to make progress this week is to build the piece you need. Repeated, that produces a new system that partially duplicates the old one, connected by whatever was convenient at the time, with no boundary anywhere and no statement of which model governs.

**The corpus names this and its version is sharper than the general observation:** reinventing rather than building on what the customer already runs is one of a small set of named failure patterns, given with warning signs observable in advance — nobody has spoken to the existing system's maintainers, the plan describes a replacement nobody asked for, the demonstration runs on invented data rather than the customer's own.

There is a second problem the first conceals. **Where a boundary does get drawn, it is usually drawn in the wrong place, because it is drawn on technical similarity rather than on ownership.** Boundaries that cut across a team's responsibility produce a permanent coordination cost that nobody costed, and the literature is explicit that in practice the political relationships between teams determine how systems get integrated.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** Anyone who has worked in an enterprise estate can do this session. A learner arriving from [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) does it with a register, which supplies the constraints each boundary has to respect.

It retrieves [S008](/wiki/seminars/S008.md), which asked when simpler technology would do. **Non-integration is the boundary-level form of that question**, and the retrieval is explicit so learners recognise it rather than meeting it as new.

The prior it works against is that integration is a good in itself — that connecting two systems is progress and leaving them separate is a failure of ambition. **The countermeasure is the cost line**, which requires the learner to write what the connection costs every year rather than what it costs to build.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate. The classification is quick and the costing is slow. Most of the session's time goes on finding out what the existing system actually does, which is the work the default response exists to avoid.

**3.2 Why adoption is listed first.** Order matters when a list is a decision procedure. Adoption listed last is adoption never reached, because by then the design has been drawn. **The literature's argument for it is unusual and worth teaching as content**: it names the refusal as emotionally driven and observes that a good upstream model may be more advanced than the team's own understanding.

**3.3 Why translation is costed as a standing cost.** A translating layer is built once and maintained continuously, and the asymmetry between the two is why it is under-costed. **The failure is not that teams do not know the layer costs something; it is that the estimate is attached to the build.**

**3.4 Why the map must describe reality including its political contours.** A map showing the arrangement you proposed rather than the one that exists causes everybody to reason about a system that is not there. The literature's rule is to map the existing terrain and take up transformations later, and **not to change the map until the change in reality is done.** It also supplies the speech test that shows the map has landed: people talk about the models and the boundaries rather than about which team's material is changing.

**3.5 Why false cognates are checked at the boundary specifically.** Two systems using one word for two different things is the failure that survives every review, because the word is familiar on both sides and neither side has any reason to question it. **This is the glossary discipline from [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md) applied at a larger scale**, and it is the same instrument.

The production-systems literature supplies the version of this failure with no vocabulary in it at all, and it is the sharpest teaching case available. **Training–serving skew** — a transformation computed one way at training and another at serving — produces no error, no exception and no obviously wrong output ([research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md)). It is a false cognate expressed in data, and it establishes the general property this session needs: **silent failure is the characteristic failure mode of a boundary**, which is why the check must be mechanical rather than intuitive. **That source is a practitioner survey with no comparative evidence for any recommended practice, a non-neutral toolchain, and figures that are properties of particular systems; none is reproduced.**

**3.6 Why the platform anchor is protocol composition rather than a build path.**

The session's structural claim is that extension beats replacement. It would be weak if it rested only on a modelling text from 2003, and it does not have to, because the current vendor comparison documentation makes the same claim in its own voice — and does so against its own commercial interest in a single-platform answer. **"These options aren't mutually exclusive. For example, a serverless agents runtime app can expose tools through MCP that Foundry agents or Copilot Studio agents consume"** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)).

Three things follow that the design uses directly. The same family names **three routes to an external system** — a tool catalogue, a model context protocol server, and an interface specification — which gives the translating layer a concrete typology instead of a box on a diagram. It names **orchestrator ownership** as the fork governing model choice, extensibility and lifecycle tooling together, which is the boundary decision restated for the agent layer. And it records that **publishing route is constrained by build choice**, which is a boundary decision taken at the start of a project that binds at its end — the exact shape of the cost this session teaches learners to write down.

The gateway documentation supplies the anticorruption layer as a purchasable object. It manages remote tool servers and agent-to-agent APIs as first-class endpoint types, and its preview unified interface exposes **"multiple backends through a single OpenAI-compatible endpoint"** with automatic format translation so that policy is applied once ([research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md)). It also insists that it **"extends API Management's existing API gateway; it's not a separate offering"** — which is this session's thesis, stated by a vendor about its own product line.

The internal platform synthesis makes the durable version: **the interoperability protocols are open protocols, not products**, and the argument that agents need standard routes to tools, data and other agents is vendor-independent ([research/06-microsoft-platform/03-accelerate-agentic-ai.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/03-accelerate-agentic-ai.md)). **That document is internal, is not publicly accessible, cannot be cited externally, and is the most perishable artefact in this repository**; where a claim from it is taught, the public documentation is the citable source, and none of its quantitative claims is repeated anywhere.

**3.7 What the platform material cannot do, and why the session says so twice.**

All of it is first-party product documentation with a selection-guidance purpose. Its authority is definitional. **It offers no adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no evidence that its recommended mappings produce better outcomes.** It compares one vendor's options against each other and not against alternatives. Several of the comparison tables were truncated in the excerpts this repository read, and none of the pages was fetched in full. **A course that teaches it as findings has taught a product catalogue**, which is why the session extracts the axes and discards the names, and why the perishability table is longer here than the session's own argument.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

Seven movements: situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 5 is the instrument.** *Is there a version of this where you do not integrate at all?* Nobody writes it down and most learners have never been asked. It is last because items 1 to 4 have to establish what integration would cost before the question has any force.

**Three options are required and each carries a cost, an owner and a change condition.** The change condition is this design's addition: a boundary decision without one is permanent by default, and permanence by default is how a temporary bridge becomes a standing tax.

**The work movement requires the translation to be written out for the largest boundary**, concept by concept, including what has no equivalent and what happens to a case that does not fit. **The cases that do not fit are where the maintenance goes**, and they are invisible in a box on a diagram.

**The date is required.** A translating layer with no end condition is a permanent tax, and accepting a permanent tax is a different decision from building a temporary bridge. **The session's demand is only that the learner say which one they are taking**, which is cheap and is almost never done.

**The review movement asks the existing system's maintainers what is wrong with the map**, and where that is impossible the impossibility is recorded on the map itself. **An unconfirmed map is usable; an unconfirmed map that looks confirmed is not**, and the distinction is the whole of the movement.

**4.1 What the platform workflow is for.** Not integration competence. It exists to make one claim physically true rather than asserted: **that the translating layer is a file with named concepts, a list of things with no equivalent, a rule for the case that does not fit, an owner, an annual cost and a date** — and that the last three are the fields nobody writes.

It has a second purpose that the session states and this paper will defend. Naming the exposed tools in the learner's own vocabulary rather than the upstream system's makes the anticorruption layer's public surface visible in a way a diagram cannot, because **a model reasons in whichever vocabulary it was handed.** A tool called by the upstream name has let the upstream model across the boundary before a single field has been mapped, and the learner can watch that happen in one prompt.

The gateway steps exist for the cost field rather than for the gateway. Per-consumer token accounting and dimensioned metrics are what make the standing cost attributable to a component, and **a boundary whose traffic cannot be attributed is a boundary whose cost cannot be written down** — which returns the movement to the artefact it began with.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The greenfield reflex.** The likeliest failure and the one the corpus names. *Countermeasure:* the requirement that adoption be considered and rejected for a stated reason rather than never appearing, plus the review movement's requirement to talk to a maintainer.

**The free translator.** The layer is a box with no owner and no line in anybody's plan. *Countermeasure:* the cost, owner and change condition fields.

**The wall you cannot afford.** The isolation is correct in principle and larger than the thing it protects. *Countermeasure:* the concept-by-concept translation write-up, which makes the layer's size visible before it is built.

**The map redrawn before reality.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's explicit prohibition, taken from the source, and the review movement, in which somebody who works on the existing system is asked what is wrong.

**The compliance rewrite.** An older service changed to conform to a new standard with no other benefit. *Countermeasure:* the standard names it, and the published playbook's own refusal to require retroactive compliance of its older services is cited as precedent. **A precedent is not evidence and this paper does not present it as one.**

**The absent maintainer.** *Countermeasure:* partial. The map must record the absence. **In many engagements the maintainer is genuinely unreachable, and the session cannot manufacture access — it can only stop the resulting map from looking authoritative.**

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence.** One specification item at [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md): where the design meets an existing system, the boundary decision is named and its standing cost is stated.

**The map itself is not assessed**, and that is deliberate. A map's accuracy depends on access the learner may not have, and marking it would penalise the engagements where access is hardest — which are the ones where the map matters most.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the boundary vocabulary is imported from conditions this session's learners will not have.**

At its strongest: the source declares two prerequisites flatly — development is iterative, and engineers have a close continuing relationship with the people who understand the domain — and says it claims nothing without them. **Integration engagements are the case where the second prerequisite is least likely to hold.** The people who understand the existing system are frequently in a different organisation, contractually unavailable, or gone. Teaching a modelling discipline whose author disclaims it in exactly this condition, for exactly this situation, is worse than the general version of this objection made at [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md).

**Conceded, and it is sharper here than at [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md).** The rebuttal is that the three boundary options survive the loss of the prerequisite even though the modelling does: you can decide to conform, translate or stay separate without a domain expert, and you will decide worse. **The declined fix is to teach only the options and drop the modelling vocabulary.** It is declined because the vocabulary is what makes the options distinguishable — without a notion of a model there is no difference between adopting one and translating to it — but the concession is real and the session's own limits section states it.

**Objection 2: requiring non-integration to appear as an option manufactures a hollow entry.**

At its strongest: a specification item that requires an option to be listed will produce the option, listed. It will be a sentence saying that not integrating was considered and rejected because the systems need to share data, which is a restatement of the problem rather than an analysis. **The requirement produces documentation and not thought**, and the entries it produces will be indistinguishable from the entries an honest consideration would produce.

**Partly conceded.** The requirement cannot force consideration and the design does not claim it can. The rebuttal is about the cost of the alternative: the option is currently absent from most such documents entirely, and an entry present and hollow is at least an entry somebody can challenge in review, while an absence cannot be challenged because nobody notices it. **The declined fix is to require a cost figure for the non-integration option rather than a sentence.** It is declined because a fabricated cost is more authoritative and harder to challenge than a hollow sentence, which would make the situation worse rather than better.

**Objection 3: anchoring the session to a protocol makes a durable argument depend on a perishable artefact.**

At its strongest: the session's thesis — extend rather than replace — is an architectural claim that predates every product named in it and will outlive them all. Attaching it to a specific tool protocol, a specific gateway's preview translation feature and a specific vendor's comparison page ties a durable lesson to material this repository itself marks as **highly perishable, read through search excerpts rather than full fetches, with several tables truncated and one consulted artefact not publicly citable at all.** When the protocol is renamed the session reads as dated, and a learner who took the mapping literally will conclude that the argument was about tooling. **Worse, the vendor's non-exclusivity statement is being used as corroboration of an architectural position, and a vendor with several products has a commercial interest in customers buying more than one of them** — so the most quotable sentence in the platform material is also the one whose motive is least clean.

**Conceded on the perishability and partly conceded on the motive.** The rebuttal on motive is narrow: the statement is corroborative rather than load-bearing, the architectural argument is carried by the modelling literature, and the vendor sentence earns its place because it is a published refusal to answer the question the page exists to answer, which is rare enough to be worth showing learners regardless of why it was written. On perishability the rebuttal is weaker and is really a design choice: **a session with no platform anchor produces no observable, and the previous version of this session had none** — learners left with a vocabulary and nothing they had watched happen. **The declined fix is to teach the boundary decision with a constructed example and no product.** It is declined because the workflow's central demonstration — that a model reasons in whichever vocabulary the tool names give it — cannot be constructed, only run. **The cost of that decision is that this session will date faster than its own argument, and the perishability table is the only mitigation offered.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Adoption will be the least frequently chosen option and the most frequently unmentioned. *Instrument:* the boundary decisions, counted by option chosen and by option considered.

**Prediction 2.** Translation costs on first pass will be stated as build costs, with no continuing component. *Instrument:* the cost fields, coded *build only* / *build and standing* / *standing only* by a second reader.

**Prediction 3.** Where learners draw a map before being told to include organisational contours, most maps will contain none. *Instrument:* first-pass maps, counted by presence of team or ownership annotation.

**Prediction 4.** Learners who spoke to a maintainer will record at least one behaviour of the existing system that contradicts its documentation. *Instrument:* the review movement's record. **If contradictions are rare, either the maintainers are not being asked the right question or the documentation in these estates is better than this design assumes, and the movement's justification should be revisited.**

**Prediction 5.** A majority of first tool definitions will carry the upstream system's vocabulary in the tool name, despite the workflow instructing otherwise. *Instrument:* the submitted tool manifest, with each name coded *upstream* / *local* / *hybrid* by a second reader. **This predicts the failure of §4.1's own countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 6.** The `unfitting_case` field will take longer to write than every other field in the boundary decision combined, and a substantial share of submissions will leave it empty or generic. *Instrument:* self-reported time per field, plus the field coded *specific* / *generic* / *absent*. **This is the session's only direct test of its claim that the exceptions are where the maintenance goes**, and a null result would mean the claim is an aphorism rather than a design input.

**Prediction 7.** Learners instructed to compare their item-5 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 3) will produce non-integration entries judged specific more often than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* a second reader's judgement, split within cohort on exercise-3 completion. **The same prediction is made across this module's sessions, so a null across all of them would indict the comparison pattern rather than any one session.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That three boundary options exist and one of them is almost never written down; that translation is paid continuously and costed once; and that boundaries follow ownership as often as they follow technology.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The boundary vocabulary comes from a source that **makes no quantitative claim of any kind**, whose evidence is named followed-up personal experience across identified projects including failures, and which declares prerequisites this session's situation frequently violates. The corpus's antipattern is practitioner judgement with warning signs and no outcome data, from material where no engagement went badly.

**The concession worth remembering.** This session applies a modelling discipline in precisely the condition its author excludes, and does it because the alternative is applying nothing.
