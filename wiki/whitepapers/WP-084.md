# WP-084 — Change, Resistance and the Adoption You Cannot Mandate

**Seminar day:** [S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md) · **Module:** [M17](/wiki/modules/M17.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · all · **Complexity class:** 6
**Platform anchor:** the AI gateway's per-consumer token metrics — `llm-emit-token-metric` with custom dimensions — against the substrate's own usage series · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (monitor) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends a day whose subject is why correct systems are not used, and whose central claim is that resistance is information before it is an obstacle. The target competence is producing an adoption position from observation rather than from report: what the population currently does, what their workaround solves, which of four conditions is failing, where the cost falls, and what the single available intervention addresses. The mechanism the design leans on hardest is a contrasting-case set matched on technical quality, with a payload deployment that was adopted, retained and used as designed by the wrong team, so that every claim in its business case is false. **The day's platform instance carries an unusual load: the agent answers the unanswerable pretest item in ninety seconds and the answer is about requests, because the gateway meters requests and a person is not a thing it meters unless somebody configured a dimension — and a dimension cannot be backfilled.** The principal risk is that the four conditions are read as a compliance checklist and convert a legitimate refusal into a communications problem. **Section 7 reports the discharge of the branch [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md) placed here under instrumentation rule 6, records the contingent reuse of the prior-course join, and records that the practical turn has supplied the fix §8 objection 2 handed to two later days.** Section 8 concedes that rule-6 compliance has destroyed this day's cross-cohort comparability, and concedes that the day's build is the largest single threat to the only irreplaceable resource the module has.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The difficulty is that the room's instinct is not merely wrong; it is the instinct that produced everything they have been rewarded for.

An engineer who ships something correct and finds it unused reaches for training and communication, and the reach is not stupidity. It follows from the belief that the artefact is the intervention and everything else is presentation — a belief the whole of the programme's technical arc has, correctly, reinforced. Item 3 of the pretest exists to record the instinct before phase 4 contradicts it, and the design's prediction is that training and communication will dominate the cohort's lists.

**The material's specific difficulty is that the correct response is usually to find out something rather than to do something**, and finding out is not an intervention in the register the room recognises. Point 2's claim — resistance is information — is the day's most portable sentence and its least actionable-sounding, which is exactly the property that makes an engineering room discard it.

The second difficulty is a construct problem the cohort is equipped for and has never applied here. **Adoption is a proxy and the day's payload is a case where the proxy is fully satisfied and the thing it stands for did not happen.** C4's usage figures are real. Every student in the room has been taught since [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) that a measurement measures something other than its construct, and none of them has applied that to a usage number, because a usage number feels like a count rather than a measurement.

The third is affective and is the one with the longest reach. **The organisation was working before the student arrived.** The workaround being replaced is visibly worse than the system replacing it, and treating it as somebody's solution to an unidentified problem requires a form of respect that is hard to extend to a spreadsheet. Point 8 is the day's most durable sentence and the one the room is least prepared to hear, and it cannot be delivered as an instruction because the instruction would be *be humble*, which is not a method.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Intrinsic load is low; the day's cost is in step 2 and in the thirty minutes at step 6.**

The interactivity is between steps 2, 3 and 7. What the population currently does determines which of the four conditions can be evidenced; the classification determines which interventions are even candidates; and step 7 requires the student to say why not the other three, which is only answerable if step 3 carries evidence rather than assertions. A student who classifies from intuition can still name an intervention, and step 7's third clause is what makes the gap visible.

Load reductions:

- **The change is the student's own** and its release record already exists.
- **The four-condition grid is supplied**, and the forecast form is [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md)'s, already held.
- **One prediction is tested, not all of them**, at step 6, so the encounter has a single purpose.
- **The three-column record format is two years old** and requires no instruction.

Expertise reversal applies at points 1 to 4 for any student with organisational experience. Point 8 is where the design wants such a student's attention, and it is the point their experience is least likely to have supplied, because a professional who has replaced a workaround successfully has usually not found out what it was for either.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *An unanswerable pretest item about your own shipped change,* item 1.
- *Writing another person's reason in their voice,* item 2, checked against what that person actually says four hours later.
- *Listing your available interventions before the four conditions exist,* item 3.
- *Cases whose decisive dimension is chosen against your cohort's own blind spot,* phase 3.
- *A payload whose usage figures are real and whose business case is false,* C4.
- *Finding what a workaround is for, unsupported,* step 2.
- *Naming the cost-bearer, unsupported,* step 4.
- *A dated, falsifiable prediction about other people's behaviour,* step 5.
- *Thirty minutes with somebody who is not there to be persuaded,* step 6.
- *Having your own field record audited for inference by a peer,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Running an intervention.* Step 7 names one; nobody executes it. Executing a change programme is a term's work and is CAP390's.
- *A resistant role-player.* The user at step 6 is real and is not performing. A constructed resister would teach the cohort to recognise a performance.
- *Quantifying adoption properly.* Step 5 asks for a rate prediction and a falsifying number, not a measurement design. Instrumenting adoption is ME390's.
- *The politics of imposed change.* Point 3's scope statement names the case where the change is imposed by somebody with no standing, and gives it to [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) rather than teaching it twice.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The four-condition frame is **safe as a diagnostic for a change the organisation has decided to make and can justify, applied to a population you can observe and ask.** It is **unsafe as a checklist for producing compliance.** The failure mode is precise and is the reason the statement is in the day rather than only in this paper: applied as a checklist, condition four — *allowed to* — becomes a permission to obtain rather than a fact to establish, and condition two — *want to* — converts C2's correct refusal into a motivation problem. **A student who runs the four conditions on a population that is right not to adopt will produce a well-evidenced plan to overcome them**, which is a worse outcome than doing nothing and is achieved entirely within the frame. The frame's two omissions are named at point 3: the person who is right not to change, and the change imposed without standing.

**The platform instance carries a second, at point 9, and it is bound to the artefact.** A per-consumer token metric is **safe for establishing which consumers of a gateway-fronted model are using it, at the granularity of whatever identity the request actually carries, over a period in which the dimension was configured.** It is **unsafe as a count of people, and unsafe for any period before the dimension existed.** Both halves are load-bearing and the second is the one students will not anticipate: **a dimension cannot be backfilled**, so a series emitted without one is aggregate permanently, and C3's rise-then-collapse is unrecoverable at week six if nobody configured the measurement at week zero. The first half fails in the way phase 6's agent case demonstrates — a shared service principal makes a population of a hundred look like a consumer of one, and the resulting curve is *the same people using it more*, which is a different fact from *more people using it* and is indistinguishable in the chart. **The artefact element that binds it is step 8's one-line requirement: say what a single value of this dimension actually is, and whether it is a person.** *This dimension is a service principal and is not a person* passes. *This dimension is the user*, unevidenced, fails, and it is the same fail condition as [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md)'s maintainer named as a function.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **item 1's unanswerability.**

A room asked what proportion of the target population is using a change they themselves shipped, and finding that nobody knows — including the student who wrote its detection commitment at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) — has a live problem rather than a topic. Nothing in the release record required an adoption measure, which the room can verify in the record in front of them, and the finding is about the programme's own artefact rather than about their competence.

The second mechanism is item 2 against step 6. The student writes what they believe a named person would say; four hours later, for the students whose named person attends, that person says something. **The match rate is reported and is not scored**, which is what allows a student whose guess was wrong to treat the result as information rather than as a mark.

Autonomy is at steps 2, 4 and 7, all unsupported. Competence is supported by the object being the student's own change and by the fact that a good adoption position can be produced without the change being adopted. Relatedness is supplied by the user at step 6 and by the phase 6 disclosure, which puts every student in the non-adopter's position.

**The affective risk is defensiveness at step 6.** A student sitting with a person who is not using something they built has an obvious available posture, which is to explain it. Countermeasures: the user is briefed that they are not there to be persuaded and the students are told this in advance; step 6's record must contain anything that contradicted the student, and a record containing no contradiction is examined; **adoption rate is not assessed and the specification says so**; and phase 6's clinic runs immediately afterwards with the students' own non-adoptions as the disclosure.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items on a change the cohort shipped. Item 1 is unanswerable and that is the finding; item 3 records the training-and-communication instinct before phase 4 names it. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction and asks not whether the agent will answer but what it will count instead.**

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Three retrievals at two years, three weeks and one week, closing with a bridge that composes [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md) and [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) into point 5.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** **Six minutes executed, then thirty-nine demonstrated.** The executed part is the agent's adoption measurement of the room's own change, which returns a number about requests. The demonstrated part is four changes matched on technical quality, with the decisive dimension conditioned on this cohort's [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md) result; they cannot be run and are **not assessed**. Sampling and branch both disclosed. Payload C4; C2 placed second. **The task-after's third item — would your number have distinguished C4 from C1 — is what makes the payload the room's problem rather than a case study.**

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Points 1, 2, 5 and 6 reached from cases; point 5 additionally from the bridge; point 8 exercised at step 2 before being named. Point 3 carries the strong-form scope statement. **Points 9 and 10 are the platform instance, placed after the argument per this module's convention; point 9 carries the second strong-form statement and supplies point 1's mechanism, and point 10 names the day's sharpest default — one that returns a plausible number rather than an error.**

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Eight steps with a real member of the target population for thirty minutes. Steps 2 and 4 unsupported. Step 7's PE301 clause is a contingent reuse. **Step 8 is the build, timeboxed to thirty of the ninety, performed first and listed last, with the user's thirty minutes timetabled ahead of it and an explicit statement that an unconfigured dimension plus a good question is the correct trade.**

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A valid survey that could not detect the obstacle, **an agent-written dashboard whose policy matched the documentation exactly and whose dimension resolved to one value**, and a disclosure about the students' own refusals.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Bilateral inference audit on the record made ninety minutes earlier, testing [WP-037](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-037.md)'s forward claim directly.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 1 against the measured rate; item 2 against what the user said; item 3's intervention distribution; **item 5's two rates, with the proportion whose own step 8 dimension collapsed to a single value reported beside them**; the [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md) branch stated to the room.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has run a change professionally will produce a competent four-condition classification and a plausible intervention. The predicted failure is **at step 2**: they will describe the workaround accurately and will not establish what it is for, because in professional practice the workaround's purpose is assumed to be inefficiency. Countermeasure: step 2's second clause requires the problem the workaround solves, do-not-know is available and expected, and the user at step 6 can be asked. **A student who marks do-not-know at step 2 and then finds the answer at step 6 has done the day.**

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is that the four conditions are filled in from the student's own model of the population, producing a fully-formed classification with no evidence behind any of it. Countermeasures: step 3 requires evidence per condition, a classification with no do-not-know marks and no named-person evidence fails, and phase 7 audits the one record the student did make.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Groups will identify C1 immediately and will usually get C3. C4 is the one that splits the room and some groups will rank it first, having read its title. The difficulty is relocated to the task-after's second half: **for C4, name who has to be told and what they have to be told.** That is uncomfortable rather than hard, and it is where the phase produces point 6. Fallback if a group answers it cleanly: ask what the deploying team should have done in week two, when the usage figures were rising and the identity of the users had not yet been checked — which has no clean answer, because checking would have been an odd thing to do and not checking is how the case happened.

**Affective failure.** Two.

The first is **defensiveness at step 6**, described in §3.3.

The second is the module's and arrives here in its most concrete form: **a student concludes that adoption is somebody else's problem.** Point 7 lists four things within an engineer's control and the list is short, and a room that hears "you cannot mandate use" without hearing the other half will file the whole day as an explanation for why non-adoption is not their fault. Countermeasures: point 7's four levers are named as real and are exercised at step 7, which requires one intervention and a reason for rejecting the other three conditions; and phase 6's case turns on a default and an approval step, both of which are on the list. **This countermeasure is adequate for the cognitive half and not for the dispositional one**, and the design has no instrument that would distinguish a student who has understood the boundary of their control from one who has found a boundary to hide behind.

Six items, one above this design's usual ceiling, accepted because points 2 and 8 are one sentence each and are the two the day is actually for.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md).

One specification item, in PE390 with CAP390. Per rule 1 the checked elements are the change and its population, the current practice with the workaround and the problem it solves or a do-not-know, the four-condition classification with evidence and do-not-know marks, the cost-bearer and benefit-holder by named party, the two predictions with falsifying numbers and check dates in the same field, the three-column record from step 6 including any contradiction, the intervention with its condition and the rejection of the other three, the named PE301 material, the phase 7 outcome, **the shipped per-consumer measurement with its weekly series and its one-line dimension statement, the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

Two hard requirements: **a four-condition classification with no do-not-know marks fails unless every condition carries evidence from a named person**, and **a measurement whose dimension is not stated in the one-line form fails.** Two examined conditions: a step 6 record with no contradiction, and an intervention whose third clause is unevidenced. **A build the platform would not support does not fail**, provided the student names the tier or preview constraint and ships the closest available thing with the gap stated; **a build that displaced the step 6 encounter does fail**, and the user's attendance record is the check.

**Adoption rate is not assessed**, and the specification says so in writing, at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) as well as here. The reason is [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §7's unchanged: an assessed rate makes step 5 strategic and converts step 6 into a persuasion exercise, which destroys the only observation the day generates.

**What this day does not license anyone to conclude.** It generates evidence that a student can produce an adoption position and conduct one non-persuasive conversation. It generates none about whether they can change an adoption outcome, because nobody executes an intervention, and the assessors' brief says so. It also generates nothing about the accuracy of the students' classifications beyond the one condition each tested at step 6.

### Discharge of the branch S081 placed on this day

**This paragraph is the report [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md)'s instrumentation block requires.**

[S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md)'s first rule-6 instrument was the phase 3 task-after category coverage — which of *absorbing*, *routing* and *licensing* the cohort produced unprompted, before any vocabulary was supplied — and it named this day's phase 3 as the response occasion, specifying that the four adoption cases be selected so that their differences fall on whichever mode the cohort did not produce.

**The element exists and it is a branch rather than a mention.** Phase 3's case set is a function of the S081 reading, and the substitution rule is stated for all three modes rather than for one: an omitted *licensing* mode makes the decisive resistance in C2 and C4 a manager declining to act on correct output; an omitted *routing* mode makes it what reaches the system at all; an omitted *absorbing* mode makes it who does the correction work afterwards. **The branch is disclosed to students at phase 3, reported at phase 8, and the classification error rate is examined for concentration on the substituted mode.** The obligation is discharged.

**A contingent reuse is recorded.** Step 7's PE301 clause is the prior-course artefact join whose first use is [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md) step 7 and which [WP-081](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-081.md) §7 records as a **proposed** remedy under instrumentation rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8. No day has run. **This reuse is contingent on S081's reported outcome, and the failure that retracts it is the one WP-081 named: if the PE301 material named at S081 is the same two items across most of the cohort, the join is checkable and empty, and it is removed here as well as there.**

**A forward claim is tested rather than asserted.** [WP-037](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-037.md) §6 stated that this day is that day's significant long-range target, on the ground that "adoption failures are almost always visible in observation and invisible in report, and the design's claim is that a student who has watched someone work has a category for the difference." **Phase 7 is the test**, and §9 prediction 4 states what result would refute it.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments. **The step 5 predictions** respond at [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) for the two-week figure, whose transfer inventory must carry measured usage against prediction per item, and at [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) for the six-week figure, which enters the student's own final defence material. **The phase 3 branch outcome** responds at [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md) phase 3, whose scope statement must name the carrying mode the receiving organisation is required to supply, with a named maintainer, and state what happens to the scope if they do not. **[WP-085](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-085.md) §7, [WP-086](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-086.md) §7 and [WP-090](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

Programme instrumentation: item 1's estimate error on a change the student shipped; item 2's voice-match rate; item 3's intervention distribution; the step 2 workaround-purpose do-not-know rate; and the phase 7 inference rate against the same cohort's [S037](/wiki/seminars/S037.md) column ratios.

ABET and CS2023 contribution: PO3 and PO5. Dispositions: **humility**, at step 2, where the workaround is somebody's solution and at step 6, where the person is not being persuaded; **integrity**, at step 6's contradiction clause, where recording what undermines your position is the honest act and omitting it is invisible; **reliability**, at step 5, where a dated prediction is a commitment somebody will check at S086.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: complying with instrumentation rule 6 has destroyed this day's cross-cohort comparability, and the design did not notice until now.**

At its strongest: phase 3's four cases are selected as a function of the preceding cohort-day's result. That is exactly what rule 6 requires — the deferral names the occasion and the occasion carries an element that consumes the result — and it is the strongest form of compliance the rule admits, because the branch changes the materials rather than adding a question.

**The consequence is that no two cohorts meet the same phase 3.** The classification error rate at phase 3, which the day reports as a figure and which §9 prediction 2 concerns, is measured on a case set chosen to be hard for that cohort specifically. A cohort that produced all three carrying modes at [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md) and a cohort that produced one will face materially different cases, and their phase 3 error rates are not the same measurement. The [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s programme self-instrumentation table lists transfer-task performance as a cadence-per-module instrument, and a branched task is not a transfer task across cohorts; it is a different task with the same name.

**The objection is conceded and it generalises beyond this day.** Every rule-6 branch in the programme that changes supplied materials rather than adding a required element has the same property, and this design has been writing such branches since [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md). Rule 6 was added to stop instruments being taken and dropped. **It has produced a class of instruments that cannot be pooled**, and nothing in the rule's statement warns about that, because the rule was written from the perspective of a single cohort.

The rebuttal is partial and is not enough. The day reports two figures rather than one: the classification error rate, which is cohort-specific and is used to teach, and **whether the errors concentrated on the substituted mode**, which is a within-cohort contrast and is comparable across cohorts because it is a ratio against that cohort's own baseline. That is a real repair for this day and it is not one the rule required, and it does not repair the other branches.

**The cheap fix exists and is declined.** A branch could be required to **add** material rather than to substitute it — five cases instead of four, with the fifth conditioned — which preserves a comparable core and costs one case's worth of time. It is declined here because phase 3 is forty-five minutes and a fifth case would push the ranking task past what the phase can hold, which is a timing argument of exactly the kind [WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 called insufficient when it was made about that day. **The honest position is that the design has traded a programme-level instrument for a within-day one, has done it repeatedly without stating it, and is stating it here for the first time.**

**Objection 2: the day teaches that adoption is a decaying rate and then measures it once.**

At its strongest: point 1 is the day's first claim and it is unambiguous — a single measurement is a point on a curve whose shape you have not observed. Step 5 requires predictions at two weeks and six weeks. **Both are single measurements, taken at [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md), and neither observes a curve.** C3's whole lesson is that a rate can rise for four weeks and collapse in the fifth; two points four weeks apart cannot distinguish C3's shape from a monotonic rise to the same value, which is the discrimination the day says matters.

The rebuttal is that two points are better than the one the release records currently contain, that the programme ends at week 90 and cannot observe more, and that the six-week point is deliberately placed after the five-week mark where C3 collapsed.

**That last clause is the strongest part of the rebuttal and it is also the concession**, because placing an observation where the design expects the interesting thing to happen is a choice made from the case set rather than from the substrate, and a student's change may decay on a different schedule entirely. The day teaches a shape and measures two points chosen against a known shape.

The fix is available and is genuinely cheap: **the substrate's usage telemetry already produces a weekly figure**, and the two prediction checks could be replaced by a curve returned to the student at S086 and S090 with their two predicted points marked on it. It costs a query and no phase time. **It was not in the design because the design wrote the predictions before checking what the substrate could return**, which is the same error as specifying a measurement without asking what the instrument supports — a thing this programme has taught since [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md).

**The fix is now taken, and by the practical turn rather than by this paper's argument.** Step 8 requires the student to configure the dimension and return **a weekly series rather than a point**, which is the curve the objection asked for, produced by the student on the day rather than supplied by staff afterwards. [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) now receive a series with two predicted points marked on it rather than two isolated readings, and [WP-086](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-086.md) §7 and [WP-090](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md) §7 must report whether the series arrived as well as whether the element exists.

**Two things about that are worth recording rather than celebrating.** The first is that the repair came from a requirement imposed for an unrelated reason — the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s build rule — and not from the design noticing its own defect; the defect was noticed here, in §8, and was handed onward, and it would still be handed onward if the build rule did not exist. **The second is that the fix is now contingent on a student completing a build**, where before it was contingent on staff running a query. That is a worse dependency, and it is the concession §8 objection 3 develops.

**Objection 3: the build is the largest single threat to the only irreplaceable thing in this day.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn and it bites hardest here, because this is the day with the most to lose.

At its strongest: the module's argument is that the constraints that matter are organisational, and its evidence is a set of encounters with real people. **This day's encounter is thirty minutes with a member of the target population who has agreed to sit with a student and not be persuaded.** That person is unpaid, unrepeatable, and under no obligation to come back. Against that, the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) requires a build, and the build this day chose is a gateway policy change with a preview-status dependency, a tier restriction and a deployment step — **the most failure-prone build in the module, placed on the day with the most valuable half-hour in it.** A group that loses twenty minutes to a policy that will not deploy has spent the module's scarcest resource on a configuration problem, and the day's own point 7 says that what an engineer controls is the default, the cost of the alternative path, the visibility of the outcome and who gets asked — none of which is what they were doing.

**There is a sharper version.** The build makes the day's finding *available without the person*. A student who ships a per-consumer series has an adoption measurement, and an adoption measurement is exactly the artefact the room mistook for the day's subject at item 1. **The design may have handed the cohort a better version of the wrong thing**, and made it easier to skip the conversation that is the right thing.

**The objection is conceded, in full, and the mitigations are stated so they can be judged rather than trusted.** The user's thirty minutes are timetabled first and the build is what compresses; the specification states that an unconfigured dimension plus a good question is the correct trade; a build the platform will not support does not fail provided the constraint is named; and **a build that displaced the encounter does fail, with the user's own attendance record as the check.** That last one is the only mitigation with teeth and it is a blunt instrument: it detects an absent student, not a distracted one.

**The cheap fix is named and is not taken.** It would be to **move the build to the between-day protocol entirely** — the dimension configured the following morning, the series returned at [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) — which costs the day nothing and preserves both the encounter and the repair to objection 2. **It is not taken for one reason and the reason is weak: the six-minute executed opening at phase 3 needs the room to have seen what the platform returns *before* phase 4 explains why, and a build completed overnight cannot supply the phase 3 moment.** But the phase 3 opening is six minutes and does not require step 8 at all — it requires only a query, which is already there. **On inspection the reason does not survive, and the honest position is that the design kept the build inside phase 5 because the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) says phase 5 produces a build, and moving it out would have required arguing with a programme page this paper is not permitted to edit.** That is a rule determining a design decision on a day whose whole subject is decisions determined by things other than the merits, and it is recorded here in those terms.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At item 1, more than half of students will give an estimate wrong by a factor of two or more against the measured rate, and the errors will be predominantly over-estimates. *Instrument:* item 1 estimates against measured usage, coded for direction and magnitude band. **The direction is the claim**: symmetric error would mean the cohort is guessing, and systematic over-estimation means they are reasoning from having shipped it, which is a different finding and the one the day assumes.

**Prediction 2.** At item 3, training or communication will appear first on more than three-quarters of intervention lists, and fewer than a fifth will name anything from point 7's four controllable levers. *Instrument:* item 3 responses coded by intervention class and by position in the list. **If the levers appear freely, point 4 is not the correction the day thinks it is** and phase 4 should reweight toward point 8.

**Prediction 3.** At step 2, the workaround-purpose do-not-know rate will exceed half, and among students whose named user attends at step 6, more than a third of those do-not-knows will be resolved in that thirty minutes. *Instrument:* step 2 marks paired with step 6 records, coded for resolution. **The second half is the claim and it is the day's argument in one number**: if asking resolves the question this readily, the reason nobody knows what workarounds are for is that nobody asks.

**Prediction 4.** At phase 7, more than a third of step 6 records will contain at least one inference recorded as an observation, and the rate will be lower than the same cohort's [S037](/wiki/seminars/S037.md) rate two years earlier. *Instrument:* phase 7 outcomes against the students' own S037 column ratios. **This is the test of [WP-037](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-037.md) §6's forward claim.** If the rate has not fallen, a student who has watched someone work does **not** have a durable category for the said/done/inferred distinction, the claim that day made about this one is wrong, and the discipline needs re-teaching rather than retrieving.

**Prediction 5.** At item 5, a large majority will predict that the agent returns a number, and **fewer than a third will name that the number is about requests rather than people. Independently, more than a third of step 8 dimensions will resolve to a single value on first deployment**, committing the error phase 6's agent case demonstrates. *Instrument:* item 5 coded on two axes; step 8 dimension cardinality recorded on first deployment and after intervention, cross-tabulated against whether the student named the error at item 5. **The cross-tabulation is the claim and it is uncomfortable for the design**: if students who predicted the error commit it at the same rate as students who did not, then predicting a failure and avoiding it are unrelated, and the entire sealed-prediction instrument measures something other than what the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) claims for it. **That would be a finding about the standard and not about this day**, and this is the first occasion in the programme on which the same student's prediction and performance on the same failure can be paired.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That resistance is information before it is an obstacle, that adoption is a decaying rate rather than a count, and that the correct first response to a correct system nobody uses is usually to find something out rather than to do something. Training and communication are what an engineering room reaches for and are rarely the answer.
**What the evidence licenses.** Contextual inquiry and the individual-level conditions for behaviour change are **class 1**, grounded in [research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md), for direction and mechanism only, with **no ordering strength or success rate asserted for any change framework.** **Bounded further 2026-07-30**, when both change sources were read in full: the element sequence is argued by entailment with **no evidence and no hedge**, the whole evidence base is the publishing firm's own material plus the author's conference polling and testimonial with **no independent evaluation cited anywhere**, and the organisational-change source **cites nothing at all by its author's own declaration** and **states no failure rate anywhere**. The class does not move — direction and mechanism was already all that was claimed — but the reason it cannot move is now on the record. The gateway metric is **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026, preview and tier-limited, with **nothing published about how well any dimension corresponds to use by a person.** The claim that a dimension cannot be backfilled is **class 3** — a design inference from what a custom metric is, not a documented behaviour.

**What to do with it.**

- Say what a single value of your dimension actually is. *This is a service principal and is not a person* passes; *this is the user*, unevidenced, does not.
- Find out what the workaround solves. *Do not know* is permitted, and asking usually resolves it.
- Configure the measurement before you need it. You cannot ask a series a question it was not emitting.
- Read a usage curve through the five classes on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md): an observed result about requests, an unsupported inference about people.

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes in full that the build is the module's most failure-prone and sits on its most valuable half-hour — the one with a person who agreed not to be persuaded — and that it may hand you a better version of the wrong thing, since an adoption measurement is what the room mistook for the day's subject at item 1. The move-it-overnight fix is named, and **the paper states that its own reason for refusing does not survive inspection.**
