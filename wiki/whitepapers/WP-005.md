# WP-005 — The Qualification Decision

**Seminar day:** [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md) ⊘ · **Module:** [M01](/wiki/modules/M01.md) · **Quarter:** [Q1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 1
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Foundry versus Copilot Studio build-path selection, recorded as an architecture decision record against the capability, timeline and control triple

> ## Release rule
>
> **§§4, 5 and 9 are post-session reading, and §7 is released only after the specification has been attempted.** §4 explains what the specification is really testing, §5 names the failure the assessment is built to produce, §7 states why the decision is unmarked, and §9 gives the predicted cohort behaviour. §§1, 2, 3 and 8 spoil nothing.

> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Specification check** | A pass/fail assessment against a closed list of elements, where nothing outside the list is considered |
> | **Disqualifying condition** | A statement, written in advance, of an observation that would stop the work rather than complicate it |
> | **Entrustment evidence** | A judgement that a learner may be trusted to perform an activity unsupervised. **None is generated here** |
> | **Entrustment-based discussion** | A short post-activity conversation that probes understanding and must include counterfactual questions |
> | **Architecture decision record (ADR)** | A numbered, dated, status-bearing record of one decision, its context and its consequences |
> | **Value-neutral context** | A description of the forces at play from which a reader cannot predict the decision that follows |
> | **BATNA** | The course of action actually available if no agreement is reached. Unknown, it is dangerous in both directions |
> | **Narrative judgement** | An assessment recorded as reasoned prose rather than as a score, because the useful information is *why* |

## In plain terms

This is the first assessment in the programme, and it marks something unusual: not the decision, but whether the learner can say what it rests on and what would change it.

That choice is deliberate and it is the paper's main argument. An assessor who marks the decision is asserting that they know which decision was right, and nobody in the room does. The brief is invented, the customer is invented, and no outcome exists to check anybody against. What can be assessed is whether a position was constructed out of separable parts — this I saw, this I was told, this I assumed — and whether it survived somebody pushing on it.

The risk of that choice is obvious and worth stating up front. **A learner can pass this assessment while holding a position that is comprehensively wrong**, and the design accepts that, because the alternative is an assessor pretending to a knowledge they do not have.

## Abstract

This paper defends assessing a qualification decision on its reasoning rather than its content. The argument runs in four parts. **First**, no outcome is available to mark against — the brief is invented, the customer is invented, and the practice corpus this programme rests on records no engagement anybody declined — so marking the decision would install a hidden preference for the answer the assessor favours and would assess agreement rather than judgement. **Second**, the separable properties of a defensible position are checkable without any claim about which decision was correct: evidence distinguished from report, conditions stated observably, authority stated honestly, reversibility named under pressure. **Third**, the entrustment literature this programme's assessment architecture is built on supplies both the boundary and the instrument — qualifying is an activity that can be handed over whole and declining is not, and the live element is an entrustment-based discussion whose defining requirement is counterfactual questioning. **Fourth**, the platform component converts one part of the decision from an opinion into a record: a build-path choice argued on capability, timeline and control, in a form whose context section a reader must not be able to predict the decision from.

The paper concedes that a wrong position can pass; that the live defence introduces an assessment mode advantaging fluent speakers; that the specification cannot detect a learner who has learned to produce its form without its substance; and that the decision-record template is borrowed from a source that contradicts itself and does not follow its own template in its own worked example.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Four sessions have produced four artefacts and no decision. That is a comfortable place to stop, and a great many engagements stop there: analysis accumulates, the decision is never made explicitly, and the work begins by default because nobody said otherwise.

**A decision that is never made cannot be wrong, and cannot be learned from.** The session exists to force the act, and to force it in front of somebody who argues, because a position held alone is not the same object as a position held against resistance.

The second problem is narrower and is about assessment rather than practice. The programme has to mark something, and the only honest thing available to mark is the reasoning.

The third problem is one the earlier version of this session did not address. A qualification decision in this field almost always contains a platform decision inside it, and a learner who can separate evidence from assumption about the *customer* while holding an unexamined preference about the *tooling* has done half the work. The build-path record exists to put the second half on the same page as the first, in a form that can be argued with.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**This-programme-only.** The session assembles four artefacts produced at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) to [S004](/wiki/seminars/S004.md) and cannot be attempted without them.

It is one of very few sessions in this stage with that entry state, and the reason is structural rather than doctrinal: the specification checks consistency between documents that exist only inside this programme. Element 4 in particular — that the decision agrees with the role boundary written at [S002](/wiki/seminars/S002.md) — has no meaning for a learner who has not written one.

The platform component assumes the documentation-checking habit established at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) and extended at [S003](/wiki/seminars/S003.md), and nothing beyond it. No prior exposure to either product is required, and a learner who has used both is at a mild disadvantage, because familiarity is the commonest source of a context section that predicts its own decision.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is moderate and almost all of it is integrative — four artefacts, previously handled separately, must be made to agree, and a fifth must be produced that agrees with all of them. The design's response is to forbid starting again, which removes the generation cost and leaves the reconciliation cost, which is the part worth paying. **A design judgement with no external warrant.**

**3.2 Why the live element is a defence, and why it must contain counterfactuals.**

A written position can be composed until it is unfalsifiable. A position defended live has to answer a specific objection in the moment, and the answer either contains new information or does not.

That much is design reasoning. What raises the live element above a viva is the shape the entrustment literature specifies for it. An **entrustment-based discussion** is a short, focused conversation, usually after the activity, that "should probe the student's deep understanding of what was done, but should include 'what-if' questions, to grasp whether the learner would know what to do what unexpected or familiar situations would arise" ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)).

**The what-if requirement is the whole distinction.** Retrospective explanation shows that a learner understood the case they met. Counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases they did not meet — and the source is explicit about why that matters: an entrustment decision implies the acceptance of risk, because it can never be fully predicted what the learner will encounter.

The same framework names three professionalism characteristics in behavioural terms: **integrity**, glossed as truthfulness in reports to a supervising clinician; **reliability**, as stable, predictable and conscientious working; and **humility**, as the ability to observe one's own limitations and the willingness to ask for help. **The third is what element 7 is actually looking at.** A learner who can name what would reverse them is displaying humility in an assessable form, which is a considerable improvement on assessing it as a disposition.

**The primer is guidance from medical education by the concept's originator, explicitly guidance rather than an evaluation**, and its transfer to engineering is this programme's inference.

**3.3 Why the assessor writes a reason rather than a mark.**

The decision to record narrative rather than a score is not a stylistic preference. Studies of assessors judging accumulated evidentiary records found that they "mainly relied on narrative feedback to inform their judgment", and that grades and qualifications "were merely used to confirm impressions based on narratives" ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). **The numbers were not the evidence.** They were a check on a conclusion already formed from the prose.

Two further findings shape the design directly. Assessors reached similar final pass/fail judgements while differing in their information-processing approaches and in the reasoning behind them — **convergence on an outcome conceals divergence in reasoning** — which is why that literature recommends that high-stakes decisions result from group discussion rather than from an individual, and why it requires assessors to substantiate their judgements. Substantiation is what makes divergent reasoning visible and therefore discussable.

And assessors were "inclined to stick to their initial judgments even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence"; their final judgements were more elaborate than their preliminary ones but not substantially different. **That is a warning about the assessor, not about the learner**, and it is the strongest available argument for requiring the reason in writing: a written reason can be compared against the evidence afterwards, and an impression cannot.

**Those studies are from medical education. The transfer to engineering assessment is this programme's inference**, and the note behind them records that several of the group's most-cited works are paywalled and were substituted with two open-access studies.

**3.4 Why known and assumed are separated on the page.**

The distinction is available to almost everyone in discussion and almost nobody in prose, because prose rewards a uniform register. Forcing two columns makes a distinction visible that a paragraph would smooth over.

The reason this particular distinction is load-bearing comes from a diagnosis of the practice corpus rather than from a literature. That corpus prepares its engineers most heavily against the objection that the tooling improved and delivery did not speed up, and its prepared answer reattributes the shortfall to the customer's own organisational constraints — while its readiness rubric is composed almost entirely of customer dispositions ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). **A model whose failure explanation is always the customer's readiness has arranged its evidence so that a disappointing result confirms rather than challenges the thesis.**

Element 2 of the specification is built directly against that. A belief attributed to nobody cannot be checked; a belief attributed to the sponsor can be, and the checking is what stops a position becoming unfalsifiable in the same way.

**3.5 Why the platform decision is recorded in the ADR form.**

The architecture decision record is used because its fields are unusually precise about a thing this session cares about. The published template specifies an ascending number and a title; a date; a status drawn from a closed vocabulary of "[Proposed/Accepted/Deprecated/Superseded]"; a context section that must be "a value-neutral description of the forces at play"; a decision section whose sentence begins "We will…" or "We have agreed to…"; and a consequences section ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md)). Records are "added as pull request in the proposed status", so the record enters review as a proposal rather than arriving settled.

Three of those carry the session. **The value-neutral requirement forbids the commonest defect in decision documents**, which is a context written to justify the decision below it. The closed status vocabulary includes `Superseded`, which gives a decision a way to be replaced rather than only to be right or wrong. And the proposed-status entry is the same move as the defence itself.

**The source has limits that must travel with it.** It has no single date; its page footer dates span several years and the unevenness is invisible in the navigation, so a citation must be pinned to a page. It contradicts itself in at least a dozen places. On decision documents specifically, its two trade-study pages disagree with each other about how many options to compare, **and its only worked trade study does not follow its own template**, omitting several required sections. The fields are used here because they are precise, not because they are validated.

**3.6 Why capability, timeline and control are the right axes.**

The published selection criteria for agent technology are stated as "your organization's technical capabilities, timeline, and control requirements" ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). The triple is worth adopting for a reason that has nothing to do with the products: it is the same trade that runs through every comparison in that family — the fast path against the controlled path — and it survives the disappearance of every product name in it.

The two candidate paths differ on all three, and the documentation is candid about the costs in both directions. Copilot Studio's published disadvantages include "limited ability to fine-tune API calls, actions, and response formatting compared with a pro-code environment", Adaptive Cards "limited to schema 1.6 and earlier", slower feature availability, and a positioning as "designed for small-scale deployments, such as department-level solutions". Foundry's basic setup "lacks network isolation" and is documented as being for rapid prototyping, against a standard setup providing "fine-grained control over data, security, and networking".

Two further published facts belong in any honest record and are easy to miss. Distribution is constrained by build choice — only certain toolchains can publish to the commercial store — so **a decision made at the start binds at the end**. And the options "aren't mutually exclusive": components on different platforms compose through MCP, which means **the unit of decision is a component and not a system.**

**This is first-party product documentation with a selection-guidance purpose.** Its authority is definitional. It offers no adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no evidence that its recommended mappings produce better outcomes. It was read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches, several comparison tables were truncated in those excerpts, and it is the most perishable material in this repository.

**3.7 Why a disposition may be assessed at all.**

The specification checks something close to a professional habit, and that requires a warrant. The 2023 curricular guidelines supply the disciplinary one: "Computer Science is a professional discipline where skills and professional dispositions are just as important as the underlying knowledge" ([research/07-accreditation-exemplars/cs2023-competency-and-disposition-materials.md](/research/07-accreditation-exemplars/cs2023-competency-and-disposition-materials.md)).

That document concedes in the same breath that the community "has not yet widely accepted the benefits of professional dispositions to the same extent". **It confers legitimacy, not compliance, and offers no validation against graduate outcomes.** Its own advice is the one this specification follows: do not grade against the competency framework directly, use it to justify the shape of assessment, and grade against locally defined observable behaviours. Every element of this specification is a locally defined observable behaviour, which is the only defensible way to assess a disposition without assessing a personality.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The specification's shape.** Seven elements, six checkable from the artefacts alone. Element 7 — that under challenge the learner names evidence which would reverse them — requires the live defence and is the reason the defence exists.

**4.2 Element 4 is the one learners do not expect.** It checks the decision against the boundary written at [S002](/wiki/seminars/S002.md), and it fails a learner who claimed at S002 that a decision was not theirs and then made it here. The check is cheap, mechanical, and catches the commonest drift in the stage: a rehearsed humility about authority evaporates the moment somebody has an opinion.

**4.3 Element 6 was added in the rebuild, and it changes what the assessment can see.** The earlier specification checked the decision and never checked the reasoning behind the platform choice inside it, which meant a learner could satisfy every element while carrying an unexamined tooling preference through the whole stage. The value-neutral context test is the discriminator: **a context section a stranger can predict the decision from is an argument, and an argument in a context section is the same defect as an unattributed belief in an evidence column.** One artefact now catches both.

**4.4 The challenger's script is narrow on purpose:** concede nothing, and make one reasonable offer that does not address the condition. That shape is chosen because the offer which does not address the condition is the actual form resistance takes in an engagement, and because it cannot be answered by restating the decision. The counterfactual questions required by §3.2 are asked after the offer, not before it.

**4.5 Why the assessor's output is prose.** Following §3.3, the assessor records a written reason naming which element carried the judgement. This costs more than a mark and buys two things: a record that can be compared against the evidence later, and a defence against the finding that identical conclusions can rest on incompatible reasoning.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The empty assumption column.** The most likely outcome is a learner presenting everything as known, because an assumption reads as a weakness in an assessed artefact. *Countermeasure:* element 2 requires each reported belief to name its source, which makes the column difficult to leave empty without visibly attributing an observation to nobody.

**Fluency advantage in the defence.** The live element favours learners who speak easily under pressure, independent of the quality of their position. *Countermeasure:* partial and unsatisfactory. Element 7 asks only whether the learner names reversing evidence, which is a low bar deliberately set so that a hesitant learner who names it passes. **The advantage is reduced and not removed, and the design has no better answer.**

**Specification mimicry.** A learner produces the form — two columns, a named observer, a stated authority, a neutral-sounding context — with no reasoning behind it. *Countermeasure:* none. The specification cannot distinguish a well-formed position from a well-formed imitation of one, and this is stated to learners rather than concealed.

**The advocating context.** The context section argues for the decision, in language that sounds descriptive. *Countermeasure:* the stranger test in workflow step 5, which is performed by the learner on themselves and is therefore the weakest control in the session. §9's third prediction expects it to fail.

**Assessor anchoring.** The assessor forms a view in the first thirty seconds of the defence and elaborates it for ten minutes, which is exactly what §3.3 predicts of assessors generally. *Countermeasure:* the written reason, which must name the element that carried the judgement and can therefore be checked against the artefact afterwards by somebody else. **That is detection rather than prevention**, and the design has nothing better.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence.** One specification check, seven elements, pass/fail, resubmittable. The assessor records a written reason naming which element carried the judgement, and not a mark.

**What is assessed** is the reasoning behind the qualification decision and its internal consistency with the artefacts that produced it. Specifically: that the problem is stated without a technology; that beliefs are separated into observed and reported with each reported belief naming its source; that at least one condition is stated in a form that could be observed to occur, with a named observer and a time; that the decision is consistent with the role boundary written at [S002](/wiki/seminars/S002.md); that the decision names the person or function whose agreement it requires; that the build-path record carries a value-neutral context, a closed-vocabulary status and at least one documented limit with the date it was checked; and that under challenge the learner names at least one piece of evidence that would reverse the decision.

**The decision is not marked, and this is the design's most consequential assessment choice.** A defended decline and a defended proceed pass identically. The reason is that no outcome exists against which either could be judged: the brief is invented, and the practice corpus behind this programme records no declined engagement to compare against. An assessor who marked the decision would be marking their own preference and calling it a standard.

That boundary follows the entrustment frame rather than being invented for convenience. **Qualifying an engagement is a plausible entrustable activity — bounded, delegable, with a beginning and an end. Declining one is not**, because a decline is an outcome rather than an activity, and its correctness depends on facts about the engagement the assessment cannot hold constant ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). Assessing the capability of the reasoning assesses the activity. Assessing whether the learner declined would assess their agreement with the assessor: the first is entrustment, the second is compliance.

The corollary is stated to learners before the assessment rather than discovered afterwards: **a comprehensively wrong position, well constructed and honestly defended, passes.** That is not a loophole. It is the boundary of what this instrument measures.

**Nothing else is assessed.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: an assessment that does not mark the decision teaches that the decision does not matter.**

At its strongest: learners optimise for what is marked. Marking only the reasoning produces engineers who can construct a defensible position about anything and have no practice at being right, which is the actual job. Worse, it lets a learner discover that a polished process is rewarded regardless of content — precisely the failure this programme diagnoses in its own source, where activity is measured and outcome is not.

**This is substantially correct and the design concedes it.** The rebuttal is narrower than the objection: no outcome exists here to mark against, so the choice is not between marking the decision and marking the reasoning, but between marking the reasoning and marking the assessor's preference. **The declined fix was to score decisions against a concealed model answer held by the assessor**, rejected because a model answer to an invented brief is an opinion with a rubric attached. **The objection stands against the whole programme's ability to assess judgement, and it is not answered here or later.**

**Objection 2: the live defence assesses composure and calls it reasoning.**

At its strongest: the two are correlated, the correlation is not the thing being claimed, and an assessment that disadvantages learners who think slowly or speak a second language is unjust regardless of what it measures well.

**Conceded.** The mitigation is that element 7 sets a low, specific bar — name one piece of reversing evidence — answerable in a sentence and not rewarding elaboration. **The declined fix was to move the defence to writing**, rejected because a written answer can be composed until the objection is no longer the one that was asked, and because the counterfactual questioning §3.2 requires cannot be conducted asynchronously without the learner researching the answer. That rejection may be wrong.

**Objection 3: the build-path record is a second assessment smuggled into the first, and it assesses vendor product knowledge.**

At its strongest: element 6 requires a learner to produce a documented limit with a date, which rewards familiarity with one vendor's documentation estate. A learner who has read those comparison pages before will produce a better record than one who has not, independent of the quality of their reasoning — and the pages themselves are the most perishable material in the repository, so the element assesses currency with a moving target.

**Partially conceded, and the mitigation is in what the element checks.** It checks that a limit was found, dated and recorded, and that the context section does not predict the decision. It does not check *which* limit, and a `NOT ADDRESSED` verdict satisfies it as fully as a stated envelope does — which is the design's answer to the perishability charge, because a documented silence is stable in a way a documented capability is not. **The declined fix was to supply the documentation excerpts to learners rather than have them fetch pages**, declined because the finding the element exists to produce is that most of a platform preference rests on nothing documented, and a supplied excerpt set answers that question in advance. A reader who judges the element to be product knowledge in disguise is making a defensible call; §9's fourth prediction is where it would show.

**Objection 4: assessing a disposition through seven observable elements is a category error, and the elements will be gamed within one cohort.**

At its strongest: what the specification wants is a habit of mind, and what it can see is a document. Once a learner knows that a non-empty assumption column passes and an empty one fails, the assumption column will be non-empty, and it will contain whatever is cheapest to write. The specification will then measure knowledge of the specification.

**Conceded, and §5 names it as specification mimicry with no countermeasure.** The design's position is that the alternative is worse: grading against a competency framework directly rather than against locally defined observable behaviours, which the curricular guidance this programme leans on explicitly advises against ([research/07-accreditation-exemplars/cs2023-competency-and-disposition-materials.md](/research/07-accreditation-exemplars/cs2023-competency-and-disposition-materials.md)). **The declined fix was to withhold the specification from learners until after submission**, declined because an assessment whose criteria are secret cannot be prepared for and produces anxiety rather than judgement — and because the release rule already withholds §7, which is the part that would most distort behaviour. **The residue is real: this specification is gameable, and the design accepts a gameable honest instrument over an ungameable dishonest one.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first submissions will contain an assumption column that is empty or contains only trivial items. *Instrument:* the artefact's two columns, with each assumption coded *consequential* / *trivial* / *absent* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Element 4 will fail more learners than any other element. *Instrument:* per-element failure counts across the cohort at first submission.

**Prediction 3.** A majority of context sections judged neutral by their authors will be judged predictive by a stranger who reads only that section. *Instrument:* the workflow's step-5 stranger test, run independently by an assessor on a sample, against the learner's own recorded judgement. **This predicts the failure of the session's own countermeasure** — the neutrality test is performed by the learner on their own writing, which is the weakest control in the design, and a rule whose self-application rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 4.** Learners with prior exposure to either product will produce build-path records with more `SUPPORTED` cells and fewer `NOT ADDRESSED` cells than learners with none, and will not more often pass element 6. *Instrument:* a self-reported prior-exposure item against the verdict distribution and the element-6 outcome. **This is the direct test of objection 3.** If prior exposure predicts element 6 outcomes, the element is assessing product knowledge and should be replaced.

**Prediction 5.** Learners whose conditions were judged incapable of firing at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) will amend them before this assessment more often than they defend them. *Instrument:* the S001 review judgement matched against the presence of a dated amendment at S005. If this fails, the S001 review is not carrying forward and the amendment prompt should move earlier.

**Prediction 6.** Assessors' written reasons will name the element that carried the judgement in a minority of cases at first use, and will converge on naming it after the reasons are read aloud in a moderation session. *Instrument:* the written reasons, coded for whether an element is named, before and after moderation. **This tests the §3.3 claim about substantiation making reasoning discussable**, and a failure would mean the written reason is ceremony and a mark would be cheaper.

**Prediction 7.** Where two assessors independently judge the same defence, they will agree on the pass/fail outcome more often than they agree on which element carried it. *Instrument:* paired assessments on a sample, with outcome agreement and element agreement recorded separately. **This is the direct test of the transfer in §3.3** — convergence on an outcome concealing divergence in reasoning — and a null result would mean the finding does not carry from portfolio assessment to engineering defence.

**Prediction 8.** Build-path records will name the technology and omit the population that will build or extend it, and the omission will persist after the §3 material is read. *Instrument:* each submitted record coded by a second reader for whether it identifies a building population and an inheriting population, scored separately, with the cohort split on whether the record chose a low-code or a pro-code path. **The design expects the omission to be worse on pro-code records**, because the pro-code path makes the delivery team the implicit answer; a null on that split would mean the asymmetry claim in §3 is wrong even if the general omission holds.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a position can be assessed on its construction without anyone claiming to know which position was right; and that the same test applies to a platform preference as to a customer belief, because both are claims whose grounds can be separated from their conclusions.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The reasoning behind separating observed from reported belief is class 2. The entrustment frame and the narrative-assessment findings are class 1 within their own literatures and class 3 in their transfer to engineering. The published platform limits are class 4 — definitional, dated and perishable. **The claim that a defended position is a better object than an undefended one is this design's own, and is untested.**

**The concession worth remembering.** You can pass this while being wrong. **The assessment measures whether you could find out that you were wrong, and it cannot tell you that you are.**

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper**, and none may be inferred from it.

**The silences that matter.** The documentation offers no adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no evidence that its recommended mappings produce better outcomes, so its selection guidance is a reasoned framework rather than a validated finding — **which is precisely why element 6 checks that a limit was found and dated, and never which limit was found.** It states no scale envelope for most of the tools it compares, so a documented silence is the expected verdict rather than an unusual one. It says nothing about how a capability boundary should be re-verified over time, or how long a recorded verdict remains good for, which is why every cell of the options table carries the date it was checked rather than the date the record was written. And it publishes no guidance at all on when a build-path decision should be superseded rather than merely regretted — **the `Superseded` status comes from the engineering playbook and not from the product documentation**, and the two sources have never been reconciled by anybody.
