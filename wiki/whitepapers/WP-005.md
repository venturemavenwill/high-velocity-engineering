## In plain terms

This is the first assessment in the programme, and it marks something unusual: not the decision, but whether the learner can say what it rests on and what would change it.

That choice is deliberate and it is the paper's main argument. An assessor who marks the decision is asserting that they know which decision was right, and nobody in the room does. The brief is invented, the customer is invented, and no outcome exists to check anybody against. What can be assessed is whether a position was constructed out of separable parts — this I saw, this I was told, this I assumed — and whether it survived somebody pushing on it.

The risk of that choice is obvious and worth stating up front. **A learner can pass this assessment while holding a position that is comprehensively wrong**, and the design accepts that, because the alternative is an assessor pretending to a knowledge they do not have.

## Abstract

This paper defends assessing a qualification decision on its reasoning rather than its content. The argument is that no outcome is available to mark against, that marking the decision would install a hidden preference for the answer the assessor favours, and that the separable properties of a defensible position — evidence distinguished from report, conditions stated observably, authority stated honestly, reversibility named under pressure — are checkable without any claim about which decision was correct. The paper concedes that a wrong position can pass, that the live defence introduces an assessment mode which advantages fluent speakers, and that the specification cannot detect a learner who has learned to produce its form without its substance.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Four sessions have produced four artefacts and no decision. That is a comfortable place to stop, and a great many engagements stop there: analysis accumulates, the decision is never made explicitly, and the work begins by default because nobody said otherwise.

**A decision that is never made cannot be wrong, and cannot be learned from.** The session exists to force the act, and to force it in front of somebody who argues, because a position held alone is not the same object as a position held against resistance.

The second problem is narrower and is about assessment rather than practice. The programme has to mark something, and the only honest thing available to mark is the reasoning.

**This-programme-only.** The session assembles four artefacts produced at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) to [S004](/wiki/seminars/S004.md) and cannot be attempted without them.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is moderate and almost all of it is integrative — four artefacts, previously handled separately, must be made to agree. The design's response is to forbid starting again, which removes the generation cost and leaves the reconciliation cost, which is the part worth paying.

**3.2 Why the assessment is a defence rather than a submission.** A written position can be composed until it is unfalsifiable. A position defended live has to answer a specific objection in the moment, and the answer either contains new information or does not. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted, and no claim is made that live defence is a more reliable instrument than written work.**

**3.3 Why known and assumed are separated on the page.** The distinction is available to almost everyone in discussion and almost nobody in prose, because prose rewards a uniform register. Forcing two columns makes a distinction visible that a paragraph would smooth over.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The specification has six elements and five of them are checkable from the artefact alone. Element 6 — that under challenge the learner names evidence which would reverse them — requires the live defence and is the reason the defence exists.

**Element 4 is the one learners do not expect.** It checks the decision against the boundary written at [S002](/wiki/seminars/S002.md), and it fails a learner who claimed at S002 that a decision was not theirs and then made it here. The check is cheap, mechanical, and catches the commonest drift in the stage, which is that a rehearsed humility about authority evaporates the moment somebody has an opinion.

The challenger's script is narrow on purpose: concede nothing, and make one reasonable offer that does not address the condition. That shape is chosen because the offer which does not address the condition is the actual form resistance takes in an engagement, and because it cannot be answered by restating the decision.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The empty assumption column.** The most likely outcome is a learner presenting everything as known, because an assumption reads as a weakness in an assessed artefact. *Countermeasure:* element 2 requires each reported belief to name its source, which makes the column difficult to leave empty without visibly attributing an observation to nobody.

**Fluency advantage in the defence.** The live element favours learners who speak easily under pressure, independent of the quality of their position. *Countermeasure:* partial and unsatisfactory. Element 6 asks only whether the learner names reversing evidence, which is a low bar deliberately set so that a hesitant learner who names it passes. **The advantage is reduced and not removed, and the design has no better answer.**

**Specification mimicry.** A learner produces the form — two columns, a named observer, a stated authority — with no reasoning behind it. *Countermeasure:* none. The specification cannot distinguish a well-formed position from a well-formed imitation of one, and this is stated to learners rather than concealed.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence.** One specification check, six elements, pass/fail, resubmittable.

**The decision is not marked and this is the design's most consequential assessment choice.** A defended decline and a defended proceed pass identically. The reason is that no outcome exists against which either could be judged: the brief is invented, and the practice corpus behind this programme records no declined engagement to compare against. An assessor who marked the decision would be marking their own preference and calling it a standard.

The corollary is stated to learners before the assessment rather than discovered afterwards: **a comprehensively wrong position, well constructed and honestly defended, passes.** That is not a loophole. It is the boundary of what this instrument measures.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: an assessment that does not mark the decision teaches that the decision does not matter.**

At its strongest: learners optimise for what is marked. Marking only the reasoning produces engineers who can construct a defensible position about anything and have no practice at being right, which is the actual job. Worse, it lets a learner discover that a polished process is rewarded regardless of content — precisely the failure this programme diagnoses in its own source, where activity is measured and outcome is not.

**This is substantially correct and the design concedes it.** The rebuttal is narrower than the objection: no outcome exists here to mark against, so the choice is not between marking the decision and marking the reasoning but between marking the reasoning and marking the assessor's preference. The declined fix was to score decisions against a concealed model answer held by the assessor, rejected because a model answer to an invented brief is an opinion with a rubric attached. **The objection stands against the whole programme's ability to assess judgement, and it is not answered here or later.**

**Objection 2: the live defence assesses composure and calls it reasoning.**

At its strongest: the two are correlated, the correlation is not the thing being claimed, and an assessment that disadvantages learners who think slowly or speak a second language is unjust regardless of what it measures well.

**Conceded.** The mitigation is that element 6 sets a low, specific bar — name one piece of reversing evidence — which is answerable in a sentence and does not reward elaboration. The declined fix was to move the defence to writing, rejected because a written answer can be composed until the objection is no longer the one that was asked. That rejection may be wrong.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first submissions will contain an assumption column that is empty or contains only trivial items. *Instrument:* the artefact's two columns, with each assumption coded *consequential* / *trivial* / *absent* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Element 4 will fail more learners than any other element. *Instrument:* per-element failure counts across the cohort at first submission.

**Prediction 3.** Learners whose conditions were judged incapable of firing at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) will amend them before this assessment more often than they defend them. *Instrument:* the S001 review judgement matched against the presence of a dated amendment at S005. **If this fails, the S001 review movement is not carrying forward** and the amendment prompt should move earlier.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a position can be assessed on its construction without anyone claiming to know which position was right.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The reasoning behind separating observed from reported belief is class 2. The claim that a defended position is a better object than an undefended one is class 3 and is this design's own.

**The concession worth remembering.** You can pass this while being wrong. The assessment measures whether you could find out that you were wrong, and it cannot tell you that you are.
