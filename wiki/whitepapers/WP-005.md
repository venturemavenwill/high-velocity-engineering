# WP-005 — Induction and the Machine That Runs Forever

**Seminar day:** [S005](wiki/seminars/S005.md) · **Module:** [M01](wiki/modules/M01.md) · **Quarter:** [Q1](wiki/quarters/Q1.md)
**Strands:** MS · CF · SY · **Complexity class:** 1
**Platform anchor:** GitHub Copilot agent mode; custom instructions as executable specification · Copilot as a proof-checking adversary · **MCP servers:** Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching induction through loop invariants rather than through the naturals, and defends requiring students to break their own proof as the terminal task of the module. The first argument is one of transfer-appropriate processing: students will encounter unbounded processes as loops, recurrences and state machines, and an induction competence encoded on numeric summation formulae is encoded in a context that does not resemble the retrieval context. The second argument is that constructing a counterexample to one's own argument is the only available discriminator between a proof that was understood and a proof that was pattern-matched, because both produce identical artefacts. The paper also defends the separation of invariance from termination as a distinct instructional object, on the grounds that their conflation is the single most consequential reasoning error in the module and that it is invisible unless deliberately provoked. **It further defends the day's terminal claim about the module's own instrument — that a reviewer whose failures correlate with yours is not a check — and defends grounding that claim in a construction rather than in a warning, by having students build an executable invariant check whose value is precisely that it does not reason.** It concedes that the day's four-loop contrast may be recoverable by elimination rather than by understanding, that the module's closing paragraph is an instrument the design cannot currently interpret, and that the correlation finding was reached by a fortunate accident of case selection rather than by design.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The module has established three things: a machine's behaviour is determined by its state and its instructions; a representation discards; an argument is addressed to a reader. None of these lets a student say anything true about a program that runs an unknown number of times. Tracing terminates at the trace's end. The gap between *determined* and *known* is real and there is exactly one technique that closes it.

Induction is that technique, and it is badly taught almost everywhere, for a specific reason. It is introduced on the naturals, with summation formulae as its canonical exercise. Students learn a ritual — assume for $n$, show for $n+1$ — that is dissociated from any process it might describe, and the ritual is portable to other summation formulae and to nothing else. When the same students later face a loop, a recursive procedure or a distributed protocol, the competence does not fire, because nothing in the retrieval situation resembles the encoding situation.

The second problem is subtler and is the reason phase 4 point 5 exists. Students conflate *the loop maintains this property* with *the loop ends*. The conflation is nearly invisible in ordinary exercises, because the exercises terminate. It becomes visible and expensive in production, where the invariant holds perfectly on a loop that never exits, and where a monitoring property that is true whenever checked says nothing about a system that has stopped checking. Separating the two obligations is cheap here and cannot be retrofitted later.

The third problem is one of module closure. M01's five days must combine into a stance, not accumulate as five topics. This day is the one where all four prior days are simultaneously necessary — the machine from S002, the representation from S003, the reader from S004, the design awareness from S001 — and the design uses that necessity deliberately, which is why the module closes here and why phase 8 asks for the paragraph.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge assumptions

- **A hand-traced program.** The running-maximum loop from S002 is the phase 5 subject. Every student has executed it by hand, so nobody spends phase 5 capacity on comprehension.
- **Widely varying induction exposure**, and — importantly — variation in *quality* rather than presence. Students who have done summation induction may be worse placed than students who have done none, because they have a ritual to apply.
- **The S004 rejection protocol,** used in phase 5 step 4 without re-explanation. This is the first day that treats a prior day's protocol as available infrastructure.
- **Four days of error-clinic norms** and one prior experience, at S003, of a personally-owned discovery.
- **An outstanding S004 commitment** — a claim the student could not defend — which phase 8 closes.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**High, and the highest in the module.** A loop-invariant proof requires holding the loop's state, the candidate property, the establishment obligation, the preservation obligation and the termination argument simultaneously, and the obligations interact: a property that is easy to establish is often hard to preserve, and strengthening it to preserve makes establishment harder. That interaction is not decomposable, which is what makes the element interactivity genuinely high rather than merely large.

Load management is aggressive and is the reason the day can carry this content in week five.

- **The subject program is already known.** Zero comprehension load in phase 5.
- **Complete fading.** Step 2 is worked with the room on a simpler loop; step 3 is a completion problem with the variant's form supplied; only steps 4 and 5 are independent. This is the module's most heavily faded task and deliberately so.
- **Phase 3 isolates the definitional question** from any proof obligation. Groups decide only whether a property *could* support an argument, not whether it does. That separation removes the interaction from the harder cognitive step.
- **Notation is not required.** Precise natural language throughout.
- **Phase 4 point 5 is given its own segment** rather than being a clause, because a distinction delivered as a clause inside a dense explanation is not received.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Breaking your own proof,* phase 5 step 5. The day's defining difficulty and its principal discriminator.
- *Committing the invariant in writing before proving anything,* which prevents the common move of discovering the invariant by writing the proof and then presenting it as though it were known.
- *The loop 4 near-invariant* in phase 3, which is the case that forces the definition to be stated precisely rather than approximately.
- *Diagnosis from behaviour alone* in phase 7 — identifying which obligation failed without being told.
- *A nine-day spaced retrieval* of S001 in phase 2, and a cross-day discrimination item.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Discovering the invariant unaided.* Students are asked to state it, not to find it in an unfamiliar loop. Invariant discovery is class-3 work and appears at S031.
- *Withholding the establishment / preservation / termination framing.* Named directly in phase 4. This is vocabulary, and withholding vocabulary produces imprecision, not insight.
- *Numeric induction as the entry point.* Refused, per §1. The naturals appear at phase 4 point 2 as a familiar special case, after the loop framing is already in play.
- *Formal verification tooling.* Would move the difficulty into the tool.
- *Assessment stakes on the same day.* The joint MS101/CF101 item is submitted afterwards.
- *Selecting phase 3's payload for its effect on the agent.* Loop 4 was chosen years before the day had a platform anchor, for its difficulty for students. **A case selected to embarrass a tool would prove nothing**, and the day tells the room the coincidence is a coincidence.

**The scope statement for the platform instance, in strong form.** The day teaches a simplification with real operational consequences: **that a check must be independent of the thing it checks, and that reasoning-shaped reviewers correlate with reasoning humans.**

That simplification is **safe** as a claim about any reviewer that reasons — a colleague, a language model, the student themselves an hour later — and as a rule for deciding when an agreeing review is evidence. It is **unsafe** in four conditions and the day states all four.

- **As a claim about executable checks.** They fail differently. Phase 5's property check catches loop 4 precisely because it does not reason about the loop, and generalising the correlation claim to it would forbid the one construction the day recommends.
- **As a claim about all correlation.** The day observed correlation on one case. **What is durable is the conditional — where a reviewer's failures correlate with yours, agreement is not evidence — and not the observation that this reviewer's do.** A student who leaves believing that agent review is worthless has taken the wrong half; it was right about loops 1, 2 and 3.
- **As a claim about this release.** If a later model resolves loop 4 correctly, **the claim in phase 4 point 7 is unchanged**, because it is a claim about what agreement licenses and not about one model's competence. The phase 3 case is then re-selected and the perishability register records that it was. This is the one platform-dependent point in the module whose durable half survives its instance failing.
- **As a claim that the check validates the loop.** The property check asserts during iteration and says nothing about a loop that stops iterating. **A student who reports it as validating the loop has collapsed the exact distinction phase 4 point 5 spent a full segment separating**, and specification item 7 fails them for it.

The artefact element that makes the boundary checkable is that item: the termination omission must be named in the student's own report, in those terms.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day's motivational structure is **closure**, and it is the module's only day designed around that.

Phase 3 sets a genuine puzzle in which loop 4 defeats nearly every group. Groups know they have not resolved it, which is the productive-failure state, and phase 4 point 4 resolves it exactly. That is a clean satisfaction and it is the strongest single moment in the module.

Phase 5 step 5 produces a different and rarer engagement: students enjoy breaking things, and being told to break their own proof converts a compliance task into a construction task. The reported affect is playful, which is unusual for a proof exercise, and the design leans on it deliberately because it is the step most likely to be skipped if it felt like more proving.

Phase 8's paragraph — what do you now believe about programs that you did not believe five days ago — is the module's competence signal. It is the first point at which students are asked to notice that something changed, and the design's expectation is that most of them can name it. Keeping the paragraphs and returning them at S090 makes the day the origin of a three-year thread, which is stated to the cohort when the paragraphs are written.

Relatedness is supported by phase 7's reciprocal diagnosis, which is cooperative rather than adversarial — a deliberate contrast with S004, so that the module does not end on conflict.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Item 1 pretests the core skill and reliably produces the postcondition-for-invariant confusion, which is then available as a shared observed fact rather than as an instructor's warning. Item 2 retrieves S004's argument C in a new context. Item 3 is the induction motivation compressed to a sentence. Item 4 is calibration.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** The programme's first genuinely spaced retrieval, at nine days, and the module's most demanding discrimination item. The ninety seconds of unresolved disagreement is deliberate: an instructor resolving a disagreement immediately removes the retrieval attempt that makes the resolution stick.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four loops, one question. The cases are constructed so that the definition of an invariant is recoverable from the contrast, which is the condition under which a definition means something. Loop 4 is the load-bearing case and the design accepts that most groups will not resolve it — that is its function.

**The four executions put the same question to the agent, one loop each, after the groups have committed their own verdicts in writing.** The prior commitment is what converts the result from an anecdote about a tool into an observation about a pair: without it, the room would see the agent get loop 4 wrong and conclude that tools are unreliable, which is the wrong lesson and one they already believe. With it, the room sees that **its own errors and the tool's coincide on the one case where a check would have been worth having**, which is a claim about correlation and is the module's terminal finding. Loops 1 to 3 are answered correctly and are load-bearing for the same reason S002's controls are.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Nine points, with the naturals demoted to a special case at point 2 and the loop invariant given the central position at point 4. Point 5 stands alone. **Point 7 is the module's closing claim about its own instrument and is placed after the failure classes because it is a claim about review**; it ends in a construction rather than in a warning, which is a deliberate departure from the way the module's other platform points end, because a warning about correlated review has no action attached and the property check does. Point 8 is the day's default-the-platform-chose-for-you point and concerns retention of the agent's own reasoning artefacts, which is why the prompt and intervention records are student-written throughout the programme. Point 9's professional transposition names three real instances, all of which are retrieved later.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps on a known program with heavy fading. Step 1's commitment, step 3's separate termination obligation and step 5's break are the payload. Step 4 reuses S004's protocol without re-teaching it, which is itself a retrieval event.

**Steps 6 and 7 are the module's only build whose value is that it is stupider than the thing it checks.** The property check does not reason about the loop; it runs it and looks, and that is why it catches the near-invariant that defeated both the room and the agent forty-five minutes earlier. The construction is small, the agent usually produces it correctly, and **the design wants it to be small** — an impressive artefact would invite students to admire the tool at the exact moment the day is arguing that admiration is not the relevant response.

Step 7's second half is the harder one and is the reason the step exists in two parts. The check misses termination entirely, and a student who reports it as validating the loop has collapsed the distinction point 5 spent a full segment separating. **The module therefore ends with one of the two obligations instrumented and the other not**, which is an honest place to end and is the state most production systems are in.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** The instructor's disclosure is a substrate monitoring invariant that silently failed, chosen so that the failure class is exactly what the day taught, at real cost, in the system the students can see. The emphasis on *why the belief survived* is the phase's content and previews S068's detectability material. **The agent case follows it and asks the room about their own reception of the argument rather than about the argument**, because the reception is the correctable part: the group found the agent's loop 4 argument convincing because it agreed with them and was better written, and every student in the room recognises that preference in themselves.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Reciprocal diagnosis from behaviour. The three-way classification — establishment, preservation, termination — is the same diagnostic shape used at S079 for incident triage, and the correspondence is not mentioned here.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Calibration resolved with the confusion rate shown. The module-closure paragraph, kept for three years. The S004 commitment closed.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with fluent summation induction will apply the ritual and produce a phase 5 artefact with the right shape. The predicted failure is that they pass steps 1 to 4 without engaging the interaction between establishment and preservation, and then fail step 5 in a revealing way — their broken loop will break the property rather than one of the three obligations. Countermeasure: step 5 requires naming *which obligation* failed, which the ritual does not supply. This is the design's principal reason for step 5's existence and it targets exactly this student. Their phase 7 partner will also press them, since diagnosis is the partner's task.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** A student with no induction background faces the module's highest-interactivity content in week five. Countermeasures: the fading is complete; the subject program is known; phase 3 separates the definitional question from any proof; and phase 4 point 4 is indexed directly to objects the student has just spent forty-five minutes with. Residual risk: step 5 may be unreachable for these students within the time, and the design's fallback is that they break the loop by inspection and receive the obligation classification from their phase 7 partner, which is weaker but not nothing.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Loops 1 to 3 are separable quickly by many groups. Loop 4 is not, and the phase's difficulty is concentrated there deliberately. If a group does resolve loop 4, the fallback is to ask them to construct a fifth loop with a property that is preserved but never established, which is a harder construction and exposes whether the resolution was general.

A distinct risk, noted in §8, is that groups reach the correct answers on loops 1 to 4 **by elimination** — recognising that four cases with four different verdicts implies one of each — without engaging the underlying question. Countermeasure: groups must state *why*, in writing, and the written justification is what phase 4 indexes to. Whether this is sufficient is unknown.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is defeat rather than cynicism. A student who cannot state an invariant in phase 1, cannot resolve loop 4 in phase 3, and stalls in phase 5 has had three consecutive failures in a single day, and this is the highest-interactivity day of the module. Countermeasures: phase 1 is explicitly framed as untaught; phase 3's loop 4 is announced afterwards as the case designed to defeat the room, with the proportion shown; phase 5 is faded so that steps 1 to 3 are reachable by everyone; and phase 7 is cooperative. The instructor is briefed to watch for the student who stops writing in phase 5 and to move them to the completion version of step 2 without discussion.

Retrieved by S006 (the invariant you did not write down — the immediate and closest retrieval, deliberately at a two-day gap), S008 (recursion as an induction hypothesis, which is the structural form of the same argument), S012 (loop-based cost arguments), S022 (amortisation, where the invariant becomes a potential function), S031 (concurrency as a specification problem, where invariants must hold under interleaving) and S079 (drift and alerting, where a monitoring invariant is exactly phase 4 point 9).

S006 at a two-day gap is unusually short and is deliberate: M02 opens by immediately re-using M01's closing technique in a programming rather than mathematical setting, which is the transfer test at the shortest possible distance and the module handover's whole purpose.

S031 and S079, at gaps of roughly three and seven quarters, are the long-range targets. Phase 7's three-way diagnosis is planted specifically for S079.

The day's own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 6, plus the module-level cumulative set in week 8.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence.

One specification item, jointly specified in MS101 and CF101 — the only such item in Q1. The joint specification is a deliberate structural statement: the same artefact is assessed as mathematics and as programming, because the module's thesis is that they are not different activities. Items 1 to 5 — committed invariant, establishment and preservation proofs, variant and termination argument, reader-directed version with the rejection recorded, and the broken loop with the correct obligation identified — are the original specification, unchanged. Items 6 to 10 are new: the runnable check, the three runs with the caught-and-missed statement, the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.

**Item 9 is the first exercise in the programme of the intervention record's exception clause and the day was selected for it.** The [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) states that an empty intervention record fails unless the artefact demonstrates the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error. On most days that exception is hard to satisfy and the honest empty record is therefore unavailable, which creates an incentive to invent entries. Here it is easy to satisfy — the broken-loop run is the demonstration — and the artefact is small enough that having nothing to correct is the expected outcome rather than a suspicious one. **A rule whose exception is never reachable is a rule that produces fabrication**, and the module puts the first reachable instance at its last day so that students meet the exception before they meet a day where they would need it and could not have it.

Programme instrumentation: the phase 1 item 1 confusion rate is the module's clearest single diagnostic and is compared against the same probe at S031; the phase 8 paragraphs are retained as the three-year cohort thread; the phase 6 disclosure count closes the module's psychological-safety series; **and the phase 1 item 5 responses close the module's frontier map, which is collated at this phase 8 and carried to [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md).** Instrumentation rule 3 binds the map: it is a sample — one cohort, five hand-checkable problems, one model, one week — and the page says so on it. Instrumentation rule 9 binds its reader: next year's cohort, by way of the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md).

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: teaching induction on loops rather than on the naturals sacrifices generality for immediate applicability.**

At its strongest: induction is a principle about well-founded orderings, and the naturals are where that principle is clearest, least cluttered and most obviously general. Teaching it as a loop technique risks producing students who can verify loops and cannot recognise the same principle in a recursive data structure, a recurrence relation, a structural definition or a proof about a protocol. The programme will need all of those, and it will have taught a special case as though it were the thing.

The rebuttal is that the generality objection assumes the naturals-first route delivers the generality it promises, and the ordinary evidence is that it does not: students taught summation induction overwhelmingly fail to recognise structural induction when they meet it. The generality was in the instructor's presentation, not in the students' encoding. The design's counter-position is that generality is built by **variability of practice across surfaces**, not by starting at the most abstract surface, and the module accordingly presents the naturals at phase 4 point 2, structural induction at point 3 and loops at point 4 — three surfaces in one session, with S008's recursion day supplying a fourth at a two-week gap.

The objection's residual force is conceded: three surfaces in fifty minutes, with only one of them practised, is thin variability. The design compensates at S008 and S022 but does not fully answer the concern within this day.

**Objection 2: phase 3's four-case contrast can be solved by elimination, and if it can be, the definition is not recovered.**

At its strongest: presenting exactly four loops with exactly four distinct verdicts signals the answer structure. A group that notices this — and groups are good at noticing assessment structure — can assign one verdict to each case by process of elimination and arrive at four correct answers having engaged the underlying question not at all. Worse, the design would not know, because the artefact is identical either way. This is the same discrimination problem the day identifies in phase 5 step 5, applied to phase 3, and the day solves it in one place and not the other.

This objection the design does not fully answer. The written-justification requirement is a partial countermeasure and it is genuinely weak, because a group reasoning by elimination can construct a plausible post-hoc justification. The structurally sound fix would be to include five loops with two sharing a verdict, or to withhold the count, and neither is currently in the design. The reason for retaining the four-case version is that the clean one-of-each structure is what makes loop 4's distinctness legible, and blurring it may cost more than it gains. That trade-off is asserted, not established. Prediction 3 in §9 is the instrument that would settle whether elimination is actually occurring.

**The four executions weaken the objection slightly and the design records the mechanism rather than claiming a fix.** A group reasoning by elimination has produced four verdicts and no account, and when the agent then affirms loop 4 the group has nothing to compare its own reasoning against — they cannot say whether they and the tool failed for the same reason, because they do not have a reason. That absence is visible to the instructor in a way the written justification alone is not. It is a diagnostic and not a countermeasure, and prediction 3 remains the instrument.

**Objection 3: the module's terminal finding about correlated review is one observation of one model on one case, and the day teaches from it.**

At its strongest, and it is the strongest objection in this paper: phase 4 point 7 makes a general claim — that reasoning-shaped reviewers correlate with reasoning humans — and its entire evidential base is that a particular model affirmed a particular near-invariant on a particular afternoon. **No published measurement supports it**, and none is likely to. The programme forbids asserting a Foundry evaluator's agreement with human judgement on the ground that none is published; this is the same shape of claim, about the same class of system, resting on less. The day is also *lucky*: the finding exists because a case chosen for its difficulty for students happened to be difficult for the tool, and a design that reports a lucky finding as a principle has done what it teaches students not to do.

The rebuttal narrows the claim rather than defending it, and the narrowing is real. **What the day is entitled to assert is a conditional — where a reviewer's failures correlate with yours, an agreeing review is not evidence — and that conditional is not an empirical claim about any model.** It follows from what a check is for, it is true of a colleague who shares your training, and it would be true if every model in existence resolved loop 4 correctly. What the room *observed* is one instance of the antecedent being satisfied, it is marked perishable, and §3.2 states explicitly that the point survives its instance failing.

**The concession is that the day cannot establish how often the antecedent is satisfied**, which is the operationally important question and the one students will actually face. A conditional with an unknown-frequency antecedent is a warning, not a rule, and the day's response — build a check that is not a reasoner — is sound advice that does not depend on the frequency. That is the best the design can do and it is less than the point's prominence in phase 4 implies.

**Objection 4: a student who has spent five days directing an agent and building one small check has learned supervision, and the module has quietly stopped teaching construction.**

At its strongest, and this is where the module's version of the dependency risk becomes concrete: the property check at step 6 is the fifth consecutive artefact in M01 that a student obtained by asking. The simulator at S002, the instructions at S003, the draft proof at S004, the check here. Each was defensible individually and the aggregate is a student who, at the end of five days on the most checkable material the programme will ever offer, has written very little themselves. **The programme's own [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) states that a student who has only built with an agent may be unable to build without one and that the design cannot currently detect it.** This module is where the pattern is set.

**This objection is conceded and the fix is cheap.** The module already contains the material for it: [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) is the first of the programme's three unassisted occasions and falls five days after this one, and it already produces an assisted-versus-unassisted comparison on one element of the student's own artefact, marked by a non-assessor and returned to the student. What it does not have is a baseline. The fix is that **each student's M01 frontier page — collated at this day's phase 8 — travels with that return**, so the student reads their unassisted result against five specific statements they wrote themselves about what the agent did well and badly on checkable material. It costs S010 an attachment, it uses an artefact that already exists, and it converts an abstract worry into a personal datum at the earliest point the programme can produce one.

**Two things the fix does not do, and both are stated because the objection deserves them.** It does not make the M01 builds unaided; the pattern the objection names is unchanged. And S010's segment is scoped to the student's *current* artefact, so the property check is not the thing rebuilt — **whether an M01 artefact should be a candidate for one of the three unassisted occasions is a question for S045, and this paper does not answer it.** The commitment recorded here is the attachment and nothing more, and S010 must discharge it. Whether three unassisted occasions across three years is enough is unknown, and the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) already records that it does not know.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** In phase 1 item 1, a majority of the cohort will state the postcondition rather than an invariant, and the rate will not differ materially between students with and without prior induction instruction. *Instrument:* phase 1 responses coded as invariant / postcondition / neither, split by the admission background survey. **If prior-instruction students perform substantially better, §1's central premise about ritual encoding is wrong for this cohort.**

**Prediction 2.** In phase 5 step 5, students who produce correct proofs at steps 2 and 3 will nonetheless misidentify the failing obligation at a substantial rate. *Instrument:* step 5 obligation identification, conditioned on steps 2 and 3 correctness. This is the discriminator claim stated as an empirical prediction; if the rates are near-identical, step 5 is not discriminating and its cost is unjustified.

**Prediction 3.** Groups will produce weaker written justifications for whichever loop they resolve last, and justification quality will correlate with resolution order rather than with case difficulty. *Instrument:* phase 3 justifications coded for quality, against the order in which the group recorded them. **This is the elimination test from §8 objection 2: order-dependence without difficulty-dependence is the signature of elimination.**

**Prediction 4.** At S031, the phase 1 item 1 probe repeated in a concurrent setting will show a lower confusion rate than at S005, and the improvement will be larger for students who completed step 5 correctly. *Instrument:* the S031 repeated probe, conditioned on S005 step 5 outcomes.

**Prediction 5.** Students whose groups affirmed loop 4 will report, at phase 6, that the agent's argument made them *more* confident in their own verdict rather than merely agreeing with it. *Instrument:* a one-line written response collected at phase 6 before the agent case is discussed, coded for whether confirmation is described as adding confidence. **If students report no confidence change, the phase 6 case has nothing to examine and the day's closing claim about reception rather than output is unsupported for this cohort.** The result branches: a cohort reporting no change is given the S010 variant in which two agreeing reviews are supplied and one is wrong.

**Prediction 6.** The intervention record will be legitimately empty for a substantial minority of students at step 6, and the proportion of *illegitimately* empty records — empty with no working broken-loop run — will be lower here than the proportion of padded records at S002 and S003. *Instrument:* intervention-record entry counts and item 9 outcomes across S002, S003 and S005. **This is the design's test of its own claim that an unreachable exception produces fabrication**, and if padding rates are equal across the three days, the exception clause is not doing the work the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) assigns it.

*What the documentation does not report that a reader would need.* Three silences and the first is the one this day is built on. **Nothing is published anywhere about the correlation between an agent's reasoning failures and a human's on the same task.** There is no error taxonomy, no per-task-class accuracy, and no statement of the conditions under which the self-correction loop terminates on wrong work — which is the property phase 3's loop 4 exhibits. The day's claim is therefore a conditional derived from what a check is for, and the observation supporting its antecedent is this room's, on one date, and is marked as such. Second, **no adherence or repeatability figure is published** for any instruction or prompt, so the design has no basis for saying how many runs would establish that a check is sound; the day raises that question and hands it to [S029](wiki/seminars/S029.md) unanswered, which is honest and is also an admission. Third, **the planning documentation states that plans are deleted and does not state what is retained** — whether any record of the reasoning survives elsewhere, for how long, or under whose control. A student who wanted to audit how a verdict was reached would need that and cannot have it. The standing prohibitions on the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](wiki/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md) bind: **no agreement with human judgement is asserted for any judged output, because none is published**, and the day's phase 4 point 7 is worded so that it would remain true if such data appeared and contradicted the room's observation.
