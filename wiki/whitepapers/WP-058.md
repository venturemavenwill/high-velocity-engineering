## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

An index that does not compare your query against everything has to skip some comparisons, and skipping comparisons means sometimes missing the right answer. That trade has a dial on it. **You have been sitting somewhere on that dial since the first day you used a vector index, and you have never set it**, because it arrived as a configuration value rather than as a decision.

When you finally do look, you will be handed a recall figure. Suppose it says the index finds ninety-eight per cent of what exhaustive search finds. Everywhere else in your education, that would mean a two per cent loss spread thinly across everything, and you would shrug. **Here it does not mean that.** The missing documents are not a random sample. Approximate structures fail in a particular place, and the particular place is disproportionately the documents that were hard to find by other means — which is to say, the documents that were the reason you built this thing instead of using keyword search. **The whole of your improvement can sit inside a loss the summary statistic calls small**, and no amount of care in reading the number will reveal it, because the number does not contain it.

So the question to ask is never *how many did I lose*. It is *which ones*.

The second half of the day is about what you then do with the answer, and it is a professional point rather than a technical one. An operating point written into a configuration file commits nobody and is checked by nobody. The same operating point written as a sentence addressed to whoever calls your service — with what happens when it fails — is an engineering artefact. **The difference between those two is the entire professional content of this material**, and it costs a paragraph.

One warning about tone. It is tempting to conclude that since every parameter has a default and nobody can measure them all, the honest position is to accept the defaults and say so. That is nearly right and it is wrong. The day asks you to choose *one*, and to write down what accepting the others committed you to.

**What this paper concedes, and it is a real concession.** The day's most memorable finding — compression eating the advantage you built the retriever for — is produced with the vendor's own remedy switched off. Rescoring exists precisely to recover what compression discards, and any team shipping a compressed index would use it. **So the headline result is stated at a configuration the day's own standard would not let a student promise**, and the one extra run that would put a third point on the curve is not in the design.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching approximate search as an operating-point decision inherited intact from S019 rather than as a specialised subject, and defends the deflationary claim that its two index families are structures the cohort met in year one carrying vectors instead of keys. Its central argument is that approximation loss concentrates rather than spreads, that it concentrates on precisely the documents a dense retriever was built to find, and that an aggregate recall figure is therefore consistent with losing the entire advantage that justified the system. It defends making the compressed index the payload because it has the best aggregate recall and the worst concentrated loss. It defends requiring a checkable promise to a caller as the day's output, on the ground that an operating point stated as a configuration value commits nobody. **It defends teaching, as content, that the vendor publishes every parameter of this trade and no recall figure at any setting — which is both the honest position and the precise reason the measurement is the student's job.** The paper reports discharge of both obligations S057 placed on this day, and reports importing WP-054's named remedy under rule 5. It records **the second instance of the pattern WP-054 §8 opened**, concedes that the day's headline criterion is measured on a sample structurally unable to contain the phenomenon, and concedes that its payload configuration is one no competent engineer would ship.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The material has an unusual difficulty profile: the mechanism is easy, the arithmetic is easy, and the mistake it produces is expensive and nearly invisible.

Students can be told in five minutes what a neighbourhood graph or a partition index does, and they will believe it, because both are structures they built in year one. They can be told that approximation trades recall for latency, and they will believe that too. **What they will then do is read a recall figure of 98 per cent as a two per cent loss**, because that is what the number means in every other context they have met, and act accordingly.

The number does not mean that here. The lost two per cent is not a random sample of the corpus. Approximate structures fail on isolated points, on small clusters and on decisions made at short range in the geometry — and those are systematically the documents that a term-matching baseline could not find and that motivated building a dense retriever. **The approximation can consume the whole of the improvement, and the aggregate figure cannot show it.** The error is not a failure of rigour; it is the correct reading of a summary statistic that summarises the wrong thing.

The second problem is one of agency. Every student in the room has been running approximate search since [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md), because everything they have used has an index with a default. Nobody has set the parameter. [S019](/wiki/seminars/S019.md) established that where you sit on a saturation curve is a decision with a cost on each side, and the students accepted that argument about queueing; the design's claim is that they have not recognised the same object here, because it arrives as a configuration value rather than as a choice.

The third problem belongs to [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) and is why this day carries a promise. An operating point recorded in a configuration file commits nobody and is checked by nobody. The same operating point written as a sentence addressed to a caller, with what happens when it fails, is an engineering artefact — and the difference between the two is the whole of the professional content in this material.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Moderate, and the module's lightest day.** This is deliberate and is the module's only load valley: [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md) and [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md) are both heavy, [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md) is dispositionally heavy, and [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) is an entrustment occasion. A day whose mechanism is two familiar structures is placed here on purpose.

The interactivity is between steps 1, 2 and 7. The budget constrains the operating point; the operating point determines what the promise can say; and a promise written before the measurement is a guess. Students who write step 7 from step 2 rather than from steps 4 and 5 produce a promise that is not about their system, and the failure is legible in the artefact.

Management:

- **No student implements an index.** All four configurations are supplied by the service, parameterised and instrumented.
- **The corpus, retriever and query set are yesterday's**, unchanged.
- **Step 2 is worked with the room**, since the parameter semantics are the only genuinely new technical content.
- **Exhaustive search is cheap at this corpus size** and is one flag, so every student has a reference without a scheduling cost.
- **The reconfiguration at step 4 is produced by the agent and read by the student**, so the phase's load is in choosing and characterising rather than in authoring.
- **Phase 3 supplies all four configurations pre-built**, so the phase is measurement and characterisation rather than construction.

**Step 3's prediction is unsupported.**

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Predicting recall from structure alone, before any measurement,* item 2.
- *Being asked what the lost documents have in common before seeing any,* item 4.
- *Committing to a four-way ordering,* phase 3.
- *A two-part reveal in which the aggregate arrives first,* phase 3.
- *Predicting which documents you will lose rather than how many,* step 3.
- *Naming a shared property the instruction did not supply,* step 5.
- *Writing a promise a caller can check,* step 7.
- *Having a partner defeat the promise undetectably,* phase 7.
- *Predicting which quantity an agent will report that does not mean what it appears to,* item 5.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Index construction algorithms and their parameters' internals.* IS203's, and the day says so.
- *Distributed and sharded index topologies.* Named and excluded; they belong with [S063](/wiki/seminars/S063.md)'s memory material.
- *Filtered and hybrid search over metadata predicates.* Named and excluded, and flagged as the case [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md) needs.
- *Index maintenance under insertion and deletion.* Named in the scope statement and deferred to [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md), with the instrument named there.
- *Dimension truncation as a compression route.* Named at point 6 and excluded; it is a property of particular embedding families rather than of the index, and it would give phase 3 a second variable.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The day's recall–latency account treats the index as static and the query distribution as the measured one. That is **safe** for a build-and-measure decision on a fixed corpus, which is this day's task and [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md)'s, and it is safe as the basis for a promise with a stated horizon. It is **unsafe** in three ways, each named with its home: an index accumulating insertions and deletions drifts away from its measured recall with nothing watching ([S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md)); a shifting query distribution moves the operating point without the parameter changing ([S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md)); and every latency figure assumes the index occupies the memory it occupied when measured, which fails the moment it is co-resident with anything else ([S063](/wiki/seminars/S063.md)). A student who carries today's numbers into a system that grows, drifts or shares memory has taken a measurement past all three of its conditions, and the specific failure — a promise that was true when written and is false now, with no alarm — is the one this material produces in production.

**The platform layer carries a fourth boundary and it is the one this day is most likely to be misquoted on.** Points 8 and 9 name three graph parameters with their defaults and ranges, two compression schemes with their storage reductions, an oversampling default, and a coupling between rescoring and the graph. Those are **safe** for reconfiguring this index on this service at this version, and safe as the basis for the day's central habit: enumerate the decisions somebody else made before you promise anything. They are **unsafe** as a description of approximate search in general — the structural material in [research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md) and [research/02-technical-foundations/jegou-product-quantization.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/jegou-product-quantization.md) is what transfers, and one vendor's exposed subset of those papers' parameters is not the same object as the papers. **And they are unsafe in a direction the day must state out loud: because the documentation publishes no recall figure at any setting, a student cannot infer from a default's existence that the default is good for their corpus.** A default is evidence that somebody chose it once, for a distribution that is not necessarily yours, and this day's whole method is that the only evidence about your corpus is a measurement on your corpus.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **item 4 read back at phase 8**.

The item asks what the lost documents have in common, before anything is shown. Most of the room writes some version of *they were further away*, which is not wrong and is not an answer. Four hours later the same students have characterised their own losses, and phase 8 reads the morning's answers back against them. The distance between the two is the day's content and it is produced entirely by the students.

The second mechanism is **I4** at phase 3. It has the best aggregate recall of the three approximations, which the groups can see, and it is last by a distance on the class that matters, which they cannot see until the second reveal. There is no error to locate — I4 is a correct index, correctly measured, and the figure that recommends it is real.

Phase 6 supplies the affective content, and the detail that carries it is that the users stopped searching rather than complaining. The recall number was correct, was reported, and was read by the person who lowered the parameter. What was missing was one question — *which documents* — and the case is constructed so that no participant was careless.

The competence risk is at step 3. Predicting which documents you will lose is genuinely hard and most students will be wrong. The instructor states before phase 5 that a wrong prediction is the expected result, that step 3's value is entirely in the comparison at step 5, and that a student who predicts correctly has usually predicted something too general to be checked.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items: two answerable from structure, three not. **Item 3 carries [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)'s probe results into a third domain** and is the first of this day's two rule-6 discharges. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction** and is administered here per instrumentation rule 8.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals and a bridge whose second clause — *who has been choosing it* — is the phase's whole value.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four configurations, everything else fixed, **measured by the room on a live service**, aggregate then per-class, **on the query class selected from [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)'s margin data**, followed by disclosure of the construction per instrumentation rule 3. The disclosure explicitly includes that a corpus with different cluster structure would concentrate less, which is §8 objection 2's subject. **The rescoring question is asked before the mechanism is explained** and is the phase's only test of whether the room's model of compression is mechanical or verbal.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Points 4 and 5 are the retained pair. Point 2 is deliberately deflationary, because the framing in which vector indexes are a specialist subject is the framing under which students accept defaults. **Point 8's second half is the documentation silence and is taught as content; point 9 names the defaults.**

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps, **reconfiguring a running index rather than describing one.** Step 3's second clause is the day's hardest demand; **step 5's final clause is [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8's remedy imported under rule 5**; step 6 is the discriminator; step 7 is the professional output. The three Copilot Practice Standard elements attach here.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A correct number, an informed decision-maker, an isolated cluster, and users who left instead of complaining — then an agent answer in which every citable figure was correct and the only figure that mattered had no source.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Defeat the promise, then say how the caller would detect it. The undetectable defeat is the phase's most useful outcome.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 4 read back against the students' own characterisations; **item 3 against the S057 probe results, required respects reported separately**; **item 5 against what each agent produced**; step 3 against step 5; promises tallied by form and by survival.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has tuned an index will rank phase 1 correctly and will know the recall–latency curve. Predicted failure: they write step 7's promise from familiarity — a recall figure at a parameter setting — which satisfies the form and is not checkable by any caller. Countermeasure: phase 7's second demand is precisely that, delivered by a peer, and a promise phrased as an internal metric is defeated undetectably in about a minute.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is step 2, where the parameters have no intuitive meaning. Countermeasures: worked with the room; four supplied configurations immediately preceding, with their parameterisations disclosed; exhaustive search available as a reference at no scheduling cost; and the instructor's statement that choosing a bad operating point produces a usable artefact for this day, because steps 5 and 6 measure the consequence rather than the choice.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** The latency ordering is straightforward and the recall ordering is guessable. The difficulty is the second reveal, and specifically explaining why the configuration with the highest aggregate recall has the worst concentrated loss. Fallback if a group explains it quickly: ask **what the corpus would have to look like for I4's loss to be spread rather than concentrated**, which is a question about the data rather than the index and has no supplied answer — and which is, as §8 objection 2 notes, the question the day's own materials are constructed to avoid.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **defaults fatalism** — the parameter is one of dozens, every one has a default, nobody can measure them all, and a student concludes that the honest position is to accept the defaults and say so. This is a more sophisticated failure than carelessness and is harder to argue with, because it is nearly correct.

Countermeasures: the day requires one parameter to be chosen, not all of them, and says so; step 1's *if nobody set it, say so* makes an unowned budget a reportable finding rather than a personal failure; phase 6's case turns on a single unasked question rather than on a missing measurement programme; and the instructor states once that the difference between accepting a default and choosing it is a sentence in a document, which is the cheapest professional act available on this day.

The accepted instance of the fifth is a build that computes its own recall against the exhaustive path and refuses to promise a figure it has not measured — which is the [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md) phase 6 agent failure detected by the artefact rather than by the student.

**Rule-6 discharge, first obligation — [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)'s step 2 probe results.** S057 named this day's phase 1 as the response occasion. **The element exists: item 3 requires a prediction, for each of the respects the cohort could not predict at [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) and probed at S057, of what compressing the vectors does to it; phase 8 reports the result against the S057 probe data with the required respects separated from the students' own.** The obligation is discharged. This is the third consecutive day on which the same measured blind spots have determined content, which is the strongest evidence available at design time that rule 6's forward links can carry.

**Rule-6 discharge, second obligation — [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)'s step 5 margin by query class.** S057 named this day's phase 3 as the response occasion. **The element exists: phase 3's per-class reveal is run on the query class where this cohort's margin over the term-matching baseline was thinnest, which is where the concentration argument bites hardest and where the payload's reversal is largest.** The obligation is discharged. As at S057, it is the weaker of the two discharges — it changes what the phase measures rather than what a student must produce.

**Remedy imported under rule 5.** Step 5's final clause — at least one shared property not among the three phase 4 supplies — is [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8's named remedy for an instrument contaminated by the instruction preceding it, applied at its second structurally identical site. Under the amended rule 5 it is a **proposed** remedy and its use here is **contingent** on S054's reported outcome; a reported failure there retracts it here. §8 objection 1 explains why importing it was obligatory rather than optional.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** The step 3 document-level predictions respond at [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) phase 5, where the shipped system's actually-lost queries under real traffic are compared against them; the step 7 promises coded for checkability respond at [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) phase 3, where the shipped system must carry a user-facing promise the user can check. **WP-060 §7 must report whether both elements exist**, per the commitment WP-051 §8 established.

**Frontier instrumentation.** Item 5's sealed prediction accuracy, coded for whether the student anticipated an unsourced recall figure specifically, is carried to the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) per instrumentation rule 9. **It is the programme's first measurement of whether a cohort can predict fabrication at a known documentation silence rather than error in general**, and the two are different capabilities: the first requires knowing that the documentation is silent, which is point 8's content. Per rule 3 the sample is stated: one cohort, one service, one agent version, on a task chosen because the silence was known to be there.

Dispositions: **reliability**, at step 7 — a promise is the artefact form of doing what you said.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day's headline instrument is administered after its answer has been taught, for the second recorded time.**

At its strongest: step 3 asks the student to predict which documents their index will lose. That prediction is collected in phase 5 — **after phase 3 has displayed exactly which documents I4 lost, and after phase 4 point 5 has named three categories: isolated points, small clusters, and short-range decisions.** A student who predicts "small clusters" at step 3 and finds small clusters at step 5 has performed a recall exercise, and step 3 against step 5 is the day's most quoted measurement.

The design cannot claim not to have known. [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 conceded exactly this failure at S054 step 3, opened it as a pattern-level observation, and named the fix: require the artefact to name one item the instruction did not supply. **Instrumentation rule 5 makes a remedy the design already owns binding wherever the same failure recurs**, and the same failure recurs here in a structurally identical form. Failing to import it would have been a rule 5 violation of the kind [WP-046](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-046.md) §8 and [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 recorded, and under the compliance register's criterion a violation of a written rule is fixed rather than recorded.

**The remedy is therefore imported, at step 5, and annotated at the point of change.** What that fixes is the contamination of the *artefact*: a characterisation drawn entirely from phase 4's three categories now fails, so a student must produce at least one grouping from their own residuals.

**What it does not fix is conceded.** The remedy repairs the artefact and leaves the instrument's ordering intact — step 3's prediction is still made after the answer has been shown, and the step-3-against-step-5 comparison still cannot distinguish a student who reasoned from one who remembered. The clean fix is to move the document-level prediction to phase 1, where item 4 already asks the general form of the question. The design declined it for a reason that is real and not sufficient: step 3's prediction is supposed to be about *the student's own operating point*, which does not exist until step 2. The honest position is that **item 4 could have been asked twice — once at phase 1 in general form and once at step 3 in specific form — and the difference between the two answers would have measured what phase 4 supplied.** That is one extra item and it is not in the design.

**This is the second instance of the pattern [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 opened**, with its proposed rule already stated there: *an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.* It is recorded, not elevated.

**Objection 2: the day's second criterion is measured on a sample that cannot contain the phenomenon.**

At its strongest: point 4 insists that recall against exact search and accuracy against relevance are different criteria, and step 5 requires both. Accuracy against relevance requires relevance judgements, and the only ones the student has are [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md)'s — around sixty queries, judged once, with a measured disagreement rate. **The documents this day is about are rare by construction: isolated points and members of small clusters.** A sixty-query sample drawn from the query log will contain almost none of them, because the log is dominated by the head. So the day teaches that the loss concentrates on rare documents and then measures its effect on a sample that is structurally almost incapable of containing them.

The consequence is specific and bad. A student following the day's method will very often find no relevance-criterion effect at all, will report that the approximation cost nothing that matters, and will be wrong for a reason [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) point 6 names exactly: the design could not have detected the effect it was looking for. The day's own material contains the diagnosis and the day does not apply it to itself.

**This is conceded without qualification, and the fix is a real technique that the corpus size makes cheap.** Exhaustive search is affordable here, so the *difference* between the exact and the approximate result set is computable for every query in the log rather than for the sixty judged ones. **Judge the diff, not the sample:** run both, take the symmetric difference of the result sets across the whole query log, and apply the relevance criterion only to the documents that differ. It concentrates the judging effort on exactly the population the day is about, it is a smaller judging task than [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md)'s, and it turns step 5's null result from an artefact of sampling into a finding. It is not in the design, and its absence means the day's most important claim is the one its own procedure is least able to demonstrate.

**Objection 3: the payload configuration is one no competent engineer would ship, and the day does not say so loudly enough.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn.

At its strongest: I4 is compression with rescoring switched off. **The service provides rescoring precisely to offset what compression discards** — fetch a wider candidate set from the compressed representation, then re-order it using the uncompressed vectors — and it publishes an oversampling default for exactly that purpose. A team shipping a compressed index would use it. So the day's most memorable finding, the one that carries points 5 and 6 and supplies the phase 3 reversal, is produced by deliberately disabling the vendor's own remedy and then reporting the damage.

That is a fair construction for teaching a mechanism and a poor one for teaching a decision. **The engineering claim a student takes away should be "compression discards short-range precision and here is what recovers it and what that costs"**, and what the day currently delivers is "compression destroyed the thing you built the retriever for." The second is more dramatic, is true only of a configuration nobody sensible chooses, and is exactly the shape of overclaim the module spends [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md) attacking. The day does ask the rescoring question at phase 3, before the explanation — but it asks *which configurations can be rescued*, which is a mechanism question, and then moves on without measuring the rescue.

The rebuttal has some force. A payload has to defeat an expectation, and the expectation being defeated — that compression's loss is uniform — is not an artefact of disabling rescoring; it is a property of what quantization does to short distances, and rescoring narrows the consequence without changing the mechanism. Teaching the mechanism at its clearest before teaching its mitigation is the ordinary and correct order.

**What is conceded is that the day stops one measurement short, and the measurement is one prompt.** The fix is **I5: the same compressed index with rescoring enabled, measured on the same query class.** It costs one configuration and one run inside a phase that already runs four. It converts the day's finding from *compression eats your advantage* to *compression eats your advantage, this recovers most of it, and here is the latency you paid* — which is a recall–latency trade with three points on it instead of one, which is what point 3 says the day is about. It would also give step 6's still-worth-it judgement a third option rather than a binary. **It is not in the design, and until it is, the day's headline result is stated at a configuration the day's own point 7 would not let a student promise.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At item 4, fewer than a quarter of students will name any property of the lost documents beyond distance or rank. *Instrument:* item 4 responses coded for whether they name a structural property. **This is the baseline the whole day is measured against**, and if it is high the room already holds point 5 and phase 3's second reveal is redundant.

**Prediction 2.** Groups will rank I4's recall against exact search below I2's, and it will be above. *Instrument:* committed phase 3 orderings against measured aggregate recall. **The claim is that students associate compression with loss and graph traversal with completeness**, and the payload depends on that association being wrong here.

**Prediction 3.** More than half of first-attempt step 7 promises will be defeated undetectably at phase 7 — that is, the partner will construct a case the caller could not have detected. *Instrument:* phase 7 outcomes coded for whether the defeat was detectable, before and after revision. **This is the day's hardest element and the prediction is that [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md)'s checkability requirement did not transfer across three quarters.**

**Prediction 4.** Step 3 predictions naming a phase 4 point 5 category will match step 5's findings far more often than predictions naming a category not on the list. *Instrument:* step 3 and step 5 pairs coded for list membership — the same instrument [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §9 prediction 4 specifies, run at a second site. **If the gap is large at both sites, the pattern is at two instances with two independent measurements**, which is the condition under which the design would elevate it to a rule at the third.

**Prediction 5.** When asked to reconfigure the index for a latency budget, a majority of agents will state a recall expectation, and fewer than a fifth of students will have predicted that at item 5. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded for whether an unsourced figure was anticipated, against the agent outputs recorded in each student's prompt record. **The claim is that a cohort cannot predict fabrication at a silence it does not know exists**, and that point 8 is therefore load-bearing rather than decorative. **If most agents decline to state a figure, the day's phase 6 case is unrepresentative and should be replaced** — and that outcome would itself be the most interesting frontier datum the module produces, because it would mean the agent is better calibrated to this documentation's silences than the students are.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That approximate search is an operating point, that you have been sitting on one since the first day you used a vector index without setting it, and that its loss does not spread. It concentrates on isolated points and small clusters, so an excellent aggregate recall figure is consistent with losing the whole improvement.

**What the evidence licenses.** Graph-structured and partition-based approximate search, the recall–latency trade, compression as a trade of precision for memory, and the non-uniform distribution of approximation error are **class 1**, grounded in [research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md), [research/02-technical-foundations/jegou-product-quantization.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/jegou-product-quantization.md) and [research/02-technical-foundations/subramanya-diskann.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/subramanya-diskann.md). The graph parameters and compression schemes are **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026. **The silence is the day's content: no recall figure is published at any parameter setting** — correctly, since it is a property of your corpus, and that is why a cited recall figure fails here and a measured one does not. The checkable-promise requirement is **class 3**.

**What to do with it.**

- Ask which documents you lost, not how many. The count is the summary that hides the finding.
- Write the operating point as a promise a caller can check, with what happens when it fails. A configuration value commits nobody.
- Place your recall number in the five classes on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md): the parameter you set is a configured control, the figure you measured against exhaustive search is an observed result, and a figure you did not measure is an unsupported inference.

**The concession worth remembering.** The day's payload — compression eating the advantage you built the retriever for — is produced with the vendor's own remedy switched off, and rescoring is what a team shipping a compressed index would use. The configuration that would put a third point on the curve is not run.
