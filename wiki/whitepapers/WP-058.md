## In plain terms

**The regime this session is built on requires the assessment to be a living document, in several places, in different words, insistently. It then supplies no way to record that it lived.**

The signature block has one row shape and no mechanism for signing again. The revision history carries no version number and no change description. The submission is one-shot with no expiry. The completed assessment is filed in a document repository rather than in the system where the work happens.

**And the per-sprint re-screen — a governance question asked inside the team's working cadence, which is the one place a governance question can still change a build — has no artefact, no checkbox and nowhere at all to record that it happened.** It is simultaneously the best-designed control in the collection and the least observable.

The consequence is entirely predictable. **The assessment is written once to clear a commercial gate and thereafter describes a system that no longer exists.**

The session's response is deliberately unglamorous. Build the version field, the change description, the re-sign-off row and the expiry. Bind the trigger to a state change rather than to a person remembering — **which is a shape the same organisation gets exactly right somewhere else and never connects: its environment withdrawal is triggered by a project's state changing in an authoritative register, not by anyone deciding it is time.** Move the artefact to where the work lives. **Then price one review and say honestly whether it will be paid**, because a review nobody can afford does not exist.

One part of the template is genuinely reusable and the session says so. **A handful of fields force a claim to become falsifiable — a named measurement, a stated threshold, a verification method, an accountable owner.** Those four turn *the system will be monitored for bias* into something that can be true or false. The rest is form-filling.

**The platform anchor exists because a working instrument has been forced to answer what a form can leave blank.** The vendor's data-posture tooling for AI re-runs an assessment on a cadence, over a sampled rather than complete scope, with a stated latency, and it expires its own results. It is worth studying not because a learner will deploy it but because it publishes **its scan scope, its cadence and its latency** — and **an engineer who cannot state those three for their own controls does not know what those controls cover.** That sentence is the durable content of the session, and no form in the regime asks for any of the three.

There is a second, less comfortable reason the assessment goes stale, and the session states it rather than blaming discipline. **The criteria in the assessment could not have been written correctly at the time it was written.** The evaluation literature names the circularity — criteria are needed to judge outputs, and judging outputs is how the criteria are discovered — and reports that some criteria are dependent on the outputs actually observed and therefore cannot be specified in advance at all. **An impact assessment enumerates harms before the system has produced anything, and then treats the enumeration as complete.** Instrumenting revision is not a concession to human weakness. It is the only structure consistent with how the criteria are formed.

## Abstract

This paper defends treating a governance regime's living-document requirement as an instrumentation problem, defends requiring an assessment to state its own resolution, and defends assessing the price of a review alongside its design.

The argument runs in four steps. **First**, a requirement asserted repeatedly and recorded nowhere is not a requirement: the regime's own form has one signature shape, no version, no change description, no expiry, and a filing location the delivery team never opens, and its best control — a re-screen inside the team's working cadence — has no artefact at all. **Second**, staleness must be visible before anybody can act on it, which is the only leverage available to a learner with no authority. **Third**, a trigger bound to a state change in a register survives while one bound to a person's memory does not — a mechanism the same organisation implements well for environment withdrawal and never connects to its governance artefact. **Fourth**, an assessment inherits the discipline every real governance instrument has been forced into: a scan scope, a cadence and a latency, each of which bounds what the assessment can be said to cover, and none of which the regime's form requests.

The paper adds a structural argument the session's earlier form lacked. **Criteria drift makes a one-shot assessment wrong by construction rather than by neglect**, because some of the criteria an assessment needs are only articulable after outputs have been observed. That converts revision from a virtue into a requirement of the method.

The session installs a falsifiable rewrite using the four template fields, a version field, a change description, a re-sign-off row, an expiry, a state-change trigger watching the previous session's one-setting-away list, a stated resolution with an explicit *outside all three* entry, relocation into the delivery system of record, and a stated price for one review with an honest judgement about whether it will be paid.

The paper concedes that instrumentation addresses visibility rather than will; that relocating the artefact creates an audit-discoverability problem the session's answer does not solve; that requiring an assessment to state its own resolution invites a precision the artefact cannot support; that the platform anchor publishes no effectiveness data of any kind and one of its consulted pages is explicitly superseded; and that most assessments will still be written once.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A governance artefact is produced under a deadline, to unblock something, by somebody who will not be reading it again.

**Every structural feature of the usual template confirms that reading.** One signature. One submission. No expiry. No version. Filed somewhere the delivery team does not go. **The document's own design tells its author that it is finished**, and no amount of text elsewhere instructing them that it is living will overcome the form in front of them.

The second problem is that the requirement, as stated, has no actor. *Refresh at every milestone* names an occasion and no person; *re-screen each sprint* names a cadence and no artefact. **A control with no actor and no record is an aspiration**, and the aspiration is doing no work at all — which is easy to miss, because the text asserting it is emphatic and repeated.

The third problem is the one learners will not raise unprompted. **A review costs somebody's time**, and in a delivery under pressure the time comes from the same people who are behind. A perfectly instrumented review that costs more than the team will pay produces nothing but a visible omission.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session needs [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)'s determination that the regime applies, and it retrieves [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) directly — the four template fields are the same falsifiability discipline applied to a governance claim rather than to a requirement.

It retrieves [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md) because a decision record and an impact assessment share a purpose and a failure: both exist so that a later reader can tell whether something still holds, and both are usually written once.

**The prior it works against is that governance is a document problem.** It is a trigger problem, a location problem and a cost problem, and the document is the least interesting part.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Low intrinsic load. The session's difficulty is that its work looks like administration, and a cohort that has just spent [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) on something intellectually sharp will undervalue it. The platform step is placed early partly to counter that: extracting a real instrument's resolution is a technical act, and it reframes the administrative work that follows as engineering.

**3.2 The opening items are a Productive Failure generation phase, and item 1 is the instrument.**

Learners are asked when a real assessment was last true, before being told anything. **The document cannot answer, and they find that out themselves.** That is the failed generation effect — attempting an answer before instruction improves later learning even when the attempt is wrong — and the binding condition is that **the generation phase must not be scaffolded** ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). The same source's second condition is why exercise 3 is a comparison prompt rather than a rewrite prompt: **presenting a correct answer beside a learner's incorrect one achieves nothing unless they are instructed to compare them.**

**Limits.** School, university and laboratory evidence; **no adult-professional study in the reference list**; a trade book in which anecdote and evidence sit in adjacent paragraphs; and the author's own model contains an **Unproductive Failure** quadrant, low performance and low learning, which is what a generation phase produces if nothing assembles the attempts afterwards.

**3.3 Why a state-change trigger outlasts a remembered one.** A trigger bound to a person degrades with turnover, workload and attention; a trigger bound to a recorded state change fires whether or not anybody is thinking about it. **The same organisation demonstrates this well elsewhere** — its environment teardown begins when a project's status changes in an authoritative register, and reusing an environment after close is a policy violation rather than an oversight, explicitly because the asset is bound to a project identity ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). **Connecting that mechanism to the assessment is this programme's move, not the source's**, and it is an argument rather than a finding. **Good mechanisms do not propagate across a document set on their own**, which is itself worth teaching.

**3.4 Why an assessment must state its resolution, and why that idea comes from a product rather than from a regime.**

The vendor's data-posture tooling for AI runs a **data risk assessment** on a recurring schedule across a sampled portion of the estate — the busiest sites rather than all of them — with item-level scanning capped at a small number of sites, one storage service excluded entirely, and an item ceiling per location above which counts may be inaccurate ([research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md)). Results are delayed after configuration, custom results take longer still, **and custom results expire.**

**Those are not small print. They are the resolution of the instrument**, and a tool that samples part of an estate on a schedule with a delay gives a posture estimate rather than a real-time control. The design's transfer is straightforward: an impact assessment is also an instrument, also samples, also runs on a cadence, and also produces an answer that is out of date the moment the system changes — **and unlike the product, it is never asked to say so.**

Two further details are adopted because they are governance judgements rather than features. The documentation flags removing a sharing link as **"an action to be used sparingly because it could prevent people from accessing the item legitimately"** — **the safest action is the most disruptive, and somebody has to decide**, which is the whole of governance in a sentence. And without an explicit policy, some workloads **audit that an interaction happened but not what was said**; knowing an interaction occurred is not knowing its content.

The strongest idea there is not a feature at all. **Generative AI "amplifies the problem of oversharing data"** by surfacing content that is obsolete, over-permissioned or ungoverned. Oversharing is not new; the reason it caused little damage is that **nobody read the over-permissioned material.** A capable retrieval system reads it all, instantly, for anyone who asks. **Deploying capable retrieval converts every dormant permissions error into a live one** — which means the estate an assessment describes changes without the team, and the assessment is therefore stale **by construction rather than by neglect.**

**The documentation's limits are severe and stated.** The data-side page consulted is explicitly the **classic** version, carries its own notice that it has been replaced and that improvements will not be backported, and **the replacement was not read.** The workload-side material was **read through search excerpts rather than a full page fetch**, so its structure is reconstructed. **No detection rates, no false-positive rates, no measurement of how much oversharing exists in a typical estate and no evaluation of the controls' effectiveness are published anywhere — none is asserted here or in the session.** Agent-level capabilities on the workload side are mid-licence-migration. It is single-vendor: **concepts transfer, mechanisms and portal paths do not.**

**3.5 Why a one-shot assessment is wrong by construction, and not merely by neglect.**

This is the argument the session's earlier form lacked, and it changes the register of the whole design from exhortation to method.

The evaluation literature names the circularity: **criteria drift** — *users need criteria to grade outputs, but grading outputs helps users define criteria* ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md)). A person cannot fully specify what counts as good before seeing outputs, because **seeing outputs is how they discover which dimensions of quality actually vary and matter.** The sharper finding is that some criteria appear **dependent on the specific outputs observed** rather than independently definable, which the authors say **"raises serious questions for approaches that assume the independence of evaluation from observation of model outputs."**

**An impact assessment is precisely such an approach.** It asks a team to enumerate harms before the system has produced anything and then treats the enumeration as complete. **Under that reading, revision is not a virtue the regime is failing to instil. It is a requirement of the method, and the form is the thing at fault.**

The design implication the same source draws is the one the session implements: **evaluation tooling should be built to expect the rubric to change** — revision, re-grading, version history — rather than treating rubric revision as exceptional.

**Limits.** The evidence is **qualitative**: the paper establishes that criteria drift is real and consequential and **does not quantify its prevalence or magnitude.** The user study is small by construction. It addresses criteria alignment rather than statistical validity. And it leaves a cost unresolved which this session inherits: **criteria revised after seeing outputs compromise the held-out status of the outputs that prompted the revision.**

**3.6 Why an expiry is a strength, borrowed from a discipline that admits its judgements decay.**

The element learners resist most is the expiry, because it reads as an admission that the work was provisional. The entrustment framework this programme's assessment architecture rests on allows an activity description to carry an **expiry date**, explicitly because a learner who has not practised the activity for a long time may have an entrustment decision that **expires** ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). **That is a discipline conceding that its own judgements decay, which most academic assessment schemes never do.**

The same framework's distinction between **ad-hoc** and **summative** decisions transfers cleanly and sharply. An ad-hoc judgement is continuous, informal and contextual; a summative one has the nature of certification, concerns future responsibilities, and requires **multiple observers in agreement and multiple observations.** **An impact assessment signed once by one person is being treated as summative on ad-hoc evidence**, and naming that is more useful than exhorting people to review more often.

**Limits.** A clinical model — medicine has patients, legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint, and any other setting must construct the stakes artificially. It is guidance from the concept's originator and an advocate, and **it reports adoption rather than effectiveness.** Its empirical items are relayed from prior studies rather than generated in it.

**3.7 Why visibility precedes action.** A version field does not cause anybody to look, and an expiry does not cause anybody to act. **They make staleness legible to a reader who did not write the document**, which is the only mechanism available to a learner with no authority. §8 gives this its strongest objection.

**3.8 Why the four fields are the reusable part.** A named measurement, a stated threshold, a verification method and an accountable owner convert a disposition into a claim. **Without all four the claim degrades quietly**: a measurement with no threshold cannot fail, a threshold with no method cannot be checked, and any of them with no owner will not be.

**3.9 Why the price is assessed.** Every control in this stage is judged by whether it can operate without its author's goodwill. **A review's price determines that more than its design does**, and a learner who has never costed one will design the thorough version and watch it never run. The corpus supplies the general case: its wellness guidance is advisory inside a system of checklists, and **advisory guidance inside a system of checklists loses to the checklists.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The movements run situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**4.1 Opening item 1 is the instrument and it is answered by looking at a real document.** *When was this last true?* is almost never answerable from the artefact itself, and a learner who discovers that about their own organisation's assessment does not need the rest of the argument. **The release rule gates the sections that would supply the answer**, for §3.2's reason.

**4.2 The instrument's resolution is extracted before the learner's own, and the order is load-bearing.** A learner asked directly to state their assessment's scan scope, cadence and latency will produce three plausible sentences. A learner who has just written down a real product's scope, cadence, latency and exclusions — sampled sites, capped item-level scanning, an excluded storage service, an item ceiling, expiring results — knows what the answer is supposed to look like, **and therefore notices that they cannot give one.** The comparison does the teaching; the definition would not.

**4.3 The version notice is recorded on purpose.** The consulted page announces that it is the classic version and has been superseded. **A learner who records that has practised the perishability discipline on a live instance rather than in the abstract**, and it rhymes deliberately with [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md), where comparing two generations of a governance regime was the whole session.

**4.4 The workflow puts the falsifiable rewrite before the instrumentation**, because doing it once reveals how many of the learner's other claims are decoration, and that discovery motivates the plumbing that follows it.

**4.5 The trigger borrows a mechanism from a different part of the same practice** and says so. The design's position is that this is the most valuable single transfer available in the module, and that its source never made the connection is worth pointing out: **good mechanisms do not propagate across a document set on their own.** The trigger is additionally pointed at [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)'s one-setting-away list, which is the enumerated set of artefacts whose governance class can change with nobody deciding to change it — **the exact population a state-change trigger exists for.**

**4.6 The expiry is required to change something visible.** An expiry whose only effect is that a date has passed is a date. The artefact must read as **stale** rather than as current, because **silence and currency are indistinguishable to a reader who did not write the document**, and that reader is the one the instrumentation exists for.

**4.7 The pricing requires an honest answer including *no*.** A learner who concludes their review will not be paid has produced a correct finding, and the design says so rather than treating it as a failed exercise. **The price is the transferable output of this session** — an organisation that learns the cost of its own governance can decide about it, and one that does not will keep asserting the requirement.

**4.8 The agent is used to attack the trigger rather than to draft it.** The prompts ask it to describe changes that would invalidate the assessment without firing the trigger, and to argue that the expiry condition will never be evaluated. **Enumerating paths through a state model is a task the tooling suits**, and the design reserves the judgement about which paths matter for the learner — the same division [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) established.

**4.9 The closing exercise is one question asked of a stranger.** *When was this last true?* aimed at the learner's own work, which is opening item 1 turned around. **The artefact has to answer without its author present**, and the symmetry is the point.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The one-shot assessment.** *Countermeasure:* the expiry and the re-sign-off row, plus the standard's warning sign — it was completed in one sitting.

**The calendar trigger.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* the standard's diagnostic — your trigger has a date in it and no actor — and the worked state-change example in the material.

**The unfalsifiable claim.** *Countermeasure:* the four-field requirement, checked field by field rather than as a whole.

**The unstated resolution.** *Countermeasure:* the resolution block with its explicit *outside all three* entry, and the platform step that shows the learner what a stated resolution looks like before asking for one. **§9's prediction 5 predicts this countermeasure producing a plausible answer rather than a true one**, which is a different failure and a harder one.

**The silent expiry.** *Countermeasure:* the requirement that something visible change when the condition is met.

**The unpriced ritual.** *Countermeasure:* the pricing element and the explicit acceptance of *it will not be paid* as a finding.

**The wrong home.** *Countermeasure:* the relocation requirement. **This is the design's most contested countermeasure** and §8 concedes what it breaks.

**The unversioned document.** *Countermeasure:* the requirement that the version field be used rather than present — more than one entry by submission.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. Five constituents are assessed at [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md): that at least one claim carries all four fields; that a version field, change description, re-sign-off row and expiry exist and the version field has been used; that the trigger is a state change with an actor rather than a date or an intention; that the assessment states its own scan scope, cadence and latency together with what falls outside all three; and that the price of one review is stated in whose time, with a stated judgement about whether it will be paid.

**Whether the review will in fact happen is not assessed**, and cannot be within a programme. **Nor is the resolution assessed for accuracy** — only for presence and for a non-empty *outside all three* entry, since accuracy would require the programme to hold a view about tooling it treats as perishable.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the problem is will, not instrumentation, and adding fields to a document nobody wants to reopen makes reopening harder rather than likelier.**

At its strongest: teams do not skip reviews because there is no row to sign. They skip them because reviewing is unrewarded, because reopening surfaces problems the team must then own, and because nothing bad happens when they do not. **Every field this session adds is friction applied to the act it wants to encourage.** A version field, a change description and a re-sign-off row make a five-minute reconsideration into a twenty-minute administrative task, and the predictable result is fewer reviews rather than more.

**Substantially conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to this session.** The design's claim is narrower than the objection assumes: **instrumentation does not produce reviews, it produces visible staleness**, which is a precondition for anybody outside the team noticing, and outside noticing is the only leverage available to a learner with no authority. Two further answers are partial. The pricing element addresses will directly, by forcing an honest statement about whether the review will be paid — a learner who answers no has produced the finding the objection describes. And the state-change trigger removes the dependence on somebody deciding to look. **The declined fix is to replace the instrumentation with an incentive design.** It is declined because this programme has no authority over incentives, no evidence about which ones work, and **a curriculum inventing an incentive scheme would be asserting a mechanism it cannot support** — which is the fault [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) teaches learners to detect. **The residual is conceded: most assessments will still be written once, and §9 predicts it.**

**Objection 2: moving the assessment out of the document repository breaks the one job it reliably does — being findable at audit.**

At its strongest: governance artefacts are produced for readers outside the team, often years later, frequently under external obligation. Those readers work from document repositories, retention schedules and records systems. **An artefact held in a delivery tool is discoverable by the team and invisible to everybody else**, and a governance record that cannot be produced when it is demanded has failed at the only moment that counts.

**Conceded in part.** The session's requirement is that the artefact be *held* where the work is, and nothing forbids a copy, a link or an export for the records system. **The declined fix is to keep the artefact in the document repository and place a reminder in the delivery system.** It is declined because a reminder is a person-dependent trigger, which is precisely the failure the workflow's trigger step names, and because a copy in the working system that is not the authoritative one will diverge and then be distrusted. **The design's own answer — export on demand — is unsatisfying and the paper will not pretend otherwise: an export is a snapshot, and a snapshot reintroduces the one-shot problem one layer down.** This is an unresolved conflict between two legitimate readers, and the session picks the one who can still change the system.

**Objection 3: requiring an assessment to state its own scan scope, cadence and latency imports a precision from a product that a document cannot honestly supply, and will produce confident fiction.**

At its strongest: the platform instrument can state its resolution because it is a program with a schedule and a query. **An impact assessment has no scan, no schedule and no query — it has a person who thought about some things.** Asking that person to state a scope, a cadence and a latency will produce three sentences that sound like measurements and are guesses, and the artefact will then read as though it had been characterised. **A stated resolution that is wrong is worse than an absent one**, because a reader discounts an absent one and trusts a stated one.

**Conceded, and the design's mitigation is partial and stated as such.** The required field that carries the load is not the three properties but the fourth: **what falls outside all three.** A learner who cannot name anything outside their assessment's coverage has claimed complete coverage, which is the false statement the field exists to prevent, and it is much harder to write a plausible-sounding lie there than in the other three. **The declined fix is to drop the resolution block and keep only an exclusions list.** It is declined because an exclusions list with no stated scope is unanchored — excluded from what? — and because the three properties are what make the exclusions list writable in the first place. **The cost of declining is that the artefact will contain three sentences of variable honesty**, and §9's prediction 5 is written to detect exactly that rather than to confirm the design.

**Objection 4: the criteria-drift argument proves too much, and taken seriously it dissolves the assessment rather than instrumenting it.**

At its strongest: if criteria cannot be specified before outputs are observed, then a pre-deployment impact assessment is not merely incomplete but **structurally incapable of doing the job it exists for**, which is to identify harms *before* they occur. The session uses this to argue for revision, but the same argument licenses a much more damaging conclusion — that the pre-deployment artefact is theatre and the only real assessment is the one written afterwards, by which point the harm has happened. **A design that cites this literature and then carries on requiring a pre-deployment assessment has not absorbed its own citation.**

**Partly conceded, and the concession is real.** The source's claim is that *some* criteria are output-dependent, not that all are; harms arising from an intended use, a data source or an affected population are specifiable in advance and are most of what an assessment is for. What is output-dependent is the long tail — the failure modes nobody imagined and the quality dimensions that turn out to matter. **The honest reading is that a pre-deployment assessment is necessarily a partial instrument whose completeness cannot be assessed at the time it is written**, which is an argument for expiry, versioning and a stated resolution rather than against the artefact. **The declined fix is to require a second, post-deployment assessment as the authoritative one.** It is declined because it would concentrate the entire artefact's weight after the point at which it can prevent anything, and because the regime this module is built on already fails in that direction — its assessment is written to clear a gate and then abandoned, and moving the weight later would make abandonment the design. **The residual is conceded: this session's instrumentation makes the incompleteness visible and does nothing to reduce it.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most triggers will be calendar-based or person-dependent rather than bound to a state change. *Instrument:* the submitted trigger, coded *state change with actor* / *calendar* / *person* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most falsifiable rewrites will supply a measurement and an owner while omitting a threshold or a verification method. *Instrument:* a four-field presence check on each rewritten claim.

**Prediction 3.** Version fields will contain a single entry at submission. *Instrument:* the revision history's entry count at the point of submission.

**Prediction 4.** Learners will judge that their review will be paid more often than an independent reader given the same price judges it will. *Instrument:* the learner's stated judgement against a second reader's, paired per learner. **If this fails, learners are already realistic about review capacity and the pricing element is confirming rather than teaching.**

**Prediction 5.** A majority of resolution blocks will state a scope, a cadence and a latency, and a majority of *outside all three* entries will be empty or generic. *Instrument:* per-field completeness, with the *outside all three* entries coded *specific to this system* / *generic* / *absent* by a second reader. **This predicts the failure of §5's newest countermeasure and is the instrument attached to §8's third objection**, and a confirmation means the resolution block is producing plausible fiction rather than characterisation.

**Prediction 6.** When learners date their own harm-register entries, a substantial share will be dated after the point at which the assessment was formally due. *Instrument:* the dated register from exercise 4, with entries coded *specifiable in advance* / *output-dependent* by a second reader. **If instead almost all entries are pre-datable, §3.5's criteria-drift argument does not apply to this artefact class** and §8's fourth objection resolves in the opposite direction from the one this paper expects.

**Prediction 7.** Learners will resist the expiry element more than any other, and will set expiry conditions that cannot be evaluated without the author present. *Instrument:* the expiry conditions, coded *evaluable by a stranger* / *requires the author* / *absent*, alongside a short free-text objection collected at the point the element is set.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a living-document requirement with nowhere to record the review is not a requirement; that a trigger bound to a state change outlasts one bound to a memory; that every governance instrument has a scan scope, a cadence and a latency, and yours has them whether or not you state them; and that a review's price decides whether it happens.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about whether reviews change systems. **The regime asserts the requirement repeatedly and publishes no evidence that any assessment was ever revised or that any revision altered anything.** The platform instrument publishes **no detection rate, no false-positive rate and no coverage measurement**, so nothing here rests on a claim that it works. The criteria-drift finding is **qualitative and unquantified**. The expiry precedent is **clinical guidance from an advocate reporting adoption rather than effectiveness.** The instrumentation this session builds is an argument about visibility, not a mechanism with a warrant.

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper.** That is a rule of this repository rather than an accident of what was read.

**The concession worth remembering.** You are being asked to build the plumbing for a review that, on the evidence available, will probably not happen. Build it anyway, price it honestly, and record the price — **because the price is the finding, and it is the only part of this a later reader can act on.**
