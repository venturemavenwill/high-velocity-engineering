## In plain terms

**The regime this session is built on requires the assessment to be a living document, in several places, in different words, insistently. It then supplies no way to record that it lived.**

The signature block has one row shape and no mechanism for signing again. The revision history carries no version number and no change description. The submission is one-shot with no expiry. The completed assessment is filed in a document repository rather than in the system where the work happens.

**And the per-sprint re-screen — a governance question asked inside the team's working cadence, which is the one place a governance question can still change a build — has no artefact, no checkbox and nowhere at all to record that it happened.** It is simultaneously the best-designed control in the collection and the least observable.

The consequence is entirely predictable. **The assessment is written once to clear a commercial gate and thereafter describes a system that no longer exists.**

The session's response is deliberately unglamorous. Build the version field, the change description, the re-sign-off row and the expiry. Bind the trigger to a state change rather than to a person remembering — **which is a shape the same organisation gets exactly right somewhere else and never connects: its environment withdrawal is triggered by a project's state changing in an authoritative register, not by anyone deciding it is time.** Move the artefact to where the work lives. **Then price one review and say honestly whether it will be paid**, because a review nobody can afford does not exist.

One part of the template is genuinely reusable and the session says so. **A handful of fields force a claim to become falsifiable — a named measurement, a stated threshold, a verification method, an accountable owner.** Those four turn *the system will be monitored for bias* into something that can be true or false. The rest is form-filling.

## Abstract

This paper defends treating a governance regime's living-document requirement as an instrumentation problem, and defends assessing the price of a review alongside its design. The argument is that a requirement asserted repeatedly and recorded nowhere is not a requirement; that staleness must be visible before anyone can act on it; and that a trigger bound to a state change in a register survives while a trigger bound to a person's memory does not. The session installs a falsifiable rewrite using four template fields, a version field, a change description, a re-sign-off row, an expiry, a state-change trigger, relocation into the delivery system of record, and a stated price for one review with an honest judgement about whether it will be paid. The paper concedes that instrumentation addresses visibility rather than will, that most assessments will still be written once, and that relocating the artefact creates an audit-discoverability problem the session's answer does not solve.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A governance artefact is produced under a deadline, to unblock something, by somebody who will not be reading it again.

**Every structural feature of the usual template confirms that reading.** One signature. One submission. No expiry. No version. Filed somewhere the delivery team does not go. **The document's own design tells its author that it is finished**, and no amount of text elsewhere instructing them that it is living will overcome the form in front of them.

The second problem is that the requirement, as stated, has no actor. *Refresh at every milestone* names an occasion and no person; *re-screen each sprint* names a cadence and no artefact. **A control with no actor and no record is an aspiration**, and the aspiration is doing no work at all — which is easy to miss, because the text asserting it is emphatic and repeated.

The third problem is the one learners will not raise unprompted. **A review costs somebody's time**, and in a delivery under pressure the time comes from the same people who are behind. A perfectly instrumented review that costs more than the team will pay produces nothing but a visible omission.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session needs [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)'s determination that the regime applies, and it retrieves [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) directly — the four template fields are the same falsifiability discipline applied to a governance claim rather than to a requirement.

It retrieves [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md) because a decision record and an impact assessment share a purpose and a failure: both exist so that a later reader can tell whether something still holds, and both are usually written once.

**The prior it works against is that governance is a document problem.** It is a trigger problem, a location problem and a cost problem, and the document is the least interesting part.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Low intrinsic load. The session's difficulty is that its work looks like administration, and a cohort that has just spent [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) on something intellectually sharp will undervalue it.

**3.2 Why a state-change trigger outlasts a remembered one.** A trigger bound to a person degrades with turnover, workload and attention; a trigger bound to a recorded state change fires whether or not anybody is thinking about it. **The same organisation demonstrates this well elsewhere** — its environment teardown begins when a project's status changes in an authoritative register, and reusing an environment after close is a policy violation rather than an oversight, explicitly because the asset is bound to a project identity. **Connecting that mechanism to the assessment is this programme's move, not the source's**, and it is an argument rather than a finding.

**3.3 Why visibility precedes action.** A version field does not cause anybody to look, and an expiry does not cause anybody to act. **They make staleness legible to a reader who did not write the document**, which is the only mechanism available to a learner with no authority. §8 gives this its strongest objection.

**3.4 Why the four fields are the reusable part.** A named measurement, a stated threshold, a verification method and an accountable owner convert a disposition into a claim. **Without all four the claim degrades quietly**: a measurement with no threshold cannot fail, a threshold with no method cannot be checked, and any of them with no owner will not be.

**3.5 Why the price is assessed.** Every control in this stage is judged by whether it can operate without its author's goodwill. **A review's price determines that more than its design does**, and a learner who has never costed one will design the thorough version and watch it never run.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 1 is the instrument and it is answered by looking at a real document.** *When was this last true?* is almost never answerable from the artefact itself, and a learner who discovers that about their own organisation's assessment does not need the rest of the argument.

**The work movement puts the falsifiable rewrite first**, because doing it once reveals how many of the learner's other claims are decoration, and that discovery motivates the instrumentation work that follows it.

**The trigger movement borrows a mechanism from a different part of the same practice** and says so. The design's position is that this is the most valuable single transfer available in the module, and that its source never made the connection is worth pointing out to a learner: **good mechanisms do not propagate across a document set on their own.**

**The pricing movement requires an honest answer including no.** A learner who concludes their review will not be paid has produced a correct finding, and the design says so rather than treating it as a failed exercise.

**The review movement is one question.** *When was this last true?* asked of a stranger holding the artefact. It is the same question as opening item 1, now aimed at the learner's own work, and the symmetry is deliberate.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The one-shot assessment.** *Countermeasure:* the expiry and the re-sign-off row, plus the standard's warning sign — it was completed in one sitting.

**The calendar trigger.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* the standard's diagnostic — your trigger has a date in it and no actor — and the worked state-change example in the work movement.

**The unfalsifiable claim.** *Countermeasure:* the four-field requirement, checked field by field rather than as a whole.

**The unpriced ritual.** *Countermeasure:* the pricing element and the explicit acceptance of *it will not be paid* as a finding.

**The wrong home.** *Countermeasure:* the relocation requirement. **This is the design's most contested countermeasure** and §8 concedes what it breaks.

**The unversioned document.** *Countermeasure:* the requirement that the version field be used rather than present — more than one entry by submission.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

Four constituents are assessed at [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md): that at least one claim carries all four fields; that a version field, change description, re-sign-off row and expiry exist and the version field has been used; that the trigger is a state change with an actor rather than a date or an intention; and that the price of one review is stated in whose time, with a stated judgement about whether it will be paid.

**Whether the review will in fact happen is not assessed**, and cannot be within a programme.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the problem is will, not instrumentation, and adding fields to a document nobody wants to reopen makes reopening harder rather than likelier.**

At its strongest: teams do not skip reviews because there is no row to sign. They skip them because reviewing is unrewarded, because reopening surfaces problems the team must then own, and because nothing bad happens when they do not. **Every field this session adds is friction applied to the act it wants to encourage.** A version field, a change description and a re-sign-off row make a five-minute reconsideration into a twenty-minute administrative task, and the predictable result is fewer reviews rather than more.

**Substantially conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to this session.** The design's claim is narrower than the objection assumes: **instrumentation does not produce reviews, it produces visible staleness**, which is a precondition for anybody outside the team noticing, and outside noticing is the only leverage available to a learner with no authority. Two further answers are partial. The pricing element addresses will directly, by forcing an honest statement about whether the review will be paid — a learner who answers no has produced the finding the objection describes. And the state-change trigger removes the dependence on somebody deciding to look. **The declined fix is to replace the instrumentation with an incentive design.** It is declined because this programme has no authority over incentives, no evidence about which ones work, and **a curriculum inventing an incentive scheme would be asserting a mechanism it cannot support** — which is the fault [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) teaches learners to detect. **The residual is conceded: most assessments will still be written once, and §9 predicts it.**

**Objection 2: moving the assessment out of the document repository breaks the one job it reliably does — being findable at audit.**

At its strongest: governance artefacts are produced for readers outside the team, often years later, frequently under external obligation. Those readers work from document repositories, retention schedules and records systems. **An artefact held in a delivery tool is discoverable by the team and invisible to everybody else**, and a governance record that cannot be produced when it is demanded has failed at the only moment that counts.

**Conceded in part.** The session's requirement is that the artefact be *held* where the work is, and nothing forbids a copy, a link or an export for the records system. **The declined fix is to keep the artefact in the document repository and place a reminder in the delivery system.** It is declined because a reminder is a person-dependent trigger, which is precisely the failure the work movement's second step names, and because a copy in the working system that is not the authoritative one will diverge and then be distrusted. **The design's own answer — export on demand — is unsatisfying and the paper will not pretend otherwise: an export is a snapshot, and a snapshot reintroduces the one-shot problem one layer down.** This is an unresolved conflict between two legitimate readers, and the session picks the one who can still change the system.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most triggers will be calendar-based or person-dependent rather than bound to a state change. *Instrument:* the submitted trigger, coded *state change with actor* / *calendar* / *person* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most falsifiable rewrites will supply a measurement and an owner while omitting a threshold or a verification method. *Instrument:* a four-field presence check on each rewritten claim.

**Prediction 3.** Version fields will contain a single entry at submission. *Instrument:* the revision history's entry count at the point of submission.

**Prediction 4.** Learners will judge that their review will be paid more often than an independent reader given the same price judges it will. *Instrument:* the learner's stated judgement against a second reader's, paired per learner. **If this fails, learners are already realistic about review capacity and the pricing element is confirming rather than teaching.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a living-document requirement with nowhere to record the review is not a requirement; that a trigger bound to a state change outlasts one bound to a memory; and that a review's price decides whether it happens.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about whether reviews change systems. **The regime asserts the requirement repeatedly and publishes no evidence that any assessment was ever revised or that any revision altered anything.** The instrumentation this session builds is an argument about visibility, not a mechanism with a warrant.

**The concession worth remembering.** You are being asked to build the plumbing for a review that, on the evidence available, will probably not happen. Build it anyway, price it honestly, and record the price — **because the price is the finding, and it is the only part of this a later reader can act on.**
