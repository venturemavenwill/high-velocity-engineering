# WP-061 — Where the Time and Memory Actually Go

**Seminar day:** [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) · **Module:** [M13](/wiki/modules/M13.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md)
**Strands:** IS · SY · ME · **Complexity class:** 5
**Platform anchor:** The PTU sizing model for Foundry Models — request shape, output-to-input ratio, cache rate · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read) · Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends converting S053's architecture into a cost model by retrieval rather than by exposition: S016 supplies that movement is the expensive act, S053 supplies that the assembly is a stack of weights, and the day's central mechanical claim — that generating one token costs bytes moved and is nearly indifferent to available arithmetic — falls out of the two in about ninety seconds at phase 2. It defends a pretest whose first and third items approach the same fact from opposite directions, and defends siting the day's binding-resource instrument at phase 1 rather than in the learning task, so that it measures the entering model rather than the morning's teaching. It defends four request shapes with the long-input case as the expected-expensive one and the short-input, long-output case as the payload, because that shape is the substrate's commonest and its cost defeats the cohort's established habit of counting tokens — and it defends executing those four against a metered deployment while computing, from the same measurements, the capacity figure the platform's own published sizing method produces. It defends a phase 5 that ships a running cost model rather than a spreadsheet, and a rule that no commercial rate may be used without the date it was retrieved. The paper reports discharge of two prior obligations: S016's promised supersession and the attention-variant deferral from WP-052 and WP-053. It concedes that the day measures resources and calls the result money, that it is a class-4 day inside a class-5 module, and that **its most persuasive new material is a vendor's formula, which is retained more readily than it is doubted.**

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The mechanism is not hard and the day is not for the mechanism.

Producing one token of output requires reading the whole weight set to perform a small amount of arithmetic. Processing an input performs a great deal of arithmetic over data already resident. Those two sentences are the day's technical content, they follow from material the cohort has held for five quarters and five weeks respectively, and a competent student can be talked through them in ten minutes.

The problem is that students have a **unit of account** that is wrong, and it is wrong in a way that has been reinforced by every interface they have used. That unit is the token. Tokens are what the interfaces count, what the documentation prices, what the context limits are stated in, and what students have spent an entire quarter measuring. The unit is not merely imprecise; it is **actively misleading**, because two requests with identical token counts can differ in cost by more than an order of magnitude depending on which side of the request the tokens sit.

A unit of account is harder to displace than a fact. A student told that cost is not proportional to tokens will agree, and will then estimate the cost of a proposed feature by counting tokens, because that is the only estimator they have. Displacing it requires giving them a better one that is equally quick, which is what arithmetic intensity is for.

The second problem is **directional**. Students reason readily from a configuration to its cost. They do not reason from a latency promise to a cost commitment, because latency feels like engineering and cost feels like somebody else's department. This day establishes the forward direction so that [S062](/wiki/seminars/S062.md) can run it backwards.

The third problem is that this is the module's first class-5 day and the cohort has never made a commitment it could not check the same afternoon. Step 7 is where that begins, and §8 argues that the day does not fully earn the class it is labelled with.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**High, and concentrated at phase 5.** The mechanism carries little intrinsic load — two phases, three memory terms, one crossover — because every element is retrieved rather than introduced. The load arrives when a student must hold a service, a set of request classes, a measurement, a confidence, a limits statement and a committed check date simultaneously, and the interactivity between them is real: the classes determine what can be measured, the measurement determines the confidence, and the confidence determines what the limits statement can honestly claim.

Management:

- **All four phase 3 shapes are specified, and the deployment and access token are supplied**, so no group time is spent constructing the cases or obtaining access to a metered service.
- **The measurement harness at step 4 is supplied**, so no phase time is spent building instrumentation.
- **Step 5's per-class arithmetic is worked with the room**, which is the heaviest scaffolding in the day.
- **The service, the traffic and the model are the cohort's own and unchanged from Q6.**
- **The architecture is not re-derived**; it is retrieved in two sentences.

**Step 3 is unsupported**, and §8 objection 2 argues it has been contaminated by phase 4 in a way the day's pretest instrument deliberately avoided.

Expertise reversal begins to apply at phase 4 point 1 for any student who has profiled a serving system professionally. For them the guidance at step 5 is redundant and the fallback in §5 applies.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Predicting a cost ratio in units the cohort has never used,* item 1.
- *Two items approaching one fact from opposite directions,* items 1 and 3.
- *Ranking four shapes before any measurement is visible,* phase 3.
- *Naming what buying the other resource would achieve,* phase 3's closing task, which converts a guess into a test.
- *Committing a check date and a divergence that will be reported five weeks later,* step 7.
- *A dual defeat requiring both the figure and its limits statement to fail,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Hardware architecture.* SY301's. The day needs one property of the machine — that movement has a finite rate — and takes it as given.
- *Quantisation, distillation and compression.* Named at point 5 as moves against the same crossover and not developed; they are IS301's, and their catalogue is perishable.
- *Batching.* [S062](/wiki/seminars/S062.md)'s, deliberately, because introducing concurrency into a day about a single request's cost would make the binding-resource question unanswerable.
- *Retrieval and pre-processing cost.* Named as an omission at point 10 and excluded from the model, which §8 objection 1 argues is a larger concession than it appears.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The two-phase decomposition and the three-term memory account are **safe** for deciding which resource binds, for predicting the direction of a change, for choosing what to buy, and for explaining why an optimisation did or did not move a number. They are **unsafe** as an account of what a service costs. They omit retrieval and pre-processing in front of the model, network movement of requests and results, the cost of holding a model resident while idle, and every fixed term that does not vary with traffic. **The omission is largest exactly where students are most likely to apply the model unsupervised — a low-traffic service they are pricing for the first time, where the fixed terms dominate and the variable terms this day teaches are the small part of the bill.** This is stated at phase 4 point 10 as a place they will be wrong rather than as a list of exclusions, and it is the coding target of §9 prediction 3.

**Scope of the platform instance, in the same form, and it is narrower than the day's own claim.** The published sizing method — request shape, output-to-input ratio, cache rate, divided by a per-model divisor — is **safe** for estimating how much dedicated capacity a described workload needs, for reasoning about the direction in which a mix change moves that requirement, and for showing that output and input tokens are not the same commodity. It is **unsafe** in four conditions and each is a place a student will otherwise go.

- **It is a capacity model and not a pricing model.** It converts a traffic description into a quantity of machine. What that machine costs is a separate schedule, and a schedule can be non-proportional to the resource in ways this arithmetic cannot see. §8 objection 1 is this condition stated as an objection.
- **It is unsafe at any date other than the one on which its inputs were retrieved.** The ratio, the divisor, the minimum deployment size and every rate are published values that change without notice and without a version number. A figure derived from them is a claim about a moment. **The day's response is not a caveat but a specification requirement: a rate used without its retrieval date fails**, which is the strong form's demand for an artefact element under which a student applying the simplification outside its safe range would fail.
- **It is unsafe as a description of a shared deployment**, whose throughput the documentation states varies with other customers' demand. The sizing method describes capacity you hold, and most students hold none.
- **It is unsafe as evidence for the durable claim.** That a vendor's capacity arithmetic exhibits the day's structure is a coincidence of design, not a proof of mechanism, and §8 objection 3 is that condition stated as an objection.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **the bridge**.

Two facts the room already holds are put next to each other and the day's central claim falls out. It takes ninety seconds, the room produces it, and the instructor's role is to ask the question and stop talking. Nothing else in the day is as cheap or as durable, and the design's whole sequencing decision — retrieving S016 and S053 immediately before phase 3 rather than after — exists to make that minute possible.

The second mechanism is **R2**. The cohort has spent a quarter counting tokens, and R2 is the shape with the fewest of them among the expensive ones, the shape their users send most, and the most expensive per unit of output. It is not an exotic case. It is what the substrate does all day, and the room has been mispricing it since Q6.

The competence risk sits at step 7, and it is new in kind. Every prior commitment in the programme could be checked before the students went home. This one is checked in five weeks, in front of the cohort, against a real bill. The instructor states before phase 5 that **every model in the room will be wrong, that the question is whether it is wrong in the direction and by the amount the student stated, and that the check is not assessed** — which is written into the specification rather than said, per the commitment at S050.

Autonomy is supported by step 1 and step 2: the service is chosen, the classes are constructed rather than supplied, and the decision the model informs is named by the student. Relatedness is supported by phase 6, which opens with a staff optimisation that was competent and useless.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items. **Item 2 is a rule-6 instrument and is sited here on purpose**, before instruction, so that it measures the entering model. Items 1 and 3 are bidirectional. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction**, the module's first frontier-calibration datum, administered here and not later per instrumentation rule 8.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals and the bridge. The bridge is the day's highest-value minute and the reason the retrieval set is ordered as it is.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four request shapes, ranked and committed before measurement, **executed against a metered deployment**, with R2 as the payload. The three closing tasks are what make the phase generative rather than confirmatory: what would buying the other resource achieve, which shape beats the cheapest one per unit of output, and which of the sizing method's three inputs is a fact about the vendor rather than about inference. The third is §8 objection 3's fix and is made.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Point 2 discharges S016's supersession commitment. Point 5 discharges the WP-052 and WP-053 deferral. Point 6 is the retained content. **Point 7 is the perishable platform point and point 8 names four defaults the platform chose**, both required by the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md); point 7 is indexed to the arithmetic the groups performed rather than asserted, per instrumentation rule 7. Point 10 is the scope statement in strong form.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps, **shipping a running cost model** rather than producing a document. Step 5 enforces specification rule 2 at the site the rule was written for, extended so that the retrieval date of every commercial rate travels in the same field as the figure. Step 7 is the class-5 element and the one §8 objection 2 examines. The artefact additionally carries the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A correct, well-measured optimisation against the wrong constraint, then **an agent-produced capacity estimate whose arithmetic was right and whose premises were nobody's to assume.** The second is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and opens the module's series on its characteristic agent failure.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Dual defeat, reusing S029's structure, contingent under rule 5 as amended.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 1 as a ratio of ratios; item 2's error rate by shape; item 3 shown beside item 1; **item 5 opened, with the generic and specific hit rates reported separately.**

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has profiled a serving system will answer items 1 to 3 correctly and will find phase 3 confirmatory. Predicted failure: they treat step 6's limits statement as boilerplate because they already know what the model does. Countermeasure: phase 7 requires their partner to defeat the limits statement specifically, and a boilerplate statement is trivially defeated — the lesson arrives from a peer rather than from an assessor. Secondary countermeasure: their phase 3 fallback is point 10's territory, which experience does not supply.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is arithmetic intensity at point 2, which is a ratio of two quantities neither of which the student has measured before. Countermeasures: the measurement harness supplies both quantities directly; phase 3's four shapes make the ratio visible as four numbers before it is named; and step 5's arithmetic is worked with the room. A student who cannot compute the ratio can still complete the artefact by measuring, which is the honest fallback and is stated to them.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** R1 and R3 are unambiguous once measured, and a group with prior exposure may rank all four correctly. The difficulty is in the closing tasks, not the ranking. Fallback if a group ranks correctly and explains all four: **give them the substrate's traffic mix from two quarters ago and ask what the aggregate cost per request was then, and whether the change since is a change in cost or a change in mix.** That question has no supplied answer, it is the point 6 material one level up, and it is the direct precursor of S064's payload — where the same group will run exactly that arithmetic against the sizing method and watch a capacity requirement move with nothing touched.

**The agent produces a defensible answer and the student stops.** New to this module and specific to the practical turn. Asked to size a deployment, the agent will produce a competent figure, and a student who has been taught that the arithmetic is the content will accept it. Countermeasure: item 5 is sealed before the agent is used, so the student has already committed to a prediction that something will be wrong; phase 6's agent case is the same task with the failure exposed; and **the intervention record fails empty**, which makes acceptance a visible artefact rather than a silent one. Residual risk: a student may manufacture an intervention to satisfy the record, and the design has no way to detect that.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **anxiety about the deferred check**, which is new to the programme at this day. A student who has never made a commitment they could not verify may over-hedge step 7 into uselessness — a divergence band so wide that nothing could falsify it — or may avoid committing a service they care about.

Countermeasures: the check's decoupling from assessment is written into the specification and stated before phase 5; step 7 requires a *divergence*, which makes an unfalsifiably wide band a visible artefact rather than a safe one; phase 6 opens with staff work that was wrong; and the instructor states that the cohort's models will be reported in aggregate at S065 with individual results private, which is the same protocol S050 used and which this cohort has already been through once.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S062](/wiki/seminars/S062.md) (one day later, where the single-request cost becomes a concurrency decision), [S063](/wiki/seminars/S063.md) (two weeks later, where the third memory term is the whole subject), [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) (where the model becomes a commitment), [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) (four weeks later, where it is billed) and [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) (four quarters later, where an adversary moves the operating point deliberately).

**S065 at four weeks is the primary target and the link is a real one rather than a topical one:** the model built at step 5 is the object the bill is compared against, and the student's step 7 commitment is what makes the comparison meaningful rather than merely interesting.

S077's retrieval is the longest and the one most likely to fail, because there the request shape is chosen adversarially rather than by users, and this day does not name that case.

Retained content: the two-phase decomposition, arithmetic intensity as the binding-resource test, the three memory terms, cost-as-a-function-of-shape, and a cost model as an instrument. **Five items, which is this design's ceiling**, and point 4's crossover is deliberately not on the list — it is a thing to be measured, not a thing to be remembered.

Own schedule: next morning, day 2, week 2, week 4.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md).

One specification item in IS301 with ME301. Per rule 1, the checked elements are the service and its decision, the request classes and their construction, the pre-measurement predictions, the per-class figures, each figure's confidence, provenance and rate retrieval date in the same field, the limits statement, the committed check date and divergence, the phase 7 outcome, **the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

Four hard requirements: **a cost figure reported only in aggregate fails**; **a figure whose confidence and provenance are recorded elsewhere than in the same field fails**; **a commercial rate used without its retrieval date fails**; and **an intervention record with no entries fails** unless the artefact demonstrates that the model's own checks could have detected an intervention-worthy error. The third is new at this day and is the §3.2 platform scope statement's artefact element, per the widening recorded on the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md): a student who carries a rate outside the date on which it was safe fails on it.

**No provisioned-throughput capability is assessed.** The sizing arithmetic is performed and the capacity read APIs are queried; creating a dedicated deployment is **demonstrated rather than operated**, per the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](/wiki/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md)'s open question on lab feasibility, and the day states the boundary to students before phase 3 rather than leaving it to be discovered.

**Discharge of the S016 commitment.** [WP-016](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-016.md) §6 recorded that S016's two-property model would be superseded at this day and that the supersession would be announced as such. **Phase 4 point 2 is the announcement, it names S016 explicitly, and it states what the two properties were missing rather than replacing them silently.** The obligation is discharged.

**Discharge of the attention-variant deferral.** [WP-052](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) §3.2 and [WP-053](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §3.2 both deferred efficient attention variants and architectural alternatives to this day. **Phase 4 point 5 discharges the deferral as a class of moves against a measurable crossover, not as a catalogue**, and marks the catalogue perishable in the day's register. The discharge is deliberately thin on specifics and the reason is on the record: a list of current variants would be assessed material with a shelf life shorter than the cohort's, which the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) forbids.

**Declared deferral, with its instrument named.** S065 phase 5 reports the realised bill against every model built today, in three categories, and **the report is not assessed.** The decoupling is written into this day's specification rather than only into S065's, so that it is a commitment made before the artefact rather than a reassurance offered after it.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions: the item 2 binding-resource error rate responds at [S063](/wiki/seminars/S063.md) phase 5 step 1, a required artefact element in which the student states the binding resource for their admission decision and shows the arithmetic; and the step 4-against-step 5 divergence responds at [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) phase 5 step 3, where the model is carried forward and its measured divergence becomes a stated uncertainty band on the capacity claim rather than being discarded. **WP-063 §7 and WP-064 §7 must report whether those elements exist**, per the commitment [WP-051](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-051.md) §8 established.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day measures resources and calls the result money.**

At its strongest: everything the day teaches is a claim about resource consumption — bytes moved, arithmetic performed, memory occupied. The module is about money. Money is not a monotone function of resource consumption. It is set by a pricing schedule somebody wrote, which prices some resources and not others, which has minimum billable units, which is renegotiated, and which can perfectly well make the bandwidth-bound request the cheaper one despite its consuming more of the scarce resource. **Phase 3's central finding — that R2 costs more than R1 — is a resource claim presented as a cost claim, and the two come apart precisely where the schedule is not proportional to the resource.**

The consequence is not academic. A student who leaves believing cost is derivable from the assembly will produce a confident estimate for a deployment priced under a schedule they have not read, and will be wrong in a direction they cannot predict.

The partial rebuttal is real but narrow. Phase 5 step 4 measures against the substrate's actual bill, so the student's own per-class figures are money figures and not resource figures. But phase 3 is the phase that teaches, and phase 3's ranking is a resource ranking.

**The practical turn has made the objection sharper rather than softer, and the day should say so.** The platform's sizing method is now the phase's second adjudicator, and **the vendor's own documentation describes it as a capacity calculation** — it converts a traffic description into a quantity of machine, and says nothing about what the machine costs. So the day now teaches an arithmetic that is explicitly not about money, inside a module about money, and the correspondence between the two is a separate schedule on a separate page.

**Half the declined fix has since been taken and the other half has not.** Step 4 now requires the student to retrieve a rate and record its date, so the currency conversion is an explicit, sourced, checkable act in the learning task — which is more than the original one column would have achieved. **Phase 3 still ranks on capacity.** The stated reason for not adding the currency ranking to phase 3 was that the schedule is perishable and the designer did not want perishable material in a teaching phase, and **that reason is wrong on its own terms**: the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) permits perishable content as an instance and forbids it only as an assessed object, phase 3 is not assessed, and the day now carries a great deal of perishable material at point 7 anyway. The omission is recorded with the reason and with the judgement that the reason does not hold.

**Objection 2: this is a class-4 day inside a class-5 module.**

At its strongest: the [Module Map](/wiki/program/07-Module-Map.md) defines class 5 as *whether to proceed at all, under uncertainty and cost.* At no point in S061 may a student decline. Step 1 requires them to name a decision the model informs; it does not permit them to conclude that the model is not worth building. Every choice on the day — the service, the classes, the measurement, the limits — is a formulation choice, which is class 4's definition exactly.

The design's rebuttal is that the class-5 property here is the **deferred consequence** rather than the proceed decision: step 7 commits a check date and a divergence, and the student is answerable at S065 for a claim they cannot verify today. That is genuinely new and it is what [Q6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md) said class 5 required.

**The rebuttal does not fully answer the objection**, and this is conceded. Q6's statement and the Module Map's definition are two different things, the day satisfies the first and not the second, and a class definition that is satisfied by either half is not a definition. The honest statement is that **S061 is a class-4 day placed in a class-5 module because the module needs a cost model in hand before it can ask anyone whether to proceed**, and that the module's actual class advance happens at [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md).

**The fix is one clause and the reason for declining it is scheduling.** Step 1 could require the student to state whether the decision is worth the cost of the model, and to permit the answer *no, and here is what I would use instead* — which would make step 1 a proceed decision and cost nothing. It is not taken because a cohort in which several students correctly decline leaves the module without the artefacts S063, S064 and S065 all consume. **That is an operational reason and not a design one, and it is recorded as such.** A programme that sets a proceed decision it cannot afford to have answered in the negative has not set one.

**Objection 3: the practical turn has replaced a claim students might doubt with a formula they will apply, and an applied formula is retained more strongly than a doubted one.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn and it is the one the design finds hardest.

At its strongest: before the turn, phase 3 produced a ranking and phase 4 explained it, and the explanation was the programme's own — arguable, incomplete, and marked as such. After the turn, phase 3 additionally produces a number using a published method with a published divisor, and the number is *correct*. Nothing about it invites doubt. A student who has computed a capacity requirement with a vendor's arithmetic, checked it against the vendor's calculator, and found agreement has had the most satisfying twenty minutes of the day — and what they have acquired is **fluency in one vendor's procedure**, which is precisely the outcome the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](/wiki/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md) names as the largest open risk in the whole redesign.

The risk is not that the formula is wrong. It is that the formula is *satisfying*, and that satisfaction is the mechanism by which product familiarity is mistaken for understanding. The programme's hedge — that the durable extractions are what is assessed — is a hedge about assessment and not about what is learned, and this paper cannot claim the two coincide.

**The objection is conceded.** The rebuttal available — that the day marks every number perishable and assesses none of them — is true and does not reach the mechanism, because perishability is a statement about the number and the objection is about the procedure.

**The cheap fix is one question and it is made.** Phase 3's third closing task now requires each group to say, of the three sizing inputs, which are facts about inference and which is a fact about this vendor's price list. Two of the three — the request's shape and the fraction served from cache — are properties of a workload and would appear in any capacity model for any provider. The output-to-input ratio is a published number that could change in a release note. **A group that can make that separation has the mechanism; a group that cannot has the procedure**, and the question costs two minutes and produces a coded artefact rather than an impression.

What remains unfixed is that the design has no measurement of whether the separation survives the day. §9 prediction 5 is the nearest available proxy and it is not the same thing, and the honest statement is that **this objection's remedy is checkable at phase 3 and its risk is realised somewhere the design cannot see.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Item 1 will under-predict the measured cost ratio for a large majority, and item 3 will over-predict the benefit of additional arithmetic for request B. *Instrument:* items 1 and 3 against measurement, **reported signed and separately**. The signed split is the claim: a cohort wrong in one direction on both has a biased model, and a cohort wrong in opposite directions has none.

**Prediction 2.** Item 2's binding-resource error rate will be substantially higher for R2 than for R1. *Instrument:* item 2 responses coded by shape and reported per shape. **If the two rates are close, the entering error is not shape-specific**, phase 3's construction is targeting the wrong dimension, and the payload should be re-chosen.

**Prediction 3.** More than half of step 6 limits statements will omit at least one of the four costs named at point 10. *Instrument:* step 6 statements coded against point 10's four omissions. **If nearly all omit one, the scope statement is being read as a disclaimer rather than as an instruction**, which is the failure the strong form was adopted to prevent and would be evidence that the strong form is insufficient on its own.

**Prediction 4.** At S065's bill, the largest category-3 term — costs that occurred and were not modelled — will be a cost point 10 names, not a mis-estimated variable cost. *Instrument:* S065 phase 5 category-3 items coded as fixed-cost omissions against variable-cost errors. **The two findings call for different repairs**: a variable-cost error means the day's model-building is at fault, and a point 10 omission means the scope statement was delivered and not acted on, which is objection 1's territory.

**Prediction 5.** A large majority of item 5 sealed predictions will state that the agent will get a number wrong, and a small minority will state that it will assert a figure whose inputs were not its to assume. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded for arithmetic-failure against premise-failure prediction, against what each student's agent actually produced. **The split is the claim.** A cohort predicting arithmetic failures holds the agent as an unreliable calculator; a cohort predicting premise failures holds it as a competent calculator operating outside its knowledge, which is the model this module needs and the one S064's error clinic depends on. **If the premise rate is near zero here and near zero again at S064, the module's frontier series has measured a cohort that never learned the distinction**, and the four agent cases are illustrations rather than instruction.

**Prediction 6.** Fewer than half of groups will correctly separate the sizing method's three inputs into workload properties and vendor-published values at phase 3's third closing task, and the modal error will be to classify the cache rate as a vendor value. *Instrument:* the phase 3 third-task artefacts, coded per input. **This is §8 objection 3's only instrument.** The predicted modal error is diagnostic: the cache rate is a property of the workload's own repetition, and a student who attributes it to the vendor has stopped distinguishing what they control from what they are told.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That your unit of account is wrong. Tokens are what every interface counts, and two requests with identical token counts can differ enormously in cost depending on which side of the request those tokens sit — because producing one token of output requires reading the whole weight set, while processing an input performs arithmetic over data already resident.

**What the evidence licenses.** The two-phase structure of inference, the memory-bandwidth bound on generation, and the quadratic attention term against the linear weight-movement term are **class 1**, grounded in [research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md) and [research/02-technical-foundations/kwon-pagedattention-vllm.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/kwon-pagedattention-vllm.md). The sizing method is **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026 from a page carrying no version number, and three silences matter: no numeric input appears on it, no function joins capacity to cost, and **no accuracy is stated for the method itself.** The four request shapes are **class 3**.

**What to do with it.**

- Ask which resource binds before optimising anything. An optimisation against the resource that is not binding is competent and useless.
- Do not price a service from this model alone. It omits retrieval, network, idle residency and every fixed term, and those dominate where you are pricing for the first time.
- **Pin the revision, host and model** — see the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md) — and carry the retrieval date in the same field as any commercial rate.
- Separate the sizing inputs that describe your workload from the one that is a published number somebody can change.

**The concession worth remembering.** The practical turn put a correct and satisfying vendor procedure into the day's generative phase, and satisfaction is the mechanism by which product familiarity is mistaken for understanding. The hedge is that only the durable extractions are assessed — a claim about assessment rather than about what is learned.
