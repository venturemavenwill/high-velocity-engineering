# WP-022 — Amortisation, Randomisation and Expected Cost

**Seminar day:** [S022](wiki/seminars/S022.md) · **Module:** [M05](wiki/modules/M05.md) · **Quarter:** [Q3](wiki/quarters/Q3.md)
**Strands:** CF · MS · **Complexity class:** 2
**Platform anchor:** Azure AI Search vector filter modes · Foundry `document_retrieval` · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends completing the guarantee taxonomy begun at S013 and defends making the individual-versus-population distinction the day's single retained sentence. The first argument is that expected-cost and high-probability guarantees are the forms most commonly quoted in engineering practice and the forms most commonly misread, because an expectation is a property of a distribution and a caller receives one draw; and that this misreading is the same error that will govern every evaluation claim from S041 onward, so it is worth installing on tractable material where the distribution can be measured directly. The second argument is that a probability without a horizon is not an operational statement, and that requiring the horizon converts a number into something a recipient can act on. The paper additionally defends the actionability test at phase 7, and defends replacing the day's constructed contrasting cases with three executed filter configurations of one search index, on the ground that a vendor's own guarantee sentence — that a requested number of results is returned *if they exist in the index* — is a count guarantee wearing the clothes of a quality guarantee, and is the most transferable reading exercise available to the day. It concedes that its potential-function treatment is a proof technique the programme uses once and abandons, that the executed cases vary a guarantee's strength rather than its form and so instantiate none of the four types the day teaches, and that the day measures a failure rate the vendor does not publish and must forbid students from generalising it.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

S013 established that a structure can carry a worst-case claim and an amortised claim simultaneously, that both are true, and that which one is adequate depends on the caller. It left two things undone.

The first is the proof. S013's amortised argument was an aggregate observation about the doubling sequence — count the copies, divide by the operations — which is correct for that case and does not generalise. The potential-function method does generalise, and without it students hold amortised reasoning as a trick that works for table doubling. Point 2 discharges this.

The second is the rest of the taxonomy. Randomised algorithms offer expected costs, and many practical algorithms offer high-probability bounds. Both are quoted constantly in engineering and both are misread in the same way: a guarantee about a distribution is heard as a guarantee about the instance in front of you.

That misreading is the day's central target and it matters far beyond algorithms. An evaluation result at S041 is a claim about a distribution of inputs; a latency percentile at S062 is a claim about a distribution of requests; a model's accuracy at S050 is a claim about a population that no individual user belongs to. The error is identical in all four places, and S022 is where it is cheapest to expose, because the distribution can be measured directly over a thousand queries in ninety minutes.

The day's third problem is communicative and is the reason the module places it here rather than in a statistics course. A probability without a horizon is not actionable. "This fails one time in ten thousand" tells a caller nothing until they know whether that means once a decade or twice a day. Step 7's horizon requirement and phase 7's actionability test are the interventions, and they are the direct precursors of S050's entitlement statements and S088's residual-risk statements.

**The day's fourth problem is one the design did not previously state, and the platform anchor made it visible.** Every guarantee the students had met until now was one the programme wrote. Real guarantees arrive in vendor sentences, and vendor sentences are the register in which the individual-versus-population error is most often *introduced* rather than committed. The module's index offers three ways to combine a filter with a vector query, and exactly one of them carries a written promise: apply the filter during traversal and **the requested number of results is returned if they exist in the index.**

That sentence is true, it is precise, and it is a count guarantee. It says nothing whatever about *which* documents come back. A reader who is looking for reassurance finds it; a reader who has been taught to ask what a guarantee quantifies over finds that the promise and the concern are about different objects. Nothing in the paper version of this day put students in front of a sentence like that, and reading one closely is a skill the programme has otherwise only asserted.

The platform also supplies the day's adjudicator, which the paper version had to hand out. An exhaustive scan of the same field returns the correct set, so *which* documents the weaker modes miss is measurable in the room rather than supplied as a distribution.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**High at point 2 and step 2, moderate elsewhere.** The potential-function argument requires holding a state, a potential, an operation and an amortised cost simultaneously, with the potential chosen so that the arithmetic works — and choosing the potential is the hard part, which is why step 2 supplies a template.

The taxonomy at point 1 is low-interactivity: four forms, each definable independently. The interactivity is in matching them to callers, which phase 3 does with supplied callers rather than derived ones.

Management:

- **The potential function is worked on a known example** — S013's table doubling — so no new problem is introduced with the new technique.
- **Step 2's template supplies the potential's form** for the two most likely cases.
- **Phase 3 supplies both the configurations and the callers,** and the configurations differ by one parameter value.
- **The index is one day old** and its exhaustive configuration is already familiar as an adjudicator, so the platform costs the day almost nothing in new comprehension.
- **One thousand queries, supplied harness.** Students measure; they do not build the harness.
- **The module's single problem domain and single index,** for the second consecutive day.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *The individual-versus-population distinction,* items 1 to 4, produced as an error before being named.
- *The unserved caller,* phase 3's closing task, which previews S024.
- *Stating the strongest guarantee you can prove rather than the one you want,* step 1.
- *The two-directional gap,* step 4 — where the proof is loose and where the observation exceeds it.
- *Reading a vendor's guarantee sentence for what it quantifies over,* point 8.
- *The actionability test,* phase 7.
- *The sealed Copilot prediction at item 5,* which asks a student to name a guarantee before they know one exists.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Deriving concentration bounds.* Stated at point 5, derived in MS103.
- *Choosing potential functions unaided.* Template supplied; this is a genuine research skill and is not the day's target.
- *Adversarial input generation against randomised algorithms.* Named at point 3, deferred to S077.
- *Tuning the approximation's parameters.* The day varies the filter's placement and holds the algorithm's parameters fixed, because varying both would confound the contrast.
- *Withholding the taxonomy.* Point 1 states it.
- *Assessment stakes on the same day.*

**The platform instance, scoped in the strong form.** The day teaches a filter's placement as an instance of the guarantee taxonomy, and the simplification must carry its safe and unsafe conditions, because it is precisely the kind of thing a student will apply to the next service they meet.

*Safe.* Reasoning about what a stated guarantee does and does not quantify over; about why the strongest guarantee here is also the slowest; about why a default can encode a value judgement; and about why the caller who needs to know *which* results were lost is served by nothing on offer. In all four the correspondence is sufficient and correct, and the last is the day's payload.

*Unsafe.* Reasoning about **how often** the weaker modes lose results. The day measures that, on one corpus, at one index size, with one filter-selectivity sweep, on one service version — and **no false-negative rate is published for any mode, anywhere, so the day's own numbers are the only ones in the room and they are a sample of one setting.** A student who converts today's observed overlap into a general failure rate has committed the error S028 exists to prevent, four days before S028 names it. Also unsafe: carrying the default's *value* forward. The defaults differ by index creation date on this very service, so a student who has learned "the safe mode is the default" has learned something that is already false for older indexes and may be false for all of them next year.

The boundary is stated to students at point 10 rather than left to this paper, per the widening recorded on the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), and **step 7's second clause — what your thousand queries do and do not license you to say — is the artefact element under which a student applying the simplification outside its safe range would fail.**

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **a promise that turns out to have been made to someone else**.

Item 1 asks what a single run of an expected-linear-time algorithm promises you. Students answer "linear time." Item 2 asks what ten thousand callers have been promised, and the answer is the same words meaning something different. Item 4 asks whether the answers differ, and most students say no before working through items 1 to 3 and yes afterwards.

That reversal, produced inside twenty-five minutes on a question that sounds like a technicality, is the day's hook. Its power comes from the fact that students recognise the sentence — "expected linear time" — as one they have used, and discover they did not know what it committed them to.

**Point 8 supplies the same reversal on a sentence somebody else wrote,** which is a materially different experience. A student who has just discovered that their own phrase was imprecise can attribute it to inexperience. A student who discovers that a carefully written vendor guarantee, in current documentation, promises a count where they read a quality, cannot — and the correct inference, that guarantee sentences must be read for what they quantify over regardless of who wrote them, is the one the day wants.

Phase 3's unserved caller supplies the day's second engagement and is deliberately uncomfortable: four configurations, four callers, and a fifth caller for whom none works. The natural response is to look harder for a configuration, and the correct response is to say that the guarantee does not exist and that nobody has measured the thing they need. That discomfort is the module's subject and S024 resolves it.

Phase 7's actionability test is brief and has an unusually clean win condition. A partner who says "I would do nothing differently" has falsified the sentence, and students find this immediately legible.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Four items constructing the individual-versus-population distinction from the students' own inconsistent answers. Item 3 forces the mapping onto real system types, which is what makes the distinction operational rather than semantic. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction** and is deliberately unanswerable at the time it is asked: the student is asked to name a guarantee the agent will surrender, and has not yet learned that the configuration carries one. Its function is to make phase 8's opening a demonstration that something arrived, and it is administered here per instrumentation rule 8.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** A bridge asking how a randomised guarantee differs from an amortised one. Both are claims about aggregates; the difference is what the aggregate is over — a sequence in one case, the algorithm's own coin flips in the other — and very few students state this unaided.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four configurations, four callers, five callers' worth of need, executed. The exhaustive configuration is the adjudicator and is available on the same field, which is what converts *which documents were lost* from a supplied number into a measured one. **The preview mode is demonstrated and not operated**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), and is excluded from the specification; it is present because the service has a fourth position and omitting it would teach a tidier world than the one that exists. The fifth caller is the payload and is sharper than the constructed version it replaces: the need is not a guarantee that has not been built but **a quantity nobody has published**, which is the exact shape of the concession S024 requires a day later.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Point 1 now carries its own scope statement — the executed cases vary a guarantee's strength and not its form, and two of the four forms have no instance on the service — which is the §8 objection-3 fix. Point 2 discharges S013's deferral and is the day's technical core. Point 3's framing of randomisation as adversary-removal rather than as average-case speed is the correct one and is rarely taught. Point 4 resolves phase 1. Point 5's operational treatment of residual probability is what step 7 formalises. **Point 8 is the day's principal gain from the platform anchor** and is the point the design would keep if it had to keep one. Point 9 names three defaults; point 10 carries the strong-form scope statement.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps at class 2, **shipping a filtered retrieval path** rather than characterising a supplied algorithm. Step 1's honesty demand is the specification's first weighted element. **Step 2's added clause — state whose claim the guarantee is and what it is conditional on — is new and closes the day's most likely dishonesty**, which is quoting a vendor sentence as though the student had established it; the paper version of this day could not produce that failure and this one produces it reliably. Step 4's two-directional gap is unusual — students expect to report only where the proof was loose, and reporting where observation *exceeded* the bound is either a measurement error or a proof error, both of which are worth finding. Steps 6 and 7 are the communication pair, and step 7's second clause is the artefact element enforcing §3.2's scope boundary.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A guarantee quoted to the wrong caller, then an agent failure. The attention to whether anyone knew the difference at the time is the instructor phase's content: this is usually not a case of someone being careless but of a distinction nobody in the conversation held. **The agent case is chosen because the agent's reasoning is correct throughout** — it cites a real documented performance difference accurately and reaches a conclusion that does not hold in the regime the system runs in. The failure lives in an unstated condition, which is the day's subject, and the reason review missed it is that the disqualifying fact was a property of the test data rather than of the diff.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** The actionability test. Thirty minutes is generous for a simple test and the generosity is deliberate — most first sentences fail, and the phase's value is in the revision.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** The retained sentence is stated: *the population receives the expectation; the caller receives a draw.* One sentence, because the retrieval schedule must carry it for two years to S049. Then item 5's sealed predictions, scored on both halves.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with probability background will handle items 1 to 4 immediately and may find point 4 obvious. Predicted failure: they produce a technically correct step 6 sentence that is unusable, because probabilistic fluency does not confer communicative discipline — the sentence will contain the right quantities and no horizon. Countermeasure: step 7's horizon requirement and phase 7's actionability test both target exactly this, and the specification's failing clause is about the horizon rather than the mathematics.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The potential function is the module's second consecutive new proof technique. Countermeasures: it is worked on a known example, the template supplies the form, and step 2 permits a direct worst-case proof if the amortised argument cannot be completed. Residual risk: a student who proves only a worst-case bound at step 1 has satisfied the honesty demand and has not practised the technique, which is an acceptable outcome the design accepts.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** The configuration-to-caller matching is usually completed. The fifth caller is where the phase bites. Fallback if a group resolves it by proposing a guarantee that does not exist: ask them to say what would have to be measured, by whom, before the guarantee could be offered — which is the S021 hypothesis move applied to guarantees, is hard, and lands directly on S029.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **the distinction dismissed as pedantry**. A student may accept that an expectation is not an instance guarantee, agree it is technically important, and continue to speak and reason as before, because the distinction has no felt consequence in a classroom. Countermeasures: phase 3's measured overlaps make the loss concrete rather than theoretical; phase 6's incident attaches a cost; **point 8 attaches the distinction to a sentence the student will meet again in a week, written by somebody with every incentive to be precise**; and phase 7's actionability test makes the sentence's failure immediate and personal. Whether these are sufficient against a distinction that is genuinely subtle is the day's open question, and Prediction 4 in §9 is the deferred test.

**Vendor sentence adopted as proof.** New with the platform anchor and predicted to be the most common specification failure. A student who has read that a mode guarantees a result count will write that guarantee into step 1 as though they had established it. Countermeasure: step 2's attribution clause and the specification's second failing clause, both stated in advance. The design notes that this failure is not a student defect — it is what engineers do with vendor documentation — and that catching it here is cheaper than catching it at S065.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by S024 (the duty to say so, one day later, where the non-existent guarantee becomes a communication and where the same index's immutable schema supplies a second impossibility), S025 (stated guarantees, where the exhaustive adjudicator becomes the ground truth of a promise), S028 (sampling and intervals, where the individual-versus-population distinction becomes the sampling distinction and where this day's forbidden generalisation is named), S041 (generalisation is the only thing that matters), S049 (clustering, power and the interval you owe the reader) and S088 (residual risk, stated plainly).

S028 at six days is the closest and is now structurally important: §3.2 forbids students from generalising this day's measured overlap into a failure rate, and S028 is where the reason is supplied. **The prohibition is issued before its justification, deliberately, and S028 carries the discharge** — which is the deferral rule satisfied by naming the day and the instrument rather than by hoping.

S041 and S049, at roughly two and three quarters, are the principal targets. A model's reported accuracy is an expectation over a population, no individual user is that population, and S041's entire content is that distinction. The design's claim is that a student who met it on a filtered retrieval meets it again as a recognition.

Retained content: the four-form taxonomy, the individual-versus-population sentence, the count-versus-quality distinction, and the horizon requirement. Four items, no mode names. The potential function is **not** retained — it is used once, at S022, and the design says so.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 6.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement.

One specification item, jointly in CF103 and MS103, **plus the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement**. **A probability stated without a horizon fails.** **A vendor's guarantee presented as the student's proof fails.**

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a result-count assertion against the exhaustive adjudicator being the accepted form, per the escape [S052](wiki/seminars/S052.md) established and [WP-048](wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 corrected.

Programme instrumentation: the phase 1 item 4 before-and-after rate is the day's learning measure; step 4's two-directional gaps are the cohort's proof-tightness baseline; the phase 7 first-sentence failure rate is the communication measure, carried to S050 and S083. **Step 7's sampling statements are the cross-course join required for the MS103 dependency**, and a cohort in which most students cannot name the MS103 material they applied is the signal that the deferral did not land — in which case S028's phase 2 carries the remediation, per instrumentation rule 6. **Item 5's sealed prediction accuracy is a frontier-calibration datum read by the following year's cohort** through the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), per instrumentation rule 9.

The day's measured overlaps are a sample and the day says so, per instrumentation rule 3: one corpus, one index size, one embedding, one filter-selectivity sweep, on queries drawn from a substrate log that is itself not a random sample of anything.

Dispositions: **integrity**, at step 1, where a student may claim a guarantee they cannot prove and no one would immediately know.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day's contrasting cases are chosen for tidiness rather than sampled from practice.**

At its strongest, in the form this objection took before the platform anchor: real systems mostly offer worst-case bounds on simple operations and measured distributions on everything else. Amortised and high-probability guarantees appear in the literature far more often than in the code an engineer will encounter, and randomised algorithms with proved expected bounds are rarer still outside specific domains. So the taxonomy was being taught at a level of completeness the students' working life would not require, and phase 3's four algorithms neatly instantiating four forms was an artefact of the teaching rather than a sample of practice.

**The platform anchor answers half of this and the answer is worth stating precisely.** Phase 3 is no longer constructed. Four filter configurations of one vector query, shipped by one vendor under one parameter, with their guarantees written down by the party who has to honour them, is not a set the designer assembled — and the earlier concession that the construction was artificial and unacknowledged no longer applies to this phase. It has been replaced by a different and sharper problem, which is objection 3.

The rebuttal to the remaining half is unchanged: the taxonomy's value is diagnostic rather than productive. Students will rarely *offer* a high-probability guarantee and will frequently *receive* one, in the documentation of a library, the specification of a service, or the claim of a vendor. Being able to read which form is being offered, and therefore what is not being promised, is the competence, and it requires the whole taxonomy even if only part of it is ever produced.

**Objection 2: the potential-function method is taught once and never used again, which is a poor use of the module's hardest half-hour.**

At its strongest: point 2 and step 2 spend the day's highest-load capacity on a technique that appears nowhere else in the programme. Nothing in Q4 through Q9 requires a potential function. The stated justification — that it turns amortised reasoning from a trick into a proof — is real, but it purchases generality the students will not spend, at the cost of capacity that could have gone to the individual-versus-population distinction, which is retrieved six times and is the day's actual payload.

This objection the design does not fully answer, and the accounting is unfavourable and has become slightly worse. The potential function is explicitly excluded from the retained content, which is an admission that the design does not expect it to survive. It is used once. Its removal would free roughly thirty minutes of the day's highest-value time — **and the day now has a concrete competing claim on that time**, because point 8 and step 2's attribution clause are both new, both cheap, and both do work the programme needs.

Two defences exist and both are weak. It closes a deferral made at S013, and the programme has made a virtue of keeping such promises — but a promise can be kept by explaining why the tool is not needed rather than by teaching it. And it is a genuinely elegant argument that some students will find valuable, which is true of a great many things the programme excludes.

The honest position is that the potential function is in the day because the design found it satisfying to include, not because the programme needs it, and that thirty minutes of the module's hardest capacity is being spent on that preference. The defensible alternative — state the general result at point 2 without the technique, cite where it is proved, and reallocate the time to point 8 and phase 7 — is available and is not taken. This remains the clearest instance in this design where a whitepaper has had to conclude that content is present for the designer's reasons rather than the student's.

**Objection 3: the executed cases vary a guarantee's strength and not its form, so the day demonstrates a ladder and teaches a taxonomy.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn.

At its strongest: point 1 teaches four guarantee *forms*, distinguished by what each quantifies over — a worst case over inputs, an amortised claim over sequences, an expectation over the algorithm's own randomness, a high-probability bound over the same. Phase 3 executes four configurations that differ along a completely different axis: how *strong* the promise is, from exact through conditional to absent to worse-than-absent. **Not one of the four executed cases is an amortised guarantee, and not one is a high-probability bound.** The day's most vivid forty-five minutes is therefore evidence for a distinction the day is not teaching, and the risk is specific and predictable: a student will leave with a mental ordering — exact is best, conditional next, none is worst — which is a real thing, is not the taxonomy, and will be reached for the next time they read a guarantee. That is a worse outcome than having run nothing, because a wrong organising schema is confidently applied.

The objection is conceded. The rebuttal available — that the day never claims the configurations instantiate the four forms — was true of this paper and was not true of the day as first drafted, where phase 3's table was headed *guarantee form*.

**The cheap fix is one sentence at point 1 and it is made.** Point 1 now states that phase 3 varied strength and not form, that amortised and high-probability guarantees have no instance on the day's service, and that a form's absence from one product is not evidence about its frequency in the world. It costs the day nothing and it prevents the specific misgeneralisation the objection names.

What remains unfixed is recorded rather than repaired, and it is the more interesting half. The design chose an anchor that instantiates a ladder because the ladder was available, executable and vivid — and then taught the taxonomy alongside it because the taxonomy is what the programme needs. **That is a case of the demonstration selecting the content rather than the content selecting the demonstration**, which is the standing risk of the platform turn across this entire redesign and is the first place in this quarter where it can be named concretely. The alternative — find a service that instantiates all four forms, or teach only what the service shows — is not available and would not be taken if it were. The honest statement is that the day now has one section justified by the syllabus and one justified by the service, that point 1's new sentence is the seam between them, and that the seam is visible to anyone who looks.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A majority will answer phase 1 item 4 negatively before working items 1 to 3, and a large majority affirmatively after. *Instrument:* item 4 collected twice.

**Prediction 2.** In phase 3, groups will predict result counts for the exhaustive and during-traversal configurations more accurately than for the after-traversal one, and the errors on the latter will consist of expecting the requested number of results to be returned. *Instrument:* per-configuration prediction accuracy with error direction coded.

**Prediction 3.** A large majority of first-attempt step 6 sentences will fail phase 7's actionability test. *Instrument:* phase 7 outcomes on first and revised sentences.

**Prediction 4.** At S041, students will spontaneously distinguish a population-level accuracy claim from an individual-level one, at a higher rate than a comparison cohort. *Instrument:* an S041 protocol coded for unprompted distinction. **This is the day's principal deferred test and the one that would justify its retained sentence.**

**Prediction 5.** A majority of item 5 sealed predictions will name a change to speed, and fewer than a fifth will name a surrendered guarantee. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded against what each student's agent produced. **A high rate on the second half would mean students already read a configuration change as a guarantee change, in which case point 8 is confirmation rather than instruction and phase 4's time can move to phase 7.**

**Prediction 6.** More than a third of step 1 submissions will state the vendor's count guarantee as the student's own established guarantee on first draft. *Instrument:* step 1 attribution coding. **This is the §5 vendor-adoption prediction and it is the one the design most expects to be right. A low rate would mean the attribution clause is unnecessary and the specification's second failing clause can be removed.**

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** The guarantee-communication failure in phase 6 and the operational treatment of residual probability follow [research/05-fde-craft/](research/05-fde-craft/collected-materials.md). The population-versus-individual distinction as it bears on evaluation claims is grounded in [research/03-measurement-evaluation/](research/03-measurement-evaluation/collected-materials.md). Algorithmic material follows [research/02-technical-foundations/](research/02-technical-foundations/collected-materials.md). The platform layering, the defaults-are-opinions position and the standing prohibitions follow [research/06-microsoft-platform/](research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md).

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** Phase 3's configurations and points 8 to 10 are taken from Microsoft Learn's Azure AI Search vector-filter and vector-query pages and the Foundry RAG-evaluators page, read 2026-07-26. What the documentation states: a filter may be applied during graph traversal, after traversal on each shard, or after the global result set is identified; **applying it during traversal "guarantees that `k` results are returned if they exist in the index"** and carries no false-negative risk; applying it after traversal "can create false negatives for highly selective filters or small `k` values" and may return fewer than the number requested; applying it after the global result set is identified has the highest false-negative risk, can return zero results for selective filters, and is in preview; **the during-traversal mode is the default for indexes created after approximately 15 October 2023 and the after-traversal mode is the default for indexes created before that date**; the documentation states in its own voice that the default "is almost always slower" and is the default anyway because "the bias favors recall and precision over speed"; the result-count parameter defaults to fifty when unspecified; and the semantic reranking score's distribution "can exhibit slight variations due to conditions at the infrastructure level," that "ranking model updates can also affect the distribution," and that thresholds set against it should not be made "too granular." The `document_retrieval` evaluator's composite metric set is taken from the same day's reading of the RAG-evaluators page.

**Three silences are recorded and two are taught.** **No false-negative rate is published for either non-guaranteeing mode**, at any filter selectivity, on any corpus — the risk is graded qualitatively as moderate and highest and is quantified nowhere. That is the silence phase 3's unserved caller is built on, and it is the reason §3.2's unsafe condition forbids generalising the day's own measurements. The reranking score's variation is called "slight" and **is not bounded, quantified or attributed to a named cause**, so a threshold's stability is unknowable from the documentation; that is the silence point 9 names to students. And the filter-mode benchmark section is, unusually, **the one place in this quarter's reading where a vendor states its own sampling** — index sizes, vector dimensions, service tier, partition and replica counts are all given — and it still reports only relative throughput and no accuracy, which is the folder-wide asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md) surviving even its best-documented case. Its relative-performance figures are perishable, are not taught as content, and are asserted nowhere in this wiki. All mode names, defaults, the default-change date and the preview boundary are marked perishable at point 10.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** Contrasting cases with a measured adjudicator, the pretesting effect, retrieval practice at expanding intervals, cognitive load and interactivity in proof technique acquisition, worked-example fading, and error management training are cited for **direction and mechanism only**. No effect size is asserted for any of them, and none is asserted for AI-assisted productivity anywhere in this paper.
