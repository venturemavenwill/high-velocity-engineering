## In plain terms

This is the stage's spine and the sentence it turns on belongs to a published playbook rather than to this design.

**A requirement that cannot name how it would be verified has not been written.**

The playbook gives a form with eight fields for capturing a requirement. Six of them are administrative: a name, a description, a priority, constraints, an owner, dependencies. **Two of them are the requirement — the measurement, and the verification method — and this session marks only those two.**

The reason for marking two of eight is not economy. It is that the other six can be filled correctly by somebody who has understood nothing. A name, a priority and a dependency list can be produced from the brief. A measurement requires knowing what quantity would change and what could observe it, and a verification method requires knowing who runs the check, on what, and when. **Those two fields cannot be bluffed, and the other six can.**

The session's second point is quieter and matters as much. Some requirements are real and cannot be verified. **They are not deleted; they are moved to a list and kept.** A requirement deleted for being unverifiable is not gone — it is invisible, which is worse, because it will still be enforced by somebody at the point where it is most expensive.

## Abstract

This paper defends assessing a requirement on two of the eight fields of a published capture form — the measurement and the verification method — and on nothing else. The argument is that the remaining six can be completed by a reader who has understood nothing, that agreement is the characteristic product of an unfalsifiable sentence, and that the adversarial test of a specification is cheaper than its review. It defends retaining rather than deleting unverifiable requirements, on the grounds that deletion converts an unmet constraint into an invisible one. It adopts the playbook's form while explicitly refusing the threshold catalogue published on the same page, which carries no citation of any kind. The paper concedes that marking two fields cannot distinguish a well-chosen measurement from a convenient one, and that using a generative instrument as the falsifier makes the assessment dependent on a capability whose behaviour is neither stable nor published.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A requirement set that everybody agrees with has almost certainly not been written yet.

**Agreement is what an unfalsifiable sentence produces.** "The system must be responsive" attracts no objection because there is nothing in it to object to — no quantity, no observer, no moment at which somebody could be shown to have missed it. The sentence performs the social function of a requirement and carries none of the engineering content, and it will survive every review precisely because reviews are where objections are raised.

The difficulty is that the defect is invisible in the artefact. A requirement document composed entirely of such sentences looks like a requirement document. It is only distinguishable from a real one by attempting to fail it, which nobody does, because attempting to fail your own document is unpleasant and nobody has asked for it.

**The session's response is to make failing the document the exercise.** Not reviewing it — reviewing produces agreement. Failing it: satisfying the words in a way the author would reject.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** The session works from the [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md) set where a learner has one, and from any existing requirement document where they do not.

It retrieves [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) deliberately. That session established that a question inviting a flattering answer will receive one, and that the defect lives in the question rather than in the respondent. **This session is the same claim about a sentence rather than a question**, and the retrieval is explicit so that learners recognise the shape rather than meeting it as new.

The prior it works against is that precision is pedantry — that insisting on a number where a word would do is an engineer's tic rather than an engineering act. **The countermeasure is not argument; it is the demonstration in the *Do it* movement**, where the word turns out to permit something nobody wanted.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is high and this is the module's heaviest session for that reason. Writing a verification method requires knowing what instrument exists, who owns it and when it runs — three facts most learners do not have and must go and find. The discovery that a requirement was a preference usually happens here.

**3.2 Why falsifiability rather than clarity.** Clarity is a property of a reader's experience and cannot be checked without a reader. Falsifiability is a property of the sentence and can be checked by trying. **The session prefers the checkable property**, which is the same move [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) made about a disqualifying condition and [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) made about a stopping rule.

**3.3 Why the unverifiable list is kept.** A requirement that cannot be verified is a constraint whose enforcement has no mechanism. Deleting it does not remove the constraint; it removes the record. **The constraint will still be applied, by somebody, at the point where it is most expensive to satisfy** — which is the argument, and it is design reasoning rather than an observation, because no source behind this programme records an instance of it.

**3.4 Why an agent rather than a person is the falsifier.** The property wanted in a falsifier is literalism: a reader who will not infer the constraint you left unstated and will not decide you must have meant something sensible. **People are poor at this and it is a virtue in them.** A generative instrument is good at it by default, at almost no cost, and can be run once per requirement rather than once per document. Its limitations are recorded in this paper's closing section and in §8.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

Seven movements: situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**The eight-field form is adopted verbatim from a published page read on 2026-07-30, and two fields are marked.** The decision to mark two is this design's and not the source's, although the source's own account of the form identifies the same two as the ones that make a non-functional requirement assessable rather than aspirational. **The design's contribution is making everything else unmarked**, which is a stronger claim than the source makes and is asserted rather than shown.

**The same page carries a large catalogue of example thresholds and it carries no citation of any kind.** This paper records that as a property of the source and the session forbids the catalogue's use as a benchmark. **The catalogue's numbers sit in the same flat declarative register as the publication's best-sourced pages and nothing on the page distinguishes them** — which is itself teaching material, developed at [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md).

**The adversarial instruction is a single sentence and it is deliberately not a checklist.** *Satisfy this requirement in a way I would reject.* A checklist of ambiguity types would produce a different exercise, in which the learner looks for known defects rather than finding out what their words permit.

**The prompt record is required, and the required content is what changed.** An unrecorded adversarial run is indistinguishable from no run. The specification asks for the prompt, the output and the revision, which is the minimum triple from which a reviewer can tell that the instrument was used against the artefact rather than for it.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The agent given the intentions.** The likeliest failure. A learner supplies the requirement plus the context that makes it obvious, and the instrument dutifully produces something sensible. *Countermeasure:* the specification requires the prompt record, and a prompt containing explanation rather than requirement is visible in it.

**The verification that is a meeting.** A method reading *reviewed and approved by* satisfies the field and verifies nothing. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires an instrument, and the review movement's human falsifier will exploit the gap immediately.

**The borrowed threshold.** A number arrives from the example catalogue and becomes the number everybody defends. *Countermeasure:* partial at this session, complete at [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md), where every threshold must carry its origin.

**The shrinking set.** A learner cannot verify a requirement and quietly drops it, and the set improves. *Countermeasure:* the unverifiable list, plus the specification item at [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) requiring it to be non-empty or its emptiness defended.

**The instrument with no owner.** The measurement names something that does not exist yet, which is legitimate, and nobody is building it, which is not. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires the instrument to be named work with an owner. **This is the weakest countermeasure in the session, because the owner can be named without being asked.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: marking two fields marks the form and not the judgement.**

At its strongest: a learner can supply a measurement that is precise, an instrument that exists, a verification method that runs — and measure entirely the wrong thing. A latency figure at a point nobody cares about, verified nightly, is a perfectly compliant requirement and a worthless one. **The scheme checks that two sentences have a particular grammatical shape.** Calling that an assessment of requirement quality is generous, and the design knows it, because the same criticism was made and conceded at [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) about stopping rules.

**Substantially conceded, and for the same reason as at [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md).** Marking whether the right thing was measured requires a standard for rightness, and no source behind this programme supplies one — the publication that supplies the form supplies numbers with no citation, which is worse than supplying none. **The declined fix is a rubric of measurement quality derived from the design's own judgement.** It is declined because it would be a threshold asserted without warrant, and once inside an assessment scheme it would be indistinguishable from a finding to every learner who met it. The paper would rather assess less and say so.

**Objection 2: the falsifier is a product, and the session's central movement therefore depends on a capability nobody has characterised.**

At its strongest: the exercise assumes a generative instrument that will attempt an adversarial reading and produce something the author rejects. **Nothing published establishes how reliably it does that, or that it does it consistently across versions, models or phrasings.** A cohort running the same exercise a year later, on a differently tuned model that has been trained to be more helpful and less literal, may find that it declines to build the bad thing at all — at which point the session's demonstration silently becomes a session in which everybody's requirements appear adequate.

**Conceded without qualification.** The documentation for the capability describes what the instrument does and publishes no measure of how often it converges, how often it reports completion on work that is wrong, or how its behaviour varies across model selections. **The declined fix is to replace the agent with a scripted set of adversarial readings supplied by the design.** It is declined because a supplied list teaches learners to look for the defects on the list, which is the failure mode [M03](/wiki/modules/M03.md) named about checklists; but the decline is a preference between two flawed instruments and not a defence of this one. **The review movement's human falsifier exists partly as a hedge against this, and it is a weak hedge.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** On the first pass, most requirements will have a verification method naming a person or a forum and no instrument. *Instrument:* the first-pass forms, coded *instrument named* / *person or forum only* / *empty* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most learners will produce an empty unverifiable list on the first pass, and most of those lists will become non-empty after the adversarial run. *Instrument:* the list, counted before and after, per learner.

**Prediction 3.** Requirements the agent broke and the human reviewer did not will outnumber the reverse. *Instrument:* the two review records, compared per requirement. **If the human reviewer breaks more, the agent movement is not earning its place and the session should be rewritten around paired human review.**

**Prediction 4.** Prompt records containing explanatory context will correlate with requirements the agent failed to break. *Instrument:* the prompt records, coded *requirement only* / *requirement plus context*, against the break outcome.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That agreement is what an unfalsifiable sentence produces; that two of the eight fields cannot be bluffed and the rest can; and that an unverifiable requirement deleted is worse than one recorded.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The form is a specification from a page read on 2026-07-30, from a publication that offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices and does not claim to. The catalogue of thresholds on the same page carries no citation and is forbidden as a benchmark here. The decision to mark two fields is this design's.

**The concession worth remembering.** This session can tell you that your requirement was checkable. It cannot tell you that you checked the right thing, and no source behind this programme could.
