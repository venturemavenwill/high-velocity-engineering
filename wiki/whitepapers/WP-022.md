## In plain terms

This is the stage's spine and the sentence it turns on belongs to a published playbook rather than to this design.

**A requirement that cannot name how it would be verified has not been written.**

The playbook gives a form with eight fields for capturing a requirement. Six of them are administrative: a name, a description, a priority, constraints, an owner, dependencies. **Two of them are the requirement — the measurement, and the verification method — and this session marks only those two.**

The reason for marking two of eight is not economy. It is that the other six can be filled correctly by somebody who has understood nothing. A name, a priority and a dependency list can be produced from the brief. A measurement requires knowing what quantity would change and what could observe it, and a verification method requires knowing who runs the check, on what, and when. **Those two fields cannot be bluffed, and the other six can.**

The session's second point is quieter and matters as much. Some requirements are real and cannot be verified. **They are not deleted; they are moved to a list and kept.** A requirement deleted for being unverifiable is not gone — it is invisible, which is worse, because it will still be enforced by somebody at the point where it is most expensive.

## Abstract

This paper defends assessing a requirement on two of the eight fields of a published capture form — the measurement and the verification method — and on nothing else. The argument is that the remaining six can be completed by a reader who has understood nothing, that agreement is the characteristic product of an unfalsifiable sentence, and that the adversarial test of a specification is cheaper than its review. It defends retaining rather than deleting unverifiable requirements, on the grounds that deletion converts an unmet constraint into an invisible one. It adopts the playbook's form while explicitly refusing the threshold catalogue published on the same page, which carries no citation of any kind. The paper concedes that marking two fields cannot distinguish a well-chosen measurement from a convenient one, and that using a generative instrument as the falsifier makes the assessment dependent on a capability whose behaviour is neither stable nor published.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A requirement set that everybody agrees with has almost certainly not been written yet.

**Agreement is what an unfalsifiable sentence produces.** "The system must be responsive" attracts no objection because there is nothing in it to object to — no quantity, no observer, no moment at which somebody could be shown to have missed it. The sentence performs the social function of a requirement and carries none of the engineering content, and it will survive every review precisely because reviews are where objections are raised.

The difficulty is that the defect is invisible in the artefact. A requirement document composed entirely of such sentences looks like a requirement document. It is only distinguishable from a real one by attempting to fail it, which nobody does, because attempting to fail your own document is unpleasant and nobody has asked for it.

**The session's response is to make failing the document the exercise.** Not reviewing it — reviewing produces agreement. Failing it: satisfying the words in a way the author would reject.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** The session works from the [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md) set where a learner has one, and from any existing requirement document where they do not.

It retrieves [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) deliberately. That session established that a question inviting a flattering answer will receive one, and that the defect lives in the question rather than in the respondent. **This session is the same claim about a sentence rather than a question**, and the retrieval is explicit so that learners recognise the shape rather than meeting it as new.

The prior it works against is that precision is pedantry — that insisting on a number where a word would do is an engineer's tic rather than an engineering act. **The countermeasure is not argument; it is the workflow's step 6**, where the word turns out to permit something nobody wanted.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is high and this is the module's heaviest session for that reason. Writing a verification method requires knowing what instrument exists, who owns it and when it runs — three facts most learners do not have and must go and find. The discovery that a requirement was a preference usually happens here. Extraneous load is deliberately low: the form is supplied, the tooling is supplied, and the difficulty is meant to be the thinking rather than the apparatus.

**3.2 Why falsifiability rather than clarity.** Clarity is a property of a reader's experience and cannot be checked without a reader. Falsifiability is a property of the sentence and can be checked by trying. **The session prefers the checkable property**, which is the same move [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) made about a disqualifying condition and [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) made about a stopping rule.

**3.3 Why the unverifiable list is kept.** A requirement that cannot be verified is a constraint whose enforcement has no mechanism. Deleting it does not remove the constraint; it removes the record. **The constraint will still be applied, by somebody, at the point where it is most expensive to satisfy** — which is the argument, and it is design reasoning rather than an observation, because no source behind this programme records an instance of it.

There is a second and better-warranted reason, and it comes from the source that concedes the objection to evaluation-driven development in its own author's voice: defining criteria before building is "similar to looking for the lost key under the lamppost (at night)" ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md)). A session that requires every requirement to be checkable, and provides no home for the ones that are not, teaches learners to shrink the requirement set to the lit patch. **The unverifiable list is the module's only structural defence against its own discipline.**

**3.4 Why the criteria are expected to move, and why that is not a failure of this session.**

The finding is a genuine circularity rather than a workflow inefficiency: users need criteria to grade outputs, and grading outputs is how users define criteria ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md)). Some criteria appear dependent on the specific outputs observed rather than definable in advance, which the authors say "raises serious questions for approaches that assume the independence of evaluation from observation of model outputs."

For this session that has a sharp consequence and it changes what §7 can assess. **A learner who writes a verification method before seeing any output has written the check they could imagine, and the criterion that would actually have caught the failure may only become articulable afterwards.** The session therefore requires the method to be dated and revisable, and the assessment at [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) marks presence and executability rather than correctness. **Its limits:** qualitative evidence establishing that drift occurs and matters, not how often or how much; a small user study by construction; and no resolution of the cost it creates, since criteria revised after seeing outputs make those outputs unusable as a held-out set.

**3.5 Why a check that ran once has not run.**

The position that completes the set is that an evaluation is an experiment: a reported score needs a standard error and a question count, and where questions come in related groups it needs a clustered one ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). The part that bears on a verification method is power — doing the analysis before the run is what turns "we tested and found nothing" into a statement about the system rather than about the test.

That is why the workflow requires repetitions and why the standard asks for an interval rather than a number. **A verification method incapable of detecting a violation of its own requirement is not a verification method**, and this is the only place in the module where that can be shown rather than asserted. **Its limits:** analytical rather than empirical; illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions and none is reproduced; and it addresses comparing scores rather than choosing what to score, so a tight interval around a meaningless metric is still meaningless — which is exactly §8's first objection.

**3.6 Why the check is written before the judge, and the ladder that makes it possible.**

The practitioner evaluation literature orders checks by cost: cheap deterministic assertions on every change, expensive judged checks on a cadence, live experimentation rarely and last ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md)). Its decomposition — features, then scenarios, then assertions — is what makes a verification method writable at all, and its break with testing convention is what makes it honest: **a complete pass rate is not required, and the pass rate is a product decision rather than a target.**

The Agent Framework expresses that ladder in an API rather than in advice, which is a genuine pedagogical asset because the architecture carries the lesson ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). A local evaluator runs checks without API calls, for inner-loop and continuous-integration use; a cloud evaluator performs model-based judging; **both can be mixed in a single run.** Three further properties are load-bearing for a session about verification methods. Repetitions exist "to detect non-deterministic behavior". Output is a pass or a fail with a `reason`, with a numeric score beneath it for scale-based evaluators — from which it follows that **a threshold is a policy choice separable from the measurement**, and that the reason string is what makes a judgement auditable. And one agent-behaviour evaluator takes an action sequence and an expected sequence and requires no model parameter at all, making it the only check in that group whose result does not depend on a judge.

**Its limits, and they bound §7 completely.** Read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches; many evaluators are preview; one consulted page is a superseded classic variant; surfaces differ by language. **No agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no threshold rationale.** The documentation accurately describes what the tooling computes and is no evidence that what it computes is right.

**3.7 Why an agent rather than a person is the falsifier.** The property wanted in a falsifier is literalism: a reader who will not infer the constraint you left unstated and will not decide you must have meant something sensible. **People are poor at this and it is a virtue in them.** A generative instrument is good at it by default, at almost no cost, and can be run once per requirement rather than once per document. Its limitations are recorded in this paper's closing section and in §8's second objection.

There is one recorded practitioner instance behind this and it is worth knowing because it runs the other way. In the corpus this programme is built on, an attempt to have an assistant add a feature end to end on a mature production codebase failed — the generated code reused outdated patterns and ignored architectural constraints — and **the workaround adopted was to make failing tests the specification and spend most of the human effort on the tests** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). That is this session's thesis arrived at from the opposite direction, under duress, by people who were not trying to teach it. It is a single instance, it is recorded in speaker notes rather than in the material's argument, it contradicts the collection's own headline claims, and **it is access-controlled internal material that no public reader can check.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

Eight movements: situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**The eight-field form is adopted verbatim from a published page read on 2026-07-30, and two fields are marked.** The decision to mark two is this design's and not the source's, although the source's own account of the form identifies the same two as the ones that make a non-functional requirement assessable rather than aspirational. **The design's contribution is making everything else unmarked**, which is a stronger claim than the source makes and is asserted rather than shown.

**The same page carries a large catalogue of example thresholds and it carries no citation of any kind.** This paper records that as a property of the source and the session forbids the catalogue's use as a benchmark. **The catalogue's numbers sit in the same flat declarative register as the publication's best-sourced pages and nothing on the page distinguishes them** — which is itself teaching material, developed at [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md). The complete reading of that publication found the same failure elsewhere in it: a cost claim about code review, a velocity claim about pairing, a defect-cost multiple attributed through a link that can no longer be followed, and vendor efficiency figures with bands wide enough to be unfalsifiable. **None of the four is reproduced in this wiki**, and the pattern rather than any individual instance is what the session teaches.

**The workflow's order is the argument.** Test set before check; local check before judged evaluator; judged evaluator before hand-labelling; hand-labelling before any number is reported. Each step is cheaper than the one after it and each makes the next one interpretable. **The step that teaches most is the one that costs nothing but attention** — comparing your own labels against the evaluator's — and it is placed before the adversarial run so that a learner arrives at step 6 knowing how much their own check is worth.

**The adversarial instruction is a single sentence and it is deliberately not a checklist.** *Satisfy this requirement in a way I would reject.* A checklist of ambiguity types would produce a different exercise, in which the learner looks for known defects rather than finding out what their words permit.

**The prompt record is required, and the required content is what changed.** An unrecorded adversarial run is indistinguishable from no run. The specification asks for the prompt, the output and the revision, which is the minimum triple from which a reviewer can tell that the instrument was used against the artefact rather than for it.

**The human falsifier at step 7 is a hedge and is known to be a weak one.** Its purpose is comparative rather than additive: the requirements the agent broke and the person did not are the ones that relied on a reader who wanted the document to work, and that list is only visible if both runs happen.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The agent given the intentions.** The likeliest failure. A learner supplies the requirement plus the context that makes it obvious, and the instrument dutifully produces something sensible. *Countermeasure:* the specification requires the prompt record, and a prompt containing explanation rather than requirement is visible in it.

**The verification that is a meeting.** A method reading *reviewed and approved by* satisfies the field and verifies nothing. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires an instrument, and the review movement's human falsifier will exploit the gap immediately.

**The borrowed threshold.** A number arrives from the example catalogue and becomes the number everybody defends. *Countermeasure:* partial at this session, complete at [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md), where every threshold must carry its origin.

**The shrinking set.** A learner cannot verify a requirement and quietly drops it, and the set improves. *Countermeasure:* the unverifiable list, plus the specification item at [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) requiring it to be non-empty or its emptiness defended.

**The instrument with no owner.** The measurement names something that does not exist yet, which is legitimate, and nobody is building it, which is not. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires the instrument to be named work with an owner. **This is the weakest countermeasure in the session, because the owner can be named without being asked.**

**The single run reported as a result.** A learner runs the check once against a stochastic system and reports the number. *Countermeasure:* the workflow sets repetitions and the standard asks for a spread. Prediction 6 in §9 predicts this countermeasure failing, because a repetition count is easy to configure and an interval is easy to omit from the sentence that gets read.

**The inherited threshold.** A cut point arrives from a default and is defended as a standard. *Countermeasure:* the threshold's `origin` and `why` fields in the workflow, which make inheritance a written admission rather than a silence. Completed at [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md).

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence.** Two specification items at [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md): that every requirement names a measurement and a verification method both executable by somebody who is not the author, and that the unverifiable list is present and not empty or its emptiness defended.

**The build record item at [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) is the downstream test of this session's work**, and it is assessed on the change the author made rather than on the build.

**Three things are deliberately excluded from assessment and are stated to learners in advance.** The pass rate is not assessed, because a pass rate is a product decision and marking it would produce learners optimising a number instead of examining a requirement. The correctness of the measurement is not assessed, for the reason §8's first objection concedes. And the adversarial run's *outcome* is not assessed, only its record — a learner whose requirements survived the attack has not thereby demonstrated anything, because the instrument is uncharacterised.

**Nothing else is assessed.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: marking two fields marks the form and not the judgement.**

At its strongest: a learner can supply a measurement that is precise, an instrument that exists, a verification method that runs — and measure entirely the wrong thing. A latency figure at a point nobody cares about, verified nightly, is a perfectly compliant requirement and a worthless one. **The scheme checks that two sentences have a particular grammatical shape.** Calling that an assessment of requirement quality is generous, and the design knows it, because the same criticism was made and conceded at [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) about stopping rules.

**Substantially conceded, and for the same reason as at [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md).** Marking whether the right thing was measured requires a standard for rightness, and no source behind this programme supplies one — the publication that supplies the form supplies numbers with no citation, which is worse than supplying none. **The declined fix is a rubric of measurement quality derived from the design's own judgement.** It is declined because it would be a threshold asserted without warrant, and once inside an assessment scheme it would be indistinguishable from a finding to every learner who met it. The paper would rather assess less and say so.

**Objection 2: the falsifier is a product, and the session's central movement therefore depends on a capability nobody has characterised.**

At its strongest: the exercise assumes a generative instrument that will attempt an adversarial reading and produce something the author rejects. **Nothing published establishes how reliably it does that, or that it does it consistently across versions, models or phrasings.** A cohort running the same exercise a year later, on a differently tuned model that has been trained to be more helpful and less literal, may find that it declines to build the bad thing at all — at which point the session's demonstration silently becomes a session in which everybody's requirements appear adequate.

**Conceded without qualification.** The documentation for the capability describes what the instrument does and publishes no measure of how often it converges, how often it reports completion on work that is wrong, or how its behaviour varies across model selections. **The declined fix is to replace the agent with a scripted set of adversarial readings supplied by the design.** It is declined because a supplied list teaches learners to look for the defects on the list, which is the failure mode [M03](/wiki/modules/M03.md) named about checklists; but the decline is a preference between two flawed instruments and not a defence of this one. **The workflow's human falsifier exists partly as a hedge against this, and it is a weak hedge.**

**Objection 3: teaching learners to run a judged evaluator over their own requirements will teach them that a green result is evidence.**

At its strongest, and it is the objection the platform anchor most deserves: the session hands learners a tool that returns a pass, a fail, a numeric score and a plausible-sounding reason, and asks them to use it on the document they most want to be adequate. **Nothing in the vendor documentation establishes that any of those judgements corresponds to a human's** — no agreement rate, no calibration data, no account of where the default thresholds came from — and the independent literature is unambiguous that model-based judges carry measurable biases the documentation does not discuss. A learner who reports a pass rate from an uncalibrated judge has produced a number with an unknown relationship to the thing it names, in a session whose entire subject is the difference between a sentence that means something and a sentence that does not.

**Conceded, and the design's response is structural rather than rhetorical.** The workflow places the deterministic local check first and the hand-labelled disagreement measurement before any judged number is reported; the standard requires the disagreement to be reported as precision and recall separately rather than as raw agreement, because in a requirements dataset the classes are always imbalanced; and §7 excludes the pass rate from assessment entirely. **The declined fix was to drop judged evaluators from the session and teach only deterministic checks**, which would have been defensible and is declined for one reason: most of the requirements a forward-deployed engineer writes about an agentic system cannot be checked deterministically, and a session that pretends otherwise would leave learners with a discipline that stops at the boundary of the interesting cases. **The cost of that decision is that learners meet an unvalidated instrument in the session that teaches validation**, and prediction 7 is the only thing that would detect the harm.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** On the first pass, most requirements will have a verification method naming a person or a forum and no instrument. *Instrument:* the first-pass forms, coded *instrument named* / *person or forum only* / *empty* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most learners will produce an empty unverifiable list on the first pass, and most of those lists will become non-empty after the adversarial run. *Instrument:* the list, counted before and after, per learner.

**Prediction 3.** Requirements the agent broke and the human reviewer did not will outnumber the reverse. *Instrument:* the two review records, compared per requirement. **If the human reviewer breaks more, the agent movement is not earning its place and the session should be rewritten around paired human review.**

**Prediction 4.** Prompt records containing explanatory context will correlate with requirements the agent failed to break. *Instrument:* the prompt records, coded *requirement only* / *requirement plus context*, against the break outcome.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of first-pass verification methods will be controls rather than checks — safeguards that cannot fail without a person failing to act — despite the standard requiring an instrument. *Instrument:* the first-pass forms, coded *check* / *control* / *mixed* by a second reader.

**Prediction 6.** Most learners will configure repetitions and then report a single figure with no interval attached. *Instrument:* the submitted configuration against the submitted result statement, coded for whether a spread is reported. **This predicts the failure of §5's countermeasure for the single-run failure mode**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 7.** Learners who hand-labelled a batch before running a judged evaluator will report a disagreement that surprises them, and will more often qualify their reported pass rate. *Instrument:* the labelled batch, and the result statement coded for whether it qualifies the judged number. **If the hand-labelling makes no difference to how the number is reported, §8's third objection stands undefended** and the judged evaluators should be cut from the session.

**Prediction 8.** Learners instructed to compare their item-1 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 4) will produce verification methods judged executable by a non-author more often than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* a second reader's binary judgement, split within cohort on exercise-4 completion. **The same prediction is made at [WP-021](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-021.md), [WP-023](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-023.md) and [WP-024](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-024.md), so a null across all four would indict the comparison pattern rather than this session.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That agreement is what an unfalsifiable sentence produces; that two of the eight fields cannot be bluffed and the rest can; that an unverifiable requirement deleted is worse than one recorded; and that a check which ran once against a stochastic system has not run.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The form is a specification from a page read on 2026-07-30, from a publication that offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices and does not claim to. The catalogue of thresholds on the same page carries no citation and is forbidden as a benchmark here. The evaluation literature establishes patterns from a single case, a qualitative study and an analytical argument, none of which measures an outcome. **The platform documentation accurately describes what the tooling computes and publishes no evidence that what it computes is right.** The decision to mark two fields is this design's.

**The concession worth remembering.** This session can tell you that your requirement was checkable. It cannot tell you that you checked the right thing, and no source behind this programme could. And it teaches validation using an instrument nobody has validated — which is either the sharpest available demonstration of its own thesis or a defect, and §8's third objection is where the difference is argued.

**The silences that matter.** **No agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator in this catalogue. No calibration data. No account of where any default threshold came from.** That is load-bearing here, because this session teaches learners to attach a pass condition to a requirement and then hands them judges whose correspondence with a person is unmeasured by anybody — which is why the workflow puts hand-labelling before any judged number is reported and why §7 excludes the pass rate from assessment. The documentation also publishes no guidance on how large a labelled sample must be before a disagreement rate is trustworthy, which is precisely the question §3.5's source answers and the tooling does not raise. It publishes no measure of how often the agent's self-correction loop converges, how often the instrument reports completion on work that is wrong, or how its behaviour varies across model selections — **and the third of those silences is directly load-bearing, because this session uses the instrument as a falsifier and has no way to characterise how good a falsifier it is.** Finally, the evaluator catalogue is a set of things that are convenient to measure, and nothing in the documentation suggests that this is the same set as the things worth measuring; **a requirement whose verification would need an evaluator nobody has built will find no support in it, and that absence is not evidence that the requirement is unimportant.**
