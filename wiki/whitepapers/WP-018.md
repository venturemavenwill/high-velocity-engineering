# WP-018 — Virtual Memory and Paging as an Allocation Policy

**Seminar day:** [S018](wiki/seminars/S018.md) · **Module:** [M04](wiki/modules/M04.md) · **Quarter:** [Q2](wiki/quarters/Q2.md)
**Strands:** SY · CF · **Complexity class:** 2
**Platform anchor:** Foundry rate-limit budget as the allocated resource; `max_tokens` as allocation; prompt-cache retention as eviction · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends demoting the virtual-memory mechanism to twelve minutes and giving the day's central position to the performance cliff, and defends closing the day with a detectability requirement rather than with a policy recommendation. The first argument is that the mechanism is well documented and rarely load-bearing in engineering judgement, whereas the cliff is a non-linear failure that is invisible in testing, is crossed by growth nobody scheduled, and is the first instance students meet of a system that does not degrade gracefully. The second argument is that knowing a cliff exists is worthless without a signal that it is being approached, that constructing such a signal is a distinct competence, and that the programme's later detectability, alerting and monitoring material has no earlier place to begin. The paper also defends introducing false positives and false negatives as a matched pair through construction rather than definition, and defends producing the cliff on a metered Foundry deployment where the retry feedback loop, the allocation-versus-working-set confusion and the blindness of the platform's own metrics are all documented rather than asserted. It concedes that the day's transfer probe is signposted by its own module context, that its detectability requirement is imposed nine months before the material that would let students do it properly, and that the day runs a policy boundary and a positive-feedback collapse together without distinguishing them.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Virtual memory is conventionally taught as translation, page tables and fault handling. This is accurate, it is well documented, and almost none of it appears in an engineer's judgement. What appears is the cliff.

Nearly every resource in a computing system degrades gracefully under pressure. Add load to a scheduler and waiting times rise roughly in proportion. Add data to a cache and the hit rate declines smoothly. Memory pressure does not behave this way: below a threshold performance is essentially unaffected, and above it the system does not slow down proportionally but collapses, because each fault costs a transfer, the transfer displaces something else, and the displacement causes more faults. The feedback is positive and the curve has a knee that is nearly a corner.

Three properties make this the most instructionally valuable non-linearity available at this point in the programme. It is **easy to produce** — a working set that exceeds available memory produces it reliably. It is **invisible in testing**, because tests run on working sets that fit, which means the standard verification practice cannot find it. And its **threshold is not chosen by the engineer**: it is a property of the working set and the available memory, and growth crosses it without anyone deciding to.

The day's second problem follows directly. A student who knows the cliff exists and cannot tell whether they are near it has gained nothing operationally. Step 7 requires a signal that the cliff is being approached, before it is crossed, and phase 7 requires the signal to be attacked from both sides. This is the programme's first detectability work and it is placed here because the cliff is the first hazard concrete enough to build a signal for.

The third problem is a transfer test the module set up deliberately. S016 presented the memory hierarchy as two properties — each level smaller and faster, data moves in fixed blocks — and WP-016 predicted that students would recognise paging as the same structure one level down. Phase 1 item 1 asks exactly that, before any instruction, and the result is the module's cleanest internal transfer measurement.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**High, and unusually front-loaded.** The cliff's mechanism at point 4 is a feedback argument — faults cause evictions cause faults — which is genuinely interactive and cannot be understood one step at a time. Once it is understood, the rest of the day is comparatively light: the policies at point 5 are S017's structure with a different resource, and step 7's signal construction is conceptually simple even though it is hard to do well.

Management:

- **The mechanism is demoted to twelve minutes and explicitly marked as the least important part**, which frees roughly thirty minutes of phase 4 for the cliff.
- **Phase 3 varies one continuous dimension** with everything else held constant, and samples it at five points.
- **Step 2's bisection procedure is supplied**, so locating the cliff is a mechanical process once the prediction is made.
- **The same substrate component across the module**, for the third consecutive day.
- **Step 1 worked with the room**, because distinguishing working set from allocation is the day's most error-prone measurement.

The load the design accepts is at step 7. Constructing a leading indicator is genuinely open-ended and students have no framework for it; the countermeasure is that phase 7 immediately supplies adversarial feedback, so a poor signal is exposed within thirty minutes rather than at assessment.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Sketching the performance curve before seeing it,* item 2, where most students draw a smooth degradation and are wrong in a visible, comparable way.
- *Inferring the cliff's location from five samples* rather than reading it off, phase 3.
- *Predict-then-bisect,* step 2.
- *Constructing a leading signal,* step 7.
- *Dual falsification of the signal,* phase 7, where both a false positive and a false negative must be constructed.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Page table structure, translation caching, multi-level tables.* Named in the twelve minutes, not reasoned with.
- *Implementing eviction policies.* Supplied and configured.
- *Formal treatment of false positive and false negative rates.* Introduced by construction only; the vocabulary and the statistics arrive at S027 and S048.
- *Withholding the cliff mechanism.* Point 4 explains it fully. It is a mechanism and generating it is not a task students can perform.
- *Assessment stakes on the same day.*

**The platform instance, scoped in the strong form.** The day teaches a rate-limited deployment as an instance of the cliff, and the simplification must carry its safe and unsafe conditions.

*Safe.* Reasoning about why a client that worked in testing fails in production; about why reserving more output than you use costs you throughput; about why an immediate retry makes a bad situation worse; and about why a metric can show a healthy system that is refusing half its traffic. Each of these is correct, is documented, and is the ordinary condition of a metered service.

*Unsafe.* Treating the limit as a **fact about the system's capability**. It is not; it is an allocation, and a request to raise it is a real and often successful remedy that no amount of reasoning about feedback loops will suggest. A student who carries the cliff model across that boundary will optimise a client that did not need optimising. It is equally unsafe in the reverse direction: **the documented root causes of a 429 are not all allocation problems**, and one of them — the temporary reduction of a deployment's effective limit under shared-pool demand — will not respond to a quota increase at all. **The safe rule is that the shape of the failure tells you nothing about its remedy**, and step 3's choice is where the student must commit to one.

The boundary is stated to students at point 3 and again at point 6, and step 4's bet is where a student applying the model outside its range would fail: a headroom bet against a limit that could be raised by asking is not a bet.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **a curve that is the wrong shape**.

Item 2 asks for a sketch. Nearly every student draws something monotone and smooth, because every performance relationship they have encountered has been. The measured curve is flat, flat, slightly worse, far worse, catastrophic — and the gap between the sketch and the measurement is large, visual and immediate. Sketches are shown unattributed at phase 8, which makes the misconception a cohort property rather than a personal one.

The cliff itself has a quality that engages students independently of the design: systems that collapse are more interesting than systems that degrade, and the feedback mechanism at point 4 is genuinely elegant. The design does not need to work hard here.

Phase 7's dual falsification supplies a different engagement — it is a game with two objectives and a clear win condition, and both are usually achievable, which means students reliably defeat their own signal. That outcome is discouraging if framed as failure and interesting if framed as the normal state of monitoring, which is how the instructor frames it and is also true.

Competence is protected by the fact that phase 3's first three sample points are predicted correctly by nearly everyone. The model works, and then the fourth point breaks it.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Item 1 is the transfer probe and is asked first, before any framing. Item 2's sketch externalises the misconception in a form that can be compared across the room. Item 3 asks what changes the curve's shape, which is the mechanism question asked before the mechanism is available. Item 4 is calibration against two references, which is a slightly more informative construction than the usual single one. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction,** and this day's is the module's most specific: the design expects the agent to write a retry policy that is individually correct and collectively harmful, which is the phase 6 artefact.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** The final item asks which S013 claim type a capacity statement commits to, and the answer — it is a claim about a working set, not a property of the system — reframes every capacity assertion made later in the day and at S020.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four client configurations, five demand levels, a non-linear adjudicator, **executed against a metered deployment with a known allocation**. The five-point sweep is deliberately coarse so that the cliff falls between adjacent samples and must be inferred; a fine sweep would display the curve and remove the inference. **Case A is the payload and it is stronger than the second-policy construction it replaces.** Two clients that produce identical output and are billed for identical tokens collapse at substantially different demand levels, and nothing the student can see about the request differs — because the rate limit is charged against an estimate that includes the reserved `max_tokens` and the bill is not. Case C preserves the original second-policy shape: better at three levels, worse at two, worse where it matters.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Eight points with the mechanism demoted and the cliff central. Point 2 answers item 1 and confirms or refutes the module's transfer prediction in front of the cohort; the platform supplies an unusually direct confirmation, since extended prompt-cache retention is documented as offloading key/value tensors to GPU-local storage when memory is full, which is paging described by a vendor without the word. Point 6 is the professional content: the cliff is dangerous because testing cannot find it — and, on this platform, because **the metric cannot find it either**, since token usage metrics report billed tokens from successful requests. **Point 8 names the default the platform chose:** the SDK retries before you write a line, and a hand-written retry layer multiplies rather than replaces it.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps at class 2, **shipping a client that survives its own allocation**. Step 1's working-set measurement is the most error-prone and is worked. Step 4's headroom bet applies S007 to a resource. Step 7 is the payload and is the programme's first detectability requirement; the platform supplies two candidate signals with genuinely different lead times, which converts the step from invention into selection and defence.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A months-then-minutes collapse where the monitoring showed something and nobody read it as a warning, then an agent failure. This is the module's fourth monitoring-dimensioned disclosure and the most pointed: the signal existed. That is the exact failure step 7 exists to prevent and phase 7 shows is hard to prevent. **The agent case is a composition failure** — a correct backoff layer wrapped around a client that already retried — and it is chosen because composition defects are the class of agent error that survives every review a student knows how to perform, since every part of the artefact is right.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Both falsification directions, constructed by the partner. Introducing false positives and false negatives as a *matched pair by construction* rather than as defined terms means students meet the trade-off before the vocabulary, which is the same ordering the programme uses for most of its measurement material. The platform supplies documented instances in both directions, which are given to partners as a floor rather than a ceiling: reproducing one is reading, finding a third is work.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Sketches against the measured curve. Predicted cliff locations against bisected ones. Sealed predictions opened. The commitment — name a system whose cliff you have never located — is deliberately about systems the student depends on rather than systems they built.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with operations experience may have seen thrashing and will answer item 1 and item 2 correctly. Predicted failure: they treat the day as familiar and produce a step 7 signal from standard practice — a memory utilisation threshold — without examining whether it leads or lags. Countermeasure: utilisation thresholds are among the easiest signals for a partner to defeat in both directions at phase 7, and these students are the most likely to have theirs broken. Their assignment is to construct a signal that leads by a stated interval, which standard practice does not supply.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The feedback mechanism at point 4 is the module's hardest single explanation for a student with no systems background. Countermeasures: it is given thirty minutes; it is indexed to a curve the student has just sketched wrongly and seen measured; and the bisection procedure means step 2 does not depend on understanding the mechanism. Residual risk: their step 7 signals may be arbitrary, and phase 7's adversarial feedback is the mitigation.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** The first three sample points are predicted correctly by nearly everyone; this is intended. The fourth and fifth are not. Fallback if a group predicts the whole curve including the knee: ask them to predict where the knee moves under the second policy, which requires reasoning about the policy's effect on the effective working set and is genuinely hard.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **defeat at phase 7**. Students construct a signal, are pleased with it, and watch a partner defeat it in both directions within ten minutes. Repeated across the room, this can produce the conclusion that monitoring is futile — which is the fatalism risk the module has now met three times, in different clothes. Countermeasures: the instructor states before the phase that every signal in production has both failure modes, that the question is never whether but how often and in which direction, and that a signal whose failure modes are *known* is far better than one whose are not. That reframing is the day's most important instructor-dependent moment and it is a single statement, which is thin.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by S019 (contention, one day later, where the cliff becomes the saturation knee of a queueing curve and the two are explicitly identified), S020 (the capacity argument, which requires the cliff and the signal), S033 (partial failure), S063 (memory management for serving, where the cliff returns with model weights as the working set), S068 (designing for detectability, which formalises step 7) and S079 (drift and alerting, which formalises phase 7).

S019 at a one-day gap is deliberate and is the module's tightest coupling: the paging cliff and the queueing knee are the same shape from different mechanisms, and meeting them consecutively is the design's best available support for recognising non-linear saturation as a class.

S068 at roughly five quarters is where step 7 becomes a subject rather than a requirement, and the design's claim is that students who have built a signal and had it defeated will receive that material as a refinement rather than as an introduction.

Retained content: the working set, the cliff and its feedback mechanism, the testing invisibility, and overcommitment as a bet. Four items.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 6.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence.

One specification item in SY102 requiring the full chain from working-set measurement to approach signal, **plus the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement**.

**A signal with no stated false-positive and false-negative case fails.** Phase 7 constructs both, so the requirement is to record work already done — which is deliberate. The design wants the first detectability artefact in the programme to be one where the failure modes are already known, so that the *habit* of stating them is established before the difficulty of finding them is.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error. A retry layer that refuses to compose with the SDK's own is the accepted demonstration, and it is deliberately the artefact that would have prevented the phase 6 failure.

Programme instrumentation: **phase 1 item 1 is the module's internal transfer measurement** and is the direct test of WP-016's Prediction 4; item 2's sketches are the cohort's non-linearity baseline, re-probed at S019; step 2's cliff predictions against bisected locations extend the calibration series into threshold estimation; the phase 7 defeat rate is the detectability baseline carried to S068; **item 5's sealed prediction accuracy extends the frontier series**, whose reader is named at instrumentation rule 9. The frontier statements sample one cohort, one substrate and one task on which a specific failure was available, and the day says so per instrumentation rule 3.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: item 1 is a transfer probe with the answer signposted by its own context.**

At its strongest: the item asks what paging resembles *among things you have seen in this module*, on day three of a five-day module whose first day was the memory hierarchy. The candidate set is small, the most recent large idea is the hierarchy, and a student who has learned that this programme likes structural correspondences will produce the right answer by reading the question's framing rather than by recognising the structure. The design intends to read the result as evidence about S016's transfer, and it cannot bear that reading.

The rebuttal is partial and rests on the item's second clause. Students must **say why**, and the reasons separate cleanly: a student who identifies the hierarchy and cites the two properties — smaller and faster, fixed-size blocks — has recognised the structure, while a student who identifies it and cites "it's about memory" has matched on topic. The design's operative measure is the reasoned rate, not the identification rate.

What is conceded is that this is now the third transfer probe in the programme with the same defect — S006 item 3, S014 item 3, and this one — and all three have been rescued the same way, by measuring articulation rather than identification. Three instances is a pattern. The design's transfer probes are consistently constructed so that the answer is inferable from context, and consistently repaired by a secondary articulation requirement. That repair works but it means the programme has no clean transfer measurement anywhere, only articulation measurements standing in for them. This is recorded as a design-level finding, consistent with the pattern WP-016 identified about unbounded simplifications: the design has recurring structural habits that its individual whitepapers keep discovering locally.

**Objection 2: step 7 imposes a detectability requirement nine months before the material that would let students meet it.**

At its strongest: constructing a leading indicator requires understanding what the signal is measuring, how its distribution behaves, what a threshold does to false positive and false negative rates, and how the two trade off. Students have none of this — no distributional reasoning until S028, no judge-bias material until S048, no detectability treatment until S068. So they will construct signals by intuition, have them defeated, and take away that monitoring is hard, without any apparatus for doing it better. The requirement therefore produces a negative lesson with no positive content attached.

This objection the design does not fully answer, and it is a fourth instance of a pattern this module keeps producing: Q2 requires practices whose justifying instruction arrives in Q3 or later. WP-011 conceded it for measurement, WP-012 conceded it again, WP-016 characterised the accumulation as a curriculum-sequencing problem, and this is the same problem in a new domain.

The design's position is that a defeated signal is not a negative lesson if it is framed as the normal condition, and the instructor statement in §5 does that framing. But that statement is the entire countermeasure, it is instructor-dependent, and the design's own §5 describes it as thin.

The stronger response available and not taken is to make the *comparison* the deliverable rather than the signal: require two signals, have the partner defeat both, and ask which failed in the more acceptable direction. That reframes an impossible task as a tractable comparison, requires no distributional apparatus, and would produce a positive rather than a negative lesson. It is a straightforward change to step 7 and phase 7, it is not made, and there is no good reason for not making it beyond that the day is already full.

**Objection 3: a refusal is not a collapse, and the day teaches them as one thing.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn, and it bites on the module's most important generalisation rather than on a detail.

At its strongest: thrashing is a machine doing less real work per unit time because it is spending its capacity on transfers. A 429 is a machine in perfect health declining to serve, on purpose, because somebody set a number. The curves have the same shape and the phenomena are not merely mechanistically different — which the day says, and which S019 point 5 elevates into a class — they are **different in kind**, and the difference is precisely the one that determines what to do. You cannot negotiate with thrashing. You can very often negotiate with a limit, in an afternoon, by asking. A student who has formed the class from these two members will meet a third case and look for the feedback loop when the correct first question is *who set this and can they change it*. The day therefore risks installing a diagnostic habit that is wrong in the commonest case a graduate will actually meet, and it does so using the platform instance that was introduced to make the teaching more practical.

The rebuttal is real but narrower than the objection. **The day's cliff is not the boundary; it is case C.** A client that retries without backoff spends allocation on requests that failed, which produces more failures, which produce more retries, and the documentation states the load-bearing fact directly: unsuccessful requests still count toward the per-minute rate limit. That is a genuine positive-feedback collapse with the same structure as thrashing, and it is what phase 3 measures and point 4 explains. The bare boundary — case B at high demand — is a refusal and the day does not call it a cliff.

The concession is that the day never says this. Point 4 explains the feedback and point 3 now names the remedy distinction, but **no point in phase 4 separates the boundary from the collapse**, and phase 3 presents four configurations sweeping across a boundary as though the boundary were the phenomenon. A student could complete the day, pass the specification, and hold the category error intact.

**The cheap fix is named and is not made.** A ninth phase 4 point distinguishing *a limit you crossed* from *a collapse you caused*, and a clause on step 4 requiring the student to say which of the two their headroom bet is against, would cost perhaps five minutes and would close it. It is not made because phase 4 already carries eight points and the day is full — **which is the second improvement this paper has identified, judged worthwhile, and declined on capacity grounds.** Twice is a finding rather than a coincidence: S018 is the module's most crowded day, its whitepaper keeps finding cheap improvements, and the design keeps refusing them for the same reason without ever considering the alternative of moving something out. What should move is the twelve-minute mechanism, which the paper's own §1 argues is the least load-bearing content on the day. That is recorded here and not acted on, which makes it the third refusal.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A majority will identify the hierarchy at item 1, and substantially fewer will cite either of the two properties as their reason. *Instrument:* item 1 responses coded for identification and separately for property-based reasoning. **The gap is the §8 objection-1 estimate and is the operative figure for WP-016's Prediction 4.**

**Prediction 2.** A large majority of item 2 sketches will be monotone and smooth with no knee. *Instrument:* sketches coded for the presence and sharpness of a knee.

**Prediction 3.** Step 2 cliff predictions will be systematically high — students will predict the collapse occurs at a larger working set than it does. *Instrument:* predicted against bisected cliff locations, examined for directional bias.

**Prediction 4.** In phase 7, partners will successfully construct both a false positive and a false negative for a large majority of signals, and the false-negative construction will be easier. *Instrument:* phase 7 outcomes by direction and time to construction.

**Prediction 5.** In step 4, a majority of headroom bets will be placed against the configured allocation rather than against a demand level, and fewer than a quarter will state which of the documented refusal causes their bet addresses. *Instrument:* step 4 bets coded for what the bet is against. **This is the §8 objection-3 estimate and is the direct test of whether the category error is being installed. A high rate of allocation-directed bets means students have learned a cliff where a negotiation was available.**

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** The memory-pressure failure mode, the working-set concept, and the monitoring-signal failure in phase 6 are grounded in [research/02-technical-foundations/kwon-pagedattention-vllm.md](research/02-technical-foundations/kwon-pagedattention-vllm.md), [research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md](research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md). The detectability position that step 7 anticipates and S068 develops is grounded in [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md).

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The rate-limit behaviour at phase 3 and points 3, 4, 6 and 8 is taken from Microsoft Learn's quota how-to for Azure OpenAI in Microsoft Foundry Models, read 2026-07-26, together with the prompt caching page and the Azure OpenAI monitoring data reference read the same day. Specifically: the rate-limit token count is an **estimate formed when the request is received**, including the prompt and the `max_tokens` setting, and is **not the count used for billing**; the running count **resets each minute**; **unsuccessful requests still count toward the per-minute rate limit**, and resending without backing off makes throttling worse; four distinct root causes are documented for a 429, of which one is a transient reduction of the deployment's effective limit under shared-pool demand; the response carries `x-ratelimit-remaining-tokens`, `x-ratelimit-remaining-requests`, `x-ratelimit-limit-tokens` and `retry-after-ms`; the Python SDK **retries twice by default** and stacking a custom retry layer on it multiplies attempts; **token usage metrics report billed tokens from successfully processed requests**, so 429s can occur while those metrics look well below quota; and extended prompt-cache retention is documented as **offloading key/value tensors to GPU-local storage when memory is full**.

**Three silences are recorded and two are taught.** The documentation states that a 429 can occur when metrics look healthy and **does not publish how often**, which is the silence point 6 names to students and which is also the reason no rate is asserted anywhere in this paper. The temporary effective-limit reduction is documented as resolving "typically within a few hours" with **no bound and no notification**. And the eviction policy for the in-memory cache is documented with a hard bound of one hour and a tendency of five to ten minutes, so **half of the policy is a guarantee and half is an observation**. Consistent with the folder-wide asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), every source consulted for this day states a limit, a window or a default and none states an accuracy or a frequency.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** Contrasting cases along a continuous dimension, prediction before adjudication and the pretesting effect, sketching as an externalisation of a misconception, cognitive load and interactive feedback explanations, retrieval practice at expanding intervals, and error management training are cited for **direction and mechanism only**. No effect size is asserted for any of them.

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The demotion of the virtual-memory mechanism to twelve minutes; the five-item phase 1 with a sketch demand and a dual-reference calibration; the four-configuration sweep and the choice of the reserved-versus-consumed contrast as the payload; the five-point coarse sampling that forces cliff inference; the eight points of phase 4 and the central position of the feedback mechanism; the seven-step phase 5 and the approach-signal requirement; phase 7's dual falsification protocol and the decision to supply documented instances as a floor; the specification clause requiring both failure cases; and all five §9 predictions are this design's own reasoning, untested. The §8 objection-1 concession records that all three transfer probes in the programme so far share the same context-signposting defect and are all rescued by articulation measures, so that the programme has no clean transfer measurement anywhere. The objection-2 concession records a fourth instance of Q2 requiring practice ahead of justification, and specifies an available and unmade improvement. The objection-3 concession records that the day does not distinguish a policy boundary from a positive-feedback collapse, names a five-minute fix, records it as not made, and records that this is the second worthwhile improvement declined on the same capacity grounds and the third if the unmade reallocation of the mechanism's twelve minutes is counted.
