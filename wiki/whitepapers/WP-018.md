## In plain terms

One person told you something. What are you now entitled to claim?

The honest answer is: something about that person. Everything beyond it — that the organisation has this problem, that engineers find this frustrating, that the release process is the bottleneck — requires an argument that runs from several cases to a population, and that argument is the work this session is about.

The corpus's discovery instrument does not make the argument. It never consolidates across informants at all, and it ships an artefact that prevents consolidation: a lookup table that converts one person's single remark directly into a prescribed intervention. **That table is efficient, usable in the room, and it makes the population question disappear.** An instrument that maps one remark to one action has decided in advance that one remark is enough.

The session's method is ordinary and slow. Write each informant up as a case, with enough context that a colleague can understand them. Cluster the observations upward by similarity before naming anything. Name the groups after they exist. Then look for the role with no observations under any heading, because **a polished hierarchy can encode a narrow study perfectly** and looks the same from outside as a broad one.

And preserve the contradiction. Two informants disagreeing about how approvals work is almost never one of them being wrong. It is a missing distinction — a role, a policy, an incentive, a context — and resolving it by choosing is how a finding becomes quietly false.

## Abstract

This paper defends team-built inductive consolidation as a required step between evidence and design, and identifies its principal modern threat as automation of the step rather than omission of it. The argument is that a claim's population is established by consolidation and by nothing else; that segment merging, dissent disappearance and single-utterance promotion each destroy the population claim without leaving a visible trace; and that the value of the consolidation step lies substantially in the disagreement it forces, which is exactly what delegating it removes. The session installs a consolidated finding carrying a named population, its cases, a preserved contradiction, a not-sampled list and a withdrawal condition. The paper concedes that the sources behind it establish no effect, that its prohibition on delegated clustering rests on one organisation's own automation marking, and that the prohibition will be widely ignored.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A practitioner returns from discovery with a set of vivid particulars and is asked what the customer needs.

Every pressure in that moment favours a short answer with a memorable quotation attached. The quotation is repeatable, it travels well in a slide, and by the time it has been repeated a few times its origin — one person, in one role, on one day, possibly the loudest person in the room — has been shed. **The quotation becomes the customer's voice, and no artefact records that it was one person's.**

The second failure is subtler and worse. Mixed answers from users, approvers and budget owners get aggregated as one customer voice, producing an apparently ambivalent signal. The team reads ambivalence and hedges. **In fact three populations answered clearly and differently**, and the hedge was manufactured by the aggregation.

The session's response is an artefact whose required fields cannot be filled without doing the consolidation: the population, the cases, the contradiction, the absence, the withdrawal condition.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) supplies questions that produce answers worth consolidating; [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) supplies the observed–reported–inferred separation that the case write-ups depend on. A learner arriving without them consolidates leading answers, which produces a confident finding about nothing.

The prior it works against is that synthesis is summarising. Learners arrive believing the task is to compress. **The session's move is that consolidation is an inference about a population, that it can be wrong, and that summarising cannot be.**

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The module's intrinsic high point. Consolidation requires holding several cases as distinct objects while simultaneously reasoning about what is common to them, and the two operations interfere. It does not decompose into simpler steps and there is no way to make it cheaper.

**3.2 Why bottom-up.** An imported taxonomy cannot be surprised. Grouping by similarity before naming, then naming the groups that exist, then grouping the groups, produces a hierarchy that is an account of what was found rather than a container the findings were poured into. **This is a method's internal argument and no comparison establishes it.**

**3.3 Why cases before themes.** A theme traced to decontextualised quotations cannot be evaluated, because a reader cannot tell whether the quotations came from people in comparable situations. Requiring each informant to exist as a case first preserves the information a theme would otherwise destroy.

**3.4 Why segmentation precedes aggregation.** Broad labels hide people with incompatible goals, workflows, buying processes and constraints, and evidence merged across them is not one signal. **A useful segment is also a findable one** — a kind of person paired with the place or behaviour through which they can be reached — which makes the segmentation checkable rather than notional.

**3.5 Why the team rather than the analyst.** Concentrating evidence and interpretation in one person creates a bottleneck who can, without any bad intent, use customer authority to settle internal arguments. The remedy is shared preparation, first-hand exposure for people making decisions, retrievable notes, and prompt review. **Direction and mechanism only.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**The finding's five fields are chosen so that each one fails loudly if the work was not done.** A population that cannot be described means no consolidation happened. Cases that cannot be listed means the themes are floating. No contradiction means dissent was resolved or never collected. No not-sampled list means nobody asked who was missing. No withdrawal condition means the claim is not a claim.

**The three review questions are fixed and blunt** — *which cases? who is not in it? what would you withdraw it for?* — and they are asked in that order because the first two are answerable from the artefact and the third is not.

**The prohibition on delegated clustering is the session's most contestable content and is placed last.** The argument is not that the machine clusters badly. It is that the clustering is not the deliverable: the deliverable is a team that has argued about which observations belong together, because the missing distinction behind a contradiction is found in that argument and nowhere else. **The strongest support available is that the organisation whose practice this stage draws on marks its own discovery work as manual in its own item-by-item automation tagging** — a judgement made by people with a commercial interest in the opposite answer.

**The agent is redirected rather than banned.** It is used to attack the finding: which cases does the claim not fit, what population have you actually described. This is [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s claim about instruments applied to the module's own artefact.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The promoted utterance.** The likeliest failure and the hardest to see. *Countermeasure:* the first review question, which requires cases in the plural.

**The merged segment.** *Countermeasure:* the requirement that the population be describable and that not everybody in the room be in it.

**The disappeared dissent.** A contradiction is resolved in a team discussion and the losing account leaves the artefact. *Countermeasure:* the preserved-contradiction field, and the requirement that the proposed distinction be stated as a hypothesis with a test rather than as a resolution.

**The loudest informant.** *Countermeasure:* partial, and deliberately deferred from [S014](/wiki/seminars/S014.md) so that the room-management problem is felt as an evidence problem here. The not-sampled list catches absence but not over-weighting, and nothing in the session catches over-weighting directly.

**Delegated clustering.** *Countermeasure:* the third review question, which a learner who did not build the hierarchy usually cannot answer. **This is a weak countermeasure and the paper says so** — a sufficiently careful learner can reconstruct an answer after the fact.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the prohibition on machine clustering is protectionism and it will be ignored.**

At its strongest: a model can group a set of interview observations quickly and competently, and the groupings will frequently be as good as a tired team's. Refusing on the grounds that the argument matters is a claim about a process benefit that nobody has measured, in a programme that teaches its learners to distrust exactly that move. And in practice the learner will use the tool, produce the finding, and answer the review questions from the finding — so the prohibition will not even be observable.

**Substantially conceded.** The prohibition rests on one organisation's own marking of its own work, which is evidence that the belief is held and not that it is correct, and this paper does not have anything stronger. The rebuttal is narrow: the review's third question is about what would withdraw the claim, and a learner who did not participate in the grouping has no basis for it beyond invention. **The declined fix is to permit machine clustering with a required human audit of each cluster.** It is declined because an audit of a proposed grouping is a different cognitive act from constructing one — it checks whether the grouping is defensible, and the session's claim is that constructing it is where the missing distinction is found. **That claim is unmeasured. A learner who acts otherwise is making a defensible bet.**

**Objection 2: preserving a contradiction is a licence to avoid deciding.**

At its strongest: engineering requires decisions. A finding that reports both accounts and proposes a hypothesis has not told the requirements author anything they can act on, and the discipline of preservation will be used to avoid the harder work of establishing which account governs. Downstream, somebody will have to choose anyway, with less information and no record of the reasoning.

**Not conceded, though the risk is real.** The session requires a proposed distinction and a named test, not a suspension. The difference between preserving a contradiction and refusing to decide is whether an artefact says what would settle it. **The declined fix is to require the contradiction be resolved before the finding is issued.** It is declined because resolution at this stage is usually resolution by preference — and the preferred account is the one that supports the work the practitioner has come to do, which is the failure this whole module exists to prevent.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first-draft findings will state a claim about "the customer" rather than about a describable population. *Instrument:* the finding's subject, coded *named population* / *organisation* / *unspecified* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Findings will more often lack a not-sampled list than lack a preserved contradiction. *Instrument:* field-presence counts across submitted findings.

**Prediction 3.** The third review question will produce more revisions than the first two combined. *Instrument:* revisions recorded per question, per learner.

**Prediction 4.** Learners who used an agent to attack the finding will revise the population statement more often than those who used it to generate the clusters. *Instrument:* the prompt record against the revision, coded by which the agent was asked for. **If the two are indistinguishable, [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s claim about instruments has no support at this scale and this movement should be rewritten as an assertion rather than a demonstration.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the population of a claim is established by consolidation and by nothing else, and that the common failures — promotion, merging, disappearance — destroy it without leaving a trace.

**What the evidence licenses.** A method and a set of checkable fields. **A set of conversations can expose mechanisms and meanings; it cannot establish prevalence.** No source here licenses turning repeated anecdotes into frequency claims, and that is the failure this session most easily produces.

**The concession worth remembering.** The prohibition on delegated clustering is the weakest-supported claim in the module. It rests on a belief held by people who work this way, and the honest description of a learner who ignores it is that they are making a defensible bet.
