## In plain terms

A workshop is a machine with an output. Most of them are run without anybody saying what the output is, and a machine with no stated output cannot be observed to have failed.

The corpus this session draws on says this better than most published material does. It states that a session ending in thanks and good feelings with no committed next step has failed, and it names awareness and enthusiasm as unacceptable outcomes. That is a genuinely uncomfortable position to put to a delivery organisation, whose commercial incentives all point the other way, and it is correct.

**The corpus then contradicts itself about whether the instrument should exist at all.** It prohibits, repeatedly, running design activity as a separate workshop rather than interleaving it into the engineering cadence. Its case studies' headline achievement is a compressed multi-day workshop. Nothing reconciles them and, as far as this repository can tell, nobody has noticed.

The session does not resolve it. It requires the learner to take a position and defend it, because a practitioner who cannot argue both sides of that question will be handed one side by whoever books the room.

The session then does something the earlier version of this design did not: it makes the learner build the opening of the workshop as configuration rather than as intention. A question order carried in the head is invisible, including to the person carrying it. Exported as a topic definition with a declared trigger, it becomes a thing with alternatives — and the question that asks the customer to endorse the solution you sell, which is inaudible in a warm room, is obvious on a screen.

## Abstract

This paper defends opening the discovery stage with the workshop's purpose rather than its technique, and defends requiring the learner to render their own opening protocol as inspectable configuration.

The argument runs in four parts. **First**, a workshop is an instrument, an instrument requires a stated output before it requires a procedure, and the customary output — alignment, awareness, enthusiasm — has no failure state and therefore no informational content. **Second**, an output with a failure state is available and is not novel: a commitment is identified by what a stakeholder gave up, in a currency of time, reputation or money, and that test is checkable by somebody who was not in the room. **Third**, the ordering of an elicitation is a design decision that practitioners make by habit and cannot inspect, and a low-code conversational authoring surface separates the trigger from the body cheaply enough that a learner can watch their own habit become a choice inside one session. **Fourth**, the corpus supplying the practice contradicts itself about whether the instrument should exist, and the design's judgement is that handing the contradiction over unresolved is worth more than resolving it, because it is the first occasion in the programme where a learner must take a position against a source that cannot help them.

The paper records an amendment made while writing it: the session as first designed had no platform anchor and no executable artefact, which left the whole of its central claim — that an unstated design decision is invisible — as an assertion the learner had to accept. The correction adds the topic-authoring workflow and is documented at §4.5 rather than applied silently.

The paper concedes that alignment is real work which this design cannot measure either; that the corpus reports no failed workshop and therefore cannot establish that any of its practice works; that the platform documentation describes what a product computes and supplies no evidence that computing it produces better engagements; and that requiring configuration risks teaching learners that an inspectable protocol is a good one.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

An engineer sent to run a discovery workshop arrives holding an agenda and no output specification.

The reason is structural. Agendas are the artefact the organisation produces; they are reviewable, shareable, and they make the two days look prepared. The output specification is not an artefact anybody asks for, and producing one costs the person producing it — because a stated output can be observed not to have arrived.

The consequence is that workshops are evaluated on the experience they produced. **The experience is real and it is not evidence.** A room can be engaged, well-facilitated, and correct about nothing, and no participant is in a position to notice.

There is a second problem underneath, and it is the one the platform work exists to reach. Even a practitioner who accepts all of the above still opens the session with a sequence of questions they have never written down. The sequence encodes decisions — what is asked first, what is asked of whom, what is never asked — and none of those decisions is available for review, because there is no artefact to review. **A design decision that leaves no artefact is not a decision anybody can be wrong about**, which is why it never improves.

The session's response is to make the output specification cheaper to write than the agenda, to make it precede the agenda in time so that the agenda is derived rather than adopted, and to make the opening protocol exist as a file.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** A reader who has run any facilitated session, in any context, has enough. A reader who has completed [Stage 1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md) additionally holds the qualification stance, which makes the failure condition easier to write because [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) already required a condition capable of firing.

The prior this session works against is near-universal: that a good session is one people were glad to have attended. Learners hold it strongly, and it is not stupid — it is a reasonable generalisation from the fact that a session people hated has usually gone wrong. The session's move is not to deny it but to point out that it is one-directional evidence.

The platform work has a genuinely mixed entry profile and the design accepts it. Learners who have built conversational agents will find the topic surface familiar and will under-rate the trigger separation, because they are used to treating triggers as routing. Learners who have not will find the surface unfamiliar and will over-build, producing an elaborate protocol before testing a simple one. **Neither group arrives holding the fact that decides the exercise: that the ordering they would have used in the room is a decision they have never made explicitly.**

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is low. The material is few concepts, weakly interacting. The surplus is spent in two places: on the contradiction in §4, which requires holding two incompatible readings of the same source and choosing between them; and on the topic export, which is the first occasion in the programme where a learner's own tacit practice is rendered as text and found to contain something they did not intend. Both are genuine element interactivity and neither decomposes.

**3.2 Why the opening items precede instruction.** Attempting an answer before instruction improves later retention of the tested material and makes the distinctions noticeable when they arrive; the design is proactive rather than reactive, in the sense that the failure is engineered rather than waited for ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). The direction is well established; **no magnitude is asserted here or anywhere in this wiki.**

Two conditions from that source constrain the design and are honoured. The task must admit multiple solutions and representations — opening item 5 does, and the source's own redesign test applies: if most learners cannot generate more than one answer, the item is wrong and must be rewritten. And the consolidation phase must run on **comparison**, not on presentation: the source reports a study in which providing correct and incorrect solutions together was insufficient and only explicit prompting to compare them produced the benefit. Exercise 4 is that prompt and is mandatory for that reason.

**Its limits.** The book was read in full for this repository; the meta-analysis behind it is verified from its abstract only; the founding journal article has not been read at all. The evidence base is school, university and laboratory, with no adult-professional study in its reference list, and the author concedes that the design fails when badly implemented and that failure is sufficient but not necessary for the awareness it produces.

**3.3 Why the failure condition is the artefact's centre.** A specification with no failure state cannot be checked by anyone other than its author. The session's requirement — that a peer strike out every line that could be true after a session which produced nothing — is a cheap external check, and cheap external checks are the only kind that survive delivery pressure.

**3.4 Why commitment is the output and how it is identified.** The replacement for alignment is not a better abstraction; it is a different kind of object. The customer-conversation literature distinguishes **commitment**, in which a stakeholder gives up something they value, from **advancement**, in which the relationship moves to a defined next stage ([research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md)). The currencies are meaningful time, reputational risk and money, and the source's diagnostic term for the alternative is exact: a friendly contact who repeatedly meets and praises without committing or rejecting is a **zombie lead**, and the real process failure is leaving with neither advancement nor rejection.

The source is equally clear about what commitment does not prove: a stakeholder may commit for political, experimental or relationship reasons, and a person without authority may be unable to commit despite a genuine need. **So the test discriminates between a warm sentence and a costly act. It does not discriminate between a good engagement and a bad one**, and the session says so.

**Its limits.** A founder-oriented practitioner handbook, not a qualitative-research method; no controlled comparison, no sampling framework, no measured effect, and conversation counts that are the author's anecdote. The local copy carries no publisher, year or ISBN, so the bibliographic record commonly cited for it is not established here.

**3.5 Why the decision must be the customer's.** A charter naming a decision the practitioner takes has specified an activity, not an outcome. The design-research literature reaches the same constraint from the other direction: project scope begins with the organisation's actual tolerance for change, the focus is expressed as people doing activities in contexts rather than as a technology or product component, and **the data collected bounds the design conclusions available later** ([research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md)). A session that extracts nothing costly has bounded its conclusions at nothing.

The same source supplies the deeper reason discovery cannot be delegated to the customer's stated wants: familiar action becomes tacit, retrospective accounts omit detail, general questions invite general answers, and requests for features elicit solutions shaped by the tools people already know. **The team cannot delegate product definition to users by asking what they want.**

**Its limits.** A method text resting on the authors' long practice and accumulated project examples. It offers procedural rationale and cases, **not a controlled comparison demonstrating that the method outperforms alternatives**, and it instructs readers explicitly not to convert its examples or prescriptions into effect-size claims. None is converted anywhere in this session.

**3.6 Why the venue claim is taught despite being unwarranted.** The ordering — difficulty determines people, people determine venue — is a single practitioner judgement with nothing behind it. It is included because it is non-obvious, it is cheap to test, and the alternative ordering is what every organisation does by default. **A claim worth testing is worth teaching as a claim.**

**3.7 Why the elicitation protocol becomes configuration, and what the platform is and is not for.**

The platform's contribution is not tooling competence and the session says so. It is that a topic separates a **trigger** — the declared condition under which this line of questioning is correct — from the body of questions, and that separation forces the practitioner to answer, at conversational scale, exactly the question the charter asks at engagement scale.

The vendor's own comparison material positions the product as low-code authoring through a drag-and-drop interface "without coding", with "hundreds of prebuilt connectors" for plug-and-play integration ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). That is the property the session exploits: the cost of rendering a habit as an artefact is near zero, so there is no excuse for not doing it.

The same documentation is unusually candid about the envelope, and this is the part a course must teach. The product is "designed for small-scale deployments, such as department-level solutions". It has a "limited ability to fine-tune API calls, actions, and response formatting compared with a pro-code environment". Adaptive Cards are "limited to schema 1.6 and earlier". Feature availability is slower than the pro-code paths. **A tool used outside its design envelope fails through governance and lifecycle rather than through function**, which means the symptoms arrive late and look like process problems.

One further axis from the same family belongs here even though the session does not exercise it. Declarative agents in this ecosystem are "limited to Copilot's orchestrator and models" and "rely on user-initiated interactions", where custom engine agents own their orchestration and can "trigger actions automatically, even without direct user input". **An agent that cannot act without being asked is architecturally different from one that can**, in failure modes and governance alike — and an elicitation protocol is, by construction, the reactive case. Naming that here is what makes [S014](/wiki/seminars/S014.md)'s escalation work legible when it arrives.

**Its limits, and they are severe.** First-party vendor selection guidance, commercially framed, comparing Microsoft options only with each other and not against alternatives. **Read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches**, so page structure is reconstructed and several tables were truncated. **No adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no evidence that the recommended mappings produce better outcomes.** Product names are in active flux — the documentation itself records one SDK as "formerly" something else — and a limitation recorded today may be closed tomorrow, so a course must not teach a limitation as permanent.

**3.8 Why the corpus is used at all, given what it lacks.**

The practice corpus is the only source available that treats the workshop as an instrument with a failure condition, and its statement of that position is sharper than anything published. It also names four antipatterns with warning signs observable **in advance** — a customer asking for a tool overview, a room of managers with few hands-on engineers, no sandbox requested, an agenda finalised before the customer is understood, labs running on sample data rather than the customer's own ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). Advance observability is what makes a warning sign usable, and it is rare.

**Its limits, and they are the module's standing caveat.** Access-controlled internal material, held here only as a white-labelled paraphrase that no public reader can check. A stratum spanning years, written by many hands, contradicting itself in more than a dozen places. **No failed workshop, no declined session, no exclusion criteria, no cost side, no quality evidence and no sustainment record anywhere in it.** Its single failure narrative is a synthetic composite and fictional by its own admission. One of its own named antipatterns is contradicted by its own workshop recipe.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The charter is deliberately four lines long.** Longer charters get written once and never consulted; the session's own [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md) failure mode is the abandoned charter, and length is the mechanism that produces abandonment.

**4.2 The contradiction is the session's centre and it is handed over unresolved.** The corpus prohibits the separate workshop and celebrates the separate workshop. The design's judgement is that resolving it for the learner would remove the session's most valuable property: this is the first time in the programme that a learner is asked to take a position against a source that cannot help them, and the discomfort of that is the content.

The position paragraph carries one constraint that makes fence-sitting impossible — the learner must name what is lost by the position they did not take. A paragraph that acknowledges both readings and commits to neither cannot satisfy it.

**4.3 The failure modes are stated as warning signs rather than as definitions**, following [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) and the corpus, which uses this format well. The warning signs here are all observable before the session rather than during it, which is what makes them usable.

**4.4 What the platform workflow is for.** Not tooling competence, and the session states this. It exists to make one thing observable that prose cannot: **that the ordering of an elicitation is a decision, and that the practitioner has been making it silently for their whole career.**

Three properties of the exercise carry the weight. The topic must be named after something it establishes, which kills the agenda item disguised as a question. The trigger must be written before the body, which forces the charter question down to conversational scale. And one question must be removed with its reason recorded — the removal is the artefact, because a deleted question leaves no trace and a recorded removal is a decision somebody can disagree with.

The hostile-respondent run is placed inside the workflow rather than in the exercises because it is the only step that produces a transcript. A room does not give you a re-readable record of your own questions failing; a test canvas does, at no cost, and the questions that collapse under compliments and generic promises are exactly the ones that feel best in person.

**4.5 A defect found in this session's own design, and the amendment.**

The session as first written had no platform anchor and produced one artefact: a charter, on paper, reviewed by a peer.

**A learner could complete it in full while every claim about invisible design decisions remained something they had been told.** The session's own thesis is that an unstated specification cannot be checked and therefore never improves — and the session applied that thesis to the workshop's output while leaving the learner's own question ordering exactly as unstated as it arrived. The stage's opening session was asking learners to externalise a customer's tacit practice using a method the learner had never had applied to their own.

The amendment adds workflow steps 2 to 6 and produce-items 5 to 7. It does **not** assess the topic, and this is deliberate: assessing a configuration artefact would produce learners optimising a file rather than examining a habit. What [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md) assesses is still the charter.

**The amendment is recorded rather than silently applied**, in keeping with this programme's convention that a defect stays legible so a reader can evaluate the correction. Assume there are others this paper has not caught.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The unfireable failure condition.** The most likely outcome is a charter whose failure state is total collapse. *Countermeasure:* the strike-out review, which removes every line compatible with a session that produced nothing.

**The alignment output.** A learner states the output as shared understanding and cannot say what would show it was absent. *Countermeasure:* the requirement that the decision be one the customer takes rather than one the learner takes, which forces a subject with agency.

**Position-taking avoided.** A learner writes a paragraph on the contradiction that acknowledges both readings and commits to neither. *Countermeasure:* the paragraph is required to name what is lost by the position not taken, which cannot be written without taking one.

**The endorsement question survives.** A learner builds a protocol containing a question that invites the customer to agree their solution category would help, and does not notice. *Countermeasure:* partial. Step 5 requires a removal and names this class first, but a learner who removes a different question has satisfied the step. Prediction 5 in §9 predicts this countermeasure failing.

**Configuration mistaken for quality.** A learner produces an elaborate, well-structured topic and concludes their elicitation is good. *Countermeasure:* the hostile-respondent run, which is designed to make a well-structured protocol produce nothing. **This is a weak countermeasure against a strong temptation**, and §8's third objection concedes it.

**Charter abandonment.** The charter is written well and never used. *Countermeasure:* none available here. It is tested at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md), where the session is run under an assigned difficulty and the charter is one of the assessed artefacts.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence.** Two specification items at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md): that the charter's failure condition is capable of firing, and that the learner can say what they observed against it.

**Never assessed:** whether the workshop the charter describes would have succeeded; whether the customer committed; the quality, structure or completeness of the topic configuration; and whether the learner's position on the contradiction is the right one.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the alignment output is real, and dismissing it is naive about how organisations work.**

At its strongest: consequential decisions in large organisations are taken by people who must first believe the same things about the situation, and the work of getting them there is real work with real value. A programme that tells engineers to dismiss it is training them to be ineffective in exactly the rooms where effectiveness matters, and the engineer who reports that a session produced no commitment will be told, correctly, that they missed the point.

**This is substantially correct and the design concedes it.** The rebuttal is narrower than the objection: the session does not claim that alignment is worthless, it claims that alignment cannot be observed and therefore cannot be a specification. **The declined fix is to permit alignment as an output when accompanied by an indicator.** It is declined because every indicator offered in review turned out to be either a commitment — in which case name the commitment — or a self-report by the person who ran the session.

**Objection 2: the contradiction is not a contradiction, it is two contexts.**

At its strongest: a prohibition on separate design workshops is about ongoing delivery, and a compressed multi-day workshop is about engagement initiation. Different phases, different instruments, no conflict — and this paper has manufactured a contradiction out of a reading failure.

**Not conceded, and the reason is specific.** The corpus states the prohibition without a phase qualifier and repeats it across documents written for different audiences, and the celebrated compressed workshops sit inside engagements that also describe ongoing delivery. **If the distinction were intended, one of the two would say so, and neither does.** The design's position is that an unstated distinction in a body of practice guidance is not a distinction its readers can act on — but this objection is the strongest available against §4 and a learner who advances it has read well. **The declined fix is to teach the two as separate instruments with separate charters**, declined because doing so would resolve on the learner's behalf the only unresolved question the stage offers them.

**Objection 3: rendering an elicitation as configuration teaches learners that an inspectable protocol is a good protocol.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the §4.5 amendment most deserves: the exercise rewards the properties a file can have — structure, naming, explicit ordering, a recorded removal — and none of those is a property of good discovery. A rigid protocol is worse than a fluent practitioner following a thread, and the interviewing literature this module rests on says exactly that: the guide exists to enable flexibility, not to force every participant through identical wording and order. Worse, a learner who has externalised their questions will follow them, and following them is the failure the corpus itself names when it says that interviewing from a checklist destroys the interview.

**Partially conceded, and this is the amendment's real cost.** Three defences, none complete. The topic is not assessed, so there is no mark to optimise. The hostile-respondent run is designed to make a well-structured protocol fail, which separates structure from usefulness inside the same session. And the required artefact is a **removal with a reason**, which is a claim about judgement rather than about completeness. **The declined fix was to have the learner write the protocol on paper and never configure it** — keeping the reflection and dropping the file — rejected because a paper protocol produces no transcript, and the transcript is the only place where the learner's own questions can be watched failing. **The cost of that rejection is exactly the rigidity this objection names, and prediction 6 is the only thing that would detect it.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A substantial majority of learners will initially state an output that has no failure state. *Instrument:* the opening item 3 answers, coded *observable* / *unobservable* / *absent* by a second reader before instruction.

**Prediction 2.** The strike-out review will remove lines from most first-draft charters, and the lines removed will concentrate on the stated output rather than on the people list. *Instrument:* the struck lines, coded by charter element, collected per learner. **If the review removes nothing, the charter format is already doing the work and the review movement is ceremonial.**

**Prediction 3.** Learners who take the interleaving position will produce narrower necessary-people lists than those who take the concentrated-instrument position. *Instrument:* the named-authority count on each charter, against the position taken. **If the two are indistinguishable, the position paragraph is decorative and should be replaced by a direct question.**

**Prediction 4.** The venue argument will be the most frequently omitted charter element. *Instrument:* presence or absence of a derived venue consequence, per submitted charter.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of first protocols will retain at least one question inviting the customer to endorse the practitioner's solution category, despite step 5 naming that class first. *Instrument:* the exported topics, coded *endorsement question present* / *absent* by a second reader. **This predicts the failure of step 5's countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 6.** Learners who built a topic will follow their own question order more rigidly in the [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md) session than learners who did not, as judged by the number of unplanned follow-ups pursued. *Instrument:* the assessor's tally of pursued threads, split within cohort on whether a topic was exported. **This is the direct test of §8's third objection and it is the prediction this paper would least like to lose.**

**Prediction 7.** Learners instructed to compare their item-5 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 4) will produce charters whose stated output names a currency more often than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* a second reader's binary judgement, split within cohort on exercise-4 completion. **The same prediction is made at [WP-010](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-010.md), so a null across both would indict the comparison pattern rather than either session.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That an instrument requires a stated output before a procedure; that the customary output of a discovery workshop has no failure state; that commitment is identifiable by what somebody gave up; and that the ordering of an elicitation is a design decision nobody has been making explicitly.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The corpus supplying the practice reports no failed workshop and no comparison, and cannot be checked by any public reader. The commitment distinction comes from a practitioner handbook with no controlled evidence. The design-research material establishes mechanism and instructs readers not to convert it into magnitudes. The learning mechanisms behind the session's structure are class 2. The platform documentation accurately describes what a product does and **supplies no evidence that using it produces better engagements.** The venue ordering is one practitioner's judgement.

**The concession worth remembering.** Alignment is real work and this session cannot measure it either. The difference between the session's position and the position it rejects is that the session says so. And note §4.5: for its first version this session taught that unstated specifications never improve while leaving the learner's own unstated specification exactly where it found it. **The failure the session names is one the session committed.**
