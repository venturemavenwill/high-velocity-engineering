# WP-008 — When Simpler Technology Would Do

**Seminar day:** [S008](/wiki/seminars/S008.md) · **Module:** [M02](/wiki/modules/M02.md) · **Quarter:** [Q1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 2
**Platform anchor:** The serverless agents runtime, Foundry hosted agents and Copilot Studio compared for one requirement — and the session-scoped compute, capacity, guardrail and evaluation costs an agent carries once it exists

> ## Release rule
>
> **§§4, 5 and 9 are post-session reading.** §4 explains why the learner argues both sides, why the agent is used twice, and why a composition check was added to the artefact after the first version shipped without one. §5 names the failure the session is designed to produce. §9 gives the predicted cohort behaviour. §§1, 2, 3 and 8 spoil nothing.
>
> **The reason is that the session's generation phase is item 4** — what the simple option would fail at, observably. A learner who has read §5 knows in advance that a generality is the predicted failure, and will produce a specific answer without having discovered that they were about to produce a vague one.

> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Sufficiency** | Whether a candidate option resolves the stated difficulty, judged against the difficulty rather than against other options |
> | **Estate** | What the customer already runs, licences or has built, as distinct from what they might acquire |
> | **Run cost** | Everything the option consumes after it works: operation, evaluation, correction and eventual removal |
> | **Zero-shot** | Applied to a domain and task with no in-domain training data — the condition almost every deployment is actually in |
> | **Orchestrator ownership** | Whether you or the product decides what the system does next; the fork that sets model choice, extensibility and lifecycle tooling together |
> | **Initiation model** | Whether the system can act without being asked. Reactive and proactive systems have different failure modes and different governance requirements |
> | **Idle cost** | What is paid when nothing is happening — the axis that separates serverless, container and credit-based hosting |
> | **Composition** | Serving different components of one system from different build paths, joined over a protocol |

## In plain terms

An openly published engineering playbook, written by a customer-embedded organisation inside a large technology vendor, opens its responsible-AI assessment with a question that reads like an insult to the people answering it: can this be solved without AI, and would simpler technology do?

It is a good question in the wrong hands. The person asked to answer it is the person whose engagement, budget and professional interest depend on the answer being no. That is not a character defect; it is the structure of the situation, and it does not improve with seniority.

The session's response is not to appeal to honesty. It is to require the simple option to be written well enough to be built, and to require its failure to be named specifically enough that somebody could watch it happen. **An option you have described properly is much harder to dismiss than one you have summarised**, and that asymmetry is the only lever the session has.

The revision this paper defends adds a second half. Once something more than a form and a report is genuinely needed, the question becomes *how much more*, and that has an answer with axes: who owns the orchestration, where the compute lives, what is paid for when nothing is happening, whether the thing can act unprompted, and where it may be distributed. Those axes are readable from vendor documentation today and they are decided at the start of a project and binding at the end.

## Abstract

This paper defends placing a sufficiency test inside qualification rather than inside design, and defends extending it from a binary — simple or sophisticated — to a comparison across named axes.

The argument runs in five parts. **First**, the choice between a simple and a sophisticated option is usually made before it is examined, and the examination is conducted by the party least able to conduct it neutrally; the countermeasure is procedural rather than motivational. **Second**, the retrieval literature supplies this stage's strongest external warrant for taking a simple option seriously: a lexical ranking function derived from stated probabilistic assumptions remains a robust baseline across dissimilar domains under zero-shot conditions, while the architectures with the best average performance are the most computationally expensive and the efficient learned ones generalise least well. **Third**, the property a sufficiency finding claims is generalisation, and a demonstration on a familiar corpus measures fit — which is why the sophisticated option always wins a demo. **Fourth**, the sophisticated option inherits the failure surfaces of both its components, and its evaluation cost is open-ended by construction because validation of such a system is feasible only during operation. **Fifth**, the build-path decision resolves into axes that outlive the products, and the vendor's own comparison states that the options are not mutually exclusive — which converts the question from *which platform* to *which platform per component*.

The paper records an amendment made while writing it: the sufficiency finding as first designed asked for a single verdict, in a domain where the documentation it cites explicitly states that the options compose. The correction is documented at §4.5 rather than applied silently.

The paper concedes that the claim engineers systematically over-select sophistication is asserted rather than shown; that the session's use of an agent to argue both sides demonstrates a property of the instrument and measures nothing; that a determined learner can satisfy every element of the artefact while having decided in advance; and that the platform comparison risks producing learners who can navigate a product catalogue rather than reason about architecture.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Qualification has produced a problem statement, a constraint register and a map of who is affected. None of that answers the question of whether the engagement, as scoped, is the proportionate response — and the proportionate response is frequently something nobody in a delivery organisation is paid to propose.

The specific failure is not that engineers choose sophisticated options. It is that **the simple option is never described well enough to lose fairly.** It appears in a sentence, is dismissed in a clause, and the record afterwards shows that alternatives were considered.

There is a second failure, quieter and more common in long-lived estates: nobody establishes what the customer already runs. A capability already licensed, already operated and already understood by the customer's own staff is a different proposition from an equivalent capability newly introduced, and the difference does not appear in a feature comparison.

The third problem is the one this revision addresses. Even a learner who correctly concludes that something more is needed then makes a build decision — and makes it, in practice, by naming a product. **A decision made by naming a product cannot be revisited, because nothing about it was written down that a later change of circumstances could contradict.** A decision made on axes can: if the requirement stops needing proactive initiation, or starts needing external distribution, the finding says what that changes.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Either**, plus the problem restatement from [S003](/wiki/seminars/S003.md) and the constraint register from [S006](/wiki/seminars/S006.md).

The session depends on the register in a way that is easy to miss. Whether a structured form is available at all depends on whether the record's field structure was found to be fluid, so a learner who classified carelessly at S006 will produce a sufficiency finding about an option that is not permitted.

The retrieval material has a split entry profile. Learners who have built search systems will know BM25 as a default in a library and may not know it is derived; learners who have not will assume lexical matching was superseded by embeddings. **Neither group arrives holding the finding that matters — that the cheap method is the one which does not collapse out of domain**, and both groups resist it for different reasons.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is low and the difficulty is motivational for the first half of the session and informational for the second. The design spends its surplus on making the unattractive option expensive to caricature, then on a comparison table whose axes are unfamiliar. The order matters: a learner who meets the build-path comparison before committing to a simple option will use the comparison to justify skipping it.

**3.2 Why the simple option must be described to a buildable standard.** An argument is harder to dismiss in proportion to its specificity, and a summarised alternative can be rejected without engaging with it. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted here or anywhere in this wiki.**

**3.3 Why the learner argues both sides.** Constructing the strongest case for a position one does not hold produces a different relationship to it than evaluating that position from outside. This is the same device used at [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md) for an absent party's objection, applied here to an option rather than to a person.

**3.4 Why the agent is used twice.** Asked to strengthen a case it will strengthen it; asked to undermine the same case it will undermine it; both outputs are fluent, and the learner has to choose. That demonstration is the session's cheapest available evidence for a claim [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) makes and does not prove: an instrument that agrees with you has not been used. **The documentation publishes no measure of how consistently these systems argue opposite sides of the same question**, which is why the learner records which argument persuaded them rather than which the agent produced.

**3.5 The retrieval case is this stage's strongest external warrant, and it is the only place in Stage 1 where a real literature says the simple thing is competitive.**

This matters disproportionately, because everything else in Stage 1 is design reasoning. Here there is something else.

BM25 is not a heuristic that happens to work. The probabilistic relevance framework derives it from stated assumptions: ranking by decreasing probability of relevance, a binary independence model from which inverse document frequency falls out once you assume no relevance information, a latent-property argument that makes the term-frequency response saturating rather than linear, and a length-normalisation term that exists because a long document may be long through verbosity or through scope and the two want opposite treatment ([research/02-technical-foundations/robertson-zaragoza-bm25-and-beyond.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/robertson-zaragoza-bm25-and-beyond.md)). **Every component answers a stated modelling question, which is why it can be argued with rather than merely used.**

BEIR supplies the empirical half and its design is the argument. Retrieval improvements had been measured overwhelmingly on a small number of datasets and most often on the one a model was trained on, which cannot distinguish a model that has learned retrieval from one that has learned a corpus. The benchmark assembles deliberately dissimilar datasets and evaluates systems across lexical, sparse, dense, late-interaction and re-ranking families under zero-shot conditions ([research/02-technical-foundations/thakur-beir.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/thakur-beir.md)). Its findings, as direction: **BM25 is a robust baseline** — not best, and it does not collapse on any domain. Re-ranking and late-interaction achieved the best average zero-shot performance at high computational cost. Dense and sparse models were more efficient and often underperformed, which the authors characterise as considerable room for improvement in generalisation.

The transferable form is not about retrieval. **A method whose worst case is acceptable is operationally different from a method whose average is higher and whose worst case is unknown**, and a sufficiency finding is a claim about worst cases rather than about averages.

**Both sources carry limits that must travel with them.** The monograph is paywalled and this repository did not retrieve its full text, so its section structure and parameter treatment are reconstructed from the wider literature rather than read; volume and page details need re-checking before formal citation. BEIR is a 2021 snapshot whose specific rankings have changed, whose eighteen datasets are a sample rather than a map, whose constituent relevance judgements differ in density and construction, and which has since become a target of optimisation — weakening its own zero-shot premise over time. **No score, ratio or magnitude from either is used anywhere in this session or this paper.**

**3.6 Why generalisation rather than fit is the property being claimed.**

BEIR's methodological complaint transfers directly, and it is the reason the session requires the query set to be written before any result is seen.

A well-chosen set of example queries against a familiar corpus will make almost any configuration look competent. That is not dishonesty; it is the ordinary consequence of choosing examples after seeing behaviour. A sufficiency finding whose case against the simple option rests on a demonstration has measured fit and reported it as capability — **which is precisely the error BEIR exists to correct, made at engagement scale instead of at benchmark scale.**

**3.7 What the sophisticated option inherits, and why its run cost cannot be bounded in advance.**

A retrieval-augmented system inherits the limitations of both of its components: vocabulary mismatch, ranking failure and incomplete recall from the retrieval half; hallucination, instruction-following failure and context sensitivity from the generation half ([research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md)). The compound failure surface is larger than either component's.

Two of that report's conclusions belong inside a run-cost estimate rather than inside a design discussion. **Validation is only feasible during operation**, because the space of queries, documents and interactions cannot be adequately anticipated before deployment. And **robustness evolves rather than being designed in.** Together they mean the evaluation line in a cost estimate is open-ended by construction — not because nobody has estimated it, but because the quantity is not the kind that can be estimated from the design.

There is a second inheritance that bounds what the sophisticated option can be asked to do at all. Kambhampati's argument is that large language models do not plan in the sense the word usually implies ([research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md)). A sufficiency argument whose crux is that the system will work out the sequence of steps itself is resting on the capability most likely to be absent and least likely to be exposed by a demonstration.

**3.8 Why the comparison is on axes rather than on products.**

The vendor comparison family is unusually good raw material precisely because it is explicit about its own axes ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). The top-level split is by service model with selection criteria stated as technical capabilities, timeline and control requirements. Beneath it sit distinctions that decide more than they appear to: whether you own the orchestrator; whether the agent can act without being asked; where the compute lives and what is paid for at rest; and whether the build choice permits the distribution route the project will eventually need.

One published statement carries more weight than the rest, and the session teaches it as the most important line in the comparison: Copilot Studio is documented as designed for small-scale deployments, such as department-level solutions. **A tool used outside its published scale envelope fails through governance and lifecycle rather than through function** — it keeps working, and the estate around it stops being manageable. That is a failure mode a feature comparison cannot show.

The documentation family is also candid in ways vendor material rarely is, listing limited fine-grained control over API calls and response formatting, less integrated professional-developer tooling, an older Adaptive Card schema ceiling, and slower feature availability as costs of the low-code path.

**Its limits are severe.** It was read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches, so its structure is reconstructed and several tables were truncated. It compares Microsoft options against each other and not against alternatives. **It offers no adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no evidence that its recommended mappings produce better outcomes.** It is the most perishable material in this repository's research set, and a limitation recorded today may be closed tomorrow.

**3.9 The counterweight: your own judgement of what is sufficient is unreliable in a known direction.**

Capability in these systems is jagged — the boundary between competence and failure is uneven and does not track how hard a task feels to a person ([research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md](/research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md)). **That note rests on an abstract, so its shape is usable and no number in it is.**

The consequence for a sufficiency finding is specific: intuitions about which tasks the sophisticated option will handle are being formed against a boundary whose shape is not the shape of perceived difficulty. Which means a finding that rests on judgement rather than on observation is resting on the faculty least suited to the question.

**3.10 The keeping cost is now documented in enough detail to be priced, and pricing it is often the argument for not building.**

§3.7 argues that the run cost of a compound retrieval system is open-ended by construction. That argument was made from a retrieval experience report, and it was the strongest available at the time. The agent build-and-runtime documentation now supplies the same conclusion from the platform's own side, in five separate places, with mechanisms rather than intuitions attached ([research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-agent-build-and-runtime.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-agent-build-and-runtime.md)).

**Compute.** A hosted agent scales per session rather than per replica, with no replica count and no warm pool; the processor and memory configured describe **one** session while billing runs across all active sessions, so an oversized default is multiplied by concurrency. The published right-sizing procedure is empirical — run a representative workload and read the telemetry — which means the figure is unavailable at the moment the sufficiency decision is taken. **That is a genuinely new fact for this session: a cost that cannot be estimated in advance is not a cost that a build-time comparison can carry.**

**Capacity.** Model capacity is allocated per subscription, per region and per model, and an increase carries no published response time, approval rate or appeal route, with a prioritisation rule favouring existing consumption ([research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-control-plane-and-governance.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-control-plane-and-governance.md)). The simple option has no such dependency, and the asymmetry belongs in the comparison rather than in a risk annex.

**Guardrails.** Agent guardrails are preview, offer no annotate-only mode, and **fully override the underlying model's guardrail rather than composing with it**, so a stage the agent's configuration does not name is a stage nobody scans — the documentation's own worked example leaves the tool call and the tool response unscanned while the model's stricter setting is in place. Keeping that correct is a standing obligation with an owner, which is a governance cost of exactly the kind §3.8 says a feature comparison cannot show.

**Evaluation.** Continuous evaluation samples at a configurable rate and silently skips runs at an hourly ceiling; conversation-level evaluation draws from a bounded lookback using either uniform sampling or an undocumented service-managed heuristic biased toward interesting conversations. **A score produced that way has a denominator nobody can state.** And the platform ships an instrument for collecting human judgements while publishing no agreement, correlation or calibration between any built-in evaluator and those judgements — an absence the source records as **confirmed by a dedicated search rather than assumed.** This paper asserts none, and the standing prohibition is strengthened rather than weakened by the confirmation.

**Retention and lifecycle.** Traces carry inputs, outputs, tool arguments, tool results and planning steps; the portal exposes a bounded window; **the platform owns no retention period at all**, delegating it to a monitoring resource somebody must configure, own and pay for. Every deployed model carries a retirement date set at launch, notice reaches subscription owners rather than the delivery team, and dedicated capacity is never auto-upgraded.

**The design's claim is that these five belong in the sufficiency finding rather than in an operating model written later**, because each of them is a cost the simple option does not carry and none of them appears in a build-path comparison. The session's second workflow pass exists to make the contrast physical: **the simple option's keeping file is mostly empty and the sophisticated option's is mostly `unknown`**, which is a different and usually more decisive comparison than the one the build table produced. That claim is design reasoning and is marked as such.

**The source's limits are the most severe in this paper.** Read 2026-07-31; four items read in full and roughly thirty pages through search-returned excerpts, so structures are reconstructed and no list is exhaustive. Preview status is pervasive and load-bearing. **Four first-party surfaces disagreed about the agent definition file on the day of reading, one formally deprecated and all rendering** — which is why the session forbids citing any of it from a single page. Every number in it is forbidden downstream, and none appears here.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The movement sequence.** Situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits. The simple option is committed in workflow step 1, before the build-path comparison is available, because a comparison table is an excellent instrument for skipping a question.

**4.2 The middle part of the artefact carries the assessment weight.** The simple option's failure must be observable — something a person could witness — rather than a property claim about scale, flexibility or future need.

**The reason is that unobservable failures cannot be wrong.** A simple option rejected because it would not scale has been rejected on a prediction nobody will ever check, and the rejection is therefore free. A simple option rejected because a named category of record cannot be expressed in a fixed form, and here is an instance of one, has been rejected on something that could turn out to be false.

**4.3 The double use of the agent is sequenced after the learner's own case is committed**, for the same reason as at [S003](/wiki/seminars/S003.md): a case written after reading a fluent argument cannot be distinguished from one written before.

**4.4 What the platform workflow is for.** Not product knowledge. It exists to make one thing observable that prose cannot: **that "simpler" resolves into named axes as soon as you are forced to fill a table**, and that a comparison a learner believed they had made in their head was a comparison on one axis with the others assumed.

The baseline step has a second purpose that is worth stating. Standing up a lexical index over the actual corpus is the only figure in the finding that nobody can argue with, and it costs an afternoon. **A learner who skips it has produced a sufficiency finding with no floor**, and every later comparison in that finding is flattered by the absence.

**4.5 A defect found in this session's own artefact, and the amendment.**

The sufficiency finding as first designed asked for three things: the simple option, its observable failure, and the capability that justifies more. It produced a single verdict.

**The documentation the session cites says, in terms, that the options are not mutually exclusive**, and gives a worked example of one build path exposing tools that another consumes over a protocol. A finding that issues one verdict for a whole system is therefore answering a question the source it rests on says is the wrong one — and in the commonest real case, where part of a system genuinely is served by a form and a report while another part needs orchestration, the single-verdict form makes the correct answer unwriteable.

The amendment adds a `composition_check` to the artefact and a fifth part to the required deliverable. **It is recorded rather than silently applied**, because the defect is instructive in a particular way: the session had read the source closely enough to quote its scale envelope and not closely enough to notice that its central structural claim invalidated the artefact's shape. **Reading for facts is not reading for design.** Assume there are others this paper has not caught.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The well-dressed foregone conclusion.** The most likely outcome is a learner who describes the simple option adequately and locates its failure in a generality. *Countermeasure:* the review movement's single question — could you recognise this failure if you saw it — which catches the generality and not the learner who has invented a specific one.

**Decided in advance, documented afterwards.** A learner who has already chosen produces a compliant artefact. *Countermeasure:* none. The specification cannot distinguish a fair comparison from a well-executed imitation of one, and this is stated rather than concealed.

**Simplicity as its own virtue.** A learner over-corrects and recommends the simple option regardless of whether it resolves the difficulty. *Countermeasure:* the finding is judged against the problem statement from [S003](/wiki/seminars/S003.md), not against a preference for restraint, and an option that leaves the stated difficulty in place fails on those grounds.

**The skipped baseline.** A learner asserts that lexical retrieval would be insufficient without standing one up. *Countermeasure:* workflow step 2 requires it and requires the queries to be written first. This is the newest countermeasure in the session and prediction 5 in §9 predicts its failure.

**Catalogue navigation.** A learner produces a fluent product comparison and cannot say which axis mattered. *Countermeasure:* the artefact requires an axis to be named before options are compared, and the rejected options carry reasons. **A learner who names the axis after filling the table has inverted the exercise**, and nothing detects that.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md) and [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md). The run-cost work begun here is the input to the do-nothing statement, and a sufficiency finding in favour of the simple option is one of the two legitimate grounds on which a refusal at [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) can rest without any party being at fault.

The idle-cost axis introduced here is the direct handover to [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md), where capacity that is held whether or not it is used becomes the worked example. **This session establishes that hosting model is a cost axis; the next establishes that a commitment to capacity is a financial position with its own failure modes.**

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence and no specification element of its own. The finding is carried into [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md), where a refusal grounded in sufficiency must show the simple option described to a buildable standard.

The build-path comparison is deliberately not assessed. **A comparison marked is a comparison optimised for the marker**, and the axis-naming step — the only part that carries judgement — is precisely the part a learner would supply last if a mark depended on the table being complete.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the claim that engineers over-select sophistication is folklore, and the session is built on it.**

At its strongest: no source in this programme measures option selection; the intuition is widely shared and widely shared intuitions about professional bias are frequently wrong; and the opposite failure — a customer left with an under-powered solution that has to be rebuilt — is at least as expensive and is not addressed anywhere in this session.

**Conceded without qualification.** The claim is asserted and not shown, and [S008](/wiki/seminars/S008.md) says so in its own limits section. The narrower defence is that the session does not require the simple option to win; it requires it to be described. That requirement is defensible on grounds of comparison quality alone, independent of whether any bias exists. **The declined fix was to add a symmetric requirement that the sophisticated option also be argued against**, rejected on time grounds — which is a scheduling argument standing in for a design one, and it is a weakness.

**Objection 2: the session's question comes from a governance instrument, and moving it into qualification lets a delivery team answer it before anyone independent sees it.**

At its strongest: the published assessment asks the question inside a review process with other people present. Asking it in week two, privately, among people with an interest, is the least reliable available placement — and it may create the appearance that the question has been handled when it has not.

**Conceded in part.** The design's position is that asking early does not discharge the governance obligation and is not claimed to; Stage 6 runs the assessment properly and with other parties present. The rebuttal is that the question asked only at governance is asked after the architecture exists, at which point the honest answer has become expensive. Asking twice is worse than asking once well and better than asking once late. **That ordering claim is this design's own and is untested.**

**Objection 3: teaching a vendor's product comparison produces catalogue navigators, not engineers.**

At its strongest, and the source's own note makes this objection against itself: teaching this material risks producing students who can navigate a product catalogue rather than reason about architecture. The products will be renamed within the programme's own duration. A graduate who can recite which path publishes to which store has learned something with a shorter shelf life than the module that taught it.

**Conceded, and it drives the session's structure rather than being answered by it.** The axes are extracted explicitly and the products reintroduced as instances; the perishability table states every durable claim without a product name; the artefact requires an axis before it permits an option. **The declined fix was to teach the axes with no products at all**, rejected because an axis with no instance is not memorable and because the department-scale statement — a vendor publishing a limit on its own product — is a specific and unusually instructive fact that no abstract treatment could supply. The cost is that some learners will retain the catalogue and not the axes, and nothing here detects that.

**Objection 4: the retrieval evidence is a snapshot and the session leans on it as though it were settled.**

At its strongest: BEIR's rankings have changed since publication, its benchmark has become an optimisation target which undermines its own zero-shot premise, its datasets are a sample of retrieval tasks rather than a map, and its constituent relevance judgements are not strictly comparable to one another. Using a four-year-old benchmark to tell a learner that the cheap method is competitive risks teaching a fact that has expired.

**Partially conceded.** The rebuttal is that the session uses the paper's *methodology and trade-off structure* and not its rankings — specifically the argument that single-dataset evaluation cannot detect the failure it is most likely to have, and the observation that efficiency and generalisation were in tension. Those are claims about experimental design, and they do not expire when a leaderboard moves. **The declined fix was to drop the retrieval material and keep the session purely procedural**, rejected because it would remove the only external warrant in the entire stage and leave Stage 1 as design reasoning throughout. **That is a judgement about the value of one real citation against the risk of its misuse, and it is presented as one.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first findings will state the simple option's failure as a property claim rather than as an observable event. *Instrument:* the stated failure, coded *observable* / *property claim* / *absent* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most estate columns will be empty or filled from memory rather than from anything the customer supplied. *Instrument:* the estate entries, coded by whether a source is named.

**Prediction 3.** Learners who found the agent's strengthening argument more persuasive than its undermining argument will revise their finding more often than those who found the reverse. *Instrument:* the learner's recorded judgement of the two agent outputs against the presence of a revision. **If this fails, the double-agent device demonstrates nothing about instrument use** and should be replaced by a written pair of cases produced by peers.

**Prediction 4.** Most build-path comparisons will differ on fewer axes than the artefact provides, and the axis most often left identical across all three options will be initiation. *Instrument:* the completed comparison tables, coded for the number of axes on which the options actually differ.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of learners will complete a sufficiency finding without standing up the lexical baseline, and will nonetheless assert that a simpler retrieval approach would be insufficient. *Instrument:* presence of a reproducible baseline in the artefact, against the presence of an insufficiency claim about retrieval. **This predicts the failure of the countermeasure added at §4.4**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 6.** Learners instructed to compare their item-4 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 3) will produce observable failure statements more often than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* a second reader's binary judgement, split within cohort on exercise-3 completion.

**Prediction 7.** Findings that answer the composition check will more often recommend a mixed outcome — simple for one component, sophisticated for another — than findings produced before the §4.5 amendment. *Instrument:* the verdict form, coded *single* / *mixed*, across cohorts before and after the amendment. **A null result would mean the composition check is a field learners fill rather than a question they answer.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the sufficiency question is asked by the wrong person at the wrong time; that the available correction is procedural rather than motivational; and that once something more is genuinely needed, the decision resolves into axes that outlive every product name attached to them.

**What the evidence licenses.** That the question is asked first in a published assessment regime is recorded from a complete reading. That a lexical baseline is robust across dissimilar domains under zero-shot conditions, while the best-performing families are the most expensive and the efficient learned ones generalise least well, is a published empirical finding — **and it is a snapshot, and no number from it appears here.** That specificity makes an argument harder to dismiss is direction and mechanism only. That engineers over-select sophistication is this design's assertion and nothing else.

**The concession worth remembering.** This session makes the simple option harder to caricature. It does nothing whatever about a learner who decided before they arrived. And note §4.5: the design read its own source closely enough to quote a scale envelope and not closely enough to notice that the same page invalidated the artefact's shape. **Reading for facts is not reading for design.**

**The silences that matter.** The comparison family publishes **no adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no evidence that its recommended mappings produce better outcomes** — so its selection guidance is a reasoned framework and not a validated finding, and this session assesses the reasoning only. It states a scale envelope for one product and **for most of the tools it compares it states none at all**, which is why the artefact records an absence rather than inferring a limit. It does not say what happens when a tool is used outside its envelope; the claim that the symptoms are governance and lifecycle rather than functional is this repository's own analysis and is marked as such in the source note. It publishes **no measure of how consistently an agent argues opposite sides of the same question**, which is exactly the property workflow step 6 depends on — and the reason the learner records which argument persuaded them rather than which the agent produced. And it is silent on the question a sufficiency finding most wants answered: what any of these paths costs to keep running once the build is finished.

**The silences in the keeping-cost material, which answer the previous paragraph's last sentence in a way that is worse than silence.** The build-and-runtime documentation does describe what an agent costs to keep — and every quantity it supplies is either unknowable in advance or unaccompanied by evidence. **The compute figure cannot be known before the workload exists**, because the published sizing method is empirical. **The evaluation denominator cannot be stated at all**: the sample rate is a setting, the hourly ceiling silently skips runs, and the conversation-level sampling heuristic is described as service-managed and biased toward interesting traces and **is documented nowhere further**, so a sample drawn by an undocumented rule has no reportable coverage. **No agreement, correlation or calibration between any built-in evaluator and human judgement is published**, an absence the source records as confirmed by dedicated search rather than assumed, and which this repository continues to forbid asserting. **No measure of tracing completeness exists** — a trace is presented as a record and never as a sample, and no page states the difference. **No evidence is published that continuous evaluation catches anything a manual review would not.** **No service level, response time or approval rate is published for a capacity increase.** And the fine-tuning guidance is the sharpest case of all: floors, starting points, production ranges and an asserted relationship between dataset size and quality are published **with no source, study or curve behind any of them.** Guidance without warrant is a different problem from silence and arguably a worse one, which is why the session keeps the ordering and discards every number.
