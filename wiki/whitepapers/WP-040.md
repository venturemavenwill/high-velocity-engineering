# WP-040 — Staying in the Problem: An Endurance Exercise

**Seminar day:** [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md) · **Module:** [M08](/wiki/modules/M08.md) · **Quarter:** [Q4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md)
**Strands:** PE · CF · SY · ME · **Complexity class:** 3
**Platform anchor:** the substrate's API Management gateway and the quota constraint behind it, unresolved · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read-only, deliberately) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends devoting an assessed six-hour day to a problem nobody will solve, and defends requiring students to record the answer they already hold before the day begins. The first argument is that premature convergence is the dominant failure in requirements work, that it is invisible because it produces confident progress on a well-executed wrong thing, and that the capacity to remain in an unresolved problem is a trainable tolerance rather than a technique — which means it must be practised under conditions where converging is not available. The second argument is that a characterisation written by a student who already held a conclusion is indistinguishable from one derived from evidence, unless the prior conclusion was recorded beforehand; item 1 makes the comparison possible and phase 7 performs it. The paper additionally defends giving the cost of endless analysis equal weight to the cost of premature convergence, and defends the day's single platform decision — that the evidence set contains a working agent-built implementation of the request, and that the day ships nothing itself — on the ground that the module's whole subject is best examined by putting the tempting answer in the room rather than by keeping it out. It concedes that the day cannot distinguish a student who stayed in the problem from one who was unable to leave it, that its entrustment judgement is made on a performance whose most important quality is invisible, and that supplying a running implementation on an assessment day is the riskiest thing this module does.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

An engineer holds a problem. At some point they stop analysing and start building, and that transition is made on a feeling — the problem seems understood, the remaining ambiguity seems tolerable, and there is work to do.

The feeling is uninformative. It arrives at the same subjective intensity whether the problem is understood or whether the engineer has simply run out of patience, and it arrives *earlier* for more experienced engineers, because experience supplies plausible answers faster. When it arrives too early the consequence is not visible failure but competent execution of the wrong thing, discovered weeks later, at which point the analysis that would have prevented it is no longer affordable.

The capacity being trained is therefore not analytical. It is a **tolerance** — the ability to remain in an unresolved state past the point where resolution becomes attractive — and tolerances are trained by exposure rather than by instruction. Hence the day's shape: a real problem, staff's abandoned approaches supplied so the easy moves are gone, six hours, and no proposals permitted.

The day's second problem is verification. A student who converged in the first hour and spent five more assembling support produces a characterisation indistinguishable from one derived from the evidence. The only way to tell is to know what they believed beforehand, which is why item 1 exists and why it is sealed. **Every student has an answer before the day starts**; asking for it is what makes phase 7's comparison possible and what makes the specification's failure clause enforceable.

The third problem is the day's most serious risk of being wrong. An exercise arguing for sustained analysis, delivered to students who will shortly enter an industry that rewards shipping, could produce engineers who will not decide. Point 3 gives the cost of endless analysis equal weight to the cost of premature convergence, and point 4 supplies the stopping criterion: **you may stop analysing when further evidence would not change what you do — not when you feel confident.** That is the day's single most transferable sentence and it is the only one the retrieval schedule carries.

**The fourth problem is what four days of this module have made unavoidable.** Students have spent the week learning that a request is a conclusion, and they have watched an agent implement one. On this day they will hold a real unsolved problem for six hours with the ability to build sitting in their hands. The design has two options and both are uncomfortable: pretend the ability is not there, or put its output in the room and make it evidence.

It puts it in the room. The evidence set contains an implementation of the request, built by an agent from the request alone, deployed and running, with its one-line prompt record attached. Nobody in the room made it and nobody is asked to endorse it. **The day's hardest question is why a working implementation is not an answer**, and it is a question that cannot be asked without the implementation — the alternative is an instructor asserting that the obvious solution would not have worked, on a problem where nobody knows.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge assumptions

- **Four days of module apparatus:** request analysis, observation, vocabulary, and constraint negotiation.
- **A four-day solution prohibition,** which ends at phase 8.
- **The S030 entrustment experience,** ten weeks old, including the scale, the narrative requirement and the returned divergent narratives.
- **Three external judgements in three days** — the user at S037, the expert at S038, the requester and capacity owner at S039 — and a fourth arriving at phase 4.
- **Four days of building with an agent against this platform,** and one clear finding from them: it produces working output on this class of task. The day depends on the room believing that, and the room has evidence.
- **Three encounters with the same measurement failure** — S037, S039 and now phase 6 — which the design treats as one of the day's two retrieval spines.
- **No experience of an unsolvable problem.** Every problem in the programme so far has had an answer somebody held.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Low intrinsic, high sustained.** Nothing on the day is technically difficult. The evidence is supplied, the apparatus is four days old, and the required output is prose.

What is demanding is duration under an unresolved state, which is a different load from any the programme has imposed. The nearest analogue is S037's non-intervention, and this is longer and less structured.

Management:

- **No new content.**
- **All evidence supplied,** including staff's abandoned approaches and the agent's implementation — which removes both the gathering work and the false starts.
- **Individual work at phase 3,** because group work produces premature consensus faster than individual work produces premature conclusions.
- **A required written output at the hour's end,** which gives the unresolved state a container.
- **The defence questions published,** including the requester's.
- **Consolidation after assessment,** as at S030.
- **The server is read-only,** which removes an entire category of available action rather than asking students not to take it.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Recording the answer you already hold,* item 1, sealed.
- *Predicting your own convergence point,* item 3.
- *Predicting what an agent would build from the request alone,* item 5.
- *Staff's abandoned approaches,* which remove the easy moves.
- *A running implementation of the request,* which removes the last one.
- *The fifth defence question* — what is wrong with the thing in front of us.
- *The requester's sixth question* — why have you not given me an answer.
- *The prior-belief comparison,* phase 7.
- *The boundary item at phase 2,* naming what the module would not have surfaced.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Any proposal.* The prohibition holds until phase 8.
- *A build.* Discussed at §4 and conceded at §8.
- *Group problem-solving.* Individual throughout phase 3.
- *A solvable problem.* Refused; a problem with an answer would reward finding it.
- *Time pressure.* The hour is generous and is meant to feel long.
- *A structured characterisation template.* The four required parts are stated and no form is supplied, deliberately — a template would supply the containment the student must generate.

**Scope on the stopping criterion, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** Point 4's criterion — stop when further evidence would not change what you do — is stated as the day's transferable rule. **Safe** where the decision space is known and the cost of further evidence is bounded, which is this day's situation and most engineering situations. **Unsafe** where the decision itself is what is under construction, since a criterion referring to what you do presupposes that the space of actions is settled; in that case the criterion is circular and the honest answer is that the day supplies none. That case is common in early product work and the programme addresses it nowhere.

**Scope on the platform instance, strong form.** The day uses a running implementation as evidence, and the correspondence needs a boundary because it is the most quotable thing on the page.

*Safe.* Distinguishing what a working system demonstrates — that something is implementable, at what cost, with what side effects — from what it establishes about whether anyone is better off. That distinction is the day's platform content and it holds for any artefact produced by anyone.

*Unsafe* in two directions and the day states both. **It is unsafe as a claim that fast implementations are suspect.** Speed is not evidence of anything about correctness, in either direction, and the day's point is precisely that the speed removed a signal rather than that it created one. A student who leaves distrusting quick work has learned a heuristic where the day taught the absence of one. **And it is unsafe as a general position on building early.** Building to learn is a legitimate and often correct move; what the day prohibits is building *as an answer* to a problem that has not been characterised, which is a different act with the same appearance. The programme's own S057 and S070 material depends on the first being available, and a student who generalises this day into a prohibition on prototyping has taken a tolerance exercise for a methodology.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **a problem that will still be unsolved tomorrow**.

The staff member who has been working on it opens the error clinic by describing where they converged too early on this exact problem. Students are working on something real, unresolved, and consequential to people they have met — and their characterisations go to staff who are still working on it, which point 9 states.

Item 1's sealed prior belief supplies the day's sharpest personal moment at phase 7. A student who compares what they believed at nine in the morning with what they wrote at three in the afternoon, and finds them substantively identical, has learned something about themselves that no instruction supplies.

**The running implementation supplies the day's most uncomfortable object.** It is not an argument, it does not advocate for itself, and it cannot be dismissed by anyone who spent the week watching agents produce exactly this. A student who wants to converge is not being asked to resist a temptation in the abstract; they are being asked to explain, out loud, to two assessors and the person who wrote the request, why the thing on the screen is not what was needed.

The requester's final question is the day's most instructive: *I asked for something specific; why have you not given me an answer?* It is asked by the person entitled to ask it, it is entirely reasonable, and it is now asked immediately after a question about a working implementation of that same specific thing — which is the hardest ordering available and is deliberate.

The competence risk is unusual: the day has no success state that feels like one. Countermeasures: the expected supervision level is stated in advance; the specification assesses the characterisation's parts rather than its correctness; and phase 8's ending of the prohibition gives the module a closure the day itself withholds.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items. Item 1 is the day's most important and its framing matters: students are told that everyone has an answer and that writing it down is what makes the day honest, rather than being asked whether they have one. Item 3's convergence-point prediction is the behavioural measure. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction**, administered here per instrumentation rule 8, and it is deliberately not a question about the agent's competence — it asks what the request contained, in a form whose answer arrives an hour later.

**Phase 2 (30 min).** Extended cumulative retrieval. The penultimate item — which day produced evidence you now believe is wrong — is unusual and is the module's self-correction check. The boundary item continues the convention from S030 and S035.

**Phase 3 (60 min).** The problem, individually, no proposals. Supplying staff's abandoned approaches is the phase's critical design decision: without it, students spend the hour discovering the obvious moves and converge on the first that survives. With it, they are past the obvious moves in ten minutes and must spend fifty in the unresolved state, which is the exercise. **The agent's implementation extends the same decision by one step**: the most obvious move of all has been made, it works, and a student who wants to converge on it must now argue with a running system rather than with an absent possibility. It is the only item in the evidence set that is operated rather than read, and it is evidence rather than a proposal — a distinction the specification enforces at its second fail clause.

**Three departures from the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](/wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) and the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) are named here.** The day has **no contrasting-case set**, and never had one; the pattern permits the substitution for assessment-bearing days, where phases 5 through 7 are replaced by an entrustment occasion. The day's **platform points sit in phase 5 rather than phase 4**, because this day's phase 4 is a defence and not a consolidation, and delivering them before the defence would supply the answer the defence tests. And **the day produces no build.** The standard requires phase 5 to ship, and this day's phase 5 is post-assessment consolidation with no whole-task phase to carry one; the build requirement is discharged four times across S036 to S039, and the module's argument — set out at [M08](/wiki/modules/M08.md) — is that the one day which ends in nothing is what keeps the other four honest. §8's third objection states the case against this.

**Phase 4 (60 min).** Fifteen-minute defences to two assessors and the requester. **Assessors have read the characterisation and formed a level and a narrative before the defence begins**, per rater discipline rule 7; this is the repair §8 previously named as absent and it is now made. The published questions run from the characterisation to its limits to its origins to its gaps, then to the artefact, then to the requester's. The fifth and sixth are ordered deliberately: the student disposes of the working implementation before facing the person who asked for it, which is harder than the reverse and is the correct order, because a student who has just satisfied a person will not then argue with a machine.

**Phase 5 (35 min).** Nine points, assessors absent. Point 3's equal weighting is the day's honest counterweight. Point 4 is the retained sentence. Point 5's unattributed spread traces differences to evidence weighting, which is the cohort discovering that characterisation is a judgement. **Point 7 is the day's platform reasoning** and its second half is the module's closing claim: an afternoon's implementation feels provisional and a six-week implementation feels committed, neither feeling is about correctness, and the agent has removed a signal engineers were using without knowing it. **Point 8 names a default** and satisfies the standard's requirement.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** Opened by the staff member who converged too early on this problem. This is the strongest available disclosure: not a general failure but a specific one, on the object in front of the room, by the person who owns it. The agent case is the module's third encounter with a single failure — a correct query against a metric whose definition excludes what was being asked — and it is placed last because **its point is that nobody did anything wrong**, which is a conclusion the room can only reach after watching the same failure produce a low-stakes error at S037 and an injustice at S039.

**Phase 7 (25 min).** Prior belief against final characterisation, compared by a partner. Per the [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s rule 3, the outcome is recorded in the artefact rather than left in the conversation — this is the first day designed under that rule.

**Phase 8 (25 min).** Sealed items including item 5, returned narratives with divergences shown, the psychological-safety instrument's fourth administration, **the long-horizon prediction check sealed**, and the prohibition's end. Ending the prohibition explicitly is the module's closure and is the only thing on the day that feels like a resolution.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with product or consulting experience will produce a fluent characterisation and will have converged earliest — experience supplies plausible answers faster, which is the mechanism §1 names. Predicted failure: their item 1 and their final characterisation match, and their evidence section is an assembled justification. Countermeasure: phase 7's comparison, whose outcome is now assessed; and the specification's failure clause, which requires an account of why the day's evidence did not change a prior belief when it did not.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** A student with no ready answer may find phase 3's hour genuinely difficult and produce a thin characterisation. Countermeasures: all evidence is supplied; the four required parts are stated; and the specification assesses parts rather than depth. Residual risk: their statement of what the evidence does not establish may be the strongest part of their artefact and their problem statement the weakest, which passes and is arguably the correct outcome for the day.

**Students who adopt the implementation.** New, and the platform decision's characteristic failure. A student who has spent the week building will read a running system as an answer and will write a characterisation shaped around it. Countermeasures: the specification's second fail clause, which fails an artefact endorsing the implementation; the fifth defence question, which requires them to say what is wrong with it in front of two assessors; and the read-only server, which means the endorsement can never become an action. Residual risk: **a student may produce a characterisation whose problem statement is the implementation's premise restated, which is endorsement in a form the fail clause does not catch**, and phase 7's prior-belief comparison is the only detector — which is weaker here than elsewhere, because the implementation was in the room before item 1 was written about it.

**Phase 3 has no adjudicator.** Unlike every other phase 3 in the programme, nothing resolves. This is the design and it is stated to students. Fallback for a student who genuinely stalls: the instructor supplies a fourth observation record rather than a hint, which restarts the evidence work without supplying a direction.

**Affective failure.** Two, and both are the day's central risks rather than side effects.

The first is **the day read as an argument against deciding**. Countermeasures: point 3's equal weighting; point 4's stopping criterion, which is a permission as much as a restraint; phase 8's explicit lifting of the prohibition; and §3.2's scope statement, delivered at point 7, which states that building to learn is legitimate and that what the day prohibits is building as an answer.

The second is **futility**. A day that produces no solution, on a problem nobody will solve, assessed by people who also do not know the answer, can read as an exercise in performance. **The running implementation makes this risk worse rather than better** — a student can now point at a working system and ask what six hours added. Countermeasures: the characterisations go to staff who are working on it; point 9 says so; Q5 reports whether any student characterisation changed the approach; and the long-horizon check at S050 and S090 gives the day a resolution the day itself cannot supply. The last is the strongest available answer and it arrives a quarter and two years late respectively.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by S046 (where do criteria come from, which is this day's problem with a criterion as the object), S050 (saying what the measurement does not license), S065 (the cost argument you present to someone who pays), S070 (ship or do not ship, where the stopping criterion becomes a release decision), S085 (negotiating scope under constraint) and S090 (the final defence).

S070 at roughly three quarters is the principal target and the correspondence is exact: a ship decision is made when further evidence would not change what you do, and a student who cannot apply that criterion will either ship early or never ship.

**The long-horizon prediction check, and what it commits later days to.** At phase 8 each student seals a prediction of what this problem will turn out to be and what evidence will settle it. Per instrumentation rule 6, the deferral names its occasions: **S050**, where the student states what their own measurement did and did not license about it, and **S090**, where the final defence asks what they got wrong and why. Rule 6 additionally requires the named day to carry an artefact element that consumes the result. **This paper cannot verify that either day does, and says so rather than assuming it**: the check is a commitment those days must discharge, it is recorded here so that a failure to discharge it is legible, and if neither day consumes it then this is an instrument with no reader and rule 9 applies. That is the honest position and it is not a comfortable one on the module's assessment-bearing day.

The retained content is one sentence — point 4's stopping criterion — plus the characterisation's four parts and the distinction at point 7 between what a working system demonstrates and what it establishes. The design deliberately retains almost nothing from this day, because its content is a tolerance rather than a technique.

Own schedule: the inter-quarter spaced sets, then the Q5 report, then S050 and S090.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**Assessment-bearing. The quarter's entrustment judgement**, on *characterise a problem from evidence and defend the characterisation to someone who asked for something else* — a precursor to EA-3, expected level 2a to 2b.

**The S030 judgement is not carried forward**, and this is stated to students. Levels do not accumulate.

Per rule 1, the checked elements are: a stated problem; its supporting evidence; an explicit statement of what the evidence does not establish; a statement of what would change it; **an account of what the agent's implementation does and does not establish**; the phase 7 prior-belief comparison outcome; the absence of a proposed solution; **and the prompt record, intervention record and frontier statement required by the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)**. **Nothing else is assessed**, including whether the characterisation is correct — which nobody knows.

**On the three standard elements at an assessment-bearing day**, because their form here is unusual and is load-bearing. The **prompt record** is supplied rather than produced: one line, the request verbatim, and the student reproduces it and states what it did not contain — which is a shorter and harder task than writing a prompt record of their own. The **intervention record is empty by construction**, since correcting the agent would be proposing and the day forbids it, **so the standard's escape clause is not a formality here but the sharpest element on the page**: the artefact must demonstrate that the student's own characterisation would have detected the error the agent made. A characterisation that could not have caught it fails, whether or not it is otherwise well written. That is the escape clause doing exactly what [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 designed it to do — rewarding the design that would have detected rather than the intervention that happened to occur — in the one setting where no intervention was permitted. The **frontier statement** names one thing the agent did that no student could have done as fast, and one thing it did badly; the second is the same for every student, and the day asks for it precisely because the uniformity is the finding.

**A characterisation substantively identical to the item 1 prior belief, with no account of why the evidence did not change it, fails.** Note the clause's shape: an unchanged belief is not a failure. An unexamined one is.

**A characterisation that endorses the agent's implementation as the answer fails.** The module's standing rule in the form the platform makes available.

Programme instrumentation: **item 4's predicted level against assigned is the second point in the programme's external-calibration series** after S030 and is compared against it; item 3's predicted convergence point against the actual is the tolerance measure; **item 5's two-part accuracy is the module's fourth and last frontier-calibration datum**; the assessor divergence rate is the second point in the rater-reliability series; the S010 instrument's fourth administration continues the psychological-safety series; **the phase 7 prior-belief match rate is the premature-convergence measure** and is the module's headline finding.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day cannot distinguish staying in the problem from being unable to leave it.**

At its strongest: the assessed behaviour is producing a characterisation and no proposal, after six hours, on an unsolvable problem. A student who deliberately resisted convergence and a student who never had anything to converge on produce the same artefact. Worse, the second student will produce a *better* one on the day's own criteria — no prior belief to contradict, no assembled justification, an evidence section unpolluted by a held conclusion — and will be assigned a higher supervision level for a capacity they do not have.

The rebuttal is that item 1 partially separates them. A student with no prior belief writes so; a student who had one and set it aside has a documented prior and a documented account of what changed. The phase 7 comparison then distinguishes three cases rather than two: prior belief held and unexamined, prior belief held and revised, and no prior belief.

The concession is that the third case is not assessed differently from the second, and it should be. **A student who arrives with no view and produces a characterisation has demonstrated less than one who arrived with a view and abandoned it**, and the specification treats them identically. The available repair — requiring the item 1 disclosure to be non-empty, and treating a claim of having had no view as an answer requiring its own account — is small and is not made. This is a genuine validity problem in an entrustment judgement, which is a worse place for it than a specification item. **The re-anchoring does not touch it and slightly worsens it**: the running implementation gives a student with no prior view something to react to, which makes an empty item 1 easier to sustain and harder to detect.

**Objection 2: the entrustment judgement is made on a performance whose most important quality is invisible.**

At its strongest: the day's target is a tolerance exercised privately during phase 3's hour. Assessors observe fifteen minutes of defence, four days after meeting the student's work, on a problem they cannot themselves resolve. What they can assess is how well the student *talks about* their characterisation — which is a communication competence, and a real one, and not the one the day claims to be building. The narrative requirement makes this worse rather than better: an assessor must name a specific observed action, and the only actions available were performed in the defence.

This objection the design does not fully answer. The gap between the trained capacity and the observable evidence is real and is not closed by any mechanism on the day.

Two things narrow it. The characterisation itself is evidence of the hour, not of the defence — an artefact that stops short of a proposal, states its own limits and accounts for a revised prior belief could not be produced by a student who converged at minute ten. And the third defence question — what did you believe before today, and what changed it — probes the hour directly.

**The repair this paper named is now made.** Assessors are instructed to form their level and narrative from the written characterisation before the defence begins, and to use the defence to test it rather than to produce it, and the instruction is stated to students as well as to assessors. That satisfies rater discipline rule 7 and closes what this paper recorded as the second entrustment occasion in the programme to lack it. **What is not closed is the finding behind it**: the design reached two entrustment occasions before anybody wrote the instruction down, which is the same propagation failure instrumentation rule 5 exists to prevent, one level up, and it is recorded rather than treated as repaired.

**Objection 3: putting a working implementation in front of students on an assessment day is the riskiest thing this module does, and its payoff is a question that could have been asked without it.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn and it is the one this paper finds hardest, because the day is assessed.

At its strongest: the entrustment judgement turns on whether a student stayed in an unresolved problem. The design has now placed, in the evidence set, a running implementation of the tempting answer — on the day, under assessment, in front of students who have spent four days learning to build with agents and are about to be told they may propose again in six hours. If it works, the day gets its sharpest question. **If it does not, the design has supplied the convergence it is assessing the absence of, and the failure will be indistinguishable from a cohort that simply converged.** No instrument on the day separates a student who was pulled toward the implementation from a student who would have converged anyway, because both produce a characterisation shaped around the request. And the cost of being wrong is not a weak seminar; it is an entrustment level assigned on a performance the design contaminated.

There is a second edge. The question the implementation makes possible — what is wrong with this working thing — could be asked hypothetically. *Suppose someone had built exactly what was asked. What would still be unsolved?* That costs nothing, carries no contamination risk, and is a defensible way to run the day.

The rebuttal is that the hypothetical is exactly what the module spent four days demonstrating does not work. **A student asked to imagine a working implementation imagines one that has the flaw they already believe in**; a student handed one has to find the flaw in the thing that exists, which is harder, is the professional task, and is the only version that can surprise them. The whole module's evidence — S036's case D, S037's availability metric, S038's generated names, S039's evidence pack — is that the difference between a thing described and a thing operated is where the learning is. Withdrawing that on the assessment day would be withdrawing it precisely where the claim is being tested.

**The objection is conceded on the contamination risk, which the design cannot eliminate.** What it has done is bound it: the implementation is **evidence and not a proposal**, it carries an explicit fail clause for endorsement, the server is read-only so no endorsement can become an act, the defence asks about it directly so an unexamined adoption is exposed under questioning, and the assessors form their level from the artefact where the adoption would be legible in writing.

**The cheap fix that is named and made** is item 5. Sealing a prediction about what the agent would build, before the implementation is seen, gives the day one piece of evidence about each student's relationship to it that is not confounded by having seen it — and it costs one pretest item. **The cheap fix that is named and not made** is running the day both ways across a cohort, half with the implementation and half without, which would settle the objection in one offering. It is not made because it would assign entrustment levels under two different conditions in the same cohort, which the [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) does not permit and should not. **That is a real constraint and it means this objection cannot be resolved empirically at the point where it matters most**, which is stated here rather than left for a reader to notice.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A large majority of item 1 responses will contain a substantive prior belief. *Instrument:* item 1 coded for presence of a specific answer. **A low rate would indicate students are answering strategically, having inferred what the day wants — and under the re-anchoring it would additionally indicate objection 1's worsened case, since the implementation gives an empty item 1 somewhere to hide.**

**Prediction 2.** Item 3's predicted convergence point will be later than the point at which students report wanting to propose. *Instrument:* paired item 3 predictions and phase 6 disclosures.

**Prediction 3.** Phase 7 prior-belief matches will occur for at least a third of students, and the rate will be higher among students with product or consulting backgrounds. *Instrument:* phase 7 outcomes split by the admission background survey. **This is the experience-converges-faster claim stated as a prediction.**

**Prediction 4.** Item 4 predicted levels will be closer to assigned levels than at S030, and the improvement will be larger for students who received divergent narratives at S030. *Instrument:* prediction error at both occasions, conditioned on S030 divergence — **which is the conditional [WP-030](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-030.md) §9 Prediction 4 named, now testable.**

**Prediction 5.** A large majority of item 5 predictions will correctly anticipate what the agent built and a small minority will name the input it lacked. *Instrument:* item 5 coded in two parts against the supplied implementation. **This is the module's fourth instance of the same shape, after [WP-036](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-036.md), [WP-037](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-037.md) and [WP-038](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-038.md) §9 prediction 5 in each case. If all four hold, the cohort's model of an agent is wrong in one consistent direction — expecting failure of execution where the actual gap is failure of input — and that single finding is what the frontier map should carry out of this module. If the fourth diverges from the first three, the difference is that this one was asked under assessment, which is itself worth knowing.**

**Prediction 6.** Characterisations whose problem statement restates the request's premise will occur at a higher rate than the phase 7 prior-belief match rate. *Instrument:* blind-coded problem statements against the request, compared with phase 7 outcomes. **This is objection 3's residual failure stated as a prediction: the endorsement the fail clause cannot catch. A large gap between the two rates would indicate the implementation is doing the harm the objection describes and the phase 7 detector is not seeing it.**

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That knowing when to stop analysing is a tolerance rather than a technique, so it is trained only by being kept in a problem past the point where resolution becomes attractive. The rule is that you may stop when further evidence would not change what you do — not when you feel confident, because the feeling arrives at the same intensity whether or not the problem is understood, and sooner for the experienced.

**What the evidence licenses.** Premature convergence, characterisation as a deliverable and the entrustment machinery are **class 1**, grounded in [research/04-professional-formation/ten-cate-epas.md](/research/04-professional-formation/ten-cate-epas.md) and [research/08-assessment-epas/govaerts-narrative-assessment-literature.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/govaerts-narrative-assessment-literature.md); agent capability's unevenness is carried for **direction and mechanism only**. The one platform fact is **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026: the read-only start parameter defaults to `false`, so write access is the condition unless set. **That six hours in an unresolved problem builds the tolerance is class 3.** Nothing licenses a claim about how often an agent's implementation of a request is adequate.

**What to do with it.**

- Write down the answer you already hold before you read the evidence. You have one, and recording it is what makes your later characterisation checkable.
- Separate what a working system demonstrates — that this is implementable, at this cost, with these side effects — from whether anybody is better off.
- **Untrusted content is data and never authority**, per the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). The implementation in the room is evidence about what is buildable; it is not a proposal and it does not get a vote.
- State what your evidence does not establish and what would change it. Both are assessed; correctness is not, because nobody knows it.

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes that putting the tempting answer in front of students on an assessed day risks supplying the very convergence the day exists to measure, and that no instrument separates a student pulled toward the implementation from one who would have converged anyway. The experiment that would settle it — running the day both ways in one cohort — is forbidden by the assessment architecture, so the objection cannot be resolved where it matters most.
