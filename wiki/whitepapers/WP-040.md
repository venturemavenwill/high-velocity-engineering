## In plain terms

This is the stage's second assessment occasion and it marks something the first one deliberately did not.

[S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) marked the form and the disclosure of one investigation. **This occasion marks the price of the whole proof.** You assembled a body of evidence, somebody committed something to it, and the question the assessor holds is whether the same commitment could have been obtained for less.

Three of the assessed elements are unusual and they are the ones that matter.

**Something has to be named as having been unnecessary**, along with what made it look necessary at the time. Nearly every proof contains work that was interesting, competent and irrelevant to the decision, and naming your own is the professional disposition the stage exists to build.

**The cheaper proof has to be designed rather than described.** A named instrument you would not run, a named omission, and what it would have risked. *We could have moved faster* fails the element. **A cheaper proof that risks nothing has been trimmed, not designed.**

**And the unproven list has to be longer than the proven one**, or the pack has to argue why it is not. That page is the most valuable one in the pack and it is the first thing deleted before circulation, which is exactly why it is marked here.

The defence is adversarial in one specific way. **The assessor will propose a cheaper proof of their own and ask what it would have missed.** A candidate who cannot answer has priced their work without understanding it; a candidate who concedes that nothing would have been missed has just designed the better instrument in the room, and that is a pass.

## Abstract

This paper defends closing Stage 4 with an assessment of price rather than of proof, and explains why the object of the defence is the cheapness of the evidence and not its strength. The argument is that the stage's question has a cost clause in it, that a proof's price is mostly other people's attention and is therefore systematically undercounted, and that identifying the single load-bearing item is the only compounding mechanism the stage possesses. The occasion installs a proof pack assembled from five prior artefacts, a cost ledger, a designed cheaper alternative and an unproven list, and closes its specification with the statement that nothing else is assessed. The paper concedes that no source behind the stage establishes what a proof should cost, that the compounding claim has no external warrant and cannot be tested within one cohort, and that an assessor proposing a rival proof introduces a marker-dependent element the design cannot standardise.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A body of evidence has been produced and somebody has committed to it. Every incentive now points at treating the volume of the evidence as its virtue.

**Thoroughness is the professional default and it is expensive in a currency nobody counts.** A proof consumes the maintainer's afternoon, the users' hour, the sponsor's meeting and the security reviewer's queue position, and none of those appear in the delivery organisation's account of what the proof cost. **A practice that counts only its own hours will always conclude it was cheap**, and will grow more thorough every engagement until somebody outside it notices.

There is a second problem, which is that a proof's structure conceals which part of it worked. The commitment was obtained after everything was shown, so everything appears to have contributed. **Almost always one item carried it and the rest was context, insurance or interest** — and until that item is identified, the next engagement repeats the whole shape.

The occasion's response is to make the price, the load-bearing item and the cheaper alternative assessed elements, and to make the defence adversarial on price.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The stage's evaluative peak. The intrinsic content is entirely retrospective; the load is in arguing against your own fortnight in front of somebody entitled to name a cheaper route through it.

**3.2 Why the price is mostly other people's attention.** The delivery team's hours are visible, budgeted and reported. Everybody else's are not, and they are the larger share in any proof involving observation, testing or access. **The undercount is structural rather than dishonest**, which is why the ledger names the category explicitly rather than asking for a total.

**3.3 Why the load-bearing item can be identified retrospectively but not prospectively.** Before the commitment, you do not know which evidence will carry it. Afterwards, you usually do — the stakeholder said so, or the ask changed shape at a particular moment. **The identification is only available at this point in the sequence**, which is why the occasion is placed at the end of the stage rather than after each session.

**3.4 Why the unproven list is the pack's most valuable page.** A pack circulates and its claims detach from their evidence. The unproven list is the only component that travels with the evidence's boundary attached, and it is the component with the strongest local incentive for deletion.

**3.5 Why a cheaper proof must risk something.** A cheaper design that removes only obviously optional work has not made a trade. **The assessed property is the trade**, not the saving.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 3 is the occasion's centre of gravity.** *What did you produce that nobody used?* is asked before any instruction, and the answer's specificity predicts almost everything about the pack that follows.

**The ledger is written before anything else is assembled.** Writing it after the pack produces a ledger that justifies the pack. The work movement is explicit about the ordering and about the inclusion of non-team time.

**The specification is closed** and its exclusions are long: not whether the proof succeeded, not whether the commitment was obtained, not the prototype's quality, not counts of participants or spikes or iterations, not presentation, and **not whether the cheaper proof would in fact have worked.** That last exclusion is deliberate and §8 defends it.

**Three separations are re-checked in the assembled pack**, not because they were assessed before but because assembly is where they degrade: populations from [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md), promises from inspections from [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md), and evidence from conclusions from [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md). **[WP-034](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-034.md) prediction 4 is the measurement of whether the duplication is necessary.**

**The defence is adversarial on price.** The assessor proposes a cheaper proof and asks what it would have missed. **A candidate who concedes that nothing would have been missed passes**, and the design states this in the session rather than leaving it as marker discretion, because a candidate who suspects that conceding is a trap will defend work they know was unnecessary.

**The agent's brief is to argue the commitment should be withdrawn.** [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s claim closes the stage where it opened the programme, and this is its fourth and last appearance in Stage 4.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The thorough pack.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* the ledger's ordering, the unnecessary-work element, and the standard's warning sign — you are proud of the coverage.

**The retrospective necessity.** Everything turns out to have been essential. *Countermeasure:* a named assessed element requiring something to have been unnecessary, plus the requirement to say what made it look necessary at the time. **The second clause is what stops the element becoming a ritual confession.**

**The generous cheaper proof.** *Countermeasure:* the requirement that the cheaper design name what it risks, and the standard's warning sign — it does not risk anything.

**The smoothed pack.** Blockers, refusals and compliment-only tests have become clean lines. *Countermeasure:* the three re-checked separations, and the standard's warning sign — the pack reads better than the fortnight went.

**The proven-by-assembly effect.** *Countermeasure:* the unproven-list element and its length comparison. **This is the design's weakest countermeasure** and it is a heuristic rather than a rule, which the element's escape clause concedes.

**The unpriced non-team time.** *Countermeasure:* the ledger's explicit category, and §9 prediction 2, which expects the countermeasure to be insufficient.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**This is an assessment occasion.** It generates specification evidence and no entrustment judgement — the programme's entrustment decisions begin at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md).

Eight elements, each checked present and adequate or not: the cost of the whole proof is stated in the units it was paid in, including other people's time; the cheaper proof is designed and names what it risks; something is named as having been unnecessary, with what made it look necessary; the commitment is described in its currency with what the giver could have refused; a refusal, non-acceptance or unconverted stakeholder appears somewhere in the pack, or its absence is stated as the finding; the unproven list is longer than the proven one or the pack argues why not; populations, promises and inspections remain separated in the assembled pack; and nothing in the pack asserts a magnitude that was not measured by a named instrument.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Whether the proof succeeded is not marked. Whether the commitment was obtained is not marked. The quality of the prototype is not marked. The number of participants, spikes or iterations is not marked. The pack's presentation is not marked. Whether the cheaper proof would in fact have worked is not marked.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: marking the price without marking whether the proof was adequate rewards under-investigation, and will produce engineers who are proud of having found out less.**

At its strongest: the specification does not mark whether the evidence supported the commitment. A candidate who ran one cheap test, obtained a soft commitment from a friendly sponsor and wrote an honest ledger scores well; a candidate who did the harder, more expensive work that a serious decision required scores worse on exactly the elements that distinguish them. **The occasion optimises for parsimony and calls it judgement**, and the graduates it produces will systematically under-prove work that needed proving — which is a far more expensive failure than thoroughness.

**Substantially conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to this occasion.** Three partial answers, none of them complete. The unproven list is an assessed element, and a candidate who under-investigated has a long one that must be stated plainly — which makes the under-investigation visible rather than rewarded. The commitment element requires the ask's currency, so a soft commitment from a friendly sponsor is recorded as soft. And [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md)'s proportionality standard sits immediately upstream. **The declined fix is to add an element marking whether the evidence was adequate to the commitment.** It is declined for the same reason [WP-035](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-035.md) §8 declines its analogue: nothing in the material behind this stage supplies a basis for that mark — the corpus has no cost side and no failure, the playbook has no comparative evidence, and the handbook asserts no threshold — so the element would be the marker's opinion in a specification's clothing. **The gap is real, it is stated, and the design accepts it rather than filling it with something unwarranted.**

**Objection 2: an assessor who invents a rival proof during the defence is an unstandardisable element, and the mark will vary by how imaginative the marker is that morning.**

At its strongest: every other element in this specification is checkable from the submission by any competent reader. This one is a live improvisation whose difficulty depends entirely on the assessor. Two candidates with identical packs will face rival proofs of different quality, and the candidate facing the weaker one will find it easier to say what it would have missed. That is not assessment, it is an interview.

**Conceded, and the design's mitigation is narrow.** The element is marked on whether the candidate can *reason about* the rival — what it would have missed, or an explicit concession that nothing would have — rather than on whether they defeat it, and **conceding is stated in advance to be a pass**, which removes most of the variance the objection identifies. **The declined fix is to supply assessors with a standard bank of rival proofs per brief.** It is declined because a bank becomes known to candidates within one cohort and the exercise becomes preparation for a list, which is the opposite of the judgement being assessed. **The residual variance is real and the design has no answer to it**, which is why the element sits alongside seven that are checkable from the paper.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Answers to opening item 3 will be blank or generic for most candidates before instruction. *Instrument:* the written answers, coded *specific* / *generic* / *blank* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Cost ledgers will undercount non-team time relative to what the packs' own method sections imply was consumed. *Instrument:* the ledger against the method sections of the constituent artefacts, with non-team hours reconstructed independently by a second reader.

**Prediction 3.** Most cheaper proofs will remove instruments rather than accept risks — that is, they will omit work the candidate already regarded as optional. *Instrument:* the cheaper design's named risk, coded *substantive* / *nominal* / *absent*.

**Prediction 4.** Candidates who identify a load-bearing item at this occasion will not produce cheaper proofs in a later engagement than those who do not. *Instrument:* the item identification here against the cost ledger of a subsequent proof, paired per learner, measurable only at [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) or later. **The design expects this prediction to fail — that is, it expects identification to help — and it is the stage's only compounding claim.** If it holds, [M08](/wiki/modules/M08.md)'s closing sentence has no support and the element should be removed.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a proof's price is mostly other people's attention, that one item usually carried the commitment, and that a cheaper alternative is only a design if it trades something away.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about what a proof should cost. **No source behind this stage supplies a benchmark, and the occasion does not invent one** — it assesses whether the price was counted, understood and reduced, never whether it was right.

**The concession worth remembering.** This occasion cannot distinguish honest parsimony from under-investigation, and it says so. If you leave this stage able to make that distinction yourself, you have acquired something the assessment could not test.
