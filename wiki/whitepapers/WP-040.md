## In plain terms

This is the stage's second assessment occasion and it marks something the first one deliberately did not.

[S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) marked the form and the disclosure of one investigation. **This occasion marks the price of the whole proof.** You assembled a body of evidence, somebody committed something to it, and the question the assessor holds is whether the same commitment could have been obtained for less.

Three of the assessed elements are unusual and they are the ones that matter.

**Something has to be named as having been unnecessary**, along with what made it look necessary at the time. Nearly every proof contains work that was interesting, competent and irrelevant to the decision, and naming your own is the professional disposition the stage exists to build.

**The cheaper proof has to be designed rather than described.** A named instrument you would not run, a named omission, and what it would have risked. *We could have moved faster* fails the element. **A cheaper proof that risks nothing has been trimmed, not designed.**

**And the unproven list has to be longer than the proven one**, or the pack has to argue why it is not. That page is the most valuable one in the pack and it is the first thing deleted before circulation, which is exactly why it is marked here.

The defence is adversarial in one specific way. **The assessor will propose a cheaper proof of their own and ask what it would have missed.** A candidate who cannot answer has priced their work without understanding it; a candidate who concedes that nothing would have been missed has just designed the better instrument in the room, and that is a pass.

There is a second price and most packs arrive without it. The proof ran on somebody's infrastructure, consumed somebody's capacity, and — if the commitment converts — obliges somebody to hold capacity whether or not it is used. **The platform states the whole economics in one clause: a provisioned deployment holds processing capacity exclusively for your use whether or not requests are being made**, which is why its billing unit is time rather than tokens. Two consequences run through the pack. **Quota is permission and capacity is availability**, and the documentation says plainly that having the first does not give you the second. And **a total is not a price** — a spend figure with no dimensions on it tells you the bill and cannot tell you which half of the proof was expensive, which is what the pack exists to say.

The vendor's own procurement guidance also states this stage's whole argument in a sentence written for a different purpose. **Reservations do not guarantee capacity: create deployments to confirm availability first, then purchase the reservation.** Verify, then commit. The intuitive order is exactly wrong, and a candidate who has understood [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md) should recognise their own proposal in it.

## Abstract

This paper defends closing Stage 4 with an assessment of price rather than of proof, and explains why the object of the defence is the cheapness of the evidence and not its strength. The argument is that the stage's question has a cost clause in it, that a proof's price is mostly other people's attention and is therefore systematically undercounted, and that identifying the single load-bearing item is the only compounding mechanism the stage possesses.

The occasion adds a second, machine-side price that the practice literature omits entirely. Dedicated model capacity is held whether or not it is used, so its billing unit is time; **quota is permission and capacity is availability**, and neither implies the other; a capacity figure is an inference from a traffic model and inherits every simplification in it, including the population the model excluded; and **a spend total with no dimensions attached cannot say which part of the proof was expensive.** The design therefore requires the pack to state the traffic model behind any capacity figure, to make spend attributable per population, and to follow the vendor's own documented verify-then-commit sequence rather than the intuitive one.

The occasion installs a proof pack assembled from five prior artefacts, a cost ledger, a designed cheaper alternative and an unproven list, and closes its specification with the statement that nothing else is assessed. The paper concedes that no source behind the stage establishes what a proof should cost, that the compounding claim has no external warrant and cannot be tested within one cohort, that an assessor proposing a rival proof introduces a marker-dependent element the design cannot standardise, and that **the platform documentation establishes how capacity is bought and offers no evidence whatever about what a proof ought to consume.**

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A body of evidence has been produced and somebody has committed to it. Every incentive now points at treating the volume of the evidence as its virtue.

**Thoroughness is the professional default and it is expensive in a currency nobody counts.** A proof consumes the maintainer's afternoon, the users' hour, the sponsor's meeting and the security reviewer's queue position, and none of those appear in the delivery organisation's account of what the proof cost. **A practice that counts only its own hours will always conclude it was cheap**, and will grow more thorough every engagement until somebody outside it notices.

There is a second problem, which is that a proof's structure conceals which part of it worked. The commitment was obtained after everything was shown, so everything appears to have contributed. **Almost always one item carried it and the rest was context, insurance or interest** — and until that item is identified, the next engagement repeats the whole shape.

The occasion's response is to make the price, the load-bearing item and the cheaper alternative assessed elements, and to make the defence adversarial on price.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The stage's evaluative peak. The intrinsic content is entirely retrospective; the load is in arguing against your own fortnight in front of somebody entitled to name a cheaper route through it.

**3.2 Why the price is mostly other people's attention.** The delivery team's hours are visible, budgeted and reported. Everybody else's are not, and they are the larger share in any proof involving observation, testing or access. **The undercount is structural rather than dishonest**, which is why the ledger names the category explicitly rather than asking for a total.

**3.3 Why the load-bearing item can be identified retrospectively but not prospectively.** Before the commitment, you do not know which evidence will carry it. Afterwards, you usually do — the stakeholder said so, or the ask changed shape at a particular moment. **The identification is only available at this point in the sequence**, which is why the occasion is placed at the end of the stage rather than after each session.

**3.4 Why the unproven list is the pack's most valuable page.** A pack circulates and its claims detach from their evidence. The unproven list is the only component that travels with the evidence's boundary attached, and it is the component with the strongest local incentive for deletion.

**3.5 Why a cheaper proof must risk something.** A cheaper design that removes only obviously optional work has not made a trade. **The assessed property is the trade**, not the saving.

**3.6 The machine-side price, and why it changes the kind of decision being made.**

The practice literature this stage draws on has no cost side at all, so the platform supplies the one it omits. A provisioned deployment **"holds a fixed amount of processing capacity exclusively for your deployment's use, whether or not requests are being made"**, in contrast to a shared deployment where **"inference capacity is shared across customers and throughput can vary with demand"** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md)).

Everything follows from that clause, and the consequence for a proof pack is not arithmetic but categorical. Capacity held whether used or not is paid for whether used or not, **so the billing unit changes from tokens to time** — and a deployment stops being a configuration setting and becomes a capacity-planning commitment, where a wrong estimate costs money continuously rather than in proportion to traffic. The four deployment categories — shared per-token, priority per-token with a latency target, provisioned per-unit-per-hour, and discounted asynchronous batch — form a clean trade space whose lesson is one sentence: **latency guarantees cost money, and the currency you pay in changes with how much predictability you want.**

The unit's four properties matter to a pack because they set floors nobody expects. It is **model-independent**, so you are buying machine rather than service; **region-specific**, so nothing carries across geographies; its throughput **varies by model**; and **each model has a minimum below which a deployment cannot be created**, which puts a floor under the cost of using a large model at all. **A pack that costed a proof on the assumption that capacity scales smoothly to zero has costed something that does not exist.**

**3.7 Permission is not availability, and the vendor states this stage's argument in its own procurement guidance.**

This is the most transferable idea in the platform layer and the paper treats it as the occasion's conceptual spine. **Quota** is **"a policy limit enforced by Azure, and it has no associated cost"**. **Capacity** is **"the actual amount of PTUs per model version that's available to be deployed"**. The documentation states the consequence directly: **"Having PTU quota doesn't guarantee that capacity is available."** Two further facts sharpen it — availability **"changes throughout the day based on customer demand"**, and releasing capacity carries **"no guarantee the same capacity is available"** later, so a reversible-looking action is not.

Then the sentence the whole stage has been walking towards, written for a billing audience: **"Reservations don't guarantee capacity. First create deployments to confirm that capacity is available, then purchase the reservation to lock in the discounted rate."** Deploy, verify, commit — and the intuitive sequence, commit then deploy, is exactly wrong.

**That is this stage's own thesis arriving from a completely independent direction**, which is the strongest thing that can be said for it in a repository this sceptical of its own reasoning. The whole of Stage 4 argues that you establish availability before you buy the right to it: a proof is the verification and the commitment is the purchase. **The convergence is not evidence — a vendor's billing guidance and a curriculum's design argument agreeing is not a finding — but it is a genuinely independent restatement**, and the occasion uses it as one rather than as support.

**3.8 A price is an inference, and a total is not a price.**

Sizing is a three-input calculation — request shape, an output-to-input ratio capturing that **"output tokens require more processing capacity than input tokens"**, and a cache rate under which **"cached tokens don't consume PTU capacity"** — combined into a normalised throughput figure and divided by a per-model divisor. **It is a capacity model rather than a pricing model: it converts a description of traffic into a quantity of machine, and every simplification in the description propagates into the answer.** A candidate's traffic description came from the [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md) window, which excluded the non-invokers by construction, so the pack must state the provenance beside the figure. **A cost figure without its traffic model is the same defect as a finding without its bound**, which [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) already assessed.

Attribution is the second half. Tokens are a shared finite resource distributed across **"different applications, developer teams, or departments within the company"**, and with more than one consumer **"you need to make sure that one app doesn't use the whole TPM quota and block other apps"** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md)). The general form is worth keeping: **a shared finite resource with no per-consumer accounting will be consumed by whichever consumer is least careful.** The mechanisms the pack uses are a token limit policy keyed on an arbitrary counter with **prompt tokens precalculated at the gateway** so an over-budget request never reaches the backend, and a token-metric policy carrying custom dimensions. **Dimensions are what make a metric answerable**: a total tells you the bill, and consumption per population tells you which half of the proof was expensive.

Two cautions belong to the occasion and neither is in the source. **Semantic caching trades correctness for cost and the similarity threshold is the dial**, with silent failures. And **logging prompts and completions creates a privacy surface the documentation does not discuss**, in the same page that recommends logging for billing and auditing. **Both are the research note's own analysis and are recorded as such**; the pack is required to state what was logged, who could read it and for how long, because nobody else will.

**The limits on both platform sources are heavy.** First-party documentation, definitional rather than empirical, single-vendor, commercially framed. The capacity page **makes no performance claim that can be checked independently**; its concrete ratios, minimums, quota defaults and prices live on companion pages that were not fetched and **are not reproduced here.** The gateway page offers **no benchmarks, no latency figures and no cost-saving measurements**; capability varies by service tier; and two of its described features are **preview**.

**3.9 Why the judgement is a defence and why it is written rather than scored.**

The occasion's form follows the entrustment literature. An entrustment decision **implies the acceptance of risk, because it can never be fully predicted what the person will encounter** ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)), which is why the defence is built on **what-if questions** rather than on a walkthrough — retrospective explanation evidences only the case the candidate met. **The assessor's rival proof is the what-if in its most useful form.** The same source names three professionalism characteristics behaviourally — integrity, reliability and humility — and **the unproven list is a humility instrument with a page number.** Its limits: a clinical model, guidance from an advocate reporting adoption rather than effectiveness, and a full evidence standard — multiple trained observers producing deliberated judgements — that **exceeds what this occasion implements.**

Why a reason rather than a mark comes from the assessor-cognition literature. Assessors reading real evidence **relied on narrative to form the judgement and used scores only to confirm impressions already formed from prose**; they **anchored early and tended to hold their initial judgement against seemingly disconfirming evidence**; and identical verdicts frequently rested on **entirely different reasoning** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). Three design consequences follow, and the occasion accepts all three: the assessor writes a reason, high-stakes judgements should not rest on one person, and the reasoning is recorded so divergence is discussable. **Limits: two studies at one medical school, one portfolio type, think-aloud methods that cannot capture fast or automatic processing; the transfer is this programme's argument.**

One discipline governs every threshold that appears anywhere in a pack: **"pass rate is a product decision"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md)), so a reported threshold must name who decided it and why. **A single case study, no control condition, no measured improvement, tooling dated to early 2024.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The movements run situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**Opening item 3 is the occasion's centre of gravity.** *What did you produce that nobody used?* is asked before any instruction, and the answer's specificity predicts almost everything about the pack that follows.

**The ledger is written before anything else is assembled.** Writing it after the pack produces a ledger that justifies the pack. The work movement is explicit about the ordering and about the inclusion of non-team time.

**The machine-side price is built rather than estimated.** The candidate derives a traffic model from what actually happened in the [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md) window, states each input beside the population it was inferred from and the population it excludes, and puts a gateway in front so that spend resolves per population rather than into a total. **The dimensions are required to match the test record's populations**, deliberately, so that the ledger and the record can be reconciled by a reader who was not there — and so that the pack can say which half of the proof was expensive rather than only what the proof cost.

**Verify-then-commit is checked as an element rather than taught as a principle.** A plan that buys a discounted rate before confirming that capacity exists fails, whatever its arithmetic shows. The rule is the vendor's own and the occasion adopts it without amendment, because it is the cheapest available demonstration that this stage's argument is not merely a curriculum preference.

**The specification is closed** and its exclusions are long: not whether the proof succeeded, not whether the commitment was obtained, not the prototype's quality, not counts of participants or spikes or iterations, not presentation, and **not whether the cheaper proof would in fact have worked.** That last exclusion is deliberate and §8 defends it.

**Three separations are re-checked in the assembled pack**, not because they were assessed before but because assembly is where they degrade: populations from [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md), promises from inspections from [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md), and evidence from conclusions from [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md). **[WP-034](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-034.md) prediction 4 is the measurement of whether the duplication is necessary.**

**The defence is adversarial on price.** The assessor proposes a cheaper proof and asks what it would have missed. **A candidate who concedes that nothing would have been missed passes**, and the design states this in the session rather than leaving it as marker discretion, because a candidate who suspects that conceding is a trap will defend work they know was unnecessary.

**The agent's brief is to argue the commitment should be withdrawn.** [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s claim closes the stage where it opened the programme, and this is its fourth and last appearance in Stage 4.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The thorough pack.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* the ledger's ordering, the unnecessary-work element, and the standard's warning sign — you are proud of the coverage.

**The retrospective necessity.** Everything turns out to have been essential. *Countermeasure:* a named assessed element requiring something to have been unnecessary, plus the requirement to say what made it look necessary at the time. **The second clause is what stops the element becoming a ritual confession.**

**The generous cheaper proof.** *Countermeasure:* the requirement that the cheaper design name what it risks, and the standard's warning sign — it does not risk anything.

**The smoothed pack.** Blockers, refusals and compliment-only tests have become clean lines. *Countermeasure:* the three re-checked separations, and the standard's warning sign — the pack reads better than the fortnight went.

**The proven-by-assembly effect.** *Countermeasure:* the unproven-list element and its length comparison. **This is the design's weakest countermeasure** and it is a heuristic rather than a rule, which the element's escape clause concedes.

**The unpriced non-team time.** *Countermeasure:* the ledger's explicit category, and §9 prediction 2, which expects the countermeasure to be insufficient.

**The total mistaken for a price.** One unattributed spend figure, so nothing in the pack can say which half was expensive. *Countermeasure:* the dimensioned token metric, with dimensions required to match the test record's populations.

**The confident traffic model.** A capacity figure derived from a short window with a self-selected population, presented without its provenance. *Countermeasure:* the requirement that each sizing input name the population it came from and the population it excludes. **The paper expects this countermeasure to be the weakest of the platform three**, because a number in a table reads as a measurement whatever caveat sits beside it.

**Commit before verify.** The plan secures a rate before anybody confirms the capacity exists. *Countermeasure:* the verify-then-commit element, which is checked from the paper and is the one platform element that requires no judgement at all.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**This is an assessment occasion.** It generates specification evidence and no entrustment judgement — the programme's entrustment decisions begin at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md).

Eleven elements, each checked present and adequate or not: the cost of the whole proof is stated in the units it was paid in, including other people's time and anything consumed; any capacity or spend figure names the traffic model it was inferred from and the population that model excludes; spend is attributable rather than total, so the pack can say which part of the proof was expensive; any commitment of capacity follows verify-then-commit, a plan that buys a discount before confirming availability being a failed element whatever its arithmetic shows; the cheaper proof is designed and names what it risks; something is named as having been unnecessary, with what made it look necessary; the commitment is described in its currency with what the giver could have refused; a refusal, non-acceptance or unconverted stakeholder appears somewhere in the pack, or its absence is stated as the finding; the unproven list is longer than the proven one or the pack argues why not; populations, promises and inspections remain separated in the assembled pack; and nothing in the pack asserts a magnitude that was not measured by a named instrument.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Whether the proof succeeded is not marked. Whether the commitment was obtained is not marked. The quality of the prototype is not marked. Whether the platform work is elegant is not marked. Whether the capacity estimate was accurate is not marked. The number of participants, spikes or iterations is not marked. The pack's presentation is not marked. Whether the cheaper proof would in fact have worked is not marked.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: marking the price without marking whether the proof was adequate rewards under-investigation, and will produce engineers who are proud of having found out less.**

At its strongest: the specification does not mark whether the evidence supported the commitment. A candidate who ran one cheap test, obtained a soft commitment from a friendly sponsor and wrote an honest ledger scores well; a candidate who did the harder, more expensive work that a serious decision required scores worse on exactly the elements that distinguish them. **The occasion optimises for parsimony and calls it judgement**, and the graduates it produces will systematically under-prove work that needed proving — which is a far more expensive failure than thoroughness.

**Substantially conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to this occasion.** Three partial answers, none of them complete. The unproven list is an assessed element, and a candidate who under-investigated has a long one that must be stated plainly — which makes the under-investigation visible rather than rewarded. The commitment element requires the ask's currency, so a soft commitment from a friendly sponsor is recorded as soft. And [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md)'s proportionality standard sits immediately upstream. **The declined fix is to add an element marking whether the evidence was adequate to the commitment.** It is declined for the same reason [WP-035](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-035.md) §8 declines its analogue: nothing in the material behind this stage supplies a basis for that mark — the corpus has no cost side and no failure, the playbook has no comparative evidence, and the handbook asserts no threshold — so the element would be the marker's opinion in a specification's clothing. **The gap is real, it is stated, and the design accepts it rather than filling it with something unwarranted.**

**Objection 2: an assessor who invents a rival proof during the defence is an unstandardisable element, and the mark will vary by how imaginative the marker is that morning.**

At its strongest: every other element in this specification is checkable from the submission by any competent reader. This one is a live improvisation whose difficulty depends entirely on the assessor. Two candidates with identical packs will face rival proofs of different quality, and the candidate facing the weaker one will find it easier to say what it would have missed. That is not assessment, it is an interview.

**Conceded, and the design's mitigation is narrow.** The element is marked on whether the candidate can *reason about* the rival — what it would have missed, or an explicit concession that nothing would have — rather than on whether they defeat it, and **conceding is stated in advance to be a pass**, which removes most of the variance the objection identifies. **The declined fix is to supply assessors with a standard bank of rival proofs per brief.** It is declined because a bank becomes known to candidates within one cohort and the exercise becomes preparation for a list, which is the opposite of the judgement being assessed. **The residual variance is real and the design has no answer to it**, which is why the element sits alongside seven that are checkable from the paper.

**Objection 3: requiring a gateway, a traffic model and a capacity plan for a prototype is exactly the thoroughness this occasion exists to punish, and the paper has failed its own test in its own specification.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the paper least wants to be right: the occasion marks candidates down for producing work nobody used, and then requires them to stand up a gateway, emit dimensioned metrics, derive a normalised throughput figure and reason about reserved capacity — **for a proof that ran for a fortnight with a handful of participants and will very likely be thrown away.** Nobody's commitment turned on the capacity plan. It is, by the occasion's own definition, work that was interesting, competent and irrelevant to the decision. **A specification that mandates its own paradigm case of unnecessary work is not merely inconsistent; it teaches the failure it names.**

**Conceded in part, and the concession is uncomfortable enough to be worth stating plainly rather than managing.** For a proof that will be discarded, the capacity plan is overhead and the objection is right. The paper's answer applies to the other case and only to it: **where the commitment obtained at [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md) funds continued work, a proof presented without its running cost has understated its own ask**, and the understatement surfaces during delivery in the currency that is hardest to renegotiate. **Verify-then-commit is the specific hazard**: a candidate who has never checked that capacity exists has made a commitment on the assumption that permission is availability, which the documentation says plainly it is not. **The declined fix was to make the platform elements conditional on whether the commitment converted.** It was declined because the condition is known only after the occasion, which would make the specification unassessable at the time it is assessed — and because a conditional element is one candidates learn to argue their way out of. **The accepted cost is that candidates whose proof was genuinely disposable will do work the occasion's own logic calls unnecessary, and the honest response available to them is to name it in the pack under the unnecessary-work element.** That is a strange outcome and the paper does not pretend otherwise: **a candidate who identifies the specification's own requirement as the unnecessary work, and argues it well, has satisfied the element.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Answers to opening item 3 will be blank or generic for most candidates before instruction. *Instrument:* the written answers, coded *specific* / *generic* / *blank* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Cost ledgers will undercount non-team time relative to what the packs' own method sections imply was consumed. *Instrument:* the ledger against the method sections of the constituent artefacts, with non-team hours reconstructed independently by a second reader.

**Prediction 3.** Most cheaper proofs will remove instruments rather than accept risks — that is, they will omit work the candidate already regarded as optional. *Instrument:* the cheaper design's named risk, coded *substantive* / *nominal* / *absent*.

**Prediction 4.** Candidates who identify a load-bearing item at this occasion will not produce cheaper proofs in a later engagement than those who do not. *Instrument:* the item identification here against the cost ledger of a subsequent proof, paired per learner, measurable only at [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) or later. **The design expects this prediction to fail — that is, it expects identification to help — and it is the stage's only compounding claim.** If it holds, [M08](/wiki/modules/M08.md)'s closing sentence has no support and the element should be removed.

**Prediction 5.** Most packs will report a spend total and few will report spend resolved to more than one population, even where the gateway policy was configured. *Instrument:* the ledger's spend section, coded *total only* / *two or more populations* / *absent*, against whether dimensioned metrics appear in the submitted policy. **A gap between the two — policy present, attribution absent — would show the mechanism was configured and not consulted**, which is the platform equivalent of a countermeasure satisfied on paper.

**Prediction 6.** Where attribution is produced, the population that consumed most will not be the population tested most. *Instrument:* the per-population spend against the [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md) session counts, paired per candidate. **If they coincide, the design's assumption that heavy use and willingness to be interviewed are different dispositions is wrong**, and the exposure-log argument in [WP-038](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-038.md) §3.7 loses its clearest downstream confirmation.

**Prediction 7.** A substantial share of capacity figures will be stated without the traffic model's excluded population beside them, despite the element requiring it. *Instrument:* the sizing section, coded for whether each input names both its source population and its exclusion. **This predicts the failure of the session's own countermeasure**, and it is included because the paper judges a caveat beside a number to be the weakest control in the whole specification — a figure in a table reads as a measurement whatever sits next to it.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a proof's price is mostly other people's attention, that one item usually carried the commitment, that a cheaper alternative is only a design if it trades something away, and that **a total is not a price and permission is not availability.**

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about what a proof should cost. **No source behind this stage supplies a benchmark, and the occasion does not invent one** — it assesses whether the price was counted, understood and reduced, never whether it was right. The platform documentation establishes how capacity is bought and how spend can be attributed, and establishes **nothing whatever about what a proof ought to consume.**

**The concession worth remembering.** This occasion cannot distinguish honest parsimony from under-investigation, and it says so. It also mandates platform work that its own logic would call unnecessary on a disposable proof, and says that too — **a candidate who names the specification's own requirement as the unnecessary work, and argues it well, has satisfied the element.** If you leave this stage able to make both distinctions yourself, you have acquired something the assessment could not test.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That the corpus contains no cost side anywhere — staffing, compute, the cost of the discovery activity itself and opportunity cost are all absent, and every claim in it is a gross benefit; that its organising thesis is that everything short of realised business value is activity rather than impact; and that of roughly thirty quantitative assertions across its delivery material, almost all measure activity rather than outcome. Recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md), from a complete reading of access-controlled material **that no public reader can check. No figure from it is reproduced anywhere in this repository.**

That the practitioner handbook treats speed as the retirement of the most consequential uncertainty rather than as a count of activities, and that it establishes no effect and no threshold — recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md), from a complete reading.

That the playbook offers no comparative evidence that its practices outperform alternatives and must not be taught as though it did — recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md), from a complete reading of all of the site's pages on 2026-07-30.

That an entrustment decision implies the acceptance of risk and therefore requires what-if questioning rather than retrospective walkthrough; that a summative entrustment standard requires multiple observers and multiple observations; and that integrity, reliability and humility are stated behaviourally — recorded at [research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md), from a complete reading of the open-access primer, **which reports adoption rather than effectiveness and whose full evidence standard this occasion does not implement.**

That assessors relied on narrative to form judgements and used scores only to confirm them, that they anchored early and held initial judgements against disconfirming evidence, and that similar verdicts rested on divergent reasoning — recorded at [research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md), **from two open-access studies in medical education at a single institution, one read in part only.**

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That costs borne outside the accounting unit are systematically undercounted; that an item's contribution to a decision is easier to identify after the decision than before it; and that a threshold reported without an owner will be read as a property of the system rather than as a policy choice. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The eleven-part pack and the eleven assessed elements; the requirement that the cheaper proof name what it risks; the unproven-list length heuristic and its escape clause; the re-checking of three separations already assessed upstream; **the requirement that a capacity figure name its traffic model's excluded population**; **the requirement that spend be attributable to the populations in the test record**; the reading of the vendor's verify-then-commit billing guidance as an independent restatement of this stage's thesis; the assessor's rival proof and the rule that conceding is a pass; the claim that identifying the load-bearing item is a compounding mechanism; the refusal of the declined fix in §8's third objection; and all seven predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The capacity model used throughout — the definition of a provisioned deployment as holding fixed processing capacity exclusively whether or not requests are being made, against shared capacity that varies with demand; the four deployment categories compared across billing, latency service level and workload type; the four properties of the capacity unit including model-independence, region-specificity, model-dependent throughput and per-model minimum deployment sizes; the definitions of quota and capacity with the published statement that quota does not guarantee capacity; intra-day capacity fluctuation and the absence of any guarantee on re-creation after release; the three sizing inputs including the output-to-input ratio and the statement that cached tokens do not consume capacity; the three routing variants trading residency against availability; spillover redirecting overflow to a shared deployment on an error response; the advice against using provisioned deployments as an autoscaling mechanism and both stated reasons; and the reservation guidance that reservations do not guarantee capacity and that deployments should be created to confirm availability before a reservation is purchased — is recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md), **fetched and read in full.**

The gateway model — the framing of the AI gateway as an extension of an existing API gateway rather than a separate offering; the five stated multi-tenant challenges; the token-quota distribution argument and its noisy-neighbour framing; the token limit policy's period options, counter-key flexibility and prompt-token precalculation; the token-metric policy with custom dimensions; the four load-balancing modes and the recommendation to route with priority to dedicated capacity; the circuit breaker taking its trip duration from the backend's `Retry-After` header; and the statement that logging supports billing and auditing — is recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md), **fetched and read in full.** Both pages read 2026-07-30. **Policy names, attributes, tier boundaries, quota defaults, model ratios and portal paths drift between revisions; every fragment in the session is illustrative and no numeric value from either page is reproduced anywhere in this wiki.**

**The silences that matter.** Neither page makes a single claim that could be independently checked: the capacity page **offers no performance data and no comparison against any other vendor's capacity model**, and the gateway page **offers no benchmarks, no latency figures and no cost-reduction measurements at all** — its benefits are plausible mechanisms rather than measured outcomes, and the paper treats them as such. The capacity page's concrete numbers — per-model ratios, minimum deployment sizes, current prices and default quota allocations — **live on companion pages that were not fetched, so no figure in this session is grounded and none is asserted.** The gateway page **does not discuss the correctness risk created by a loose semantic-caching threshold**, which is a silent failure mode, nor **the privacy surface created by logging prompts and completions**, in the same page that recommends the logging for billing and auditing; both cautions in §3.8 are the research note's own analysis and are marked as such there. Neither page discusses the latency, cost or operational burden the gateway itself adds. And most consequentially for this occasion: **nothing in either document says anything about what a proof, a pilot or an experiment ought to consume.** The documentation establishes how capacity is bought and how spend is attributed. **The entire claim that a proof should be presented with its price attached is this design's, and no vendor has any reason to make it.**
