# WP-067 — Attribution, and Who Is Responsible for It

## In plain terms

The number moved. Somebody will say your system moved it, and **in most delivery organisations nobody is assigned the job of establishing that.**

The structural shape is worth stating exactly, because it is the module's thesis. **The cost side is engineered and the return side is elicited.** Consumption is forecast by named engineers, instrumented by them and refined against telemetry from their own system. Return is chosen by the customer from a menu, measured with instrumentation the customer may not possess, over a period nobody specifies. **The causal step between them is performed by nobody and reviewed by nobody.**

The framework this module teaches from then does something sharper and worse than leaving the step out. **Several of its metric definitions define themselves as the change *attributable to* the system.** A measure defined that way cannot be evidence that the system caused a change, because the attribution has been placed inside the definition. It also poses the confounding question correctly — how would you separate the value of the system from the value of the learning, if a previous solution already exists — **and answers it nowhere.**

The session teaches three moves and one errand.

**Find the word.** Search every definition for language that assumes causation and rewrite so that the measure describes an observable quantity and the attribution becomes a separate claim. Keep the original beside it. **This is [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md)'s discipline in a financial document.**

**List what else could have caused it**, including the customer's own improvement at the task, and downgrade to an association wherever you cannot separate them.

**Disclose the asymmetry.** Where one side of your ratio came from telemetry and the other from a workshop, say so on the page carrying the ratio.

The errand is the session. **Go and ask a person to own the causal claim.** Most will decline, and a recorded refusal is a stronger artefact than an unowned claim.

## Abstract

This paper defends requiring a named individual to be responsible for establishing causation in any value claim, and defends treating a recorded refusal as a satisfactory outcome. The argument is that a claim whose attribution is assigned to nobody is not a claim; that a metric defined as the change attributable to a system presupposes what it is offered to demonstrate; that the customer's own learning is part of most observed improvements and belongs to them; and that a ratio with an instrumented denominator and an elicited numerator will read as a measurement. The session installs an attribution statement with a named person, a list of competing explanations with at least one plausible, rewritten definitions retaining their originals, a downgrade to association where separation was not achieved, and a disclosure of the instrumentation asymmetry. The paper concedes that it teaches responsibility for a question without teaching a method for answering it, and that the named-person requirement imposes an accountability the design cannot show anybody benefits from taking.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Causation in a delivery engagement is everybody's assumption and nobody's job.

**The mechanism is organisational rather than intellectual.** The engineer measures the system. The customer measures the outcome. The commercial owner assembles both into a claim. **At no point does anybody hold the question of whether the second followed from the first**, because the question belongs to a role that does not exist and would be uncomfortable to occupy if it did.

The result is a claim with no author. It is quoted, it travels, and asked where it came from the answer is a document. **A claim whose attribution is assigned to nobody is not a claim; it is a sentence somebody will use.**

There is a second problem which is a language failure with a structural cause. **A definition that contains its own attribution cannot be interrogated**, because the interrogation has been foreclosed by the vocabulary. Ask what the measure shows and the answer is the change the system caused. Ask how you know the system caused it and the answer is that the measure shows it. **The circle is short enough that nobody notices they are inside it.**

And a third, which is the one the framework itself raises and abandons. **Something was already in place, and the customer improved at the task while using what you built.** Both contributions are real, both are inside your measurement window, and neither is yours.

The session's response is a name, a list, a rewrite and a downgrade.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session consumes the confounder list and the baseline record from [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md), and cannot be attempted without them.

It assumes [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md), because the attribution statement is a decision record for a claim rather than for a design, and it inherits that session's fields: what was decided, on what grounds, by whom. It assumes [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md), because a learner who has already required every work item to carry a named owner will recognise what is being asked and will also recognise how uncomfortable it was the first time.

**It leans hardest on [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md).** A value inferred, its marker stripped, then counted as evidence by a later stage — that is the same path a circular metric takes, and the learner has already traced it end to end in a security artefact.

**The prior it works against is that attribution is a statistical problem.** Learners expect to be taught a method. **They are taught an ownership question**, and the paper says so rather than letting them discover it in §8.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is low. The session's cost is social: it requires a learner to walk into somebody's office and ask them to be accountable for a judgement that organisation has arranged not to make.

**3.2 Why a name and not a role.** A role is occupied by whoever holds it at the time, and accountability assigned to a role is discharged by the role's existence. **A name can be asked, can answer, and can refuse** — and the refusal is information, which a role cannot produce.

**3.3 Why the original definition is retained beside the rewrite.** Deleting the circular definition removes the evidence that it was circular. **Retaining it makes the rewrite legible as a correction rather than as a preference**, and it lets a later reader see what the organisation had been claiming. This is the same reason this programme's own self-correction record keeps superseded positions visible.

**3.4 Why the learning confound is separated out.** It is the confound most likely to be claimed and least likely to be listed, because claiming it is flattering and listing it is not. **The customer got better at the task while using the system**, and some of that improvement is theirs in a sense that would survive the system's removal. A claim that absorbs it has taken credit for the customer's competence.

**3.5 Why the asymmetry must be disclosed on the page carrying the ratio.** An appendix disclosure is a disclosure to the reader who was already suspicious. **The page that travels is the page that needs the mark**, which is the same argument [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) makes about provenance and the reason both sessions place it identically.

**3.6 Why a recorded refusal satisfies the requirement.** If refusal failed the element, learners would find a compliant name — somebody junior, somebody leaving, somebody who did not understand the question. **Making the refusal a passing outcome removes that incentive**, and the refusal is the honest state of most value claims in this industry.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 1 — who, by name, is responsible for establishing that your system caused the change — is the instrument.** Most answers are a role nobody holds, a team, or a silence, and all three are the finding.

**The work movement's first step is a text search**, which is deliberately mechanical. *Attributable to*, *driven by*, *resulting from*, *enabled by*. **A learner who is asked to think about circularity will not find it; a learner who is asked to grep for four phrases will**, and the definitions they find will surprise them.

**The second step makes the confounder list worse rather than shorter**, and states why: the list is not an admission, it is the input to everything that follows, and a short list means the analysis has not begun. That inverts the instinct a learner arrives with.

**The fourth step is the session and it is an errand rather than an exercise.** The learner has to obtain an acceptance or a refusal from a real person. **The refusal is a passing outcome and the session says so before the learner goes**, because otherwise the errand becomes a negotiation the learner is motivated to win.

**The review movement watches what the named person asks for before they will sign.** That request is the work the statement is still missing, and it is almost always the same thing: a way of ruling out one competing explanation that was listed and not addressed.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The circular metric surviving.** The likeliest failure, because the definitions were handed to the learner and carry institutional authority. *Countermeasure:* the mechanical search, and the [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) element requiring that no definition presuppose attribution and that originals be retained.

**The unassigned responsibility.** *Countermeasure:* opening item 1, the errand, and the [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) element that accepts a refusal and rejects a silence.

**The compliant name.** A learner finds somebody who will sign without understanding. *Countermeasure:* partial only — the review movement watches what the signer asks for, and a signer who asks for nothing is visible. **The design has no stronger defence and §8 concedes it.**

**The short confounder list.** *Countermeasure:* the second work step's explicit instruction to make it worse, and the warning sign — your competing-explanation list contains only implausible alternatives.

**The undowngraded claim.** Separation was not achieved and the sentence still asserts causation. *Countermeasure:* the [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) element requiring the downgrade wherever separation was not achieved, and the classification element requiring the claim to be labelled.

**The appendix disclosure.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that the asymmetry be disclosed on the page carrying the ratio.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the session requires a named person to own a question it does not teach anybody to answer, which is an unfair transfer of risk to whoever signs.**

At its strongest: the learner is sent to obtain an accountability for a judgement that cannot be discharged with the evidence available. **There is no experiment, no control, no counterfactual and no separation method anywhere in this module.** The person who accepts is accepting responsibility for a conclusion nobody can reach, and they will carry it after the engagement ends and the learner has gone. **A design that manufactures accountability without capability has not improved anything; it has found somebody to blame.**

**Conceded, and it is the sharpest objection in the module.** The session teaches ownership of a question and not a method for the question, and it says so in its own limits movement. **The rebuttal is narrow and it is about what the named person actually accepts.** The element requires them to own the *establishing*, which includes the legitimate outcome that it was not established and the claim was downgraded — **the accountability is for the honesty of the claim's class, not for the truth of a causal proposition.** That distinction is real and it is thinner than the objection, because in practice a signature will be read as endorsing the number. **The declined fix is to require a separation method — a comparison group, a staged rollout, an interrupted time series — as an element.** It is declined because none is available in most commercial engagements, the source demonstrates none, and requiring one would produce fabricated methods rather than real ones. **The transfer of risk is real and is accepted.**

**Objection 2: a recorded refusal passes the element, so the cheapest route through this session is to ask somebody certain to say no.**

At its strongest: the design has made the refusal a passing outcome to prevent learners finding a compliant signatory. **It has thereby made the null outcome free.** A learner who wants an easy pass asks the busiest, most senior, most obviously unavailable person, records the decline, and satisfies the element without ever having attempted a real attribution conversation. **The element then measures whether the learner sent an email.**

**Conceded in part.** The counterweight is that a refusal does not remove the other three elements: the competing explanations still have to be listed and addressed, the definitions still have to be rewritten, and the claim still has to be downgraded and defended at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) — **and a downgraded claim is what the learner then has to report at [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) to somebody who wanted the stronger version.** The refusal is free; its consequences are not. **The declined fix is to require that the refusal include the refuser's stated reason.** It is declined narrowly and it is close to being wrong: recording the reason would strengthen the artefact, and it is declined only because a reason supplied under pressure is unverifiable and would become a field learners write on the refuser's behalf. **The design accepts the gap and records that this is the element it is least confident about.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most candidates will answer opening item 1 with a role, a team or nothing. *Instrument:* the submitted opening answers, coded *named individual* / *role or team* / *absent* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most candidates will find at least one circular definition in material they were handed. *Instrument:* the count of rewritten definitions per candidate, with the source of each recorded as *supplied* / *authored by the candidate*.

**Prediction 3.** Most attribution requests will be refused. *Instrument:* the attribution statement's outcome field, coded *accepted* / *refused* / *not asked*.

**Prediction 4.** The learning confound will be absent from most competing-explanation lists before the third work step. *Instrument:* the list recorded before and after that step, with a presence check for a customer-capability entry. **If it is present before, the confounder work at [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md) is carrying more than expected and this session's third step can be shortened.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a claim with no named attributor is not a claim; that a definition containing its own attribution forecloses the question it is offered to answer; and that a ratio with an instrumented denominator and an elicited numerator will read as a measurement.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about causation in any engagement. **The framework poses the confounding question correctly and answers it nowhere**, and there is no worked separation anywhere in the corpus. The named-person requirement is class 3 and comes from this repository's own reading of the material rather than from the material.

**The concession worth remembering.** You are being taught who owns a question and not how to answer it. **Expect refusal, record it, and downgrade** — and notice that the person who refused was being asked for something the whole industry has arranged not to supply.
