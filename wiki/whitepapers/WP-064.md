## In plain terms

This session teaches the one thing the source material genuinely does well, and marks the two things it leaves out.

**What it does well is the cost side.** Consumption is forecast by engineers, instrumented by engineers, and refined against telemetry from the system itself. That is a real method, done properly, and this session teaches it as content rather than as specimen. Forecast, instrument, refine — and the third step is the one that separates a model from a proposal.

**The first thing it leaves out is any cost side in its own value claims.** Staffing, compute, the cost of the discovery activity itself, opportunity cost — all absent. **Every claim in the corpus is a gross benefit**, in a body of work whose organising thesis is that only realised value counts.

**The second is the handover.** The one place the right instinct appears — that the customer should be left holding the cost-modelling capability, because they will need it after the team disengages — sits as a reflection inside a narrative rather than as a step in any procedure. Nobody is required to transfer the model and nobody is required to check that it runs.

The session's two additions follow directly. **The delivery cost goes in the same document as the running cost.** And **somebody on the customer's side runs the model, with the learner watching and not touching anything.**

The unit matters more than it looks. **A figure per compute hour is a bill. A figure per unit of the customer's work is a model**, because only the second lets the customer ask their own questions of it.

And the session's durable claim sits underneath all of that. **Cost in these systems is a design property rather than an operational surprise, because the architecture determines the token path and the token path is the bill.** Prompt length, output length, context window, shared prefixes, batching behaviour and cache reuse are all decisions, and each sets a term in the invoice before any traffic exists. Prefill and decode are different workloads with different bottlenecks; generation is dominated by moving memory rather than by arithmetic; the key-value cache rather than the parameter count limits long context; and what caps batch size caps throughput. **None of that is a billing fact. All of it is an architecture fact that becomes a billing fact.**

The commitment half is the same argument pointed at procurement. Dedicated capacity is **held whether or not requests are being made**, which is why its billing unit changes from tokens to time. **Quota is permission and costs nothing. Capacity is availability and changes through the day. A reservation is a discount on a meter and is neither.** The vendor's own instruction is the concrete instance and it is the sentence this session exists to install: **reservations do not guarantee capacity — deploy, verify, then commit.** A commitment purchased before the shape of the workload is known is a bet dressed as procurement.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching cost modelling inside an operations module as a design constraint rather than a commercial exercise, and defends two requirements the source material does not make: that the delivery cost appear beside the running cost, and that a named person on the customer's side has run the model.

The argument runs in five steps. **First**, a cost expressed in infrastructure units cannot be interrogated by the customer whose work it prices, so changing the denominator changes which questions the artefact can answer. **Second**, cost is set at design time and merely observed at run time, because the architecture determines the token path — a claim warranted by the inference and serving literature rather than by any pricing document. **Third**, commitment is a distinct decision with its own epistemology: quota is permission, capacity is availability, a reservation is a discount, and the documented order is deploy, verify, then commit. **Fourth**, a benefit with no cost beside it is a proposal rather than a result, which is the source corpus's own thesis applied to the source corpus's own artefact. **Fifth**, a capability the recipient has not exercised has not transferred.

The session installs a cost model in units of the customer's work, carrying an explicit token path with the architectural decision that sets each term, a capacity posture with evidence that capacity was confirmed before any commitment, per-consumer accounting at the gateway, observability as a line, at least one cost-bearing design decision with its untaken alternative and an honest statement of whether cost was a criterion, the delivery cost, and a handover run under observation.

The paper concedes that the transfer requirement is assembled from a single reflective sentence in material containing no exit procedure at all; that no source reports any outcome for a transferred cost model; that requiring the delivery cost may be professionally unavailable to a learner whose organisation does not disclose it; and that **one of the retrieval sources it leans on was not read in full and no experimental result from it is asserted anywhere.**

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A running system has a cost, and in most delivery arrangements nobody on the engineering team ever sees it as a number they can act on.

**Finance sees a bill. The team sees an architecture.** The mapping between them is performed once, during a procurement conversation, by somebody who was not present for either. By the time anybody asks which design decision is expensive, the decision has been made, the system is running, and the answer is expressed in units that cannot be traced back to a choice.

**The consequence is not overspending. It is that cost never functions as a constraint.** A constraint is something that changes a decision, and a figure nobody can attribute to a decision cannot change one. It becomes, instead, a quarterly surprise handled by procurement.

The reason this is fixable is that **the attribution exists and is simply not written down.** Cost in these systems is not emergent. Prompt length is a retrieval decision. Output length is a template decision. Context window is a chunking decision. Shared prefixes and cache reuse are prompt-architecture decisions. Batch behaviour is a serving decision. **Each of those sets a term in the bill at design time**, and a model that starts from the rate card rather than from the token path has skipped the only part that could have changed anything.

There is a second problem, which belongs to procurement rather than to engineering and is routinely got backwards. **Committing to capacity is a bet on a workload whose shape you may not yet know**, and the platform's own documentation says so in an unusually direct way: quota does not guarantee capacity, capacity fluctuates with demand through the day, releasing capacity offers no guarantee of getting it back, and a reservation discounts a meter rather than holding a machine. **The correct order is deploy, verify, then commit**, and the intuitive order is exactly wrong.

And there is a third, which is this stage's problem specifically. **The cost of a system is borne after the delivery team leaves**, and the capability to model it usually leaves with them. **A customer who inherits a running system and no cost model has inherited an obligation they cannot forecast**, and every subsequent decision they make about the system will be made without the input that mattered most.

The session's response is a model in the customer's units, built from the token path, carrying a capacity posture with its evidence, refined against telemetry, containing the delivery cost, and run by the customer while the learner watches.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is moderate and mostly arithmetic. The difficulty is elsewhere: the session requires a learner to put their own delivery cost next to the benefit, in a document a commercial reader will see, which is a political act performed by somebody with the least authority in the room.

**3.2 Why the unit must be the customer's.** A cost model is an instrument for asking questions, and the questions belong to whoever is paying. **A model denominated in infrastructure units answers questions about infrastructure**, which the customer does not have and cannot form. Changing the denominator is not presentation; it changes which questions the artefact can be used for.

**3.3 Why refinement against telemetry is the load-bearing step.** A forecast is a set of beliefs arranged arithmetically. **Refining it against what actually happened is the only step that lets the world disagree with it**, and it is the step the source does properly and most teams skip. A model that has never been corrected by observation is a proposal that has been formatted.

**3.4 Why observability appears as a line.** [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) required signals to be deleted and gave a discipline for choosing which. **The discipline has no teeth while telemetry is free**, and it is free precisely because it is accounted as overhead. Making it a line is what connects the two sessions.

**3.5 Why a gross benefit is not a result.** This is the corpus's own thesis turned on the corpus. **A delivery can satisfy every agreed criterion and still produce nothing of value** — and a benefit stated without its cost cannot establish that it produced any, because the comparison the thesis demands has only one side. The session's requirement is not an additional standard; **it is the source's own standard applied to the source's own artefact.**

**3.6 Why the customer must run it, not be shown it.** Demonstration establishes that the demonstrator can operate the thing. **The only observation that establishes transfer is the recipient operating it unaided**, and the places they get stuck are the specification for the runbook [S072](/wiki/seminars/S072.md) will write. That evidence is unobtainable any other way and is discarded the moment the learner reaches for the keyboard.

**3.7 Why the token path is written before the price, and why the warrant is mechanical.**

The session's durable claim — that cost is a design property — would be an assertion if it rested on a pricing page. It rests instead on the inference and serving literature, where the mechanism is visible.

**Inference is not one operation.** Prefill, in which the input prompt is processed and many tokens are handled in parallel, is compute-bound; decode, in which output tokens are produced one at a time, is **dominated by moving weights and cache through memory rather than by arithmetic** ([research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md)). That is why raw arithmetic capability predicts token latency poorly, and why a long prompt and a long answer have different cost structures at equal token counts. The same work identifies **the key-value cache rather than the parameter count as the binding constraint on long-context serving**, with the number of key-value heads as the lever. And it reports results as a **Pareto frontier** rather than as a figure of merit, because latency and utilisation trade against one another — **so there is no universally optimal deployment, and the configuration is selected from the application's requirements.** A team that has not stated its latency requirement has not selected a configuration; it has accepted one.

The serving side supplies the throughput half. **Batching is the primary throughput lever**, and what caps batch size is memory occupied by a key-value cache that grows and shrinks dynamically and whose eventual size is unknown because the number of output tokens is unknown ([research/02-technical-foundations/kwon-pagedattention-vllm.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/kwon-pagedattention-vllm.md)). Naive allocation wastes memory through **fragmentation** and **redundant duplication** where requests share a prefix; both reduce how many requests fit, and therefore what the system can serve. The remedy is a direct transplant from operating systems — fixed-size non-contiguous blocks with a table mapping logical positions to physical ones — which bounds the waste and turns prefix sharing into a mapping problem.

**Read together, these license the session's central instruction: write the token path first.** Prompt length, output length, context window, prefix sharing and cache reuse are architectural decisions, and each sets a term in the bill before traffic exists. **Cost is decided at design time and merely observed at run time.**

**Limits on both, and they are strict.** The inference results are specific to one accelerator family, one model and one context length, and predate several now-standard techniques; **the reasoning transfers and the coefficients do not.** The serving comparison is against systems of its own period at matched latency and is not a permanent property; paging introduces overheads of its own; and the paper says nothing about model quality, evaluation or safety. **No figure from either appears in this paper or in the session.**

**3.8 Why the retrieval trade is used as the second worked instance.**

Retrieval makes the same point with a cleaner surface, because the trade is a parameter rather than an emergent property. **Memory, not computation alone, is the binding constraint at scale**, and compression is what makes large-scale similarity search fit at all: a vector is split into parts, each quantised against its own small codebook, with the effective codebook the product of the small ones ([research/02-technical-foundations/jegou-product-quantization.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/jegou-product-quantization.md)). **Code length, sub-space count, codebook size and probe count define the operating point** — shorter codes mean more vectors in memory and lower recall — so **an index is designed against a budget rather than selected from a catalogue**, and the recall cost of aggressive compression degrades results gradually rather than failing visibly.

The storage tier then dictates the algorithm rather than merely configuring it. On disk the binding cost is random reads, so **minimising traversal hops matters more than minimising distance computations**, and the design response is a hybrid of compressed navigation in memory and full-precision data on disk ([research/02-technical-foundations/subramanya-diskann.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/subramanya-diskann.md)). The same work names the metric that determines cost at scale and that vendors characteristically omit: **density, meaning how many items a single machine can serve.** Recall and latency without density is an incomplete claim, because machine count dominates the bill.

**A serious limit on the first of these, recorded rather than smoothed.** The compression paper's **full text was not retrieved** — the deposit endpoint returned an automated challenge and the publisher version is paywalled — so its account is reconstructed from established secondary knowledge of the method. **No experimental result, dataset, code length or recall figure from it is asserted anywhere**, and it must be obtained and checked before any assessed use. The disk-index paper is open and read, but its results belong to a particular benchmark and hardware era whose baselines have since advanced, and **no figure from it appears here.**

**3.9 Why commitment is treated as a separate decision with its own epistemology.**

The capacity documentation supplies the session's sharpest single distinction, and it is the reason the anchor is a commitment mechanism rather than a price list. A provisioned deployment **holds a fixed amount of processing capacity exclusively for your deployment's use, whether or not requests are being made** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md)) — and everything follows from that clause, including the change of billing unit from tokens to time.

The unit of capacity is **model-independent**, **region-specific**, delivers **model-dependent throughput**, and carries a **minimum deployment size** per model, which puts a floor under the cost of using a large model at all. **What you buy is machine, not service.**

Then the distinction: **quota** is a policy limit scoped per subscription, region and deployment type, and **it has no associated cost**; **capacity** is what is actually deployable now; and the documentation states in a call-out that **"Having PTU quota doesn't guarantee that capacity is available."** Capacity **changes throughout the day based on customer demand**, and releasing it offers **no guarantee the same capacity is available** later — **so releasing is a one-way door in practice even though it looks reversible.** Sizing is an inference from a traffic model rather than a price lookup, built from request shape, an **output-to-input ratio** capturing that output tokens require more capacity than input tokens, and a **cache rate**, where **cached tokens do not consume capacity**. **This is a capacity model, not a pricing model**, and every simplification in the traffic description propagates into the answer.

**The instruction the session installs is the documentation's own:** a reservation is **a financial discount applied to the billing meter, not to individual deployments**, and **"Reservations don't guarantee capacity. First create deployments to confirm that capacity is available, then purchase the reservation to lock in the discounted rate."** The same page advises against using provisioned deployments as an autoscaling mechanism, for two stated reasons — capacity might not be available when you need to scale back up, and continuous hourly billing at high utilisation typically exceeds reservation pricing. **Deploy, verify, then commit** is therefore not this design's advice; it is somebody else's documented guidance, which is why it is the strongest sentence in the session.

**Its limits:** first-party product documentation, **fetched and read in full**, definitional rather than empirical; single-vendor with an obvious commercial framing and a vendor's choice architecture; **perishable** in names, defaults, ratios, minimums and portal paths; and **every concrete number — per-model ratios, minimum sizes, prices, default allocations, regional availability — lives on companion pages that were not fetched, and none is asserted here.**

**3.10 Why fairness under contention belongs in a cost session.**

The gateway documentation states the argument cleanly enough to teach with no product attached. Tokens are the scarce resource; providers assign quota per unit time; that quota must be distributed across applications, teams or departments; and **as a portfolio grows you must ensure that one application does not consume the whole quota and block the others** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md)). **A shared finite resource with no per-consumer accounting will be consumed by whichever consumer is least careful** — which is a cost failure before it is an availability failure, and is invisible until the second consumer appears.

Three mechanisms follow. The **token limit policy** enforces a rate or a longer-period quota keyed on **any counter key**, with **prompt-token precalculation** at the edge so an over-limit request never reaches the backend — **fail fast: reject before paying to discover the rejection.** **Semantic caching** reuses prior completions selected by vector proximity rather than exact match, which is a genuine cost mechanism carrying a correctness risk **the documentation does not discuss at all**: the similarity threshold is a correctness dial, and set loosely it returns an answer to a question that was not asked, silently. And **priority load balancing** exists specifically to saturate dedicated endpoints first, which is **the direct operational consequence of having bought capacity that is paid for whether or not it is used.** The documentation also attaches the caveat that adding gateway capacity does not scale the model backends — **scaling the front door does not widen the corridor behind it.**

**Its limits:** first-party documentation with a commercial frame, fetched and read in full; capability availability varies by service tier; parts are preview; **no performance or cost-saving data of any kind**; and it does not discuss the latency or operational burden the gateway itself adds.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The eight movements run situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**Opening item 2 — which design decision in your system is the most expensive to run, and was it chosen for that reason — is the instrument.** It is asked before instruction and almost nobody can answer the second half affirmatively, which is the session's content: **cost is a design property that was not treated as one.** Item 1 remains the second instrument and separates a bill from a model in a single answer.

**4.1 The opening items are an unscaffolded generation phase.** The design is Productive Failure — generate, fail, then be taught — with the binding condition that the generation phase must not be helped, because guidance that lowers the failure rate lowers the learning ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). This is why the release rule gates §§4, 5 and 9. **Its limits are recorded: a trade book resting on school, university and laboratory evidence, with no adult-professional study in its reference list.**

**4.2 Why exercise 3 is a comparison rather than a rewrite.** The same research tested exactly this: presenting a correct answer beside a learner's wrong one produced no benefit, and only the condition *instructed to compare them* learned more. Exercise 3 forbids rewriting the item-2 answer until the learner has written one sentence naming the difference — **and the criterion deliberately marks the honest "no, cost was not a criterion" as the stronger answer**, because it locates a class of decision the team makes without a cost input, which is actionable, whereas a retrofitted justification is not.

**4.3 The workflow writes the token path before any price.** This is the ordering that makes the durable claim experienceable rather than assertable: a learner who prices from a rate card learns arithmetic, and a learner who prices from the token path discovers that a chunking decision made three sessions ago dominates the bill. **Exercise 2 is the demonstration — move one architectural parameter and watch the capacity requirement move** — and it is placed early because the sensitivity is what makes the rest credible.

**4.4 Why the capacity posture carries evidence rather than a decision.** Recording *we committed* is a statement of policy. Recording *we deployed, confirmed capacity on this date, then committed* is a statement a successor can check, and it is the only form in which the documented order survives the person who followed it. **The artefact asks for the sequence actually followed, including the wrong one**, because a record that only permits the correct answer collects no information.

**4.5 The handover step is the session's design.** The learner sits with a customer engineer while they run the model, touches nothing, and records where they got stuck. **The prohibition is the movement**; without it the step becomes a demonstration with a spectator, and the evidence it was designed to produce disappears. The closing exercise narrows it to one question with a definite answer — what would it cost to double the volume of one thing they care about — because a general invitation to explore is not testable.

**4.6 Why the agent is turned on the model rather than used to build it.** The two prompts are adversarial by construction: argue that each claimed measurement is an estimate in a measurement's clothing, and find the term that dominates at ten times the volume. **Both are locating operations, which is what the tooling is comparatively good at**, and neither asks it to decide what matters. Rejections are recorded, on the standing rule that an agent which agreed with you was not used as an instrument.

**4.7 The efficiency-warning failure mode is drawn from a contradiction inside one public document**, where a sustainability checklist recommends a set of measures that the page immediately beneath it argues may each be net negative in context. It is used as a specimen of an internal contradiction, not as a claim about any technique, **and the session says so** — because the alternative would be to make exactly the unwarranted claim the specimen exists to warn about.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The infrastructure unit.** The likeliest failure and the most invisible, because the model will look professional. *Countermeasure:* opening item 1, and the [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) element requiring cost per unit of the customer's work.

**The price-list model.** The learner starts from a rate card and never writes a token path, so nothing in the model attributes cost to a decision. *Countermeasure:* the ordering of the workflow, and the artefact's requirement that each token-path term name the architectural decision that set it. **§9's prediction 5 predicts this countermeasure failing.**

**The commitment before the confirmation.** A reservation purchased before a deployment established that capacity exists. *Countermeasure:* the capacity posture's evidence field and the requirement to record the sequence actually followed; and exercise 1, which forces the permission, availability and price distinction before any commitment is discussed.

**Dedicated capacity used as autoscaling.** *Countermeasure:* named in the failure list with its warning sign, and the material's statement of the two reasons the documentation itself gives.

**The gross benefit.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's explicit requirement that the delivery cost sit in the same document, and the warning sign — your value document has one column and it is the good one.

**The forecast that never met telemetry.** *Countermeasure:* the artefact's requirement that every remaining estimate be marked and dated, and the agent prompt that attacks measurements masquerading as such.

**Free observability.** *Countermeasure:* the line item, which is an assessed element, and the direct dependency on [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md)'s deletion list; plus exercise 5, which requires at least one signal to survive the question test and fail the price test.

**The cache set loosely.** A cost mechanism tuned for hit rate, silently answering the wrong question. *Countermeasure:* the material states that the source documentation does not discuss the correctness risk at all, so the learner is told in advance that no documentation will raise it for them.

**The model you take with you.** The failure the session's last two movements exist against. *Countermeasure:* the named customer-side person with a date, assessed at [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md), and the requirement that they have run it rather than been trained on it.

**The learner reaching for the keyboard.** *Countermeasure:* the prohibition, stated in both the workflow and the closing exercise, because it will be broken silently and out of kindness.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: requiring the delivery cost in the value document asks a learner to disclose commercial information that is not theirs to disclose, and will teach a behaviour that gets them into trouble.**

At its strongest: day rates, margins and staffing costs are commercially confidential in most delivery organisations, frequently contractually so. **A graduate who puts them in a customer-facing document has not been rigorous; they have breached a confidence**, and the session is teaching an artefact that a competent employer would prohibit. Worse, the requirement is unevenly available: a learner inside a permissive organisation can satisfy it and a learner inside a restrictive one cannot, so the element marks organisational policy rather than the learner's judgement.

**Conceded in part, and the requirement is narrower than the objection assumes — but the session does not currently say so and that is a defect.** What the element needs is that **the cost of delivery appears as a magnitude the customer can weigh against the benefit**, not that its composition is disclosed. A total, a band agreed with the commercial owner, or a stated effort in days all satisfy the argument, and none requires disclosing a rate. **The declined fix is to drop the element where an organisation prohibits disclosure.** Declined because the prohibition is almost always about composition rather than about totals, and dropping the element would license the gross benefit the whole session is built against. **The residual problem is real: a learner in a genuinely restrictive setting will have to negotiate for the total, and this session gives them no help with that negotiation.**

**Objection 2: the transfer requirement is invented, and the session teaches it with the confidence of a practice.**

At its strongest: the corpus contains no exit procedure of any kind. It mechanises the teardown of the delivering organisation's own assets to the level of a register-triggered state machine, and describes transferring nothing — no receiving owner, no exit criteria, no acceptance, no support taper. **The one sentence this session builds on is a reflection inside a business-value narrative, not a step in a procedure**, and constructing a required, assessed transfer act from it is inference from an absence, which is weak.

**Conceded without qualification.** The requirement is class 3 and the session says so. **The rebuttal is only that the absence is itself diagnostic**: the model on offer is withdrawal as estate hygiene rather than withdrawal as transfer of a going concern, its question is whether the delivering organisation's house is clean rather than whether the customer can run the system, **and only the second is an engineering problem.** That is an argument, and arguments are not findings. **The declined fix is to move the transfer requirement out of this session and into [Stage 8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md), where transfer is the subject.** It is declined because the cost model is the one artefact whose transfer must be tested while the system is still being changed — a model handed over at the end has never been corrected by anybody but its author. **The placement is a judgement and the warrant for it is nil.**

**Objection 3: anchoring a durable claim about cost on one vendor's capacity model teaches procurement for a product, and the mechanism it claims to rest on is borrowed from papers whose results the session refuses to use.**

At its strongest, and this is two objections braided together. **The commitment material is a single vendor's design**, documented by that vendor, with a choice architecture that is that vendor's, and the deploy-verify-commit rule is a fact about how one company allocates capacity rather than a principle. A cohort taught it will carry a procurement habit and mistake it for engineering judgement. **And the mechanical warrant underneath is thinner than it appears**: the session leans on inference and serving literature for the claim that architecture sets cost, then declines to reproduce any figure from either, and leans on retrieval literature of which **one source was not read in full at all.** A design that cites four technical papers and uses no result from any of them is decorating an argument with citations.

**Partly conceded on the first, and squarely rejected on the second.** On vendor specificity: the durable claim is not the product's rule but the distinction underneath it — **permission, availability and price are three different things that a procurement conversation collapses into one** — and that distinction transfers to reserved instances, committed-use discounts, capacity contracts and every other reserved-capacity arrangement a graduate will meet. The vendor's documentation is used because it is unusually explicit about the distinction, not because the product matters, and the perishable half is quarantined in the perishability table and in the requirement to date every verification.

On the mechanical warrant: **the refusal to reproduce figures is the point rather than a weakness.** What the inference and serving literature establishes is the *direction and the mechanism* — that decode is memory-bound, that the key-value cache limits context, that batch size caps throughput, that compression trades recall for memory — and each of those is an architectural fact that holds independently of the coefficients measured on any particular accelerator. **A magnitude taken from a 2022 accelerator and applied to a 2026 deployment would be exactly the defect this repository exists to prevent.** The unread retrieval source is a genuine deficiency and is flagged in the session's own limits, in the further reading, and here: **no experimental result from it is asserted anywhere, and it must be obtained before any assessed use.**

**The declined fix is to teach the cost mechanism from a measured local benchmark instead of from the literature** — have each learner benchmark their own deployment and derive the terms empirically. Declined on two grounds. It would displace the session's actual subject with an infrastructure exercise most cohorts cannot resource. **And a locally measured coefficient would be repeated**, travelling further than the caveats attached to it, which is the failure mode this repository was built around. **The cost of declining is that the learner leaves with a correct mechanism and no numbers, which feels less useful than it is.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners' first answer to opening item 1 will name an infrastructure unit. *Instrument:* the submitted opening answers, coded *customer work unit* / *infrastructure unit* / *absent* by a second reader, before instruction.

**Prediction 2.** Most cost models will not carry observability as a line until the third work step. *Instrument:* the model recorded before and after that step, with a presence check for the line.

**Prediction 3.** Cost models will carry the delivery cost as a total more often than as a composition, where they carry it at all. *Instrument:* the delivery-cost line, coded *total or band present* / *composition disclosed* / *absent*. **This is the instrument attached to §8's first objection**, and a high absent rate would mean the narrowing conceded there has not reached the session's own text.

**Prediction 4.** Where the cost-bearing decision is identified, most learners will report that cost was not a criterion when it was made. *Instrument:* the named decision's provenance field, coded *cost was a criterion* / *cost was not a criterion* / *cannot establish*. **If most report that cost was a criterion, either the cohort's systems are unusual or the field is being answered aspirationally, and the element should require the decision record from [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md) as evidence.**

**Prediction 5.** A majority of first-draft models will contain a price before they contain a token path, and a substantial minority will never acquire one. *Instrument:* the model's revision history or, absent that, a presence check for the token-path section coded against whether each term names the decision that set it. **This predicts the failure of §5's countermeasure against the price-list model**, and it is the prediction this paper most expects to be confirmed.

**Prediction 6.** After exercise 2, learners will identify a different decision as cost-bearing than the one they named in opening item 2. *Instrument:* the item-2 answer against the cost-bearing decision recorded in the artefact, coded *same* / *different* / *cannot tell*. **If they are mostly the same, either the cohort's intuitions are good or the sensitivity exercise is not being run**, and the two are distinguishable only by whether the exercise's recorded output exists.

**Prediction 7.** Capacity postures that record a commitment will more often assert the deploy-verify-commit order than evidence it. *Instrument:* the evidence field, coded *deployment identifier and date present* / *order asserted without evidence* / *order not stated*. **A large gap means the documented sequence is being repeated as a slogan**, which is the failure mode a rule inherited from documentation is most prone to.

**Prediction 8.** Learners will intervene during the handover despite the prohibition. *Instrument:* an observer recording *intervened* / *did not intervene* per learner, with the elapsed point of intervention noted where it occurred.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a cost denominated in infrastructure units cannot function as a constraint; that **the architecture sets the token path and the token path sets the bill**, so cost is decided at design time and merely observed at run time; that permission, availability and price are three different things and a commitment made before the workload's shape is known is a bet; that a benefit stated without its cost fails the source's own thesis; and that a capability the recipient has not exercised has not transferred.

**What the evidence licenses.** The forecast-instrument-refine method is class 1 — verified as the practice of the source organisation — **and nothing establishes that it produces better forecasts than any alternative, because no cost outcome is reported anywhere in the corpus.** The mechanical claim is class 2 in this paper's usage: **direction and mechanism from the inference, serving and retrieval literature, with every magnitude deliberately withheld** because the coefficients belong to particular hardware, models and eras. **One of the four technical sources was not read in full and no result from it is asserted.** The commitment material is class 4 — grounded in vendor documentation read in full, definitional rather than empirical, **with every concrete number living on companion pages that were not fetched.** The transfer and delivery-cost requirements are class 3. **No source reports whether any delivered system survived, was maintained by the customer, or was still running months later.**

**Where the design is thinnest.** §8's third objection. The mechanism is warranted and unnumbered; the commitment rule is a vendor's; the unread retrieval source is a real gap. All three are recorded rather than smoothed.

**The concession worth remembering.** You are being taught a transfer act assembled from one sentence in material that contains no exit procedure. **Hold it as an argument**, and notice that the argument's strength comes entirely from the absence it is filling. **The one thing here that is not this design's argument is the deploy-verify-commit rule**, which is somebody else's documented guidance and is the strongest sentence in the session for exactly that reason.
