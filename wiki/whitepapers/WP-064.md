# WP-064 — Cost as a Design Constraint

## In plain terms

This session teaches the one thing the source material genuinely does well, and marks the two things it leaves out.

**What it does well is the cost side.** Consumption is forecast by engineers, instrumented by engineers, and refined against telemetry from the system itself. That is a real method, done properly, and this session teaches it as content rather than as specimen. Forecast, instrument, refine — and the third step is the one that separates a model from a proposal.

**The first thing it leaves out is any cost side in its own value claims.** Staffing, compute, the cost of the discovery activity itself, opportunity cost — all absent. **Every claim in the corpus is a gross benefit**, in a body of work whose organising thesis is that only realised value counts.

**The second is the handover.** The one place the right instinct appears — that the customer should be left holding the cost-modelling capability, because they will need it after the team disengages — sits as a reflection inside a narrative rather than as a step in any procedure. Nobody is required to transfer the model and nobody is required to check that it runs.

The session's two additions follow directly. **The delivery cost goes in the same document as the running cost.** And **somebody on the customer's side runs the model, with the learner watching and not touching anything.**

The unit matters more than it looks. **A figure per compute hour is a bill. A figure per unit of the customer's work is a model**, because only the second lets the customer ask their own questions of it.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching cost modelling inside an operations module, as a design constraint rather than a commercial exercise, and defends two requirements the source material does not make: that the delivery cost appear beside the running cost, and that a named person on the customer's side has run the model. The argument is that a cost expressed in infrastructure units cannot be interrogated by the customer whose work it prices; that a benefit with no cost beside it is a proposal rather than a result; and that a capability the recipient has not exercised has not transferred. The session installs a cost model in units of the customer's work, refined against the system's own telemetry, carrying observability as a line, naming at least one cost-bearing design decision with its untaken alternative, and handed over under observation. The paper concedes that the transfer requirement is assembled from a single reflective sentence in material containing no exit procedure at all, that no source reports any outcome for a transferred cost model, and that requiring the delivery cost may be professionally unavailable to a learner whose organisation does not disclose it.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A running system has a cost, and in most delivery arrangements nobody on the engineering team ever sees it as a number they can act on.

**Finance sees a bill. The team sees an architecture.** The mapping between them is performed once, during a procurement conversation, by somebody who was not present for either. By the time anybody asks which design decision is expensive, the decision has been made, the system is running, and the answer is expressed in units that cannot be traced back to a choice.

**The consequence is not overspending. It is that cost never functions as a constraint.** A constraint is something that changes a decision, and a figure nobody can attribute to a decision cannot change one. It becomes, instead, a quarterly surprise handled by procurement.

There is a second problem which is this stage's problem specifically. **The cost of a system is borne after the delivery team leaves**, and the capability to model it usually leaves with them. **A customer who inherits a running system and no cost model has inherited an obligation they cannot forecast**, and every subsequent decision they make about the system will be made without the input that mattered most.

The session's response is a model in the customer's units, refined against telemetry, containing the delivery cost, and run by the customer while the learner watches.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The model prices the signals that survived [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) and the components of the system model from [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md).

It assumes [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md), where the cost of doing nothing was sized; **that session established that a cost with no comparison is not a cost**, and this one applies it to the running system rather than to the decision to start. It assumes [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md), because naming a cost-bearing decision with its untaken alternative is a trade study in a smaller container and inherits its standard. And it assumes [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md), because the minimal valuable slice is the first place a learner met the idea that a unit of work should be a unit the customer recognises.

**The prior it works against is that cost is somebody else's discipline.** Learners arrive treating it as a commercial function, which is exactly the belief that makes it stop being a constraint.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is moderate and mostly arithmetic. The difficulty is elsewhere: the session requires a learner to put their own delivery cost next to the benefit, in a document a commercial reader will see, which is a political act performed by somebody with the least authority in the room.

**3.2 Why the unit must be the customer's.** A cost model is an instrument for asking questions, and the questions belong to whoever is paying. **A model denominated in infrastructure units answers questions about infrastructure**, which the customer does not have and cannot form. Changing the denominator is not presentation; it changes which questions the artefact can be used for.

**3.3 Why refinement against telemetry is the load-bearing step.** A forecast is a set of beliefs arranged arithmetically. **Refining it against what actually happened is the only step that lets the world disagree with it**, and it is the step the source does properly and most teams skip. A model that has never been corrected by observation is a proposal that has been formatted.

**3.4 Why observability appears as a line.** [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) required signals to be deleted and gave a discipline for choosing which. **The discipline has no teeth while telemetry is free**, and it is free precisely because it is accounted as overhead. Making it a line is what connects the two sessions.

**3.5 Why a gross benefit is not a result.** This is the corpus's own thesis turned on the corpus. **A delivery can satisfy every agreed criterion and still produce nothing of value** — and a benefit stated without its cost cannot establish that it produced any, because the comparison the thesis demands has only one side. The session's requirement is not an additional standard; **it is the source's own standard applied to the source's own artefact.**

**3.6 Why the customer must run it, not be shown it.** Demonstration establishes that the demonstrator can operate the thing. **The only observation that establishes transfer is the recipient operating it unaided**, and the places they get stuck are the specification for the runbook [S072](/wiki/seminars/S072.md) will write. That evidence is unobtainable any other way and is discarded the moment the learner reaches for the keyboard.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 1 — what does one unit of your system's work cost, and what is the unit — is the instrument.** It separates a bill from a model in a single answer and it is asked before instruction, so that the learner's default denominator is visible before it is corrected.

**The work movement's sixth step is the session's design.** The learner sits with a customer engineer while they run the model, touches nothing, and records where they got stuck. **The prohibition is the movement**; without it the step becomes a demonstration with a spectator, and the evidence it was designed to produce disappears.

**The review movement narrows it to one question — what would it cost to double the volume of one thing they care about — because a single question with a definite answer is testable and a general invitation to explore is not.** The learner may watch and may not help, and whatever the customer engineer could not do is the model's real specification.

**The efficiency-warning failure mode is drawn from a contradiction inside one public document**, where a sustainability checklist recommends a set of measures that the page immediately beneath it argues may each be net negative in context. It is used as a specimen of an internal contradiction, not as a claim about any technique, **and the session says so** — because the alternative would be to make exactly the unwarranted claim the specimen exists to warn about.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The infrastructure unit.** The likeliest failure and the most invisible, because the model will look professional. *Countermeasure:* opening item 1, and the [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) element requiring cost per unit of the customer's work.

**The gross benefit.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's explicit requirement that the delivery cost sit in the same document, and the warning sign — your value document has one column and it is the good one.

**The forecast that never met telemetry.** *Countermeasure:* the ordering of the work movement, and the standard's requirement that every remaining estimate be marked and dated.

**Free observability.** *Countermeasure:* the line item, which is an assessed element, and the direct dependency on [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md)'s deletion list.

**The model you take with you.** The failure the session's last two movements exist against. *Countermeasure:* the named customer-side person with a date, assessed at [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md), and the requirement that they have run it rather than been trained on it.

**The learner reaching for the keyboard.** *Countermeasure:* the prohibition, stated in both the work and review movements, because it will be broken silently and out of kindness.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: requiring the delivery cost in the value document asks a learner to disclose commercial information that is not theirs to disclose, and will teach a behaviour that gets them into trouble.**

At its strongest: day rates, margins and staffing costs are commercially confidential in most delivery organisations, frequently contractually so. **A graduate who puts them in a customer-facing document has not been rigorous; they have breached a confidence**, and the session is teaching an artefact that a competent employer would prohibit. Worse, the requirement is unevenly available: a learner inside a permissive organisation can satisfy it and a learner inside a restrictive one cannot, so the element marks organisational policy rather than the learner's judgement.

**Conceded in part, and the requirement is narrower than the objection assumes — but the session does not currently say so and that is a defect.** What the element needs is that **the cost of delivery appears as a magnitude the customer can weigh against the benefit**, not that its composition is disclosed. A total, a band agreed with the commercial owner, or a stated effort in days all satisfy the argument, and none requires disclosing a rate. **The declined fix is to drop the element where an organisation prohibits disclosure.** Declined because the prohibition is almost always about composition rather than about totals, and dropping the element would license the gross benefit the whole session is built against. **The residual problem is real: a learner in a genuinely restrictive setting will have to negotiate for the total, and this session gives them no help with that negotiation.**

**Objection 2: the transfer requirement is invented, and the session teaches it with the confidence of a practice.**

At its strongest: the corpus contains no exit procedure of any kind. It mechanises the teardown of the delivering organisation's own assets to the level of a register-triggered state machine, and describes transferring nothing — no receiving owner, no exit criteria, no acceptance, no support taper. **The one sentence this session builds on is a reflection inside a business-value narrative, not a step in a procedure**, and constructing a required, assessed transfer act from it is inference from an absence, which is weak.

**Conceded without qualification.** The requirement is class 3 and the session says so. **The rebuttal is only that the absence is itself diagnostic**: the model on offer is withdrawal as estate hygiene rather than withdrawal as transfer of a going concern, its question is whether the delivering organisation's house is clean rather than whether the customer can run the system, **and only the second is an engineering problem.** That is an argument, and arguments are not findings. **The declined fix is to move the transfer requirement out of this session and into [Stage 8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md), where transfer is the subject.** It is declined because the cost model is the one artefact whose transfer must be tested while the system is still being changed — a model handed over at the end has never been corrected by anybody but its author. **The placement is a judgement and the warrant for it is nil.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners' first answer to opening item 1 will name an infrastructure unit. *Instrument:* the submitted opening answers, coded *customer work unit* / *infrastructure unit* / *absent* by a second reader, before instruction.

**Prediction 2.** Most cost models will not carry observability as a line until the third work step. *Instrument:* the model recorded before and after that step, with a presence check for the line.

**Prediction 3.** Learners will intervene during the review movement despite the prohibition. *Instrument:* an observer recording *intervened* / *did not intervene* per learner, with the elapsed point of intervention noted where it occurred.

**Prediction 4.** Where the cost-bearing decision is identified, most learners will report that cost was not a criterion when it was made. *Instrument:* the named decision's provenance field, coded *cost was a criterion* / *cost was not a criterion* / *cannot establish*. **If most report that cost was a criterion, either the cohort's systems are unusual or the field is being answered aspirationally, and the element should require the decision record from [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md) as evidence.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a cost denominated in infrastructure units cannot function as a constraint; that a benefit stated without its cost fails the source's own thesis; and that a capability the recipient has not exercised has not transferred.

**What the evidence licenses.** The forecast-instrument-refine method is class 1 — verified as the practice of the source organisation — **and nothing establishes that it produces better forecasts than any alternative, because no cost outcome is reported anywhere in the corpus.** The transfer requirement and the delivery-cost requirement are class 3. **No source reports whether any delivered system survived, was maintained by the customer, or was still running months later.**

**The concession worth remembering.** You are being taught a transfer act assembled from one sentence in material that contains no exit procedure. **Hold it as an argument**, and notice that the argument's strength comes entirely from the absence it is filling.
