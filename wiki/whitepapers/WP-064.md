# WP-064 — Capacity Is Not Quota

**Seminar day:** [S064](wiki/seminars/S064.md) · **Module:** [M13](wiki/modules/M13.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](wiki/quarters/Q7.md)
**Strands:** SY · ME · IS · **Complexity class:** 5
**Platform anchor:** PTU quota against PTU capacity; hourly billing; Azure Reservations; the gateway's rate limit against its period quota · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (write) · Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends separating four quantities that present identically — demand, capacity, quota and budget — on the ground that each has a different owner, a different cadence and a different kind of argument, and that the cost of confusing them is paid in approaching the wrong party rather than in computing the wrong number. **It defends doing that on a platform whose own documentation carries the distinction as a call-out**: quota is defined as a policy limit with no cost, capacity as what is deployable now, and the two are separated by a sentence saying that holding the first does not give you the second. It defends four situations sharing one presenting symptom, with a mix shift as payload, because a mix shift is a capacity figure expiring while nobody touches the system and requires the two preceding days to be composed — and it defends having the room **produce three of the four itself**, since a quota refusal costs nothing to obtain and a period-budget refusal has a different status code from a rate refusal. It defends repeating S020's opening item at a nine-quarter gap and re-probing S039's constraint-mobility baseline in the same pretest. It defends a learning task whose conforming answer set includes declining to proceed, which is where the module's complexity class is actually earned. It reports discharge of two rule-6 obligations from S061 and S063, **and repair of the stage-versus-assert defect the previous revision recorded as the third instance of what is now instrumentation rule 7.** It concedes that quota is taught as negotiable with nobody to negotiate with, that its own scope statement binds this paper and not the artefact, and — the objection that bites hardest — that **the one refusal the day exists to teach is the one refusal the cohort cannot personally receive.**

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Four quantities present identically. When any of them binds, requests are refused, users complain, and a dashboard turns a colour. The presenting symptom carries no information about which one it is.

That would be a diagnostic problem and a mildly interesting one. What makes it the module's most consequential day is the second half: **each of the four is owned by a different party, and the four parties respond to entirely different arguments.**

- Capacity is argued with a measurement. The counterparty owns the system.
- Quota is argued with a purpose. The counterparty granted it and is protecting something.
- Budget is argued with a break-even. The counterparty signs.
- Demand is not argued with at all; it is forecast, and the forecast is the input to the other three.

So a misdiagnosis is not a wrong number. It is a conversation held with the wrong person, in which everything said is true and nothing said is relevant. **[S039](wiki/seminars/S039.md) taught that a stated constraint has a purpose and that finding the purpose moves it. That method applies to exactly one of the four**, and a student who applies it to capacity will try to negotiate with physics.

**On this platform the distinction is not the design's to argue for.** The documentation defines quota as a policy limit enforced by the provider with no associated cost, defines capacity as the amount actually available to deploy, and puts a call-out between them stating that holding the first does not guarantee the second and that an under-supplied region will simply fail the deployment. A second page adds that a reservation is a discount applied to a billing meter and does not guarantee capacity either. **Three sentences, written by the party that benefits least from admitting any of them**, and together they make the day's central claim a fact to be encountered rather than a distinction to be taught. That is unusual and the design should not waste it: the room is refused for quota inside two minutes, is refused on a period budget at the gateway inside five, and reads about the capacity refusal because it cannot obtain one. **§8 objection 4 is about that last clause and is the paper's hardest.**

The second problem is that the cohort now has a capacity figure and believes it is a number. [S062](wiki/seminars/S062.md) and [S063](wiki/seminars/S063.md) have between them established that the achievable concurrency depends on the request mix and that the refusal threshold moves with it, and neither day drew the conclusion: **a capacity figure carries a date and a mix, and it expires when either moves, without anybody changing anything.** Situation D exists to make that conclusion unavoidable, and the platform's own sizing method makes it arithmetic — two of its three inputs describe the traffic.

The third problem is the one the whole module has been building toward, and it is not technical. **This day asks a student to say "do not proceed" about a system their cohort operates, on evidence they produced, to a decision that has a date.** Every prior task in the programme had a conforming answer that was a plan. This one does not.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Conceptually high, technically low, and the module's only day with that shape.** Nothing on the day requires a measurement the students have not already taken. The load is in holding four quantities apart while each is described by numbers of the same kind, and in the interaction at step 7, where the recommendation depends on which quantity binds, which depends on a capacity figure whose validity depends on a mix.

Management:

- **All four situations are supplied complete**, with identical dashboard shapes and first-hour notes.
- **Every measurement step 2 requires already exists** from S061, S062 and S063; nothing is re-measured.
- **Step 6's break-even is worked with the room** and is deliberately simple arithmetic.
- **Step 3's divergence computation is supported.**
- **The demand change is supplied** rather than forecast, because forecasting is ME301's and would double the day.

**Step 7 is unsupported.** Expertise reversal applies to points 5 and 6 for any student with commercial procurement experience; the §5 fallback is written for them and moves them onto point 2's mix argument, which experience does not supply.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Answering a nine-quarter-old question about a system you now operate,* item 1.
- *Naming an owner for a number, before owners have been mentioned,* item 2.
- *Re-predicting constraint mobility against your own five-quarter-old answer,* items 3 and 4.
- *Four situations with one presenting symptom,* phase 3.
- *Stating the evidence that distinguishes your classification from three others,* phase 3.
- *Using a model that diverged, widened, rather than discarding it,* step 3.
- *Committing a recommendation whose conforming form may be refusal,* step 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Demand forecasting.* ME301's. The change is supplied, and building a forecast here would make the day about the forecast.
- *Contract negotiation.* [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md)'s, and §8 objection 2 argues the deferral is too long.
- *Financial modelling beyond a break-even.* Excluded; the break-even is the only piece of finance an engineer must be able to produce unaided, and everything beyond it belongs to whoever signs.
- *Multi-tenant and shared-capacity arrangements.* Named at [S063](wiki/seminars/S063.md) point 10 and left there.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** Point 5's commitment-shape enumeration — pay-per-use, reserved rate, and mixtures — is **safe** as a frame for choosing between the two families on a service whose demand you forecast yourself, on a single account, at a price that does not vary within the period. It is **unsafe** as a menu: it omits pre-emptible capacity, transfer or resale of unused commitment, and within-period price variation, **and in each of those three cases point 6's break-even is not the calculation that decides the question.** A student who computes a break-even where commitment is tradeable has computed the wrong quantity and will be confident in it. This is stated at phase 4 point 5, and §8 objection 3 records that **the day's artefact does not check that the student stayed inside the conditions the statement names.**

**Scope of the platform instance, in the same form, and its unsafe condition is temporal before it is anything else.** The commercial mechanics taught at points 5, 6, 9 and 10 — what quota is, what capacity is, what an hourly meter does, what a reservation discounts — are **safe** for deciding which party to approach, for structuring an ask, and for stating why an approval does not secure a machine. They are **unsafe** in three conditions.

- **Unsafe at any date other than the one on which the figures were retrieved.** Every rate, ratio, minimum size and discount is published without a version number on a page that changes without notice. A break-even is therefore a claim about a moment, and **the day's artefact element for this is the requirement that a break-even computed from an undated rate fails** — which is the strong form's demand that the unsafe condition have a failure attached, per the widening recorded on the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md).
- **Unsafe as a description of any other provider's commercial model.** The four quantities and their owners generalise; the mechanism by which quota is granted, the unit capacity is sold in, and the coupling between a discount and a deployment do not.
- **Unsafe as a guide to what a purchase secures.** This is the condition the documentation itself states twice, and it is the one students will most want to forget, because it contradicts what a purchase means everywhere else in their lives.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **situation C**.

A service degrades. No error occurs. No engineer did anything wrong. Every dashboard is green and stays green throughout. The cause is that money ran out before the period did, and the students discover that a system can fail for a reason that has no representation anywhere in the system. **Nothing else in six quarters has made that point**, and it is the affective centre of the module: cost is not an afterthought produced by a different department, because a different department's calendar can take your service down.

**The practical turn adds something to C that the design did not anticipate and now relies on.** The room causes C themselves, at the gateway, by exhausting a period budget — and the refusal that comes back is a *different status code* from the one a rate limit produces. Two refusals, visibly different, on a screen, one meaning *slow down* and the other meaning *you are finished until the calendar turns*. **The distinction the whole day is about is a two-character difference in a response**, and it arrives before any explanation.

Situation D carries the technical weight and is the payload for a different reason — it requires the previous two days to be composed and it defeats the room's expectation that capacity is a thing you measure once.

The second mechanism is **item 1's nine-quarter repeat**. A student answers a question they answered in their second quarter, about a system that is now theirs, and phase 8 shows both answers. The programme's spacing schedule stops being a policy at that moment and becomes a personal record.

The competence risk sits at step 7, and it is unusual in kind. A student who recommends *do not proceed* is telling their own cohort not to do something they want to do. The instructor states before phase 5 that refusal is a conforming answer, that it is written into the specification as one, and that the assessed quality is the evidence and the check date rather than the direction of the recommendation. **The specification says so in writing**, because a specification silent on the point will be read as requiring a yes.

Autonomy is supported by step 7's genuine three-way choice; competence by the fact that every measurement the day needs is already the student's own; relatedness by phase 6, which opens with a staff request that was true in every sentence and wrong, and closes with an agent plan that was correct in every step and expensive.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items carrying two named baseline re-probes — S020's opening question at nine quarters and S039's mobility items at five — and **the module's fourth sealed Copilot prediction**, whose subject is what an agent assumes it has and whom it does not ask.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals and a bridge that breaks a framework rather than introducing one.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four situations, one presenting symptom, D as payload and C as the affective centre. **Three of the four are produced by the room**; the fourth is declared as demonstrated rather than operated before the phase begins. **The phase now closes with two tasks rather than one**, the second being the party you would approach and the sentence you would say — which is §8 objection 1's repair.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Points 1 to 4 consolidate phase 3. Point 5 carries the scope statement. Point 6 enforces specification rule 2 and adds the retrieval-date requirement. **Point 8 is now consolidation of what phase 3 produced rather than assertion.** **Point 9 is the perishable platform point and point 10 the defaults point.**

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps plus a day-specific element. **Step 3 is S061's rule-6 receiving element; step 4 is S063's.** Step 7 is the module's class-5 element and permits refusal. The deliverable is a commitment in deployable form, and the artefact carries the prompt record, the intervention record, the frontier statement, and a quantity-attribution for every agent-produced figure.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A request that was true throughout and routed wrongly — then **an agent plan that was correct in every step, executed in the order a reasonable person would use, which bought a discount on a meter nothing was running on.**

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Reclassification with the resulting party named — the day's second rehearsal of point 8, now arriving after a first one at phase 3.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 1 against step 7; item 2's owner-omission rate; items 3 and 4 shown beside the same students' S039 answers; **item 5 opened, with the assumption count and the unconsulted-party count reported separately**; D's misclassification rate as the headline, with the phase 3 routing answers beside it.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has bought cloud capacity commercially will classify all four situations and will find points 5 and 6 elementary. Predicted failure: they treat the day as procurement and miss point 2, which is the only claim on the day their experience does not contain. Countermeasure: their phase 3 fallback is D's mechanism specifically — what would have to be true of the mix for the refusal threshold to have moved that far — and step 4's threshold-distance requirement cannot be answered from commercial experience at all.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is step 6, which is the first finance any student in the programme has produced. Countermeasures: the break-even is deliberately one division and one comparison; it is worked with the room; the demand change is supplied rather than forecast; and a student who cannot defend the forecast may state the break-even with the forecast marked as supplied, which is honest and is specification-conforming.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** A group that classifies A, B and C quickly is doing well; the difficulty is D and the evidence demand. Fallback if a group also classifies D correctly: **ask them what measurement, taken routinely, would have made D visible before it bound — and then whether that measurement would have been affordable to take continuously.** That question has no supplied answer, it is [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s territory arriving two weeks early, and it converts a diagnostic success into a design problem.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **learned powerlessness**. Three of the four quantities are owned by somebody else. A student may conclude that the engineering position is structurally weak — that you measure, you report, and other people decide — which is a demoralising and partly accurate reading, and it is the precise opposite of the disposition Q7 exists to build.

Countermeasures: point 3 establishes that one of the three is negotiable and gives the method; point 7 establishes that headroom expressed correctly is a thing a payer can buy, so the engineer controls whether the ask is actionable; step 7's third clause requires the student to draft the grantor request rather than to note that one is needed; and the instructor states once that the engineer is the only party in the system who can say which quantity binds, which is not a weak position. **§8 objection 2 argues that the countermeasures are rhetorical because the day supplies no counterparty.**

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md) (one week later, where the three numbers are presented to a payer), [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) (six weeks later, where this day's commitment meets the closed billing period), [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md) (four quarters later, where an adversary exhausts whichever quantity is weakest), [S083](wiki/seminars/S083.md) (risk communication) and [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md) (scope negotiation, where all four quantities are on a table with a counterparty).

**S070 at six weeks is the primary target and is the module's only forward link outside its own five days.** It is also the mechanism by which the quarter's class-5 claim is realised: a commitment made in week 64 meets a bill closed in week 68 and is defended in week 70.

S085's retrieval is the most important and the furthest away, and §8 objection 2 argues that the distance is a design error rather than a schedule.

Retained content: the four quantities with owners and cadences, capacity as a function of the mix, quota as a constraint with a purpose, budget as a rate, and the routing consequence. **Five items.** The commitment shapes at point 5 are deliberately not retained; they are perishable and the register forbids assessing them.

Own schedule: next morning, day 2, week 1, week 6.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md).

One specification item in SY301 with ME301. Per rule 1, the checked elements are the demand change, the four quantities with owners, cadences, dates and sources, the carried model with its divergence band in the same field, headroom as threshold distance in demand units, the binding quantity with distinguishing evidence and its named refusal, the commitment shape with break-even and forecast confidence and rate retrieval dates in the same field, the recommendation with check date and falsifying number, the grantor request where quota binds, **the quantity-attribution of every agent-produced figure**, the phase 7 outcome, **the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

Three hard requirements: **capacity, quota and budget presented as one number fails**; **a recommendation without a check date and a falsifying number fails**; and **a break-even computed from an undated rate fails.** One explicit permission: **a properly evidenced *do not proceed* passes**, stated in the specification because silence would be read as requiring assent. The third requirement is new and is §3.2's platform scope statement given an artefact element, per the widening recorded on the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md). **It does not close §8 objection 3**, which concerns a different unsafe condition, and the paper is explicit that a partial closure is not a closure.

**No perishable commercial detail is assessed.** Quota mechanics, unit definitions, provisioning modes, deployment categories, routing variants, prices and discounts are all listed as perishable in the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) and appear only as instances.

**Demonstrated rather than operated, and therefore not assessed:** being refused for capacity while holding sufficient quota, and purchasing a reservation. §8 objection 4 argues that this is the most consequential gap in the quarter and that the day's compensations do not close it.

**Rule-6 discharges, both reported as required by [WP-051](wiki/whitepapers/WP-051.md) §8.**

- [S061](wiki/seminars/S061.md) named this day as the response occasion for its step 4-against-step 5 divergence, specifying an artefact element in which the cost model is carried forward and its divergence stated as an uncertainty band rather than discarded. **The element exists as phase 5 step 3 and is an assessed checked element. The requirement that a divergent model be widened rather than dropped is what makes the element a response rather than an acknowledgement**, since discarding a bad model is the action a student would otherwise take and it destroys the measurement. The obligation is discharged.
- [S063](wiki/seminars/S063.md) named this day as the response occasion for its item 3 threshold-position error, specifying that headroom be expressed as distance to the measured refusal threshold in the units of the demand change. **The element exists as phase 5 step 4, is assessed, and the prohibition on utilisation percentages is written into the checked elements rather than into the warrant.** The obligation is discharged.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions: the phase 3 classification error rate, and specifically situation D's misclassification rate, responds at [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md) phase 3, where presenting capacity, quota and budget as three numbers with three owners is a required element and its violation is a published failure condition; and the step 6 commitment shape with its break-even responds at **[S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)**, where the cost position defended in the release decision must carry this day's commitment together with the realised spend of the billing period closing in week 68. **WP-065 §7 and WP-070 §7 must report whether those elements exist.** The second is a commitment on a day outside this module and is recorded as such.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day's most portable claim is the one it asserts. — Recorded, and now repaired.**

At its strongest, as the previous revision put it: point 8 — which number binds determines who you approach and what you ask them for — is the sentence students will use most often after they leave, it has the clearest engineering consequence of anything on the day, and **the room resists it**, because engineers escalate technically when the constraint is commercial and item 2 is constructed to catch exactly that: a student produces an excellent number and no owner.

Nothing generated it. Phase 3 staged the *classification* and stopped there; its closing task asked which measurement was missing, which is diagnostic. Phase 7 rehearsed the routing after phase 4 had supplied it, and phase 6 illustrated it in a case that was told rather than produced. A student who left holding point 8 held it because they were told, and this paper's own §1 says that a belief with an engineering consequence, held wrongly and confidently, is not dislodged that way.

**The fix cost two minutes and is made.** Phase 3's four situations already had four distinct owners built into them; the phase now requires each group to name **the party they would approach and the sentence they would say, for each situation, before the causes are revealed.** That generates point 8 rather than asserting it and produces a routing-error rate the day previously did not have. The reason previously given for declining it — that phase 3 would over-run — was a timing estimate rather than a design argument, and the phase's supplied-materials structure exists precisely so that the time is available. **The practical turn made the repair easier rather than harder**, because three of the four situations are now produced by the room rather than read, and the reading time they displace is what the routing task uses.

**The finding stands on the record even though the day now complies.** This was the third instance of the pattern [WP-049](wiki/whitepapers/WP-049.md) §8 opened and [WP-053](wiki/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §8 recorded as a second, and the argument it made — that a claim the day expects students to resist must be staged, not asserted — has since been adopted as **instrumentation rule 7** on the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md), which names this paper as one of the three instances. Repairing the day does not retract the instance, and it is left legible rather than tidied away, in the same way the scope-rule widenings are.

**Objection 2: the day teaches that quota is negotiable and supplies nobody to negotiate with.**

At its strongest: point 3 is the day's most consequential professional claim — quota is a stated constraint with a purpose, and [S039](wiki/seminars/S039.md)'s method moves it. S039 taught that claim with four negotiation transcripts, a real requester, and a phase 5 in which a constraint was actually moved. **This day has none of those.** The student writes a grantor request at step 7's third clause, and nobody receives it. The negotiation itself is deferred to [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md), four quarters away.

**The practical turn makes this objection worse rather than better, which is not what the design expected.** There *is* a real grantor and the students can reach it: additional quota is requested through a published form. But a form is not a counterparty. The documentation states that approval "might take several days" and gives no bound; it states no criterion; and **it nowhere says what the limit is protecting**, which is precisely the thing S039's method requires you to discover before you can move anything. So the day now contains a real, live, reachable grantor whose purpose is undiscoverable and whose response time is unbounded — an excellent illustration of a constraint you cannot negotiate with, and **exactly the wrong illustration for the claim point 3 is making.**

A claim that a constraint is movable, taught with no opportunity to move one and with a live counter-example in front of the room, produces a belief rather than a competence — and the specific failure it produces is a graduate who tells a vendor's account manager that quota is negotiable and cannot conduct the conversation that follows. That is worse than not raising it.

**The fix was available at no additional staffing cost and was declined.** [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md), one week later, puts two real parties in front of every student. **One of them could have been the quota grantor rather than a second payer.** The design's reason is that S065's two counterparties are constructed to differ about *money* rather than about *permission*, so that the entrustment judgement is about a cost argument and stays about one. That is a coherent reason and it is weaker than what it displaced: the quarter has exactly two occasions with real counterparties and spends both on the same kind of conversation.

This is conceded and not resolved. The deferral to S085 is recorded here so that [WP-085](wiki/whitepapers/WP-085.md) is on notice that it is receiving a competence taught four quarters earlier with no rehearsal, which is the condition the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s deferral rule exists to make visible.

**Objection 3: the day's scope statement binds this paper and not the artefact — the second instance of a question the compliance register left open.**

At its strongest: §3.2 states, in the strong form, that point 5's commitment-shape frame is unsafe where commitment is tradeable, where capacity is pre-emptible, or where price varies within the period, **and that in all three cases the break-even at point 6 is the wrong calculation.** Nothing in the day's checked elements requires the student to state that their break-even was computed under the conditions where it applies. A student may therefore produce a fully conforming artefact containing a break-even that does not decide the question, and the specification cannot see it.

The [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s compliance register records this shape at one instance, from [WP-049](wiki/whitepapers/WP-049.md) §8, and states plainly why it was left open: a rule requiring every scope statement to appear as an assessed artefact element would be substantially more expensive than the four widenings that preceded it, and one instance is not evidence that the gap is load-bearing. The register then says: *"It is stated here so that a second instance is recognised as a second instance."*

**This is the second instance**, and it is recorded as one. The paper does not argue for closing the question, because the cost argument the register gives has not changed. It argues only that the entry be made, in the register's own terms:

> \| [WP-064](wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 — [S064](wiki/seminars/S064.md) restricts its commitment-shape frame and its break-even to non-tradeable, non-pre-emptible, within-period-flat pricing, and no artefact element requires a student to state that their break-even was computed inside those conditions \| Scope rule, strong form \| **Recorded** \| Second instance of the binding-surface question opened at [WP-049](wiki/whitepapers/WP-049.md) §8; the rule as written binds the whitepaper and not the day, and whether it should bind both remains the open question \|

The cheap fix exists and is named for completeness: add *the conditions under which this break-even is the deciding calculation* to step 6's checked elements. One clause. It is not taken here because taking it at one day and not at the other twenty-three would make the register's open question harder to see rather than easier, and the question is worth more open than quietly answered at a single site.

**Objection 4: the one refusal the day exists to teach is the one refusal the cohort cannot receive.**

This is the objection that bites hardest on the practical turn, and it is the paper's most serious.

At its strongest: the day's title is a claim about the difference between permission and availability. The room can obtain the permission refusal — a deployment request beyond quota, refused in seconds at no cost. It can obtain the budget refusal at the gateway. It can produce the mix-driven expiry with arithmetic. **It cannot obtain the refusal that makes the title true**: being told that you have the permission, that you have the money, that the arithmetic is right, and that the machine is not there. Producing that requires holding quota the programme does not hold, in a region under load, at a moment of somebody else's choosing.

So the day teaches three of its four situations by experience and its central one by reading. **And it is not an arbitrary one of the four.** The three the room produces are all cases where a limit somebody wrote was reached. The fourth is the case where nothing was reached, nothing was exceeded, everything was permitted and the answer was still no — which is the case with no analogue anywhere else in the programme, and the only one that could not be reconstructed from S020's apparatus.

The available rebuttals are three and none is sufficient. The room reads the capacity API and watches availability differ by region and by hour, which demonstrates that capacity is dynamic and does not demonstrate being refused. The recorded session shows the refusal happening to somebody else, which is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s permitted fallback and is explicitly not assessed. And the documentation states the fact in two places, which makes it citable and not experienced. **A programme whose entire argument is that a student cannot learn that quota is not capacity by being told has, at the one day where it matters most, told them.**

**The objection is conceded without qualification.** What the design can say is what the gap costs and what it does not. It does not cost the classification: a student can distinguish the four quantities, name their owners, and route an ask correctly having produced three refusals and read about the fourth, and phase 3's routing task measures exactly that. **What it costs is the affective half** — the specific professional experience of a correct argument meeting an unavailable world, which is the experience [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md)'s payer A is supposed to supply one week later and supplies in a different currency. That substitution is a guess and this paper cannot evidence it.

**Two fixes are named and both are outside this design's authority.** The first is institutional: a small standing quota allocation in a region under sustained demand, held by the programme, whose purpose is to be exhausted. It would cost nothing in money — quota has no cost, which is the day's own point 9 — and it would require an approval the programme has not sought. **The second is cheaper and is a compromise: run the deployment attempt at a size chosen so that it fails, and record which refusal came back.** A subscription with zero quota gives a permission refusal every time; a subscription with a small allocation, asked for slightly more capacity than the region has spare, gives whichever refusal the world produces that morning — and **the uncertainty is the lesson.** The design has not taken it because it cannot guarantee the second refusal ever occurs, and a phase whose payload is contingent on regional demand is a phase that fails silently in a quiet week.

That reason is honest and it is also the shape of the objection restated: **the day cannot manufacture a scarcity it does not experience, and a programme that teaches capacity from a position of abundance is teaching the wrong side of it.** Recorded, unresolved, and named in [M13](wiki/modules/M13.md) and [Q7](wiki/quarters/Q7.md) so that it is visible to anyone deciding whether to run this module.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At item 2, a majority will name a number and omit an owner. *Instrument:* item 2 responses coded for the presence of a named owner. **This is the day's cleanest evidence for point 1 and it is taken before any instruction**; if a majority do name owners, the day's central distinction is already held and phase 3's construction is over-built.

**Prediction 2.** Situation D will be misclassified as a capacity event by more than half the cohort, and situation C will be classified correctly by more than half but will produce the largest number of unprompted written comments. *Instrument:* phase 3 classifications coded per situation, with free-text volume recorded per situation. **The split is the claim: D is the cognitive payload and C is the affective one, and the design predicts they will not be the same case.**

**Prediction 3.** Items 3 and 4 will show more constraints predicted movable than the same students predicted at S039, and the increase will be concentrated on the commercial constraints rather than the technical ones. *Instrument:* items 3 and 4 against the same students' S039 items 1 and 4, coded by constraint type. **If the increase is uniform across both types, the cohort has become generally more optimistic rather than having learned that commercial constraints have purposes**, which is a different and less useful outcome.

**Prediction 4.** Fewer than one in six recommendations at step 7 will be *do not proceed* or *proceed under a named condition that is not met*. *Instrument:* step 7 recommendations coded by direction, with the evidence quality coded independently. **If the refusal rate is near zero while evidence quality is high, the specification's explicit permission is not being believed**, and the countermeasure — stating it in writing — has failed at the first day it was tried.

**Prediction 5.** At phase 3's second closing task, the routing-error rate will be highest for situation C and lowest for situation A, and the modal error on C will be to name a technical owner. *Instrument:* the phase 3 routing answers coded per situation against the correct party. **This is the instrument objection 1's repair created and the day previously did not have.** The predicted pattern is point 8's claim stated as a measurement: engineers route commercially-caused failures technically, and C is the situation with no technical cause at all.

**Prediction 6.** Fewer than one in five item 5 sealed predictions will name a *party* the agent fails to consult, while a majority will name a *quantity* it assumes. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded into the two categories, against what the agent produced. **The gap between the two rates is the module's frontier finding.** A cohort that predicts an agent will assume capacity it does not have is reasoning about the agent's knowledge; a cohort that predicts it will not ask the signatory is reasoning about the agent's position in an organisation, which is the harder model and the one point 8 requires.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The commercial mechanics at phase 3 and phase 4 points 5, 6, 9 and 10 are taken from Microsoft Learn's provisioned throughput concepts page for Foundry Models and its billing and cost-management companion, together with the API Management token limit policy reference, **all read on 2026-07-26**, and from the repository notes [provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md](research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md) and [apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md). What the documentation says: that quota is a policy limit enforced by the provider, granted per subscription, per region and per deployment type, with no associated cost, and with separate pools per deployment type; that capacity is the amount per model version actually available to deploy, allocated at deployment time and held for the deployment's lifetime; that **holding quota does not guarantee that capacity is available and an under-supplied region fails the deployment**; that capacity availability changes through the day with customer demand across regions and models; that deleting or scaling down a deployment releases capacity with no guarantee of getting it back; that additional quota is requested through a form and approval "might take several days"; that a provisioned deployment is billed per unit of capacity per hour from creation to deletion regardless of tokens consumed and **cannot be paused**; that the documentation advises against scaling such deployments with traffic, for two stated reasons; that a reservation is a one-month or one-year financial discount applied to the billing meter rather than to a deployment, purchased per deployment type, loosely coupled, with excess deployed capacity billed hourly and unused reserved quantity not refunded on scale-down; that **reservations do not guarantee capacity and the correct sequence is deploy, confirm, then purchase**; that overflow from a saturated dedicated deployment can be redirected to a standard one and is then billed per token; and, from the gateway, that exceeding a rate returns one status code while exceeding a period quota returns another. **What version it says it at:** none of these pages carries a version number or revision date; the provisioned-throughput material exists in two live variants for two portal generations, and **the two differ in at least one respect — one labels the overflow capability as preview and the other does not.** That discrepancy is recorded rather than resolved and is shown to students as an instance of why an as-of date is not optional.

**Six silences are recorded and three are taught.** **No price, per-model throughput divisor, minimum deployment size, output-to-input ratio or reservation discount percentage appears on any page read**; all are deferred to pricing pages and a calculator, which is why every commercial figure in the day is student-retrieved and dated and why an undated rate fails the specification. **Quota approval time is given as "might take several days" with no bound and no criterion**, and **the purpose the quota protects is nowhere stated** — which is the silence §8 objection 2 turns on and the reason S039's method has nothing to work with here. **Capacity availability is stated to vary through the day and is not characterised**: no distribution, no seasonality, no regional comparison, so the day can say that availability moves and cannot say how much. **The additional latency introduced by prioritising dedicated capacity before overflow is stated to exist and is not quantified.** **Rate and quota enforcement at the gateway is described as approximate with no bound published.** And consistent with the folder-wide asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), **the material states limits, periods, sequences and status codes throughout and states no accuracy or frequency anywhere.**

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** Long-gap repetition of a prior assessment item, baseline re-probing, contrasting cases with the presenting symptom held constant, pre-commitment before disclosure, role-based reclassification in peer articulation, and error management training are cited for **direction and mechanism only. No effect size is asserted for any of them.** **No quota limit, price, discount, commitment term, provisioning mode, throughput divisor or break-even utilisation is asserted for any named provider or product**; all commercial figures in the day are the substrate's own or are retrieved by students with their dates, are marked perishable, and are excluded from assessment. **No effect size is asserted for AI-assisted productivity anywhere in this paper.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The four-quantity separation with owners and cadences; the four situations with one presenting symptom and D as payload; the decision to have the room produce three of the four; the nine-quarter repeat of S020's opening item; the requirement that a divergent cost model be widened rather than discarded; headroom expressed as threshold distance in demand units; the undated-rate fail condition; the explicit specification permission for refusal; and all six §9 predictions are this design's own reasoning, untested. **The §8 objection-1 finding is now recorded as repaired**: the two-minute fix is made at phase 3, the instance stands on the record because it is one of the three that produced instrumentation rule 7, and the practical turn is noted as having made the repair cheaper rather than more expensive. The §8 objection-2 concession is unresolved, is **worsened rather than helped by the platform** — a reachable grantor with an undiscoverable purpose is the wrong illustration for a claim that purposes can be found — and puts [WP-085](wiki/whitepapers/WP-085.md) on notice that it inherits an unrehearsed competence. The §8 objection-3 finding **records the second instance of the compliance register's open question about the scope rule's binding surface, in the register's own terms, and declines to close it**; the new undated-rate condition closes a different unsafe condition and the paper is explicit that this is not a partial closure of the recorded one. **The §8 objection-4 concession is unqualified and is the paper's most serious: the day's central refusal is demonstrated rather than operated, the substitution costs the affective half of the lesson rather than the classification, both available fixes lie outside this design's authority, and the honest statement is that a programme teaching capacity from a position of abundance is teaching the wrong side of it.**
