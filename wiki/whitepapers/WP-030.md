## In plain terms

This is the stage's second assessment and its class boundary.

Every previous assessment in this programme told the candidate what to produce. **This one does not.** The candidate chooses the scope at which the design is written, and the scope determines the standard they will be marked against.

The published convention supplies four shapes — a task, a feature or story, a milestone or epic, and a technical investigation — each with an explicit heading list. **The escalation from feature scope to milestone scope adds exactly one heading, and that heading is about operation.**

That is the sharpest claim the publication makes about design writing, and it is worth stating precisely because it is unusually testable. **A design that leaves the scope of a single piece of work acquires a runtime owner.** Everything else in the milestone template is already in the feature template; the difference is the point at which somebody must be named who will run the thing after it exists.

It is a convention, not a finding, and the assessment says so. **But it is a convention you can check against any design document in minutes**, which is worth more than a claim you cannot check at all.

## Abstract

This paper defends an assessment in which the candidate selects the standard they are marked against, and in which the assessor's own preference about scope is explicitly excluded from the marking. The argument is that design writing proportionate to the decision is the judgement being tested, that the published four-scope convention makes the judgement checkable through a single distinguishing heading concerning operation, and that marking whether the candidate chose the scope an assessor would have chosen would return the session to a supplied criterion and undo the class boundary. It marks seven items including that at least one risk be carried unmitigated, that open questions name who would close them, and that thresholds be carried unchanged from the requirement set. The paper concedes that the four scopes have no warrant beyond one organisation's convention, that excluding the assessor's scope preference makes the central judgement almost unfalsifiable, and that the sources record no instance of the failure the assessment exists to catch.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Design writing is produced at one size in most organisations, and the size is whatever the template happens to be.

**The consequence runs in both directions and neither is neutral.** A task-sized decision written at milestone scope generates ceremony nobody reads, which trains everybody to stop reading design documents. A milestone-sized decision written at task scope omits the section where somebody would have had to be named as the operator, and the omission is not noticed at the time — it is noticed when the thing is running and nobody owns it.

**The second failure is the expensive one and it is invisible at the moment it is committed.** Nothing about a well-written task-scope design signals that it should have been a milestone-scope design. It reads as clear and proportionate. The gap only becomes visible when the design leaves the room that produced it.

There is a third problem, which is why the assessment attaches the constraint register. **A design can be internally excellent and inconsistent with the constraints it was supposed to respect**, and nobody checks, because checking requires holding two documents open and walking one against the other. It is dull, mechanical work and it catches things nothing else catches.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only, and both modules of the stage are load-bearing.** The design carries non-functional inputs from [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md), frozen inputs from [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md), a boundary decision from [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md), and a chosen option from [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md). **A candidate missing any of these submits a design with a hole in a named place.**

The prior it works against is that a bigger document is a better one. **The countermeasure is the defence**, which must argue from the decision the design carries rather than from the amount of work involved.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Evaluative rather than intrinsic, exactly as at [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md). The content was produced in earlier sessions; the load is in choosing a standard with none supplied and defending it, which is what complexity class 3 means in this programme.

**3.2 Why scope rather than quality is the class boundary.** Quality of architecture requires a standard for good architecture, which this programme does not have and no source behind it supplies. **Scope selection requires a judgement about the decision the design carries, which the candidate can defend from their own artefacts.** The second is assessable without inventing a threshold; the first is not.

**3.3 Why the operational heading is the claim worth teaching.** It is precise, it distinguishes two adjacent scopes by exactly one thing, and it can be checked against any design document. **The claim it embodies — that leaving the scope of a single piece of work means acquiring a runtime owner — is a substantive statement about what changes when a design grows**, rather than a statement about length.

**3.4 Why the defence precedes the design.** A scope chosen after the document has begun is a scope chosen to fit the document. **This is the same argument as [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md)'s about stopping rules, [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md)'s about decision contexts and [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md)'s about criteria** — its fifth appearance in this programme and the last one in this stage.

**3.5 Why an unmitigated risk is required.** A register in which every risk is mitigated has usually mitigated them by writing the word. **An honest unmitigated list is worth more than a complete mitigated one**, and the source for that position is a published threat model in which every identified threat is marked unmitigated — which is what an honest model looks like at the point it is first written.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**Seven specification items, published in [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) and repeated in §7 here.** Each checked present and adequate, or not.

**The scope defence is submitted before the design and its ordering is verifiable from the submission.** This is the assessment's only structural protection and everything else rests on it.

**The assessor's own scope preference is explicitly excluded from the marking**, and this is stated in the session so that candidates do not attempt to guess it. **What is marked is whether the candidate defended their choice against the decision the design carries.** Marking whether they chose correctly would require a supplied standard for correct scope, which would return the session to class 2 and undo the boundary the stage exists to reach.

**The register walk is required and is deliberately mechanical.** For each frozen input, point at the place in the design where it is accommodated. Entries with nowhere to point are either constraints the design violates or constraints that were never real, **and both are findings that belong in the document.** This movement catches more than the rest of the assessment and costs the least.

**The review movement uses a reader from a different scope.** Handing the design to somebody who would have written it larger or smaller, and asking which headings they missed, produces the scope argument from outside. **If they miss nothing, the candidate probably wrote at the larger scope without noticing**, which is the more common of the two errors among candidates who have just been told scope matters.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The milestone design with no operational owner.** The failure the whole convention exists to catch. *Countermeasure:* item 1, plus opening item 4, which asks who runs this after it exists and is submitted before the design.

**The essay at every scope.** *Countermeasure:* the defence, which must argue from the decision rather than from the work.

**The fully mitigated risk register.** *Countermeasure:* item 5, which requires one risk carried unmitigated with that stated.

**The abandoned open question.** A question listed as open because listing it is easier than resolving it, naming nobody who could close it. *Countermeasure:* item 4, which requires each open question to name who would close it and what would close it.

**The perpetual draft.** *Countermeasure:* item 6, which is trivially checkable and routinely failed.

**The design with unauthored constraints.** The attached register is the first version and none of its entries has an authority. *Countermeasure:* item 3. **This is the item most likely to fail for reasons outside the candidate's control**, because an authority that could not be identified at [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) will still be unidentified here, and the item accepts an explicit marking of absence for that reason.

**The scope chosen to fit a document already started.** *Countermeasure:* the prior submission of the defence, which is the only structural protection and can still be defeated by a candidate who drafts first and submits second.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**Seven specification items, published in [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) and repeated here.** Each checked present and adequate, or not.

1. The scope is chosen, defended, and correct for the decision the design carries. A design committing somebody to running something is written at the scope with an operational heading; one that does not, is not. **Writing at a larger scope than the decision requires fails for the same reason as writing at a smaller one.**
2. Non-functional requirements appear as inputs with their measurements and verification methods carried unchanged from [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) and [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md). A threshold that changed is either an error or a decision, and if it is a decision it has a record.
3. The frozen inputs are attached and each names its authority or is explicitly marked as having none.
4. Open questions are present and actually open — each names who would close it and what would close it.
5. Risks name mitigations, and at least one risk is carried unmitigated with that stated.
6. The in-progress marking is correct at the moment of submission.
7. Where the design meets an existing system, the boundary decision from [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md) is named and its standing cost is stated.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Not the quality of the architecture, not the technology chosen, not the length of the document, not whether anybody accepted the design, not whether the design is one an assessor would have produced, and **not whether the scope chosen is the scope an assessor would have chosen.**

**Entrustment evidence generated: none.** This is a specification check at complexity class 3.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: excluding the assessor's scope preference makes the central judgement almost unfalsifiable.**

At its strongest: item 1 says the scope must be *correct for the decision the design carries*, and then the specification says the assessor's own view of the correct scope is not marked. **What remains is whether the candidate produced a defence**, and a defence is a paragraph. A candidate who writes at task scope for a decision that plainly commits an operator, and defends it fluently, has satisfied an item whose stated content they have failed. **The item's words and the item's operation do not match**, which is worse than a narrow item, because it reads as though something substantive is being checked.

**Substantially conceded, and it is the stronger objection in this paper.** The rebuttal is that item 1 is not alone: a design carrying an operational commitment and written without an operational heading fails item 1 *through* opening item 4 and through the review movement, both of which produce evidence independent of the assessor's preference. **The declined fix is to publish a mapping from decision types to scopes and mark against it.** It is declined because such a mapping would be a threshold asserted without warrant — the same refusal made at [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) for stopping rules, [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) for measurement quality and [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md) for which constraints to press — and once inside an assessment scheme it would be indistinguishable from a finding to every learner who met it. **The paper would rather assess less and say so, and this is the fourth time this stage has made that trade.**

**Objection 2: the four scopes have no warrant, and the assessment treats a wiki convention as an engineering standard.**

At its strongest: four scopes, with those particular heading lists, exist because a group of engineers wrote them down. **There is no evidence that four is the right number, that the boundaries fall in the right places, or that the one distinguishing heading is the right distinguishing heading.** The publication offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices and does not claim to. A programme building an assessment on it has borrowed authority the source never claimed.

**Conceded.** The rebuttal is about what the convention buys rather than what it establishes: it is checkable, which almost nothing else in this stage is, and a claim a learner can test against their own artefacts in minutes has a different educational value from a claim they must accept. **The declined fix is to have the candidate define their own scope taxonomy and defend it.** It is declined because it is a session's work rather than an assessment's, and because a taxonomy invented for an assessment would be defended rather than used — but the fix is better than this paper is comfortable admitting, and a later revision of this stage should consider it.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most candidates will choose a larger scope than the decision requires, and the review movement's reader will miss no headings. *Instrument:* the reader's response, coded *missed headings* / *missed none*, against the scope chosen.

**Prediction 2.** Item 5 will be the most frequently failed specification item, and item 6 the least. *Instrument:* per-item pass rates, reported without aggregation.

**Prediction 3.** The register walk will surface at least one constraint per candidate that the design neither accommodates nor violates — a constraint that was never real. *Instrument:* the walk's record, counting entries with nowhere to point, classified by the candidate as *violated* or *not real*.

**Prediction 4.** Candidates whose scope defence was drafted before the design will change scope more often than those who drafted them together. *Instrument:* the submission ordering against any recorded scope change. **If no candidate ever changes scope, the defence is decorative and the movement should be rewritten as a declaration.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That design writing should be proportionate to the decision it carries; that leaving the scope of a single piece of work means acquiring a runtime owner; and that an unmitigated risk stated is worth more than a mitigated one asserted.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The four scopes are one organisation's convention from pages read on 2026-07-30, published without comparative evidence and without claiming any. **The sources record no instance of the failure this assessment exists to catch**, because the practice corpus records no engagement that went badly at all.

**The concession worth remembering.** This assessment names a substantive standard in item 1 and then declines to enforce its substance, for the same reason it has declined four times in this stage: enforcing it would require asserting a threshold nobody has warranted. **That trade is defensible and it is not free, and a reader should hold the paper to it.**
