## In plain terms

This is the stage's second assessment and its class boundary.

Every previous assessment in this programme told the candidate what to produce. **This one does not.** The candidate chooses the scope at which the design is written, and the scope determines the standard they will be marked against.

The published convention supplies four shapes — a task, a feature or story, a milestone or epic, and a technical investigation — each with an explicit heading list. **The escalation from feature scope to milestone scope adds exactly one heading, and that heading is about operation.**

That is the sharpest claim the publication makes about design writing, and it is worth stating precisely because it is unusually testable. **A design that leaves the scope of a single piece of work acquires a runtime owner.** Everything else in the milestone template is already in the feature template; the difference is the point at which somebody must be named who will run the thing after it exists.

It is a convention, not a finding, and the assessment says so. **But it is a convention you can check against any design document in minutes**, which is worth more than a claim you cannot check at all.

The assessment also requires one artefact the design itself cannot supply: **a decision record for the largest irreversible choice, in which the rejected option has been argued at its strongest by something that is not its opponent.** The reason is narrow and practical. The person writing a record is the person who rejected the alternative, so the alternative gets written at exactly the strength needed to justify rejecting it — and when the constraint that decided the choice moves, as this module has spent two sessions arguing constraints do, **there is nothing on file worth reopening.** A generative instrument is good at arguing a position and bad at deciding one, so it is given the first task and none of the second.

## Abstract

This paper defends an assessment in which the candidate selects the standard they are marked against, and in which the assessor's own preference about scope is explicitly excluded from the marking. The argument is that design writing proportionate to the decision is the judgement being tested, that the published four-scope convention makes the judgement checkable through a single distinguishing heading concerning operation, and that marking whether the candidate chose the scope an assessor would have chosen would return the session to a supplied criterion and undo the class boundary. It marks eight items including that at least one risk be carried unmitigated, that open questions name who would close them, that thresholds be carried unchanged from the requirement set, and that a decision record exist for the design's largest irreversible choice with its rejected option argued at full strength by a reading the candidate did not write.

The paper records an amendment made while writing it: the specification's original seven items assessed a design and no decision, so a candidate could pass in full with a largest choice that was unrecorded, unargued and unreopenable — in a module whose two preceding sessions both argue that constraints move. The correction adds item 8 and is documented in §4 rather than applied silently.

The paper concedes that the four scopes have no warrant beyond one organisation's convention, that excluding the assessor's scope preference makes the central judgement almost unfalsifiable, that requiring an adversarial reading risks assessing prompt execution rather than judgement, and that the sources record no instance of the failure the assessment exists to catch.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Design writing is produced at one size in most organisations, and the size is whatever the template happens to be.

**The consequence runs in both directions and neither is neutral.** A task-sized decision written at milestone scope generates ceremony nobody reads, which trains everybody to stop reading design documents. A milestone-sized decision written at task scope omits the section where somebody would have had to be named as the operator, and the omission is not noticed at the time — it is noticed when the thing is running and nobody owns it.

**The second failure is the expensive one and it is invisible at the moment it is committed.** Nothing about a well-written task-scope design signals that it should have been a milestone-scope design. It reads as clear and proportionate. The gap only becomes visible when the design leaves the room that produced it.

There is a third problem, which is why the assessment attaches the constraint register. **A design can be internally excellent and inconsistent with the constraints it was supposed to respect**, and nobody checks, because checking requires holding two documents open and walking one against the other. It is dull, mechanical work and it catches things nothing else catches.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only, and both modules of the stage are load-bearing.** The design carries non-functional inputs from [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md), frozen inputs from [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md), a boundary decision from [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md), and a chosen option from [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md). **A candidate missing any of these submits a design with a hole in a named place.**

The prior it works against is that a bigger document is a better one. **The countermeasure is the defence**, which must argue from the decision the design carries rather than from the amount of work involved.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Evaluative rather than intrinsic, exactly as at [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md). The content was produced in earlier sessions; the load is in choosing a standard with none supplied and defending it, which is what complexity class 3 means in this programme.

**3.2 Why scope rather than quality is the class boundary.** Quality of architecture requires a standard for good architecture, which this programme does not have and no source behind it supplies. **Scope selection requires a judgement about the decision the design carries, which the candidate can defend from their own artefacts.** The second is assessable without inventing a threshold; the first is not.

**3.3 Why the operational heading is the claim worth teaching.** It is precise, it distinguishes two adjacent scopes by exactly one thing, and it can be checked against any design document. **The claim it embodies — that leaving the scope of a single piece of work means acquiring a runtime owner — is a substantive statement about what changes when a design grows**, rather than a statement about length.

**3.4 Why the defence precedes the design.** A scope chosen after the document has begun is a scope chosen to fit the document. **This is the same argument as [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md)'s about stopping rules, [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md)'s about decision contexts and [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md)'s about criteria** — its fifth appearance in this programme and the last one in this stage.

**3.5 Why an unmitigated risk is required.** A register in which every risk is mitigated has usually mitigated them by writing the word. **An honest unmitigated list is worth more than a complete mitigated one**, and the source for that position is a published threat model in which every identified threat is marked unmitigated — which is what an honest model looks like at the point it is first written.

**3.6 Why a decision record is a different artefact from a design, and why both are assessed.**

A design document describes a system. A decision record describes one choice, at one time, by named people, with a status. The published convention keeps them separate deliberately, and two of its fields carry the separation.

The **status vocabulary** is drawn from **"[Proposed/Accepted/Deprecated/Superseded]"**, and it contains no value meaning *we changed our mind* ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md)). A reversal is a new record with a new date, so **the record is durable while the design is not** — which is why a design can be rewritten freely and a record cannot. Records are stored under a fixed directory and **"added as pull request in the proposed status"**, so the artefact enters review as a proposal rather than arriving settled.

The **context section** must be **"a value-neutral description of the forces at play"**, and this is the field the assessment actually cares about. **A context from which a reader can infer the decision was written after the decision**, which makes the record a ratification rather than a proposal, and a ratification cannot be reopened honestly by somebody who was not present. This is the same claim the stage has made about criteria at [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md) and about stopping rules at [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md), and it is its final appearance here.

**The publication's limits bind everything above.** It offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices and does not claim to; its pages carry footer dates spread across several years; roughly a fifth of it is stubs or link lists; **it contradicts itself in at least a dozen places without noticing**; and it asserts four unsupported magnitude claims that this repository records as specimens and reproduces nowhere.

**3.7 Why the rejected option must be argued by something that is not its opponent.**

The weakest part of every decision record is the rejected option, and the reason is structural rather than careless: **the person writing the record is the person who rejected it.** A rejection recorded in its author's own summary is recorded at the strength required to justify the rejection, which is by construction the weakest form anyone has put it in.

The cost is deferred and specific. When the constraint that decided the choice moves — and [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) and [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md) have both argued that constraints move — **the record cannot be reopened, because the option that would now win was never written down in a form worth reconsidering.** A team in that position redoes the analysis from nothing, and usually reaches the same answer, because the artefact framing the question is the one that dismissed the alternative.

Hence the platform anchor. **A generative instrument is well suited to arguing a position and unsuited to deciding one**, and this session gives it exactly the first task. The division is the same one [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md) §3.6 drew between retrieval and judgement, at its final appearance in this stage.

**3.8 What the instrument cannot be trusted with, stated once more because this is the last time.**

Capability in these systems is **jagged**: the boundary between competence and failure is uneven and does not track human-perceived difficulty, and outside it the failure mode is a confident wrong answer rather than a refusal ([research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md](/research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md)). **That note rests on an abstract; its shape is usable and no number in it is.**

Two design consequences follow and both are implemented in the workflow. The pasted argument is edited **for accuracy and never for strength**, because a fluent argument for a technically impossible option is exactly what the jagged boundary produces. And the agent is withheld the candidate's decision, because an instrument given a conclusion elaborates it — the claim [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) made about an instrument that agrees with you, arriving for the fifth and final time.

**3.9 Why the consequences section is where the platform documentation earns its place.**

A consequences section is usually written as a list of things that will be nicer or worse. The useful entries are the ones that **bind later from a choice made now**, and the vendor comparison supplies the cleanest available instance: **publishing route is constrained by build choice** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). A decision taken in the first week determines what distribution is available in the last, and nothing in the intervening work surfaces it.

That is precisely the class of consequence a decision record exists to make visible before it becomes irreversible, and it is why this paper treats the consequences section as an assessed field rather than a courtesy. **The documentation is first-party, definitional, highly perishable, read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, and publishes no adoption, cost or outcome data of any kind.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**Eight specification items, published in [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) and repeated in §7 here.** Each checked present and adequate, or not.

**An eighth item was added while this paper was being written and is recorded here rather than applied silently.** The original seven assessed a design and no decision. A candidate could satisfy all of them with a design whose largest irreversible choice was made without a record, argued nowhere, and unreopenable the moment the constraint that decided it moved — **in a module whose two preceding sessions both argue that constraints move.** The stage's own thesis was absent from the stage's terminal assessment. Item 8 adds the record, and assesses its context, its status, its consequences and the strength of its rejected option, and **not whether the decision was correct.**

**What the platform workflow is for.** Not tooling. It exists to make one property of a record observable at the moment it is written rather than a year later: **that a rejected option recorded by the person who rejected it is recorded at the strength required to justify the rejection, and that this is visible the instant a second voice argues the same option.** The candidate reads both versions side by side, which is the cheapest possible demonstration of a defect that normally surfaces only when somebody tries to reopen a decision and finds nothing there to reopen.

**The scope defence is submitted before the design and its ordering is verifiable from the submission.** This is the assessment's only structural protection and everything else rests on it.

**The assessor's own scope preference is explicitly excluded from the marking**, and this is stated in the session so that candidates do not attempt to guess it. **What is marked is whether the candidate defended their choice against the decision the design carries.** Marking whether they chose correctly would require a supplied standard for correct scope, which would return the session to class 2 and undo the boundary the stage exists to reach.

**The register walk is required and is deliberately mechanical.** For each frozen input, point at the place in the design where it is accommodated. Entries with nowhere to point are either constraints the design violates or constraints that were never real, **and both are findings that belong in the document.** This movement catches more than the rest of the assessment and costs the least.

**The review movement uses a reader from a different scope.** Handing the design to somebody who would have written it larger or smaller, and asking which headings they missed, produces the scope argument from outside. **If they miss nothing, the candidate probably wrote at the larger scope without noticing**, which is the more common of the two errors among candidates who have just been told scope matters.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The milestone design with no operational owner.** The failure the whole convention exists to catch. *Countermeasure:* item 1, plus opening item 4, which asks who runs this after it exists and is submitted before the design.

**The essay at every scope.** *Countermeasure:* the defence, which must argue from the decision rather than from the work.

**The fully mitigated risk register.** *Countermeasure:* item 5, which requires one risk carried unmitigated with that stated.

**The abandoned open question.** A question listed as open because listing it is easier than resolving it, naming nobody who could close it. *Countermeasure:* item 4, which requires each open question to name who would close it and what would close it.

**The perpetual draft.** *Countermeasure:* item 6, which is trivially checkable and routinely failed.

**The design with unauthored constraints.** The attached register is the first version and none of its entries has an authority. *Countermeasure:* item 3. **This is the item most likely to fail for reasons outside the candidate's control**, because an authority that could not be identified at [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) will still be unidentified here, and the item accepts an explicit marking of absence for that reason.

**The scope chosen to fit a document already started.** *Countermeasure:* the prior submission of the defence, which is the only structural protection and can still be defeated by a candidate who drafts first and submits second.

**The context that leaks its decision.** The record's context section is written after the choice and a reader can state the conclusion from it alone. *Countermeasure:* item 8, plus exercise 4, which puts the context in front of somebody who does not know the answer and asks them for it. **This is the strongest countermeasure in the assessment because it is empirical rather than editorial.**

**The straw rejection.** The rejected option is recorded in the weakest form anyone has put it in. *Countermeasure:* item 8's requirement that the argument be produced by something other than its opponent, and the instruction to edit for accuracy and never for strength. **A candidate can still paste a weak argument and call it the strongest one**, and prediction 6 predicts exactly that.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**Eight specification items, published in [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) and repeated here.** Each checked present and adequate, or not.

1. The scope is chosen, defended, and correct for the decision the design carries. A design committing somebody to running something is written at the scope with an operational heading; one that does not, is not. **Writing at a larger scope than the decision requires fails for the same reason as writing at a smaller one.**
2. Non-functional requirements appear as inputs with their measurements and verification methods carried unchanged from [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) and [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md). A threshold that changed is either an error or a decision, and if it is a decision it has a record.
3. The frozen inputs are attached and each names its authority or is explicitly marked as having none.
4. Open questions are present and actually open — each names who would close it and what would close it.
5. Risks name mitigations, and at least one risk is carried unmitigated with that stated.
6. The in-progress marking is correct at the moment of submission.
7. Where the design meets an existing system, the boundary decision from [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md) is named and its standing cost is stated.
8. **A decision record exists for the design's largest irreversible choice**, carrying a value-neutral context, a status from the published vocabulary, a consequences section naming what binds later, and the rejected option argued at its strongest by a reading the candidate did not write — together with the observation that settled it and the condition under which the record would be superseded. **The correctness of the decision is not assessed.**

**Nothing else is assessed.** Not the quality of the architecture, not the technology chosen, not the length of the document, not whether anybody accepted the design, not whether the design is one an assessor would have produced, not whether the recorded decision was the right one, and **not whether the scope chosen is the scope an assessor would have chosen.**

**Entrustment evidence generated: none.** This is a specification check at complexity class 3.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: excluding the assessor's scope preference makes the central judgement almost unfalsifiable.**

At its strongest: item 1 says the scope must be *correct for the decision the design carries*, and then the specification says the assessor's own view of the correct scope is not marked. **What remains is whether the candidate produced a defence**, and a defence is a paragraph. A candidate who writes at task scope for a decision that plainly commits an operator, and defends it fluently, has satisfied an item whose stated content they have failed. **The item's words and the item's operation do not match**, which is worse than a narrow item, because it reads as though something substantive is being checked.

**Substantially conceded, and it is the stronger objection in this paper.** The rebuttal is that item 1 is not alone: a design carrying an operational commitment and written without an operational heading fails item 1 *through* opening item 4 and through the review movement, both of which produce evidence independent of the assessor's preference. **The declined fix is to publish a mapping from decision types to scopes and mark against it.** It is declined because such a mapping would be a threshold asserted without warrant — the same refusal made at [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) for stopping rules, [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) for measurement quality and [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md) for which constraints to press — and once inside an assessment scheme it would be indistinguishable from a finding to every learner who met it. **The paper would rather assess less and say so, and this is the fourth time this stage has made that trade.**

**Objection 2: the four scopes have no warrant, and the assessment treats a wiki convention as an engineering standard.**

At its strongest: four scopes, with those particular heading lists, exist because a group of engineers wrote them down. **There is no evidence that four is the right number, that the boundaries fall in the right places, or that the one distinguishing heading is the right distinguishing heading.** The publication offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices and does not claim to. A programme building an assessment on it has borrowed authority the source never claimed.

**Conceded.** The rebuttal is about what the convention buys rather than what it establishes: it is checkable, which almost nothing else in this stage is, and a claim a learner can test against their own artefacts in minutes has a different educational value from a claim they must accept. **The declined fix is to have the candidate define their own scope taxonomy and defend it.** It is declined because it is a session's work rather than an assessment's, and because a taxonomy invented for an assessment would be defended rather than used — but the fix is better than this paper is comfortable admitting, and a later revision of this stage should consider it.

**Objection 3: making a generative instrument argue the rejected option outsources the intellectual work the assessment claims to be testing.**

At its strongest, and it is the objection the eighth item most deserves: the candidate is required to produce the strongest case for an option they rejected, and is then told to obtain that case from a machine and paste it in. **The reasoning that would have produced it — inhabiting a position you disagree with well enough to state it at full strength — is the actual professional skill**, and the assessment now checks that the candidate ran a prompt. Worse, the instrument's capability boundary is uneven and its failure mode outside that boundary is a fluent, confident and wrong argument, so a candidate may paste in a persuasive case for something technically impossible and satisfy an item whose whole purpose is to preserve a real alternative for later reconsideration. **The item may be assessing prompt execution and calling it judgement.**

**Partly conceded, and the substitution risk is real.** Three defences, none complete. The item assesses four things beyond the pasted argument — the value-neutral context, the status, the binding consequences, and the observation that settled the choice — and all four are the candidate's own writing. The instruction to edit for accuracy and never for strength puts the technical judgement back with the candidate, since deciding whether a fluent argument is possible is exactly the judgement the jagged boundary demands. And withholding the decision from the instrument means the candidate must be able to state the forces without the conclusion, which is the same discipline the context section tests. **The declined fix is to require the candidate to write the rejected option's case themselves.** It is declined because that is what a decision record already nominally requires and what it reliably fails to produce — the author of a rejection writes it at the strength that justifies the rejection, every time — and a requirement that has failed for as long as the format has existed is not repaired by restating it. **The cost of that rejection is exactly the substitution this objection names, and prediction 6 is the only thing that would detect it.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most candidates will choose a larger scope than the decision requires, and the review movement's reader will miss no headings. *Instrument:* the reader's response, coded *missed headings* / *missed none*, against the scope chosen.

**Prediction 2.** Item 5 will be the most frequently failed specification item, and item 6 the least. *Instrument:* per-item pass rates, reported without aggregation.

**Prediction 3.** The register walk will surface at least one constraint per candidate that the design neither accommodates nor violates — a constraint that was never real. *Instrument:* the walk's record, counting entries with nowhere to point, classified by the candidate as *violated* or *not real*.

**Prediction 4.** Candidates whose scope defence was drafted before the design will change scope more often than those who drafted them together. *Instrument:* the submission ordering against any recorded scope change. **If no candidate ever changes scope, the defence is decorative and the movement should be rewritten as a declaration.**

**Prediction 5.** A majority of first-draft context sections will leak the decision, judged by a reader who does not know it being able to state it. *Instrument:* the exercise-4 reader's answer, coded *inferred correctly* / *could not infer*, collected before revision. **This is the direct test of §3.6's central claim about ratification**, and a null result would mean the value-neutral requirement is easier to meet than this paper assumes and could be dropped.

**Prediction 6.** A minority of pasted adversarial arguments will be judged, by a second reader blind to their provenance, to be weaker than the candidate's own argument for the option they chose — despite item 8 requiring the strongest case. *Instrument:* the two arguments presented unlabelled and ranked for strength. **This predicts the failure of item 8's countermeasure and is the direct test of §8's third objection**, and it is the prediction this paper would least like to lose.

**Prediction 7.** Candidates who withheld their decision from the instrument will paste arguments judged more specific than those who supplied it. *Instrument:* a within-cohort split on the prompt form recorded at exercise 2, with the pasted arguments coded *specific* / *generic* by a second reader. **If the two prompt forms produce indistinguishable output, the instruction to withhold is ceremony and should be removed rather than repeated.**

**Prediction 8.** Candidates instructed to compare their item-3 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 3) will more often change scope than candidates who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* a second reader's record of scope changes, split within cohort on exercise-3 completion. **The same prediction is made at [WP-026](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-026.md), [WP-027](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-027.md), [WP-028](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-028.md) and [WP-029](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-029.md), so a null across all five would indict the comparison pattern rather than any one session.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That design writing should be proportionate to the decision it carries; that leaving the scope of a single piece of work means acquiring a runtime owner; and that an unmitigated risk stated is worth more than a mitigated one asserted.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The four scopes are one organisation's convention from pages read on 2026-07-30, published without comparative evidence and without claiming any. **The sources record no instance of the failure this assessment exists to catch**, because the practice corpus records no engagement that went badly at all.

**The concession worth remembering.** This assessment names a substantive standard in item 1 and then declines to enforce its substance, for the same reason it has declined four times in this stage: enforcing it would require asserting a threshold nobody has warranted. **That trade is defensible and it is not free, and a reader should hold the paper to it.** And note §4's amendment: for the whole of this module's design the stage's terminal assessment contained no decision record, in a module whose central argument is that constraints move and that a design's largest choices must remain reopenable. **The failure the stage names is one the stage committed. Assume there are others this paper has not caught.**

**The silences that matter.** **The decision-record convention publishes no guidance at all on how a rejected option should be recorded**, beyond the existence of a consequences section — no requirement that alternatives be stated, no standard for their strength, and no acknowledgement that the author of a rejection is a poor witness for it. Item 8 is therefore an addition to the convention rather than an application of it, and this paper marks it as such. The publication also states no criterion for when a decision warrants a record at all, so a candidate deciding what counts as *the largest irreversible choice* is exercising judgement the source does not scaffold. On the platform side, **the documentation publishes no measure of how varied an agent's output is across repeated prompts, and none of how its argumentation changes with model selection** — which bears directly on item 8, because the whole movement depends on a second voice producing something the candidate did not already hold, and nothing published establishes how often it does. It publishes no adoption, cost or outcome data for any build path, so the binding consequence used as the exemplar is definitional rather than measured. And **neither source anywhere acknowledges that a decision record's most valuable content is the part written for a reader who has not arrived yet**, which is the argument this session rests on and which is this repository's own.
