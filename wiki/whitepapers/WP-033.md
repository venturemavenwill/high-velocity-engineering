## In plain terms

*Is this feasible?* has no answer format, no instrument and no failing outcome. It will therefore be answered by whoever is most senior in the room, and the answer will be a disposition rather than a finding.

Underneath it are several questions that do have answers, and getting from one to the other is the session's work. A question is admissible here only when it has an answer format — a number, a yes, a count, a transcript, a demonstration on inputs somebody else chose — an instrument that could produce it, a stated cost for running that instrument, and a decision that moves in a named direction depending on the answer.

The session's first move is a split that learners resist because it feels bureaucratic and turns out to be the whole thing. **Uncertainty about whether anybody wants this, will fund it and is permitted to have it is settled by talking to people. Uncertainty about whether it can be built, whether the data supports it and whether the model behaves is not, and no amount of talking will settle it.** Merging the two is the commonest failure in a feasibility set and it is invisible from inside, because both kinds of question can be asked in the same meeting.

Its second move is about positive results. A demonstration on a chosen example establishes something about that example. This matters more than it used to, because there is a well-attested pattern in which assistance improves work inside a system's capability and degrades it outside — with the boundary not following intuitions about difficulty, and the failures being persuasive rather than obvious. **No magnitude for that is asserted anywhere in this wiki**, and the habit it licenses is narrow: decide which side of the boundary you think you are on, before you use the instrument, in writing.

Its third move is the one that saves the most time and is used least. **A large share of technical feasibility questions have already been answered, in public, and are settled by reading rather than by running.** Whether a model is available in the region the data must stay in. Whether a deployment type exists at the shape the workload needs. Whether the tool the brief names is published as suitable for the scale the brief assumes. The reason nobody reads is not laziness; it is that the question was never stated precisely enough for a page to answer it. **A precise question turns a week of investigation into a citation and a date** — and where the documentation is silent, the silence is the finding and never a licence to infer a limit.

## Abstract

This paper defends converting felt feasibility uncertainty into a set of questions each carrying an answer format, an instrument, a price and a decision. The argument is that an unformatted question is resolved by authority rather than by evidence, that market-facing and system-facing uncertainties require categorically different instruments, and that a positive result obtained on inputs the demonstrator chose licenses very little. The session installs a six-column question set, a routing rule inherited from [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md), an ordering rule placing the cheapest disqualifier first, and an explicit written capability-boundary judgement recorded before the instrument is used.

It adds a fourth instrument class the earlier drafts of this design omitted: **the documented answer.** A precise question can frequently be settled from first-party documentation in minutes, and two research literatures supply the two ways this goes wrong. **The planning literature shows that some feasibility questions have a published negative answer**, so that a design resting on a model verifying its own reasoning can be settled without a spike. **The retrieval-benchmarking literature shows that generalisation across domains is itself a feasibility question whose answer is frequently no** — a score on the dataset a system was trained on is not evidence about a new corpus — which is the general form of the chosen-example defeater the session already teaches.

The paper concedes that the ordering rule has no external warrant; that the capability-boundary literature it draws on has been read here at abstract depth only and rests on a narrow evidential base outside the boundary; that the source corpus contains no cost side of any kind and therefore cannot rank any instrument by price; that the documented answers it relies on come from first-party documentation whose authority is definitional and which publishes no outcome data at all; and that **the documentation instrument the workflow runs on has no research note in this repository, so its warrant is carried forward from [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) and is unverified here.**

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Feasibility arrives as a feeling and gets resolved as a hierarchy.

The mechanism is simple and nearly universal. Somebody asks whether the thing is possible. The most confident engineer present says it should be fine. Nobody has a way to disagree that is not a challenge to that person's competence, so the question closes. **The room has not made a mistake — it has made a decision using the only instrument available to it, which was somebody's disposition.**

There is a second, quieter version. A team knows the question is real, and defers it: feasibility will be established during the build. That is not deferral, it is reclassification. **A feasibility question answered during delivery is not a decision, it is a defect**, and it arrives with a schedule attached.

The session's response is to require an answer format before an instrument, an instrument before a run, and a price before either — and to order the whole set by how cheaply each question could stop the work rather than by how interesting it is.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** Two retrievals are load-bearing.

[S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) supplied the routing discipline — some uncertainties cannot be resolved by talking to anybody — and this session is where it is applied under maximum temptation, because a confident colleague's assurance is the cheapest instrument in existence and settles nothing. **The same rule, applied twice, thirteen sessions apart, and the second application is harder than the first.**

[S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md) supplied the charter, whose evidence clause is this session's model: evidence named before it exists. The question set is a charter's evidence clause, generalised across every uncertainty in the design.

The prior the session works against is that feasibility is a property of a system rather than of a question about a system. Learners arrive expecting to assess a design; the session requires them to assess several claims that a design happens to make.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** High, and the module's intrinsic peak. Converting a felt uncertainty into a formatted question, selecting an instrument that could produce that format, and pricing it — all before knowing the answer — does not decompose into simpler steps, and it must be held for several uncertainties at once. The load is the simultaneity rather than any single row.

**3.2 Why the two classes need separating before instruments are chosen.** Whether people want the outcome, will fund it and may have it is reducible by conversation. Whether a system can be built, whether the data supports it and whether a model behaves is not. **A team that has not made the split will use a conversation on the second class, because conversations are cheap and available**, and the resulting row looks identical on paper to a properly routed one.

**3.3 Why chosen inputs are the central defeater of a positive result.** A demonstration on an example the demonstrator selected is evidence about that example. The generalisation from it is not weak, it is absent, and the strength of the impression is uncorrelated with the strength of the evidence.

**3.4 Why the capability-boundary judgement is written before the instrument is used.** The pattern this rests on is that assistance helps inside a capability boundary and hurts outside it, that the boundary does not follow human intuitions about difficulty, and that out-of-boundary performance can fall below unassisted performance — which implies the failures are adopted rather than detected. A judgement made afterwards is contaminated by the output. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted, and the boundary's location moves with the tooling.**

**3.5 Why the cheapest disqualifier goes first.** Ordering by cost-to-disqualify maximises the expected saving from an early stop. This is an argument from decision cost and has no measurement behind it in this repository.

**3.6 Why a documented answer is an instrument, and why some questions are already closed.**

The design's fourth instrument class is reading, and it deserves a theoretical defence because learners treat it as preparation rather than as evidence.

Some feasibility questions have a published negative answer. The planning literature holds that auto-regressive language models **cannot, by themselves, do planning — and cannot do self-verification either**, on the ground that self-verification "is after all a form of reasoning" ([research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md)). The constructive position — models as **"universal approximate knowledge sources"** paired with an **external verifier** in a two-way critique loop — is the architecture this programme's charters already implement.

**Read as a feasibility instrument, that closes a class of design question without a spike.** A row whose design depends on the model checking its own plan can be answered by citation. A row whose design generates candidates and checks them outside the model cannot, and needs a run.

**The limits constrain what may be concluded and they are stated wherever the claim is used.** It is a **position paper, not an experiment**; its claims are arguments and have been contested; it does not specify how to build a verifier for an open-ended domain; its verifier-construction step still depends on human review; and **it addresses auto-regressive models as of early 2024**, so models explicitly trained for reasoning postdate the argument and the picture must be re-checked rather than assumed static. **It licenses a question to ask and an instrument not to rely on. It does not license a prohibition on any design**, and a session that taught it as one would be doing exactly what §8's first objection accuses the design of elsewhere.

**3.7 Generalisation is a feasibility question, and its published answer is the general form of the chosen-example defeater.**

§3.3 states the chosen-input problem as a logical point. The retrieval-benchmarking literature supplies it as a measured one, in a setting where somebody went and checked.

The complaint that motivated that work is exactly this session's: improvements were being measured overwhelmingly on a small number of datasets and most often on the one the model was trained on, **which cannot distinguish a system that has learned the task from one that has learned a corpus, a query style and a local notion of relevance.** Since almost every deployment involves a domain with no in-domain training data, **the quantity practitioners need is generalisation, and it was not being measured** ([research/02-technical-foundations/thakur-beir.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/thakur-beir.md)).

Three of its findings transfer to a feasibility set without needing a number. **A long-standing lexical baseline did not collapse on any domain**, which makes a robust floor operationally different from a higher average with an unknown worst case. **The best zero-shot performance came from the most expensive families and the cheaper ones often underperformed**, so efficiency and generalisation were in tension — the shape of the trade a team meets whenever a demonstration is cheap and a deployment is not. And **quality is a property of the system and the domain jointly**, which is the sentence to write into a question set.

**The limits are recorded and are used to bound the claim rather than to decorate it.** The results are a snapshot; **the specific rankings have since changed and the work is used here for its methodology and trade structure, not for which system led.** Eighteen datasets are a sample of retrieval tasks and not a map of them. The constituent datasets differ in the density and construction of their relevance judgements, so their per-dataset scores are not strictly comparable. Zero-shot was the chosen condition, so nothing here speaks to what modest in-domain adaptation would recover. **And the benchmark is now itself an optimisation target, which weakens its own zero-shot premise over time. No figure from it is reproduced in this paper or in the session.**

**3.8 Why a result from a small run must state its uncertainty or state that it has not.**

A feasibility run is an experiment and inherits every limit of one. A reported result needs to carry how many cases it rests on, and where cases arrive in related groups — several questions about one document, one question in several forms — **a naive treatment will make the result look more precise than it is** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). The discipline the session takes from this is the one that belongs before the run: **state what difference this could have detected.** Without it, *we found no problem* and *we could not have found this problem* are the same sentence and the finished document does not distinguish them.

**The design's operational form of that is the `could_have_detected` field**, written into the question set before the first case is scored, and carried into [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md)'s evidence section. **A finding from a small sample that states no uncertainty overclaims, in the direction that flatters whoever ran it.**

That source is **analytical rather than empirical**; its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions; and it addresses comparing scores rather than choosing what to score, so a tight result around a badly chosen question is still meaningless. **No number from it appears here.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 3 is the instrument.** Learners are shown a successful demonstration on a chosen example and asked what they have learned. The confident answer and the correct answer differ sharply, and the gap is more effective as an experience than as an instruction.

**The class split is required before the instrument column may be filled**, structurally rather than by advice, because a learner who chooses instruments first will retro-fit the classes to the instruments they picked.

**The corpus's buried refusal is used as the session's worked example**, and its choice is deliberate. On a mature production codebase the assistant could not be asked to add a feature end to end — the generated code reused outdated patterns and ignored architectural constraints — and the workaround was to make failing tests the specification. **That passage has a question, an instrument, a negative answer and a next step: it is a feasibility finding.** It also contradicts its own collection's headline claims about speed, and it sits in speaker notes. The session asks the learner where a finding of that shape would end up in their own organisation and who would see it, which is a question about institutions rather than about method and is the session's only treatment of that theme.

**The review movement blanks the instrument column** and asks a peer to supply one per row. Rows for which no peer can propose an instrument are not questions yet, and the session's instruction is to rewrite them or move them into the accepted column of the [S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md) record with an owner. **That is a deliberate release valve**: not every uncertainty must be investigated, and pretending otherwise is how a feasibility set becomes a project.

**4.1 What the platform workflow is for.**

Not catalogue knowledge, which will be stale within a term. It exists to make one claim physically true: **that a large share of what a team calls feasibility is settled by reading, in minutes, and that the reason nobody reads is that the question was never stated precisely enough for a page to answer it.**

The workflow is arranged so that the learner discovers this rather than being told it. Rows are written with an answer format first; the documentation instrument is pointed at them one at a time; and at least one row that had been scheduled for an investigation is expected to close in minutes — usually a region, a deployment type or a modality. **The saving is the demonstration.**

Two further design decisions carry weight.

**The instructions file forces the absence into view.** Requiring an in-sentence citation on every platform claim, and requiring *not found in the documentation* rather than an answer from general knowledge, is what makes a silence visible against a background of fluent prose. **Grounding makes an answer accurate within a corpus and silent outside it, and nothing in an ungrounded output tells you which side of that line you are on** — [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s claim, met here for the second time on a question where the learner has a stake in the answer.

**A recorded absence is a finding and never a licence to infer a limit.** A tool that publishes no scale envelope has not published a large one. This is the specific error the workflow is arranged to prevent, and it is prevented by moving the row to a different pile rather than by warning against it.

**Two costs the design accepts and records.** First, **this repository holds no research note for the documentation server the workflow runs on.** Its behaviour is carried forward from [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) and is unverified here; the session says so in the workflow rather than in a footnote, because a learner relying on an instrument should know its warrant. Second, the catalogue material is **highly perishable** — which is why every documented answer is required to carry the date it was read, and why no answer produced in this session may be reused in a later one without re-checking.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The conversational answer to a technical question.** The likeliest failure and the one [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) predicted. *Countermeasure:* the class column, filled before instruments, and the standard's warning sign — your instrument for *will the model do this* is an interview.

**The chosen example.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that the set name what would make a positive answer untrustworthy, and the explicit *who chose the input* field carried into [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md)'s evidence section.

**The interesting first.** The learner investigates the part they want to build. *Countermeasure:* the ordering rule and the standard's warning sign — the cheapest disqualifier is still unrun.

**The persuasive wrong answer.** Output was fluent, structured and confident, and was accepted. *Countermeasure:* the written boundary judgement made before use, held separately from the result so that the two can be compared. **This is weak and the paper says so.** A written judgement does not confer the ability to detect a plausible error; it only makes the failure legible afterwards.

**The unanswerable question that survives review.** *Countermeasure:* the peer instrument-supply exercise, and the release valve into the accepted column.

**The inferred limit.** The documentation does not state a boundary and the learner concludes there is none, or concludes there is a restrictive one. *Countermeasure:* the instructions file's required *not found in the documentation* wording, and the workflow's instruction to move the row to a different pile rather than to answer it. **A tool that publishes no scale envelope has not published a large one**, and the countermeasure works by changing where the row goes rather than by asking the learner to remember a caution.

**The undated answer.** A documented answer is recorded without the date it was read, and is reused a term later. *Countermeasure:* the `read_on` field, which is structural, and the standing rule that a platform claim without a date is not a claim. **This is the countermeasure most likely to erode**, because the cost of omitting it is paid by somebody else, later.

**The over-read citation.** A page is cited that is adjacent to the question rather than answering it — the model is available in the region, but not for the deployment type the design needs. *Countermeasure:* workflow step 8's adversarial prompt, which asks the instrument to argue that the cited page does not settle the question asked. **The countermeasure uses the instrument against its own earlier output**, which is the only arrangement this programme trusts.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. Two specification items at [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md): that at least one question was answered negatively or the absence is flagged as suspicious, and that the evidence section records who chose the inputs.

**Never assessed:** the number of questions, whether the answers were favourable, or whether the learner's capability-boundary judgement turned out to be correct. **The judgement is assessed for having been made and recorded, never for accuracy** — which is a real weakness and §8 takes it seriously.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the boundary judgement is unfalsifiable as taught, and assessing it on existence rather than accuracy teaches learners that the ritual is the skill.**

At its strongest: the design asks learners to write down whether they think a task is inside or outside a system's capability, then explicitly declines to mark whether they were right. That is worse than not asking. It trains the production of a confident-sounding artefact and provides no feedback signal at all, which is the exact failure the programme criticises the corpus for — an instrument arranged so that no result can disconfirm anything.

**Conceded, and the concession is uncomfortable because the parallel is exact.** The design's reason is that marking accuracy requires a ground truth about a boundary that moves between cohorts and sometimes between weeks, and a mark awarded against a boundary that has since moved is noise wearing a grade's clothing. **The declined fix is to score the judgement against the observed outcome within the session.** It is declined because the observed outcome is a single instance on a single task, which is precisely the evidential weakness this paper attributes to the literature it cites, and importing it into assessment would be inconsistent. **What the design does instead is require the judgement and the result to be recorded separately so that a cohort accumulating them can be studied**, which is a promise about future evidence rather than a rebuttal. §9 prediction 3 is the test.

**Objection 2: the two-class split is a false dichotomy, and enforcing it will cause learners to misroute the many uncertainties that are genuinely both.**

At its strongest: most consequential uncertainties in real engagements are mixed. Whether a workflow can be automated is simultaneously a question about the system's capability and about whether the people doing it now will accept the result — and the second half determines what *capable* even means. A rule that forces each row into one column will push learners to treat the technical half as the question and the human half as context, which is the failure the whole programme was built to prevent.

**Partly conceded.** The split is a routing heuristic and not an ontology, and mixed uncertainties are real. The rebuttal is that a mixed uncertainty routed to a single instrument is the failure the split is designed to catch: the correct handling is to split the *row*, not to pick a column. **The declined fix is to add a third class for mixed uncertainties.** It is declined because a third class becomes the default — it is the comfortable answer for every row — and the discipline the session is trying to install is the decomposition rather than the label. **That reasoning is design reasoning and nothing warrants it**, and a cohort that routed well while using a mixed class would refute it.

**Objection 3: teaching learners to settle feasibility questions from vendor documentation is teaching them to accept a commercially interested party's account of its own product as evidence.**

At its strongest: the documentation this session treats as an instrument is written by the organisation selling the thing. It compares its own options against each other and not against alternatives. It **publishes no adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no evidence that any of its recommendations produce better outcomes**, so its recommendations are reasoned assertions. It is candid in places — the network-isolation admission and the department-scale statement are stronger concessions than vendor material usually contains — and candour in a few places is exactly what makes the rest persuasive. **A learner trained to close feasibility rows by citing a vendor has been trained to mistake a definitional claim for a measured one**, and the programme elsewhere spends whole sessions teaching them not to.

**Partly conceded, and the concession fixes the scope of the instrument rather than defending it.** The rows this design routes to documentation are **definitional rather than comparative**: is this available here, does this deployment type exist, is this modality supported, what scale is this positioned for. Those are questions the vendor is the authority on, in the same way a manufacturer is the authority on what is in the box. **The rows the design does not route to documentation are every question about whether the thing works**, and the session's ordering puts a run behind each of them. The distinction is stated in the material and enforced by the `answer_format` column: a yes-no about availability is settleable by citation, a count-per-case about behaviour is not.

**The declined fix is to require every documented answer to be confirmed by a run.** It is declined on cost — it converts the cheapest instrument in the set into the most expensive, and the resulting practice would settle nothing by reading, which is the failure the session exists to correct. **The cost of declining is real: a learner will at some point cite a page that has since changed, or a page adjacent to their question, and act on it.** The `read_on` field and step 8's adversarial check are the mitigations, and neither is strong. **§9's prediction 6 is the test of whether the over-read citation is common enough to reopen this.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Before instruction, most learners' answers to opening item 3 will treat a successful demonstration on a chosen example as evidence of general capability. *Instrument:* the written answers, coded *general* / *instance-limited* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** In the completed sets, a substantial share of system-facing uncertainties will carry a conversational instrument. *Instrument:* the class and instrument columns, cross-tabulated, with the classification checked by a second reader.

**Prediction 3.** Recorded capability-boundary judgements will be more often wrong for tasks the learner rated as difficult than for tasks they rated as routine. *Instrument:* the pre-use judgement, the learner's difficulty rating, and the observed outcome, collected per row across the cohort. **This is the prediction that would give §8 objection 1 its answer, and it requires more than one cohort to run.**

**Prediction 4.** Learners will order their sets by interest rather than by cost-to-disqualify, and the first-run row will more often be the one they most wanted to build. *Instrument:* the run order against the stated instrument costs, with the learner's own ranking of interest collected separately at the start.

**Prediction 5.** At least one row per learner that had been assigned a run or a spike will be closed by a documented answer in the workflow, and learners will systematically under-estimate how many such rows their set contains. *Instrument:* the instrument column as first written, against the instrument column after step 5, counted per learner. **If this fails, the platform anchor is not earning its place** and the documentation instrument should be taught as preparation rather than as evidence.

**Prediction 6.** A minority of documented answers will cite a page adjacent to the question rather than one that settles it, and step 8's adversarial check will find more of these than the learner's own review did. *Instrument:* the filled `answer` and `read_on` fields, independently re-read against the question by a second reader, with the step 8 record compared. **This is the test of §8's third objection**, and a high adjacent-citation rate would mean the documented answer is a weaker instrument than this paper treats it as.

**Prediction 7.** A majority of learners will fail to produce a recorded absence at all on the first pass, and a majority of those will have an instructions file that did not reach the request payload. *Instrument:* the exercise 2 submission, coded for whether an absence was recorded, against the learner's payload check. **This predicts the failure of §5's inferred-limit countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure that depends on a configuration nobody verified is an assumption. If it holds, the payload check must move earlier in the workflow rather than be reinforced in the material.

**Prediction 8.** Rows carrying a `could_have_detected` line written before the run will distinguish *no problem found* from *no problem detectable* in the [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) finding more often than rows without one. *Instrument:* the question set's field presence against the finding's evidence section, coded by a second reader. **This is the only test of §3.8's addition to the artefact**, and a null result would mean the field is ceremony and should be removed rather than defended.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That feasibility is several questions rather than one, that conversation and construction settle different families of them, and that a positive result is only as strong as the person who chose the inputs.

**What the evidence licenses.** A routing heuristic from a practitioner handbook that establishes no effect and says so; a capability-boundary pattern read here at abstract depth only, whose out-of-boundary result rests on a single task; and one organisation's buried record of a technical limit. **Direction and mechanism throughout. No magnitude.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The session requires a judgement it declines to mark, for a reason it states, and it has arranged to be told later whether that was right. Hold it as an open item rather than as a settled design.

**Verified in this repository.** That conversation reduces uncertainty about whether people want, will fund and may have an outcome, and cannot establish that a technically difficult system will work — so that when the second family dominates a direct test must be run earlier. Recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md), from a complete reading of the source, **which reports no controlled comparison, no sampling method, no measured effect and no validated threshold.**

Recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md), from a complete reading of access-controlled material **that no public reader can check. No figure from it is reproduced here.**

That a capability boundary exists which does not track human intuitions about task difficulty, and that assisted performance outside it can fall below unassisted performance — recorded at [research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md](/research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md), **from the published abstract only; the full paper was not read.** The finding concerns one organisation's population on bounded tasks with one model generation, and its out-of-boundary result rests on a single task. **No figure from it is reproduced anywhere in this repository.**

That a retrieval benchmark was constructed specifically because improvements had been measured on a small number of datasets and most often on the training dataset; that it evaluated systems spanning lexical, sparse, dense, late-interaction and re-ranking architectures under zero-shot conditions across eighteen publicly available datasets; that a long-standing lexical method proved a robust baseline which did not collapse on any domain; that re-ranking and late-interaction models achieved the best average zero-shot performance at high computational cost while dense and sparse models were more efficient and often underperformed; and that the authors characterise the last as considerable room for improvement in generalisation — recorded at [research/02-technical-foundations/thakur-beir.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/thakur-beir.md), **verified against the paper's abstract page. **Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That a question with no stated answer format is resolved by the authority present rather than by evidence; that a judgement recorded after an output has been seen is contaminated by it; and that an investigation is an experiment whose detectable difference should be considered before it runs, with related groups of cases making a naive result look more precise than it is. The note for the last is held in this repository but was not re-read in full for this paper. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted for any of these.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The six-column question set; the requirement that the class be assigned before the instrument; the ordering rule placing the cheapest disqualifier first; the release valve into the [S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md) accepted column; the decision to assess the boundary judgement on existence rather than accuracy; the treatment of a documented answer as a first-class instrument and the restriction of it to definitional rather than comparative questions; the `read_on` and `could_have_detected` fields; the instruction to move a row rather than infer a limit when documentation is silent; and all eight predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** That build paths are split by service model — platform-as-a-service, software-as-a-service and infrastructure-as-a-service — with selection criteria stated as **"your organization's technical capabilities, timeline, and control requirements"**; that Foundry's internal split runs between **prompt agents** defined declaratively with **no agent compute** and **hosted agents** running containerised code on managed container compute; that the **basic setup "lacks network isolation"** and is positioned for rapid prototyping while the standard setup provides fine-grained control over data, security and networking; and that **Copilot Studio is "designed for small-scale deployments, such as department-level solutions"** — all recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md), **read 2026-07-30 through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches, with several comparison tables truncated in the excerpts read.** That note marks the material **highly perishable** and records that the Agent Framework SDK is documented as formerly Semantic Kernel.

**The behaviour of the documentation server the workflow runs on is carried forward from [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md) and was not re-verified for this paper. No research note for it exists in this repository at all**, which is a gap in the research layer rather than a caveat about the tool, and the session states the absence to learners in the workflow rather than in a footnote.

**The silences that matter.** The documentation **publishes no adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no evidence that its recommended mappings produce better outcomes** — so it can settle what a product is and can settle nothing about whether choosing it works, which is exactly the boundary §8's third objection turns on. **It states a scale envelope for one option and states none for most of the others**, which is why the design's response to a silence is to move the row rather than to infer a bound: an absence here is the normal case rather than the exception. It **publishes no measure of how quickly its own regional availability, deployment types or capability boundaries change**, so the `read_on` date is required without any published guidance on how long an answer stays good. And it **says nothing about the difference between a question a page can settle and a question a page cannot** — the distinction this whole session is built on has no vendor support, and none is claimed.
