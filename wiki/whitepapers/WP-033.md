## In plain terms

*Is this feasible?* has no answer format, no instrument and no failing outcome. It will therefore be answered by whoever is most senior in the room, and the answer will be a disposition rather than a finding.

Underneath it are several questions that do have answers, and getting from one to the other is the session's work. A question is admissible here only when it has an answer format — a number, a yes, a count, a transcript, a demonstration on inputs somebody else chose — an instrument that could produce it, a stated cost for running that instrument, and a decision that moves in a named direction depending on the answer.

The session's first move is a split that learners resist because it feels bureaucratic and turns out to be the whole thing. **Uncertainty about whether anybody wants this, will fund it and is permitted to have it is settled by talking to people. Uncertainty about whether it can be built, whether the data supports it and whether the model behaves is not, and no amount of talking will settle it.** Merging the two is the commonest failure in a feasibility set and it is invisible from inside, because both kinds of question can be asked in the same meeting.

Its second move is about positive results. A demonstration on a chosen example establishes something about that example. This matters more than it used to, because there is a well-attested pattern in which assistance improves work inside a system's capability and degrades it outside — with the boundary not following intuitions about difficulty, and the failures being persuasive rather than obvious. **No magnitude for that is asserted anywhere in this wiki**, and the habit it licenses is narrow: decide which side of the boundary you think you are on, before you use the instrument, in writing.

## Abstract

This paper defends converting felt feasibility uncertainty into a set of questions each carrying an answer format, an instrument, a price and a decision. The argument is that an unformatted question is resolved by authority rather than by evidence, that market-facing and system-facing uncertainties require categorically different instruments, and that a positive result obtained on inputs the demonstrator chose licenses very little. The session installs a six-column question set, a routing rule inherited from [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md), an ordering rule placing the cheapest disqualifier first, and an explicit written capability-boundary judgement recorded before the instrument is used. The paper concedes that the ordering rule has no external warrant, that the capability-boundary literature it draws on has been read here at abstract depth only and rests on a narrow evidential base outside the boundary, and that the source corpus contains no cost side of any kind and therefore cannot rank any instrument by price.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Feasibility arrives as a feeling and gets resolved as a hierarchy.

The mechanism is simple and nearly universal. Somebody asks whether the thing is possible. The most confident engineer present says it should be fine. Nobody has a way to disagree that is not a challenge to that person's competence, so the question closes. **The room has not made a mistake — it has made a decision using the only instrument available to it, which was somebody's disposition.**

There is a second, quieter version. A team knows the question is real, and defers it: feasibility will be established during the build. That is not deferral, it is reclassification. **A feasibility question answered during delivery is not a decision, it is a defect**, and it arrives with a schedule attached.

The session's response is to require an answer format before an instrument, an instrument before a run, and a price before either — and to order the whole set by how cheaply each question could stop the work rather than by how interesting it is.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** Two retrievals are load-bearing.

[S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) supplied the routing discipline — some uncertainties cannot be resolved by talking to anybody — and this session is where it is applied under maximum temptation, because a confident colleague's assurance is the cheapest instrument in existence and settles nothing. **The same rule, applied twice, thirteen sessions apart, and the second application is harder than the first.**

[S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md) supplied the charter, whose evidence clause is this session's model: evidence named before it exists. The question set is a charter's evidence clause, generalised across every uncertainty in the design.

The prior the session works against is that feasibility is a property of a system rather than of a question about a system. Learners arrive expecting to assess a design; the session requires them to assess several claims that a design happens to make.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** High, and the module's intrinsic peak. Converting a felt uncertainty into a formatted question, selecting an instrument that could produce that format, and pricing it — all before knowing the answer — does not decompose into simpler steps, and it must be held for several uncertainties at once. The load is the simultaneity rather than any single row.

**3.2 Why the two classes need separating before instruments are chosen.** Whether people want the outcome, will fund it and may have it is reducible by conversation. Whether a system can be built, whether the data supports it and whether a model behaves is not. **A team that has not made the split will use a conversation on the second class, because conversations are cheap and available**, and the resulting row looks identical on paper to a properly routed one.

**3.3 Why chosen inputs are the central defeater of a positive result.** A demonstration on an example the demonstrator selected is evidence about that example. The generalisation from it is not weak, it is absent, and the strength of the impression is uncorrelated with the strength of the evidence.

**3.4 Why the capability-boundary judgement is written before the instrument is used.** The pattern this rests on is that assistance helps inside a capability boundary and hurts outside it, that the boundary does not follow human intuitions about difficulty, and that out-of-boundary performance can fall below unassisted performance — which implies the failures are adopted rather than detected. A judgement made afterwards is contaminated by the output. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted, and the boundary's location moves with the tooling.**

**3.5 Why the cheapest disqualifier goes first.** Ordering by cost-to-disqualify maximises the expected saving from an early stop. This is an argument from decision cost and has no measurement behind it in this repository.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 3 is the instrument.** Learners are shown a successful demonstration on a chosen example and asked what they have learned. The confident answer and the correct answer differ sharply, and the gap is more effective as an experience than as an instruction.

**The class split is required before the instrument column may be filled**, structurally rather than by advice, because a learner who chooses instruments first will retro-fit the classes to the instruments they picked.

**The corpus's buried refusal is used as the session's worked example**, and its choice is deliberate. On a mature production codebase the assistant could not be asked to add a feature end to end — the generated code reused outdated patterns and ignored architectural constraints — and the workaround was to make failing tests the specification. **That passage has a question, an instrument, a negative answer and a next step: it is a feasibility finding.** It also contradicts its own collection's headline claims about speed, and it sits in speaker notes. The session asks the learner where a finding of that shape would end up in their own organisation and who would see it, which is a question about institutions rather than about method and is the session's only treatment of that theme.

**The review movement blanks the instrument column** and asks a peer to supply one per row. Rows for which no peer can propose an instrument are not questions yet, and the session's instruction is to rewrite them or move them into the accepted column of the [S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md) record with an owner. **That is a deliberate release valve**: not every uncertainty must be investigated, and pretending otherwise is how a feasibility set becomes a project.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The conversational answer to a technical question.** The likeliest failure and the one [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) predicted. *Countermeasure:* the class column, filled before instruments, and the standard's warning sign — your instrument for *will the model do this* is an interview.

**The chosen example.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that the set name what would make a positive answer untrustworthy, and the explicit *who chose the input* field carried into [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md)'s evidence section.

**The interesting first.** The learner investigates the part they want to build. *Countermeasure:* the ordering rule and the standard's warning sign — the cheapest disqualifier is still unrun.

**The persuasive wrong answer.** Output was fluent, structured and confident, and was accepted. *Countermeasure:* the written boundary judgement made before use, held separately from the result so that the two can be compared. **This is weak and the paper says so.** A written judgement does not confer the ability to detect a plausible error; it only makes the failure legible afterwards.

**The unanswerable question that survives review.** *Countermeasure:* the peer instrument-supply exercise, and the release valve into the accepted column.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. Two specification items at [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md): that at least one question was answered negatively or the absence is flagged as suspicious, and that the evidence section records who chose the inputs.

**Never assessed:** the number of questions, whether the answers were favourable, or whether the learner's capability-boundary judgement turned out to be correct. **The judgement is assessed for having been made and recorded, never for accuracy** — which is a real weakness and §8 takes it seriously.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the boundary judgement is unfalsifiable as taught, and assessing it on existence rather than accuracy teaches learners that the ritual is the skill.**

At its strongest: the design asks learners to write down whether they think a task is inside or outside a system's capability, then explicitly declines to mark whether they were right. That is worse than not asking. It trains the production of a confident-sounding artefact and provides no feedback signal at all, which is the exact failure the programme criticises the corpus for — an instrument arranged so that no result can disconfirm anything.

**Conceded, and the concession is uncomfortable because the parallel is exact.** The design's reason is that marking accuracy requires a ground truth about a boundary that moves between cohorts and sometimes between weeks, and a mark awarded against a boundary that has since moved is noise wearing a grade's clothing. **The declined fix is to score the judgement against the observed outcome within the session.** It is declined because the observed outcome is a single instance on a single task, which is precisely the evidential weakness this paper attributes to the literature it cites, and importing it into assessment would be inconsistent. **What the design does instead is require the judgement and the result to be recorded separately so that a cohort accumulating them can be studied**, which is a promise about future evidence rather than a rebuttal. §9 prediction 3 is the test.

**Objection 2: the two-class split is a false dichotomy, and enforcing it will cause learners to misroute the many uncertainties that are genuinely both.**

At its strongest: most consequential uncertainties in real engagements are mixed. Whether a workflow can be automated is simultaneously a question about the system's capability and about whether the people doing it now will accept the result — and the second half determines what *capable* even means. A rule that forces each row into one column will push learners to treat the technical half as the question and the human half as context, which is the failure the whole programme was built to prevent.

**Partly conceded.** The split is a routing heuristic and not an ontology, and mixed uncertainties are real. The rebuttal is that a mixed uncertainty routed to a single instrument is the failure the split is designed to catch: the correct handling is to split the *row*, not to pick a column. **The declined fix is to add a third class for mixed uncertainties.** It is declined because a third class becomes the default — it is the comfortable answer for every row — and the discipline the session is trying to install is the decomposition rather than the label. **That reasoning is design reasoning and nothing warrants it**, and a cohort that routed well while using a mixed class would refute it.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Before instruction, most learners' answers to opening item 3 will treat a successful demonstration on a chosen example as evidence of general capability. *Instrument:* the written answers, coded *general* / *instance-limited* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** In the completed sets, a substantial share of system-facing uncertainties will carry a conversational instrument. *Instrument:* the class and instrument columns, cross-tabulated, with the classification checked by a second reader.

**Prediction 3.** Recorded capability-boundary judgements will be more often wrong for tasks the learner rated as difficult than for tasks they rated as routine. *Instrument:* the pre-use judgement, the learner's difficulty rating, and the observed outcome, collected per row across the cohort. **This is the prediction that would give §8 objection 1 its answer, and it requires more than one cohort to run.**

**Prediction 4.** Learners will order their sets by interest rather than by cost-to-disqualify, and the first-run row will more often be the one they most wanted to build. *Instrument:* the run order against the stated instrument costs, with the learner's own ranking of interest collected separately at the start.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That feasibility is several questions rather than one, that conversation and construction settle different families of them, and that a positive result is only as strong as the person who chose the inputs.

**What the evidence licenses.** A routing heuristic from a practitioner handbook that establishes no effect and says so; a capability-boundary pattern read here at abstract depth only, whose out-of-boundary result rests on a single task; and one organisation's buried record of a technical limit. **Direction and mechanism throughout. No magnitude.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The session requires a judgement it declines to mark, for a reason it states, and it has arranged to be told later whether that was right. Hold it as an open item rather than as a settled design.
