> ## Release rule — this is an assessed occasion, and the usual framing is false here
>
> **The standard "read this after the day, not before" is wrong for this paper and applying it blind would misdescribe the occasion.** This is an entrustment day, and a substantial part of what a release rule would normally protect **is published to students before phase 1 by design**: the five named failure conditions, the symmetric provision that a refusal of the engagement with its reasons is a pass at the same levels as an agreement, the phase 4 defence questions, and the final walk-away question — *name the point at which you would have walked away; did you reach it* — which §3.3 states is published in advance and asked of everyone precisely so that discovering you had no floor is a finding rather than an ambush. **§7's statement of the checked elements and the failure conditions therefore tells a student nothing the brief will not**, and reading it early is not contamination.
>
> **What is contamination is different in kind, and it is the reason this block exists.** Item 2 asks the student to write, cold, the point at which they would refuse — and the day's central measurement is the comparison between that and element 7. §1 states in three sentences why almost nobody writes a floor, that a floor held only as a feeling moves without the holder noticing, and that this is what item 2 and element 7 exist to catch. §3.2 gives every difficulty, gives the seven elements, and gives both strong-form scope statements. §3.3 names B's refusal as the engine and gives the phase 4 final question with its rationale. §4 states item 5's expected answer outright. §5 gives both fallbacks. §8 objection 2 gives the two-branch entrustment finding; objection 3 gives the build discharge. **§9 prediction 2 states the day's most important measurement and the direction the design expects, and prediction 5 states what item 5 is for.** A student who reads those before phase 1 has been handed the instrument rather than the brief.
>
> **The vendor paragraph in `## Evidence status` is the sharpest spoiler anywhere in this range, and the paper itself says why.** It quotes the grantor's published refusal criterion verbatim. §4 states that phase 6 points 7 and 8 are placed **after the assessed work** for the reason instrumentation rule 8 gives — that telling the room the criterion before the encounter would make the encounter a test of following an instruction.
>
> **A reader should notice that the paper is not internally consistent about this and should not assume it has been resolved.** §4's phase 2 entry and §8 objection 1 both state that the twelve-minute rehearsal has the student read that same published criterion, through the Learn MCP Server, **before** the encounters, and objection 1 treats reading it as the rehearsal's principal improvement. Those two statements cannot both describe the same day. **Nothing here resolves it**, and until it is resolved a reader cannot tell whether the Evidence status quotation spoils phase 6 or merely restates phase 2.
>
> **Pre-day reading, if you want any:** §2, §3.1, §6, and the failure conditions and defence questions at the top of §7, which you receive in the brief anyway.
>
> **Post-day reading:** everything else, including the Abstract, §§1, 3.2, 3.3, 4, 5, 8 and 9, the student-facing close and Evidence status.

## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

Two people want incompatible things and neither can evaluate the other's constraint. One runs the service and knows what they want; they have no idea what it costs in quota. The other holds the quota and knows what it is for; they have no idea what the first has been promised. They will not meet.

**You are the only person who can see both. That is a position, not an advantage.** You can produce an agreement this afternoon by telling each of them something true and partial, and nobody but you will ever know you did it. The programme has been circling that question since week thirteen; this is the first occasion where both parties act on the answer.

Three things are being judged, and none of them is charm.

**Did you write the floor down before you went in?** Everybody has a sense of what they will not agree to. Almost nobody writes it out first, because writing it feels like anticipating a fight. A floor that exists only as a feeling moves under pressure without you noticing it move — and afterwards you will remember having held it. So the day asks for it in a sealed item at the start and compares it against the finished scope, and then asks you, out loud, in front of both counterparties: *where would you have walked away, and did you get there?*

**Does your out-of-scope list assign anything to anybody?** "Data migration is out of scope" survives every review and commits nobody. "Data migration is out of scope and belongs to the platform team, who have not been told" is the same fact stated so that somebody has to act. Only the second has been negotiated.

**And what do you do when the second party refuses to resource what the first has already accepted?** Re-cut the scope and tell them both, and you have done well. Tell neither, and you have done the commonest thing in professional practice and failed the occasion. The order is fixed — service owner first — precisely so that this is available to watch.

**What this paper concedes, and it is a real concession.** This occasion judges a competence the programme taught four quarters ago and rehearsed for twelve minutes on the morning of the judgement. The larger repair — an unassessed grantor encounter the week before — was declined for a reason the paper calls its own weakest, the structural fix belongs a module and a quarter upstream, and the cohort is told all of this out loud at phase 6. **There is a second concession and it is sharper: the entrustment statement pre-commits to the same four actions whichever way its measurement comes out, which is a decision that the measurement will not matter, written in a form that reads like rigour.** The paper names that as the second instance in the programme of satisfying a rule's form while defeating its purpose.

## Abstract

This paper defends an entrustment judgement on a negotiation in which two real counterparties want incompatible things and neither can evaluate the other's constraint, and defends assessing the scope statement written before the room rather than the conduct inside it. It defends a floor committed in advance as the only verifiable form of a negotiating position, an out-of-scope list required to name an assignee per item, and a warranty required to state what happens when it is not met. It defends putting the service owner first so that a scope is committed before the resource is refused. **The day builds nothing, which is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s provision for an assessment-bearing occasion; the module's four build records travel into the defence instead, and the quota rehearsal is executed against the student's own allocation and the grantor's own published refusal criterion rather than against a supplied position.** **Section 7 reports the discharge of the obligation [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) placed here on constructed-pressure predictiveness, together with three further rule-6 branches and the measure [WP-039](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-039.md) §9 carried forward.** Section 8 concedes the quota-rehearsal deficit [WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 served on this day, concedes that the entrustment statement's two-branch pre-commitment has arranged for the measurement's result to change nothing, and faces the objection that reading four build records at a defence is a discharge in form only.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Four days have produced statements about an organisation. None of them commits anybody, and every one of them is the kind of artefact a student can produce alone.

**The problem this day must solve is that the student is the only party who can see both constraints, and that this is a position rather than an advantage.** The service owner knows what they want and does not know what it costs in quota. The grantor knows what the quota is for and does not know what the service owner has been promised. Neither will meet the other. The student holds both, and can therefore produce an agreement by telling each party something true and partial — which is the [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) question, unresolved through [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) point 3 and [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) point 2, arriving at the first occasion where both parties act on the answer and the student is the only person who could ever know that they had.

Three specific difficulties sit under it.

**The floor is not written.** Every student will have a sense of what they will not agree to, and almost none will have written it down before entering the room, because writing it feels like anticipating a fight. A floor that exists only as a feeling moves under pressure without the student noticing it move, and the movement is unrecoverable afterwards — including by the student, who will remember having held it. This is why item 2 and element 7 exist and why the day's central instrument is a comparison between them.

**The exclusion is not an assignment.** An out-of-scope list is the easiest thing in a scope statement to write and the least likely to have been negotiated. "Data migration is out of scope" is a sentence that survives every review and commits nobody; **"data migration is out of scope and is the platform team's, who have not been told"** is the same fact stated so that somebody has to act. The design's position is that the second form is the only one that has been negotiated, and the first is a decision deferred with a sentence in front of it — which is [WP-070](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §1's conditional-reflex finding in a different register.

**The agreement that one party will not resource.** This is the day's real problem and it has no clean answer. A student who agrees a scope with A, is refused by B, and re-cuts the scope has done well. A student who agrees with A, is refused by B, and tells neither has done the commonest thing in professional practice and has failed the occasion. The design's claim is that the second is a failure of nerve rather than of method, and the fixed order — A first — is what makes it available to observe.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Nothing is built and the load is entirely dispositional**, per [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §3.1 and [WP-070](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §3.1, both of which this paper accepts unchanged.

The interactivity that remains is between elements 4, 5 and 7. What is excluded determines what the four quantities have to cover; the binding quantity determines what B can refuse; and the floor is the point at which the student stops re-cutting. A student discovers, usually during the B encounter, that the item they excluded most casually is the one whose assignee would have solved the resource problem — which is the artefact's most productive moment and is why the three elements are adjacent.

Management:

- **Nothing is built on the day.** The scope statement is committed before phase 3.
- **All seven elements are assembled from the four preceding days' artefacts**, and the form is supplied.
- **Assessors observe and do not intervene**; staff answer questions of fact and refuse questions of judgement.
- **Consolidation comes after the assessed work**, so nobody negotiates while holding a framework.

The binding constraint on the day is phase 4: fifteen minutes per student in groups of four, with two assessors and both counterparties present simultaneously. That is four people in a room for an hour per group, which is the heaviest staffing demand in the programme and is the sharpest instance of the [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s open staffing question.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Writing your refusal before the room,* item 2, checked at phase 4's last question.
- *A scope committed before the negotiation and not repairable during it,* phase 3.
- *An out-of-scope list that must name an assignee per item,* element 4.
- *A warranty that must state its own failure behaviour,* element 3.
- *A concession made on behalf of somebody absent, with a named teller,* element 2.
- *A carrying mode the receiving organisation must supply, with a named maintainer,* element 6.
- *A counterparty who refuses and will not say why,* encounter B.
- *Both counterparties in the room together at the defence,* which forecloses telling each a different partial truth.
- *A five-month-old class-3 negotiation returned with today's record beside it,* phase 5 part three.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Bad faith.* Neither counterparty is briefed to obstruct. B's reticence is the ordinary condition, not a construction.
- *A third party at the table.* Multi-party negotiation with three present principals is beyond what one occasion can assess and beyond what the substrate supplies.
- *Constructing the evidence on the day.* [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §3.1's argument, accepted.
- *An adversarial commercial counterparty.* [WP-065](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-065.md) §3.2 named the misaligned-counterparty case as this day's; **it is not run here.** The grantor's interests differ from the student's and are not opposed to them, and a counterparty who benefits from the student's transparency is a different skill the programme does not teach. **This is a deferral the design makes and does not discharge**, and no later day carries it.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The seven-element scope statement is **safe as the form of an engagement scope where you or a colleague will operate the delivered thing, where both counterparties are inside one organisation or in a relationship with an established escalation path, and where the commitment is for a bounded term with a review point.** It is **unsafe as the form of a contractual scope between organisations with no shared authority above them.** The failure is specific: element 4's assignments have no force across an organisational boundary, because nobody can assign a problem to a party in another company; element 7's floor is a negotiating position rather than a term, and stating it in writing before a commercial negotiation discloses your reservation price; and element 3's warranty in [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s three-part form is an engineering statement and not a contractual one, and presenting it as though it were will produce a commitment the student's employer is bound by and did not make. **In a cross-organisational commercial negotiation the correct move is to say that this form does not apply and to route the commitment through whoever holds contracting authority**, which is a person, not a process, and which this day does not teach. This is stated to students at phase 6 and **it is not a checked artefact element**; [WP-083](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-083.md) §8 argued for binding one scope statement to its artefact and the same argument would apply here, and this paper declines it because the case does not arise on the substrate and a checked element for an inapplicable case is a formality.

**The platform instance carries a second, at point 7.** The vendor's published quota-increase criterion — that requests are processed in the order received and that **priority goes to customers who actively use their existing allocation** — is **safe as preparation for a request to this grantor, at this date, for a model family that supports increases at all.** It is **unsafe as a theory of how grantors behave.** The failure is the one this module is most likely to produce because it is the one a concrete fact invites: a published rule is memorable, it is true, and a graduate who carries it as a general principle has learned something about one vendor's allocation policy and will apply it to a colleague, an internal platform team or a supplier for whom it is false. **The day states the boundary at point 7 and it is not bound to the artefact**, and the reason is the same one this paper gives for the seven-element form: element 5 already requires the four quantities with their owners and cadences, and adding a clause requiring the student to disclaim a generalisation they may not have made would collect a disclaimer rather than a judgement. **That is a judgement the design is making twice in one paper and it should be read as one decision rather than two.**

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **B's refusal.**

Every prior negotiation in the programme ended with an outcome the student could hold. [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md)'s requester agreed or refused and the student's position stood either way. [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md)'s payers could decline the ask and the ask survived. **Here the second counterparty's refusal invalidates something the first one accepted**, and the student is holding a commitment they have made and cannot resource. That is a genuinely uncomfortable ninety seconds and it is the occasion's whole content.

Autonomy is complete: the scope is the student's, refusal of the engagement is a pass, and the floor is theirs to set. Competence is supported by nothing being built and by every element tracing to a specific prior day's artefact. Relatedness is supplied by both counterparties being colleagues of the institution whose service this is, and by phase 5 part one, at which the design reports its own concession to the room.

The affective risk is at phase 4's final question — *name the point at which you would have walked away; did you reach it* — asked of every student in front of both counterparties. A student who discovers publicly that they never wrote a floor they meant has been shown something about themselves in the presence of the people they were negotiating with. **The countermeasure is that the question is published in advance, is asked of everyone, and that discovering you had no floor is treated in the narrative as a finding rather than as a fault**, on the same footing as [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s least-honest-encounter question and [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md)'s least-defensible-number question. It is the third day to use that construction and the design records that as approaching instrumentation rule 2's threshold.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items. Item 2 is new to this occasion and is the day's central instrument. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction and is the programme's sharpest frontier item: the agent will answer the technical question completely and cannot reach the question the day is about.**

**Phase 2 (35 min).** Seven retrievals across the module and two quarters, a twelve-minute unassessed quota rehearsal **executed against the student's own allocation and the grantor's published refusal criterion** and named as insufficient in the same breath, and a bridge that produces the day's problem from [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md)'s consent standard. The phase runs fifteen minutes over the pattern's twenty and the rehearsal is the departure.

**Phase 3 (60 min).** Seven-element scope statement committed beforehand; two encounters of twenty-five minutes, A first. Elements 2, 5 and 6 are the rule-6 receiving elements. **The day carries no contrasting cases and no build, both of which the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](/wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) permits for an assessment-bearing occasion, and both departures are stated in the day rather than only here.**

**Phase 4 (60 min).** Level formed from the statement and the encounters first, per rule 7. Both counterparties present together for the first time. **A published question requires the student to take one of the module's four measurements and say what it does not measure**, which is where the build records are read. Perturbation in EA-1's form with the absent-party term added.

**Phase 5 (35 min).** Levels sealed first. Three carry-forward parts, none assessed: the constructed-pressure verdict reported to the room, the quota deficit with the cohort's own [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) requests returned, and the [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md) comparison written by the assessors.

**Phase 6 (35 min).** Eight points, assessors absent. Point 5 says the quota deficit out loud. **Points 7 and 8 are the programme's last platform points and are placed after the assessed work for the reason instrumentation rule 8 gives: telling the room the grantor's published criterion before the encounter would have made the encounter a test of following an instruction.**

**Phase 7 (40 min).** A staff member's undelivered scope, read as written; **an agent-assembled feasibility note that was right about every figure and wrong about the question**; and a disclosure about a commitment made four hours earlier.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 1 against element 1; item 2 against the floor; item 3 against element 5 with the [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) rate beside and uncompared; **item 5 opened and the module's five frontier readings shown as a series with their sampling stated**; predicted against awarded; the two carry-forward aggregates; the psychological-safety series.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has negotiated professionally will produce a fluent scope and will handle A well. The predicted failure is **at element 4**: they will produce an exclusion list, because in professional practice that is what an exclusion list is, and assigning each item to a named party is an unusual and slightly confrontational demand. Countermeasure: it is a named failure condition, published before phase 1, and B's encounter frequently turns on it — an unassigned exclusion is the resource nobody has budgeted.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is paralysis in the B encounter: a refusal with no reason given, twenty-five minutes, and a commitment already made to A. Countermeasures: refusal of the engagement is a declared pass; the seven elements are supplied and each traces to a prior day's artefact; staff answer questions of fact; and [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md)'s purpose-inquiry method is retrieved at phase 2 and rehearsed for twelve minutes against the student's own allocation immediately before the encounter.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** A student whose scope B happens to be able to resource has had a straightforward occasion and not a defective one. The difficulty is relocated to phase 4's perturbation, and specifically to *what if the quota refusal is permanent rather than this term's* — which is the [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) commitment-shape question with no arithmetic available, and which a student whose scope was resourced has no prepared answer to. Fallback if a student handles all three perturbations: ask what they would tell A if B's refusal arrived in week three rather than today, and who would tell them.

**Affective failure.** Two, and they are opposite.

The first is **the floor discovered to be absent**, described in §3.3.

The second is the module's characteristic risk arriving where it costs most: **a student who refuses the engagement, passes, and reads the pass as a courtesy.** [M14](/wiki/modules/M14.md) met the same shape at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) with not-shipping, and the countermeasures transfer: the failure conditions are symmetric and published before phase 1; phase 6 point 2 shows the cohort's split without names; and the instructor states once that the programme has awarded its highest levels on this occasion in both directions. **A refusal is additionally the correct answer more often here than at S070**, because a scope that cannot be resourced and cannot be re-cut is a scope nobody should agree, and the design says so.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) at one week (where what was agreed becomes what must transfer), [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) (where element 3's warranty becomes a claim in a safety case), [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) (where element 2's concession becomes a residual risk borne by somebody who was not consulted), [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) at four weeks (where the warranty and the floor are the handover artefact's objects, and where the first instrument responds) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) at five weeks (where the second responds and the whole engagement is defended).

**[S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) is the consequential retrieval**, and it is the only occasion in the programme where a commitment is checked against what actually survived a term rather than against what the student predicted. The floor written this morning is either still the floor in week 89 or it moved, and the reason it moved is the content.

Retained content is procedural and short: the seven elements; the exclusion-as-assignment rule; the warranty's failure clause; the floor written before the room; and the position of holding two constraints nobody else can see. Five items.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 1, and at S089.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**The quarter's first entrustment judgement.** Activity: *negotiate scope under constraint with a party who can say no.* Expected **3a**; 3b available and rare. Precursor to EA-3 and EA-1 and neither.

Level formed from the scope statement and the two encounters before the defence, per rater discipline rule 7. Narrative substantiation mandatory and required to cite specific moments from the statement and from both encounters.

Five named failure conditions, published before phase 1, plus the symmetric provision that a refusal of the engagement with its reasons and conditions is a pass at the same levels as an agreement.

Per rule 1 the checked elements are the seven elements of the scope statement as listed at phase 3, the two negotiation records including a refusal, the phase 5 part-two paragraph, **and the module's four prompt records, intervention records and frontier statements, read at the defence.** **Nothing else is assessed** — not whether the engagement was agreed, not whether the grantor granted anything, and not whether any frontier judgement was correct.

**A warranty of a cost position whose enforcement the student did not build fails**, per phase 6 point 8 and element 3's failure clause: an Azure budget alerts and does not cap, so warranting a ceiling the platform does not supply is warranting a thing that does not exist.

**This day produces no build.** The [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) provides for that on assessment-bearing days, and the module's four build records are read here instead through phase 4's published question. **They do not form the level.** The [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) is explicit that the build record is the weakest evidence class it holds and is never used alone; on this occasion it is used exactly as rater discipline rule 7 provides, to test a level formed from the scope statement and the encounters. **The question the defence asks of them is not *how did you build this* but *what does this measurement not measure*, which is a claim about the scope resting on it.**

**Phase 5's results are not assessed**, and the specification says so in writing.

### Discharge of the obligation S070 placed on this day

**This paragraph is the report [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s instrumentation block requires.**

[S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s first rule-6 instrument is the [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)-against-S070 comparison field — the programme's only direct measurement of whether conduct under constructed pressure predicts conduct under real pressure — and it named this day, specifying that "**[S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md)'s entrustment statement must state whether levels awarded under constructed pressure are treated as predictive, on the evidence of this comparison, and must say what it does differently if they are not.**"

**The statement exists and it states the position under both branches, before the result is known.** If the comparison shows real-pressure conduct at or above constructed-pressure level, the programme treats a designed-occasion level as **weak** evidence — weak because [WP-070](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §8 objection 1 conceded the comparison is under-dosed and that a null is as likely to be a dose effect as a pressure effect. If it shows real-pressure conduct systematically below, the programme treats designed-occasion levels as **overestimates**, and the statement names four things this day does differently: **both counterparties are real and neither is played, so the assessed occasion has no constructed element at all; the assessors are not shown any prior level for any student before recording their own; today's level is stated in the assessors' brief and the student's record to be no evidence about EA-1 or EA-3; and the comparison's direction is reported to the cohort at phase 5 part one, with its dose, so that a student whose file contains an overestimate is told.**

**All four are in force today regardless of which branch holds.** The reason is that the design will not choose its procedure after seeing the number, and the cost is examined in §8 objection 2, which argues that this converts a measurement into a ritual.

**Three further rule-6 branches are discharged.** [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) named this day for its party-set movement instrument: **element 2 is the receiving element, it is checked, its absence is a named failure condition, it is a published defence question, and where the S082 cohort figure was low the concession is additionally prompted inside the A encounter.** [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) named this day for its decision-identification rate as a branch: **where that rate was low, each counterparty's decision and date are supplied as given material rather than required, so that an entrustment occasion does not turn on a competence the prior day found absent; where it was high, the student establishes both.** [S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md) named this day for its carrying-mode branch outcome: **element 6 is the receiving element and is checked.**

**One carried measure is discharged.** [WP-039](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-039.md) §9 carried its phase 7 absent-party acceptance rate forward to this day as the agreement-recording measure. **Phase 5 part three retrieves each student's [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md) outcome and absent-party result and sets it against today's element 2 and today's record**, written by the assessors in the sealed field, costing the student no phase time, reported to the cohort in aggregate at phase 8 and returned individually in private. It is not assessed.

**One deferral is received and not discharged.** [WP-065](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-065.md) §3.2 named the misaligned commercial counterparty — where the other party's interest is opposed to the student's employer's and the transparency the programme rewards can be exploited — as this day's case. **It is not run.** Both counterparties here are inside the institution and neither benefits from the student's candour. No later day carries it and the programme does not address it, which is recorded here rather than left implicit.

**What this day does not license anyone to conclude.** It generates evidence that a student can commit a scope, hold or move a floor, and carry information between two parties who cannot evaluate each other's constraints. It generates none about conduct in a commercial negotiation, none about EA-1 or EA-3, and — per the phase 5 part-one branch — none that transfers to any other occasion. The assessors' brief says all three.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments, both responding outside this module. **The element 7 floor-holding rate** responds at [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md), whose warranty must restate this day's floor and state whether it survived the term and what moved it. **The element 2 absent-party concession record** responds at [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md), whose perturbation set must include *what if the party who was not in the room is the one who bears it*, answered from the student's own S085 record. **[WP-089](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-089.md) §7 and [WP-090](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

Programme instrumentation: item 1 against element 1; item 2 against the phase 4 answer; item 3 against element 5, with the [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) classification rate beside it and uncompared per rule 2; assessor divergence; the A-agreed-B-refused rate and how many students told A; and the psychological-safety series.

ABET and CS2023 contribution: PO3, PO5 and PO7. Dispositions: **integrity**, at element 2 and at phase 4's *what did you agree to that you cannot warrant*; **humility**, at B's refusal; **reliability**, at element 7, where the floor written in the morning is the one the record will be read against in week 89.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: this occasion judges a competence the programme taught four quarters ago and never rehearsed, and a five-minute retrieval item does not repair that.**

[WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 objection 2 served this notice on this paper explicitly, and it is worth restating at its strength rather than paraphrasing it. [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) point 3 teaches that quota is a stated constraint with a purpose and that [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md)'s method moves it. **S039 taught that claim with four negotiation transcripts, a real requester, and a phase 5 in which a constraint was actually moved. S064 has none of those** — the student writes a grantor request at step 7's third clause and nobody receives it. WP-064's conclusion was blunt: "**a claim that a constraint is movable, taught with no opportunity to move one, produces a belief rather than a competence — and the specific failure it produces is a graduate who tells a vendor's account manager that quota is negotiable and cannot conduct the conversation that follows. That is worse than not raising it.**" It then recorded the deferral so that this paper "is on notice that it is receiving a competence taught four quarters earlier with no rehearsal, which is the condition the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s deferral rule exists to make visible."

**The notice is accepted in full, and the damaging form of it is that this occasion is where the rehearsal would have to be and it is an entrustment judgement.** A competence first practised at the occasion where it is judged has not been rehearsed. Nothing in the day's design changes that, and a student who is refused by B and cannot conduct the purpose-inquiry conversation is being assessed on a gap in the timetable.

**The design takes the cheapest available repair and states that it is not enough.** Phase 2 carries a twelve-minute quota move, unassessed, immediately before the encounters, **and the practical turn has made it a materially better twelve minutes than the five it replaces.** The student now reads their own subscription's usage against its limit and the regions in which the model can be deployed, through the Azure MCP Server; reads the grantor's **published refusal criterion** through the Learn MCP Server — that requests are processed in order and that priority goes to customers already using their allocation; and then writes the ask against both. **That converts an invented position into a real one and an imagined grantor into one with a stated rule.** It does not convert twelve minutes into a quarter. **Twelve minutes against [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md)'s ninety is a token, and phase 6 point 5 says so to the cohort in those terms**, together with what would actually fix it — WP-064's own named fix, a quota grantor at [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) phase 3 in place of a second payer, which is not this day's to make and must be argued at [M13](/wiki/modules/M13.md).

**One thing the improvement does change and it should be recorded rather than claimed.** The published criterion is the kind of fact [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md)'s method exists to find: a stated constraint with a purpose, discoverable before you ask. **A student who reads it and shapes the request around it has performed the S039 move once, against a real grantor's real rule, and that is more than the design previously offered.** Whether performing it once against a document is a rehearsal for performing it against a person is exactly the question [WP-070](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §8 conceded the programme has no instrument for.

**A second and larger repair was considered and declined.** [S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md)'s phase 5, one week earlier, has a real user present for thirty minutes and could have carried an unassessed grantor encounter instead. It is declined because S084's phase 5 is fully committed to an adoption position with its own rule-6 receiving element, because displacing that would break the [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md) branch, and because **an unassessed rehearsal of the assessed activity in the week before the occasion converts the occasion into a second attempt.** That last reason is the honest one and it is also the weakest, since a second attempt at a competence is what a rehearsal is. **The objection is conceded and not resolved: the day is judging on twelve minutes of preparation for one of the two encounters, it says so to the students, and the structural fix belongs one quarter and one module upstream.**

**Objection 2: the entrustment statement pre-commits to the same four actions under both branches, which means the measurement it reports cannot change anything.**

At its strongest: [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s rule-6 obligation required this day's entrustment statement to say whether constructed-pressure levels are treated as predictive and **what it does differently if they are not.** The statement answers both, which satisfies the rule's letter. But read the four actions. Both counterparties are real — that was true before the comparison existed and is a property of the module. The assessors are not shown prior levels — a good change, and one the design would want regardless. Today's level is not evidence about EA-1 or EA-3 — which follows from [WP-070](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §8's under-dosing concession and not from the result. The direction is reported to the cohort — a transparency commitment, not a response.

**None of the four is contingent on the finding.** The design has written a two-branch statement in which both branches produce identical conduct, and has presented that as rigour. **Instrumentation rule 4 requires an instrument to state what its result changes.** This instrument's result changes nothing about this day, and rule 6 — which requires the deferred response to name an occasion carrying an element that consumes the result — is satisfied because an element exists and reports the number. **Consuming a number and being changed by it are not the same act, and the design has just discovered that its own rules do not distinguish them.**

The rebuttal has one real part and it is not enough. Deciding a procedure after seeing a result is how a design flatters itself, and the pre-commitment is the correct discipline for avoiding it. **But pre-committing to the same thing under both branches is not pre-commitment; it is a decision that the measurement is not going to matter, taken in advance and written in a form that reads like the opposite.**

**The fix exists, is cheap, and is declined.** A genuinely branch-dependent action is available: **under the overestimate branch, the programme could publish a correction to every entrustment level awarded on a constructed occasion — a stated adjustment in the student's record and in the reliability position the [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) already publishes — and under the other branch it could not.** That is a real difference, it costs a paragraph in a record, and it is the action the finding would warrant. It is declined because amending awarded levels on the strength of an instrument [WP-070](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §8 conceded is under-dosed would be a worse error than the one it corrects, and because a correction applied to eight occasions' levels on two minutes of comparison evidence is exactly the over-reading this programme teaches students to refuse.

**That reason is good and it does not rescue the statement.** If the instrument is too weak to warrant any differential action, then the honest form of the entrustment statement is *this measurement is too weak to change what we do, we are reporting it because you generated it, and the response rule 6 requires does not exist here* — which is a concession, not a branch. **The design has written a branch it cannot honour rather than a concession it can, and this paper is recording that as the second instance in the programme of satisfying a rule's form while defeating its purpose**, after [WP-084](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-084.md) §8's finding that rule-6 branches which substitute materials destroy cross-cohort comparability. **Two instances is not a pattern and it is where the first two of anything begin.**

**Objection 3: this day discharges the build requirement by reading somebody else's homework, which is compliance rather than practice.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn, and on this day it bites from the opposite direction to the rest of the module.

At its strongest: the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) says a day with no build does not exist in this programme, and provides for assessment-bearing days by moving the three records into the defence. **This day takes that provision and it is the programme's largest single use of it** — four builds, none made today, read at a defence about a scope negotiation, through one published question. A sceptical reader can say that the question is answerable from the artefact without the student having done any of it, that a defence with ten published questions will spend perhaps ninety seconds on this one, and that **a standard whose stated purpose is to make the difference between directing a build and accepting one visible has, at the module's assessment occasion, been discharged by a conversation.** Worse, the argument for the exemption — [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §3.1's rule that a student must not construct the evidence being contested — is an argument against building *the scope*, not against building anything, and the design has used it wider than it reaches.

**Two parts of the rebuttal are real.** The published question is not *how did you build this*; it is **what does this measurement not measure**, and that is a question about the scope in front of the counterparties rather than about the tool. A student whose element 5 rests on a usage figure they cannot bound has a defect in their scope, and the counterparties are in the room to hear it. And the records are not evidence of nothing: the [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) states that **a student who cannot account in conversation for an instruction in their own prompt record has produced a record they did not write or did not read**, and that check runs here as it does everywhere else.

**The objection is conceded on the part the rebuttal does not reach.** Ninety seconds is ninety seconds, and the design has no evidence that a defence question is a comparable instrument to a build. **The cheap fix is named and is declined: the day could require a small build — a query returning the four quantities of element 5 with their as-of dates — in the between-day protocol before the occasion**, which would cost the assessed day nothing and would give the defence something made rather than something inherited. **It is declined because the four quantities are element 5 and element 5 is assessed**, so a build producing them would be the student constructing part of the contested evidence the night before, which is [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §3.1's case exactly rather than a wider reading of it. **The decline is sound and the concession stands: the strongest available discharge of the build requirement on this day is a question, and a question is weaker than a build.** It is recorded here, and the module's compensating position is that four builds preceded it in four weeks.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** More than half of students will write a floor at item 2, and fewer than half of those will hold it against B. *Instrument:* item 2 paired with element 7 and with the phase 4 walk-away answer, coded for whether the floor was written, whether it was reached, and whether it moved. **The pairing is the claim.** If floors are written and held at similar rates, the design's central hypothesis at phase 6 point 2 — that writing it before the room is what determines holding it — is unsupported and the element is a formality.

**Prediction 2.** More than a third of students will agree a scope with A that B declines to resource, and **fewer than half of those will tell A before the defence.** *Instrument:* the two encounter records paired, coded for the A-agreed-B-refused condition and for whether A was informed. **The second figure is the day's most important measurement**, and if it is high the occasion's central difficulty is not a difficulty and phase 3's fixed order can be relaxed.

**Prediction 3.** At element 4, more than half of out-of-scope items will name an assignee who has not been told, and the modal excluded item will be one whose assignment would have resolved the resource constraint. *Instrument:* element 4 entries coded for assignee presence and for whether the assignee was informed, cross-tabulated against the B encounter's refusal grounds. **The cross-tabulation is the claim**: if excluded items and refused resources are unrelated, §1's account of why exclusions matter is wrong and element 4 is bookkeeping.

**Prediction 4.** In the phase 5 part-three comparison, students' [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md) absent-party results will not predict their element 2 quality at a two-year gap. *Instrument:* the S039 phase 7 outcome against element 2's completeness, coded by the assessors in the sealed field. **This is the prediction the design expects to lose and reports anyway**, because [WP-039](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-039.md) §6 asserted that day was this one's rehearsal, and a rehearsal that does not predict is a claim about the retention schedule that has failed rather than a claim about the students.

**Prediction 5.** At item 5, **more than three-quarters will predict that the agent answers the quota question completely, and fewer than a fifth will state that it cannot reach whether the grantor will agree.** Across the module, **the second rate will not rise from S081 to S085.** *Instrument:* item 5 coded on two axes at each of the module's five days, reported as a five-point series per cohort. **The second clause is the claim and it is the module's only test of whether frontier calibration is learnable within a module rather than merely measurable.** A rising series means the cohort is acquiring a boundary; a flat one means the design is taking five readings of a fixed disposition and reporting them as a trajectory, which is the failure instrumentation rule 9 exists to name. **The series is one cohort, one substrate, five staff-chosen tasks, and it is reported with that sampling attached** per instrumentation rule 3; it describes these five days and not the agent.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That you are the only party who can see both constraints, and that this is a position rather than an advantage: you can produce an agreement by telling each side something true and partial, and nobody but you will ever know you did. A floor you did not write down moves under pressure without your noticing, and an exclusion with no assignee commits nobody.

**What the evidence licenses.** Principled negotiation, the interest behind a stated position and the discipline of a declared walk-away are **class 1**, grounded in [research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md). The quota material is **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026: a published order-of-receipt rule, priority to customers already using their allocation, and an instrument its own vendor states still reports quota for retired models — with **no grant rate and no distribution of decision times published anywhere.** **The seven-element form, the exclusion-as-assignment rule and the floor are class 3.**

**What to do with it.**

- Write the floor before the room. It is the only form of a negotiating position anybody can check afterwards, including you.
- Assign every exclusion to a named party and say whether they have been told.
- Do not warrant a ceiling the platform does not enforce. A budget alerts and does not cap.
- Ask of your own four build records what the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md) asks: **a control is not evidence.** A grantor's published criterion tells you how to shape a request and nothing about what they will decide.

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper accepts in full that this occasion judges a competence the programme taught four quarters earlier and rehearsed on the morning of the judgement. The larger repair was declined for a reason the paper calls its own weakest, the structural fix belongs a module and a quarter upstream, and the day says so to the cohort out loud. **If the refusal is where you first meet this conversation, you are entitled to say that too.**
