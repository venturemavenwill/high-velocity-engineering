## In plain terms

The most consequential sentence in any governance regime is the one deciding whether the regime applies, and it is the sentence nobody reads.

The regime this module draws on was found in two generations and **the change between them is the single best governance idea in the collection.**

**The older test asked whether the team had built or fine-tuned a model.** That is an effort test, and it was reasonable when the deliverable was a model.

**The newer test asks whether an artefact capable of AI behaviour crossed the boundary into the customer's control**, such that they can execute it, test it or operationalise it. That is a surface test, and it closes a hole the effort test cannot.

The hole is not hypothetical. **When the deliverable is a configured agent rather than a trained model, an effort test lets the highest-velocity work in modern delivery escape governance entirely** — no model work was done, so nothing applies, and the customer is now running something that acts. The surface test also survives the *next* change in how systems are assembled, because it asks about a boundary rather than about a technique, and boundaries persist while techniques do not.

**Then the same regime draws its line at authored artefacts and excludes configuration using existing controls.** That is defensible on ownership grounds and indefensible on harm grounds, and both halves are true at once: a carelessly configured off-the-shelf agent in a hiring workflow can do exactly the damage the regime exists to prevent, and it is out of scope.

**The platform anchor exists because the vendor's own product taxonomy contains the axis the test needs and does not draw the conclusion.** It separates an agent that is a configuration from an agent that is your code on compute you pay for; and, more consequentially, it separates an agent that relies on user-initiated interaction from one that can trigger actions automatically without direct user input. **The second is the governance fork**, because a reactive system fails in front of the person who asked and a proactive one fails in an empty room. The documentation records the capability difference accurately and says nothing about scope. That conclusion is the session's, and the session says so.

The session's assessed work is that the learner writes their own test and then **breaks it twice** — once on harm, once on cost. A test whose author has found no fault in it has not been read by its author.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching a governance scope test as a surface question about artefact transfer rather than an effort question about model work; defends recording each deliverable's **initiation model** as a first-class field of that test; and defends requiring learners to identify both a case their own test wrongly excludes and a case it wrongly includes.

The argument runs in four steps. **First**, an effort test lets agentic and low-code delivery escape governance without any rule being broken, because effort and risk stopped being the same variable once systems became assemblable from other people's components. **Second**, a boundary-based test survives changes in assembly technique because boundaries outlast techniques, and it becomes usable rather than merely correct when the boundary is located in credentials instead of in an organisational description. **Third**, the property that most changes what governance must cover — whether the system can act without being asked — is a configuration choice rather than an engineering one, is documented by the vendor as a capability difference with no governance conclusion attached, and is invisible to any test that asks how much work was done. **Fourth**, the two defences learners reach for instead — that the model is aligned, and that a human is in the loop — both fail on inspection: alignment is trained behaviour defined relative to somebody else's labelling pool and evaluated on settings that are not agentic operation, and the human in the loop of a proactive system is frequently supplying a credential rather than a judgement.

The session installs a scope test in artefact-transfer form, a boundary located in credentials, a full classification of deliverables carrying orchestrator ownership and initiation model, an explicit record of which artefacts sit one setting away from a different governance class, two self-identified failures of the test, an open-deviations list, and a plan for the wrongly excluded case that does not depend on the regime applying.

The paper concedes that artefact transfer is a diffuse and continuous trigger where model training was an observable one; that requiring a private supplement to a public rule carries its own hazards; that the claim about proactive systems being a distinct governance object is the design's own argument and is untested; that anchoring the classification to one vendor's taxonomy imports that taxonomy's boundaries; and that no source establishes that any scope determination has ever prevented anything.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Governance applies or it does not, and the decision is made once, early, by whoever fills in a form. **Everything downstream — assessment, review, approval, records — depends on a determination nobody revisits.**

An effort test makes that determination on the wrong variable. It asks how much model work was done, which was a reasonable proxy for risk when producing a model was the expensive and consequential act. **It is now a proxy for nothing.** The most consequential deliverable a team can hand over is frequently the one that took the least model work: a configured agent with tool access, an instruction set, an evaluation harness, a prompt file. Under an effort test all of that is out of scope, **and no rule has been broken.**

The second problem is that the corrected test has a boundary in it, and boundaries are easy to describe and hard to locate. **A team that can describe its boundary organisationally and not in credentials cannot apply its own test**, because the question *has this crossed?* becomes a matter of interpretation at exactly the moment interpretation favours the answer that avoids work.

The third is that scope tests fail in two directions and only one of them is ever discussed. **A regime that catches everything is routed around**, and an unenforced rule protects nobody while consuming the goodwill a narrower one would have had.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session requires the trust boundaries from [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) and the necessity record from [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md), and it retrieves [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md), because extending what already runs means inheriting somebody else's boundary — which is where most real scope ambiguity comes from.

**The prior it works against is that scope is somebody else's decision.** It is made by whoever fills in the form, and in most engagements that is the engineer.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Low intrinsic load, high classificatory load. The test is a paragraph; applying it honestly to a real deliverable list is where the session's time goes.

**3.2 The opening items are a Productive Failure generation phase, and item 2 is the instrument.**

Learners are asked to apply an effort test to a configured agent handed over with no model code written, before being told what is wrong with effort tests. **The answer is *out of scope*, they produce it themselves, and the consequence lands without argument.** That is the failed generation effect — attempting an answer before instruction improves later learning even when the attempt is wrong — and the binding condition is that **the generation phase must not be scaffolded**, because guidance that reduces the failure rate reduces the learning ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Hence the release rule.

The same source's second condition is why exercise 3 exists: **consolidation must explicitly prompt comparison**, because presenting a correct answer beside a learner's incorrect one achieves nothing unless they are instructed to compare them.

**Limits.** School, university and laboratory evidence; no adult-professional study in the reference list; a trade book in which anecdote and evidence sit in adjacent paragraphs; and the author's own model contains an **Unproductive Failure** quadrant — low performance and low learning — which is what a generation phase produces if nothing assembles the attempts afterwards.

**3.3 Why a boundary outlasts a technique.** A test keyed to how a system was made must be rewritten every time systems are made differently, and the rewrite always lags the practice. **A test keyed to a transfer of control does not care how the artefact was produced.** That is the durability argument and it is structural rather than empirical.

**3.4 Why the boundary must be located in credentials.** *Can execute, test or operationalise* is a capability claim, and capability is held by whoever has the credentials and the ability to change the thing. **An organisational description of a boundary cannot answer the question and a credential map can**, which converts an interpretive judgement into a lookup.

**3.5 Why the initiation model is a recorded field rather than a remark.**

The vendor's own agent taxonomy separates two forks that learners conflate, and only one of them is a governance fork.

The first is hosting. **Prompt agents** use declarative configuration, are managed by the platform with **no agent compute**, and suit agents that include no custom code. **Hosted agents** run **your containerised code** on managed container compute ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). That fork tells you who is paying for and operating compute. It is a real distinction and it is not, on its own, a governance one.

The second is the governance fork. **Declarative agents** are designed for individuals, are limited to the host's orchestrator and models, and **"rely on user-initiated interactions"** — they do not support proactive interaction. **Custom engine agents** are fully customisable including model and orchestration, support group collaboration, are available inside and outside the productivity suite, and **"enable agents to trigger actions automatically, even without direct user input."**

**The design's claim, which is its own and is stated as such, is that this axis changes the failure model rather than the feature list.** A reactive system fails in front of a person who asked for something; the human in the loop is a property of the interaction rather than a control anybody designed. **A proactive system fails in an empty room**, on a schedule or a trigger, with the first reader potentially being the person affected. Every control this module has installed so far assumes a reader, and the proactive option removes the reader **without announcing that it has** — which is why the session records it as a field and additionally records whether the artefact is one setting away from acquiring it.

A third axis matters and is easy to miss: **owning the orchestrator is the fundamental fork**, determining model choice, extensibility and lifecycle tooling together. A team that took the host's orchestrator handed over a configuration; a team that brought their own handed over a system.

**The documentation's limits.** First-party and single-vendor; **read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches, with several comparison tables truncated**, so feature lists are not exhaustive and the page structure is reconstructed. **Highly perishable** — product names and feature boundaries are actively changing, and a limitation recorded today may be closed tomorrow. **No adoption, cost, failure-rate or outcome data of any kind**, and it compares the vendor's options only against each other. It also draws no governance conclusion from the initiation difference; that conclusion is this design's.

**3.6 Why the alignment defence does not close the question.**

The commonest reason a team gives for placing an artefact out of scope is that the model underneath it is aligned. That is true and much narrower than it sounds.

Instruction following is a **post-training property**, not an emergent consequence of scale; the paper establishing the standard method opens by stating that making models bigger does not inherently make them better at following user intent, and names its target failure modes as untruthful, toxic, or simply not helpful ([research/02-technical-foundations/ouyang-instructgpt.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/ouyang-instructgpt.md)). Its pipeline has three stages — demonstrations, a reward model trained on human comparisons, and optimisation against that learned proxy — **each of which can fail independently.**

Two of its own recorded limits are the ones a scope test needs. **The result is defined relative to a particular labeller pool, a particular set of labelling instructions and a particular prompt distribution**, none of which is universal and all of which the paper documents. And **human preference is not correctness**: optimising for preference can produce confident, agreeable, wrong answers. The authors state the models still make simple mistakes.

The successor method removes the reinforcement-learning stage by reparameterising the reward so the objective is reachable with a classification loss on preference pairs ([research/02-technical-foundations/rafailov-dpo.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/rafailov-dpo.md)). Its limits carry the sentence that matters here: **it inherits every weakness of the preference data**, including annotator bias, sycophancy incentives and the preference-versus-correctness gap; and **its reported results cover controlled sentiment generation, summarisation and single-turn dialogue — multi-turn and agentic settings are outside the evaluated scope.**

**The conclusion the session draws is deliberately modest. None of this says a deployed model is unsafe. It says the alignment defence is not available as a reason to put an artefact out of scope**, because the behaviour being relied on was trained against somebody else's preferences and evaluated on settings that are not the deployment.

**3.7 Why capability closes the remaining escape route.** The planning literature holds that auto-regressive models **cannot by themselves plan**, and cannot self-verify **"because self-verification is after all a form of reasoning"** ([research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md)). **A system that cannot reliably plan, cannot check its own work, and can act without being asked is a combination worth naming**, and no scope test returning *out of scope* for it is doing its job. A position paper; its claims are arguments and have been contested; scoped to auto-regressive models as of early 2024. **The architectural discipline transfers; the empirical claim is not settled.**

**3.8 Why one specimen carries most of the teaching weight.**

The most useful worked example available to this programme is a multi-agent pipeline built inside a customer-embedded engineering organisation to produce security plans. It is well designed — a folder used as a blackboard with named artefacts and explicit refusals, early conversion of prose into stable identifiers so that later stages become joins, deliberately lossy indexes imposing an information diet on subagents ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md)).

Two of its findings are why the session is shaped the way it is. **Every input stage reads untrusted repository content, feeds it to a model that writes files, and the chain terminates in an authenticated write to an external tracking system**, with nothing anywhere addressing content in the analysed repository attempting to influence the analysis. And **there is no approval gate anywhere**: human decisions exist as inputs, there are placeholders shaped like sign-off with blanks for a reviewer and a date, and **they are a form to fill in rather than a precondition for anything.** The one unavoidable human authenticates the write, **and is being asked for a credential rather than for a judgement.**

**That is what an out-of-scope determination looks like from the inside, produced by people who plainly knew what they were doing.** Nothing constructed for a classroom would be this good or fail this instructively.

**Limits.** Internal and access-controlled; paraphrased and never quoted; **it cannot supply an external warrant for any claim.** No outcome evidence exists for any of it, and the scar tissue in its prompts implies observed failures that are nowhere recorded, so **the strongest evidence that the design works cannot be cited.** The source contains operational attack detail that is not reproduced here and should not be. Every concrete integration in it is perishable.

**3.9 Why the test must be broken twice.** Over-inclusion and under-inclusion have different costs and different remedies, and a learner who checks only one direction produces a test that fails silently in the other. **The wrongly excluded case is a harm argument; the wrongly included case is a compliance-economics argument**, and this programme has already established, at [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md), that the same instinct can be right in one direction and disastrous in the other.

**3.10 Why the excluded case gets a plan anyway.** A harm you have identified does not stop being a harm because your rule missed it. **The professional position is that the rule bounds the organisation's obligation and not the engineer's knowledge**, which is an argument rather than a finding, and §8 gives it its strongest objection.

**3.11 Why the non-conformance state is carried into the artefact.** The regime's clearest maturity signal is that it added a documented, approvable way to say *we cannot comply yet*. **A compliance regime with no legitimate route for that gets quietly ignored** — people do not stop shipping, they stop recording. The scope-test artefact therefore carries an open-deviations list, and the design states explicitly that **an empty list is not evidence of compliance.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The movements run situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**4.1 Opening item 2 is the argument in a single question.** *You configured an agent, wrote no model code, and handed it over — in scope under an effort test?* The answer is no and every learner sees the consequence immediately. Nothing else in the session needs to persuade them, which is why the release rule gates the sections that would supply the answer early.

**4.2 The workflow forbids softened verbs.** *Provided*, *shared* and *made available* describe things a customer cannot necessarily run, and a test using them is a test about documents. **The verb carries the whole determination**, which is why it is named as an object of attention rather than left to drafting.

**4.3 The classification is over three axes rather than one, and the third is the point.** Orchestrator ownership, whether the artefact runs the team's code on compute somebody pays for, and **whether it can act without being asked.** The first two are answerable from the vendor's taxonomy in a minute each. The third is answerable in the same minute and is the one that changes what the regime has to cover.

**4.4 The one-setting-away field is separate on purpose.** A learner who records only the current state produces a classification that is true today and silently wrong after a configuration change. **Recording the distance to the other governance class converts a snapshot into something with a trigger attached**, which is exactly what [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md) then instruments. The design expects this field to be the most frequently under-reported one, and §9's prediction 4 measures it.

**4.5 The classification starts with the awkward items on purpose.** A learner who classifies the obvious deliverables first arrives at the awkward ones with a pattern already established and applies it. Starting there makes the test do work.

**4.6 The open-deviations list is required and its emptiness is not accepted as compliance.** The regime's own maturity signal was adding a documented, approvable non-conformance state, and the design carries that forward with one addition: the artefact says in its own comments that an empty list is not evidence. **A regime with no route for *we cannot comply yet* does not produce compliance; it produces silence**, and silence looks identical to compliance in every report.

**4.7 The agent attacks the axis the learner is worst at.** The prompt asks it to enumerate deliverables that could be made to act unprompted by changing a setting rather than by writing code, and to say which of those the learner's test lets through. **This is a search problem over a configuration surface, which is a task the tooling is genuinely suited to** — and the session reserves the governance judgement for the learner, which is the same division of labour [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) established.

**4.8 The review is independent classification rather than critique.** Two people applying the same paragraph to the same list, disagreeing, is the only cheap evidence available about the wording — **and the wording is the only part of a scope test that is ever used.** The closing question is deliberately not a governance question: *which of these would you be uncomfortable being responsible for?* elicits a set built from intuition rather than from rules, and the gap between that set and the in-scope set is the session's most useful diagnostic.

**4.9 The platform is used additively rather than critically.** The learner records the taxonomy and adds a governance reading the documentation does not contain. **The session says explicitly that the documentation is accurate and is answering a different question**, because the lesson is about the boundary of a product comparison, and that lesson is clearer when the comparison in front of the learner is a good one.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The effort test in disguise.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* the standard's diagnostic — your test's answer depends on how much work you did.

**The unlocatable boundary.** *Countermeasure:* the credential requirement, and the warning sign — you cannot say who can change the artefact next month.

**The excluded configuration.** *Countermeasure:* the worked example of the regime's own authored-artefact line, given in the material so the learner sees a competent organisation make the choice before looking for their own.

**The initiation model unrecorded.** *Countermeasure:* the field in the artefact and the standard's warning sign — your classification would read identically for a chatbot and for a scheduled trigger. **§9's prediction 3 predicts this countermeasure partially failing.**

**The alignment defence.** The learner puts an artefact out of scope because the model underneath is aligned. *Countermeasure:* exercise 5, which requires the learner to state whose preferences it encodes and on what settings that alignment was evaluated. **A learner who cannot answer has not been given a reason to distrust the model; they have been shown that the defence was never available.**

**The faultless test.** *Countermeasure:* both break-it elements are named artefacts rather than reflections, so their absence is visible.

**The everything test.** *Countermeasure:* the pricing requirement on the wrongly included case. **This is the design's weakest countermeasure**, because the cost of over-inclusion falls on people the learner will never meet.

**The orphaned exclusion.** *Countermeasure:* the final element, and the standard's warning sign — your answer is that it is out of scope.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. Four constituents are assessed at [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md): that the test asks about a boundary crossing rather than about effort; that the boundary is described in credentials and control rather than organisationally; that every classified deliverable carries an initiation model; and that both a wrongly excluded and a wrongly included case are named, with the excluded one carrying a plan that does not depend on the regime applying.

**Whether the test is correct is not assessed**, because no standard for correctness is available to this programme. **Nor is the classification assessed for accuracy** — only for completeness and for the presence of the awkward items, since accuracy would require the programme to hold a view about products it deliberately treats as perishable.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: artefact transfer is a diffuse and continuous trigger, and a test built on it is unenforceable.**

At its strongest: model training was an observable event with a log entry. Artefacts cross boundaries constantly and informally — a screenshot in a chat, a prompt pasted into a message, a notebook shared for a demonstration, a configuration exported so somebody could reproduce a result. **A test that catches all of those is unworkable; a test that draws a line somewhere in the middle is arbitrary, and arbitrariness is exactly what the effort test was criticised for.** The regime has traded an enforceable bad test for an unenforceable better one.

**Conceded in part, and the rebuttal is narrower than the objection.** The verb does more work than the objection allows: *can execute, test or operationalise* is a capability claim, not a receipt claim, and a screenshot conveys no capability. The credential requirement then makes the remaining determination a lookup rather than a judgement — whoever can run it and change it holds it. **The declined fix is to enumerate the artefact types in scope.** It is declined for the reason the session exists: **an enumeration ages exactly as fast as the delivery model changes**, which is the failure the effort test already demonstrated, and replacing one obsolescent list with another obsolescent list is not progress. **The residual enforceability gap is real, and the design's only answer is that a diffuse test applied honestly beats a crisp test that misses the consequential case.**

**Objection 2: requiring a plan for the wrongly excluded case teaches that governance is optional and that private engineering judgement supersedes an organisational rule.**

At its strongest: an organisation cannot function if every engineer maintains a personal supplement to its compliance regime. The supplementary work is unbudgeted, unreviewed and unaccountable, and a harm handled privately by a well-meaning engineer is handled by somebody with no mandate, no specialist knowledge and no oversight. **That is worse than a known gap in a public rule, because the gap can at least be argued about.**

**Conceded in part, and the design's answer turns on where the plan is recorded.** The element does not ask for a private supplement. **It asks for the excluded case and its handling to be written into the same assessment**, where a reviewer sees them, where the organisation can adopt or refuse them, and where they become the evidence a regime needs in order to widen its own test. That is the opposite of private. **The declined fix is to require escalation of the gap instead of handling it.** It is declined because escalation with no proposed handling is a complaint, and because the regime this module draws on has no route for receiving one: its non-conformance state covers an inability to comply, not an inadequacy of the test itself. **A learner who escalates into that regime has filed nothing, and the harm remains unhandled while the paperwork looks correct.**

**Objection 3: the claim that a proactive system is a distinct governance object is asserted, and the vendor documentation this session anchors to explicitly does not draw that conclusion.**

At its strongest: the documentation records an initiation difference as a capability difference, alongside orchestration control, model choice, collaboration scope and hosting location. **The session promotes one of those five axes to governance significance on no authority but its own**, then teaches learners to record it as a first-class field, then predicts they will under-report it — which is the shape of a design that has decided its answer and is measuring compliance with it. A team that follows this will spend attention on the initiation model and less on orchestration ownership or model choice, and nothing establishes that the allocation is right.

**Conceded on the warrant and defended on the reasoning, which is the honest split.** The claim is class 3 — design reasoning with no external warrant — and the paper labels it as such in its evidence status rather than dressing it as a documented fact. The reasoning is nonetheless checkable and short: every control this module installs, from the review record to the impact assessment to the sign-off, presumes somebody reads an output; a system that acts on a trigger produces outputs when nobody is reading; therefore the module's controls degrade for that class in a way they do not for the others. **The other four axes change what a system can do. This one changes whether the module's own instruments work.** **The declined fix is to record all five axes with equal weight and let the learner decide.** It is declined because a scope test that treats every distinction as equally salient is a checklist, and checklists get completed rather than read — but the cost is real: **a learner drilled on one axis will under-attend to the others**, and §9's prediction 6 is written to detect that rather than to confirm the design.

**Objection 4: anchoring the classification to one vendor's taxonomy teaches a product catalogue and imports that catalogue's blind spots.**

At its strongest: the axes learners are taught to classify on are the axes one supplier chose to compare its own products along, in documentation that compares them against nothing else, that was read through search excerpts with tables truncated, and that is changing fast enough that a limitation recorded this term may be closed next. **A learner who has internalised that taxonomy will classify a system from a different supplier by looking for the nearest match rather than by asking what the system does**, which is the exact failure the session claims to prevent.

**Conceded, and partly mitigated by construction.** The workflow requires the axes to be written down and dated before any product is named, and the session's perishable table pairs every durable claim with the version-bound instance that illustrates it — which is the repository's standing method for exactly this hazard. The deeper mitigation is that the axes chosen are ones with non-vendor meanings: who decides what happens next, who pays for the compute, and whether the thing can act unasked are questions about any system. **The declined fix is to teach the axes with no product attached.** It is declined because a learner who has never applied an axis to a real configuration surface cannot tell an axis that matters from one that reads well, and because the documentation's honesty about its own products' limits is itself part of the teaching. **The cost is that a cohort taught this way will be fluent in one supplier's vocabulary**, and the design's only answer is to name the risk here and to require the dated axis list as the assessed form.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first tests will be effort tests in new wording. *Instrument:* the submitted test, with a second reader determining whether its answer varies with the amount of work done.

**Prediction 2.** Independent classification will disagree on at least one deliverable for most learners. *Instrument:* the paired classifications from the review exercise, compared item by item.

**Prediction 3.** A majority of classifications will record the initiation model for the obvious agent deliverables and omit it for the non-agent ones — the harness, the prompt file, the exported configuration. *Instrument:* per-deliverable field completeness, stratified by deliverable type. **This predicts the partial failure of §5's initiation-model countermeasure**, and the omission matters because a scheduled trigger is usually attached to the unglamorous artefact rather than to the one everybody is watching.

**Prediction 4.** The one-setting-away field will be the least completed field in the artefact, and where completed will under-count. *Instrument:* completeness per field across the cohort, plus a second reader independently enumerating the settings for a sample and comparing counts.

**Prediction 5.** Wrongly included cases will be absent more often than wrongly excluded cases. *Instrument:* the presence of each named case, coded per learner. **If this fails, the over-inclusion cost is more salient to learners than the design expects and §3.9's symmetry argument needs revisiting.**

**Prediction 6.** Learners will classify orchestrator ownership less accurately after this session than a cohort taught the five axes with equal weight. *Instrument:* a common classification task on a system none of the cohort built, marked for accuracy per axis, across two cohorts taught differently — or, absent two cohorts, per-axis accuracy compared against the axis the session emphasised. **This is the instrument attached to §8's third objection**, and a confirmation means the design bought its emphasis at a measurable cost.

**Prediction 7.** The set of deliverables a reviewer would be uncomfortable owning will differ from the learner's in-scope set. *Instrument:* the two sets compared per learner, with the difference recorded in both directions.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a scope test keyed to a boundary crossing outlasts one keyed to a technique; that the boundary has to be locatable in credentials to be usable; that whether a system can act without being asked is the property that most changes what governance must cover; and that a test its author cannot break has not been read by its author.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about harm prevented. **No source available reports a case in which a scope determination stopped anything**, and the regime this is drawn from publishes no evidence that any assessment it triggered ever changed a system. The comparison between its two generations is evidence about coverage, obtained by reading both, and is not a measurement of consequence. **The proactive-systems claim is this design's own argument and §8's third objection concedes it has no external warrant.** The alignment material licenses only the narrow conclusion that the alignment defence is unavailable — it says nothing about whether any particular model is safe, and this paper asserts nothing of the kind.

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper.** That is a rule of this repository rather than an accident of what was read.

**The concession worth remembering.** The best governance idea in the material has a hole its own authors chose, on ownership grounds, knowing it. Your test will have one too, and finding it is the only part of this you control.

Recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md), from a complete reading of **access-controlled material that no public reader can check. No figure from it is reproduced anywhere in this repository.**

**The silences that matter.** **The documentation draws no governance conclusion from the initiation difference.** It records proactive triggering as a capability alongside model choice and hosting location, and says nothing about what changes in oversight when a system can act unasked — which is the whole of this session's argument and is therefore the session's own, not the vendor's. **It publishes no adoption, cost, failure-rate or outcome data for any build path**, so nothing here rests on any claim that one option produces better results than another. **It compares the vendor's options only against each other**, and states no scale envelope for most of the tools it compares. **And it says nothing at all about what happens to a governance classification when a setting changes** — there is no documented notion of an artefact moving between classes, which is precisely the gap the one-setting-away field exists to record and which [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md) exists to instrument.
