# WP-057 — Dense Retrieval and the Geometry of Similarity

**Seminar day:** [S057](wiki/seminars/S057.md) · **Module:** [M12](wiki/modules/M12.md) · **Quarter:** [Q6](wiki/quarters/Q6.md)
**Strands:** IS · ME · **Complexity class:** 4
**Platform anchor:** Azure AI Search vector fields, vector profiles and integrated vectorization · Foundry embedding deployments · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (Search) · Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching dense retrieval as a criterion-construction act rather than as an optimisation, on the ground that the positives are given and the negatives are the decision that nobody in the room has yet noticed they are making. It defends holding architecture, corpus and positives fixed across four negative constructions so that every observed difference is attributable to the one variable, and defends making the randomly-negatived model the payload because it wins the evaluation it was implicitly built for. It defends the day's single hard requirement — that a retriever be evaluated a second time against negatives drawn differently — as the operational form of S041's fourth leak class in the case where nothing was copied. **It defends adding the chunking trap to a day that was already full, on the ground that the day's entire argument is about what a vector represents and the students had never been told that a vector does not represent a document.** The paper reports discharge of the obligations S051 and S056 placed on this day. It records **the third instance of the pattern opened at WP-049 §8**, that this design stages what it expects students to resist and asserts what it finds obvious, and argues for its elevation. It concedes that the second evaluation the day mandates has no adjudicator, and concedes that the day now teaches a decision it does not assess a measurement of.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Students can already state that an embedding's notion of similarity was chosen. [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md) spent a day on it and they hold it. The problem is that holding it as a fact about other people's models does not produce the behaviour that matters, which is noticing the moment you are making the choice yourself.

Training a retriever presents as an engineering task with a loss curve. Every visible affordance — the harness, the metric, the checkpoint — invites the student to treat it as optimisation. **The decision that determines what the model learns is made before any of that, in a step that has no metric attached and usually no deliberation: what counts as a negative.** Positives are easy and rarely disputed. Negatives are constructed, and the construction is the model's decision boundary.

The second and harder problem is the evaluation. Once a student has chosen a negative construction, the natural evaluation set is built the same way, because the same intuition about what a distractor looks like produces both. Nothing is copied. No document appears in both sets. Every leak-audit check from [S041](wiki/seminars/S041.md) passes. **The evaluation nonetheless measures the training objective**, because what training and test share is the construction, and construction is precisely the thing an evaluation exists to vary. This is the hardest idea in [M12](wiki/modules/M12.md) and it is the reason so many retrievers improve on a held-out set and change nothing a user experiences.

The third problem is one of ownership rather than of content. Two modules have now taught students to interrogate somebody else's similarity definition. This day hands them the pen, and the design's claim is that the interrogation habit does not transfer automatically to one's own construction — that a student fluent at asking *similar to what* about a pretrained encoder will train one without asking it. Phase 2's bridge is built to make the transfer explicit rather than hoping for it.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**High, and the module's second peak after [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md).** The day's mechanical content is thin — one lookup the cohort met five days ago — and its load is entirely in phase 5, where a student must define a training set, define a probe set, train, and then evaluate twice under two different constructions.

The interactivity is between steps 1, 3 and 6. The relevance definition determines what a positive is; the negative construction determines what the model can separate; and step 6's second evaluation is interpretable only against both. A student who amends their definition after seeing step 5's results has produced an artefact in which nothing can be attributed.

Management:

- **The architecture, harness, corpus, positives and all four phase 3 configurations are supplied**, loaded as four vector fields on one index so that groups query rather than build.
- **The relevance definition is not rewritten** — it is [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md)'s, and an amendment must carry a reason.
- **Step 3 is worked with the room**, since negative construction is the new decision.
- **Training runs are small and pre-budgeted**, and no student waits on a long job inside the phase.
- **The skillset and index schema at step 5 are produced by the agent and read by the student**, which is the only reason a chunking and embedding pipeline fits inside the phase at all.
- **Two probe dimensions are supplied as required**, so step 2 begins from something rather than from nothing.

**Step 4's prediction is unsupported.**

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Being asked for negatives immediately after positives, and finding you have no source,* item 2.
- *Predicting a four-way ordering before any results,* phase 3.
- *A staged reveal in which the first evaluation is the misleading one,* phase 3.
- *Probing on respects you were told you could not predict,* step 2.
- *Stating how each of your two pair sets was drawn,* step 3.
- *Evaluating twice and reporting both,* step 6.
- *Having a partner erase your improvement by changing the negatives,* phase 7.
- *Predicting what an agent will get wrong in a pipeline you have never configured,* item 5.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *The loss functions and training dynamics.* IS203's. The day is indifferent to which contrastive objective is used and says so.
- *Multi-vector and late-interaction retrieval.* Named at point 6 and excluded; building one requires the index material that arrives tomorrow.
- *Reranking implementation.* Named at point 7, developed at [S058](wiki/seminars/S058.md) and [S061](wiki/seminars/S061.md).
- *Distillation from a stronger scorer.* Named in the scope statement as outside the day's account.
- *Chunking strategy comparison.* **Named at point 8 and deliberately not run as a fifth contrasting case.** The day already varies one thing; varying two would destroy the attribution phase 3 depends on. §8 objection 3 argues this is the wrong call.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** Points 3 and 4 characterise retriever training by its negative construction. That is **safe** as a standing obligation for any retriever a student trains or fine-tunes under a supervised contrastive objective, which is the substrate's case and the case a graduate will meet first: name your negatives, and evaluate against negatives drawn differently. It is **unsafe** as a general theory of retrieval training. It omits objectives with no explicit negatives, distillation from a cross-encoder, and unsupervised pretraining — all in current use, none of which this day equips a student to reason about. A student who carries the short form outward will read the majority of published training descriptions as underspecified when in fact they are describing something else, and will make a confident wrong criticism in a review. The boundary is stated at point 10 and in the day's perishability register.

**The platform layer carries its own scope statement and it is unusually consequential, because the platform layer here is not an illustration.** Point 8 states that the thing embedded was a chunk rather than a document, that chunk boundaries decide what a single vector must represent, that the splitter's length unit and the embedding model's limit unit do not align, and that document-level fields including permission metadata reach chunks only if projected. Those are **safe** as an account of what happens in this pipeline on this service at this version, and as a standing obligation anywhere: **before you interpret a retrieval number, find out what unit was embedded.** They are **unsafe** in two directions that will catch somebody. They are unsafe as a description of any other chunking implementation — the parameter names, the default mode and the recommended sizes are one vendor's, and a different product will fragment differently for the same nominal setting. And they are **unsafe as guidance on what a chunk size should be**: the documentation supplies a starting point and states that the optimum depends on content shape, document density and query type, which means **the only defensible chunk size is a measured one and this day does not require a student to measure it.** That gap is §8 objection 3's subject.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **item 2**.

Item 1 asks where the positives come from, and the room answers instantly: clicks, links, question-answer pairs, annotations. Item 2 asks where the negatives come from and the room stops. Most students write *the rest of the corpus* or write nothing. The silence is the finding, and it arrives four minutes into the day with no framework attached: **there is a decision here that they were about to make without noticing.**

The second mechanism is **D1** at phase 3. It wins, clearly, on the evaluation the students are given first, and the groups who predicted it feel correct for about eight minutes. Then two further evaluations arrive and D1 is last on both. There is no error to find — D1's training was correct, its evaluation was correct, and the two were built by the same intuition.

Phase 6 supplies the affective content and is deliberately decision-free: the evaluation set predated the training pipeline, so the shared construction arose from ordinary sequencing and nobody chose it. The detail that carries the phase is that the team would have had to notice a relationship between two artefacts built months apart by different people.

The competence risk is at step 6. A student whose two evaluation numbers differ enormously may read that as evidence they trained badly. The instructor states before phase 5 that a large gap is the expected result, that it is the day's most important measurement, and that its size is a property of the evaluation design rather than of the model.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items built around the positive/negative asymmetry. **Item 4 imports the cohort's [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md) blind spots** and is the first half of this day's rule-6 discharge. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction** and is administered here per instrumentation rule 8.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals and a bridge whose last clause transfers ownership of the similarity question to the student.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four negative constructions, everything else fixed, a staged three-part reveal, **all of it executed against a live index.** **The third evaluation's query class is selected from [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md)'s step 4 data**, discharging the second obligation. **D4 is conditional**: where the tenant cannot provision a managed-compute endpoint for the catalogue vectorizer, it is demonstrated rather than operated and no specification element depends on it, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Points 3 and 4 are the retained pair; point 4 is the module's hardest idea. Point 6 is asserted rather than demonstrated, which §8 examines. **Point 8 is the chunking trap and is IS203's named anchor; point 9 names the defaults.**

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps, **producing a shipped vector index rather than a report.** **Step 2 is the artefact element [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md) named.** Step 5 makes the chunking configuration an assessed decision. Step 6 is the hard requirement; step 7 is the discriminator. The three Copilot Practice Standard elements attach here.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A quarter of no movement, an evaluation set that predated the pipeline, and no decision anywhere — then an agent-generated pipeline with zero overlap, checked with three short-answer queries.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Dual defeat: erase the improvement, then justify the scheme against the substrate. A validated remedy, having run at S029, S047, S048 and S053.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 2's sources tallied including the blanks; item 3 against the three evaluations; **item 4 and step 5 against the S051 unpredicted respects**; **item 5's sealed predictions against what each agent produced**; the cohort's step 6 gaps as a distribution.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has trained retrievers will name hard-negative mining at item 2 and will predict phase 3 correctly. Predicted failure: they treat step 6 as a formality because they already mine hard negatives, and produce a second evaluation whose negatives are drawn the same way as the first with cosmetic differences. Countermeasure: phase 7's partner is required to erase their improvement, and a second evaluation built to be survivable is trivially erased — the lesson delivered by a peer rather than by an assessor. §8 objection 2 argues this countermeasure does not close the gap.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is step 3 and, behind it, the fear that a bad negative choice ruins the day's work. Countermeasures: step 3 is worked with the room; all four phase 3 constructions are supplied as worked examples immediately beforehand; training runs are small and re-runnable; and the instructor states that a model trained on weak negatives is a usable artefact for this day because step 6 measures the difference rather than the quality.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Characterising D4 is straightforward and D3's click-derived behaviour is familiar from [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md)'s E3. The difficulty is explaining why D1 wins the first evaluation, which most groups can describe and few can attribute. Fallback if a group attributes it quickly: ask **what evaluation would make D3 look best, and whether any real user population would produce it** — a question about the query distribution rather than about training, with no supplied answer.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **evaluative paralysis** — if every evaluation shares a construction with something, and any improvement can be erased by re-drawing negatives, a student may conclude that no retrieval claim is measurable. This is a sharper risk here than at [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md) because the day's own hard requirement supplies the erasing move.

Countermeasures: D2 is a construction that survives all three evaluations and the day says so plainly, which establishes that the problem has a solution; step 7 asks for a hybrid decision, which is a constructive output; phase 7's second clause requires the erasing scheme to be realistic, so the move is not free; and the instructor states once that the purpose of two evaluations is to bound a claim rather than to abolish it.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S058](wiki/seminars/S058.md) (where the approximation is applied to whatever margin this day produced), [S059](wiki/seminars/S059.md) (where the shared-construction argument is applied to a public benchmark and becomes the benchmark trap), [S067](wiki/seminars/S067.md) (offline against online, where the gap this day predicts is measured live), [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md) (adversarial inputs, where an attacker selects a point in the geometry) and [S078](wiki/seminars/S078.md) (authorisation at retrieval time, where a relevant document may not be returnable).

**S059 at roughly two days is the immediate target and S067 at roughly six weeks is the real one.** This day can only demonstrate the training-evaluation construction leak on the student's own small models; S067 is where a student watches an offline improvement fail to arrive online, which is the same mechanism with users as the held-out set.

S077's retrieval is the one most likely to fail, because the day does not frame the geometry as an attack surface and a student would have to make the connection unaided.

Retained content: the retriever as a corpus-scale lookup, the training signal as the relevance definition, negatives as the decision boundary, shared construction as a leak, score non-comparability, and reranking as a compute trade. Six items.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 6.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement.

One specification item in IS203. Per rule 1, the checked elements are the relevance definition with any amendment justified; the probe set covering the supplied required respects plus two of the student's own; positives and negatives with their draws stated; the pre-committed predictions; **the shipped vector index with its chunk size, overlap and splitter mode recorded as decisions**; per-class results; both evaluation numbers together; the loss class with the hybrid decision; the phase 7 outcome; **and the prompt, intervention and frontier records.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

Four hard requirements: **a retriever evaluated only against negatives drawn as its training negatives were drawn fails**; **a probe set omitting a supplied required respect fails**; **a chunking configuration reported as a value rather than as a decision fails**; and **an intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the pipeline's own checks could have detected an intervention-worthy error. The accepted instance of the last is an assertion that the query-time vectorizer's model matches the model that produced the index, or a chunk-length check expressed in the embedding model's own unit — which is the [S057](wiki/seminars/S057.md) phase 6 agent failure detected by the artefact rather than by the student.

**D4 is not assessed where the tenant could not provision it.** The condition is stated to the room at phase 3 and recorded here, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s rule that a capability the programme cannot operate is demonstrated and not examined.

**Rule-6 discharge, first obligation — [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md)'s step 3 prediction accuracy by respect.** WP-051 §8 committed this paper to stating in §7 whether the day carries the artefact element S051 named, and where. **It does. The element is phase 5 step 2**, which supplies the respects the cohort could not predict at S051 as *required* probe dimensions, and a probe set omitting one fails the specification. The obligation is discharged, and it is discharged as a fail condition rather than as a prompt, which is the distinction WP-051 §8 said rule 6 could otherwise be satisfied without. A second, weaker element exists at phase 1 item 4, where the same respects are used as pretest predictions so that phase 8 can report whether naming a blind spot closed it.

**Rule-6 discharge, second obligation — [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md)'s step 4 prediction accuracy by query class.** S056 named this day's phase 3 as the response occasion. **The element exists: the third of phase 3's three evaluations is run on the query class this cohort mis-predicted worst at S056, and it is one of the two evaluations that reverse the payload's apparent ordering.** The obligation is discharged. It is a weaker discharge than the first because it changes what the phase measures rather than what a student must produce, and no specification element depends on it.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** The step 5 margin over the baseline by query class responds at [S058](wiki/seminars/S058.md) phase 3, whose index configurations are evaluated on the class where the margin was thinnest; the step 2 probe results on S051's unpredicted respects respond at [S058](wiki/seminars/S058.md) phase 1, whose pretest asks what quantisation does to those same respects. **WP-058 §7 must report whether both elements exist**, per the commitment WP-051 §8 established.

**Frontier instrumentation.** Item 5's sealed prediction accuracy, coded for whether the named error concerned a model, a dimension or a chunk boundary, is carried to the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) per instrumentation rule 9. Per rule 3 the sample is stated: one cohort, one corpus, one service, one agent version, on a task chosen because a silent failure was known to be available in it.

Dispositions: **humility**, at step 7.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day's representational claim is the one it does not demonstrate, and this is the third time.**

At its strongest: point 6 states that one vector must serve every question a document answers, and that a document which is the right answer to three unrelated queries cannot be near all three regions at once. It is the day's only claim about the *representation* rather than about the training, it is the argument the design offers for an entire family of methods it declines to build, and **none of the four phase 3 configurations varies the document representation at all.** D1 to D4 differ in their negatives. The capacity limit is delivered by assertion at phase 4 and is reinforced by nothing.

A student leaves believing point 6 because they were told, which is exactly the condition [WP-051](wiki/whitepapers/WP-051.md) §1 says cannot dislodge a belief the student has no reason to hold. Worse, point 6 is the claim most likely to matter in the students' next five years, because multi-vector methods are where the field is moving and a student who cannot say why will treat the choice as fashion.

**The fix is nearly free and the design should have taken it.** The corpus already contains documents that answer several unrelated queries — [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md)'s query sets identify them. **Supplying, for one such document, its single-vector neighbourhood and the three query regions it fails to reach simultaneously would cost one figure and no phase time.** If the document reaches all three, point 6 is false at this corpus scale and the day has found something more interesting than the claim it intended to make.

**This is the third instance of the pattern [WP-049](wiki/whitepapers/WP-049.md) §8 opened and [WP-053](wiki/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §8 recorded as a second: this design stages what it expects students to resist and asserts what it finds obvious once stated.** The three instances are structurally identical. [WP-049](wiki/whitepapers/WP-049.md)'s design-effect expression, [WP-053](wiki/whitepapers/WP-053.md)'s layers-are-not-stages, and this day's single-vector capacity limit are each a claim the designer finds obvious, each a claim the room does not hold, and each delivered by exposition in a day whose entire method is that exposition does not work. In all three the staging was available and cheap.

Per the programme's convention that three instances elevate an observation to a rule, **this whitepaper argues for elevation, and the rule's form is already determined by the three cases.** It is stated in §9's fourth prediction as a testable claim and reported to the programme rather than written into the shared pattern pages, which this paper does not amend.

**Objection 2: the second evaluation the day mandates has no adjudicator, which makes the hard requirement satisfiable and unfalsifiable at once.**

At its strongest: step 6 requires the student to evaluate against negatives *drawn differently* from their training negatives, and says nothing about which different draw is the right one. Every retrieval improvement can be erased by negatives adversarial enough and preserved by negatives lenient enough, and the student picks. A student who wants a small gap picks a lenient draw and satisfies the specification; a student who wants a dramatic finding picks a hostile one and also satisfies it. **The day's single hard requirement checks that a second number exists, not that it means anything** — which is precisely the defect [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 3 names, a discrimination the design performs and then discards before assessment.

Phase 7's second clause is offered as the countermeasure and it does not close the gap. It constrains the *partner's* constructed scheme to be realistic; it does not constrain the student's own step 6 draw, and the two are assessed separately.

**This is conceded without qualification.** The fix is specific, cheap, and available only because of the substrate: **require step 6's negatives to be drawn from observed production distractors — documents that were returned to real users for that query and not acted on.** That is a draw nobody chooses, it is different from any training construction by definition, it makes the second number a fact about the deployment rather than about the student's taste, and the substrate has been logging it since Q3. The design did not take it, and in declining it the day teaches a discipline while assessing an artefact that cannot distinguish exercising the discipline from performing it.

**Objection 3: the day now teaches a decision it does not require anybody to measure, which is the exact failure it spends the rest of its time attacking.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn and it is the sharper of the two conceded here.

At its strongest: point 8 tells students that chunk boundaries decide what a vector represents, that the same corpus fragments wildly differently under different settings, and that the vendor's recommended size is a starting point whose optimum depends on their content. Step 5 then requires them to **record** their chunk size, overlap and mode as decisions with expected costs. **It does not require them to run a second configuration.** So the day's newest and most consequential claim — that this parameter determines every downstream number — is delivered by exposition and evidenced by nothing the student produced, while forty-five minutes earlier the same day held architecture, corpus and positives fixed precisely so that one variable's effect could be attributed.

The consequence is worse than an unevidenced claim. A student who writes *chunk size 2,000, overlap 500, because that is the recommended starting point and my documents are long* has satisfied step 5 completely and has performed the exact act point 9 defines as accepting a default without choosing it. **The specification cannot distinguish a recorded decision from a recorded default with a sentence attached**, which is [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 3's failure: a discrimination the design performs and then discards.

The rebuttal is real and it is a load argument rather than a principle. Phase 3 varies negatives. Varying chunking as well would give two variables and destroy the attribution the whole phase exists to produce, and §3.1 already records this as the module's second-heaviest day.

**But that defends not varying chunking in phase 3, and the objection is about phase 5, which is a different object.** Step 5 already builds the index once. **This is conceded without qualification, and the fix is to build it twice:** require the student to index at their chosen configuration and at one other, evaluate both against their own [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md) judgements, and report the pair — exactly as step 6 already requires them to report a pair of evaluation numbers. The second index costs the agent one prompt and the service one indexer run. It converts the day's most quotable new claim from an assertion into the student's own measurement, it gives point 8 the evidence §8 objection 1 correctly complains that point 6 lacks, and it makes *the only defensible chunk size is a measured one* a sentence the artefact demonstrates rather than a sentence the instructor says. **It is not in the design, and its absence means this day has repeated at point 8 the precise defect it records at point 6** — which, if the elevation argued above is granted, is now the fourth instance of the same pattern in one design and is no longer a suspicion.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At item 2, a majority of students will either give no source for negatives or name an undifferentiated draw from the corpus. *Instrument:* item 2 responses coded by source, with blanks counted separately. **The blanks are the measure**, and if there are few the asymmetry §3.3 depends on does not exist for this cohort.

**Prediction 2.** Groups will predict D2 or D3 to win phase 3's first evaluation, and D1 will win it. *Instrument:* committed phase 3 orderings against the three evaluations' results. **If groups predict D1, they already hold point 4** and the staged reveal is redundant.

**Prediction 3.** For a majority of students, the gap between step 6's two evaluation numbers will exceed the margin they report over the [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md) baseline at step 5. *Instrument:* paired step 5 margins and step 6 gaps. **If this holds, most of the cohort's reported improvements are smaller than the effect of choosing an evaluation construction**, which is the module's central claim measured on the students' own work.

**Prediction 4.** Item 4's predictions on [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md)'s unpredicted respects will be more accurate than S051's were, and those respects will nonetheless remain the cohort's worst probe dimensions at step 5 relative to the two respects each student chose themselves. *Instrument:* item 4 and step 5 probe accuracy, split by required respect against student-chosen respect. **If the required respects are now predicted as well as the student's own, naming a blind spot closed it** — which would be a finding about rule 6's mechanism rather than about retrieval, and would be the first evidence in the programme that a named response occasion changes an outcome.

**Prediction 5.** At item 5, a majority will name a model or dimension choice as the agent's likely error and fewer than a tenth will name a chunk boundary; and chunk-boundary errors will be the most common entry in the cohort's intervention records. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded model-versus-dimension-versus-boundary, against the intervention records. **The claim is that students cannot predict failure in a stage they did not know existed**, which is the jagged-frontier finding in [research/04-professional-formation/](research/04-professional-formation/collected-materials.md) applied to a pipeline rather than to a task. If boundary predictions are common, point 8 is arriving too late and belongs at [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md).

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** Dual-encoder retrieval and its relation to attention as a similarity lookup, the dependence of a learned retriever's discrimination on its negative sampling, hard-negative mining from lexical candidates, the non-comparability of similarity scores across models, single-vector representational capacity limits and the multi-vector alternative, and two-stage retrieve-then-rerank architectures are grounded in [research/02-technical-foundations/](research/02-technical-foundations/collected-materials.md). The leak argument is [S041](wiki/seminars/S041.md)'s and follows [research/03-measurement-evaluation/](research/03-measurement-evaluation/collected-materials.md). The representation frame is [S011](wiki/seminars/S011.md)'s and [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md)'s, following [research/01-course-structure/](research/01-course-structure/collected-materials.md).

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The platform content at points 8 and 9 is taken from Microsoft Learn's Azure AI Search chunking, integrated vectorization, index-projection, vector-index and hybrid-ranking pages, read 2026-07-26: that chunking is performed by a skill within an indexer pipeline and that integrated vectorization couples a chunking skill, an embedding skill and a query-time vectorizer; that the text splitter has a default mode, a maximum length, an overlap constrained to less than half that length, and a take-count defaulting to all; that the documentation publishes a worked example in which one book yields chunk counts differing by more than two orders of magnitude across six parameter settings, and recommends a specific starting size and overlap while stating that the optimum depends on document shape, query type and the models in use; that characters are explicitly stated not to align with tokens and that token-based splitting is available only in preview; that index projections carry parent fields onto child chunks and that the default projection mode retains parent documents; that a query-time vectorizer must match the embedding model that produced the index; and that `@search.score` for a vector query is reported in a bounded range that differs from the range of the cosine metric it may be configured with. **The permission consequence at point 8's third clause — that chunking moves permission metadata from indexer field mappings to index projections, and that without the projection chunk-level references are not filtered — is from the document-level access control overview, whose analysis is carried in [research/06-microsoft-platform/azure-ai-search-and-acl-patterns.md](research/06-microsoft-platform/azure-ai-search-and-acl-patterns.md); most of that page describes preview API surface and is marked accordingly.**

**Three silences are recorded and two are taught.** **No retrieval quality figure is published for any chunking configuration**: the documentation gives chunk counts, size recommendations and overlap recommendations, and states no measured effect on relevance for any of them, which means the claim that chunking matters is one the student must evidence and the vendor does not. **No enforcement exists for the vectorizer-model match** — the documentation states the requirement and the service does not check it, so a mismatch is a silent failure by design and is taught at point 9 as one. And **no latency figure is published for permission-metadata synchronisation**, which is recorded here because point 8's third clause depends on it and [S078](wiki/seminars/S078.md) inherits it.
