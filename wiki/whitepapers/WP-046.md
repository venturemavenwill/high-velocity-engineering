# WP-046 — Instructions an Executor Actually Receives

**Seminar day:** [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) · **Module:** [M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) · **Quarter:** [Q5](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 3

## In plain terms

Your team has a coding standard. Somebody wrote it, most people read it once, and it has never stopped a change from being merged.

That is the ordinary condition of a written standard, and it is not a discipline failure. It is an **attachment** failure. A standard filed near the work is received by whoever goes looking; a constraint attached to the work is received on every change that matches it. **The difference is between conformance checked by reading and conformance checked by execution**, and it is the only genuinely new property in this area.

The corpus behind this stage states the durable half in one line — move engineering artefacts out of documents and slides into the repository, because that is what makes them actionable. The pinned configuration corpus takes it to its conclusion, separating what an executor receives into kinds that behave differently: constraints applied automatically to matching work, entry points a person invokes deliberately, orchestrators with declared roles, and reusable procedures.

**The taxonomy is a product decision. The distinction underneath it is not.** An artefact that is *applied* is a different object from one that is *invoked*, and confusing them is why most standards are never received.

Three things follow that the session takes seriously.

**An instruction set is only received if the host attaches it, and only obeyed if the model complies**, and the file guarantees neither. The corpus's own rule that fetched content is data rather than authority is a declaration whose effectiveness depends on things outside it — which is why the session tests the boundary rather than teaching it.

**A standard is assessable exactly where it names an artefact.** The openly published handbook's own engineering checklist demonstrates it: most items are mechanically checkable, a substantial minority are not falsifiable as written, and one section stops asserting conditions partway down and starts asking questions instead.

**And the verification step is the session.** The learner does not confirm that the file exists. The learner opens the request record and finds the instruction text inside it. That is a small, dull, physical act, and it converts a belief about a repository into an observation about a delivery.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching agent configuration as an attachment problem rather than as a tooling skill. The argument runs in three steps: that a standard nothing receives cannot have constrained anything; that attachment converts conformance from a reading act into an execution act, which is the property distinguishing a constraint from an aspiration; and that receipt is an observation about a host, a model and a date rather than a property of a file, so it must be verified in the request payload rather than inferred from the repository. The session installs an instruction set held with the work, each constraint carrying a conformance mode and a provenance, plus an authority statement naming a stop condition the executor may not satisfy for itself, plus a receipt record naming the prompt, the place looked and the text observed. Attachment is demonstrated positively and negatively; the untrusted-content boundary is exercised rather than trusted; and a generative instrument is used once, to defeat the learner's own constraints rather than to improve them. The paper grounds the discipline in the post-training literature, which establishes that instruction following is trained behaviour with named failure modes and not obedience. It concedes that the corpus supplies no evidence that configured constraints change what is built, that the session's central claim is close to a tautology, that half the anchor is a vendor's format at one revision, and that the boundary test characterises a configuration rather than a rule.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Between [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) and here, the learner acquired something they cannot yet use: an untestable residue — architectural constraints no check can express, each with a named holder.

A holder is a person, and a person is not present at every change. **The residue therefore has to live somewhere that the work encounters**, and the ordinary answer — a document — is the answer that has been failing for as long as coding standards have existed.

The problem gets sharper the moment an executor arrives that has never seen the codebase. A colleague who has been on the team for a year carries the constraints in their head and applies them without reading anything. **A new executor carries nothing**, and the constraints that were never written down are precisely the ones most likely to be violated: outdated patterns reused, architectural boundaries crossed, conventions the team could not have articulated if asked.

There is a second problem, which the session cannot solve and must not conceal. **An instruction is a request.** Whether it is delivered depends on the host; whether it is followed depends on the model. Neither is a property of the file, and a learner who leaves believing that writing the constraint enforces it has acquired a false confidence more dangerous than none.

And there is a third, which is the one this design is organised around. **Between the constraint and the change there are three independent failure points — the file, the delivery and the compliance — and all three fail silently.** A learner who checks only the first will conclude from a compliant-looking output that the whole chain worked. That inference is unsound in a specific direction, and §3.7 says why.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** Coding standards are universal professional experience. What is new is the conformance mode, the authority statement, and the demonstration of receipt.

It assumes [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md)'s untestable residue, which is the raw material for the *checked by review* and *not checked at all* entries. It assumes [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md)'s decision record and [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md)'s specification discipline, because a constraint's provenance is one of those two or is a preference, and the session requires it to be said which.

The prior the session works against is that configuring an agent is a tooling activity. **It is taught here as a standards activity** whose novelty is that the standard is executable, which is why the artefact is assessed on its constraints and not on its syntax.

**This is a class 3 session:** nobody supplies the constraints, nobody supplies the conformance modes, and nobody says which constraints belong in the honest third class. The learner decides all three, and deciding that a constraint is unenforceable is a correct answer.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** High procedural load, low intrinsic load, and the setup cost of the anchor lands here and at [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md) and nowhere else. **A cohort that spreads the setup across five sessions will spend the module on tooling**, which is the failure [M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) is least able to survive.

**3.2 Why attachment is the operative property.** A constraint applied to matching work is received on every change that matches; a constraint filed elsewhere is received on the occasions somebody looks. The second is not a weaker version of the first — **it is a different mechanism with a different failure mode**, and it fails silently. The corpus makes the distinction structural rather than rhetorical by keeping four artefact kinds in separate trees with separate application rules, and by separating authoring source, packaging manifest and generated output so that the last is explicitly non-canonical ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)).

That separation carries one consequence worth extracting for its own sake: **maturity and retirement are expressed through membership and metadata rather than inferred from a file's age.** A standard that nobody has retired is still in force, and organisations routinely believe otherwise about documents that have gone quiet.

**3.3 Why the conformance mode must include *not checked at all*.** A set in which everything is checked has been written optimistically, and optimism about coverage is the mechanism by which the untestable residue disappears. **The honest category has to exist or the dishonest one gets used.** The third class is also where the session's most important constraint lives: the human-review stop condition, which no execution can establish, because there is no artefact whose existence proves a person decided something.

**3.4 Why the authority statement names a stop condition.** The corpus prohibits an agent from recording a human's approval and requires a workflow to stop where a human review is required and absent. **That is the distinction between a workflow that includes a human and one that can complete without one**, and it is the single most transferable rule in the corpus because it survives every change of product.

**3.5 Why untrusted content is data.** Material an executor fetches, reads or is handed may contain instructions. The corpus's rule is that such instructions are reported rather than obeyed, and it applies the rule uniformly to fetched pages, uploaded documents, source repositories, tool output and issue bodies. **The rule is durable; its enforcement is not.** The corpus's own transparency statement concedes the ground: it ships no model and no safety classifier, host behaviour varies, and human oversight remains necessary. §4 explains why the session therefore tests rather than teaches it.

**3.6 Why a standard is assessable where it names an artefact.** This is the openly published handbook's checklist read as a specimen ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md), pages read 2026-07-30). The items that name a thing that exists can be checked by somebody holding that thing — a locked default branch either is or is not locked. The items that describe a condition of a team cannot: "The agile process is clearly defined within team" has no observable that separates satisfaction from failure. And one section slips out of declarative form partway down and begins asking questions, so items in the same list are doing different grammatical work. **The generalisation is the session's marking rule for the learner's own set**, and it is worth more than the checklist.

The handbook's limits are heavy and must travel with every use. It has no single date — its pages carry individual footer dates spread across several years. Roughly a fifth of it is stubs, link lists or tool catalogues. It contradicts itself in at least a dozen places without noticing, including on branch naming, which differs across three pages. **And it asserts four unsupported magnitude claims, one of which states a cost outcome with no evidence, no baseline and no source. This repository records all four as specimens and reproduces none of them.**

**3.7 Why receipt must be observed, and why compliant-looking output is not evidence of it.**

This is the subsection the session is actually built on, and it is grounded in the post-training literature rather than in the design's own reasoning.

Instruction following is not a property models have; it is a property that was installed. The foundational result states the gap directly: making language models bigger does not inherently make them better at following a user's intent, and unaligned models produce outputs that are "untruthful, toxic, or simply not helpful to the user" ([research/02-technical-foundations/ouyang-instructgpt.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/ouyang-instructgpt.md)). The remedy was a three-stage pipeline — demonstrations, a preference model trained on rankings, then optimisation against that preference model — and **the three stages can each fail independently.** The note records the limits that matter here: the result is defined relative to a particular labeller pool, a particular set of labelling instructions and a particular prompt distribution, none of them universal; **human preference is not the same as correctness**, so optimising for preference can produce confident, agreeable, wrong answers; and the authors state that the models still make simple mistakes.

The later work sharpens the point rather than softening it. The separate preference model can be removed entirely, because a policy together with its reference model already encodes a reward function, collapsing the arrangement into a classification loss — and the authors characterise the pipeline they replace as "a complex and often unstable procedure" ([research/02-technical-foundations/rafailov-dpo.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/rafailov-dpo.md)). **The note's recorded limit is the sentence an instruction author needs: the method changes the optimiser, not the supervision, and it inherits every weakness of the preference data — annotator bias, sycophancy incentives, and the gap between preference and correctness.** Its evaluated scope is sentiment control, summarisation and single-turn dialogue; multi-turn and agentic settings are outside it, which is the setting the learner works in.

Two consequences follow, and together they are the warrant for the workflow's fourth step.

First, **compliance is an empirical property of a host and a model on a date, not a property of a file.** Nothing in the alignment literature licenses an assumption that a written rule is followed, and the DPO note's sycophancy caution says the opposite is a live risk.

Second, and less obviously, **an output that looks compliant is weak evidence that the instruction arrived at all.** A system trained toward agreeableness produces plausible, well-formed, apparently conforming work for reasons unrelated to your configuration. **A learner who infers delivery from output has confounded three failure points into one observation**, and the payload check is the cheapest available way to separate them. It settles exactly one of the three — delivery — and the session says so rather than implying it settles more.

**3.8 Why the instrument is used to defeat the set rather than to improve it.** A generative instrument asked to improve a document adds to it; asked to defeat one, it must locate a weakness, and locating is a harder operation than adding. **The constraints it can satisfy literally while violating are exactly the constraints that name a form rather than an artefact**, which is §3.6's criterion arriving as a procedure instead of a judgement. This is [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s claim about an instrument that agrees with you, at its fifth appearance, and the design says so each time.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 2 is the instrument and it produces the session's first constraint.** *What does an executor that has never seen your codebase get wrong first?* The answer is almost always the constraint the team holds in its head, which is the one that will actually fire and the one nobody has written.

**Opening item 4 is the diagnostic and it is expected to fail.** *How would you establish that a constraint reached the thing doing the work?* Most learners answer with a file path. That answer is an artefact claim standing in for an observation claim, and the substitution is invisible to the person making it — which is why exercise 3 requires comparison against the criterion rather than rereading it.

**Receipt is demonstrated, not asserted, and it is demonstrated twice.** Once against a matching path, where the instruction text must be found in the request record; once against a non-matching path, where it must be absent. **The negative case is what distinguishes a scoped constraint from a switched-on one**, and it is the half learners omit, because the positive case feels like success and stops the investigation.

**The pin is recorded before anything else.** Commit, package version, host, model, date. Everything observed afterwards is conditioned on those five things, and a record without them is a record about a moving object. This is the module's standing device and §8's answer to the perishability objection.

**The boundary test is an observation, not a lesson.** The learner hands the executor material containing an instruction that attempts to change its authority, and watches. The session states plainly that the learner is not testing the rule but finding out whether it is enforced in their setting. **That framing is the point**: it is the difference between believing a control and characterising one, which is the programme's central methodological commitment arriving in its most concrete form.

**4.1 Why the review movement uses another team rather than a rubric.** They make one small change using the set alone, without asking anything. Every place they guessed is an unwritten constraint; every place they asked a colleague is an unattached one. **This is the only measurement of an instruction set available inside a fortnight**, and it measures receipt by a person, which is the closest available proxy for receipt by anything else.

**4.2 Why the handbook's checklist is used as a specimen mid-session** rather than as an opening example. It arrives immediately after the learner assigns conformance modes to their own constraints, so that the falsifiability judgement is exercised on somebody else's document while it is fresh on their own. Reversing the order teaches a rule; this order produces a comparison.

**4.3 Why a second artefact was added, and why it is the agent's own definition.**

An instructions file is a constraint that arrives *alongside* work. It is the right first object because its delivery is observable and its failure is silent, which is what the payload check exists to expose. **But it leaves the session's argument resting on one artefact whose receipt the learner must go and find.** There is a blunter case available on the same platform, and it closes the argument rather than illustrating it: **a file that is not delivered alongside the agent but constitutes it.**

The vendor publishes an open declarative specification for defining an agent — model, instructions, tools, connections — in two shapes, a concrete executable definition and a parameterised template wrapping one, with **two substitution layers, one resolved at initialisation from prompted values and one at deploy or run from the environment's own file** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-agent-build-and-runtime.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-agent-build-and-runtime.md)). **That is the session's thesis in the platform's vocabulary**: a specification the runtime genuinely receives, diffable and reviewable rather than clicked into a portal, and one reviewed file working across environments.

**The design's reason for adding it is the second half, not the first.** The schema **has no field for skills**, the tooling does not fetch them, and the documentation says so outright; and there is an explicit instruction **not to declare the project endpoint as an environment variable**, because the platform injects it and a declaration risks shadowing the platform value — a constraint on the file that the file cannot state about itself. **So the definition file demonstrates both halves of the session at once:** an executor does receive it, and it is not the whole specification, and the part it cannot express does not disappear because there was no field for it. **That part is [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md)'s untestable residue arriving on a second artefact, and §4's addition is to require the same conformance mode and the same named holder for it.**

One field is included in the exercise deliberately because it is mis-read by competent people. **Two protocol values are defined, one carrying platform-managed conversation history and one carrying none.** Choosing the second moves state into code nobody has written, in a line most reviewers will read as a transport preference. **A file whose most consequential line looks like plumbing is a file a reviewer approves without reading**, and the repair the session teaches is a comment naming the consequence rather than a better reviewer.

**The limits are the heaviest in the module and the design assesses none of the syntax.** The note is **the most perishable material in this repository and it perished visibly while being read**: the schema reference was fetched and read in full and most of the surrounding surface reached only through search excerpts with tables truncated, **so no field list is exhaustive.** Several consulted pages are explicitly superseded and still render, preview status is pervasive, and **the material is definitional rather than evidential.** And the schema reference **opens by declaring the model it documents deprecated** in favour of a single consolidated file, while three other first-party surfaces described that model as current on the same day — **which is [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s specimen and is examined there.** Here it is only the reason the pin and the provenance column exist, and the reason the session tells learners to verify against the tooling version they are running rather than against this page.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The assumed delivery.** The likeliest failure and the one the anchor's verification step exists against. The file is right, the glob looks right, nobody has seen the text arrive. *Countermeasure:* the payload check, which cannot be passed by a document. **This is the strongest countermeasure in the session because it is mechanical.**

**The unattached standard.** Excellent prose, filed where nothing reaches it. *Countermeasure:* the demonstration movement, and the other-team review.

**The unscoped glob.** Everything matches, the executor receives a wall of constraints on every change, and attends to none of them in particular. *Countermeasure:* the negative demonstration against a non-matching path. **The countermeasure detects the condition and does nothing about the attention problem, which §9 predicts will persist.**

**The essay instruction.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that a constraint be statable as something checkable in one sentence.

**The unfalsifiable constraint.** *Countermeasure:* the conformance mode, the specimen exercise on the handbook's own checklist, and the instrument's defeat run. **The first two are judgements and the third is a procedure**, which is why the defeat run was added.

**The generated-tree edit.** The learner changes packaged output rather than authoring source, and the change vanishes at the next build. *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that the set enter the repository through the same route as code, and the corpus's own declaration that its generated tree is not canonical.

**The delegated approval.** *Countermeasure:* the authority statement's required stop condition, and the standard's warning sign — your workflow completes without a person having said anything.

**The obedient fetch.** *Countermeasure:* the boundary test, which produces an observation whichever way it comes out. **It also produces an over-reading, which §9 predicts and exercise 5 is designed to catch:** one successful trial of a stochastic system is an anecdote, and the corpus concedes that host behaviour varies.

The session also serves [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) backwards. The untestable residue produced there had a named holder and no home; here it acquires one, and the *not checked at all* class is the honest name for what it is. **That is the strongest practical use of the residue anywhere in the stage, and it is not a use [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) describes.**

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. The instruction set is submitted at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md), where one property is assessed: that each constraint states its conformance mode and that the *not checked at all* entries are present.

**The set's effectiveness is not assessed anywhere**, because no source establishes that configured constraints change what is built and the design declines to invent an instrument for it. **The receipt record is likewise not assessed for its result, only for its existence and its pin** — a learner who looked, found nothing, and recorded the absence with the host and version has met the standard exactly as well as one who found the text.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the session teaches a vendor's file layout and calls it a principle, and the whole thing will be obsolete before a cohort graduates.**

At its strongest: the artefact-kind taxonomy is one company's product decision at one revision. Paths, counts, names and package versions moved during the period this repository's reading describes, and the note says so in its first paragraph. A learner who acquires this set of distinctions has acquired a configuration format, and the programme has spent its scarcest resource — a session in a delivery stage — on something with a shelf life measured in months.

**Substantially conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to this session.** Three answers, none complete. The assessed artefact is the learner's own constraints with their conformance modes, not the vendor's structure, and it would survive a total change of format. The durable claim is stated at §3.2 and is about attachment, not about kinds. And the pin exists precisely so that the perishable half is labelled as perishable — **the perishable table in the session records the version-bound instance beside every durable claim, which is the repository's standing device for exactly this problem.** **The declined fix is to teach attachment without an anchor.** It is declined because attachment cannot be demonstrated without a host that attaches something, and a session that asserted the distinction rather than showing it would be the documentation exercise this one exists to replace. **The shelf life is real; the mitigation is labelling rather than avoidance.**

**Objection 2: the central claim is a tautology, and a session built on a tautology is a session about nothing.**

At its strongest: *a constraint nothing receives cannot have constrained anything* is true by definition. The paper concedes it in the session's own limits section. Everything interesting — whether attached constraints improve code, whether they reduce the failures a new executor produces, whether a model complies with them at any useful rate — is exactly what the paper says is unestablished.

**Conceded, and the design's response is that the tautology is the useful part.** The proposition nobody disputes is nonetheless violated by most engineering organisations, which maintain standards that are not received and believe themselves standardised. **A session that installs a checkable version of an undisputed claim is doing more work than one that installs a disputed claim nobody can test.** The declined fix is to add an element assessing whether the set improved the executor's output. It is declined for the reason §7 gives: no source supplies a basis, the comparison requires producing the increment twice, and [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) already assesses the weaker and honest version — whether the candidate can say how they would find out.

**Objection 3: the payload check trains a superstition, because it verifies the one link in the chain that was never the problem.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the design finds hardest. Delivery is the *most* reliable of the three failure points. Hosts attach files that match their globs; that is the feature working as documented, and it rarely fails. The two links that actually decide whether a constraint holds are compliance — which §3.7 establishes is unreliable in a known direction — and the quality of the constraint itself. **By making the cheap, mechanical, almost-always-passing check the session's named verification step, the design risks manufacturing exactly the confidence it says it is dismantling**: a learner who has watched the text arrive may reasonably conclude that the configuration is working, which is the inference the paper elsewhere calls unsound. Worse, the check is passable by ceremony. A learner can find the string, tick the box, and understand nothing about why the string being present does not mean the rule is in force.

**Partly conceded, and the concession is real.** The rebuttal has two parts. First, delivery fails more often than the objection allows, and it fails in ways nobody notices — an over-broad glob delivers the constraint to work it should not govern, a narrow one silently excludes the files that matter, and a build-output edit delivers nothing at all. The negative demonstration exists for this reason and is the half of the check that is not ceremonial. Second, and more importantly, **the design does not rest the compliance question on the payload check; it rests it on the defeat run and on exercise 5's requirement to write down what the observation does not establish.** Those two are the compliance instruments, and both are designed to come out badly.

**The declined fix is to replace the payload check with a compliance measurement — running the same task many times with and without the instruction set and comparing conformance rates.** It is declined on cost and on honesty. The cost is that a properly powered comparison of a stochastic system is beyond a session, and an underpowered one produces a result indistinguishable from no result, which is [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md)'s finding arriving here. The honesty cost is worse: a small unpowered comparison would give learners a number, and this programme's standing prohibition exists because numbers of that kind are what get repeated. **The design would rather teach a check that is honest about being partial than a measurement that is dishonest about being one.** The cost of that choice is the one the objection names, and §9's prediction 5 is written to detect it.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first instruction sets will contain no *not checked at all* entries. *Instrument:* the conformance-mode column, before and after the work movement.

**Prediction 2.** The constraint produced from opening item 2 will be the one most often violated in the review movement's other-team test. *Instrument:* the other team's output, coded against the set's constraints by a second reader.

**Prediction 3.** A majority of learners will answer opening item 4 by naming an artefact rather than an observation, and a majority of those will still name an artefact after the demonstration unless exercise 3's comparison step is enforced. *Instrument:* opening item 4 answers coded *artefact* / *observation*, against the same coding of the exercise 3 rewrite, split on whether the comparison sentence was written. **The second half is the test of the comparison condition itself**, and a null result would mean the productive-failure structure is not carrying its weight in this session.

**Prediction 4.** Learners will perform the positive demonstration and omit the negative one unless it is separately required. *Instrument:* the receipt record, coded for presence of a non-matching-path check.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of learners who complete the payload check will subsequently describe their constraint as *enforced* rather than *delivered* in the [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) defence. *Instrument:* the defence transcript, coded for the verb applied to a configured constraint. **This predicts the failure of the session's own named verification step**, in exactly the form §8's third objection describes, and it is included because a countermeasure whose side effect is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 6.** The untrusted-content boundary will hold on the term's host and model for most learners, and the minority for whom it does not will be unable to explain the difference. *Instrument:* the boundary-test record against the recorded pin, per learner. **This prediction is about a configuration, not about a rule**, and a result under one pin licenses nothing about another.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a constraint which is not attached to the work is not received; that *applied* and *invoked* are different kinds of artefact; that an executor may not record a human's approval; and that receipt is an observation about a host, a model and a date rather than a property of a file.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about code quality. The corpus's architecture claims are warranted and every benefit word in its prose is not. **It contains evaluation machinery and no committed results.** The alignment literature licenses direction and mechanism only, and its evaluated scope is not agentic.

**The concession worth remembering.** The verification step this session is named for settles delivery and nothing else. It is the cheap link in the chain, and §8 concedes that making it the visible one may buy false confidence. **Watch for yourself which verb you reach for afterwards.**
