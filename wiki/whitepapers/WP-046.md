## In plain terms

Your team has a coding standard. Somebody wrote it, most people read it once, and it has never once stopped a change from being merged.

That is the ordinary condition of a written standard, and it is not a discipline failure. It is an **attachment** failure. A standard filed near the work is received by whoever goes looking; a constraint attached to the work is received on every change that matches it. **The difference is between conformance checked by reading and conformance checked by execution**, and it is the only genuinely new property in this area.

The corpus behind this stage states the durable half in one line — move engineering artefacts out of documents and slides into the repository, because that is what makes them actionable. The pinned repository takes it to its conclusion, separating what an executor receives into kinds that behave differently: constraints applied automatically to matching work, entry points a person invokes deliberately, orchestrators with declared roles, and reusable procedures.

**The taxonomy is a product decision. The distinction underneath it is not.** An artefact that is *applied* is a different object from one that is *invoked*, and confusing them is why most standards are never received.

Two things follow that the session takes seriously.

**An instruction set is only received if the host attaches it and only obeyed if the model complies**, and the file guarantees neither. The repository's own rule that fetched content is data rather than authority is a declaration whose effectiveness depends on things outside it — which is why the session tests the boundary rather than teaching it.

**A standard is assessable exactly where it names an artefact.** The playbook's own engineering checklist demonstrates it: most items are mechanically checkable, **a substantial minority are not falsifiable as written**, and one section stops asserting conditions partway down and starts asking questions instead.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching agent configuration as an attachment problem rather than as a tooling skill. The argument is that a standard nobody receives cannot have constrained anything; that attachment converts conformance from a reading act into an execution act, which is the property that distinguishes a constraint from an aspiration; and that the artefact-kind taxonomy of the pinned repository is a product decision resting on a durable distinction between applied and invoked artefacts. The session installs an instruction set held with the work, each constraint carrying a conformance mode and a provenance, plus an authority statement naming a stop condition the executor may not satisfy for itself. Attachment is demonstrated by observation rather than asserted, and the untrusted-content boundary is tested on the term's own host and model. The paper concedes that the repository supplies no evidence that configured constraints change what is built, that the session's central claim is close to a tautology, and that the boundary test measures a configuration rather than a rule.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Between [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) and here, the learner acquired something they cannot yet use: an untestable residue — architectural constraints no check can express, each with a named holder.

A holder is a person, and a person is not present at every change. **The residue therefore has to live somewhere that the work encounters**, and the ordinary answer — a document — is the answer that has been failing for as long as coding standards have existed.

The problem gets sharper the moment an executor arrives who has never seen the codebase. A colleague who has been on the team for a year carries the constraints in their head and applies them without reading anything. **A new executor carries nothing**, and the constraints that were never written down are precisely the ones the practitioner testimony at [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) reports being ignored: outdated patterns reused, architectural constraints violated.

There is a second problem which the session cannot solve and must not conceal. **An instruction is a request.** Whether it is delivered depends on the host; whether it is followed depends on the model. Neither is a property of the file, and a learner who leaves believing that writing the constraint enforces it has acquired a false confidence more dangerous than none.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** Coding standards are universal professional experience. What is new is the conformance mode, the authority statement and the demonstration of attachment.

It assumes [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md)'s untestable residue, which is the raw material for the *checked by review* and *not checked at all* entries. It assumes [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md)'s decision record and [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md)'s specification discipline, because a constraint's provenance is one of those two or is a preference, and the session requires it to be said which.

The prior the session works against is that configuring an agent is a tooling activity. **It is taught here as a standards activity** whose novelty is that the standard is executable, which is why the artefact is assessed on its constraints and not on its syntax.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** High procedural load, low intrinsic load, and the setup cost of the anchor lands here and at [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md) and nowhere else. **A cohort that spreads the setup across five sessions will spend the module on tooling**, which is the failure [M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) is least able to survive.

**3.2 Why attachment is the operative property.** A constraint applied to matching work is received on every change that matches; a constraint filed elsewhere is received on the occasions somebody looks. The second is not a weaker version of the first — **it is a different mechanism with a different failure mode**, and it fails silently.

**3.3 Why the conformance mode must include *not checked at all*.** A set in which everything is checked has been written optimistically, and optimism about coverage is the mechanism by which the untestable residue disappears. **The honest category has to exist or the dishonest one gets used.**

**3.4 Why the authority statement names a stop condition.** The pinned repository prohibits an agent from recording a human's approval and requires a workflow to stop where a human review is required and absent. **That is the distinction between a workflow that includes a human and one that can complete without one**, and it is the single most transferable rule in the corpus because it survives every change of product.

**3.5 Why untrusted content is data.** Material an executor fetches, reads or is handed may contain instructions. The rule is that such instructions are reported rather than obeyed. **The rule is durable; its enforcement is not**, and §4 explains why the session tests rather than teaches it.

**3.6 Why a standard is assessable where it names an artefact.** This is the playbook's checklist read as a specimen: the items that name a thing that exists can be checked by somebody holding that thing; the items that describe a condition of a team cannot, and one section slips out of declarative form entirely. **The generalisation is the session's marking rule for the learner's own set.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 2 is the instrument and it produces the session's first constraint.** *What does an executor who has never seen your codebase get wrong first?* The answer is almost always the constraint the team holds in its head, which is the one that will actually fire and the one nobody has written.

**Attachment is demonstrated, not asserted.** The learner makes a change the constraint matches and observes whether it applies. **This is the movement that distinguishes the session from a documentation exercise**, and it is where the pin is recorded, because everything observed is a property of that combination rather than of the idea.

**The boundary test is an observation, not a lesson.** The learner hands the executor material containing an instruction that attempts to change its authority, and watches. The session states plainly that the learner is not testing the rule but finding out whether it is enforced in their setting. **That framing is the point**: it is the difference between believing a control and characterising one, which is the programme's central methodological commitment arriving in its most concrete form.

**The review movement uses another team as the instrument.** They make one small change using the set alone. Every place they guessed is an unwritten constraint; every place they asked a colleague is an unattached one. **This is the only measurement of an instruction set available inside a fortnight.**

**The playbook's checklist is used as a specimen mid-session**, immediately after the learner assigns conformance modes to their own constraints, so that the falsifiability judgement is exercised on somebody else's document while it is fresh on their own.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The unattached standard.** The likeliest failure and the one the session is named against. *Countermeasure:* the demonstration movement, which cannot be passed by a document.

**The essay instruction.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that a constraint be statable as something checkable in one sentence.

**The unfalsifiable constraint.** *Countermeasure:* the conformance mode, plus the specimen exercise on the playbook's own checklist. **The countermeasure is a judgement rather than a criterion and §8 concedes it.**

**The generated-tree edit.** The learner changes packaged output rather than authoring source, and the change vanishes at the next build. *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that the set enter the repository through the same route as code, and the pinned repository's own declaration that its generated tree is not canonical.

**The delegated approval.** *Countermeasure:* the authority statement's required stop condition, and the standard's warning sign — your workflow completes without a person having said anything.

**The obedient fetch.** *Countermeasure:* the boundary test, which produces an observation whichever way it comes out.

No entrustment evidence. The instruction set is submitted at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md), where one property is assessed: that each constraint states its conformance mode and that the *not checked at all* entries are present.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the session teaches a vendor's file layout and calls it a principle, and the whole thing will be obsolete before a cohort graduates.**

At its strongest: the artefact-kind taxonomy is one company's product decision at one revision. Paths, counts, plugin names and package versions moved during the period this repository's reading describes. A student who learns this set of distinctions has learned a configuration format, and the programme has spent its scarcest resource — a session in a delivery stage — on something with a shelf life measured in months.

**Substantially conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to this session.** Three answers, none complete. The assessed artefact is the learner's own constraints with their conformance modes, not the vendor's structure, and it would survive a total change of format. The durable claim is stated at §3.2 and is about attachment, not about kinds. And the pin exists precisely so that the perishable half is labelled as perishable — **the perishable table in the session records the version-bound instance beside every durable claim, which is the repository's standing device for exactly this problem.** **The declined fix is to teach attachment without an anchor.** It is declined because attachment cannot be demonstrated without a host that attaches something, and a session that asserted the distinction rather than showing it would be the documentation exercise this one exists to replace. **The shelf life is real; the mitigation is labelling rather than avoidance.**

**Objection 2: the central claim is a tautology, and a session built on a tautology is a session about nothing.**

At its strongest: *a constraint nobody receives cannot have constrained anything* is true by definition. The paper concedes it in the session's own limits section. Everything interesting — whether attached constraints improve code, whether they reduce the failures the [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) testimony describes, whether a model complies with them at any useful rate — is exactly what the paper says is unestablished.

**Conceded, and the design's response is that the tautology is the useful part.** The proposition nobody disputes is nonetheless violated by most engineering organisations, which maintain standards that are not received and believe themselves standardised. **A session that installs a checkable version of an undisputed claim is doing more work than one that installs a disputed claim nobody can test.** The declined fix is to add an element assessing whether the set improved the executor's output. It is declined for the reason §7 gives: no source supplies a basis, the comparison requires producing the increment twice, and [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) already assesses the weaker and honest version — whether the candidate can say how they would find out.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first instruction sets will contain no *not checked at all* entries. *Instrument:* the conformance-mode column, before and after the work movement.

**Prediction 2.** The constraint produced from opening item 2 will be the one most often violated in the review movement's other-team test. *Instrument:* the other team's output, coded against the set's constraints by a second reader.

**Prediction 3.** A majority of learners will discover at the demonstration movement that at least one constraint they believed attached was not. *Instrument:* the demonstration record, coded *attached as expected* / *not attached* per constraint.

**Prediction 4.** The untrusted-content boundary will hold on the term's host and model for most learners, and the minority for whom it does not will be unable to explain the difference. *Instrument:* the boundary-test record against the recorded pin, per learner. **This prediction is about a configuration, not about a rule**, and a result under one pin licenses nothing about another.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a constraint which is not attached to the work is not received; that *applied* and *invoked* are different kinds of artefact; and that an executor may not record a human's approval.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about code quality. The pinned repository's architecture claims are warranted and every benefit word in its prose is not. **It contains evaluation machinery and no committed results.**

**The concession worth remembering.** Half of this session is a vendor's format at one revision and will be wrong soon. The pin exists so you can tell which half.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That the practice corpus instructs engineers to move engineering artefacts out of documents and slides into markdown in the repository because that is what makes them actionable — recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md), from a complete reading of access-controlled material **that no public reader can check.**

That the open playbook's engineering checklist is mechanically checkable in most of its items and not falsifiable as written in a substantial minority, that its security section changes grammatical form partway down, and that its only treatment of generated code is a commit-authorship convention distinguishing agentic authorship from assistance — recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md), from a complete reading of the site on 2026-07-30.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That a rule applied automatically to matching work is received differently from a rule available on request; that a category which is uncomfortable to use will be avoided if a comfortable one is available; and that a control's existence is not evidence of its operation. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The instruction set's five parts; the three conformance modes and the requirement that the third be populated; the provenance requirement; the demonstration-of-attachment movement; the other-team review as the set's measurement; the generalisation of the playbook's checklist property into a marking rule; and all four predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The agent-configuration repository was read at commit `cf29fb457b0fe62745bb71592ec9394b834957f4`, published package version 3.3.101, and every path, count and structural claim is as of that revision. **It separates four artefact kinds; it separates authoring source, packaging manifest and generated output and declares the generated tree non-canonical; it prohibits an agent from recording a human's approval and requires a workflow to stop where a required human review is absent; and it treats fetched pages, documents, repositories, tool output and issue bodies as data rather than authority, requiring embedded instructions to be reported rather than obeyed.** Its own transparency statement records that it ships no model and no safety classifier, that host behaviour varies, and that human oversight remains necessary — **so whether the boundary holds is a property of the host and model, not of the file.** It contains evaluation machinery and **no committed result corpus**, and the revision read was not all-green. **No benefit claim from its prose is adopted anywhere in this paper.**

The host is GitHub Copilot agent mode as recorded in [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md)'s reading of 2026-07-30. **That reading has not been repeated for this paper and is carried forward as read**: the agent determines its own steps and iterates rather than stopping after one response, and its confirmation prompt before a terminal command or a non-built-in tool is a dismissible default rather than a boundary, auto-confirmable per session, per solution or permanently. **The documentation published no measure of how often the self-correction loop converges or how often the agent reports completion on work that is wrong**, and the agent-mode page carried two product version ranges simultaneously at that reading.
