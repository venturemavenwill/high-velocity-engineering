# WP-045 — A Model You Are Willing to Defend

**Seminar day:** [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md) · **Module:** [M09](wiki/modules/M09.md) · **Quarter:** [Q5](wiki/quarters/Q5.md)
**Strands:** IS · ME · SG · PE · **Complexity class:** 3
**Platform anchor:** Foundry model deployment behind a feature flag · Foundry cloud evaluation · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server (preview) · Azure MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends assessing a deployment decision rather than a model, and defends treating a defended refusal to deploy as a full pass. The first argument is that the competence the programme certifies is judgement under consequence, that a model's accuracy is a property of the model while a deployment decision is a property of the engineer, and that a specification rewarding deployment would convert the day into a performance target and destroy the judgement it exists to observe. The second argument is that requiring three specific failure modes, each with an affected party and a frequency, is the only formulation under which "what will it do badly" cannot be answered in generalities. The paper additionally defends two things the day now carries. **The first is the unassisted decision hour**, one of the programme's three occasions on which a bounded portion of assessed work is performed without agent assistance, defended in full at §7. **The second is the day's opening observation**: this platform will make a deployment decision about a fine-tuned model whether or not the student makes one, using thresholds that are fixed, unpublished and unmodifiable, and reporting only whether the model is deployable — which is the clearest available instance of the difference between a decision made and a decision delegated. It concedes that the day's headline verification is a sealed self-report whose honesty it cannot check, that the design has twice retrofitted rules onto days it had already written, that the unassisted hour is aimed at ground the design chose because it expects to win there, and that the deployment exposes real users to a student's judgement on the strength of a five-week-old feature flag.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Four days have produced a model, a bounded estimate, a justified capacity and a threat model. Every one of those is an artefact about the model. None of them is a decision.

The decision is the day's subject and it is a different kind of thing. A model with an excellent held-out figure may be undeployable because its failure mode falls on a group who cannot tolerate it; a model with a mediocre figure may be deployable because its failures are cheap and detectable. **The deployment question is not answered by the evaluation question**, and students who have spent four days improving a number will attempt to answer it that way.

Phase 6 point 2 makes this the cohort's own finding: the deploy and do-not-deploy split, shown unattributed, does not track model quality. It tracks what the student concluded about consequence.

The day's second problem is the shape of "what will it do badly." Asked this, students produce generalities — it may be less accurate on rare cases, it might not generalise to new users — which are true, unfalsifiable and useless to anyone deciding whether to accept the model. Step 3's requirement of **three specific failure modes, each with an affected party and a frequency**, is the formulation under which generalities cannot be submitted. A student who cannot name who experiences the failure has not identified a failure mode; they have identified an unease.

The third problem is what the programme is willing to reward. **A defended decision not to deploy is a full pass**, stated before phase 1. This is not a concession to cautious students. It is the design's position that the competence being certified includes declining, that a specification rewarding deployment would make refusal costly, and that a costly refusal is not a decision.

The fourth problem is verification the design owes itself. [WP-041](wiki/whitepapers/WP-041.md) §5 conceded that M09's negativity was front-loaded and deferred its resolution to this day, naming the instrument: **item 1, what are you willing to claim about this model.** A cohort that will claim nothing indicates the deferral failed, and phase 8 reports the result.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge assumptions

- **Four module artefacts** about one model, all the student's own.
- **The S020 capacity-argument structure,** which phase 3's individual-decision-with-factual-support reproduces.
- **The S040 characterisation discipline,** ten weeks old, whose entrustment activity was a precursor to the same terminal activity family.
- **Two prior entrustment experiences** at S030 and S040, neither carried forward.
- **The substrate's disclosure conditions,** which now cover deliberate disturbance and instrument adoption and which this deployment falls under.
- **A real substrate user** willing to attend a defence and be affected by the outcome.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Low intrinsic, very high evaluative.** No new content. The model exists, the evidence exists, and everything demanded is synthesis, decision and defence.

The evaluative load is the module's highest: an entrustment judgement, a defence to two assessors and an affected user, and — for those deploying — a live deployment the same afternoon.

Management:

- **No new content whatever.**
- **Individual work with factual staff support only** at phase 3, matching S020.
- **The required output is enumerated in six parts.**
- **The defence questions are published, including the user's.**
- **The expected level is stated in advance,** and so is the fact that not deploying passes.
- **Consolidation and clinic follow the assessment.**
- **Non-deploying students have an equivalent phase 5 task** assessed identically, so the day does not fork into a real half and a waiting half.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Item 1's willingness question,* sealed, which is also the module's verification instrument.
- *Item 2's honest gap* — what you would like to claim and cannot.
- *Three failure modes with parties and frequencies,* step 3.
- *A withdrawal signal with a threshold,* step 5.
- *The user-facing sentence,* step 6, and the user's own question at phase 4.
- *Writing what would change your decision,* for non-deploying students.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *New content.* None.
- *Model improvement.* The model is what it is. A day permitting further training would produce four hours of tuning and no decision.
- *Comparative assessment.* No student's model is judged against another's.
- *Any reward for deploying.* Explicitly refused and stated to students.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The entrustable activity is a bounded precursor to EA-1 and is stated as such. **Safe** as evidence that a student can form and defend a deployment position on a component they built, with a known evaluation and a bounded blast radius. **Unsafe** as evidence about EA-1 itself, which concerns a ship decision on a system the student did not build, under time pressure, with consequences they will not personally observe — none of which this day reproduces. The judgement recorded here should not be read as a partial EA-1 rating and the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s non-accumulation rule already prevents it.

**The platform instance, scoped in the strong form.** The day deploys a model to real users through an agent, and reads the platform's own deployment gate as a contrast case.

*Safe.* Reasoning about the difference between a decision made and a decision delegated; about why a binary outcome with an unpublished threshold is not a substitute for a position; about why a deployment you have not read back is a deployment you are assuming; and about why the four decisions at phase 6 point 3 are not four opinions about the same question. In each case the platform supplies a concrete instance of a distinction the day argues on other grounds.

*Unsafe* in three directions. **First, as a description of what the safety gate is for.** It is a harm gate and not a quality gate; a model that passes it has been found not to produce harmful content above a threshold, which says nothing whatever about whether it serves its users, and a student who reads deployability as endorsement has inverted the day. **Second, as a claim the day can verify.** The thresholds are not published and only the binary outcome is recorded, so the day can state what the gate reports and cannot state what it measured — and it says so. **Third, as a general property of model deployment.** The gate applies to fine-tuned models; a student deploying a catalogue model unchanged does not meet it, and would be wrong to conclude that nothing was checked or that something was.

**The unassisted hour, scoped.** *Safe* as evidence about whether a student can form and write a position from artefacts they already hold. *Unsafe* as evidence about whether they could build the artefacts without an agent, which it does not test and does not claim to — conceded in full at §8's objection 3. The comparison is reported and is not used to award a level, which is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s requirement and is stated to students before phase 1 rather than discovered by them afterwards.

The boundary is delivered to students in the day's opening paragraphs and at phase 6 point 3 rather than left to this paper. **The artefact element under which a student applying it outside its safe range would fail is step 3**, whose three failure modes must each carry an affected party and a frequency — a formulation no gate output and no evaluator score can satisfy.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **a user in the room who will be affected**.

Phase 4's fourth question — *what should I know before this affects me* — is asked by someone the model will actually touch, and it cannot be answered with a figure. Students prepare for this question more than for the three technical ones, and their step 6 sentence exists because of it.

The permission not to deploy is the day's second and less obvious mechanism. Told in advance that refusal passes, students stop optimising for an outcome and start reasoning about one, and the phase 3 hour changes character accordingly. The design's observation is that the deploy rate falls and the quality of both decisions rises.

Phase 6 point 4 — what the users said, reported by the module lead — is the day's most-cited moment. Users are consistently more interested in what the model does badly than in what it does well, which reverses the students' preparation priorities and is a more durable lesson than any instruction about audience.

The competence risk is that a student's model is poor and they know it. The specification's indifference to accuracy is stated before phase 1, and a student who deploys nothing and defends the refusal well receives the same level as one who deploys.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items. Item 1 doubles as the module's verification instrument, which is stated in the seminar page rather than concealed — a student who knows the question is also a measurement answers it no less honestly, and the design prefers the disclosure. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction** and is placed before the unassisted hour deliberately: the student predicts what the agent will produce while they still have to produce it themselves, and the prediction is resolved at phase 8 by running the agent on the same brief.

**Phase 2 (30 min).** Extended cumulative retrieval with the module's integration and boundary items. The boundary item — a failure none of the five days would surface — is the fourth instance of this convention and its answers are collected for M10's design.

**Phase 3 (60 min), unassisted.** Individual, factual support only, six required outputs, **no agent in the loop**. Every artefact the decision draws on was built with an agent over four days and is fully available; the material is assisted and the judgement is not. Step 3 is the specification's weighted element. Step 5's threshold requirement is what distinguishes a withdrawal signal from an intention. The provision is defended at §7 and its aim is conceded at §8.

**Phase 4 (60 min).** Fifteen minutes to two assessors and one affected user. **Assessors form a provisional level from the artefact before the defence and record whether the defence moved it** — this is the first occasion designed under rater discipline rule 7, added after [WP-040](wiki/whitepapers/WP-040.md) §8, and the movement record is new instrumentation.

**Phase 5 (35 min).** Deployment for those deploying; an equivalent written task for those not. The equivalence is deliberate: a day in which the non-deploying students watch would communicate that refusal is the lesser path regardless of what the specification says. **The deployment is this day's build** and carries the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement, which on this day span the whole module rather than the phase. **The read-back requirement is new**: the deployment's name, status and quota are read back and the confirmation is part of the artefact, on the module's standing ground that a system you have not observed is a system you are assuming. It matters at S050, where a student who cannot say which deployment served a response cannot investigate one.

**Phase 6 (35 min).** Seven points, assessors absent. Point 2's unattributed split is the day's instruction. **Point 3 is the day's four-case requirement discharged**, and it is placed here rather than at phase 3 for a reason the design considers load-bearing: the students had to make the fourth decision before they could see what distinguished it. Four decisions about one model, three of them made by something that does not know who the users are — the platform's harm gate returning a binary against an unpublished threshold, the evaluator threshold returning a pass label against a default of three, the four infrastructure checks returning four yes-or-no answers, and the student's own position. Only the fourth was defended an hour ago and only the fourth could be. Point 6 states the programme's position on refusal explicitly, after the judgements are made rather than before, so that it reads as a principle rather than as reassurance. **Point 7 states the S050 commitment** with its instrument and its sources named, per instrumentation rule 6.

**Phase 7 (40 min).** Opened by a staff member who deployed and withdrew, with what they had said beforehand. The beforehand detail is the phase's content and is the direct model for step 3. **The agent case is the module's strongest single piece of evidence for the unassisted hour**: a readiness summary that was accurate in every particular, complete against its inputs, and contained no person — no sentence naming who would receive a wrong answer, or how often. The failure modes were absent rather than misstated, and an absence does not fail a review that checks statements. The thing that was missing had never been written down for the agent to find, which is why the student spent an hour writing it without help. **Firewall applies** and the assessors are absent.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Sealed items opened; **item 5 opened and then run**, with the agent given the same six-output brief and all five days of artefacts; the assisted-versus-unassisted comparison recorded in its fixed form; the WP-041 verification reported on both readings; narratives returned with the defence-movement record; and the psychological-safety instrument's fifth administration.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with deployment experience will produce a confident decision and a well-formed artefact. Predicted failure: their three failure modes will be the standard ones — distribution shift, rare classes, data quality — rather than the ones their own evidence supports, because professional practice supplies a template. Countermeasure: the dispositions observation is precisely whether stated failure modes match the student's own evidence, and an assessor holding the artefact first, per rule 7, can check the step 3 claims against the S041 audit and S043 curve before hearing anything.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** A weak model and a first entrustment on a deployment decision is a demanding combination. Countermeasures: accuracy is not assessed; refusal passes; the questions are published; the expected level is stated. Residual risk: their step 3 frequencies may be guesses, and the specification requires an affected party and a frequency rather than a defensible frequency — which is a deliberate lowering, since the alternative excludes students whose evidence cannot support one.

**Phase 2's boundary item is too open.** As at S030 and S040, some students produce nothing. The cohort-level answer is what the design uses.

**Affective failure.** Two.

The first is **the user's question landing as an accusation**. A student presenting a model with three named failure modes to the person who will experience them may become defensive. Countermeasure: the user is briefed to ask rather than to challenge, and the question is published so it is not a surprise.

The second is **the non-deploying student experiencing the day as a failure**, whatever the specification says. Countermeasures: the equivalent phase 5 task, the pre-stated permission, phase 6 point 6's explicit position, and the fact that the assessors' narrative names an observed action rather than an outcome. The design's honest view is that this risk is not fully eliminable and that the deploy rate should be watched: a cohort in which nearly everyone deploys has probably not believed the permission.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by S046 (where do criteria come from, which asks who chose the standard this model was judged against), S050 (where the deployed models' behaviour is reported against the stated failure modes), S060 (shipping retrieval into production), S066 (the release gate), S070 (ship or do not ship — the direct successor at class 5) and S090.

S070 at roughly two quarters is the principal target and the relationship is explicit: S045 is a deployment decision about a component the student built with evidence they generated, and S070 is a release decision about a system under cost and time pressure with evidence they did not fully control. The design treats this day as S070's rehearsal.

**S050's report is the day's own adjudication** — each student's three stated failure modes are compared against what the deployed models actually did, which is the programme's third long-horizon prediction check after S020 and S040.

Retained content: the deployment position's six parts, the withdrawal-signal threshold requirement, and the user-facing sentence. Three items.

Own schedule: two inter-module retrieval sets, then the S050 report.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**Assessment-bearing. The quarter's first entrustment judgement**, on *train, evaluate and deploy a learned component, and state what it will do badly* — a precursor to EA-1, expected 2a–2b, not carried forward from S030 or S040.

Per rule 1, the checked elements are: a stated decision, an evaluation summary with licensing sentence and the uncalibrated clause on any judged score, three failure modes each with party and frequency, an accept statement, a withdrawal signal with threshold, a user-facing sentence, **the confirmed deployment read-back for those deploying**, and **the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement**. **Nothing else is assessed, including accuracy and including whether the model was deployed.**

**A defended decision not to deploy is a pass. A deployment with no stated failure modes is not.**

### The unassisted-work provision, defended

The [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) records a risk in unusually blunt terms: **a student who has only ever built with an agent may be unable to build without one, and this programme cannot currently detect that.** It then names three occasions on which a bounded portion of assessed work is performed without agent assistance, and this is the second of them. Four questions have to be answered for the provision to be a design rather than a gesture.

**What is bounded, and why this hour.** Phase 3's sixty minutes. Everything the decision rests on — the evaluation, the run history, the curve, the threat model, the four-check result — was built with an agent across four days and remains fully available. **The material is assisted; the judgement is not.** The hour was chosen because it is the only part of the day whose content is a position rather than an artefact, and a position is precisely what the programme claims an agent cannot supply. Bounding the training or the deployment instead would measure tool fluency under deprivation, which is not a competence anyone needs and which no employer will ever require.

**Why it must not be used to award a level.** Two reasons, and the second is the stronger. The first is validity: an assisted-versus-unassisted difference is confounded with typing speed, with writing fluency, with English as an additional language, and with how much of the day's anxiety a particular student is carrying at 11am. The second is incentive. **A comparison that affected a level would be a comparison students prepared for**, and the thing it would then measure is preparation for the comparison. Keeping it out of the judgement is what keeps it informative, and it is the same reasoning the programme applies to the phase 1 pretest and to the error clinic's firewall.

**What is actually compared, and how.** At phase 8, after the level is formed and the assessors have gone, each student runs the same six-output brief through Copilot with their four days of artefacts attached, and reads the assisted draft against what they wrote by hand. The comparison is recorded in a fixed six-part form — for each required output, whether the assisted draft was better, worse, or unable to produce it at all. **The form is fixed so that the result can disagree with the design.** A free-text reflection would produce a narrative, and a narrative written by a student who has just been assessed will tend to confirm whatever the day appeared to be arguing.

**What the design expects, stated so that it can be wrong.** The expectation is that the assisted draft is better on the evaluation summary and the accept statement, comparable on the withdrawal signal, and worse on the three failure modes and the user-facing sentence — because the first two are syntheses of material on file and the last two require knowing what a particular person will do with an answer. **If the assisted draft is better on the failure modes, the programme's central claim about what an agent cannot do is wrong.** That is a claim this programme makes in its first paragraph and repeats for three years, and it should be exposed to disconfirmation somewhere. This is the cheapest place to expose it.

**What it does not do**, conceded at §8 objection 3: it does not test whether a student could build without an agent, which is the risk the standard actually names.

### The deployment to real users, defended

The day puts a student-built model in front of people who did not enrol in a course, and the programme should be able to say why that is acceptable. Four conditions hold and all four are stated before phase 1.

**The blast radius is bounded twice.** A feature flag, and a fraction of traffic behind it. The flag is the withdrawal mechanism and the student holds it, which matters more than its technical properties: a student whose withdrawal signal has no mechanism has written an intention, and step 5 requires a threshold precisely so that the mechanism has something to act on.

**The affected parties are told**, under the [Substrate and Infrastructure](wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page's disclosure conditions, and one of them is in the room. The user's question at phase 4 — *what should I know before this affects me* — is not a pedagogical device; it is the disclosure being made in person, to the person, by the student responsible for it.

**The decision precedes the deployment.** Phase 5 follows phase 4, and the judgement is formed before anything ships. A design in which the deployment came first would be assessing a fait accompli, and the student's position would be a justification rather than a decision.

**The prediction is adjudicated.** The three failure modes written today are compared at [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md) against what the deployed models actually did, using the deployment's monitoring metrics and the substrate's incident record. **A model that fails in a way the student named puts them in a different position from one that fails in a way they did not**, and five weeks is short enough that the comparison is about this cohort rather than about the next one.

The residual exposure is real and is not eliminated: a bounded fraction of real people receive a worse answer than they otherwise would, for a term, so that students learn what it is to be responsible for one. The programme's position is that this is the smallest version of that experience it can construct and that a version with no exposure would teach nothing, and §8's objection 4 states the case against.

### Instrumentation

**Item 1 is the [WP-041](wiki/whitepapers/WP-041.md) §5 verification** and is reported to the cohort on both readings — the sealed responses, and the step 3 failure-mode statements coded for specificity, which §8 argues is the better instrument. Item 4's predicted level against assigned is the external-calibration series' third point after S030 and S040. **The defence-movement record is new** and measures how often the oral defence changes a level formed from an artefact, which is the first direct evidence about rule 7's premise. The deploy rate is the permission-credibility measure named in §5. **The assisted-versus-unassisted comparison is reported to the student and to the programme, is not seen by the assessors, and is not used to award a level.** **Item 5's sealed prediction accuracy** is the module's closing frontier datum and is read by the following year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), per instrumentation rule 9.

The frontier statements are a sample and the day says so, per instrumentation rule 3: one cohort, one substrate, one task, five days, on a module whose builds staff designed to contain known traps.

Dispositions: **integrity**, in whether step 3's failure modes match the student's own S041 audit, S042 run count, S043 curve and S044 check results — checkable, because all four are on file.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: item 1 is a sealed self-report and the design has made it the module's headline verification.**

At its strongest: [WP-041](wiki/whitepapers/WP-041.md) §5 deferred the module's negativity risk to this day and named item 1 as the instrument. Item 1 asks students what they are willing to claim, in a sealed envelope, on a day when they know a deployment decision is coming and know the question will be reported. The response is shaped by what they intend to defend two hours later, not by what they believe. And the design has disclosed that item 1 is a measurement, which — whatever the honesty argument for disclosure — guarantees that the answer is composed with the measurement in mind.

The rebuttal is that the alternative instruments are worse. An undisclosed measurement is a deception the programme has committed against; a post-hoc question measures the day rather than the module's cumulative state; and a question asked of a cohort not about to make a decision measures nothing.

The concession is that the design has, once again, **built the instrument it could rather than the instrument it needed** — the same finding [WP-029](wiki/whitepapers/WP-029.md) §8 recorded about M06's fatalism verification, in the same form, one quarter later. What would actually measure this is the step 3 failure-mode statements, which are produced under assessment, are checkable against evidence, and do not depend on self-report: **a cohort whose failure modes are all hedged non-claims has the negativity problem, and a cohort whose failure modes are specific does not.** That reading is available from an artefact the day already collects, and the design named the weaker instrument instead. The verification should be re-specified against step 3.

**Objection 2: the design has now twice retrofitted a rule onto a day it had already written, and both retrofits are visible in the wiki.**

At its strongest: [S041](wiki/seminars/S041.md)'s specification clause was rewritten after [WP-041](wiki/whitepapers/WP-041.md) found it self-contradictory, and [S044](wiki/seminars/S044.md)'s step 1 acquired an infrastructure clause after [WP-044](wiki/whitepapers/WP-044.md) found an outright rule violation. Both carry parenthetical notes recording the correction. Meanwhile [S042](wiki/seminars/S042.md)'s rule 3 failure — a search log required and withheld from the only phase positioned to check it — was identified and left standing. The design is therefore fixing some violations and recording others, with no stated criterion, which means the wiki's compliance state is a function of which whitepaper happened to press hardest.

This objection is correct and the design has no principled criterion. What it has is an implicit one, visible in the pattern: **outright contradictions and rule violations have been fixed; findings that require a design judgement have been recorded.** S041's clause contradicted its own justification. S044 violated a written rule. S042's is a judgement about whether a phase should receive a third document, which is a design decision rather than a compliance failure.

That distinction is defensible and it has never been stated, which means it is not a criterion but a rationalisation constructed here. The honest position is that **the design should hold a compliance register** — the known violations of its own rules, their status, and the reason for each fix-or-record decision — and that it does not, with the consequence that its rule-application behaviour is only reconstructible by reading forty-five whitepapers. This is the third pattern-level finding about how rules propagate through this design, after [WP-042](wiki/whitepapers/WP-042.md) §8 and [WP-044](wiki/whitepapers/WP-044.md) §8, and it is the one that suggests a mechanism rather than a habit.

**Objection 3: the unassisted hour is aimed at ground the design chose because it expects to win there.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn and it is the one this paper finds hardest to answer.

At its strongest: the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) names a specific risk — **a student who has only ever built with an agent may be unable to build without one.** That is a claim about building. The provision's response is to remove the agent from sixty minutes of writing prose about artefacts the student already holds, having built every one of them with an agent over the preceding four days. **A student who is dependent in exactly the way the standard fears would sail through that hour.** They would have a working model, a bounded estimate, a justified capacity and a threat model, all agent-built, and would spend the hour deciding what to do about them — which is the one thing their dependency does not impair.

Worse, the design has *predicted* which way the comparison will fall, and has chosen as the unassisted portion the exact activity it believes an agent performs worst. That is not a measurement; it is a demonstration with a control group. **The programme has three occasions on which to test its largest self-identified risk and has spent the second of them on ground it selected for being favourable.**

The objection is conceded in full. There is no rebuttal on the merits and the design does not attempt one.

**Two cheap fixes are made and neither addresses the objection.** The comparison is recorded in a fixed six-part form rather than as a reflection, so that the result can disagree with the design's prediction rather than being narrated into agreement with it; and the prediction itself is stated in §7 in falsifiable terms, so that an assisted draft which beats the student on failure modes is a recorded refutation of the programme's central claim rather than an awkward anecdote. Both make the instrument honest about what it measures. **Neither makes it measure the right thing.**

**What follows is a commitment rather than a repair.** Under instrumentation rule 6, a deferred response must name its occasion and that occasion must carry an artefact element that consumes the result. **The occasion is [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md)**, the third and last of the unassisted occasions, and the element it must carry is a bounded *build* performed without agent assistance — small, scoped, and on a task whose shape is known — with its result compared against the same student's S010 and S045 records. That is the only occasion left on which the standard's actual risk can be tested, and the fact that the programme reaches its final week before testing it is a finding about the design's sequencing that this paper records and does not defend.

**Objection 4: a bounded fraction of real people receive a worse answer so that students can learn what responsibility feels like.**

At its strongest: the disclosure conditions, the feature flag and the traffic fraction are all real and none of them changes the fact that the substrate's users are being used. They did not enrol. The benefit accrues to the students and to the programme; the cost, however small, falls on people whose alternative was a system that worked slightly better. Calling the exposure bounded describes its size and not its justification, and a programme that would not accept this arrangement from a vendor should examine why it accepts it from itself.

The design does not consider this objection answerable by argument, only by design, and states its position plainly: **an engineering education in which nothing a student builds ever reaches anyone is an education in which the central competence is never exercised.** Every element of the day that costs the programme something — the refusal-passes rule, the decision preceding the deployment, the user in the room, the S050 adjudication — exists to make the exposure purchase something the student could not otherwise obtain.

**One cheap fix is made.** The withdrawal signal must carry a threshold *and* the mechanism is in the student's hands, so that the person who accepted the exposure is the person who can end it. A design in which withdrawal required a staff decision would place the cost on users and the control elsewhere.

**What remains unfixed is that the programme has no measure of the harm it causes here.** It measures the deploy rate, the defence movement and the S050 prediction match; it does not measure what the affected users experienced, and the [Substrate and Infrastructure](wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page's disclosure conditions require notice rather than consequence-tracking. **S050 is the natural home** — it already reports the deployed models' behaviour against the students' stated failure modes to the people affected — and adding one question to that report, asking the affected parties what it cost them, would cost nothing and is not currently specified. It is recorded here rather than repaired, because specifying another day's instrument is a design judgement and this paper has already made two.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A large majority of item 1 responses will contain at least one substantive claim. *Instrument:* item 1 coded for substantive versus hedged content. **This is the [WP-041](wiki/whitepapers/WP-041.md) §5 verification as currently specified, and §8 argues it should be superseded by a step 3 reading.**

**Prediction 2.** The deploy rate will be below three-quarters. *Instrument:* phase 3 step 1 decisions. **A near-universal deploy rate indicates the permission not to deploy was not believed, whatever the specification said.**

**Prediction 3.** In fewer than a third of cases will the defence move the provisional level formed from the artefact. *Instrument:* the rule 7 movement record. **This is the first direct evidence about rule 7's premise; frequent movement would indicate either that artefacts are uninformative or that defences are doing work the rule assumes they should not.**

**Prediction 4.** Students with deployment experience will produce step 3 failure modes rated less well-matched to their own S041 and S043 evidence than students without. *Instrument:* blind matching of step 3 claims against the student's own prior artefacts, split by the admission background survey.

**Prediction 5.** In the assisted-versus-unassisted comparison, the assisted draft will be rated better on the evaluation summary and the accept statement for a majority of students, and worse on the three failure modes and the user-facing sentence for a majority. *Instrument:* the fixed six-part comparison form, blind-rated. **This is the programme's central claim about what an agent cannot do, exposed to disconfirmation. If the assisted draft is rated better on the failure modes, the claim is wrong and the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s first paragraph requires revision.**

**Prediction 6.** At [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md), fewer than half of the deployed models will have failed in a way their student named at step 3, and the match rate will be higher for students whose failure modes cited their own S041 audit than for those whose did not. *Instrument:* the S050 comparison of stated failure modes against monitoring metrics and incident records, coded for match and split by whether step 3 cited prior evidence. **A very high match rate indicates the failure modes were chosen for checkability rather than for likelihood, which is a different failure from the one the design is guarding against and would require step 3 to be rewritten.**

**Prediction 7.** A large majority of item 5 sealed predictions will say the agent will produce a complete and accurate summary, and a small minority will name the specific absence — that no sentence will contain a person. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded against the agent output each student generates at phase 8.

Second: **no agreement-with-human figure is published for any evaluator**, so the uncalibrated clause carries into the evaluation summary at step 2 and the phase 6 table reports the pass label without endorsing it. Third, recorded and not taught: **no rate is published for how often the safety gate blocks a model**, so nothing here licenses a claim about how often the platform's decision and the student's would differ. Consistent with the folder-wide asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), **the documentation states gates, categories, defaults and routing behaviour throughout and states no accuracy anywhere.** No detection or false-positive rate is asserted for any Defender or Purview capability, because none is published.

The §8 objection-1 concession states that the module's headline verification is a sealed self-report contaminated by anticipated assessment, that this repeats [WP-029](wiki/whitepapers/WP-029.md) §8's finding one quarter later, and that a better instrument — the step 3 statements — is already collected; **both readings are now reported at phase 8.** The objection-2 concession states that the design fixes some of its own rule violations and records others with no stated criterion, reconstructs the implicit one, and concludes that **a compliance register should exist**. **The objection-3 concession states that the unassisted hour is aimed at ground the design chose because it expects to win there, that it does not test the risk the standard actually names, that both cheap fixes make the instrument honest without making it correct, and commits [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md) to carrying a bounded unassisted build compared against the same student's S010 and S045 records — while recording that reaching the final week before testing the design's largest self-identified risk is itself a sequencing finding.** The objection-4 concession states that real users bear a small real cost for the programme's benefit, that the design answers this by construction rather than by argument, and that **the programme has no measure of the harm it causes here** — naming S050's report as the natural and unspecified home for one.
