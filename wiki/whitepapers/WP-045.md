## In plain terms

This is the stage's first assessment occasion and it marks something narrower than the title suggests.

**Whether the increment works is not the subject.** The subject is whether you can say where its boundary was, who moved it, when, and what you let past when the date stopped moving.

Three assessed elements are unusual and they are the ones that matter.

**Every departure from the slice definition must be marked and dated.** A departure discovered at shipping is recorded as a departure, not as a revision. The design's position is that **an undated exclusion was not a decision, it was an outcome**, and dating is the cheapest available test of which one happened.

**The capacity statement travels unrevised.** A statement that can be edited at assembly will be, and the softened version is always the one that circulates.

**The what-this-does-not-establish page must be specific.** Not *this is an early increment* — named inferences: that it works at the volume described, that it behaves on data nobody saw, that the recipient's colleagues would use it. **That page is the pack's most valuable one and the first deleted before circulation**, which is why it is marked here as it was at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md).

The defence is adversarial in one specific way. **The assessor names something in your increment and asks why it was not an exclusion.** A candidate who can only answer *because it was needed* has not drawn a boundary. A candidate who concedes it could have been excluded and says what that would have cost has answered properly, and that is a pass.

## Abstract

This paper defends closing the delivery module with an assessment of the boundary of an increment rather than of the increment. The argument is that a delivery is distinguished from a result by whether its exclusions were decided, that dating is the only cheap evidence of decision available, and that the artefacts most likely to be softened at assembly — the review's unread boundary, the capacity statement, the refusals — are exactly the ones that carry the stage's content. The occasion installs an eight-part increment pack assembled from four prior artefacts, defended against an assessor holding only the proof pack and the slice definition, and closes its specification with the statement that nothing else is assessed. The paper concedes that the occasion cannot mark whether the boundary was well drawn, that no source supplies a basis for such a mark, and that requiring exclusions to be dated rewards learners with better note-keeping as much as learners with better judgement.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

An increment has shipped. Two forces now act on the account of it, and they act in the same direction.

**The first is assembly.** A pack is written after the fortnight, by somebody who knows how it came out, from records made under pressure. Every rough edge has an obvious smoothing available: the blocker that was worked around becomes a decision, the review that was cut short becomes a review, the refusal that was actually a deferral becomes a refusal.

**The second is the increment's own political effect.** The corpus behind this stage records it accurately: working software is hard to argue with, so resistance dissolves in front of a running increment in a way it does not in front of a document. **That is true, and it is the mechanism by which an increment ends an argument without settling the disagreement.** A pack assembled after a successful demonstration inherits the room's agreement and records it as consensus.

The occasion's response is to assess the properties those two forces destroy — dates, unread boundaries, refusals, unrevised statements — and to make the defence about the boundary rather than about the outcome.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The occasion assembles four artefacts and cannot be attempted without them: the slice definition from [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md), the scope ledger from [S042](/wiki/seminars/S042.md), the specification from [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md), and the review record and capacity statement from [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md). The commitment from [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md) is what the increment is answerable to.

The prior the occasion works against is that shipping is the achievement. Candidates arrive expecting the increment's existence to carry the assessment, and the design's first move is to make the increment's contents unassessed.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The module's evaluative peak. The intrinsic content is retrospective; the load is in defending a small thing in front of somebody entitled to say it should have been smaller.

**3.2 Why a dated exclusion is a decision.** Intent is unobservable and reconstructible. A date is neither. **The date does not prove the reasoning was good; it proves the reasoning happened before the outcome**, which is the only property of a decision that survives hindsight.

**3.3 Why the capacity statement must be unrevised.** [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) produced it under a specific pressure. Any subsequent edit is made with knowledge of how the fortnight ended, and the edit direction is predictable. **Freezing the artefact preserves an estimate made in ignorance of its outcome**, which is a different instrument from one made after.

**3.4 Why the recipient must have been able to refuse.** This is [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md)'s requirement re-checked at the point where it can be verified rather than asserted, and it is re-checked because an increment's recipient frequently changes between definition and delivery, always in the direction of somebody more available and less able to decline.

**3.5 Why nothing about correctness is marked.** No source behind this stage supplies a basis for marking whether a slice was right, whether an exclusion was safe, or whether an increment was adequate. **The corpus reports no failed delivery and no post-deployment record; the playbook offers no comparative evidence.** An element marking those things would be the assessor's opinion in a specification's clothing, and [WP-040](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-040.md) §8 established the precedent for declining it.

**3.6 Why the occasion certifies nothing, and says so.**

The assessment literature this programme draws on separates **ad-hoc entrustment**, taken continuously and informally whenever the work matches what somebody can do, from **summative entrustment**, which "has the nature of certification" and is a decision about future responsibilities. The standard for the second is explicit: it **"should include an agreement between multiple observers that this entrustment is justified, to avoid subjective bias, and multiple observations"** ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)).

**This occasion has one assessor and one observation. It therefore certifies nothing about future responsibility and the paper states that rather than letting the format imply otherwise.** What it marks is a closed specification, once, in front of somebody.

One mechanism is borrowed intact. The **entrustment-based discussion** is a short conversation after the activity that "should probe the student's deep understanding of what was done" and **must include what-if questions**, because retrospective explanation shows only that the candidate understood the case they met, while counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases they did not. **That is the warrant for the assessor naming something and asking why it was not an exclusion** — it is not adversarial theatre, it is the only part of the format that reaches the unencountered cases, and §4 states it in advance for that reason.

**Limits:** a primer by the concept's originator — guidance rather than evaluation — whose empirical items are reported from prior studies rather than generated by it.

**3.7 Why the specification is closed, and where the grading machinery comes from.**

The closed specification is borrowed from a running course rather than invented. **Specification grading** marks components pass or fail against a published specification with no partial credit, and its stated justification is agency rather than efficiency: a clear specification lets a candidate decide knowingly to skip a component with known consequences. **Oral verification** earns the grade by explaining the work to somebody, which is the move that makes the artefact's provenance stop being the evidence — and the same course draws the consequence explicitly, permitting unrestricted tooling in producing work because the explanation must be one's own ([research/08-assessment-epas/cmu-oral-defense-and-token-economy-patterns.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/cmu-oral-defense-and-token-economy-patterns.md)).

**This occasion adopts both, which is why nothing in §7 concerns how the pack was produced.** A candidate may assemble it with any assistance available. They may not have the defence assisted, and the defence is where the marks are.

**Limits:** one institution's published course design, with no outcome evaluation attached to any of its mechanisms.

**3.8 Why the pack is mostly prose, and what the assessor will actually do with it.**

When assessors judge an accumulated evidentiary record, **"assessors mainly relied on narrative feedback to inform their judgment… Grades and qualifications were merely used to confirm impressions based on narratives"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). The numbers were a check on a conclusion already formed from the prose.

Two further findings shape the design directly. **Anchoring is real** — assessors "were inclined to stick to their initial judgments even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence", and their final judgements were more elaborate than their first but not substantially different. **The order of the pack therefore matters more than its completeness**, which is why the departures and the what-this-does-not-establish page are required early rather than appended. And assessors reaching identical outcomes frequently differ entirely in their reasoning, driven by beliefs about which evidence is trustworthy and by performance theories that **"tended to deviate from the formal assessment criteria"** — which is the strongest available argument for a specification that says what is marked and then says that nothing else is.

**Limits:** small qualitative think-aloud studies using mock portfolios in a clinical education setting; the primary study is not by the author it is usually attributed to; several of the field's most-cited works are paywalled and were not consulted, and the substitution is recorded deliberately.

**3.9 Why the gate is the anchor, and why a gate that has never fired is a finding rather than a failure.**

The occasion needs one platform property and only one: **something between the built artefact and its recipient that is capable of stopping the release.** A hosted agent supplies it, because there is a build, an artefact and a promotion step for a gate to sit in front of; a declarative agent with no agent compute has fewer places to put one ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)).

The design's contribution is the **two honest states** rule, and it exists because the usual third state is dishonest. A gate that has never blocked anything is not thereby a bad gate; it is a gate whose evidential status is unestablished, and a pack presenting it as a control has claimed something it cannot support. **Requiring the candidate to choose — it blocked something and here is what, or it never has and here is why I believe it would — converts an untested control into a stated position**, which is assessable, whereas an untested control presented as tested is not.

The platform makes both states cheap, because results carry a `reason` string alongside the pass or fail, and **the reason is what makes a judgement auditable rather than merely numeric** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). It also makes the threshold visible as **a policy choice separable from the measurement**, which is why §7 marks a named and justified threshold rather than a particular value.

**3.10 Why the pack must state the smallest regression the gate could have caught.**

A gate is a claim about detection capability, and an evaluation is an experiment. **Power analysis before the run is what turns "we tested and found nothing" into a statement about the system rather than about the test** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). Applied to a release gate, the consequence is blunt: **a gate whose case set could not detect the difference it exists to prevent will pass a broken increment and emit a green artefact saying so**, and nothing downstream distinguishes that from a genuine pass.

Two further disciplines are required of the pack rather than of the candidate's judgement. **Paired comparison** against the incumbent on the same cases removes variation the two versions share and costs nothing. And **variance must not be suppressed by changing a setting whose purpose is something else** — lowering a sampling temperature to stabilise a gate shifts what is measured. The platform's **repetitions** mechanism states the underlying point in its own documentation: one sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement.

**Limits:** the measurement source is analytical rather than empirical; its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions; and it addresses comparing scores rather than choosing what to score, so a tight interval around a badly chosen gate criterion is still meaningless. **No number from it appears here.**

**3.11 Why the limits page must name inferences drawn from the production literature and not only from delivery practice.**

The what-this-does-not-establish page fails in a predictable way: candidates name limits of scale and data and miss the ones that are properties of systems. The production literature supplies the two that packs most often omit. **Training-and-serving skew** — different preprocessing at build and at serve — is described as a common failure that is hard to detect **because the system continues to return plausible answers**; and **monitoring divides into functional and non-functional**, where the second matters regardless of the first, since "if the system is down, it really doesn't matter how good your model is" ([research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md)). The same source supplies the threshold at which any of it is warranted — **not every project needs a pipeline, and the test is whether the thing has users** — which this occasion's increment satisfies by construction, since it has a recipient who could refuse it.

**Limits:** a practitioner survey by authors mostly from one large organisation, with that organisation's toolchain as the default and both worked pipelines built in it; alternatives are named briefly. **Strong evidence about how one organisation operates production systems and no evidence at all as a comparative evaluation of practices.**

**3.12 Why a departure is recorded rather than absorbed.**

The cheapest discipline in the occasion is borrowed from an internal delivery standard: once a specification is settled, a change to it is **recorded as a decision rather than made as an edit** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-fde-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-fde-delivery-practice.md)). The reason is the same one §3.2 gives for dating: **an edited artefact loses the fact that it changed, and with it the reason.** The same standard requires that completion include a human having reviewed every generated artefact, which is where §7's residue-holder element comes from.

**Limits:** internal, paraphrased and never linked; a standard still being settled, reporting no outcome, no evaluation and no comparison against any other way of working.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 2's second clause is the occasion's centre of gravity.** *On what day did you decide it?* Almost every candidate can name an exclusion; far fewer can date one, and the dating exercise is the work movement's first and longest step because most of the dates are not written down anywhere.

**The assessor holds only the proof pack and the slice definition.** They have not seen the ledger, the specification, the review record or the capacity statement before the defence, which means those four artefacts have to carry themselves.

**The specification is closed and its exclusions are long**: not whether the increment works, not whether the recipient was pleased, not code quality, not counts of anything, not whether the slice was right, not whether the exclusions should have been excluded, not presentation.

**Two elements have escape clauses rather than thresholds.** An increment received only by the delivery team is submitted with that stated as its finding; a review record in which everything was read is checked against its own time budget. **Both are passes**, and stating that in the session prevents candidates fabricating the harder-looking answer.

**The defence's adversarial move is the assessor naming a candidate for exclusion.** A candidate who concedes and prices the concession passes. **This is stated in the session in advance**, for the same reason [WP-040](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-040.md) §4 states its analogue: a candidate who suspects that conceding is a trap will defend work they know was unnecessary.

**4.1 What the platform workflow is for, and why it is the only place in the module where the tooling can refuse.**

Every earlier session in [M09](/wiki/modules/M09.md) uses its anchor to make something *visible*. This one uses its anchor to make something *stop*.

The design's requirement is minimal and deliberately so: a build, an artefact, and an evaluation stage between the artefact and the recipient whose failure prevents promotion. **Everything else about the deployment is unassessed.** What the arrangement produces that no written artefact can is a moment at which a control the candidate built exercises authority over a release the candidate wanted — and the candidate's account of that moment, or of its absence, is the assessable object.

The workflow also closes a loop opened at [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md). A slice that began as a declaratively configured agent and now runs as containerised code has crossed a published boundary, and **that crossing is a departure with a date** — one of the departures assessors most often find unmarked, because it feels like an implementation detail and is a hosting decision.

**The anchor's limits are recorded in §7's ninth element rather than left to judgement.** No evaluator's agreement with human judgement is published anywhere in this documentation, so **a pack asserting one has asserted something nobody has measured**, which is exactly the failure the element about unmeasured magnitudes exists to catch — arriving, at the close of the module, in the candidate's own artefact rather than in somebody else's document.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The tidy pack.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* the unrevised capacity statement, the requirement that the review record still say what was not read, and the standard's warning sign — the pack reads better than the fortnight went.

**The undated exclusion.** *Countermeasure:* the dating exercise as the first step of the work movement, and the assessed element requiring departures to carry dates.

**The internal recipient.** *Countermeasure:* the assessed element requiring the recipient to have been in a position to refuse, with the escape clause that makes the honest answer submittable.

**The retrospective specification.** Tests present, written after. *Countermeasure:* the element requiring the pack to *show* that tests arrived in the same change rather than assert it, and the standard's warning sign — no check in the pack has ever been seen red.

**The increment that ended the argument.** Everyone agreed once it ran and nobody recorded what they had disagreed about. *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign only. **This is the design's weakest countermeasure** — there is no assessable trace of an argument that stopped — and §8 does not defend it.

**The softened capacity statement.** *Countermeasure:* the unrevised requirement, which is checkable against the [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) submission and is therefore the specification's most mechanical element.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**This is an assessment occasion.** It generates specification evidence and no entrustment judgement — the programme's entrustment decisions begin at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md).

Nine elements, each checked present and adequate or not: every departure from the slice definition is marked and dated, with a departure discovered at shipping recorded as a departure rather than a revision; the recipient is named and was in a position to refuse, or the pack states an internally received increment as its finding; tests arrived with the code in the same change, shown rather than asserted; the review record still says what was not read, and a record in which everything was read is checked against its stated time budget; something was refused, in the ledger or the review, and the pack names it; the capacity statement is unrevised and is reconciled against what the increment produced; the untestable residue has a holder and the pack says whether that person looked; nothing in the pack asserts a magnitude that was not measured by a named instrument; and the what-this-does-not-establish page exists and names inferences rather than gesturing at limits.

**Three further elements are checked, added with the release gate and specific to it.** The gate is in one of its two honest states — it blocked something and the pack says what, with the reason recorded, or it has never blocked anything and the pack says why the candidate believes it would. The pack states the smallest regression the gate could have caught, or states that it cannot and treats that as a finding. And the evaluation's split strategy and every threshold are named and justified, with **no evaluator's agreement with human judgement asserted anywhere**, because none is published.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Whether the increment works is not marked. Whether the recipient was pleased is not marked. The quality, elegance or size of the code is not marked. The number of items shipped, refused or recorded is not marked. Whether the slice was the right slice is not marked. Whether the exclusions should have been excluded is not marked. Whether the gate ever fired is not marked — only whether the pack states which of the two honest states it is in. How the pack was produced is not marked, and any assistance may be used in producing it. The pack's presentation is not marked.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: marking dates rather than judgement assesses note-keeping, and will select for candidates with good administrative habits over candidates with good engineering instincts.**

At its strongest: two candidates make identical decisions for identical reasons. One keeps a running record and one does not. The specification marks the first as having decided and the second as having drifted, and the difference between them is clerical. Worse, the fix is trivially gameable — a candidate who learns that dates are marked will produce dates — so the element selects for candidates who read the specification carefully, which is not the competence being assessed.

**Substantially conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to this occasion.** Two partial answers. Dating is required against the ledger, the review threads and the specification, which are artefacts produced during the fortnight for other reasons and are therefore harder to backfill coherently than a standalone date column. And the design's claim is deliberately weak: §3.2 says the date proves the reasoning happened before the outcome, not that it was good. **The declined fix is to mark the quality of the exclusion reasoning instead.** It is declined because no source behind this stage supplies a basis for it — the corpus reports no failed delivery, the playbook offers no comparative evidence — so the element would be marker opinion, and the design would rather assess something narrow and real than something broad and invented. **The gameability is accepted and disclosed.**

**Objection 2: freezing the capacity statement assesses a candidate's earlier self and punishes learning.**

At its strongest: a candidate who wrote a poor capacity statement at [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) and understands the problem far better by [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md) is required to submit the poor one. That is the opposite of what a formative sequence should do, and it teaches candidates to write cautious, unfalsifiable statements at [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) because they will be held to them.

**Conceded in part.** The mitigation is that the element requires the unrevised statement **plus a reconciliation** against what actually happened, so the learning is submitted alongside the estimate rather than instead of it, and a candidate whose reconciliation shows they now understand the problem better has satisfied the element well. **The declined fix is to allow the statement to be revised with the original retained in an appendix.** It is declined because an appendix is not read at a defence and the revised version becomes the operative one, which is the exact softening the element exists to prevent. **The incentive towards cautious statements at [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) is real**, and [WP-044](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-044.md) §9 prediction 2 is the instrument that would detect it.

**Objection 3: adding a release gate to an assessment occasion makes the marks depend on a platform, and the occasion now fails for reasons that have nothing to do with the candidate.**

At its strongest: the three gate elements require a deployment. A candidate whose customer estate forbids the platform, whose subscription is not provisioned, whose quota is exhausted, or whose slice was correctly built as a declarative agent with nowhere obvious to put a gate, now cannot satisfy three of twelve elements for reasons that are not about their engineering. **The occasion has imported a dependency on the most perishable material in the programme into the one place where a candidate's progression is decided** — and this repository's own reading records that documentation as read through search excerpts, with several tables truncated, and with product names and capability boundaries actively changing. Worse, a gate is precisely the kind of element an assessor can check quickly, so the mechanical elements will crowd out the narrative ones in a defence where narrative is what the assessment literature says carries the judgement.

**Conceded on the dependency and declined on the remedy.** Two answers. The elements are written about the candidate's **account** rather than about the deployment: the two-honest-states element is satisfied by a pack that says the gate never blocked anything and why the candidate believes it would, which requires no deployment to have blocked and no platform to have cooperated; and the smallest-regression element is satisfied by an honest statement that it cannot be answered. **A candidate with no gate at all satisfies all three by saying so and saying what follows** — which is the same escape-clause pattern §4 uses for the internal recipient and the fully read review, and it is stated in the session in advance for the same reason. **The declined fix is to remove the gate elements and assess the pack alone.** It is declined because the module's entire argument is that a practice which only survives calm conditions is not a practice, and a release control that has never been tested against a release nobody wanted stopped is the purest available instance of that: assessing it on paper would teach the module's thesis in the one place the module could have demonstrated it. **The crowding-out risk is real and undefended — the design has no instrument for how an assessor allocates attention within a defence — and prediction 7 is written to detect it.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most candidates will be unable to date a majority of their exclusions from records made during the fortnight. *Instrument:* the exclusion list's date column, with each date classified *evidenced from a contemporaneous artefact* / *asserted* / *absent* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Capacity statements will be reconciled optimistically — candidates will conclude that supply was adequate more often than their change volumes support. *Instrument:* the reconciliation against the pack's own change record, by a second reader.

**Prediction 3.** A substantial number of packs will name a recipient different from the one in the [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md) slice definition, and the substitution will be towards somebody less able to refuse. *Instrument:* the two recipient fields, paired per candidate, with the change classified *more able to refuse* / *less able* / *equivalent*.

**Prediction 4.** Candidates who concede the assessor's proposed exclusion will not score lower on any other element than those who defend against it. *Instrument:* element-level marks, paired against the concession outcome. **If this fails, the rule that conceding is a pass is not operating**, and the assessor briefing rather than the specification is at fault.

**Prediction 5.** Most packs will report a gate that has never blocked anything, and a majority of those will be unable to name the smallest regression it could have caught. *Instrument:* the two gate fields, coded together per pack. **A gate that has never fired is not a failure of the candidate**; a gate that has never fired and cannot be characterised is a failure of the pack, and this prediction separates them.

**Prediction 6.** At least one pack per cohort will assert or imply an evaluator's agreement with human judgement despite the specification forbidding it and the documentation publishing none. *Instrument:* the pack, scanned for claims about evaluator validity, coded *asserted* / *implied* / *absent* by a second reader. **This predicts the failure of the module's most-repeated prohibition**, and it is included because a prohibition whose breach rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 7.** Assessors will spend more of the defence on the three gate elements than their share of the specification warrants, and packs with a fired gate will attract disproportionate attention relative to packs without one. *Instrument:* timed coding of the defence by element, against each element's share of the specification. **This predicts the failure §8's third objection names**, and a positive result would require the assessor briefing to change rather than the elements to be removed.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That an undated exclusion was not a decision; that a statement which can be edited at assembly will be; and that an increment which ends an argument has not settled a disagreement.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about whether the boundary was well drawn. No source behind this stage supplies a benchmark for that and the occasion does not invent one.

**The concession worth remembering.** This occasion assesses something narrow and real in preference to something broad and invented, and the narrow thing is partly clerical. If you leave able to tell a well-drawn boundary from a well-documented one, you have acquired something the specification could not test.
