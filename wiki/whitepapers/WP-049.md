## In plain terms

[S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) was about code you cannot explain. This session is about **behaviour** you cannot explain — the executor did something you did not ask for, or failed to do something you did, and you have no account of why.

The two are different problems and only one of them can be read. Code sits still while you study it. Behaviour happened once, under conditions you did not fully record, on a host and a model that will both have moved by next term.

There is a substantial industry of instrumentation for this — traces, spans, logs, dashboards, evaluation over stored runs — and the session uses it. **But the session opens on what the instrumentation does not supply, and that absence is the teaching object rather than a complaint.**

**No measure is published of how often the agent's iteration converges, and none of how often it reports completion on work that is wrong.** Not a poor figure; no figure. **So you are operating a system whose base rate of confident false completion is unknown to you and, so far as this repository can establish, unpublished by the people who built it.** Every practice in the session follows from that one fact: you cannot compute a rate from your own week, so every entry in your register is one observation under one pin, and the sentence recording the missing measure sits at the top of the artefact rather than in a footnote.

One correction is carried forward because learners consistently mis-remember it in the reassuring direction. **The approval prompt before a terminal command or a non-built-in tool is a dismissible default, not a boundary** — auto-confirmable per session, per solution or permanently, with the vendor's own quickstart material instructing the reader to select the autopilot option, which removes approvals entirely. **The thing that looks like the last line of defence is a preference the documentation recommends switching off.**

The pinned configuration corpus supplies the review vocabulary and, more usefully, the honesty. **Orientation is separated from judgement**, **depth is scaled to assessed risk**, **the lenses are fixed** and **severity is normalised** so that two reviewers produce comparable findings. Then it tells you what its own checks are worth: its behavioural graders test **output shape, not correctness**, and some checks are **advisory** while others are **authoritative** — with which one a check is depending on **the tier it runs in rather than on the check itself.** The revision this programme pins is not all-green: one job failed, most were skipped, and none of that is visible in a summary.

The session's hardest item is the last one. **What would you have to see to conclude the executor's explanation of itself was true?** The obvious move — ask it why — produces another output from the same process, subject to exactly the scrutiny you were trying to shortcut. **An explanation you cannot corroborate is a hypothesis with good grammar.**

## Abstract

This paper defends a session organised around a documented absence and assessing an investigation's stopping rule rather than its conclusion. The argument runs in four steps. That unexplained executor behaviour is a normal working condition rather than an exceptional one, so a design treating it as exceptional teaches a disposition its graduates will abandon. That the base rate against which any single anomaly would have to be interpreted is unpublished, so an observation is not a rate and the artefact must say so at the top rather than imply otherwise. That the two available failures are opposite — accepting a self-explanation, and spending an engagement on an anomaly of no consequence — so a design guarding against only one produces the other. And that the distinction between a check with a consequence and a check without one is a property of where the check runs, is invisible in a summary, and is the distinction most likely to be misread. The session installs an inexplicability register with a silence note and pre-written stopping rules, a check inventory whose labels must be established rather than assumed, and an evaluation over stored traces rather than a re-run. The paper concedes that the register cannot distinguish a diligent investigation from a well-written account of one, that everything observed is a property of a single pinned configuration, and that the gap it formerly declared — no research note covering the product surface its own anchor names — **is now closed, while the two silences that stood beside it are confirmed rather than repaired**: no measure of tracing completeness is published, so a trace is a record and never a stated sample, and no measure of self-correction convergence exists.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

An engineer working with a delegate will regularly be unable to explain what the delegate did. That is not a failure state; **it is the ordinary condition**, and a programme that treated it as exceptional would teach a disposition its graduates would abandon in a fortnight.

What is teachable is what you do about it, and there are two ways to get it wrong that pull in opposite directions.

**The first is accepting the explanation you were given.** Asking the executor why produces a fluent, plausible, internally consistent account, and there is no signal in the account distinguishing a true one from a confabulated one.

**The second is investigating forever.** An anomaly with no consequence can absorb a day, and the day comes out of the increment. An engineer who investigates everything is as unemployable as one who investigates nothing.

There is a third problem sitting behind both, and it is the one this repository cares most about. **The instruments that appear to resolve the question mostly do not.** A green check tells you a check passed. Whether that is also a statement about the work depends on what the check does, and whether a failing check would have stopped anything depends on where it runs.

And there is a fourth, which the session's anchor is chosen to make unavoidable. **Even a perfect investigation of a single anomaly cannot tell you whether the anomaly was unusual**, because the base rate is not published. A team that has seen one confident false completion does not know whether it has seen the only one this year or the first of many. **That is not a gap the team can close by working harder, and a design that implied otherwise would be teaching learners to manufacture a rate out of an anecdote.**

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** Every professional has met a tool doing something inexplicable. What is new is the register, the silence note, the stopping rule, and the requirement to establish a check's tier empirically.

It assumes [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s adversarial disposition, which the session deliberately re-installs on top of the instrumental one the module has been building. It assumes [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md)'s review record, which is the instrument being turned on an executor. It assumes [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md)'s audit, because a region you cannot explain and a behaviour you cannot explain need to be told apart.

The prior the session works against is that unexplained behaviour indicates a defect somewhere findable. **Often there is no finding available**, and the professional skill is deciding that on purpose rather than by exhaustion.

**This is a class 3 session:** nobody supplies the anomaly, nobody supplies the stopping rule, and nobody says which checks matter. The learner decides all three, and deciding to ship an unexplained behaviour is a correct answer provided the decision is recorded with what will be watched.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate intrinsic load and a genuine cognitive-flexibility demand: the session requires the learner to hold two dispositions towards the same object and switch between them deliberately.

**3.2 Why observability is the frame rather than debugging.** The production machine-learning literature imports the idea from control theory and states the governing asymmetry directly: **you can only control a system to the extent that you can observe it** ([research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md)). It then makes the idea operational with two properties that are worth more than the slogan — **alertability**, meaning thresholds that make a failure unambiguous, and **actionability**, meaning enough signal to support finding a cause. A stream that supports neither is cost rather than observability, and the same source's standing review rule follows: **collect what you need rather than what you can.**

Two of its named sensitivities explain why an anomaly's cause is so often not where the anomaly appeared. **Entanglement** — changing one thing changes everything, so an upstream difference resurfaces somewhere unrelated. **Configuration** — versions, parameters and lists live in configuration, where a small error produces radically different behaviour that ordinary software tests will not catch. **Both are arguments for a stopping rule rather than for a completion criterion**, because a search whose target may be anywhere has no natural end.

**That source's limits are heavy and must travel with it.** It is practitioner guidance from a commercial publisher, not research: no study design, no comparison group, no measurement that its practices outperform alternatives. Three of its four authors worked at one large vendor, so its default toolchain is that vendor's. Some of its foundational claims rest on **anonymous practitioner testimony** that cannot be checked or followed up. And **every quantity in it is a property of a particular system after a particular training run**; mechanism and direction transfer, magnitudes do not, and **no figure from it appears here.**

**3.3 Why the stopping rule rather than the outcome.** An investigation's conclusion depends on the anomaly, which is not under the learner's control. **The rule is under their control and is the transferable part** — an anomaly abandoned on a stated rule is professionally handled, and one abandoned through boredom is not, even where the code is identical.

The practitioner literature's honest position sharpens the argument by refusing to supply a rule. On how much data to read, it offers a heuristic — **keep reading until you are not learning anything new** — alongside the flat statement that **you can never stop looking at data, and no free lunch exists** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md)). That is intellectually honest and operationally unusable: **a heuristic terminating when you stop learning terminates when you stop paying attention, and the two are indistinguishable from the inside.** The design's addition is to require the rule in advance; the addition is this design's and not the source's, and §8 concedes what it costs.

The same source supplies the trace vocabulary the session uses. **A trace is a logical grouping of log records; for a conversational system it is the conversation**, and the practical requirement on tooling is that searching, filtering and reading traces are its essential features, with a small domain-specific viewer usually beating a generic one. **It is one practitioner's essay resting on one company's case study, with no control condition, no measured improvement and no released dataset; its tooling references are dated and only the pattern transfers.**

**3.4 Why a check's tier must be established by breaking something.** Configuration can be read and misread; consequence cannot. **A check whose consequence you have never observed is a check whose tier you have assumed**, and the pinned corpus's own account is that this distinction is the one most likely to be misread by somebody looking at a green summary ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)).

**3.5 Why an explanation from the executor is a claim, and why the reason is structural.**

The usual framing — be sceptical of what a tool tells you — is a disposition and disposition is not a mechanism. The mechanism comes from the planning literature, and it is exact. The position paper argues that auto-regressive models cannot by themselves plan, and closes the escape route in the same breath: **they cannot self-verify either, because self-verification is after all a form of reasoning** ([research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md)). **An explanation of past behaviour is a verification claim about that behaviour**, produced by the same process, with the same reliability, fluent for the same reasons the original output was fluent. The constructive half supplies the remedy: correctness comes from an **external** verifier and flexibility from the generator, and neither is trusted with the other's job. **Corroboration must come from outside the thing being corroborated.**

Two further sources close the two remaining escape routes. If the learner reaches for a model as a judge of the anomaly, **self-enhancement bias** means a judge sharing a family with the system under test is not an independent instrument, and **limited reasoning** means a judge cannot reliably grade a problem it cannot itself solve — which is precisely the situation on an unexplained behaviour ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/zheng-mt-bench-chatbot-arena.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/zheng-mt-bench-chatbot-arena.md)). And if the learner trusts their own reading of the anomaly's plausibility, capability with these systems is **jagged**, so the failure mode outside the boundary is a persuasive wrong answer in the register of a correct one rather than an error ([research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md](/research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md)).

**Limits on all three.** The position paper's claims are arguments and have been contested; it targets auto-regressive models as of early 2024, and reasoning-trained models postdate it. **The architectural discipline is what transfers and it is correct whether or not model reasoning improves.** The bias work predates current models; **its taxonomy has held up better than its figures, none of which is reproduced here**, and agreement with human preference is not correctness in any case. The frontier note was written from an abstract; **the shape is usable and no number in it is.**

**3.6 Why the failing job is looked for rather than described.** The pinned revision is not all-green. Learning that from this page teaches a fact; finding it in the record teaches how much looking a mostly-green summary requires, **which is the normal condition of every dashboard a supplier will show them.**

**3.7 Why review is decomposed rather than performed by judgement.** Fixed lenses, risk-scaled depth and a normalised severity vocabulary are what make two reviewers' findings comparable. **Comparability is the property that lets a finding survive the reviewer**, and it is the durable half of the corpus's review subsystem.

**3.8 Why evaluation over stored traces rather than a re-run.**

The capability that makes a trace more than an archive is that **stored responses can be evaluated "from logs or previous runs… without re-running the agent"** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). That single fact separates production evaluation from synthetic testing: synthetic queries test what you thought to ask, and a trace contains what occurred. For an anomaly investigation this is decisive, because the thing you want to interrogate has already happened and re-running it interrogates a different event.

Two further details are adopted directly. **Repetitions exist "to detect non-deterministic behavior"**, which is the documentation's own acknowledgement that a single sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement — and therefore that an anomaly seen once is an observation, not a rate. And the tool-usage catalogue decomposes failure into **selection, input accuracy, call success and output utilisation**, of which **two — the right tool with wrong arguments, and a successful result ignored — produce a system that reports success and is wrong.** That is the class this session is named after, and having four words for it instead of *it did something odd* is most of the diagnostic value.

**The source's silence is the point rather than a footnote.** It is definitional and accurate about what the tooling computes, and it publishes **no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold.** Most of these evaluators are model-judged. **No agreement figure is asserted anywhere in this paper or in the session.** The pages were read through search excerpts rather than full fetches; most agent evaluators are preview; one consulted page is an explicitly superseded variant.

**3.9 A gap in the anchor that the design declared, and has now closed — without closing the silences it was standing beside.**

Earlier revisions of this paper recorded, correctly, that the anchor named tracing and observability in the vendor's agent platform and that **this repository held no research note covering that surface**. The session accordingly taught the shape of a tracing practice and refused its syntax. **That absence is now closed** by [research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-agent-build-and-runtime.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-agent-build-and-runtime.md), and the design's response is to teach the surface and keep the refusal of assessment.

What the note supplies is a route rather than a vocabulary. The platform adopts OpenTelemetry semantic conventions and stores traces in Application Insights; **server-side capture requires no code change once an Application Insights resource is connected**; **message content is off by default** behind an environment variable whose documentation warns in the same sentence that the content may contain personal data; and the published multi-agent conventions define a span **`execute_task`**, child spans under **`invoke_agent`** for **`agent_to_agent_interaction`**, **`agent.state.management`** and **`agent_planning`**, and the attributes **`tool.call.arguments`** and **`tool.call.results`** under **`execute_tool`**. **The documentation's own statement of why this is needed is this session's problem in the vendor's words** — steps nest, an agent invokes a tool that uses a process that invokes another tool, and when a top-level output is wrong it is genuinely hard to determine where the error entered. A **Conversation** view exposes ordered actions, run steps and tool calls searchable by response or trace id, **which is what makes reconstruction possible without re-running an event that will not repeat.**

**The design's judgement is that closing this gap changed the session's instrument and not its argument, and the distinction is load-bearing.** §4 now requires a span walk with an *accounted for* / *not accounted for* mark at each level, because a route that can be followed is a route that can be assessed for where it ran out. **The stopping rule, the silence note and the treatment of a self-explanation as a claim are all unchanged**, because none of them was waiting on the note.

**And the silences the anchor names are confirmed rather than repaired, which is a stronger result than the one this paper previously recorded.** The note reports, from dedicated search rather than from non-encounter, that **no measure of tracing completeness exists** — nothing states what proportion of an agent's decisions appears in a trace, whether a step can go unrecorded, or how a reader would detect a missing span, so **a trace is presented as a record and never as a sample** — and that **no measure of self-correction convergence exists.** §9's prediction 7 is re-pointed accordingly: it now instruments the completeness assumption rather than the citation gap.

**The note's limits are the heaviest in this repository and travel with every claim above.** It is **the most perishable material here and it perished visibly while being read**: four pages read in full, roughly thirty reached only through search excerpts with tables frequently truncated, several consulted pages explicitly superseded and still rendering, and preview status pervasive. **It is definitional and not evidential** — an accurate account of what the platform is configured to do, and no evidence that doing so produces any outcome. **Every number in it is a dated platform parameter and none is reproduced in this paper or in the session.** The governance note beside it records that **tracing is off by default, that only an account owner or owner may enable it, and that anyone holding Log Analytics Reader on the connected workspace can read prompts and completions** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-control-plane-and-governance.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-control-plane-and-governance.md)), with the same genre limits and two interpretive thresholds asserted on no stated basis that are reproduced nowhere.

**3.10 Why an openly published standard on observability is used as a specimen.** The handbook supplies one genuinely under-taught distinction — **logs, metrics and traces separated by data volume and processing cost rather than by semantics**, with tracing framed as an optimisation instrument rather than a reactive one ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md), pages read 2026-07-30). It also supplies the sharpest available demonstration of why a standard is cited by page and by date: **its observability section is the most rotted part of the site.** Its tracing page is about ten lines and defines neither a span nor propagation; it quotes a roadmap years out of date; several tools it recommends are archived or deprecated. **A section about observing systems that has not itself been observed for years is a better lesson than any of its content**, and the handbook contradicts itself in at least a dozen places without noticing and asserts four unsupported magnitude claims that **this repository records as specimens and reproduces nowhere.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 5 is the instrument and it is the module's hardest question.** *What would you have to see to conclude the executor's explanation of itself was true?* Learners who answer with an external observation have understood; learners who answer with *ask it to elaborate* have not, and the distribution is the session's opening measurement.

**4.1 Why the silence note sits at the top of the artefact.** It would be easy to state the missing measure once, in the material, and let it be absorbed. **The design instead requires it as the first line of the register**, because the failure it guards against is not ignorance of the absence but forgetting it under the pressure of writing a summary. A register whose first line says *this is not a rate* is much harder to summarise as *this rarely happens*, and the summary is where the error is actually committed.

**The stopping rule is written before the investigation.** Written after, it is a description of when the learner ran out of patience.

**4.2 Why one check's tier is established by making it fail.** The learner breaks something the check should catch and watches what happens to the change. **This is the movement that converts a belief about a pipeline into a fact about it**, and it is the concrete form of the programme's standing commitment that an instrument must be characterised before its outputs are trusted. It is also the session's only mechanically verifiable element, which matters because §8's first objection is about everything else being self-reported.

**The failing job is found rather than shown.** The session says the revision is not all-green and does not say where.

**The trace is evaluated rather than re-run**, for §3.8's reason: re-running interrogates a different event, and the anomaly you care about has already happened.

**The register requires the two shapes of not-knowing to be distinguished** — *I do not know* and *nobody could know* — because they call for different next steps, and collapsing them produces either wasted investigation or premature closure.

**The disposition switch is stated explicitly in the work movement.** For three sessions the executor was an instrument to delegate to; here it is a subject to interrogate. **Both have to be available at once**, and an engineer who only delegates will not notice an anomaly while one who only interrogates will never ship.

**4.3 Why the span walk is marked *accounted for* level by level rather than summarised.**

A summary of a trace is a claim about the trace, and it is written by the person with the strongest incentive to have found something. **Marking each level of the descent — `execute_task`, `agent_planning`, `invoke_agent`, `execute_tool` — as accounted for or not accounted for converts a narrative into a record of where the account ran out**, which is the only part of the walk that is diagnostic. The design's expectation, stated in advance so it can be falsified, is that the tree settles the **what** far more often than the **why**: `tool.call.arguments` names the thing that was touched, and `agent_planning` shows that a step existed without showing why it was chosen.

**The walk closes with a completeness line, and that line is the assessed thinking rather than the tree.** Because **no measure of tracing completeness is published**, a span tree is a record the learner is not entitled to read as a sample — so *the trace does not show it* and *it did not happen* are different claims and only the first is available. **This is the failure the arrival of a usable tracing surface makes newly possible**, and it is the one §9's prediction 7 now instruments: a session that previously refused the syntax could not produce it, and a session that teaches the syntax can.

**The content-recording comparison is run rather than described**, for the same reason the check's tier is established by breaking it. Message content is off by default; the learner runs the task twice and finds out what each trace can answer. **The deliverable is not the second trace but the redaction rule and the named set of people who may read the workspace it lands in**, because anyone holding Log Analytics Reader there can read prompts and completions and a learner who cannot name that set has not enabled content recording, they have leaked it.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The rate from one observation.** The failure the silence note exists against, and the one that survives longest because it is the most natural thing to say. *Countermeasure:* the silence note at the head of the register, and exercise 5's requirement to scan the register for frequency words. **Both are textual and §9's prediction 5 predicts them failing.**

**The self-explanation accepted.** *Countermeasure:* the register's separate columns for the claim and its corroboration, and the standard's warning sign — your evidence column contains the executor's own words.

**The green summary.** *Countermeasure:* the check inventory, and the requirement that at least one label be established by making the check fail.

**The advisory read as a gate.** *Countermeasure:* the same movement, plus the standard's warning sign — you have never seen that check red.

**The approval mistaken for a boundary.** The learner cites the confirmation prompt as the control that would have caught the anomaly. *Countermeasure:* the situation's carried-forward correction, and the standard's warning sign — you cannot say whether it is still switched on or who switched it off.

**The investigation with no floor.** The opposite failure and the one most designs forget. *Countermeasure:* the stopping rule, written first and assessed as the artefact.

**The unregistered ship.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that a shipped inexplicability be marked as shipped with what will be watched in production.

**The reattributed anomaly.** Everything is the host's, the model's or the customer's fault. *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — your register contains no mistake of your own — and [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md), which marks the same failure at the assessment.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. Both artefacts are submitted at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md), where two properties are assessed: that the register is present and non-empty or its emptiness defended, and that every check cited in the defence is labelled advisory or authoritative with the basis stated.

**The investigations' conclusions are not assessed.** Whether an anomaly was ever explained is not marked anywhere in the stage.

**Nor is the silence note assessed for its content**, only for its presence and for the absence of frequency claims elsewhere in the register — which is a check on the artefact rather than on the learner's understanding, and §8 concedes the difference.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: assessing a stopping rule rewards a well-written account of an investigation over a diligent investigation.**

At its strongest: a learner who writes an elegant stopping rule and follows it for ten minutes satisfies the element. A learner who spends real effort, finds nothing, and writes their rule afterwards because they did not know the convention, fails it. The instrument measures compliance with a documentation practice and is presented as measuring professional judgement.

**Substantially conceded.** Two partial answers. The register requires *what made you stop* to be consistent with *what you did to establish why*, so a rule with no corresponding activity is visible in the same entry rather than in a separate one. And the element sits beside a mechanically checkable one — the check inventory's basis column, where *I made it fail and watched* is verifiable against the pipeline's own history. **The declined fix is to assess investigation effort directly.** It is declined because effort is not the competence: an engineer who investigates thoroughly with no stopping rule is the failure mode §5 names as the investigation with no floor, and marking effort would reward it.

**Objection 2: everything the session observes is a property of one host, one model and one revision, so nothing it teaches transfers.**

At its strongest: the tier establishment, the anomalies in the register and the failing job are all facts about a configuration that will not exist in a year. The session is a supervised tour of a snapshot. Its durable content — *check things, write down when you stopped* — could be taught without any of it, in a fraction of the time.

**Conceded on the observations and rejected on the method.** The session's own limits section states that a result seen on one host with one model is not a result about the corpus, and every claim in the perishable table is paired with its version-bound instance. **What transfers is the act of characterising an instrument before trusting it**, and that act cannot be practised on a description — a learner who has never broken a check to find out what it does will read a green summary the way everybody else does. **The declined fix is to teach the principles without the anchor.** It is declined for [WP-046](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-046.md) §8's reason and for a sharper one here: the pinned corpus is the clearest available worked example of a sincere, well-engineered control system with no committed evidence that its controls work, published by the vendor whose platform the programme teaches. **A constructed example would teach the same lesson worse, because nothing about this one is careless.**

**Objection 3: building a session on a documented absence is unfalsifiable, self-serving and will age badly the moment the absence is filled.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the design finds hardest. A missing measure is not evidence of anything. **The session takes the fact that a vendor has not published a convergence rate and converts it into a disposition — heightened suspicion — which no observation could disconfirm**, since any anomaly confirms the caution and the absence of anomalies confirms that the caution was working. That is the structure of an unfalsifiable belief and this repository is normally hostile to it. It is also selective: **no vendor publishes a convergence rate for any comparable tool**, and no compiler ships with a measured rate of silently wrong optimisations either, so singling out this documentation implies a standard nobody in the industry meets and makes the absence look like a defect rather than a norm. And it is fragile in an embarrassing direction — **if the measure is published next term, the session's opening argument evaporates**, and a design whose central claim can be deleted by a documentation update was resting on something thinner than it appeared.

**Partly conceded, and the third part is conceded outright.** On unfalsifiability: the session's operational content is not a disposition but a prohibition with an instrument — **do not state a frequency, and here is the check for whether you did** — and prediction 5 is written to detect its failure, which is exactly what an unfalsifiable design cannot offer. On selectivity: the objection is right that the absence is a norm, and **the design's response is that a norm is precisely what a curriculum should make visible**, since an engineer who believes their tools come with characterised error rates will make worse decisions than one who knows they do not; the session states the absence as a working condition rather than as an accusation, and the wording is deliberate. On fragility: **conceded without qualification.** If the measure is published, this session's opening must be rewritten, and the honest response is that a design tied to a documented state of the world should be revisable when the world changes rather than insulated from it.

**The declined fix is to replace the absence with a cohort-generated estimate** — have every learner log every completion claim against whether the work was in fact correct, and pool the results. It is declined on three grounds and the third is the one that settles it. The denominator is unavailable, because a learner cannot establish that work was correct any more reliably than the executor could. The pooled sample would span different hosts, models, tasks and skill levels, so the aggregate would answer no question anybody asked. **And a number produced that way would be repeated** — this repository exists partly because well-formed unsupported figures travel further than the caveats attached to them, and manufacturing one to fill a gap the design is teaching learners to respect would be the most direct self-contradiction available to it. **The cost of declining is that the session's central fact remains an absence, which is less satisfying to teach and, prediction 5 suggests, harder to retain.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most answers to opening item 5 will propose asking the executor a further question rather than seeking external corroboration. *Instrument:* the written answers, coded *external corroboration* / *further questioning* / *absent*, by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most check inventories will contain labels established by reading configuration rather than by observation, before the work movement. *Instrument:* the basis column, coded *observed* / *read* / *assumed*, before and after.

**Prediction 3.** A majority of learners will mislabel at least one check's tier before establishing it empirically. *Instrument:* the pre-movement label against the post-movement observation, per check.

**Prediction 4.** A majority of learners will attempt to break a check and find that nothing happens — the check goes red and the change proceeds — and will report surprise. *Instrument:* the tier-establishment record coded for outcome, with the learner's stated expectation recorded beforehand.

**Prediction 5.** Despite the silence note, a majority of registers will contain at least one frequency word applied to an observation with a sample of one. *Instrument:* the registers, scanned for frequency vocabulary and coded against the sample size of the entry it qualifies. **This predicts the failure of §5's weakest countermeasure and of §8's third objection's rebuttal**, and it is the prediction this paper most expects to be confirmed.

**Prediction 6.** Registers will contain more entries attributing anomalies to the executor than entries attributing them to the learner's own instruction set or plan. *Instrument:* the register's attribution, coded by a second reader. **If the imbalance is extreme, the reattribution failure is operating at cohort scale**, and [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s corresponding failure mode should be promoted to an assessed element.

**Prediction 7.** Having walked a span tree, a majority of learners will treat it as a complete account of the run — concluding *it did not happen* from *the trace does not show it* — in at least one register entry. *Instrument:* the register and the span-walk record, coded by a second reader for whether an unrecorded step is treated as an absent one, and for whether the completeness line was written at all. **This is the instrument attached to §3.9's surviving silence**, and a confirmation means the arrival of a usable tracing surface has bought a false confidence that its absence did not.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a green check is a claim about a check; that advisory and authoritative are properties of where a check runs; that an explanation produced by the thing being explained is a claim requiring external corroboration; and that an observation without a published base rate is not a rate.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about whether any of this catches anything. The corpus has graders, validators, conformance lanes and baseline comparisons, and **stores no results** — the location for them is an empty placeholder. The evaluation documentation **publishes no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator.** And the central measure this session is organised around is **not published at all.**

**The concession worth remembering.** §8 concedes that if the missing measure is ever published, this session's opening argument disappears. **That is the correct kind of fragility for a design tied to a documented state of the world**, and it is why the silence is recorded with a date.
