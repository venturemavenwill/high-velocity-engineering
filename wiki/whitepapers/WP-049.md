## In plain terms

[S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) was about code you cannot explain. This session is about **behaviour** you cannot explain — the executor did something you did not ask for, or failed to do something you did, and you have no account of why.

The two are different problems and only one of them can be read. Code sits still while you study it. Behaviour happened once, under conditions you did not fully record, on a host and a model that will both have moved by next term.

The pinned repository takes review seriously enough to decompose it, and the decomposition is the durable content: **orientation is separated from judgement**, so establishing the facts of a change is a different act from forming an opinion about it; **depth is scaled to assessed risk** rather than to preference; **the lenses a reviewer applies are fixed** rather than left to habit; and **severity is normalised** so that two reviewers produce comparable findings.

Then it does the thing that makes it a good teaching object rather than a good product. **It tells you what its own checks are worth.** Its behavioural graders test **output shape, not correctness**, and it says so in its own documentation. Some checks are **advisory** and others **authoritative**, and which one a check is depends on **the tier it runs in rather than on the check itself.** The revision this programme pins is not all-green: one job failed, most were skipped, and none of that is visible in a summary.

The session's hardest item is the last one. **What would you have to see to conclude the executor's explanation of itself was true?** The obvious move — ask it why — produces another output from the same process, subject to exactly the scrutiny you were trying to shortcut. **An explanation you cannot corroborate is a hypothesis with good grammar.**

## Abstract

This paper defends a session in which the assessed artefact of an investigation is its stopping rule rather than its conclusion. The argument is that unexplained executor behaviour is a normal working condition rather than an exceptional one; that the two available failures are opposite — accepting a self-explanation, and spending an engagement on an anomaly of no consequence — so a design that guards against only one produces the other; and that the distinction between a check with a consequence and a check without one is a property of where the check runs, is invisible in a summary, and is the distinction most likely to be misread. The session installs an inexplicability register with stopping rules and a check inventory whose labels must be established rather than assumed. The paper concedes that the register cannot distinguish a diligent investigation from a well-written account of one, and that everything observed is a property of a single pinned configuration.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

An engineer working with a delegate will regularly be unable to explain what the delegate did. That is not a failure state; **it is the ordinary condition**, and a programme that treated it as exceptional would teach a disposition its graduates would abandon in a fortnight.

What is teachable is what you do about it, and there are two ways to get it wrong that pull in opposite directions.

**The first is accepting the explanation you were given.** Asking the executor why produces a fluent, plausible, internally consistent account, and there is no signal in the account distinguishing a true one from a confabulated one. **The vendor publishes no measure of how often the agent reports completion on work that is wrong**, so the base rate is unavailable to the reader.

**The second is investigating forever.** An anomaly with no consequence can absorb a day, and the day comes out of the increment. An engineer who investigates everything is as unemployable as one who investigates nothing.

There is a third problem sitting behind both, and it is the one this repository cares most about. **The instruments that appear to resolve the question mostly do not.** A green check tells you a check passed. Whether that is also a statement about the work depends on what the check does, and whether a failing check would have stopped anything depends on where it runs.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** Every professional has met a tool doing something inexplicable. What is new is the register, the stopping rule, and the requirement to establish a check's tier empirically.

It assumes [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s adversarial disposition, which the session deliberately re-installs on top of the instrumental one the module has been building. It assumes [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md)'s review record, which is the instrument being turned on an executor. It assumes [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md)'s audit, because a region you cannot explain and a behaviour you cannot explain need to be told apart.

The prior the session works against is that unexplained behaviour indicates a defect somewhere findable. **Often there is no finding available**, and the professional skill is deciding that on purpose rather than by exhaustion.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate intrinsic load and a genuine cognitive-flexibility demand: the session requires the learner to hold two dispositions towards the same object and switch between them deliberately.

**3.2 Why the stopping rule rather than the outcome.** An investigation's conclusion depends on the anomaly, which is not under the learner's control. **The rule is under their control and is the transferable part** — an anomaly abandoned on a stated rule is professionally handled, and one abandoned through boredom is not, even where the code is identical.

**3.3 Why a check's tier must be established by breaking something.** Configuration can be read and misread; consequence cannot. **A check whose consequence you have never observed is a check whose tier you have assumed**, and the pinned repository's own account is that this distinction is the one most likely to be misread by somebody looking at a green summary.

**3.4 Why an explanation from the executor is a claim.** It is another output of the process under examination. Recording it as a claim with a corroboration requirement is the same discipline [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md) applied to promised data and [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md) applied to spike findings: **separate what you were told from what you established.**

**3.5 Why the failing job is looked for rather than described.** The pinned revision is not all-green. Learning that from this page teaches a fact; finding it in the record teaches how much looking a mostly-green summary requires, **which is the normal condition of every dashboard a supplier will show them.**

**3.6 Why review is decomposed rather than performed by judgement.** Fixed lenses, risk-scaled depth and a normalised severity vocabulary are what make two reviewers' findings comparable. **Comparability is the property that lets a finding survive the reviewer**, and it is the durable half of the repository's review subsystem.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 5 is the instrument and it is the module's hardest question.** *What would you have to see to conclude the executor's explanation of itself was true?* Learners who answer with *check the code* have understood; learners who answer with *ask it to elaborate* have not, and the distribution is the session's opening measurement.

**The stopping rule is written before the investigation.** Written after, it is a description of when the learner ran out of patience.

**One check's tier is established by making it fail.** The learner breaks something the check should catch and watches what happens to the change. **This is the movement that converts a belief about a pipeline into a fact about it**, and it is the concrete form of the programme's standing commitment that an instrument must be characterised before its outputs are trusted.

**The failing job is found rather than shown.** The session says the revision is not all-green and does not say where.

**The register requires the two shapes of not-knowing to be distinguished** — *I do not know* and *nobody could know* — because they call for different next steps, and collapsing them produces either wasted investigation or premature closure.

**The disposition switch is stated explicitly in the work movement.** For four sessions the executor was an instrument to delegate to; here it is a subject to interrogate. **Both have to be available at once**, and an engineer who only delegates will not notice an anomaly while one who only interrogates will never ship.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The self-explanation accepted.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* the register's separate columns for the claim and its corroboration, and the standard's warning sign — your evidence column contains the executor's own words.

**The green summary.** *Countermeasure:* the check inventory, and the requirement that at least one label be established by making the check fail.

**The advisory read as a gate.** *Countermeasure:* the same movement, plus the standard's warning sign — you have never seen that check red.

**The investigation with no floor.** The opposite failure and the one most designs forget. *Countermeasure:* the stopping rule, written first and assessed as the artefact.

**The unregistered ship.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that a shipped inexplicability be marked as shipped with what would be watched in production, which connects to [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md).

**The reattributed anomaly.** Everything is the host's, the model's or the customer's fault. *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — your register contains no mistake of your own — and [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md), which marks the same failure at the assessment.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: assessing a stopping rule rewards a well-written account of an investigation over a diligent investigation.**

At its strongest: a learner who writes an elegant stopping rule and follows it for ten minutes satisfies the element. A learner who spends real effort, finds nothing, and writes their rule afterwards because they did not know the convention, fails it. The instrument measures compliance with a documentation practice and is presented as measuring professional judgement.

**Substantially conceded.** Two partial answers. The register requires *what made you stop* to be consistent with *what you did to establish why*, so a rule with no corresponding activity is visible in the same entry rather than in a separate one. And the element sits beside a mechanically checkable one — the check inventory's basis column, where *I made it fail and watched* is verifiable against the repository's own history. **The declined fix is to assess investigation effort directly.** It is declined because effort is not the competence: an engineer who investigates thoroughly with no stopping rule is the failure mode §5 names as the investigation with no floor, and marking effort would reward it.

**Objection 2: everything the session observes is a property of one host, one model and one revision, so nothing it teaches transfers.**

At its strongest: the boundary test, the tier establishment, the anomalies in the register and the failing job are all facts about a configuration that will not exist in a year. The session is a supervised tour of a snapshot. Its durable content — *check things, write down when you stopped* — could be taught without any of it, in a fraction of the time.

**Conceded on the observations and rejected on the method.** The session's own limits section states that a result seen on one host with one model is not a result about the corpus, and every claim in the perishable table is paired with its version-bound instance. **What transfers is the act of characterising an instrument before trusting it**, and that act cannot be practised on a description — a learner who has never broken a check to find out what it does will read a green summary the way everybody else does. **The declined fix is to teach the principles without the anchor.** It is declined for [WP-046](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-046.md) §8's reason and for a sharper one here: the pinned repository is the clearest available worked example of a sincere, well-engineered control system with no committed evidence that its controls work, published by the vendor whose platform the programme teaches. **A constructed example would teach the same lesson worse, because nothing about this one is careless.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most answers to opening item 5 will propose asking the executor a further question rather than seeking external corroboration. *Instrument:* the written answers, coded *external corroboration* / *further questioning* / *absent*, by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most check inventories will contain labels established by reading configuration rather than by observation, before the work movement. *Instrument:* the basis column, coded *observed* / *read* / *assumed*, before and after.

**Prediction 3.** A majority of learners will mislabel at least one check's tier before establishing it empirically. *Instrument:* the pre-movement label against the post-movement observation, per check.

**Prediction 4.** Registers will contain more entries attributing anomalies to the executor than entries attributing them to the learner's own instruction set or plan. *Instrument:* the register's attribution, coded by a second reader. **If the imbalance is extreme, the reattribution failure is operating at cohort scale**, and [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s corresponding failure mode should be promoted to an assessed element.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a green check is a claim about a check; that advisory and authoritative are properties of where a check runs; and that an explanation produced by the thing being explained is a claim requiring corroboration.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about whether any of this catches anything. The repository has graders, validators, conformance lanes and baseline comparisons, and **stores no results** — the location for them is an empty placeholder.

**The concession worth remembering.** The stopping rule is assessed because the conclusion cannot be, and a well-written rule is not a diligent investigation. If you leave able to tell those apart in your own work, you have something this session could not mark.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The agent-configuration repository was read at commit `cf29fb457b0fe62745bb71592ec9394b834957f4`, published package version 3.3.101, and every structural claim is as of that revision. **It decomposes review into orientation separated from judgement, depth tiers scaled to risk, fixed lens checklists and a normalised severity taxonomy, persisting findings under stable identifiers.** Its own documentation states that its agent-behaviour checks are regex-graded and **test output shape and not correctness**; that behaviour conformance is **advisory by default** and lacks established branch calibration; that baseline equivalence covers a narrow contract with unresolved work; and that **a check can be advisory at pull-request tier while the nightly run is authoritative.** Its transparency statement records that the project ships no model and no safety classifier, that host behaviour varies, and that human oversight remains necessary. **It contains evaluation machinery and no committed result corpus, and at the revision read one job failed while most were skipped — that failure is part of the evidence and is not reproducible from the tree alone.** No benefit claim from its prose is adopted anywhere in this paper.

The host is GitHub Copilot agent mode as recorded in [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md)'s reading of 2026-07-30, **carried forward and not re-read**: the documentation publishes no measure of how often the self-correction loop converges, or how often the agent reports completion on work that is wrong. **Those two silences are the reason this session exists**, and a cohort that finds either measure published should treat this paper as due for revision.
