## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

A number arrives with an interval around it. The interval looks like a property of the data. It is not — **it is a property of a procedure applied to a structure**, and the structure is almost never the one the arithmetic assumes.

Four hundred requests from twelve people are not four hundred independent observations. Four hundred outputs scored by four judges are not four hundred independent judgements, and after the previous day you know exactly why: whatever each judge gets systematically wrong, they get wrong on every output they touched. Four hundred sessions from one afternoon are four hundred samples of that afternoon.

The important part is that ignoring this **errs in one direction only.** Where things within a cluster resemble each other — which is the ordinary case for users, sessions, documents, machines and judges — assuming independence makes your interval too narrow and your claim too confident. That asymmetry is why the specification fails an independence-assuming interval by default while still letting you argue for one: a default that errs towards overconfidence would be indefensible, and this one errs the other way.

There is a second failure that looks identical and is not, and the day separates them deliberately. An interval can be **too narrow**, which is an arithmetic problem. Or it can be perfectly computed about a population nobody cares about, which is a sampling-frame problem wearing statistics clothing. Students who compress the day into *clustering makes intervals wider* walk straight into the second.

And one question that comes before all of it. *No significant difference* is not a finding until you know the smallest effect your design could have detected. **A study that could not have found the thing you care about has not told you the thing is absent** — and computing power afterwards, from the effect you happened to observe, is a restatement of the interval you already have.

**What this paper concedes, and it is a real concession.** The machinery the day supplies holds where cluster membership is categorical and order inside the cluster means nothing. It does not hold where position carries information — consecutive requests in a session, anything with a trend — and the day presents exactly such a case among its four. **Nothing in the artefact stops a student naming *time window* as a clustering factor and applying the expression to it**, which satisfies every checked element while doing what the scope statement calls unsafe. The fix is one extra sentence per named factor. It is named, it is cheap, and it is deliberately not made.

## Abstract

This paper defends discharging an explicit twenty-one-day deferral as the day's organising device, defends holding a point estimate fixed while varying only the collection story, and defends a specification that fails an independence-assuming interval by default while permitting an argued exception. The central argument is that the independence assumption is not neutral: where intra-cluster correlation is positive, which is the ordinary case for users, sessions, documents, machines and judges, ignoring it errs in exactly one direction, and a default that fails in the direction of overconfidence is therefore justified in a way a symmetric default would not be. The paper further defends separating two failures students merge — an interval that is too narrow, and an interval correctly computed about a population nobody cares about — by making one of the four contrasting cases an instance of the second rather than the first. It additionally defends running the day's structures against a real evaluation service, on the ground that the service produces a defensible-looking comparison from a sample count it did not choose the unit for, and labels *no effect* and *could not have found one* with the same word. It concedes that the day supplies machinery for exchangeable clustering and none for temporal dependence while presenting a temporal case among its four; that its own most-needed correction was originally delivered by assertion, which is logged as the first instance of a pattern-level observation; and that the platform staging which now repairs that omission arrived from the practical revision rather than from the design noticing, which is further evidence for the same observation.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

[S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md) taught sampling and intervals and stated, in the day itself, that clustering was being deferred and would return with the machinery to handle it. The deferral was made because S028 sits in a class-2 module and the honest treatment requires a student who can formulate a claim, choose a unit of analysis and defend both — which is a class-4 capability the programme did not then have.

The problem this day inherits is therefore precise: students hold a correct procedure resting on an assumption they were told was false and have not been able to relax.

The assumption is false almost everywhere. Four hundred requests from twelve users are not four hundred independent observations. Four hundred outputs scored by four judges are not four hundred independent judgements — and after [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) the students know exactly why, since each judge's systematic component is shared by every output that judge touched. Four hundred sessions from one afternoon are not four hundred independent samples of anything but that afternoon.

The design's second problem is the question that precedes the interval. A design that could not have detected the effect you care about has not produced a negative result. Students routinely read "no significant difference" as evidence of no difference, and the correction is not a caution but a construction: state the smallest effect worth acting on, then ask what the design would need.

The third problem is the day's most-needed correction. Post-hoc power computed from the observed effect is a transformation of what the interval already reports and cannot rescue a study that has run. It appears in reports students will be asked to review, it looks like diligence, and it is empty.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Highest in the module, and stated as such on the module page.** The day combines unfamiliar inferential machinery with a class-4 formulation demand, which is the combination the programme otherwise avoids.

The interactivity is between steps 1, 2 and 3 of phase 5: the claim determines the population, the population determines the unit, and the unit determines which of the enumerated factors is a clustering factor rather than the unit itself. None of the three can be settled alone.

Management, which is unusually heavy:

- **Steps 1 to 3 are all scaffolded,** which is a departure — most class-4 days scaffold one step.
- **Step 2 has a supplied candidate-factor checklist** of eight items worked with the room.
- **Phase 3 supplies all four structures and the point estimate,** so no group spends the phase constructing data.
- **The criterion, judge and model are all the student's own** and are between one and nine days familiar.
- **MS201 carries the computation.**

**Step 4 alone is unsupported.** The pre-commitment survives because it is the module's standing discipline and because it costs nothing in working memory — it is a number written down, not a design produced.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *The item 2 structural reveal,* which changes no datum and destroys the interval.
- *Ranking four structures before computing,* phase 3.
- *Predicting effective sample size before computing it,* step 4.
- *Requiring all three numbers to be reported,* step 5 — the interval under the argued structure, the interval under independence, and the service's own comparison.
- *The nearest claim not supported,* step 7, which is much harder than a general disclaimer.
- *An adversarial completeness test on the enumeration,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Fitting the models by hand.* MS201's. The seminar argues; it does not derive.
- *Bayesian treatments.* Named as an alternative and not developed. MS301's.
- *Multiplicity corrections.* Point 7's neighbourhood, mentioned in one sentence at phase 4 and not taught. S067 revisits it with a reason to care.
- *Temporal dependence machinery.* Absent, and the subject of §8 objection 1.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** Point 2's design-effect expression holds under the standard exchangeable model, in which every pair within a cluster shares one correlation. **Safe** for users, machines, documents and judges — cases where cluster membership is categorical and no ordering within the cluster is meaningful. **Unsafe** wherever position within the cluster carries information: consecutive requests in a session, observations adjacent in time, or any process with a trend. In those cases the expression is not conservative in a knowable direction; it may understate or overstate, and the day does not say which. A student applying it to a time series has used an identity outside the model that makes it an identity.

**The platform instance, scoped in the strong form.** The day uses a real evaluation comparison as its worked case of a defensible-looking claim with an undefended structure, and that use needs its own boundary.

*Safe.* Reasoning about what a reported sample count counted; about why a t-test over rows is the right arithmetic on the wrong unit when the rows are clustered; about why an evaluation level and a split strategy are unit-of-analysis decisions wearing configuration clothes; and about why *inconclusive* is not a result. Each of those is a property of any tool that computes a comparison from data it did not collect, which is every such tool.

*Unsafe.* Treating the service's output as wrong. **It is arithmetically correct and it is honest about its own method** — the comparison returns the statistical method it used, the sample count it used and the standard deviation it computed, which is more than most internal reports supply. The day's claim is that correct arithmetic on an undefended structure produces an indefensible claim, and a student who leaves believing the tool is broken has learned the opposite of the lesson. Also unsafe as a claim that a student should not use the comparison: they should, and then supply the two things it cannot. Point 11 states this boundary to students.

**One thing is not scoped and the omission is deliberate.** The day still does not require its temporal restriction to be carried into the artefact, and §8 objection 1's concession stands unamended. The scope rule's widening — that a day must carry an artefact element under which a student applying a simplification outside its safe range would fail — **applies from [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md)'s review forward and this day is not retrospectively amended**, so the [compliance register](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) row remains and the failure remains legible.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **a number that survives its own data being unchanged**.

Item 2 adds no observation, removes none, and alters no value. It supplies twelve user identifiers. The interval that was defensible at item 1 is indefensible at item 2, and every student can see this before anyone has said the word *clustering*. The mechanism is the same one S026 used and it is the strongest opening the measurement strand has.

Phase 3's payload is **structure C**, and it is placed to defeat the expectation the phase itself creates. After three structures whose lesson is "the interval is too narrow", students reach a structure whose interval is fine and whose problem is that it describes one afternoon on one deployment version. Students who have compressed the day into "clustering makes intervals wider" are stopped by it, and the two failures — a statistics error and S028's sampling-frame error in statistics costume — are separated at the moment the compression forms rather than after.

Phase 6 supplies the affective content. The report that reversed contained the team identifiers. Nothing was hidden; a question was not asked. That is the form the students' own failures will take, and phase 6 says so.

The competence risk is at step 5, where students who cannot get the computation to run will read the difficulty as a conceptual failure. The instructor states before phase 5 that step 5 is a tooling step, that MS201 owns it, and that arriving at phase 7 with steps 1 to 4 done and step 5 unfinished is a better artefact than the reverse.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** A result in the form students meet it, then twelve user identifiers. Item 4's quantitative prediction is collected because §9 prediction 1 needs it and because a prediction students get badly wrong is the cheapest possible motivation for phase 4.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals and a bridge answered by the room. The bridge — why the second observation from a user carries less than the first — is the day's whole conceptual content, and it is elicited rather than told because the room can reach it.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** One estimate, four collection stories, a committed ranking, then the intervals, **now run as four real evaluation comparisons rather than supplied as a handout.** Structure D is the S048 judges, which makes the crossed design concrete rather than abstract and repays the previous day. **Two further runs follow and neither was asked for**: the same conversations scored at turn level and at conversation level, which manufactures a different n from the same traffic by a parameter that defaults when omitted; and the platform's own comparison over structures A and B, which returns `Inconclusive` for B under a legend that defines the label as *too few examples, or p ≥ 0.05*. That second run is this paper's own §8 objection-2 repair and is discussed there.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Eleven points. Point 2 carries the scope statement and is presented as a modelled identity rather than a finding — a distinction the day is otherwise about. Point 4 supplies the specification's justification before the specification is met, which matters because the specification is unusually prescriptive. Point 7 is the correction, now staged at phase 3 rather than asserted here. Point 8 is the retained list and gives the day its title. **Point 9 audits that list against a real output** and finds two of the five absent by construction, which is the form in which students will actually fail to supply them. **Point 10 names two defaults that decide the sample size** and point 11 marks the perishable layer, which are the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s two required additions.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps, three scaffolded, one bare, **shipping the report the comparison does not write.** The three-number requirement at step 5 expresses the cost of the assumption in the only unit that persuades anyone, which is its own width, and adds the service's own figure alongside because that is the number the student's colleagues will be shown.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** The reversed decision, with attention on the identifiers being present in the original report — then the agent case, which is the same failure with a service in the loop. The agent's report was accurate in every figure because every figure was the service's own, and **checking a claim against the system that produced it is the review most people perform**, which verifies transcription and nothing else.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Adversarial completeness on the enumeration, numbers withheld. Per rule 5 this reuses [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md)'s structure including the withholding, and per rule 3 a partner who finds nothing is recorded as a result.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 4 resolved; step 4 predictions split by direction; the pooled checklist entering the substrate and carried to S067.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with statistics background will produce the correct interval quickly. Predicted failure: they compute and skip step 1, arriving at a defensible interval about a claim they never stated. Countermeasure: step 7 requires the nearest unsupported claim, which cannot be written without step 1; and phase 7's partner is asked for a factor, which is an enumeration test rather than a computation test.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is step 5's tooling. Countermeasures: MS201 ownership stated explicitly; the instructor's pre-phase statement that steps 1 to 4 matter more; the eight-item checklist; and all four phase 3 structures supplied with their intervals, so the shape of the answer is known before the student produces one.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** A group that ranks all four correctly has still to explain structure C, which is the phase's difficulty and is not a ranking question. Fallback: ask what would have to be true for structure C's interval to be the one you want, which is a sampling-frame question and returns them to S028.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **paralysis**: if every interval is wrong until its structure is argued, and the structure is usually unknown, a student may conclude that no measurement can be reported. This is the module's dispositional load reaching its peak, and it is the failure most likely to persist past the day.

Countermeasures: point 8 is a list of five things to *supply*, not five reasons to withhold; the specification permits independence to be argued rather than forbidding it; structure A exists in phase 3 precisely so that one of the four is fine; and the instructor states that a reported interval with its structure named is more useful than no number, even when the structure is unflattering. The last is stated once and is the day's constructive framing.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by S051 (evaluation reading, at one week), S060 (retrieval evaluation, where relevance judgements are clustered by query and by annotator), S067 (offline measures against online reality, which is the day's real test), S073 (agent evaluation over episodes, where the episode is the cluster) and S079 (drift alerting, where an alerting threshold is a power question in disguise).

**S067 at roughly six weeks is the consequential target.** An offline interval and an online outcome that disagree are usually explained by a dependency structure the offline measurement ignored, and this day supplies the only frame the programme offers.

S079's retrieval is the one most likely to fail: a threshold that fires too often and a threshold that cannot detect the drift you care about are the two errors point 6 describes, and nothing in this day names the alerting case.

Retained content: the dependency-structure argument, the unit-of-analysis rule, the direction of the independence error, power as a prior question, and the five things owed to a reader. Five items, one of which is itself a list of five — which is a compression risk and is noted.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 6.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at S050.

One specification item in ME201. Per rule 1, the checked elements are: a stated claim with its population; the clustering-factor enumeration including the judge; a justified unit; a pre-committed effective-sample-size prediction; all three numbers from step 5 with a statement of what the service's sample count counted; the smallest detectable effect; the supported claim; the nearest unsupported claim; the phase 7 outcome; and the prompt, intervention and frontier records. **Nothing else is assessed.**

**An independence-assuming interval fails unless independence is argued.** The default's asymmetry is its justification, per point 4, and the exception is genuinely available.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error. The accepted instance is a report generator that refuses to emit a claim until a unit of analysis has been named, which is the phase 6 agent failure built into the artefact.

Programme instrumentation: **the step 4 prediction against the computed value, split by direction, is the day's headline measure**; the phase 7 found-factor rate is a baseline carried to S067; the pooled checklist is a substrate artefact with a review date, per [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md); and **item 5's sealed prediction accuracy is the day's frontier-calibration datum**, read by the following year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) per instrumentation rule 9.

The frontier statements are a sample and the day says so, per instrumentation rule 3: one cohort, one substrate, one comparison task on data whose clustering staff already know about.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day presents a temporal case and supplies no temporal machinery.**

At its strongest: point 2's design-effect expression is stated under exchangeability, and the scope statement concedes that the exchangeable model is often wrong about time. Structure C is a time problem. The day handles it by reframing — structure C's interval is fine and its population is wrong — which is a legitimate and instructive move, but it is not an analysis, and a student who meets genuine autocorrelation has been shown that the case exists and given nothing to do about it. Worse, the reframing may teach that temporal dependence is always a population problem, which it is not: consecutive requests within a session are dependent in a way no sampling frame fixes.

The partial rebuttal is that the alternative is not available inside 320 minutes. Block bootstrap, autoregressive error structures and time-series methods are a course, not a phase, and MS301 owns them. Given the choice between naming the gap and pretending the exchangeable expression covers it, naming it is correct, and the scope statement names it in the strong form.

What is conceded is that **the day's own framing does not carry the restriction into the artefact**. Step 2 asks students to enumerate clustering factors; a student who names "time window" and then applies the design-effect expression to it has satisfied every checked element while doing something the scope statement says is unsafe. The costless fix is to require step 3's justification to state, for each named factor, whether the exchangeable treatment applies to it — which is one additional sentence per factor and would make the restriction operative rather than declarative. It is not in the design, and it should be. Recorded in the [compliance register](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md).

**Objection 2: the day's most-needed correction was the only content it did not stage.**

At its strongest, as first written: phase 4 point 7 — that post-hoc power computed from the observed effect is empty — was labelled the day's most-needed correction, and was delivered as a paragraph of exposition. Every other load-bearing claim in the day was staged: item 2 stages the clustering reveal, structure C stages the population error, phase 6 stages the unasked question. Point 7 was asserted. The programme's own [design pattern](/wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) holds that a correction the student has not felt does not survive the term, and the design had applied that principle everywhere except to the claim it says matters most.

This objection the design concedes without qualification, and **the staging now exists**. The proposed fix was a fifth pretest item supplying a report that concludes *no significant difference* and reports a post-hoc power figure. What is in the design instead is cheaper and sharper: phase 3's second unasked run, where the platform's comparison returns `Inconclusive` under a published legend defining the label as *too few examples, or p ≥ 0.05*. The two states the correction exists to separate share a cell colour and a word, in a vendor's own interface, and the room meets the confusion before phase 4 names it.

**The concession is not withdrawn, for two reasons, and the second is the more important.** First, the day did assert the claim before it staged it, and the whitepaper standard's convention is that the failure remains legible rather than being tidied away. Second, **the staging arrived from a platform revision rather than from the design noticing.** Nobody re-read point 7, judged it under-staged and built a reveal for it; a reveal turned up while somebody was reading a legend for another purpose.

The concession is worth generalising, and it is recorded here as the **first instance of a pattern-level observation: the design stages the claims it expects students to resist, and asserts the claims it believes are obviously true once stated.** Point 7 felt obvious to whoever wrote it — the argument is short and airtight — and that is precisely the property that got it demoted from a staged reveal to a paragraph. Under this reading the pattern is not laziness but a systematic mis-estimate: perceived obviousness to the designer is uncorrelated with memorability to the student, and the design has been using it as a proxy. One instance is not a pattern; per the programme's convention this observation is recorded and left standing, and will be elevated to a rule if it reaches three. **That the omission was eventually repaired by accident rather than by inspection is not a correction of the observation but a further instance of it**, and the [instrumentation rule 7](/wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) that was later built from this observation depends on the record standing.

**Objection 3: the platform layer makes this day's most durable content look like tooling commentary.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn.

At its strongest: the day's own perishability register says that nothing of substance in it is perishable, that the machinery predates every technology the programme teaches and will outlast them, and that this is the most durable day in the module. The revision then adds a legend, two parameter names, a payload field and a preview marking, and puts them in phase 3 where the room's attention is highest. **A student's memory of a day is disproportionately of what they operated**, and what they operated here was a portal comparison. There is a real risk that the retained content becomes *the Foundry comparison has a misleading label* rather than *an interval is a property of a procedure applied to a structure* — and the first will be false within two years while the second will not.

**The objection is conceded.** The design's response is structural rather than reassuring: point 11 states the asymmetry to students explicitly, the perishability register lists the five durable claims against their instances, and the specification assesses the three numbers and the two claims rather than any configuration. But that is the same response the design gives everywhere, and it has never been tested.

**The cheap fix is one sentence and it is made.** Point 11 now states that the service is present in the day to show that a correct arithmetic pipeline can produce an indefensible claim, which is a fact about claims and not about the service, and that the day's platform layer is deliberately the thinnest in the module for that reason. **What remains unfixed is that the day has no non-platform instance of the same failure in phase 3** — a printed report from a domain with no vendor in it, ranked alongside the four structures, would give the room a second instance from which the durable claim is the only common factor. That is one artefact and half an hour of staff time, and it is not in the design.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Item 4's median predicted widening will be less than half the actual. *Instrument:* item 4 predictions against the computed structure-B interval.

**Prediction 2.** More than half of groups will misplace structure C in the phase 3 ranking, and the misplacement will be in the direction of expecting it to be wide. *Instrument:* committed rankings, coded for C's position and direction of error.

**Prediction 3.** Step 4's effective-sample-size predictions will be biased high — students will over-estimate how much information their data contain — and the bias will be larger for students whose data have a judge as a clustering factor. *Instrument:* predictions against computed values, split by direction and by presence of a judge factor.

**Prediction 4.** Phase 7 partners will find an unnamed clustering factor in more than half of cases, and the most frequently missed factor will be the judge. *Instrument:* phase 7 outcomes, coded by factor named. **This is the prediction the design least wants to be right about**, since the judge was characterised the previous day and is item six on the supplied checklist.

**Prediction 5.** In phase 3's `Inconclusive` run, a majority of groups will initially read the label as evidence of no effect rather than as an absence of evidence, and will revise only when asked which of the two it is. *Instrument:* the committed reading before the question, against the reading after. **This is the objection-2 staging's own test.** If groups read it correctly unprompted, the correction is less needed than the day assumes and point 7 can return to being a paragraph.

**Prediction 6.** A large majority of item 5 sealed predictions will state that the agent will get something wrong, and a minority will name the unit-of-analysis error. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded for generic-versus-specific failure identification, against what each student's agent produced.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That an interval is a property of a procedure applied to a structure, not of the data alone; that the structure is almost never independent, because users, sessions, documents and judges cluster; and that ignoring that errs in one direction only, which is why an independence-assuming interval fails unless independence is argued.

**What the evidence licenses.** Sampling structure, unit of analysis and power asked before a study runs are **class 1**, grounded in [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md). The design-effect expression and the emptiness of post-hoc power are **class 2**, cited for direction and mechanism only. The comparison output and its significance legend are **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026, and the silence is exact: no minimum detectable effect is reported anywhere, so `Inconclusive` cannot be resolved into its two meanings. The four-structure construction and the asymmetric default are **class 3**.

**What to do with it.**

- State the claim and its population before computing. The unit of analysis follows from the population, not the rows in your file.
- Enumerate your clustering factors and include the judge; it is the one most often missed.
- Treat *no significant difference* as unresolved until you know the smallest effect the design could detect.
- Sort what the service returned by the five classes on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md): its comparison is an observed result, and that the system improved is an unsupported inference until the structure is argued.

**The concession worth remembering.** The day supplies machinery for exchangeable clustering, presents a temporal case it cannot analyse, and does not carry that restriction into the artefact: name *time window* as a clustering factor, apply the design-effect expression, and you satisfy every checked element while doing what the scope statement calls unsafe. The fix is named and deliberately not made.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** Sampling structure, interval interpretation, unit of analysis, the direction of the independence error, and power as a question asked before an eval is run — including the minimum detectable effect and its inversion for a fixed eval — are grounded in [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md). The judge-as-clustering-factor link follows [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md)'s design and the same material.

**Point 7's claim was previously listed here and is reclassified.** That post-hoc power computed from an observed effect is empty is not verified in this repository: Miller treats power prospectively throughout — sample size, minimum detectable effect, and whether an eval can answer the question at all — and does not address the post-hoc form. The claim is a standard result of the same kind as the design-effect expression and is classed with the general-knowledge citations below.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** Three things are taken from Microsoft Learn, all read 2026-07-26. **The comparison output** is from the evaluation-results page: comparing two or more runs uses statistical t-testing; a baseline run may be set; each cell carries a significance result with a published legend — `ImprovedStrong` and `DegradedStrong` at p ≤ 0.001, `ImprovedWeak` and `DegradedWeak` at 0.001 < p ≤ 0.05, `ChangedStrong` and `ChangedWeak` where the desired direction is neutral, and **`Inconclusive` for *too few examples, or p ≥ 0.05*** — and hovering a cell gives the sample size and p-value. The corresponding API objects, from the Foundry REST reference read the same day, carry an average, a run identifier, a **sample count** and a **standard deviation** per run, and a **delta estimate**, a **p-value** and a treatment-effect type per comparison, together with a field naming the statistical method used. **The unit-of-analysis parameters** are from the cloud-evaluation page: an evaluation level of `turn` scores each turn independently, `conversation` scores the whole conversation, **omitting it defaults to `turn`**, turn-only evaluators cannot be used at conversation level, and conversation-level evaluation is marked preview. **The split strategies** are from the Agent Framework evaluation page: last turn is the default, full and per-turn are the alternatives, custom splitters are supported, and the page states in its own words that how you split determines what you are evaluating. The CI extensions for GitHub Actions and Azure DevOps, both preview, state that their results include confidence intervals and tests for statistical significance.

**Three silences are recorded and two are taught.** **No minimum detectable effect is reported anywhere in the comparison output**, which is why `Inconclusive` cannot be resolved into its two meanings from what the service returns, and which is the silence phase 3 stages and point 9 names. **No dependency structure or unit of analysis appears in any field**, which is not a defect — the service did not collect the data and cannot know either — but is the reason two of point 8's five items are absent by construction. And **no agreement with human judgement is published for any evaluator producing the scores being compared**, which the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](/wiki/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md) prohibits any page in this wiki from filling; a t-test over uncalibrated scores is an exact statement about an unvalidated quantity, and the day says so. **No intra-cluster correlation magnitude is asserted anywhere**; every number in phase 3 is computed on the day's own supplied structures.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** The design-effect expression is a standard result under the exchangeable model and is presented as such, with its model stated and its scope restricted. **Point 7's emptiness of post-hoc power is a standard result of the same kind, is presented as a correction rather than as a finding, and is cited for direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is attached to it anywhere in the day.** Contrasting cases with a fixed target quantity, pre-commitment before computation, expanding-interval retrieval, adversarial completeness testing, and error management training are cited for **direction and mechanism only**. No effect size is asserted for any of them, and none is asserted for AI-assisted productivity anywhere in this paper.
