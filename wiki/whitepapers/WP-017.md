## In plain terms

Ask an expert how they do something and you will get a shorter, tidier version than the thing they actually do. This is not evasion. Familiar action becomes tacit — the steps that are always taken stop being available to introspection — and a retrospective account is built from what remains.

So an instrument made entirely of questions has a ceiling, and the corpus's discovery instrument is made entirely of questions. **Nobody in it is ever asked to demonstrate a deployment, share a screen during a pipeline run, or set up an environment while somebody watches.**

The session's method is to collect three accounts of the same activity and refuse to reconcile them. **Told** is the description. **Taught** is what happens when the person has to explain it to somebody who must do it tomorrow, which forces in the steps a describer assumes. **Done** is the performance, on their own machine, with their own data, on a real case. Where the three diverge is the finding, and averaging them away destroys it.

Two things get added that the corpus never asks about. **Data** — where it came from, who may see it, whether it is correct, whether it is labelled, whether anybody consented — asked of somebody who would know. And **consent**, which the corpus omits entirely from material that describes recording sessions and collecting telemetry from people at work.

Then the session adds a third source that neither the corpus nor the method's original setting had cheaply available. **The work leaves artefacts** — the pipeline definition, the pull-request template, the runbook, the tickets, the rota — and those were produced by the work rather than by anybody's memory of it. Indexing them makes one specific thing findable that watching does not: **the step present in the artefacts and absent from every account.**

And it creates a duty. An index is a copy, copies do not inherit their originals' access rules, chunking discards permissions unless they are deliberately carried onto every chunk, and a revocation is not immediate. **All three failures produce a system that appears to work**, which is why the session makes the learner produce two of them on purpose.

## Abstract

This paper defends direct observation as a required component of discovery and gives the reason as a property of expertise rather than of honesty. The argument is that tacit routinisation makes self-report systematically incomplete, that the incompleteness is invisible to both parties, and that a discovery instrument composed entirely of self-report therefore has a ceiling no improvement in question quality can raise. The session installs a told–taught–done sequence, an observation record that separates observed from reported from inferred, an interpretation checked in context with the person, and a data-and-consent block the source instrument does not contain.

It then defends a third source alongside the account and the observation: **the artefacts the work produced**, indexed and queried against the described process. The argument for it is that an artefact is not subject to the mechanism that makes self-report incomplete, so it exposes a divergence class that neither asking nor watching reaches — and that the cost of assembling one has fallen far enough to change what a discovery engagement can include. The paper then treats the resulting duty as part of the design rather than as a caveat: an index is a copy that does not inherit its source's access rules, chunking discards permissions unless they are deliberately projected, and enforcement lags the source system by an interval nobody publishes. **The session produces two of those failures deliberately, because all three are silent.**

The paper concedes that the contextual design method behind it establishes no effect; that its consent guidance is a practitioner account from one jurisdiction and is not legal advice; that the session's refusal of the simulated informant is an argument nobody has tested; and — in its strongest objection — that the design creates in a learner's hands exactly the exposure it spends a section warning them about, with mitigations that are partial and a declined fix whose cost is named.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

The discovery instrument a learner will be handed is a list of questions, and it will feel complete.

Its incompleteness has a specific shape. It cannot recover the steps the practitioner does not know they take, the exceptions that are handled without being noticed, the artefacts that are consulted reflexively, the interruptions that structure the work, or the workaround so old that it has become the process. **None of that is withheld. It is absent from the account because it is absent from the account-maker's access to their own practice.**

There is a second problem, which is that a request for a feature is shaped by the tools the person already has. A customer asking for a faster pipeline is describing their pipeline. A customer observed working reveals where the time actually goes, which is frequently not where the request pointed.

The session's response is to add a mechanism that does not depend on the informant's introspection, and to be explicit that it is an addition rather than a replacement.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) is required. A learner who has not repaired a question set will use observation as a supplement to a leading interview and will get a demonstration of the answer they wanted.

The prior it works against is that observation is what you do when the interview was inadequate. Learners treat it as a fallback. **The session's move is that the two answer different questions**, and that no amount of interviewing settles the ones observation settles.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate to high. The three passes are individually simple; the load is in maintaining the separation between observed, reported and inferred while the activity is running, which requires holding a categorisation and a narrative at once.

**3.2 Why context is the unit.** People know their own activities intimately but do not ordinarily articulate their structure; general questions get general answers; and requested features are shaped by known tools. The response is a master–apprentice stance — the person is the practitioner, the observer is learning — disciplined by staying close to ongoing action and specific past events, investigating with the person rather than receiving a tour, stating interpretations aloud so they can be corrected, and using the project's concern to decide what to pursue while treating surprises as possible corrections to that concern. **This is a method's internal argument, not a finding.**

**3.3 Why the interpretation must be checked in the room.** An interpretation formed and recorded silently is the observer's view of somebody else's work, and it hardens. Checking it while the context is present costs a sentence and produces either a confirmation or the more valuable case — a correction that reveals the distinction the observer was missing.

**3.4 Why divergence is preserved rather than resolved.** Told, taught and done disagreeing is a signal about which parts of the practice are visible to the practitioner, and resolving it by preferring one account discards exactly that signal. **The instruction to explore the divergence rather than average it away is a practitioner rule; nothing measures it.**

**3.5 Why an artefact is a second source rather than a convenience.**

The contextual design method collects artefacts because they are part of the evidence, alongside relations, terminology and interruptions. The design's addition is to treat them as **an independent source with a different bias profile**, rather than as supporting material for an account.

The argument is short. An artefact was produced by the work rather than by the recollection of the work, so the mechanism that makes self-report incomplete — tacit routinisation — does not operate on it. The pipeline definition contains the step nobody mentions because a pipeline definition cannot forget. **That makes a specific class of divergence discoverable which no amount of watching would surface: the step present in the artefacts and absent from every account, including the one you observed.**

What is new is not the idea but its cost. Collecting and cross-referencing a customer's artefacts used to be a week's work; a searchable index over them is now an afternoon's, which changes what a discovery engagement can reasonably include. **The design takes that as a genuine opportunity and immediately incurs its liability**, which is §3.6.

**3.6 An index is a copy, and copies do not inherit their originals' access rules.**

This is the reason the platform movement is not optional decoration in an observation session. The moment a learner indexes somebody else's material they have created a second copy with its own permission model, and the permissions do not travel with the content ([research/06-microsoft-platform/azure-ai-search-and-acl-patterns.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/azure-ai-search-and-acl-patterns.md)).

The documentation is clear about why this matters more when the index feeds generation: a conventional interface returning a forbidden document shows a link the user cannot open, whereas a retrieval-augmented system reads the content into the answer. **The leak is the substance rather than the listing.**

Three of its facts are load-bearing for a discovery record. The generally available approach is a **security filter**, which the documentation itself calls a workaround relative to identity-recognised permission filters, because it is a string comparison and nothing at the service validates that the caller is who the filter says — **the trust boundary sits in the application code.** Native enforcement performs two checks in sequence, application authorisation then per-document user authorisation, which is the correct general shape and worth extracting from the product. And the guidance to **prefer group access over individual access** is an operational judgement a discovery team will otherwise learn the hard way.

**3.7 The two failures that produce a system which appears to work.**

Both are named in the same documentation and both are silent.

**Chunking discards permissions unless they are projected.** When a skillset chunks documents the permission metadata moves from indexer field mappings to index projections, and without that projection the chunk-level references are not filtered. An unprojected chunk is an unprotected chunk, and chunking is standard practice. **This is the single most instructive failure available in the topic and it takes minutes to produce deliberately**, which is why the session produces it deliberately.

**Enforcement lags the source system.** Query-time checks evaluate the caller's claims against permission metadata already in the index, so a revocation takes effect only after synchronisation. The documentation states this repeatedly and **publishes no latency figures**, which means the size of the window is unknown and must be measured per deployment. The session has the learner measure it, because a number they produced is the only number they have.

**Its limits, and they are severe.** Most of the page describes a preview API surface that changes without notice; the terms place compliance, geographic-boundary and testing responsibility explicitly on the implementer; the four approaches are compared on fit rather than on security strength; and the source is single-vendor, so the general principles transfer and the mechanisms do not.

**3.8 Why a retrieval defect is located before it is fixed.**

The learner's index will return something unhelpful, and the instinct will be to change the query or the model. The failure taxonomy for retrieval-augmented systems locates defects by stage — corpus, retrieval, context assembly, generation — and the first of them, **missing content, is a fact about what was indexed** ([research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md)). In this session that is not a technical inconvenience; it is a finding, because a missing artefact is a step nobody produces a record for.

**Its limits.** An experience report from three deployments; no frequency data, no severity ranking, no controlled comparison; and this repository's note records that the specific failure-point names are reconstructed rather than quoted.

One further caution transfers from the index literature and it is epistemological rather than operational: approximate-nearest-neighbour recall is measured against a ground truth computed by exact search over a fixed dataset, and **says nothing about whether the retrieved items are the right results for the user's actual information need** ([research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md)). A perfectly functioning index can answer the wrong question, and so can a perfectly conducted observation. **Verified from the abstract; its 2016 and 2018 comparisons are against the open-source state of the art of that period, and the explanatory account of greedy routing in this repository's note is a reconstruction.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**The order told–taught–done is fixed and is not arbitrary.** Told first, because a description given after a demonstration is contaminated by the demonstration. Taught second, because teaching surfaces assumed steps while still being cheap. Done last, because it is the most expensive to arrange and because the earlier two supply the questions worth asking during it.

**The record's separation is the artefact, not the content.** The review movement removes the section labels and asks a peer to restore them. A learner who cannot be reconstructed has the separation in their head rather than in the document, and the document is the only part that survives them.

**The data block is written by the learner because the source does not supply one.** This is an addition and the session says so. Its questions are directed at the person who would know, which is rarely the sponsor who issued the invitation.

**The simulated informant is refused explicitly.** The corpus proposes substituting a model's simulation of a user for a user and offers no discussion of validity — in a collection that opens by warning against building for an imagined user. **The programme's position is that a simulated informant is not an informant. That is design reasoning with no external warrant**, it is stated as such, and the learner is invited to argue against it in writing.

**What the platform movement is for, and it is two things.**

The first is evidential. Indexing the artefacts makes a divergence discoverable by query rather than by memory: the learner searches for the artefact each described step should have produced, and both empty results are findings — a step with no artefact, and an artefact with no step. **That second case is the tacit routine, located mechanically.**

The second is a governance lesson the session would otherwise have to assert. A discovery engineer who has copied a customer's runbooks into a search service has taken on a duty, and the two failures in §3.7 are the ones that will not announce themselves. **The design requires the learner to produce the chunk-permission leak deliberately and to time a revocation**, because both produce a system that appears to work and neither is memorable when read.

The consent-and-handling section of the record and the index's own fields are deliberately the same information in two places. **A record that says what was excluded, and an index that carries the same exclusion as a field, are the difference between a stated intention and an enforced one** — and the session is explicit that the field is a filter rather than a guarantee, because the trust boundary sits in the application code.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The polished demonstration.** The likeliest failure. The person performs a clean version and the observer records the clean version. *Countermeasure:* the requirement that *done* uses the most recent real case rather than a chosen one, and the standard's warning sign — nothing went wrong.

**The unchecked interpretation.** *Countermeasure:* the record must contain at least one correction the person made. A record with no corrections is treated as evidence that no interpretation was stated aloud.

**The averaged divergence.** *Countermeasure:* the record's structure has room for three accounts and the standard requires at least one preserved divergence.

**The captured credential.** The observer records something they were not entitled to because it was on screen. *Countermeasure:* the consent-and-handling section, which requires stating what was excluded. **This is partial and the paper does not overstate it — a section in a record does not prevent a screenshot**, and the session is explicit that the governing rules are the customer's and this programme does not know them.

**Observation as ceremony.** A direct test would have answered the question faster. *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — you already knew what you would see — and the routing exercise at [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md).

**The indexed leak.** A learner copies a customer's artefacts into an index without carrying the permissions, and produces exactly the exposure the session is warning about. *Countermeasure:* the permission field is required before content is loaded, and the chunk leak is produced deliberately so the learner has seen it. **This is a real countermeasure and an incomplete one** — a lab index built from material the learner was permitted to see is not the case that matters, and nothing here transfers the habit to an engagement where nobody is watching.

**The artefact promoted over the person.** A learner finds the index more informative than the observation and stops checking interpretations with the practitioner. *Countermeasure:* partial, and the design is uneasy about it. The record still requires a correction the person made, so a learner who abandoned the conversation fails that requirement — but **the requirement can be satisfied early and then ignored**, and the failure would look like efficiency. Prediction 6 in §9 is the only thing that would detect it.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: observation is expensive, intrusive and frequently impossible, and a method that requires it will simply be skipped.**

At its strongest: enterprise customers will not grant an outsider a seat beside an engineer performing a production deployment. Security will refuse, the data is regulated, the schedule does not permit it, and the person being watched has a job to do. A design that makes observation a requirement guarantees either a fabricated observation record or an abandoned method, and the corpus's omission of observation may be a realistic accommodation rather than an oversight.

**Partly conceded.** Access is a real constraint and the contextual design source anticipates it, offering reconstruction from traces, cross-role study for long processes, accompaniment for mobile work, and review of recordings with the participant where direct observation is impossible. The rebuttal is that these are adaptations with named costs rather than substitutes, and that **the adaptation must be recorded so the resulting claim's warrant is visibly weaker.** The declined fix is to make observation optional where access is difficult. It is declined because *difficult* is judged by the person who would have to arrange it. **Whether the requirement survives contact with a real security review is untested and the paper does not pretend otherwise.**

**Objection 2: refusing the simulated informant will not survive, and the refusal is stated more confidently than the evidence supports.**

At its strongest: a model that has ingested a great deal of writing about how engineers work can produce plausible accounts of a workflow at negligible cost, and the practical alternative is frequently not a real informant but no informant at all. Refusing the simulation on principle, with no validity study in either direction, is a preference presented as a standard — and the paper's own complaint about the corpus is that it asserts without warrant.

**Conceded, and the symmetry is uncomfortable.** The refusal has no more warrant than the proposal it refuses. The design's position is narrow: a simulation cannot supply the class of evidence this session exists to obtain, because the whole argument for observation is that practitioners cannot articulate their own tacit steps — and a model trained on articulations has access to exactly the articulated layer. **The declined fix is to permit a simulated informant where no real one is available, with the substitution recorded.** It is declined because a recorded substitution in a discovery packet becomes an unrecorded one by the time the packet is summarised, and that is design reasoning too. **A learner who finds this unconvincing should say so; the session asks for the argument in writing.**

**Objection 3: the session teaches learners to build the exposure it spends a section warning them about.**

At its strongest: a discovery engineer is a guest. The session hands them a pattern for copying a customer's runbooks, deployment tickets and internal chat into a search service they control, in an engagement with no data-processing agreement written for it, under consent obtained verbally for an observation. The warnings about chunk projection and revocation lag are good warnings, and they are also an admission that the artefact the learner is being taught to build is one whose failure modes are silent. **A design that genuinely believed those warnings would teach the analysis without the index** — have the learner list the artefacts and read them, which requires no copy, no permission model and no revocation window.

**Substantially conceded, and it is the strongest objection in this paper.** The liability is real, it is created by the session, and the mitigations are partial: a permission field is a filter whose trust boundary is in the application, a consent line in a record does not bind anybody, and the documentation places compliance responsibility on the implementer rather than on the platform. The rebuttal is narrow and has two parts. First, **the artefact-versus-account divergence is discoverable at scale only by query**, and a learner who reads a dozen documents by hand will find the divergences they were already looking for, which is the failure the whole session exists to prevent. Second, **the copy will be made whether or not this programme teaches it** — the tooling is a day's work and the commercial pressure to use it is considerable — so the choice is between a learner who has produced the chunk leak deliberately in a lab and one who produces it accidentally in an engagement.

**The declined fix is to run the movement on synthetic artefacts supplied by the programme rather than on the customer's own.** It is declined because a synthetic corpus has no tacit steps in it: the divergence the session is looking for exists only because a real organisation produced real records inconsistently with its own account, and a corpus written to contain a divergence teaches the learner to find one that was planted. **The cost of that rejection is exactly the exposure this objection names**, and prediction 5 is the only thing that measures whether the mitigation holds.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners' *done* pass will surface at least one step absent from their *told* pass. *Instrument:* the two accounts, compared by a second reader for steps present in one and not the other.

**Prediction 2.** Records will contain fewer stated-and-corrected interpretations than the standard requires. *Instrument:* count of recorded corrections per record.

**Prediction 3.** In the label-restoration review, peers will most often misclassify inference as observation. *Instrument:* the restoration errors, coded by direction.

**Prediction 4.** Learners who wrote a written argument against the simulated-informant refusal will produce more specific consent-and-handling sections than those who did not. *Instrument:* the sections, coded for specificity, against whether an argument was submitted. **This prediction has a plausible confound in general conscientiousness and the design does not control for it.** Recorded rather than fixed.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of first indexes will omit the permission projection onto chunks, and a majority of those learners will not discover it until instructed to query as an unentitled identity. *Instrument:* the submitted index definition, coded for projection presence, against whether the leak was reported before or after step 3. **This is the direct test of §8's third objection's mitigation.** If most learners find it only when told to look, the claim that a deliberate lab leak inoculates against an accidental engagement leak is unsupported and the movement should be restructured or dropped.

**Prediction 6.** Learners whose index returned an artefact with no corresponding described step will record fewer stated-and-corrected interpretations than those whose index did not. *Instrument:* correction counts against a binary coding of whether an unaccounted artefact was found. **This predicts the failure of §5's artefact-promotion countermeasure** — the more the index gives, the less the learner asks the person.

**Prediction 7.** Most learners will report a measurable window between revoking an entitlement and the document ceasing to be retrievable, and most will not have anticipated that a window exists. *Instrument:* a single free-text item at the close, coded for whether the revocation lag is named as surprising; plus the measured window submitted with the record, whose value is recorded per deployment and **is not compared across learners, because it is a property of their environment rather than of their work.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That self-report has a ceiling caused by the nature of expertise, that told–taught–done exposes it, that the divergence is the finding, and that the artefacts the work produced are a third source with a different bias profile from either account.

**What the evidence licenses.** A method and its internal argument. The contextual design source states directly that its effectiveness rests on practitioner experience rather than comparative evidence. The platform documentation is definitional: it describes what the service does, publishes no latency for the failure that matters most, compares its approaches on fit rather than on safety, and places the compliance duty on you. **Direction and mechanism only, and nothing in this session is compliance guidance — the rules governing your observation are the customer's, the jurisdiction's and your employer's, and this programme does not know them.**

**The concession worth remembering.** Two. The refusal of the simulated informant is as unwarranted as the proposal it refuses, and the design holds it on a narrow argument about what a simulation can have access to. And the session hands you a pattern for copying somebody else's confidential material into a service you control, having told you that its most consequential failures are silent. **That is the objection this paper concedes most fully, and the mitigation is that you will have produced the leak yourself, in a lab, before you produce it anywhere else.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The fixed told–taught–done order; the label-restoration review; the requirement that a record contain a correction; the addition of a data-and-consent block the source does not supply; the treatment of artefacts as an independent source with a different bias profile rather than as supporting material; the decision to index a customer's own artefacts rather than a synthetic corpus; the claim that a deliberately produced chunk leak inoculates against an accidental one; and the refusal of the simulated informant, which is an argument and is marked as one. All seven predictions in §9, one of which carries an uncontrolled confound.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** That an index is a copy whose permissions must be deliberately propagated from ingestion through query execution; that four distinct approaches exist — security filters, generally available and API-agnostic; identity-recognised permission filters; sensitivity-label evaluation; and native SharePoint permission ingestion — with published selection criteria reducing to the principle of reusing the authoritative permission system; that the documentation itself characterises the security-filter pattern as a workaround relative to identity-recognised permission filters, and that it is a string comparison; that native enforcement performs two checks in sequence, first the application's index-read permission and then per-document user and group permissions carried on a separate request header; that group access is recommended over individual access for manageability; that chunking moves permission metadata from indexer field mappings to index projections and that without projection chunk-level references are not filtered; and that query-time enforcement evaluates the caller's claims against permission metadata already stored in the index, so source-side permission changes take effect only after synchronisation — all recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/azure-ai-search-and-acl-patterns.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/azure-ai-search-and-acl-patterns.md), from a full fetch and reading of the page.

**The silences that matter.** **No synchronisation-latency figure is published anywhere in that documentation**, so the size of the revocation window is unknown and must be measured per deployment — which is why the session has the learner measure it and why no value from any learner is compared against another's. **The four approaches are compared on fit and never on security strength**, so the documentation cannot tell a reader which is safer, and this repository does not supply that analysis either. The preview terms **place responsibility for compliance, geographic data boundaries, testing and responsible-AI mitigations explicitly on the implementer**, which means the platform documents a capability and declines to document a duty. **Most of the page describes a preview API surface** that may change without notice; feature availability differs between API versions and SDK packages, so any lab must pin versions; and the whole source is single-vendor, so its principles transfer and its mechanisms do not. Finally, **the ranking and relevance surface of the same service was not consulted for this repository at all**, so nothing in this paper or its session may be relied on for how results are ordered — an omission recorded here rather than quietly filled in.
