## In plain terms

Ask an expert how they do something and you will get a shorter, tidier version than the thing they actually do. This is not evasion. Familiar action becomes tacit — the steps that are always taken stop being available to introspection — and a retrospective account is built from what remains.

So an instrument made entirely of questions has a ceiling, and the corpus's discovery instrument is made entirely of questions. **Nobody in it is ever asked to demonstrate a deployment, share a screen during a pipeline run, or set up an environment while somebody watches.**

The session's method is to collect three accounts of the same activity and refuse to reconcile them. **Told** is the description. **Taught** is what happens when the person has to explain it to somebody who must do it tomorrow, which forces in the steps a describer assumes. **Done** is the performance, on their own machine, with their own data, on a real case. Where the three diverge is the finding, and averaging them away destroys it.

Two things get added that the corpus never asks about. **Data** — where it came from, who may see it, whether it is correct, whether it is labelled, whether anybody consented — asked of somebody who would know. And **consent**, which the corpus omits entirely from material that describes recording sessions and collecting telemetry from people at work.

## Abstract

This paper defends direct observation as a required component of discovery and gives the reason as a property of expertise rather than of honesty. The argument is that tacit routinisation makes self-report systematically incomplete, that the incompleteness is invisible to both parties, and that a discovery instrument composed entirely of self-report therefore has a ceiling no improvement in question quality can raise. The session installs a told–taught–done sequence, an observation record that separates observed from reported from inferred, an interpretation checked in context with the person, and a data-and-consent block the source instrument does not contain. The paper concedes that the contextual design method behind it establishes no effect, that its consent guidance is a practitioner account from one jurisdiction and is not legal advice, and that the session's refusal of the simulated informant is an argument nobody has tested.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

The discovery instrument a learner will be handed is a list of questions, and it will feel complete.

Its incompleteness has a specific shape. It cannot recover the steps the practitioner does not know they take, the exceptions that are handled without being noticed, the artefacts that are consulted reflexively, the interruptions that structure the work, or the workaround so old that it has become the process. **None of that is withheld. It is absent from the account because it is absent from the account-maker's access to their own practice.**

There is a second problem, which is that a request for a feature is shaped by the tools the person already has. A customer asking for a faster pipeline is describing their pipeline. A customer observed working reveals where the time actually goes, which is frequently not where the request pointed.

The session's response is to add a mechanism that does not depend on the informant's introspection, and to be explicit that it is an addition rather than a replacement.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) is required. A learner who has not repaired a question set will use observation as a supplement to a leading interview and will get a demonstration of the answer they wanted.

The prior it works against is that observation is what you do when the interview was inadequate. Learners treat it as a fallback. **The session's move is that the two answer different questions**, and that no amount of interviewing settles the ones observation settles.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate to high. The three passes are individually simple; the load is in maintaining the separation between observed, reported and inferred while the activity is running, which requires holding a categorisation and a narrative at once.

**3.2 Why context is the unit.** People know their own activities intimately but do not ordinarily articulate their structure; general questions get general answers; and requested features are shaped by known tools. The response is a master–apprentice stance — the person is the practitioner, the observer is learning — disciplined by staying close to ongoing action and specific past events, investigating with the person rather than receiving a tour, stating interpretations aloud so they can be corrected, and using the project's concern to decide what to pursue while treating surprises as possible corrections to that concern. **This is a method's internal argument, not a finding.**

**3.3 Why the interpretation must be checked in the room.** An interpretation formed and recorded silently is the observer's view of somebody else's work, and it hardens. Checking it while the context is present costs a sentence and produces either a confirmation or the more valuable case — a correction that reveals the distinction the observer was missing.

**3.4 Why divergence is preserved rather than resolved.** Told, taught and done disagreeing is a signal about which parts of the practice are visible to the practitioner, and resolving it by preferring one account discards exactly that signal. **The instruction to explore the divergence rather than average it away is a practitioner rule; nothing measures it.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**The order told–taught–done is fixed and is not arbitrary.** Told first, because a description given after a demonstration is contaminated by the demonstration. Taught second, because teaching surfaces assumed steps while still being cheap. Done last, because it is the most expensive to arrange and because the earlier two supply the questions worth asking during it.

**The record's separation is the artefact, not the content.** The review movement removes the section labels and asks a peer to restore them. A learner who cannot be reconstructed has the separation in their head rather than in the document, and the document is the only part that survives them.

**The data block is written by the learner because the source does not supply one.** This is an addition and the session says so. Its questions are directed at the person who would know, which is rarely the sponsor who issued the invitation.

**The simulated informant is refused explicitly.** The corpus proposes substituting a model's simulation of a user for a user and offers no discussion of validity — in a collection that opens by warning against building for an imagined user. **The programme's position is that a simulated informant is not an informant. That is design reasoning with no external warrant**, it is stated as such, and the learner is invited to argue against it in writing.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The polished demonstration.** The likeliest failure. The person performs a clean version and the observer records the clean version. *Countermeasure:* the requirement that *done* uses the most recent real case rather than a chosen one, and the standard's warning sign — nothing went wrong.

**The unchecked interpretation.** *Countermeasure:* the record must contain at least one correction the person made. A record with no corrections is treated as evidence that no interpretation was stated aloud.

**The averaged divergence.** *Countermeasure:* the record's structure has room for three accounts and the standard requires at least one preserved divergence.

**The captured credential.** The observer records something they were not entitled to because it was on screen. *Countermeasure:* the consent-and-handling section, which requires stating what was excluded. **This is partial and the paper does not overstate it — a section in a record does not prevent a screenshot**, and the session is explicit that the governing rules are the customer's and this programme does not know them.

**Observation as ceremony.** A direct test would have answered the question faster. *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — you already knew what you would see — and the routing exercise at [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md).

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: observation is expensive, intrusive and frequently impossible, and a method that requires it will simply be skipped.**

At its strongest: enterprise customers will not grant an outsider a seat beside an engineer performing a production deployment. Security will refuse, the data is regulated, the schedule does not permit it, and the person being watched has a job to do. A design that makes observation a requirement guarantees either a fabricated observation record or an abandoned method, and the corpus's omission of observation may be a realistic accommodation rather than an oversight.

**Partly conceded.** Access is a real constraint and the contextual design source anticipates it, offering reconstruction from traces, cross-role study for long processes, accompaniment for mobile work, and review of recordings with the participant where direct observation is impossible. The rebuttal is that these are adaptations with named costs rather than substitutes, and that **the adaptation must be recorded so the resulting claim's warrant is visibly weaker.** The declined fix is to make observation optional where access is difficult. It is declined because *difficult* is judged by the person who would have to arrange it. **Whether the requirement survives contact with a real security review is untested and the paper does not pretend otherwise.**

**Objection 2: refusing the simulated informant will not survive, and the refusal is stated more confidently than the evidence supports.**

At its strongest: a model that has ingested a great deal of writing about how engineers work can produce plausible accounts of a workflow at negligible cost, and the practical alternative is frequently not a real informant but no informant at all. Refusing the simulation on principle, with no validity study in either direction, is a preference presented as a standard — and the paper's own complaint about the corpus is that it asserts without warrant.

**Conceded, and the symmetry is uncomfortable.** The refusal has no more warrant than the proposal it refuses. The design's position is narrow: a simulation cannot supply the class of evidence this session exists to obtain, because the whole argument for observation is that practitioners cannot articulate their own tacit steps — and a model trained on articulations has access to exactly the articulated layer. **The declined fix is to permit a simulated informant where no real one is available, with the substitution recorded.** It is declined because a recorded substitution in a discovery packet becomes an unrecorded one by the time the packet is summarised, and that is design reasoning too. **A learner who finds this unconvincing should say so; the session asks for the argument in writing.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners' *done* pass will surface at least one step absent from their *told* pass. *Instrument:* the two accounts, compared by a second reader for steps present in one and not the other.

**Prediction 2.** Records will contain fewer stated-and-corrected interpretations than the standard requires. *Instrument:* count of recorded corrections per record.

**Prediction 3.** In the label-restoration review, peers will most often misclassify inference as observation. *Instrument:* the restoration errors, coded by direction.

**Prediction 4.** Learners who wrote a written argument against the simulated-informant refusal will produce more specific consent-and-handling sections than those who did not. *Instrument:* the sections, coded for specificity, against whether an argument was submitted. **This prediction has a plausible confound in general conscientiousness and the design does not control for it.** Recorded rather than fixed.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That self-report has a ceiling caused by the nature of expertise, that told–taught–done exposes it, and that the divergence is the finding.

**What the evidence licenses.** A method and its internal argument. The contextual design source states directly that its effectiveness rests on practitioner experience rather than comparative evidence. **Direction and mechanism only.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The refusal of the simulated informant is as unwarranted as the proposal it refuses. The design holds it on a narrow argument about what a simulation can have access to, and says so rather than dressing it as a finding.
