# WP-009 — The Cost of Doing Nothing

**Seminar day:** [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md) · **Module:** [M02](/wiki/modules/M02.md) · **Quarter:** [Q1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 2
**Platform anchor:** Azure API Management AI gateway and provisioned throughput — sizing token cost and capacity commitment for the do-nothing case as well as the do-something case

> ## Release rule
>
> **§§4, 5 and 9 are post-session reading.** §4 explains why an absent baseline is a passing answer and records the amendment that added a personal-cost block to the artefact, §5 names the failure the session is designed to produce, and §9 gives the predicted cohort behaviour. §§1, 2, 3 and 8 spoil nothing.
>
> **§3.8 is the exception and may be read before the session.** It is where [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md)'s concession about psychological safety is discharged, and a learner who is about to be asked what a refusal costs them personally is entitled to know why they are being asked.

> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Baseline** | A measurement of the current state, taken with a named instrument, against which a later measurement could be compared |
> | **Attribution** | Establishing that an observed change was caused by the work rather than coinciding with it |
> | **Do-nothing option** | The course in which the engagement does not happen, priced as an option rather than treated as the origin |
> | **BATNA** | The best course actually available if no agreement is reached; the standard against which any offer is judged |
> | **Trip wire** | A pre-set threshold, worse than the hoped-for result and better than the alternative, that triggers reassessment rather than a decision |
> | **Quota / capacity** | Permission to deploy a quantity, versus that quantity actually being available to deploy now |
> | **Idle cost** | What is paid while nothing is happening — the axis on which dedicated capacity differs from shared |
> | **Counter key** | The dimension a consumption limit is enforced against; a fairness decision wearing a configuration setting's clothes |

## In plain terms

This session exists to stop the previous four from producing a cynic.

A learner who has been taught to look for disqualifying conditions will find them, because they are everywhere. Nothing in an exercise punishes a refusal that should not have happened, so the incentive runs entirely one way. The correction is to put the alternative on the page: if this does not happen, something continues, somebody keeps paying for it, and that is a cost like any other.

The session also carries the programme's first serious encounter with measurement, and it is a negative one. The framework it works against is unusual and instructive: **it contains no exaggerated figures because it contains no figures at all.** It tells you to establish a baseline and never shows one being established. It catalogues metrics and never reports one measured. It ships models that will produce numbers, and asserts nothing those numbers could be checked against. That is a worse failure than over-claiming, and it is the one worth recognising, because an over-claim is locatable and refutable and this is neither.

And it does one thing the earlier version of this session did not. **It prices the refusal to the person making it.** Speaking up benefits the organisation later and uncertainly; the cost lands on you immediately and certainly. A programme that teaches refusal and never says that has sold you a capability with the price tag removed.

## Abstract

This paper defends requiring the alternative to be priced before a refusal is permitted; defends accepting an explicitly absent baseline as a complete answer; and defends extending the artefact to price the refusal's cost to the person making it.

The argument runs in five parts. **First**, a refusal without a costed alternative is indistinguishable from a preference, and the module preceding this session has made refusal easy in every respect except this one. **Second**, the commonest measurement failure in delivery is not exaggeration but non-closure, in which a well-built apparatus produces figures that rest on nothing checkable — and non-closure is harder to refute than over-claiming because there is no proposition to check. **Third**, an absent baseline is not a gap in the paperwork; it is a decision, already taken, that the outcome will be unknowable, and the measurement literature's minimum-detectable-effect argument gives the general form of why. **Fourth**, the do-something side has an economics most engineers meet first in production, and two of its properties belong in a qualification decision: capacity held whether or not it is used must be paid for whether or not it is used, and permission to deploy is not the same as availability to deploy. **Fifth**, the refusal itself has an asymmetric price which falls on the individual, and pricing it is how this programme discharges the concession [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md) made about teaching a voice behaviour as though it were an individual skill.

The paper records an amendment made while writing it: the do-nothing statement as first designed priced the customer's alternative and not the learner's, which meant the whole module could be completed while treating refusal as free to the person refusing. The correction is documented at §4.5 rather than applied silently.

The paper concedes that requiring a costed alternative may deter legitimate refusals; that the named-attributor requirement is untested; that the session hands learners a diagnosis they will rarely have the standing to act on; and that pricing a personal risk may be either useless, because the price is unknowable in advance, or harmful, because naming it makes it vivid.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Three problems, and the third is new to this revision.

**First, refusal has to be expensive.** [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md) predicts refusal-as-reflex as a failure mode and names this session as its countermeasure. That commitment is discharged here: [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) will not accept a refusal that does not carry the cost of the alternative inside it.

**Second, the cost side of a delivery proposal is engineered and the return side is elicited**, and the asymmetry is invisible because both arrive in the same document. Consumption is forecast by engineers, instrumented by engineers, and refined against telemetry from their own system. Return is chosen by the customer from a menu, measured by the customer using instrumentation the customer may not possess, over a period nobody specifies, with causation assumed. A ratio whose denominator is well instrumented and whose numerator is not is not a well-instrumented ratio, and it will feel like one.

**Third, the programme has an outstanding debt.** [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md)'s fourth objection concedes that teaching refusal without teaching the conditions that permit it is close to negligent, that the session does not address it, and that this session and [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) do — by requiring the cost of the refusal to be sized alongside the cost of the work. **That concession named a mechanism and the earlier version of this session did not implement it.** §4.5 records the repair.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Either**, plus the decision from [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md) and the run-cost work from [S008](/wiki/seminars/S008.md).

Learners with finance, operations or measurement backgrounds arrive holding the baseline concept and usually not the attribution one. Learners from build backgrounds frequently hold neither, and are more likely to produce a cost of proceeding than a cost of not.

The platform half has a third profile. Almost nobody arrives holding the quota-versus-capacity distinction, because it is the kind of thing that is learned when a deployment fails rather than when a document is read. **That is precisely why it is taught from documentation here**: the alternative is to learn it from an outage in an engagement somebody is paying for.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is the highest in the stage and higher than the earlier version carried. The artefact now requires six distinguishable things, and learners routinely collapse the first two, skip the fourth, and treat the sixth as decoration. The design accepts the load because every one of the six is a place where a proposal is normally silent, and a shorter artefact would be a shorter silence.

**3.2 Why an absent baseline is a passing answer.** A learner who invents a baseline to fill a column has produced exactly the failure this session is against. Declaring the absence, and naming who would have to produce a measurement, is both honest and actionable; **the design's position is that a recorded absence is more useful than a fabricated presence, and this is an argument rather than a finding.**

**3.3 Why attribution is assigned to a person.** Responsibility that is not assigned is not held. The requirement makes visible a gap that otherwise appears nowhere: in most proposals no party is responsible for establishing that an observed change was caused by the work, and several metric definitions presuppose the causal claim inside their own wording.

**3.4 An absent baseline is a decision that the outcome will be unknowable, and the measurement literature gives the general form.**

Miller's argument is that an evaluation is an experiment and that the decisive quantity for reading a null result is the **minimum detectable effect** — the smallest true difference an instrument of that size and design could reliably register ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). His practical consequence is that "we ran it and saw no difference" is not a finding until you can state what you could have seen, and his paper's structure demonstrates the stakes: a single worked comparison carried through the whole argument reverses its apparent winner once the analysis is done correctly.

The transfer to this session is direct and it is this design's own. **If nobody counted the thing before, then afterwards nobody can say whether it changed** — and that is not a documentation gap, it is a decision about knowability that has already been taken, silently, by nobody in particular. The session's requirement to name who would have to produce a baseline converts an ambient absence into an assigned one.

**Its limits.** The paper is analytical rather than empirical; most of its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions and are worked examples rather than constants; it addresses comparing scores rather than choosing what to score; and its machinery assumes questions drawn from a well-defined population, which a hand-picked set of examples is not. **No number from it appears in this paper or its session.**

**3.5 BATNA is the right instrument, and the trip wire is the device learners have never met.**

Fisher and Ury's alternative-to-agreement is precisely the object this session asks learners to construct: the course actually available if no agreement is reached, and the standard against which any proposed package is judged ([research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md)). Their argument against a bottom line is the reason the session does not ask for one: a bottom line offers psychological protection, hides information, and prevents consideration of packages that meet interests in an unexpected shape. **An unknown alternative is dangerous in both directions** — a party may accept less than it was worth, or reject more.

The **trip wire** is the less familiar device and the more useful one here. It is a threshold worse than the hoped-for result and better than the alternative, set in advance, whose function is to force reassessment before drift becomes commitment. It is not a refusal condition, and a learner who writes one that reads like a refusal condition has produced a bottom line under a softer name.

**Its limits.** Practitioner framework, developed by analytic distinction and worked case rather than controlled comparison; its historical cases illustrate mechanism without isolating causes; **it reports no evaluation of the complete method**, and no effectiveness claim for teaching it is warranted by it.

**3.6 Why capacity economics belongs in a qualification session.**

The platform documentation states one clause from which everything else follows: a provisioned deployment holds a fixed amount of processing capacity exclusively for that deployment's use, whether or not requests are being made ([research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md)). Capacity held regardless of use is paid for regardless of use, which is why the billing unit changes from tokens to time, and why the decision becomes a capacity commitment rather than a configuration setting.

Two properties from that page are qualification-relevant rather than operational. **Quota is not capacity**: quota is a policy limit with no cost, capacity is what is actually available now, capacity fluctuates through the day with demand across all customers, releasing it carries no guarantee of getting it back, and the documentation states plainly that having quota does not guarantee capacity. **And a reservation is a discount on a billing meter rather than a hold on anything**, so the correct sequence is deploy, verify, commit — while the intuitive sequence, commit then deploy, is exactly wrong.

The gateway material adds the fairness half ([research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md)). Tokens are a shared finite resource distributed across consumers; with one application a limit on the deployment suffices; as a portfolio grows, the concern is that one application does not consume the whole allocation and block the others. **A shared finite resource with no per-consumer accounting will be consumed by whichever consumer is least careful**, and the counter key that a token-limit policy is enforced against is therefore a fairness decision rather than a technical one. The corresponding observability point is equally general: metric dimensions are what make spend attributable, and attribution is what makes governance possible.

**Both sources' limits.** Definitional first-party documentation. The concrete ratios, minimum deployment sizes, prices, default allocations and regional availability live on companion pages this repository did not fetch, and none is asserted. The gateway page argues for a gateway and does not discuss the cost, latency or operational burden the gateway itself adds; capability varies by service tier; several capabilities are preview; **it publishes no performance data and no cost-saving measurement of any kind**; and it is silent on the privacy consequences of the prompt logging it recommends for billing and auditing.

**3.7 Why the platform material is taught as economics rather than as configuration.**

A qualification session has no business teaching policy syntax, and this one does not — the fragment in the workflow exists so that the counter key becomes a written decision rather than an inherited default.

What the material contributes is a shape that transfers: **a cost that accrues on a schedule unrelated to usage, a permission that is not an availability, and a bill that cannot be governed until it is attributable.** All three are properties of shared infrastructure generally, and all three are invisible in a proposal that prices only the build.

**3.8 The refusal has an asymmetric price, and this is where [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md)'s concession is discharged.**

[WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md) §3.3 states that refusal is a voice behaviour and that voice behaviour is conditional on psychological safety, an environmental property. Its §8 objection 4 concedes, partially, that teaching the disposition without the environment equips a learner to take a risk the programme has not helped them assess, and names this session and [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) as where the cost of the refusal is sized.

**Discharging that concession means pricing, not reassurance.** The programme cannot make anybody's workplace safe and it can stop implying the price is zero. Edmondson's account supplies the structure: withholding is a fast risk calculation, and its payoffs are asymmetric — voice benefits the organisation later and with low certainty, silence benefits the individual immediately and with high certainty ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)). Her line that nobody was ever fired for silence is the compressed version.

The artefact therefore requires a two-column refusal cost — to the customer, and to the learner — and requires the learner's column to name what would make the cost survivable and what their own alternative is if overruled. **Those are BATNA questions applied to the refusal rather than to the engagement**, which is why the negotiation material and the safety material meet here rather than in separate sessions.

**The evidence position must be stated exactly.** The mechanism is used; no magnitude is taken. The book read for it is a trade account by the construct's originator, whose literature chapter has no search protocol, reports no disconfirming study, and whose cases are secondary and outcome-selected, with no trial of its framework reported. **The quantitative synthesis of that literature is a separate source and is used at [WP-010](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-010.md)**, where more weight rests on it. And nothing anywhere establishes that writing the cost down makes a refusal more likely, or safer.

**3.9 The counterweight, and it is the module's central risk.**

A module that spends four sessions making refusal available and one making it expensive can still produce a learner who reads the whole thing as permission to decline.

**Refusal is not the sophisticated default.** A refusal that costs the customer more than the work would have cost them is a bad refusal however well delivered, and the do-nothing statement exists to make that arithmetic visible rather than to supply a formality. The session says this in its own material rather than leaving it to this paper, because a learner who reads only the session must still meet it.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The movement sequence.** Situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits. The do-nothing column is written in workflow step 1, before any platform material, because the platform material is about the other future and a learner who meets it first will price the engagement and call the result a comparison.

**4.2 The attribution line is the session's instrument.** It is a single line, it takes moments to write, and in the great majority of cases the honest content is a name that nobody can supply. The design's claim is that writing "nobody" is a different act from not asking, and that the difference persists into how a learner reads the next proposal they are handed.

**4.3 The supplied brief is constructed so that the baseline genuinely does not exist.** Records are returned and reworked, nobody can say how often, and nobody has looked. That is not an omission in the brief. **It is the normal condition, and a session which supplied a baseline would be teaching against the situation learners will actually meet.**

**4.4 What the platform workflow is for.** To make one thing observable that prose cannot: that both futures can be written in the same units, and that when they are, the asymmetry appears on the page — one side engineered from telemetry, the other elicited from opinion.

It also surfaces the at-rest cost, which is the single item most often missing from a proposal, because a capacity commitment consumes money on a schedule unrelated to whether anybody is using the system. **A learner who has never priced idle time will price a build and call it a cost.**

**4.5 A defect found in this session's own artefact, and the amendment.**

The do-nothing statement as first designed had four elements: what continues, who pays it, the baseline or its declared absence, and the attributor.

Every one of those prices the *customer's* alternative. None of them prices the learner's. Under that design, a learner could complete this module — the module whose stated purpose is to make refusal expensive — while treating refusal as free to the person making it, and the artefact would have been complete and correct.

**That is not a small omission, because [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md) had already conceded the exact point and named this session as the discharge.** The concession was made, the mechanism was named, and the artefact did not implement it. The paper defending the artefact then cited the concession approvingly.

The amendment adds a fifth element for the cost of proceeding stated in comparable units, a sixth for the cost of the refusal in two columns, and workflow step 7 requiring the learner's own column to name what would make the cost survivable and what their alternative is if overruled. **The amendment is recorded rather than silently applied**, because the failure is a general one worth seeing: a design can concede a defect, name its remedy, schedule the remedy into a later component, and never build it — and nothing in the concession catches that. Only reading the artefact against the promise catches it. Assume there are others.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The fabricated baseline.** The most likely outcome is a plausible figure with no instrument behind it, produced because a blank column reads as incomplete work. *Countermeasure:* the specification treats a declared absence as complete, and learners are told so before the exercise rather than after it.

**Cost of proceeding, twice.** A learner prices the engagement in both columns and calls one of them the alternative. *Countermeasure:* the do-nothing column requires an event with a frequency and a bearer, which an engagement cost cannot satisfy.

**The diagnosis without standing.** A learner correctly identifies that nobody owns attribution and has no authority to assign it. *Countermeasure:* none. The session produces a finding the learner usually cannot act on, which is unsatisfying and is stated plainly rather than dressed as empowerment.

**The heroic refusal.** A learner prices the customer's cost and leaves their own column empty or rhetorical. *Countermeasure:* the artefact requires four specific things in that column — immediacy, certainty, a survivability asset and an alternative if overruled — and exercise 3 requires the learner to compare it against their own opening answer. This is the newest countermeasure and prediction 5 in §9 predicts its failure.

**False precision from the platform half.** A learner produces a capacity estimate with more confidence than its inputs support. *Countermeasure:* workflow step 2 requires the learner to write down which of the three sizing inputs they are guessing. **A sizing model is an inference from a traffic model, and the traffic model bounds the answer** — the countermeasure states this and cannot enforce it.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md). The do-nothing statement is required inside the refusal, which is the structural mechanism by which this session makes the next one expensive.

The personal-cost block added at §4.5 is retrieved twice: at [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md), where the sponsor's reasonable offer is designed to make accepting socially cheap, and in Stage 9, where the question becomes what an engagement did not establish. **A learner who has written down what would make a refusal survivable has an asset at S010 that a learner who has not does not**, and whether that is true is prediction 6.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence and no specification element of its own. The statement is carried into [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md), where element 4 of that specification requires the cost of the refusal to the customer to appear in the refusal itself.

**The learner's own cost column is deliberately not assessed anywhere in the programme.** Marking it would require an assessor to judge somebody's account of their own workplace risk, which is not a judgement a specification can make and not one this design will pretend to. It is required to exist and its contents are the learner's.

**Nothing else is assessed.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: requiring a costed alternative will suppress refusals that should happen.**

At its strongest: the parties best placed to price the alternative are the ones with an interest in proceeding, the cost of inaction is the easiest quantity in any proposal to inflate, and a rule that no refusal is permitted without one hands the sponsor a procedural weapon. The programme spends [M01](/wiki/modules/M01.md) making refusal available and then attaches a condition to it.

**Conceded in part, and the concession is precise.** The rule as written does not require the cost to be large or even measurable — a do-nothing statement whose content is that nothing continues, stated with a bearer and a frequency of never, satisfies the specification. What it forbids is silence. **The declined fix was to make the statement optional where the disqualifying condition is legal or ethical**, rejected because a learner will classify every refusal that way if the classification removes work. That rejection is a judgement about learner behaviour and it has no evidence behind it.

**Objection 2: the session teaches a critique of measurement to people who will never be asked to measure anything.**

At its strongest: the attribution question is answered, if at all, by a sponsor's finance function years after the engineer has left. Teaching an engineer to notice that nobody owns it produces frustration and no capability.

**Conceded.** The narrower defence is that the engineer is frequently the only person who sees both the instrumented cost side and the uninstrumented return side in the same week, and that a proposal is easier to challenge before it is signed than a result is to challenge after it is reported. Whether an engineer with that observation can do anything with it is untested, and the same limitation is conceded at [S002](/wiki/seminars/S002.md) about recognition without authority.

**Objection 3: asking a learner to price their own professional risk is either useless or harmful.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the §4.5 amendment most deserves: the cost of speaking up is unknowable in advance, because it depends on a manager's reaction, an organisation's mood and events nobody can forecast. Asking somebody to write it down produces a fiction. Worse, it may produce a chilling effect — a learner who has spent an hour enumerating what refusing might cost them is a learner who has rehearsed the reasons not to, which is the opposite of what the module intends. And the research the requirement rests on says the property is environmental, so asking an individual to assess it is asking the wrong party.

**Partially conceded, and the chilling-effect risk is real and unmeasured.** Three things are said in defence, and none of them fully answers it. The block asks for structure rather than magnitude — immediacy, certainty, survivability assets, an alternative — which is answerable without forecasting anybody's reaction. The alternative on offer is silence about the price, and a programme that teaches a costly act while implying it is free has misled rather than protected. And the assets the block asks for are the ones that actually change the calculation: a dated written record, a named person who has seen the same observation, an agreed escalation threshold. **The declined fix was to move the personal-cost work into the pastoral track and out of the artefact**, rejected because an unassessed, unartefacted concern in a programme built on artefacts is a concern nobody will meet. **The cost of that rejection is that the risk in this objection remains live, and prediction 7 is the only thing that would detect it.**

**Objection 4: teaching a vendor's capacity model inside qualification manufactures false precision.**

At its strongest: the sizing method converts a traffic description into a machine quantity, and in week two the traffic description is a guess. A learner who has walked the calculation will produce a number, that number will appear in a document, and its provenance will be lost by the third meeting — which is the inference-laundering failure this stage names at [S006](/wiki/seminars/S006.md), reproduced by a session two days later.

**Conceded, and the countermeasure is thin.** Workflow step 2 requires the learner to record which of the three sizing inputs they are guessing, which is the same repair [S006](/wiki/seminars/S006.md) prescribes and is no stronger here than it is there. The rebuttal is that the alternative — no cost side at all until the build stages — is what produces the proposals this session diagnoses. **The declined fix was to teach the economics without the calculation**, rejected because the structure of the calculation is where the guesses become visible, and an economics lesson with no arithmetic in it does not expose which term is soft.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first statements will contain a baseline figure with no named instrument. *Instrument:* the baseline field, coded *measured with instrument* / *asserted* / *declared absent* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most attribution lines will name nobody, and most learners will record this as a finding only after the review movement. *Instrument:* the attribution field before and after review, coded *named* / *nobody, recorded* / *blank*.

**Prediction 3.** Learners who produced a do-nothing statement with a frequency and a bearer will produce fewer refusals at [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) than those whose statement was categorical. *Instrument:* the S009 artefact's form against the S010 decision. **If this fails, the session is not making refusal expensive**, which is its stated purpose, and the commitment made at [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md) is undischarged.

**Prediction 4.** Most cost-of-proceeding entries will price the build and omit what is paid at rest, even among learners who completed the platform workflow. *Instrument:* the cost-of-proceeding block, coded for whether an idle-cost line is present and non-generic.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of personal-cost columns will be present but generic — naming discomfort rather than immediacy, certainty, a survivability asset and an alternative. *Instrument:* the learner's own column, coded against the four required properties. **This predicts the failure of the countermeasure added at §4.5**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 6.** Learners whose personal-cost column names at least one survivability asset will accept the sponsor's accommodation at [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) less often than those whose column names none. *Instrument:* the presence of a named asset at S009 against the sponsor's binary record of offer acceptance at S010. **This is the session's only test of whether pricing the refusal changes anything**, and a null result would mean the §4.5 amendment is bookkeeping.

**Prediction 7.** Learners will not report increased reluctance to refuse after completing the personal-cost block, and any observed shift will be towards more specific rather than fewer refusals. *Instrument:* a single self-report item at the close of S009 and the refusal count at S010, compared against cohorts run before the amendment. **This is the direct test of §8's third objection, and it is the prediction this paper would least like to lose.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the alternative is an option with a price; that a refusal which does not carry that price is a preference; that the deepest measurement failure in delivery work is an apparatus that produces numbers while asserting nothing; and that the refusal itself has a price which falls on you first.

**What the evidence licenses.** The diagnosis of the value framework is recorded from a complete reading and is the strongest thing here. The minimum-detectable-effect argument is a real analytical result and its transfer to baselines is this design's own. The negotiation devices are practitioner constructs with no controlled evaluation. The capacity and gateway material is definitional vendor documentation with **no performance, cost-saving or outcome data of any kind**, and none of its numbers appears here. That pricing the alternative changes decisions is unwarranted. That a named attributor changes anybody's behaviour is unwarranted. That writing down the cost of a refusal makes the refusal more likely, or safer, is unwarranted and is the thing this session most wants to be true.

**The concession worth remembering.** You will finish this session able to see a gap you have no authority to close. The programme does not pretend otherwise, here or at [S002](/wiki/seminars/S002.md). And note §4.5: this design conceded a defect in week one, named this session as the repair, and then built an artefact that did not repair it. **A scheduled concession is not a discharged one.**
