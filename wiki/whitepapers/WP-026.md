## In plain terms

This session opens the stage's second half, and it teaches an idea that **has no evidence behind it at all.** That is stated here, in the session, in the module page and in the stage page, because it is the single most important thing a reader can know about the material.

The idea is a classification. Every element of a problem space is placed on an axis running from frozen to fluid, and the axis is attached to a rule: **even in a space that is almost entirely frozen there is something that can be changed, and constraint is not an excuse for doing nothing.**

It comes from the practice material behind this programme, where it is the most transferable original idea in the collection and where **it is never once used.** No problem space is classified. No engagement is described in which the classification changed a decision. No account is given of what it costs to produce or how often it is wrong. **The material contains no reference, footnote, study or citation of any kind, anywhere**, so there is nothing behind the classification except the assertion that it is useful.

The session teaches it anyway, and the reason is stated rather than concealed: **it names a failure mode nothing else in this programme names, and it is cheap enough to test that a learner can find out for themselves in an afternoon.** That is a reason to try a method. It is not a reason to believe it.

What the session adds to the source is the part that can be checked. **A constraint with no named authority is a rumour**, and finding out how many entries in a real constraint list have no author is an empirical exercise with an answer.

The session also does something the source never contemplates: it points the learner at a governance product and has them read the *coverage* of it rather than its findings. That move is where the module's title earns itself. A constraint sourced from a posture report looks frozen and is in fact a statement about a periodic sample of part of an estate, with a delay, produced by a tool that publishes no measure of how well it detects anything. **Frozen and appears-frozen are different, and the difference is discoverable in ten minutes by reading the instrument instead of the output.**

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching an unevidenced classification, and defends being explicit about the absence rather than quietly grounding the session elsewhere. It argues that the classification's value is diagnostic rather than predictive: it produces a register in which assumed constraints are separated from stated ones and unauthored constraints become visible, both of which are checkable properties. It requires the axis to carry more than two values, so that *movable at a price* is expressible and the price is named. It imports two devices from the negotiation literature — knowing the alternative to agreement, and setting a threshold that forces reassessment before drift — carrying that source's own statement that it establishes no effect. The paper concedes that the classification may be an elaborate way of writing down what an experienced engineer already knows, that its cost is unmeasured, and that the corpus supplying it contradicts its own rule elsewhere in the same body of material.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Constraints arrive as a list and the list has no provenance.

**Nothing about the artefact distinguishes a contractual obligation from something an engineer said in the second week.** Both appear as a line. Both are repeated in meetings. Both are eventually cited to explain a design decision, and by then neither has an author, because the process by which an assumption becomes a constraint is repetition and repetition erases origin.

The consequences run in both directions. **A team that treats an assumption as a constraint gives away design freedom it had.** A team that treats a real constraint as an assumption discovers it late, in a review, at the point where changing the design is most expensive.

There is a second problem that the first conceals. **A constraint as stated is an answer, not a need.** *It must run in their own tenant*, *it must use the existing identity provider*, *the data cannot leave the country* — these are solutions somebody chose, to a problem they had. The problem may be satisfiable another way, and finding out requires asking why the constraint exists and why the obvious alternative was not taken. **Nobody asks, because the constraint is presented as a fact and facts do not invite questions.**

A third problem is newer and is why this session has a platform anchor. **An increasing share of the constraints handed to an engineer now arrive from a governance tool rather than from a person**, and a tool's output looks more like a fact than a person's statement does. It carries a timestamp, a count and a product name. What it does not carry, unless the reader goes and gets it, is the scope of what was scanned, the cadence at which the scan runs, and the delay before a result appears — and a constraint derived from a periodic sample of part of an estate is a different object from a constraint derived from a contract. **The artefact hides that difference more effectively than a slide does.**

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** A practitioner arriving from industry has met constraint lists and can do this session. A learner arriving from [M05](/wiki/modules/M05.md) has a specification, which makes the register shorter and sharper, because a constraint is only interesting against a commitment it forecloses.

It retrieves [S006](/wiki/seminars/S006.md) and [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md). The first asked what constraints you cannot see yet; the second established who was not in the room. **The register is where both answers land**, and the entries with no named authority are usually the ones whose owner was never invited.

**This is the stage's first class 3 session.** Nobody supplies the classification, nobody supplies the number of values on the axis, and nobody supplies the standard for what counts as adequate evidence that something is frozen. The learner chooses all three and defends them.

**The platform half has a genuinely mixed entry profile and the design accepts it.** Learners with a governance or security background will find the posture tooling familiar and will under-rate the resolution point, because they are used to reading such reports as descriptions of an estate. Learners without it will find the surface unfamiliar and will over-trust the output, because a report with a product name on it reads as authoritative. **Neither group arrives holding the fact that decides the movement: that no detection rate or false-positive rate is published for any of it.** Learners with no tenant access at all do four of the seven steps and record the absence, which is a legitimate completion rather than a degraded one.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate and mostly investigative. The classification itself is quick; finding out who owns each entry is slow, involves other people, and is where the session's time goes.

**3.2 Why the axis must have more than two values.** A binary classification forces every entry into *frozen* or *fluid*, and the interesting entries are neither. **Most constraints are movable at a price, and the price is the engineering content.** A register that records *not possible* has recorded a refusal; one that records what it would cost, and who would pay, has recorded a decision somebody could take.

**3.3 Why the interest is recorded alongside the position.** A position is an answer. The interest is the need that makes the answer attractive, and interests are frequently satisfiable in more than one way while positions are not. **Recording only the position guarantees that any later conversation is a contest of wills**, which is the failure the negotiation literature is organised around.

**3.4 Why the alternative and the threshold are written before the conversation.** Knowing what you will do without agreement is what stops you accepting less than that alternative, and not knowing it is what makes people accept less. The threshold is a different device: **it exists to force a pause rather than to bind**, and its purpose is to prevent drift into a commitment nobody chose. Both are named devices from that literature and both are carried with its limit attached.

**3.5 What the classification is actually for.** This paper claims a diagnostic function and no more. **The register's value is that it makes two things visible that are otherwise invisible: which constraints nobody stated, and which constraints nobody owns.** Both are checkable. Neither requires the axis to be a good model of anything.

**3.6 Authority is distributed, has named components, and is therefore findable.**

The session's demand that every frozen entry name a role rests on a claim about organisations rather than about registers: that the capacity to move something is held by identifiable people in identifiable positions, and that a coalition of them has a describable composition. Kotter's account gives that composition four parts — position power, expertise, credibility and leadership — and states that individual sponsorship is not sufficient because **"Individuals alone... never have all the assets needed to overcome tradition and inertia except in very small organizations"** ([research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md](/research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md)). Read in reverse, that supplies the register's failure condition: **"One well-placed blocker can stop an entire change effort"**, and a blocker you have not named is one you cannot address.

**The warrant here is weak and must be stated as such.** The author declares that he cites no evidence and draws no example or major idea from any published source except his own writing. Every case in the book is pseudonymous. Several of the book's most-quoted numbers are derived by inspecting his own diagrams. **This paper takes the structural claim — that authority has components and is distributed — and nothing else.**

The adoption literature contributes the diagnostic habit rather than a finding: identify which element is missing before choosing an intervention, and expect resistance to concentrate at the layer whose span of control the change actually moves ([research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md)). **That model is proprietary, its supporting research is practitioner self-reported and commercially interested, no independent evaluation is cited anywhere in it, and its diagnostic instrument is an anonymous self-rating with a threshold whose inter-rater agreement is unreported.** It licenses direction and mechanism. Nothing numerical from it appears here or in the session.

The domain-modelling literature converges on the same point from a technical direction, and the convergence is the strongest thing this section can say. Evans records that **"model divergences are as likely to come from political fragmentation and differing management priorities as from technical concerns"** and that **"In practice, political relationships between teams often determine how systems are integrated"** ([research/05-fde-craft/evans-domain-driven-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/evans-domain-driven-design.md)). Three literatures with no connection to one another — change management, adoption diagnosis and software architecture — independently locate the binding constraint in ownership rather than in content. **None of them measures anything, so the convergence raises a prior and establishes nothing.**

**3.7 Why the platform anchor is a governance product rather than a build tool.**

Every other session in this stage points a learner at something they are constructing. This one points them at something they are not allowed to change, which is the correct instrument for its subject.

Microsoft Purview's data-security posture management for AI carries the clearest published statement of why a retrieval deployment is a security event: **"generative AI amplifies the problem of oversharing data"** by surfacing content that is obsolete, over-permissioned or ungoverned ([research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md)). The generalisation the session teaches from it — that deploying capable retrieval converts every dormant permissions error into a live one — is this repository's reading of that sentence and not a claim the documentation makes.

Three features of the product make it the right teaching instrument, and all three are properties of its *documentation* rather than of its behaviour.

It **states its own coverage**. The default assessment samples part of the estate on a weekly cadence, with a multi-day delay before first display, an expiry on custom results, item-level scanning capped at a small number of sites, and one major store excluded outright. Very little vendor documentation is that explicit, and the explicitness is what lets a learner compute the resolution of their own instrument rather than guess it.

It **names the disruptive remedy as disruptive**. Removing a sharing link is flagged as **"an action to be used sparingly because it could prevent people from accessing the item legitimately."** That is the authority question in one line: the safest action is the most costly, and the register must say who decides.

It **publishes no effectiveness data at all**. No detection rate, no false-positive rate, no coverage measurement, no evaluation of any control. **The documentation is an accurate description of what the tooling computes and no evidence about whether what it computes is right**, and this repository asserts no such figure anywhere. The design consequence is that a register entry citing this tool must carry the tool's scope, cadence and latency, because those are the only quantitative facts available about it.

**Two further silences are load-bearing.** The consulted page is explicitly the **classic** version and states that it has been superseded, so some described behaviour may already be historical. Agent-level discovery and posture move to a separate licence on a stated date, which changes what a learner can even reach. The companion posture-management material was read through search excerpts rather than a full fetch, so its structure is reconstructed.

**3.8 Why the register is a file rather than a table.**

The design requires a structured file with an explicit `status` enumeration on the authority field, and the reason is a claim about readers rather than about tooling. **In a table, a blank cell and a deliberately empty cell are the same glyph.** A register in which *no authority is known* is recorded by omission is indistinguishable from one in which the author did not get to that row, and the distinction is the entire diagnostic value of the artefact. Requiring `none-known` as a value forces the author to make a claim they can be held to. This is design reasoning with no external warrant, and it is the cheapest structural intervention in the module.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

Seven movements: situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 3 is the instrument.** *Which items did you assume rather than hear?* It is the longest list in most first registers and nobody expects it to be. It is placed third because items 1 and 2 have to produce the list and its authorities before the provenance question can bite.

**The register's four required fields are this design's, not the source's.** The source supplies an axis. Authority, provenance, evidence and interest are added here because they are the fields that make the register checkable, and the source's version is not.

**The work movement uses the two negotiation questions rather than one.** *Why does the constraint exist* is the obvious question and produces a rationale. *Why was the obvious alternative not taken* is the informative one, because answering it requires reconstructing the choice as the constraint's holder sees it, including consequences for people they must answer to. **The second question is where a constraint that looks arbitrary from outside stops looking arbitrary.**

**The review movement is a single question asked repeatedly**, exactly as at [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md), and for the same reason: the failure is mechanical, so the check is mechanical. *Who says?* Items with no answer move to a list headed *assumed*, and the length of that list is the session's result.

**4.1 What the platform workflow is for.** Not tooling competence, and not governance competence either. It exists to make one thing physically observable in an hour: **that a claim which everybody in the room treats as a fact about the organisation is, on inspection, a fact about a periodic sample of part of an estate.** The learner reads the scan scope, the cadence and the latency before reading a single result, writes all three into the register, and then watches the confidence of the room drop when those three are read aloud.

A second and quieter purpose is the notification path. Where the tooling offers to notify a site owner, it discloses the owner's name — which is the authority the learner has spent two days failing to find by asking people. **The instrument built for remediation turns out to be the best available instrument for attribution**, and noticing that is a transferable habit rather than a product fact.

**4.2 Why the workflow forbids remediation.** The learner is explicitly told to read the Identify tab and change nothing. Three reasons. They do not own the estate. The disruptive action is flagged as disruptive by the vendor's own documentation and requires a decision the learner is not entitled to take. And the session's subject is *whose decision it is* — a learner who remediates has answered that question by acting rather than by finding out, which is precisely the failure the module is about.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The anonymous constraint left anonymous.** The likeliest failure. The learner records the item, cannot find an owner, and leaves the field blank rather than marking it. *Countermeasure:* the standard distinguishes *no authority named* from *no authority known*, and requires the second to be explicit. **A blank field and a marked absence look different to a later reader and that is the whole point.**

**The register that is a complaint.** Every entry written as an obstacle placed in the learner's way. *Countermeasure:* the interest field, which cannot be filled while the entry is a complaint, and the standard's requirement that the register be showable to the customer.

**The position accepted as a constraint.** *Countermeasure:* the two-question move in the work movement, applied to the three constraints that matter most. **Applied to three rather than all, because applying it to all is not affordable and a design that pretends otherwise will be ignored.**

**The invented constraint.** The learner assumed something, said it aloud, and it entered the register with their own authority behind it. *Countermeasure:* the provenance field and opening item 3, which asks for the answer before the register makes it embarrassing.

**The all-frozen register.** *Countermeasure:* none at this session, deliberately. **[S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md) exists for it**, and confronting it here would pre-empt the session where it can be worked on properly.

**The instrument mistaken for the estate.** A learner reads a posture report and records its findings as facts about the organisation. *Countermeasure:* the workflow's ordering, which requires scope, cadence and latency to be written into the register before any result is. **This is a structural countermeasure to a cognitive failure and this paper does not expect it to hold** — prediction 5 in §9 predicts it failing, because a number arrives with more authority than the caveat attached to it.

**The register that is a governance audit.** A learner with tenant access produces an assessment of the customer's permission estate instead of a register of their own design's constraints. *Countermeasure:* the artefact standard, which requires every entry to bear on the specification. **A constraint is only interesting against a commitment it forecloses**, and an entry that forecloses nothing is a finding about somebody else's system that the learner has no standing to report.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence, and no specification item at [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) marks the register's classification.** What is marked there is narrower: that the frozen inputs are attached and that each names its authority or is explicitly marked as having none.

**That narrowing is deliberate and is a consequence of the evidence position.** Marking whether an item was classified correctly would require a standard for correct classification, and there is none — not in the source, not here, and not anywhere this design could find. **The assessment marks the property that can be checked without a standard: whether the register says who says.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: teaching an unevidenced classification is teaching a folk method with a diagram.**

At its strongest: the axis has no worked instance, no reported use, no cost, no failure mode and no citation. **An experienced engineer already knows which constraints are real, learns it by asking, and does not need a taxonomy to do it.** What the session adds may be nothing but ceremony — a document that formalises a judgement the learner would have made anyway, and that will be produced once for the assessment and never again. Worse, formalising it may harm: a register makes the classification look settled, and a learner who has written *frozen* next to an item is less likely to test it than one who never wrote anything down.

**Substantially conceded, including the last clause, which this paper had not anticipated and cannot answer.** The rebuttal is narrower than the objection. The session's claim is not that the axis models anything; it is that producing the register makes two specific things visible — unstated constraints and unowned ones — and that both are checkable and both are routinely missed. **The declined fix is to teach the two checks without the axis**, which would be honest and lighter. It is declined because the axis is what makes the checks systematic rather than occasional, and because a programme that strips an idea down to the part it can defend, and then does not say what it removed, has done the thing this repository criticises elsewhere. **It is a weak reason and the objection stands.**

**Objection 2: the source contradicts itself, and the session inherits the contradiction rather than resolving it.**

At its strongest: the same body of material that says constraint is not an excuse also explains disappointing delivery outcomes by pointing at the customer's organisational constraints. **A practice cannot hold both.** If constraint is never an excuse, the prepared explanation is illegitimate; if the prepared explanation is legitimate, the rule is a slogan. Teaching the rule without resolving the contradiction hands learners an inconsistency and calls it nuance.

**Conceded, and the contradiction is real and is named at [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md) rather than here.** The rebuttal is about what a course can honestly do with a source that contradicts itself: it can resolve the contradiction on its own authority, or it can hand it over. **The declined fix is to resolve it — to state that the rule binds the engineer and the explanation is illegitimate.** That is this design's own view and it is declined as a teaching move, because asserting a resolution the source does not contain, in a programme whose whole method is to distinguish what is warranted from what is asserted, would be exactly the failure it teaches learners to detect. **The contradiction is handed over intact and both readings are available.**

**Objection 3: pointing learners at a governance product teaches them to treat a vendor's coverage limits as the shape of the problem.**

At its strongest: the session's platform movement takes a tool whose scope is a sample of one storage product on one vendor's estate, and uses it to teach a general lesson about constraint authority. A learner will come away believing that *the resolution of an instrument* means scan scope, cadence and latency, because those are the three fields this particular product happens to expose. **The tool's documentation has supplied the concept, and the concept now has the tool's shape.** Worse, the product is superseded, mid-licence-migration and read partly through search excerpts, so the learner is being taught a vocabulary from an artefact that will not survive the term. A course that teaches coverage-thinking from a general argument would produce the same habit without the contamination.

**Partly conceded, and the contamination is real.** The rebuttal is that the general argument is what learners already fail to act on — the abstract instruction *state what your evidence covers* has been available in every methods course any of them has taken, and constraint registers still arrive without provenance. The product is used because it is one of very few vendor artefacts that **states its own coverage explicitly**, which makes the abstraction extractable from a concrete case rather than asserted at one. **The declined fix is to teach the resolution concept from a constructed example with no product attached.** It is declined because a constructed example cannot surprise anybody, and the session's mechanism depends on the learner discovering that a claim they had accepted rests on a sample — which requires the claim to have been genuinely accepted first. **The cost of that decision is exactly the contamination the objection names, and the perishability table is the only mitigation offered.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** In first registers, entries whose provenance is *assumed* will outnumber entries the learner expected to mark that way. *Instrument:* opening item 3's written answer against the completed register, compared per learner.

**Prediction 2.** Most learners will produce a binary register on the first pass, with no entry marked movable at a price, until the standard forces one. *Instrument:* the first-pass registers, counted by number of distinct values used on the axis.

**Prediction 3.** At least one constraint per learner will be reclassified after the two-question move, and the reclassifications will cluster on constraints stated as solutions rather than as properties. *Instrument:* reclassified entries, coded by the form of the original statement.

**Prediction 4.** Learners who write the alternative before the conversation will report fewer accepted-but-unwanted outcomes than those who do not. *Instrument:* self-report against a written record of whether the alternative preceded the conversation. **This prediction is the weakest in the paper — it relies on self-report about a counterfactual — and if it cannot be instrumented better it should be withdrawn rather than measured badly.**

**Prediction 5.** A majority of registers whose entries derive from the governance tooling will omit at least one of scan scope, cadence and latency, despite the workflow requiring all three to be written before any result is read. *Instrument:* the submitted register, with each tool-derived entry coded for the presence of all three fields. **This predicts the failure of §5's own countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 6.** Learners who used the notification path to discover a site owner will name a human authority for more of their entries than learners who only asked colleagues. *Instrument:* the `authority.status` field counted across registers, split on whether the tooling was reachable. **If this fails, the platform movement is a demonstration rather than an instrument**, and the session should say so rather than implying the tool changed the register.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That constraints arrive without provenance and acquire authority by repetition; that a constraint with no named authority is a rumour; and that a stated constraint is an answer whose underlying need may be met another way.

**What the evidence licenses.** **Almost nothing, and this paper says so more plainly than any other in the stage.** The classification is unevidenced and unused in its own source. The negotiation devices come from a case-based framework whose authors state that it establishes no effect. The change and adoption literature carries no independent evaluation between them. The platform documentation describes what a tool computes and publishes no measure of how well it computes it. The only claims here with any grip are the checkable ones: whether a constraint was stated, whether anybody owns it, and what a governance instrument actually covers.

**The concession worth remembering.** Writing *frozen* next to an item may make a learner less likely to test it, and this paper has no answer to that.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That the corpus proposes classifying every element of a problem space on a frozen-to-fluid axis; that it attaches a rule stating even an almost entirely frozen space contains something that can be changed and that constraint is not an excuse; that this is the most transferable original idea in the collection; **that no worked instance of the classification appears anywhere in approximately ninety documents**; that the same material elsewhere reattributes disappointing delivery outcomes to the customer's own organisational constraints, paired with a readiness rubric composed almost entirely of customer dispositions; and that **there is not one reference, footnote, study, dataset or citation anywhere in the collection** — all recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md), **from a reading of access-controlled material that no public reader can check.**
