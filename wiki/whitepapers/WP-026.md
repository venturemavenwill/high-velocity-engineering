## In plain terms

This session opens the stage's second half, and it teaches an idea that **has no evidence behind it at all.** That is stated here, in the session, in the module page and in the stage page, because it is the single most important thing a reader can know about the material.

The idea is a classification. Every element of a problem space is placed on an axis running from frozen to fluid, and the axis is attached to a rule: **even in a space that is almost entirely frozen there is something that can be changed, and constraint is not an excuse for doing nothing.**

It comes from the practice material behind this programme, where it is the most transferable original idea in the collection and where **it is never once used.** No problem space is classified. No engagement is described in which the classification changed a decision. No account is given of what it costs to produce or how often it is wrong. **The material contains no reference, footnote, study or citation of any kind, anywhere**, so there is nothing behind the classification except the assertion that it is useful.

The session teaches it anyway, and the reason is stated rather than concealed: **it names a failure mode nothing else in this programme names, and it is cheap enough to test that a learner can find out for themselves in an afternoon.** That is a reason to try a method. It is not a reason to believe it.

What the session adds to the source is the part that can be checked. **A constraint with no named authority is a rumour**, and finding out how many entries in a real constraint list have no author is an empirical exercise with an answer.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching an unevidenced classification, and defends being explicit about the absence rather than quietly grounding the session elsewhere. It argues that the classification's value is diagnostic rather than predictive: it produces a register in which assumed constraints are separated from stated ones and unauthored constraints become visible, both of which are checkable properties. It requires the axis to carry more than two values, so that *movable at a price* is expressible and the price is named. It imports two devices from the negotiation literature — knowing the alternative to agreement, and setting a threshold that forces reassessment before drift — carrying that source's own statement that it establishes no effect. The paper concedes that the classification may be an elaborate way of writing down what an experienced engineer already knows, that its cost is unmeasured, and that the corpus supplying it contradicts its own rule elsewhere in the same body of material.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Constraints arrive as a list and the list has no provenance.

**Nothing about the artefact distinguishes a contractual obligation from something an engineer said in the second week.** Both appear as a line. Both are repeated in meetings. Both are eventually cited to explain a design decision, and by then neither has an author, because the process by which an assumption becomes a constraint is repetition and repetition erases origin.

The consequences run in both directions. **A team that treats an assumption as a constraint gives away design freedom it had.** A team that treats a real constraint as an assumption discovers it late, in a review, at the point where changing the design is most expensive.

There is a second problem that the first conceals. **A constraint as stated is an answer, not a need.** *It must run in their own tenant*, *it must use the existing identity provider*, *the data cannot leave the country* — these are solutions somebody chose, to a problem they had. The problem may be satisfiable another way, and finding out requires asking why the constraint exists and why the obvious alternative was not taken. **Nobody asks, because the constraint is presented as a fact and facts do not invite questions.**

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** A practitioner arriving from industry has met constraint lists and can do this session. A learner arriving from [M05](/wiki/modules/M05.md) has a specification, which makes the register shorter and sharper, because a constraint is only interesting against a commitment it forecloses.

It retrieves [S006](/wiki/seminars/S006.md) and [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md). The first asked what constraints you cannot see yet; the second established who was not in the room. **The register is where both answers land**, and the entries with no named authority are usually the ones whose owner was never invited.

**This is the stage's first class 3 session.** Nobody supplies the classification, nobody supplies the number of values on the axis, and nobody supplies the standard for what counts as adequate evidence that something is frozen. The learner chooses all three and defends them.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate and mostly investigative. The classification itself is quick; finding out who owns each entry is slow, involves other people, and is where the session's time goes.

**3.2 Why the axis must have more than two values.** A binary classification forces every entry into *frozen* or *fluid*, and the interesting entries are neither. **Most constraints are movable at a price, and the price is the engineering content.** A register that records *not possible* has recorded a refusal; one that records what it would cost, and who would pay, has recorded a decision somebody could take.

**3.3 Why the interest is recorded alongside the position.** A position is an answer. The interest is the need that makes the answer attractive, and interests are frequently satisfiable in more than one way while positions are not. **Recording only the position guarantees that any later conversation is a contest of wills**, which is the failure the negotiation literature is organised around.

**3.4 Why the alternative and the threshold are written before the conversation.** Knowing what you will do without agreement is what stops you accepting less than that alternative, and not knowing it is what makes people accept less. The threshold is a different device: **it exists to force a pause rather than to bind**, and its purpose is to prevent drift into a commitment nobody chose. Both are named devices from that literature and both are carried with its limit attached.

**3.5 What the classification is actually for.** This paper claims a diagnostic function and no more. **The register's value is that it makes two things visible that are otherwise invisible: which constraints nobody stated, and which constraints nobody owns.** Both are checkable. Neither requires the axis to be a good model of anything.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

Seven movements: situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 3 is the instrument.** *Which items did you assume rather than hear?* It is the longest list in most first registers and nobody expects it to be. It is placed third because items 1 and 2 have to produce the list and its authorities before the provenance question can bite.

**The register's four required fields are this design's, not the source's.** The source supplies an axis. Authority, provenance, evidence and interest are added here because they are the fields that make the register checkable, and the source's version is not.

**The work movement uses the two negotiation questions rather than one.** *Why does the constraint exist* is the obvious question and produces a rationale. *Why was the obvious alternative not taken* is the informative one, because answering it requires reconstructing the choice as the constraint's holder sees it, including consequences for people they must answer to. **The second question is where a constraint that looks arbitrary from outside stops looking arbitrary.**

**The review movement is a single question asked repeatedly**, exactly as at [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md), and for the same reason: the failure is mechanical, so the check is mechanical. *Who says?* Items with no answer move to a list headed *assumed*, and the length of that list is the session's result.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The anonymous constraint left anonymous.** The likeliest failure. The learner records the item, cannot find an owner, and leaves the field blank rather than marking it. *Countermeasure:* the standard distinguishes *no authority named* from *no authority known*, and requires the second to be explicit. **A blank field and a marked absence look different to a later reader and that is the whole point.**

**The register that is a complaint.** Every entry written as an obstacle placed in the learner's way. *Countermeasure:* the interest field, which cannot be filled while the entry is a complaint, and the standard's requirement that the register be showable to the customer.

**The position accepted as a constraint.** *Countermeasure:* the two-question move in the work movement, applied to the three constraints that matter most. **Applied to three rather than all, because applying it to all is not affordable and a design that pretends otherwise will be ignored.**

**The invented constraint.** The learner assumed something, said it aloud, and it entered the register with their own authority behind it. *Countermeasure:* the provenance field and opening item 3, which asks for the answer before the register makes it embarrassing.

**The all-frozen register.** *Countermeasure:* none at this session, deliberately. **[S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md) exists for it**, and confronting it here would pre-empt the session where it can be worked on properly.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence, and no specification item at [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) marks the register's classification.** What is marked there is narrower: that the frozen inputs are attached and that each names its authority or is explicitly marked as having none.

**That narrowing is deliberate and is a consequence of the evidence position.** Marking whether an item was classified correctly would require a standard for correct classification, and there is none — not in the source, not here, and not anywhere this design could find. **The assessment marks the property that can be checked without a standard: whether the register says who says.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: teaching an unevidenced classification is teaching a folk method with a diagram.**

At its strongest: the axis has no worked instance, no reported use, no cost, no failure mode and no citation. **An experienced engineer already knows which constraints are real, learns it by asking, and does not need a taxonomy to do it.** What the session adds may be nothing but ceremony — a document that formalises a judgement the learner would have made anyway, and that will be produced once for the assessment and never again. Worse, formalising it may harm: a register makes the classification look settled, and a learner who has written *frozen* next to an item is less likely to test it than one who never wrote anything down.

**Substantially conceded, including the last clause, which this paper had not anticipated and cannot answer.** The rebuttal is narrower than the objection. The session's claim is not that the axis models anything; it is that producing the register makes two specific things visible — unstated constraints and unowned ones — and that both are checkable and both are routinely missed. **The declined fix is to teach the two checks without the axis**, which would be honest and lighter. It is declined because the axis is what makes the checks systematic rather than occasional, and because a programme that strips an idea down to the part it can defend, and then does not say what it removed, has done the thing this repository criticises elsewhere. **It is a weak reason and the objection stands.**

**Objection 2: the source contradicts itself, and the session inherits the contradiction rather than resolving it.**

At its strongest: the same body of material that says constraint is not an excuse also explains disappointing delivery outcomes by pointing at the customer's organisational constraints. **A practice cannot hold both.** If constraint is never an excuse, the prepared explanation is illegitimate; if the prepared explanation is legitimate, the rule is a slogan. Teaching the rule without resolving the contradiction hands learners an inconsistency and calls it nuance.

**Conceded, and the contradiction is real and is named at [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md) rather than here.** The rebuttal is about what a course can honestly do with a source that contradicts itself: it can resolve the contradiction on its own authority, or it can hand it over. **The declined fix is to resolve it — to state that the rule binds the engineer and the explanation is illegitimate.** That is this design's own view and it is declined as a teaching move, because asserting a resolution the source does not contain, in a programme whose whole method is to distinguish what is warranted from what is asserted, would be exactly the failure it teaches learners to detect. **The contradiction is handed over intact and both readings are available.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** In first registers, entries whose provenance is *assumed* will outnumber entries the learner expected to mark that way. *Instrument:* opening item 3's written answer against the completed register, compared per learner.

**Prediction 2.** Most learners will produce a binary register on the first pass, with no entry marked movable at a price, until the standard forces one. *Instrument:* the first-pass registers, counted by number of distinct values used on the axis.

**Prediction 3.** At least one constraint per learner will be reclassified after the two-question move, and the reclassifications will cluster on constraints stated as solutions rather than as properties. *Instrument:* reclassified entries, coded by the form of the original statement.

**Prediction 4.** Learners who write the alternative before the conversation will report fewer accepted-but-unwanted outcomes than those who do not. *Instrument:* self-report against a written record of whether the alternative preceded the conversation. **This prediction is the weakest in the paper — it relies on self-report about a counterfactual — and if it cannot be instrumented better it should be withdrawn rather than measured badly.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That constraints arrive without provenance and acquire authority by repetition; that a constraint with no named authority is a rumour; and that a stated constraint is an answer whose underlying need may be met another way.

**What the evidence licenses.** **Almost nothing, and this paper says so more plainly than any other in the stage.** The classification is unevidenced and unused in its own source. The negotiation devices come from a case-based framework whose authors state that it establishes no effect. The only claims here with any grip are the two checkable ones: whether a constraint was stated, and whether anybody owns it.

**The concession worth remembering.** Writing *frozen* next to an item may make a learner less likely to test it, and this paper has no answer to that.
