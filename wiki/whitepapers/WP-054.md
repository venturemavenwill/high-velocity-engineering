## In plain terms

**A system retrieves ten passages. Three come from documents the asker may not read. The generator reads all ten and writes a paragraph. No document was returned, no link was shown, nothing in the transcript records a violation — and the asker now knows what was in those three documents.**

That is the session, and the claim it turns on is older than everything it is demonstrated on. **Authorisation is a property of the retrieval path, not of the answer.** Once content has been read into a computation, the computation's output is not governed by the content's permissions. **Trimming after retrieval is not trimming**; it is tidying a result set that something already consumed.

Three consequences follow and they are the durable content. The leak in a retrieval-augmented system is the substance rather than a listing, because a conventional search interface at worst shows a link somebody cannot open. Access control therefore has to happen before generation. Which means it has to happen in retrieval. Which means **the permission model has to live in or alongside the index** — and an index is a copy, and copies do not inherit their originals' access rules.

Three specific failures make this concrete and all three are quiet. **An unprojected chunk is an unprotected chunk**, and chunking is standard practice. **Enforcement always lags the source**, so a revocation is not immediate and nobody publishes the window. And **the set an index returns is computed by machinery that knows nothing about permissions** — approximate retrieval means recall is an operating point rather than a guarantee, so the authorised set and the returned set are produced by different systems whose agreement nothing enforces.

Then the session turns the same instrument one layer up, on the learner's own plan. **A chain infers a value, is forbidden to mark the inference, resolves conflicts optimistically, drops the provenance section when it assembles the delivered document, and then scores the document by counting non-empty cells in the table it just filled in.** A plan can score well on encryption because an earlier step assumed encryption.

**Those two halves are one structure.** Something enters qualified — by a permission, by a provenance — and leaves unqualified, and the next step consumes the unqualified thing as though it had been established. **That is what this session means by laundering, and it is why the two halves are one session rather than two.**

The thing worth sitting with is that **no step in either sequence is wrong.** Trimming after retrieval is a reasonable implementation. Banning hedges inside cells is correct, and [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) argued for it. Resolving conflicts in the more secure direction is a defensible default. Excluding a long justification section from a delivered document is an editorial judgement anyone would make. **Every participant is behaving well and the composition is a defect none of them can see**, because seeing it requires holding the whole path at once and no role in the path does.

## Abstract

This paper defends making one claim the intellectual centre of the assurance stage: **authorisation is a property of the retrieval path rather than of the answer**, and a system that retrieves what a user may not read and then summarises it has leaked.

**First**, the durable argument. An index is a copy and copies do not inherit their originals' access rules. Retrieval-augmented generation makes propagation urgent rather than merely advisable, because the leak is the substance rather than a listing. Enforcement must therefore precede generation, which places it in retrieval, which places the permission model in or beside the index. **This chain of consequences predates the platform by decades and is the reference-monitor position restated for a new class of system.**

**Second**, the structural argument this programme is unusually placed to make. Approximate nearest-neighbour retrieval trades bounded recall for speed by construction; recall is a chosen operating point reported alongside latency, throughput and density precisely because each trades against the others. **The candidate set is therefore computed by the index's structure and the permitted set by the identity system, and nothing guarantees their agreement.** Filtering after retrieval filters an approximate set that something already read; filtering during retrieval changes what the traversal can reach. **Neither retrieval source says anything about security; the property is imported, and the paper says so.**

**Third**, the three quiet failures the session demonstrates rather than describes: an unprojected chunk is an unprotected chunk; a revocation is not immediate and the window is unmeasured because no latency figure is published anywhere; and a filter constructed by an application is a claim rather than a check, which the vendor's own documentation concedes by calling that pattern a workaround.

**Fourth**, the upstream instance. The chained pipeline this module rests on infers a value, forbids the marker, drops the provenance at assembly and then counts the cell as evidence. The session traces one value and requires a two-defect statement, because marker loss and optimistic resolution are orthogonal and **neither fix subsumes the other.**

The paper concedes four things. That the compositional critique is available only from a vantage point no participant occupies. That teaching learners to distrust scores risks producing engineers who report nothing. That **no measurement exists anywhere of how often any of this occurs or what it costs** — not the leak rate, not the revocation window, not the frequency of unprojected chunks. And that most of the anchor documentation describes a preview surface which will have moved before a cohort finishes the year.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A learner who can build a retrieval feature believes authorisation is a filter they add.

**It is not, and the belief is the defect.** A filter is a mechanism the application supplies; authorisation is a check something performs on the application's behalf. The difference is where the trust sits, and it is invisible in a system that works — because a system with a correct filter and a system with a check behave identically until the filter is wrong, and when the filter is wrong nothing in the interaction says so.

The second problem is that **the failure produces no artefact.** A retrieval that returned content the asker was not entitled to leaves a well-formed answer, a normal latency, a clean log and a satisfied user. There is no exception, no denial, no audit entry and no citation pointing at the document that leaked. **The generic instruction — be careful with permissions — is already believed by everybody and changes nothing**, because nobody who ships this defect thinks they are being careless.

The specific version is actionable and this session teaches only the specific version. **There is a step at which content is read before authorisation is evaluated, and it has an address.** There is a step at which permissions are dropped from a derived object, and it has an address. There is an interval during which a revoked entitlement still holds, and it has a length nobody has measured. Each of those can be pointed at, argued about and fixed.

There is a third problem, addressed in the session's last third. **The learner's own artefacts contain the same structure** — a value inferred, a marker stripped, a cell later counted as evidence — and they will not look for it unless required to.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session works on the learner's own retrieval path and their own artefacts, and cannot be attempted without both. It assumes a running increment with a retrieval feature, and [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md)'s numbered flows and attribute table with its provenance side-channel.

It retrieves [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) and [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) directly. Comprehension debt is the same phenomenon at a different scale — output that exists, passes and is not understood — and [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) installed the habit of writing down what an agent did that you could not explain. **This session asks a harder version: what did it do that you could explain, and that was still not authorised?**

It assumes [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md), because a mapped control is the thing the scoring step will count, and because the surface distinction taught there is what makes *the boundary sits in the application* an intelligible sentence rather than a slogan.

**The prior it works against is that authorisation is a filter you add.** A learner who can build a retrieval feature believes exactly that, and the belief is invisible because a correct filter and a real check behave identically until the filter is wrong. **The session's method is to demonstrate the difference on the learner's own system rather than on a supplied example, because a supplied example is always somebody else's mistake.**

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The evaluative peak of the entire stage. The intrinsic content is small; the difficulty is holding a multi-step composition in mind while each step argues persuasively for itself. **That is the same cognitive demand the defect exploits**, which is why the session works on one path and one value rather than on the pattern.

**3.2 Opening item 1 is a generation phase whose expected output is an absence.**

Productive Failure is two-phase and order-dependent, and its mechanism is the **failed generation effect** — attempting an answer before instruction improves later learning even when the attempt is wrong ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Item 1 asks the learner to name the step at which the asker's identity is evaluated against a document's permissions. **In most systems no such step exists**, and the learner discovers this by trying to write it down.

The design condition that binds hardest here is **do not scaffold the generation phase.** A learner told in advance that the answer is usually *there isn't one* writes that sentence without the discovery, and the discovery is the entire teaching event. The release rule gates §§4, 5 and 9 for that reason.

The consolidation condition binds equally: the book reports Loibl and Leuders finding that **presenting correct and incorrect solutions side by side achieves nothing on its own — only the condition instructed to compare them learned more.** Exercise 3 is that instruction.

**The source's limits travel with it.** Trade book; school, university and laboratory evidence; **no adult-professional study in its reference list**; several underlying studies including Loibl and Leuders reported at second hand through the book rather than read directly.

**3.3 An index is a copy, and the three consequences that follow.**

The governing fact is stated in the access-control documentation and generates everything else: **a search index is a copy of source content, and copies do not automatically inherit the access rules of their originals** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/azure-ai-search-and-acl-patterns.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/azure-ai-search-and-acl-patterns.md)). The documentation frames document-level access control as **"essential for building secure AI agentic systems grounding data, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) applications, and enterprise search solutions that require authorization checks at the document level."**

Three consequences follow in order and the order is the argument. **The leak in a retrieval-augmented system is the substance rather than a listing** — a conventional search interface that returns a forbidden document at worst shows a link nobody can open, while a retrieval-augmented system reads the contents into an answer. **Therefore access control must happen before generation.** **Therefore it must happen in retrieval.** **Therefore the permission model must live in or alongside the index.**

The documentation's own candour supplies the sharpest teaching object. Its selection guidance offers four approaches with a single organising rule — **reuse the authoritative permission system rather than reimplementing it** — and then contrasts the application-supplied string filter against the native pattern in its own words: the native metadata **"is recognized as Microsoft Entra ID authentication, whereas the security trimming workaround is simple string comparison."** **The word *workaround* is the documentation's.** What that establishes is where the trust sits: a string filter is only as trustworthy as the code supplying the string, and nothing at the service validates that the caller is who the filter says. **The boundary is in the application.**

The native pattern's structure is the design insight worth teaching independently of the product. **Two checks in sequence** — the application's own permission to query the index, then, given an extra token on the request, the per-document check against the user's claims. **Application authorisation and user authorisation are separate concerns evaluated separately.** A system that collapses them has not simplified; it has deleted a check.

**Its limits are heavy and mostly forward-looking.** Most of the page describes a **preview** API surface that changes without notice. **No measurements of synchronisation latency, no failure-rate data, and no security assessment of the four approaches against each other** — they are compared on fit rather than on strength. The documentation **explicitly disclaims responsibility for compliance, geographic boundaries, testing and mitigations**, placing all of it on the implementer. Feature availability differs between API versions and packages.

**3.4 Why the returned set is not the authorised set, and why that is structural.**

This is the argument this programme is unusually placed to make, because it requires holding a retrieval-systems literature and a security documentation set at the same time.

Retrieval at scale is approximate by construction. The graph index underneath most vector search is a hierarchy of proximity graphs over nested subsets, searched by greedy descent from a sparse top layer downwards, with links separated by characteristic distance scale ([research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md)). It exists because exact search degenerates to a linear scan at scale, and **approximate methods trade a bounded amount of recall for large gains in speed.** The neighbour-selection heuristic in that work matters specifically at high recall and on clustered data — which is to say, the regime every production corpus occupies.

The disk-resident line of work states the trade in the form a capacity planner needs, reporting recall, latency, throughput and density **together**, on the explicit methodological grounds that each can be traded against the others so that **a claim stated on only one axis is uninformative** ([research/02-technical-foundations/subramanya-diskann.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/subramanya-diskann.md)). **Recall is an operating point you choose, not a property you have.**

Compose that with authorisation and the consequence is exact. **The candidate set is computed by the index's structure; the permitted set is computed by the identity system; nothing guarantees their agreement, because they are different machinery answering different questions.** Filter after retrieval and you filtered an approximate set that something already read — and if a reranker, a cache or a log touched it first, the filter is downstream of the exposure. Filter during retrieval and the filter interacts with the traversal: a greedy search that cannot enter an excluded region may not reach what lies beyond it, so the authorised set and the reachable-under-filter set differ as a function of the index's shape rather than of anybody's policy.

**Both sources are algorithms and systems papers that say nothing whatever about security.** The property is imported here and the security consequence is this programme's inference, not a finding either paper reports. **Their evidence is measured recall-versus-throughput behaviour against contemporary baselines on standard benchmarks; every quantity in them is a property of a particular index on a particular dataset, and none is reproduced here.**

**3.5 The three quiet failures, and why each is demonstrated rather than described.**

**Chunking discards permissions.** When a skillset chunks documents — standard practice for retrieval — **the permission metadata fields move from indexer field mappings to index projections**, and without that projection **"chunk-level references aren't filtered."** The parent document is correctly permissioned; the derived chunks are not; retrieval returns chunks. **Everything looks correct from every position except the one that checks a chunk**, which is why the session has learners break it rather than read about it.

**Enforcement lags the source.** Query-time enforcement evaluates the caller's claims against **"the permission metadata that's already stored in the index"**, and source-side permission changes are **"only reflected in search results after that metadata is synchronized to the index."** The preview terms state a timing lag directly. **No latency figure is published anywhere**, so the revocation window is unknown and must be measured per deployment — which is why the session makes the learner measure theirs and forbids generalising it.

**Two asymmetries make the lag worse in the plausible direction.** Changes to items with unique permissions refresh on each successful indexer run, while **changes inherited from a parent scope require an explicit resynchronisation** — and the inherited case is both more common and the one that does not self-heal. And the collaboration platform's own information-management policies that gate user access **"aren't evaluated, ingested, or honored at query time"**, an explicit stated gap between source enforcement and index enforcement.

One privilege consideration belongs in the learner's [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) registry rather than here: ingesting those permissions requires **full-control permission across all sites** in order to read content and access-control lists. **Requiring full control over everything in order to read permissions is a real privilege consideration and should be taught as one.**

**3.6 Why the failure taxonomy's silence is itself informative.**

The standard engineering taxonomy for retrieval-augmented systems turns "the answer was wrong" into a locatable stage, built from three deployments in three domains ([research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md)). Several of its named failures are retrieval-path failures with security consequences the taxonomy does not read that way: a document that should have been retrieved and was not is a quality failure, and a document that should not have been retrieved and was is the same stage from the other side.

**Access control is a failure mode that taxonomy does not centre**, and noticing that a widely used diagnostic vocabulary has no word for your problem is a finding about the field rather than about the paper.

Its strongest claim binds this session's whole approach: **validation "is only feasible during operation"**, because the space of queries, documents and interactions cannot be adequately anticipated before deployment, and robustness therefore evolves rather than being designed in. **A permissions defect will not appear in pre-release testing**, because pre-release testing asks the questions you thought of with the identities you had. That is the warrant for the session's two-persona demonstration: it is the smallest available operational validation.

**Its limits:** an experience report, not a study. **No frequency data, no severity data, no controlled comparison**, and three specific systems rather than a representative sample. It predates widespread agentic retrieval and multi-hop patterns. **Its seven failure-point names are recorded in this repository as reconstructed rather than quoted** and are not relied on here.

**3.7 Why the same structure appears one layer up, and why the two halves are one session.**

The chained pipeline this module rests on **infers missing security properties from best practice, forbids marking those inferences in the table, and resolves conflicts in the more secure direction. Provenance survives only in a separate justification section which the assembly stage is explicitly instructed to exclude. The review stage then scores by counting non-empty cells in that same table** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md)).

**That is structurally identical to the retrieval case.** Content enters with a permission; retrieval drops it; generation produces an answer carrying none; a reader treats the answer as knowledge. A value enters with a provenance; the format forbids the marker; assembly drops the justification; a scorer treats the cell as evidence. **In both, something enters qualified and leaves unqualified, and the next step consumes the unqualified thing as though it had been established.**

Three further findings from that source establish what a score is worth. **The proxy checks are trivially satisfiable** — most reduce to whether some mitigation mentions a thing, and the operative definition of a *specific* mitigation is that it names a concrete technology, **so naming a technology you have not deployed scores full marks.** **The scores measure documents rather than systems**, which one constraint states plainly while the presentation contradicts everywhere. And **self-verification is self-report**: a checklist run by the model against its own output is a prompt-engineering device that improves quality and **is not a control**, because the thing checked and the thing checking are the same. **Rendering it into the deliverable with its ticks intact gives a configured control the visual language of an audit trail.**

**That source is internal and access-controlled: paraphrased here, quoted nowhere, attributable to nothing, and supplying no external warrant for any claim. It reports no outcome evidence and not one calibration constant citing a study, a measurement, a pilot or a rationale. None is reproduced here.**

**3.8 Why the two defects need separate fixes.** Marker loss and optimistic resolution are orthogonal. **Neither fix is a superset of the other**, and a learner who conflates them proposes one change and believes the problem solved. Restoring the provenance section yields traceable values that still lean in a known direction; neutralising the conflict rule yields honest values with no trace. **The requirement to state both is what makes opening item 5 hard**, because a single change addressing both is rare and finding one is the exercise.

**3.9 Why the double score is a reporting format rather than a refusal.** A learner taught only to distrust scores will decline to produce one, and the space will be filled by somebody who will not decline. **Reporting two numbers is a form that survives contact with an audience**, and the gap between them is more informative than either.

**3.10 Why the smallest change is the assessed thinking.** Any competent learner can design a better path. **A redesign requires authority the engineer does not have and time the engagement does not have**, and it will not happen. A change to one artefact or one step, whose cost is stated, is the only class of fix that gets made.

**3.11 Why this is urgent rather than merely correct.** The data-posture argument closes the loop from [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md): generative AI **"amplifies the problem of oversharing data"** by surfacing content that is obsolete, over-permissioned or lacking governance controls ([research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md)). **Deploying capable retrieval converts every dormant permissions error in an organisation into a live one.** The permissions were always wrong; nobody was reading. **First-party and definitional: no detection rate, no false-positive rate and no coverage measurement is published — assert none.** One page behind that note is explicitly superseded; the other was read through search excerpts.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The movement sequence.** Situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**4.2 The opening items walk the retrieval path in order without naming the argument**, so that the learner assembles it themselves. Item 1 is where the room goes quiet. Item 3 — *somebody's access is revoked at nine, they ask at five past nine* — has no comfortable answer, and the honest one is a sentence about a window nobody has measured.

**4.3 Why the session demonstrates a leak rather than describing one.** The two-persona exercise is the documentation's own suggested lab and it is the only thing that settles the argument. **A described leak is agreed with and forgotten; an observed one changes what a learner builds next week.** It is also the smallest available instance of the taxonomy's claim that validation is only feasible during operation.

**4.4 Why chunk projection is broken on purpose.** It takes minutes, it is silent, and **every check a learner would normally write passes while it is broken.** Requiring them to then name the check that *would* have caught it is where the session's most durable practical output comes from: a per-persona retrieval test at chunk granularity, which almost nobody writes.

**4.5 Why the revocation window is measured rather than looked up.** Because it cannot be looked up. **No latency figure is published anywhere**, and a learner who has watched their own content stay retrievable after a revocation will never again write a plan whose implicit assumption is zero.

**4.6 Why the laundering trace comes after the retrieval work and not before.** A learner who meets the pipeline critique first treats it as a criticism of somebody else's tool. A learner who has just watched their own system launder access recognises the same shape in their own plan. **The ordering is what converts a critique into a self-diagnosis.**

**4.7 The attack step asks for the read-before-check by name.** An agent handed a trace will confirm it, because a trace is internally coherent. **Walking a path looking for the step where content is read before authorisation is evaluated is a different operation, and it has to be asked for.**

**4.8 The double score is required in both directions**, and a learner whose two numbers match is directed to the likeliest explanation rather than the flattering one: their provenance section was empty when they started, which means the marking failed at [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) rather than that the artefact is sound.

**4.9 The final exercise turns the instrument on the learner's own checklists**, and is placed last deliberately. A learner who meets it first defends their own work; a learner who has just traced two laundering paths recognises the third.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The trimmed answer.** Documents filtered out after something already read them. *Countermeasure:* the trace's explicit `position_relative_to_retrieval` field and its companion — what read the untrimmed set — which converts an architectural fact into a question with an answer.

**The unprojected chunk.** *Countermeasure:* breaking it on purpose, plus the requirement to name the check that would have caught it. **This is the design's strongest countermeasure in the session**, because the demonstration is unambiguous and takes minutes.

**The assumed revocation.** *Countermeasure:* the measured window, and the standard's warning sign — the word *revoked* appearing with no window beside it.

**The application-as-boundary.** A string filter treated as an authorisation check. *Countermeasure:* exercise 3's criterion, which names the most common failing answer explicitly: a correct description of a security filter offered as an answer to a question about authorisation.

**The general complaint.** *Countermeasure:* the step-name requirement, and the standard's warning sign — your trace has no step names in it.

**The big fix.** *Countermeasure:* the affordability constraint, and the standard's warning sign — your fix requires more than one team to agree.

**The single-defect reading.** The most likely failure in the second half. *Countermeasure:* the two-defect statement as a named artefact, plus §3.8's argument that neither fix subsumes the other. **The design expects this countermeasure to be partially effective and §9 predicts the residual.**

**The clean re-score.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's direction to the likelier explanation. **This is a weak countermeasure** because it depends on a learner accepting an unflattering reading of their own earlier work.

**The audit-trail costume.** *Countermeasure:* exercise 7's explicit search of the learner's own artefacts, run closed book.

**The score as measurement.** *Countermeasure:* the discomfort test in the standard — would you be willing to write *this measures the document, not the system* directly underneath your number?

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. Four constituents are assessed at [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md): that the authorisation trace names every step including those where nothing is compared; that the revocation window is stated as measured or unmeasured and never as zero; that the plan distinguishes observed from inferred values where the reader meets them; and that any self-verification present is classified as a configured control rather than presented as an audit trail.

**The smallest change is not assessed at [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md)** and is not assessed anywhere. ## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the compositional critique is hindsight from a vantage point nobody occupies, and it is therefore unactionable.**

At its strongest: the defect is visible only to a reader holding every step's definition at once. No participant in a real chain does — that is what a division of labour is. Each author solved their own problem correctly. **Criticising the design for an emergent property is criticising it for being a system**, and the lesson a learner takes away is a vague obligation to see the whole, which is not a skill, not teachable, and not staffable.

**Substantially conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to this session.** Two answers, neither complete. The first is positional: **a forward-deployed engineer is unusually likely to hold the whole chain**, because they arrive from outside, own the outcome rather than a step, and are the only person who reads the delivered artefact and the working files. That is the role this programme is training and it is why the session sits here rather than in a security curriculum. The second is that the session converts the observation into something staffable by requiring the smallest affordable change rather than an awareness. **The declined fix is to add a chain-wide provenance-audit step.** It is declined for the module's standing reason: that step would be another instruction to a model inside the same chain — a configured control checking configured controls — and it would report its own compliance. **The cheaper executable alternative already exists and is what the session teaches: keep the provenance in the delivered document, where any reader can apply exercise 6's single question without the design's cooperation.**

**Objection 2: teaching learners to distrust scores will produce engineers who report nothing, and a customer with no summary is worse served than one with an imperfect number.**

At its strongest: an executive audience requires a summary. Refusing to provide one does not create a more careful reader; it cedes the summary to whoever is willing to write it, who will be less scrupulous. **A programme that spends a session dismantling the only communicable artefact in the process, and offers no replacement, has made its graduates useless in the room where the decision is taken.**

**Conceded in part.** The session does not require zero numbers; it requires two, and a stated gap. **The double score is a reporting format designed precisely to survive that room** — it is communicable, it is short, and it makes the limitation part of the message rather than an omission the reader has to detect. **The declined fix is to teach a corrected single score.** It is declined because there is no basis for a correction: every weighting, band boundary and partial-credit fraction in the source material is an assertion with no derivation behind it, and inventing a correction factor here would commit exactly the fault the session exists to expose. **A programme that condemns undeclared constants will not ship one of its own.** The residual difficulty — that two numbers are harder to present than one — is real and the session does not solve it.

**Objection 3: the structural claim about approximate retrieval is imported from two papers that say nothing about security, and importing a property is not the same as establishing one.**

At its strongest: the graph-index literature reports recall-versus-throughput behaviour on benchmark datasets. It does not discuss adversaries, entitlements or leakage, and nothing in it licenses a conclusion about whether the authorised set and the returned set diverge in practice, in what direction, or by enough to matter. **The session presents an inference as though it were a finding, in a module whose whole subject is that distinction.** That is the sharpest possible form of the objection because it accuses the paper of the fault it teaches.

**Conceded, and the session and this paper both label it explicitly rather than defending it.** The property imported — that the returned set is approximate, and that recall is a chosen operating point rather than a guarantee — **is reported by both sources and is not in dispute.** The security consequence drawn from it is this programme's reasoning, is classified below as design reasoning with no external warrant, and is stated in the session as an inference. **The declined fix was to remove the argument entirely and teach only the documented failures** — chunk projection and the revocation window, both of which are stated by the vendor. It is declined because those two are instances and the approximation argument is the reason such instances keep appearing: **a permission model bolted onto machinery that was designed for speed rather than for entitlement will keep producing them, and a learner who holds only the instances will patch each one as it arrives.** The cost is that the module's most general claim is its least warranted, and the paper would rather say so than trade the claim for comfort.

**Objection 4: the session's anchor is mostly a preview API surface, and a module centred on it is centred on something that will not exist in its current form by the time a cohort graduates.**

At its strongest: most of the access-control page describes a preview surface which the documentation itself warns changes without notice. Three of its four approaches are preview. Header names, role names and resynchronisation operations will move. **A learner who has memorised the two-stage check has memorised a fact with a short life, and the session's most vivid exercises — the leak demonstration, the chunk break, the revocation measurement — are all bound to it.**

**Substantially conceded, and the design accepts the perishability in exchange for the demonstration.** The durable claims are separated from the instances in the session's perishability register, and they are the ones a graduate should hold: an index is a copy; authorisation belongs to the retrieval path; an unprojected derived object is unprotected; enforcement lags the source; a filter your own code constructs is a claim rather than a check. **None of those depends on a header name.** **The declined fix was to teach the durable claims with no product attached.** It is declined because the three exercises that actually change behaviour — watching a persona receive content it cannot open, watching unprojected chunks come back, watching a revoked entitlement persist — **cannot be performed against an abstraction.** The cost is that this session will need re-verification before every offering rather than once a term, and the platform map already requires that.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will be unable to name a step at which the asker's identity is compared against a document's permissions, and will instead describe a filter their application constructs. *Instrument:* the written answer to opening item 1, coded *names a check* / *names a filter* / *names nothing* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** In the two-persona exercise, the unauthorised persona will receive the substance of a restricted document for most learners at first attempt. *Instrument:* the two transcripts, with the restricted content's presence in the unauthorised answer judged by a second reader.

**Prediction 3.** Most learners will answer opening item 4 — where do the permissions live once documents are chunked — from assumption rather than from inspection, and will be wrong. *Instrument:* the written answer against the evidence field in the submitted chunk-projection statement.

**Prediction 4.** A substantial proportion of measured revocation windows will exceed the duration of the session, so the learner records *still retrievable when I stopped* rather than an elapsed time. *Instrument:* the revocation-window statements, coded *measured* / *exceeded session* / *not attempted*. **If almost every window is short, the session over-weights this failure and should say so.**

**Prediction 5.** Most first traces will describe a tendency rather than name a step. *Instrument:* the submitted trace, coded *step-level* / *stage-level* / *tendency* by a second reader.

**Prediction 6.** Most learners will identify marker loss and not optimistic resolution. *Instrument:* the two-defect statement, coded for which defects appear, recorded per learner.

**Prediction 7.** Learners whose [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) provenance section was empty will report identical double scores and will conclude their artefact is sound rather than that their marking failed. *Instrument:* the two scores paired with the learner's stated conclusion, against the provenance section's content.

**Prediction 8.** Proposed smallest changes will be process additions more often than artefact or step changes. *Instrument:* the proposed change, coded *artefact change* / *step change* / *process addition*. **If this holds, the affordability constraint is not doing its work and the workflow should name the permitted classes of change rather than describing them.**

**Prediction 9.** Learners instructed to compare their item-1 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 3) will produce authorisation traces containing a `none` row more often than learners who read the same criterion without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* the submitted trace, coded for the presence of an honestly-marked uncompared step, across two cohorts — or, absent two cohorts, a within-cohort split on completion of exercise 3. **This is the session's only direct test of the mechanism §3.2 rests on.**

**Prediction 10.** In exercise 7, a majority of learners will find at least one self-verification checklist in their own artefacts, and a minority will find one rendered into a deliverable with its ticks intact. *Instrument:* the classification submitted, audited by a second reader against the learner's artefacts. **This predicts the failure of the session's own final countermeasure**: if the minority is near zero, the audit-trail costume is a defect of the specimen rather than a habit the cohort has, and the exercise should be re-scoped to reading rather than self-inspection.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That authorisation is a property of the retrieval path rather than of the answer; that a system retrieving what a user may not read and then summarising it has leaked; that an index is a copy and copies do not inherit access rules; and that the same laundering structure — something entering qualified and leaving unqualified — recurs one layer up in the learner's own plan.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about frequency or consequence. **No source available to this programme reports how often unauthorised content reaches a generated answer, how long any revocation window is, how often chunk projection is missed, or whether any of this has contributed to a real harm.** The vendor documentation is definitional and publishes no latency measurement, no failure-rate data and no comparison of its four approaches on security strength. **The structural argument about approximate retrieval is imported from papers that are not about security, and §8's third objection concedes it.**

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper**, including the algorithmic ones, which are properties of particular indexes on particular datasets and transfer to nothing.

**The concession worth remembering.** Everybody in both paths was doing their job well. That is not a mitigating detail — **it is the finding.** The systems you build will be made of steps that are each defensible, and nobody will be assigned to notice what they add up to. **Your own plan already contains one; you found it in the second half of the session.**
