## In plain terms

The register from [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) comes back nearly all frozen. The design that would actually work is not available, and the one that is available is worse, and the engineer knows exactly how much worse.

**What happens next is the session.** In practice it is one of three things, and two of them are failures.

**Learned helplessness.** Everything is frozen, so nothing is attempted, and the eventual disappointment is attributed to the constraints. It is convenient, it is unfalsifiable, and it is usually partly true — which is what makes it durable. The engineer's judgement disappears into somebody else's decisions and nobody can afterwards say what it would have contributed.

**The shadow build.** The unavailable design gets built anyway, quietly, because it is better. It works, people start depending on it, and it is now an unowned dependency inside somebody else's estate with no operator, no support and no record. This failure is committed by good engineers and it feels like initiative.

**The third response is the one the session teaches**, and it has two halves that must be produced together. **Write the unavailable design down properly** — well enough that somebody could build it if the constraint moved — and name the single constraint that forecloses it. Then **find the smallest thing you can change without asking anybody, choose it for what it would demonstrate about that constraint, and start it.**

The rule behind this comes from the practice corpus and **it is an assertion with nothing behind it.** No instance of a frozen space being opened appears anywhere in that material. Worse — and this is taught rather than hidden — **the same material elsewhere explains disappointing delivery outcomes by pointing at the customer's constraints**, which is the move this rule forbids. The contradiction is handed to the learner intact.

## Abstract

This paper defends a session built on an unevidenced rule, in a source that contradicts the rule elsewhere in its own material, and defends handing the contradiction to the learner rather than resolving it. It argues that the unavailable design must be written to a buildable standard rather than sketched, because a sketch cannot be acted on if the constraint moves and because writing it properly is what separates an argument from a complaint. It requires the available change to be chosen for its evidential value about the foreclosing constraint rather than for ease, and requires the unfreezing argument to be written from the constraint holder's interest and against a standard independent of both parties. The paper concedes that the session may be teaching learners to spend effort on arguments that will not succeed, that it has no way to distinguish a genuine available change from a convenient one, and that the corpus's contradiction may indicate the rule is a slogan rather than a method.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

**The engineer's judgement is the thing being wasted, and its waste is invisible.**

When a design is foreclosed by a constraint, the analysis that produced the design does not go anywhere. It is not written down, because writing down a design you cannot build feels like self-indulgence. It is not communicated, because the audience for it is the person who holds the constraint and approaching them feels like escalation. **So it evaporates**, and what remains is a diminished design with no record that a better one was available or what it would have required.

The consequence appears later and is attributed elsewhere. When the delivered system underperforms, the constraint is cited — correctly, and unfalsifiably, because nobody wrote down what the alternative was or what it would have cost to get there. **The explanation cannot be checked because the evidence for it was never produced.**

There is a second problem the session addresses in the same movement, and it is about what the engineer does with the interval. **A constraint is not usually reconsidered on argument alone.** It is reconsidered when something makes the reconsideration cheap or makes the risk it guards against look smaller. Producing that something is engineering work, it is usually small, and it is almost never the work anybody plans.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session consumes the register from [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) — specifically its authorities and its interests — and the boundary decisions from [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md), which determine what the fallback design can actually reach. **A learner without both writes the unavailable design and has nowhere to address the argument.**

The prior it works against is that constraint is the end of a design conversation rather than the start of a different one. **That prior is reasonable, is frequently correct, and is why this session's countermeasures are all structural rather than motivational** — telling somebody that constraint is not an excuse changes nothing, and requiring them to name the smallest thing they can change without permission changes what they do that week.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The module's highest, and the load is affective rather than intrinsic. Writing down a design you are not allowed to build, in enough detail that somebody could build it, and then starting something smaller without resentment, is uncomfortable in a way none of the other sessions is.

**3.2 Why the unavailable design is written to a buildable standard.** Two reasons and both are structural. **If the constraint moves, a sketch cannot be acted on and a design can** — and constraints move on timescales nobody predicts, usually because something else changed. And the discipline of writing it properly is what converts a grievance into an argument, because a design has to be internally consistent and a complaint does not.

**3.3 Why the foreclosing constraint must be named singly.** Nothing can be argued against a situation. **A constraint fog is functionally identical to learned helplessness** — it produces no addressee, no request and no test — and the requirement to name one constraint is the session's mechanism for detecting the fog rather than for solving it.

**3.4 Why the available change is chosen for evidence rather than for ease.** The available change's purpose is to make the frozen constraint reconsiderable: to show that the risk it guards against is smaller than assumed, that the alternative works, or that the cost is bearable. **A change chosen for being easy satisfies the session and teaches nothing**, and this is the most likely way to comply without learning.

**3.5 Why the unfreezing argument is written from the holder's interest and against an independent standard.** A constraint is a position and it is held for reasons that are usually good from where the holder stands. Arguing from your own preference produces a contest of wills; **arguing from a standard independent of both parties gives the holder something they can tell the people they must answer to**, which is the mechanism the negotiation literature identifies and which that literature explicitly declines to attach any effect to. That source also supplies the caution the session repeats: **a criterion is not independent merely because it is numerical**, and both parties must ask who selected it, what it omits and whether it applies to this workload.

**3.6 Why the platform anchor is a pair of topologies rather than a feature.**

The session needs a frozen constraint that every learner shares, that is genuinely not theirs to move, and whose reason is legible enough to argue with. Deployment topology is the best available instance, and one vendor documents it unusually plainly: the **basic setup** is **"for rapid prototyping and individual development when speed and ease of access are priorities"** and **"lacks network isolation"**, while the **standard setup** provides **"fine-grained control over data, security, and networking"**, in public-networking and private-networking variants ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)).

**The design's reading of that pair is the module's thesis in its sharpest available form, and it is not the obvious reading.** The obvious reading is a recommendation: choose the controlled option. The design rejects it. **Prototyping topology and production topology are different systems, and the transition between them is a deliberate act rather than a late discovery** — which makes the fast topology's missing isolation a property of prototyping rather than a defect of a product. A team that treats the move as a configuration change has frozen a constraint it never examined, in its own plan, by omission.

That reframing is what makes the topology usable as this session's material rather than as a deployment lecture. **The production topology is frozen and the prototyping topology is not**, so the pair contains a genuine available change: build in the one you can reach, and produce the evidence the exception argument needs. Most learners never think of it, because a topology reads as an environment rather than as a lever.

**3.7 What the production topology consists of, and why composition is the real argument.**

An unfreezing argument that addresses a single setting will lose, and the posture-management documentation says why without meaning to. Its four infrastructure-as-code checks — **private endpoints, restricted endpoints, managed identity, identity-based authentication** — are a short and checkable baseline, and three of the four eliminate keys or network exposure ([research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md)). But the capability that sits above them is **attack path analysis**, which exists because of **"toxic combinations"** — individually acceptable configurations composing into a breach — with grounding and fine-tuning named as the two moments a model touches proprietary data by construction.

**The design consequence is the session's most transferable instruction: argue at the level the holder is accountable at.** A constraint that looks arbitrary at the level of one setting is frequently rational at the level of a composition, and a learner who names their own toxic combination before the holder does has demonstrated that they are reasoning where the accountability sits. **This is design reasoning built on a documented capability, not a finding**; the documentation publishes no detection rate, no false-positive rate and no coverage measurement for any of it, one consulted page is explicitly superseded, and agent-level capability moves to a separate licence on a stated date.

**3.8 Why a gateway is where most available changes are found.**

A gateway is the rare component that can be added in front of something without changing the something, which makes it structurally the most likely place for a change requiring nobody's permission. The mechanisms matter less than the property, but they are worth naming because they map onto what compliance functions actually worry about: **managed identities remove the need for keys rather than storing them better**; a token-limit policy enforces per-consumer limits keyed on an arbitrary counter key, with prompt tokens precalculable at the edge; and **metric dimensions are what make consumption attributable, and attribution is what makes governance possible** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md)).

**Attribution and blast radius are the two things a constraint's holder is usually protecting**, and both are addressable at a boundary the engineer owns. That is why the workflow's available change is a gateway policy rather than a redesign. **First-party documentation, definitional rather than empirical, with no benchmarks and no cost measurements, and capability varying by service tier.**

**3.9 Why the fallback needs a declared gating metric.**

The protest design is this session's most likely product and the standard alone will not prevent it. The production-systems literature supplies the structural countermeasure: the **optimising metric and gating metric** pair, whose transferable form is **declare the constraint before you start adding capability, and stop at the constraint rather than at the point of diminishing returns** ([research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md)).

Declared before the fallback is designed, the gating metric makes the fallback a design with a stated boundary. Declared afterwards, it is a justification for whatever was built. **The ordering is the whole of the intervention**, and it is the same ordering argument the programme makes about criteria at [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md). **That source is a survey by its own description, offers no comparative evidence for any recommended practice, has a non-neutral toolchain, and every figure in it is a property of a particular system.**

**3.10 Why a second prohibition is taught, and why it is an identity decision.**

§3.6's topology pair teaches a prohibition expressed as a **missing capability**: the fast option lacks a property, and the gap is closable by provisioning differently. That is one of three shapes a prohibition takes, and a session built on it alone leaves learners unable to recognise the other two.

The second shape is **an option the platform supplies and declines to recommend**, and the identity material supplies the cleanest available instance ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-365-and-agent-identity.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-365-and-agent-identity.md), read 2026-07-31). Three identity types exist for an agent; two are explicitly not recommended, and the reasons are named rather than gestured at. The human-user model fails because access policies built for people "don't apply correctly to agents", risk detections degrade, and **governance processes "can incorrectly remove agent access"** — a leaver routine deprovisioning a working agent. The application model fails on lifespan: service principals "carry the expectation of long-term stability, known ownership, and managed lifecycle", while an agent "might exist for minutes… or might be created and destroyed thousands of times per day". **An identity model assuming permanence, applied to an ephemeral population, produces orphaned credentials faster than anyone can clean them up.**

The design's use for this is not to install a preference. It is that **the decision must be defended by what it declined**, which is a different and harder act than defending what it chose — and it is the same discipline §3.2 asks of the unavailable design and §3.5 asks of the unfreezing argument, applied to a decision the learner is actually allowed to take.

Three properties of the replacement make the defence writable rather than rhetorical. **Blueprint count is named by the vendor as a security boundary decision**, because a blueprint holds the credentials for every identity minted from it, which gives the learner a number to justify on blast radius rather than on convenience. **A sponsor is required at creation and a technical owner is not**, with sponsorship transferring automatically on departure — the strongest available example in the platform strand of an accountability obligation the directory enforces rather than records, and precisely what the declined option cannot supply. And **agent activity is attributed by a property on existing event types rather than by a new one**, so an audit estate built before the property existed folds agent activity into ordinary activity and produces confident, complete-looking output. That last is a governance failure with no error message, which makes it the right kind of cost to put in a rejected-option column.

Two further items are carried to [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) rather than argued here, because they are constraints rather than trades: a published per-principal cap on identity creation, and a creation permission that **cannot be revoked** — a control whose only settings are on and gone.

**3.11 Why the third shape is taught, and why the session's own apparatus is useless against it.**

The third shape is **a component that works, and whose terms surrender protections the customer holds.** The worked instance is a web-reaching retrieval source whose documented terms disapply the data-protection addendum, place data outside the platform's compliance and geographic boundary, and **waive elevated government-cloud commitments "to include data sovereignty"** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-iq-intelligence-layer.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-iq-intelligence-layer.md), read 2026-07-31).

**This is included because it is the case the rest of the session cannot handle, and saying so is more useful than pretending otherwise.** §3.4's available change exists to make a constraint reconsiderable by producing evidence about the risk it guards against. **No evidence changes a waived contractual commitment.** There is no gateway in front of it, no policy over it and no architecture around it. The correct output is the unavailable design written properly, plus an available change pointed somewhere else entirely — at whether the private corpus can be made to cover the question without the component at all.

The discomfort is deliberate and is the point. The same vendor's published evaluation reports that adding a web source recovers ground lost where a private corpus does not cover the question. **So the capability that most helps is the one an entire class of customer may not use, and both facts are true at once.** A learner who can hold that without resolving it has understood the module; one who resolves it in either direction has not. **The headline figure attached to that evaluation is prohibited in this repository, appears nowhere in the session or this paper, and only direction and mechanism transfer.**

**Its limits, and they bound §3.10 and §3.11 together.** The identity material was read under three months after that product reached general availability, **with preview status varying feature by feature**, several capabilities behind a further restricted programme, and three announced capabilities carrying a stated date now past that **were not re-verified.** The terms page was read at **search-excerpt depth only**, so its structure is reconstructed and its wording is what the excerpt returned. **Not one detection rate, false-positive rate, coverage measurement, incident-reduction figure or availability target appears anywhere in either source.** Both establish what a platform is and neither is evidence that using it reduces anything. **The argument that ephemerality breaks both existing identity models is reasoning rather than measurement**, and it is taught because it is legible and correct-looking, not because it has been tested.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

Seven movements: situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 3 is the instrument and most learners answer it with *nothing*.** The session exists to make that answer expensive. It is placed third so that items 1 and 2 have already established that a better design exists and that a single constraint forecloses it — which is what makes *nothing* look like a claim rather than a description.

**Two artefacts are required together and neither is acceptable alone.** The unavailable design without the available change is a complaint with a diagram. The available change without the unavailable design is activity with no argument behind it.

**The work movement specifies an order: design first, argument second, change third.** The design is written while the learner is still annoyed, because that is when it is easiest to write and hardest to write fairly. The argument is written afterwards, from the other side of the table, which requires the annoyance to have been discharged into the design first.

**The change is started before any decision about the design.** This is the movement's sharpest instruction and it inverts the sequence most learners expect. **The available change is the evidence the decision needs, and there is no order in which the evidence comes second.**

**The review movement is one question.** *Do you need anybody's permission for this?* Most first attempts fail it, because the changes people reach for first are the visible ones and visible changes require sanction.

**4.1 What the platform workflow is for.** It exists to convert a sentence into an experience. A learner told that prototyping and production topologies are different systems will agree and forget. A learner who provisions the fast topology, reads the documented statement that it lacks network isolation, and then writes that sentence into their own notes beside the date, has met the constraint rather than been informed of it.

**The `Topology` metric dimension is this design's invention and it is the movement's honest core.** Every number the learner produces this term is labelled with the fact that it came from a topology the review would reject. **A prototype result presented without that label is the single most common way an engineer loses credibility with a compliance function**, and the label costs nothing to attach and is almost never attached.

The workflow deliberately builds *part* of the unavailable design rather than all of it. The purpose is evidence about one constraint, not a demonstration — and a learner who builds the whole thing has produced the shadow build §5 warns about, in sanctioned form and therefore harder to notice.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Learned helplessness.** The failure the session exists against. *Countermeasure:* the required available change and the review movement's single question. **Neither is a countermeasure to the disposition; both are countermeasures to acting on it this week.**

**The shadow build.** *Countermeasure:* the standard names it explicitly and names the warning sign — being pleased that nobody has noticed. **This is a weak countermeasure against a failure that is genuinely tempting**, and the session has no stronger one.

**The protest design.** The fallback is deliberately unimpressive so everyone can see what the constraint cost. *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that the fallback be good at what it is, with its compromises acknowledged rather than resented, plus the observation that a design produced under protest is a design nobody will maintain.

**The constraint fog.** *Countermeasure:* the single-constraint requirement, and the warning sign that an unfreezing argument with more than one addressee has not been narrowed.

**The easy available change.** *Countermeasure:* the standard requires the change to be chosen for what it demonstrates and to name who would read the result. **A learner can satisfy both by writing a plausible sentence, and the design has no detection.**

**The argument that embarrasses.** Correct, well reasoned, and makes the recipient look negligent. *Countermeasure:* the warning sign about forwarding, which is a usable test and is the only one available.

**The prototype presented as production.** A learner gathers evidence in a topology with no network isolation and presents it without saying so. *Countermeasure:* the `Topology` dimension required in the workflow's metric emission, which attaches the caveat to the data rather than to the covering note. **This is the strongest countermeasure in the session**, because it is mechanical and survives the learner forgetting.

**The available change that becomes a dependency.** The evidential build works, somebody starts using it, and it is now an unowned component in somebody else's estate. *Countermeasure:* partial — the production-systems rule that a pipeline is built when a thing has users and not before, stated in the material. **A rule stated in material is not a countermeasure and this paper does not pretend otherwise**; the session's only real defence is that the change is required to be small.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence and no specification item at [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) marks the unavailable design or the available change.**

That is a deliberate and uncomfortable omission. **What this session produces is a disposition and a piece of writing, and the disposition cannot be assessed without assessing whether the learner would in fact have persisted** — which this programme declines to do, on the same reasoning [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) gave for never assessing whether a learner would in fact have declined an engagement.

The consequence is that the module's most difficult session contributes nothing to either assessment. **This paper records that as a cost rather than defending it.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the rule is a slogan, and the source's own contradiction is the evidence.**

At its strongest: the same body of material that says constraint is not an excuse also uses constraint as its prepared explanation for disappointing delivery, paired with a readiness rubric composed almost entirely of the customer's dispositions. **A practice that explains its own shortfalls by the customer's constraints has arranged its evidence so that a disappointing result confirms rather than challenges its thesis.** Read that way, the rule is not a method the organisation follows; it is something it says to its engineers while doing the opposite institutionally. Teaching it as a professional discipline, on that authority, is teaching a slogan and dressing it as practice.

**Conceded, and this is the strongest objection in the stage.** The rebuttal does not defend the source. It defends the session on different grounds: the rule is worth teaching because the failure it names is real and observable independently of the source — engineers do stop, judgement does evaporate, and nobody writes down what was foreclosed — and because the session's artefacts are checkable even if the rule is not. **The declined fix is to drop the rule and teach only the two artefacts.** It is declined because the artefacts without the rule have no reason to exist, and a session that produced them without saying why would be worse. **The contradiction is stated in the session, in the module, and here, so that a learner can decide for themselves whether the rule survives its source.**

**Objection 2: the session may be teaching learners to spend effort on arguments that will not succeed.**

At its strongest: constraints in enterprise estates are frequently immovable for reasons that are entirely sound — regulation, contract, a decision taken at a level the engineer will never reach. **An engineer who writes an unavailable design, builds an evidential change, and constructs an argument, for a constraint that was never going to move, has spent delivery time on a private grievance and will be judged for it.** The session has no test for which constraints are worth pressing, and supplies none, and the negotiation literature it draws on cannot supply one either because it establishes no effect.

**Conceded, and the session's own answer is deliberately limited rather than adequate.** The available change is required to be small enough to start without permission, which bounds the wasted effort to something the learner can absorb; and the argument is required to be written from the holder's interest, which is the form most likely to be read. **The declined fix is a screening test for which constraints to press.** It is declined because any such test would be a threshold asserted without warrant — precisely what [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) refused to do for discovery sufficiency and [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) refused to do for measurement quality — and a fabricated screening rule would be worse than none.

**Objection 3: teaching learners to build in the prototyping topology teaches them to normalise an unsafe one.**

At its strongest, and it is the objection the platform anchor most deserves: the session instructs a learner to provision a deployment shape whose own documentation states that it lacks network isolation, and then to generate evidence in it and take that evidence to a compliance function. **Every incentive afterwards runs one way.** The evidence is real, the topology is convenient, the deadline is close, and the learner has just been taught that building in the fast shape is a legitimate professional move. A cohort trained this way will produce engineers who reach for the uncontrolled topology first and rationalise it second, which is the opposite of what a stage on frozen constraints should produce. The `Topology` label is a convention, and conventions are the first thing dropped under pressure.

**Partly conceded, and the concession is uncomfortable because the objection identifies a real incentive the design creates.** Three defences, none complete. The prototyping topology is the one the learner already has — the session does not create the access, it makes the learner say out loud what the access lacks. The label is attached to the *data* rather than to the covering note, so it survives the note being rewritten. And the workflow forbids building the whole design and requires the change to be small enough to start without permission, which bounds what can quietly become production. **The declined fix is to run the workflow only in the controlled topology.** It is declined because most learners cannot obtain one, and a workflow only the well-provisioned can execute would teach the module's lesson to exactly the people who least need it. **The cost of that decision is the incentive this objection names, and prediction 6 is the only thing that would detect it.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will answer opening item 3 with *nothing* or with something requiring permission. *Instrument:* the written answers, coded *nothing* / *requires permission* / *genuinely available* by a second reader, recorded before the review movement.

**Prediction 2.** Available changes will more often be chosen for ease than for evidential value, judged by whether the learner can name who would read the result. *Instrument:* a single question put in the review movement, recorded per learner.

**Prediction 3.** Learners unable to name a single foreclosing constraint will also be unable to name an addressee for the unfreezing argument. *Instrument:* the two fields, compared per learner. **If the two dissociate, the constraint-fog diagnosis is wrong and the single-constraint requirement should be reconsidered.**

**Prediction 4.** Unavailable designs written before the unfreezing argument will be more specific than those written after. *Instrument:* paired documents from a cohort where the order is deliberately reversed, judged unlabelled by a second reader. **This requires a cohort to be taught the session in the wrong order on purpose, which may not be defensible, and if it is not the prediction should be withdrawn rather than left unmeasurable.**

**Prediction 5.** After provisioning the prototyping topology and reading its stated limitation, learners will more often name deployment topology as their foreclosing constraint than they did at [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md). *Instrument:* the foreclosing constraint named at S026 against the one named here, compared per learner. **If topology does not move up the list, the platform movement is a demonstration rather than an instrument**, and the session should say so rather than implying the provisioning changed anything.

**Prediction 6.** A minority of learners will present prototyping-topology evidence in their unfreezing argument without any statement of the topology it came from, despite the metric dimension carrying it. *Instrument:* the submitted argument, coded for whether the topology is stated in the prose and not only in the data. **This predicts the failure of §5's strongest countermeasure and is the direct test of §8's third objection**, and it is the prediction this paper would least like to lose.

**Prediction 7.** Fallback designs with a gating metric declared before the design was written will be judged *designed* rather than *diminished* by a second reader more often than those where it was declared after. *Instrument:* the two documents' timestamps against a blind judgement of the fallback. **This is the only test of §3.9's ordering claim**, and a null result would mean the gating metric is ceremony.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the engineer's foreclosed judgement is the thing being wasted and its waste is invisible; that a constraint you cannot name singly cannot be argued against; and that a constraint is reconsidered when something makes the reconsideration cheap, not when somebody argues well.

**What the evidence licenses.** **Nothing.** The rule is asserted by a source with no citation of any kind and no instance of its own use, and the same source contradicts it elsewhere. The negotiation material establishes no effect by its authors' own statement. **The session is design reasoning throughout and says so in every movement that carries a claim.**

**The concession worth remembering.** This is the module's hardest session and it contributes to no assessment, because what it teaches is a disposition and this programme does not assess whether a learner would have acted on one.

**The silences that matter.** **The setup-options guidance states that the fast topology lacks network isolation and nowhere states what a team must do to move between the two topologies, how long that takes, or what it costs** — which is precisely the transition this session calls a deliberate act, and the documentation supplies no basis for planning it. The posture-management material publishes **no detection rate, no false-positive rate and no coverage measurement for any control**, so nothing establishes that its baseline checks close what they are claimed to close, and it publishes no guidance on how a team should *argue* for an exception, which is the entire content of this session. The gateway documentation argues for a gateway and does not discuss the latency, cost or operational burden the gateway itself adds — the exact figure a learner needs when proposing one as an available change.
