# WP-070 — Ship or Do Not Ship: First Rehearsal

**Seminar day:** [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) · **Module:** [M14](/wiki/modules/M14.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md)
**Strands:** ME · SY · SG · PE · **Complexity class:** 5 · **Assessment-bearing**
**Platform anchor:** the student's own four-day chain — a Foundry evaluation gate in CI, a trace or response evaluation of live traffic, a signal with a liveness path, and an arrangement with one enforced element · **MCP servers:** none in use during the assessed phases; the Foundry, Azure and Learn MCP Servers are available for questions of fact only

## Abstract

This paper defends an entrustment judgement on a decision made under evidence the design states in advance is insufficient, and defends naming the insufficiency as a structural fact rather than as a shortfall the student should have closed. It defends assessing what the student is willing to be wrong about rather than whether they were right, defends a decision artefact whose items are the four days' outputs assembled rather than anything built on the day, and defends requiring the student to state the strongest case against their own decision — the mechanism S069's best-performing arrangement isolated. **It defends one addition made by the practical turn: a ninth artefact item stating what of the evidence was produced by an agent, what was verified, what is being relied on unchecked, which gate settings were decided rather than accepted, and what the gate's judge has been shown to do — which is not a tooling audit but a statement about what the evidence licenses, in the register where this cohort's evidence is now produced.** It defends a defence in which the two questions students are least prepared for come from a real user and from the person being asked to accept an undetectable failure. **Section 7 reports the discharge of both concessions WP-050 §8 made and named this day for**, and reports the seven rule-6 obligations placed here by S060, S064, S065 (two), S066, S068 and S069. It concedes that the comparison built to answer the first of those concessions is under-dosed in the only form that could answer it, that the day cannot simultaneously be a real occasion and a rehearsal and has not said which it is, **that item 9 was introduced as an assessed element with no rehearsal until a fix was made, and that the practical turn has changed how expensive the assessed evidence is to produce without the design having asked what that does to the judgement.**

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Four days have produced a gate with a validated error structure, a measured and attributed offline-online gap, a detection design with a named recipient and an explicit list of what will not be seen, and a governance arrangement that was negotiated or refused. The work is good. It does not add up to a sufficient case for releasing anything.

**That is not a failure of the four days and it is the problem the day must solve.** A student who believes the evidence should have been sufficient will read its insufficiency as their own shortfall, and will respond in one of two ways. They will gather more — which is available, is defensible, is what everyone will praise them for, and does not converge, because the gap [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) taught does not close. Or they will ship on the gate and call the gate the reason, which is [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md) point 3 exactly.

The day's target is the third response, and it is the one nobody has taught them: **decide what you are willing to be wrong about, say so in front of the people it will happen to, and state how you will find out.**

Three specific difficulties sit underneath, and none of them is cognitive.

**The conditional reflex.** A student who does not want to decide will ship under conditions. Conditions are legitimate and are frequently the right answer; a condition with nobody assigned to check it is a ship with a sentence in front of it, and it will be produced by a substantial share of the cohort because it is the only move that feels like judgement while deferring the judgement. The specification treats an unverified condition as an unconditional ship for that reason.

**The answerability horizon.** This is what makes the occasion class 5 rather than class 4. [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md)'s measurement was defended in the room and settled there. [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s finding was communicated and the encounter ended. **Today's decision produces users who are affected next week**, by something the student will or will not detect, and the student remains answerable either way. Phase 2's bridge asks the room to name that difference and the room can.

**The two-sided failure.** [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md) established that a wrong block leaves no record and [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) gave it a face in eleven weeks that appear in no metric. A cohort that has spent four days learning what evidence does not license is at maximum risk of the failure [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §1 named — the one that looks like rigour and is rewarded everywhere else — expressed here as a refusal to ship anything. The design's answer is to make a defended decision not to ship a pass at the same levels as a ship, and to say so before phase 1, which removes the incentive without removing the option.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Nothing is built and the load is entirely dispositional.** This follows [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §3.1's argument and this design accepts it unchanged: a judgement about a decision under pressure is invalid if the student is simultaneously constructing the evidence being decided on.

The interactivity that remains is between artefact items 3, 4 and 5. The accepted failures determine what the detection commitment must cover; the detection commitment determines which failures fall into the undetectable set; and item 5 assigns owners to that set — which is where a student discovers that the failures they were most willing to accept are the ones with neither a signal nor an owner. That discovery is the artefact's most productive moment and it is why the three items are adjacent and in that order.

Management:

- **Nothing is built on the day.**
- **The four inputs were produced on days S066 to S069** and are in front of the student.
- **The decision artefact's form is supplied**, so no time is spent on structure.
- **Assessors observe and do not intervene**; staff answer questions of fact and refuse questions of judgement.
- **Consolidation comes after the assessed work**, so nobody performs while holding a framework.

Phase 4's fifteen minutes per student, in groups of four, is the binding constraint on the day and is the reason there are two assessors rather than three. The [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s open question about staffing is live here in its sharpest form: this occasion requires two assessors, one user and one accountable owner in the room simultaneously, per group of four, for an hour.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Committing a decision before re-reading your own evidence,* item 1.
- *Writing the strongest argument against yourself before the artefact,* item 2, and again inside it at item 8.
- *An artefact whose conditions require named verifiers,* item 1.
- *Stating who owns the failures you cannot detect, including when the answer is nobody,* item 5.
- *A user's question that cannot be answered in technical terms,* phase 4.
- *An accountable owner asking what they are being asked to accept,* phase 4.
- *Stating what you did not check, in a document that will be judged,* item 9.
- *Perturbation in EA-1's form,* phase 4.
- *A five-month-old record of what you failed to anticipate, returned with a demand that you say what changed,* phase 5.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *A second assessor pair or a second occasion.* EA-1 requires four occasions and three assessors; this is one and two, and the entrustment statement says so. Multiple-occasion discipline is ME390's.
- *Bad-faith parties.* Every person in phase 4 is acting in good faith, as at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md). A hostile decision-owner is a different skill and is not in the programme.
- *The written form.* Today's decision artefact is written but is defended orally within hours. A record that outlives the conversation and cannot be repaired by the next sentence is [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md)'s and [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md)'s.
- *Choosing between competing changes under a fixed budget.* [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md)'s, five days earlier, deliberately separated so that today's decision is about one change's risk rather than about allocation.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The nine-item decision artefact is **safe as the form of a release decision on a system you operate, whose users you can name, and where you or a colleague hold the reversal path.** It is **unsafe as the form of a release decision whose consequences are irreversible or whose affected population you cannot enumerate** — a change that writes data that cannot be un-written, a change to a system used by people you have no channel to, a change in a domain where the failure injures somebody. Item 6 assumes a reversal path exists; item 7 assumes there is a user who can be told; item 5 assumes there is somebody who could own an undetectable failure. **In an irreversible or unenumerable case the artefact's structure will produce a confident-looking decision on three false premises**, and the correct move is to say that this form does not apply and to escalate under [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 4. The substrate's domain constraint — failure is inconvenient rather than harmful — is what makes the form safe here and is not a property the students' future systems will share. This is stated to students at phase 6.

**Item 9's own scope, in the same form.** The tooling position is **safe wherever the student can enumerate what produced their evidence**, which on this platform they can, because the settings are documented, the sampling rules are named, and the judge's silence is a matter of record. It is **unsafe as a general audit form**: an evidence chain assembled from several tools, several people and several years — which is the ordinary condition of any system older than the person operating it — will not yield three clean lists, and a student who produces three anyway has invented them. **The honest output in that case is the boundary itself: here is where I stopped being able to establish provenance.** The day states this with the item rather than in this paper, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s widening, and item 9's third list — what you are relying on without having checked — is where a student applying the form outside its range would fail, because an empty third list on a four-day chain is not credible and the assessors are instructed to say so.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **the accountable owner's question at phase 4**: *what are you asking me to accept that you cannot detect?*

Every student has an answer to it in their artefact at item 5, and for a large share of the cohort that answer will be *nobody owns this*. Writing that in a document at phase 3 is uncomfortable. Saying it to the person who will carry it, who is in the room because the substrate is real, is a different act — and it is the act the module has been building toward since [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) step 3 asked students to decide what they would not observe.

The second mechanism is item 2 against item 8. The student writes the strongest argument against their own position at phase 1, before re-reading anything, and again inside the artefact after sixty minutes with four days of their own evidence. Phase 8 shows them both. **A student whose item 8 is weaker than their item 1 built a case against themselves designed to be beaten**, and they can see it without being told, which is why the assessors' narrative names it rather than penalising it.

The third is phase 6 point 2: the cohort's ship, do-not-ship and conditional split shown unattributed, with what distinguished the groups. The design's claim — which is a prediction and could be wrong — is that the split will not track the quality of the evidence and will track the detection design.

Autonomy is complete: the decision is the student's, and both directions are passes. Competence is supported by the fact that nothing is built and every input is theirs. Relatedness is supplied by the user and the accountable owner, both of whom are affected by the answer.

The competence risk is at phase 5 part two, where a student reads their own five-month-old list of failures they did not anticipate. [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §3.3 described that as uncomfortable in a way nothing else in the programme is, and returning it a second time with a demand attached could read as a reproach. **The framing is set before phase 5: the requirement is to have used the information, nothing about it is assessed, and a student whose list is long has more to say at this phase rather than less.**

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items, none recorded. Item 2 is the [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) A4 mechanism applied before the artefact, and its pairing with item 8 is the day's cheapest instrument. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction and is asymmetric by construction**: what would an agent say, and what could it not say. It is placed on an assessment-bearing day because the frontier map's most valuable datum is the one taken when the stakes are real, and it is safe there because phase 1 is never recorded.

**Phase 2 (30 min).** Seven retrievals, two module-boundary items, and a bridge that names what a class-5 occasion has that a class-4 one does not.

**Phase 3 (60 min).** Nine artefact items, nothing built. Items 4 and 5 are the [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) and [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) rule-6 elements and item 2's cost clause is [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md)'s; item 1's verifier clause is a fail condition; item 8 is the A4 mechanism's first use in the design. **Item 9 is the practical turn's only addition to the assessed artefact**, is composed of four elements rehearsed at S066 to S069, and carries its own scope boundary.

**Phase 4 (60 min).** Level formed from the artefact first, per rule 7. Four questioners, of whom two are not assessors. The perturbation set is EA-1's. **One published question is new — which part of this did you check yourself — and it is a question about evidence rather than about tools.**

**Phase 5 (35 min).** Levels sealed first. Three carry-forward comparisons — two discharging [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §8 and one discharging [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) — none assessed.

**Phase 6 (35 min).** Eight points, assessors absent. Point 5 restates not-shipping-as-a-result at the class where it costs something. Point 6 names two forward occasions with their instruments. **Points 7 and 8 are the module's two required platform points, delivered here as a single seven-item list of what was decided for the room across five days, and placed after the assessed phases so that nothing taught on the day can be performed back at the people judging it.**

**Phase 7 (40 min).** A staff member's reversed change, with their prior failure-mode statement read as written rather than summarised. **Then the module's fifth and last agent case, and the only one whose failure is an omission rather than a defect.**

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 1 against the artefact; item 2 against item 8; predicted against awarded level; **item 5's frontier result; the three item 9 cohort counts**; the three cohort figures from S050, S066 and S069; assessor narratives with divergences; the psychological-safety series.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has shipped professionally will produce a fluent artefact and a confident defence. Predicted failure: **fluency at item 5**, where the honest answer is often *nobody owns this* and a practised engineer will supply a plausible owner instead, because naming an owner is what they have always done. Countermeasure: the accountable owner is in the room at phase 4 and is briefed to ask whether they were consulted. A named owner who has not been asked is worse than an honest *nobody*, and the assessors are instructed to treat it as such. **A second predicted failure is at item 9**, where the same student will produce a short third list because *checking things* is what they do; the countermeasure is the defence question, which asks what would have to be true for the unchecked part to be wrong and which a short list makes harder rather than easier to answer.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is paralysis at phase 3: four days of evidence, none of it sufficient, and a decision required in sixty minutes. Countermeasures: both directions are declared passes before phase 1; the artefact form is supplied; the eight items are each answerable from a specific prior day's output; and staff answer questions of fact throughout.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** A student for whom the decision is obvious — the change is plainly safe, or plainly not — has a real situation and not a defective one. The difficulty is relocated to phase 4's perturbation: *what if the interval were twice as wide; what if the affected population were the one that cannot complain; what if the gap you attributed to population was the criterion.* The third is the module's own material turned on the student and has no prepared answer. Fallback if a student handles all three: ask what they would need to see, in the first week after release, to conclude they had been lucky rather than right.

**Affective failure.** Two, and they are opposite.

The first is **relief-driven overconfidence** in students who ship and are not immediately contradicted. Countermeasure: point 6 names [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), where the shipped changes' behaviour is reported against today's detection commitments, so nobody leaves believing the decision has been settled.

The second is the module's characteristic risk and the more serious: **a student who does not ship, is passed, and privately reads the pass as a courtesy.** A cohort that believes not-shipping is tolerated rather than correct will ship next time. Countermeasures: the failure conditions are symmetric and are stated before phase 1; phase 6 point 2 shows the split without names, so nobody's decision is singular; phase 6 point 5 restates the position at the class where it costs; [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s eleven-week case has already established that a wrong block is a real harm rather than a safe default, so the room does not read not-shipping as costless either; and the instructor states once that the programme has awarded its highest levels on this occasion to students in both directions.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) (where the shipped changes' behaviour is reported against the detection commitments made here), [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) (forensics, where a decision record made in advance is what a blameless review reads), [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md) (scope negotiation at class 6, whose material is these decision records), [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) (the safety case), [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) (residual risk stated plainly, which is item 3 written for a receiving organisation) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md).

**S079 at roughly one quarter is the consequential retrieval** and is the first occasion in the programme at which a student's own detection commitment is checked against what actually happened. The link is instrumented: the phase 3 item 4 detection-commitment quality is the instrument, and S079's phase 3 must carry a case in which the signal itself stopped.

**S085 at roughly two quarters is where the decision becomes a negotiation.** Today the student decides and defends; at S085 the decision is contested by someone with a competing claim on the same capacity, and the artefact written today is the position they are negotiating from.

Retained content is procedural and short: the eight-item form; the condition-with-a-verifier rule; the accepted-failure triple of party, frequency and detectability; the case-against-your-own-decision demand; and not shipping as a result. Five items.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 4, and at S079.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**The quarter's second entrustment judgement, and the first rehearsal of EA-1 on a live system with real users.** Expected 2b to 3a; 3b available and rare.

Level formed from the artefact before the defence, per rater discipline rule 7. Narrative substantiation mandatory and required to cite specific moments from both the artefact and the defence.

Named failure conditions: a ship with no stated failure modes; a decision whose accepted failures have neither a detection commitment nor a named owner nor a statement that both are absent; a conditional ship with no named verifier, assessed as unconditional; **a decision reporting a judged score as evidence of quality with no statement of what its judge has been shown to do**; and — as a narrative finding rather than a level condition — an item 8 counter-argument weaker than the student's own item 1. **A defended decision not to ship is a pass at the same levels as a ship.**

**The fourth condition is new with the practical turn and is stated in durable terms deliberately.** It does not name a vendor, a service or a documentation state; it says that a score reported as quality evidence must arrive with what is known about its judge. On this platform the answer is *nothing is published*, which is a complete answer and is expected to be the common one. **Naming the uncalibration and shipping anyway is a pass**; the condition bites only on silence, and it is [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s standard applied to the evidence the student actually holds rather than a new demand.

Per rule 1, the checked elements are the decision with its conditions and verifiers, the evidence position with the gap's attribution and distinguishing evidence, three failure modes with party, frequency and detectability, the four-part detection commitment including its own failure case, an owner or an explicit none per undetectable failure, the reversal path with its cost, the user-facing sentence, the case against with the student's answer, **the tooling position — three lists, the decided-against-accepted settings statement, and the statement of what the gate's judge has been shown to do**, and the phase 5 carry-forward paragraph. **Nothing else is assessed** — not whether the change shipped, not the quality of the change, and **not the four days' prompt, intervention or frontier records**, which are in front of the assessors as context and are the material item 9 is written from.

**Phase 5's results are not assessed**, and the specification says so in writing. [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §7's argument is accepted unchanged: an assessed prediction check is a strategy, and a student marked down for a large category 3 will state every conceivable failure next time, which drives the number to zero and destroys the quantity.

### Discharge of the two concessions WP-050 §8 made and named this day for

**This paragraph is the report [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §8 requires, and both concessions are discharged.**

**Concession one — no evidence that constructed pressure predicts real pressure.** WP-050 §8 objection 1 conceded, unqualified, that the design had no evidence that behaviour under constructed pressure predicts behaviour under real pressure; that S070 puts each student in front of a real release decision with a real audience; that comparing each student's S050 level against their S070 conduct would test the day's central assumption directly; and that **"it requires one field in the S070 record."** **The field exists.** It is written at phase 5 part one, by the two assessors, **after today's level has been recorded and sealed**, so that the comparison cannot contaminate the judgement it is testing. It has two parts: the whole-occasion comparison WP-050 asked for, and a comparison scoped to the part of today that is S050's own activity — the answer to the user's question at phase 4 and the item 7 user-facing sentence. Individual results are returned privately; the cohort aggregate and its direction are reported at phase 8. §8 objection 1 of this paper argues that the second part, which is the only part capable of answering the concession, is under-dosed.

**Concession two — a prediction check with no occasion consuming it.** WP-050 §8 objection 2 conceded that category 3 — failures that occurred on a student's deployed model and were not stated in advance — was measured, reported and dropped, and proposed carrying it forward "to their next failure-mode statement, at S066 or S070, and require them to say what they have changed," noting that this "costs one artefact element and preserves the decoupling entirely, since the requirement is to have used the information rather than to have scored well on it." **The element exists at both named days.** It was built at [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md) phase 5 step 6 as a checked artefact element, and it is confirmed here at phase 5 part two, where the student states what they changed and points at the item of today's artefact where the change is visible. **The decoupling is preserved exactly as WP-050 specified**: presence is checked, content is not assessed, and the specification says so in writing.

**Rule-6 discharge.** Seven obligations land on this day and **all seven elements exist.**

- **[WP-066](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-066.md) §7** named this day for the step 6 category-3 recatch rate. **That is phase 5 part two; the cohort rate is reported at phase 8.**
- **[WP-068](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-068.md) §7** named this day for the phase 7 signal-death latency, specifying that the decision artefact's detection commitment state what happens if the signal itself fails. **That is artefact item 4's final clause and it is a checked element.**
- **[WP-069](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §7** named this day for the unowned-entry count and the step 7 refusal rate. **The first is artefact item 5, reported at phase 8; the second is the last published question of the defence.**
- **[WP-060](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-060.md) §7** named this day's phase 3 for its ship / conditional / decline split against awarded levels, asking whether declining there predicts anything about conduct here. **That is phase 5 part three**, written by the assessors into the same sealed-after-level field as the S050 comparison, costing the student no phase time, and reported at phase 8 as a transition table. It is not assessed, for the same reason parts one and two are not: it is a measurement of the programme rather than of the student.
- **[WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §7** named this day for its step 6 commitment shape and break-even, specifying that the cost position defended in the release decision carry that commitment together with the realised spend of the billing period closing in week 68. **That is artefact item 2's cost clause**, it is a checked element with the forecast and its confidence required in the same field per specification rule 2, and it is the subject of a published defence question. This is the module's only inbound link from M13 and it is the mechanism by which a commitment made in week 64 against a forecast becomes, in week 70, a number the student cannot argue with.
- **[S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md)** named this day for two instruments and **both elements exist.** The first — the supervision-level distribution and the phase 4 least-defensible-number responses, measuring whether the cohort can hold a cost position under pressure from a party with an interest — required that the same students defend a cost position in front of a real audience with the two distributions reported together. **That is artefact item 2 defended at phase 4 in front of a real user and the accountable owner, with the two distributions reported side by side at phase 8.** The second — the phase 5 category-3 composition — required that the cost position state which category-3 term is now accounted for and which is not. **That is artefact item 2's final clause and it is a checked element.** Per instrumentation rule 2 the S065 and S070 distributions are reported and **not** compared, since the elicitation is shared and a difference between them would measure familiarity with the protocol as much as any change in the students.

**A proposed countermeasure has its first use here.** [WP-069](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §7 recorded A4's mechanism — requiring the person who wants the change to state the case against it — as a **proposed** remedy under rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8. Artefact item 8 is its first use, and item 2 is a second administration of the same demand before any evidence is re-read. Any later reuse is contingent on this day's reported outcome.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments, both responding outside this module. The **S050-against-S070 comparison** responds at [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md), whose entrustment statement must state whether constructed-pressure levels are treated as predictive on this evidence, and what it does differently if they are not. The **item 4 detection-commitment quality** responds at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) phase 3, whose alerting cases must include one in which the signal itself stopped, selected against whichever monitor failure mode this cohort omitted. **WP-079 §7 and WP-085 §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

Their readers are **the following year's cohort, through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md)**, and **[S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md)**, where the same question is asked at the final and where a bounded portion of the assessed work is performed without agent assistance, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s dependency measure. **Per instrumentation rule 3 the sampling is disclosed to students**: one cohort, one occasion, under assessment conditions — a different sampling frame from every other frontier datum in the programme, and the difference is why it is worth taking.

ABET and CS2023 contribution: PO2 and PO7. ## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the field built to answer WP-050's first concession is under-dosed in the only form that could answer it.**

At its strongest: WP-050 asked whether constructed-pressure conduct predicts real-pressure conduct. The whole-occasion comparison cannot answer that, because S050 assessed a communication activity — state what an evaluation does not license, to a party who wants it to license more — and this day assesses a decision activity. **A student who is 2b at S050 and 3a here has not been shown to transfer anything; they have been shown to be better at deciding than at communicating**, which is a fact about them and not about pressure. The design recognised this and added the scoped comparison: S050's activity against the same activity today, at the user's question and the user-facing sentence.

The scoped comparison is the right instrument and it is under-dosed. **At S050 the student performed the activity four times, over sixty minutes, with trained role-players calibrated across the cohort. Here they perform it once, for perhaps two minutes, inside a defence about something else, with a user who is a real person and therefore not calibrated at all.** Two minutes against sixty is not a comparison of the same capability under two pressures; it is a comparison of a rehearsed performance against an incidental one, and a divergence is at least as likely to be a dose effect as a pressure effect.

The fix is cheap and is declined. **Ten minutes of dedicated time with the user, outside the defence, on the same task S050 set — say what your evidence does not license, to someone who wants it to license more — would make the comparison interpretable**, and ten minutes is three percent of a three-hundred-and-five-minute day. It is declined because it would add a ninth phase to an assessment-bearing day already at the outer limit of the pattern's budget, and because the extra ten minutes come out of phase 4, which is the assessed occasion. **That is a real constraint and the honest position is that the design has chosen a cheaper answer to WP-050's concession than the concession deserved**, and has recorded the field as discharging it. The discharge is genuine — the field exists and WP-050 asked for a field — and the inference it supports is weaker than WP-050 expected.

**Objection 2: the day cannot be both a real occasion and a rehearsal, and it tells students it is both.**

At its strongest: the entrustment statement says *this is a rehearsal, and it is named as one* and, four lines later, *the users are real and the change is real*. Both cannot be fully true. **The substrate's users are not rehearsing.** If the consequence is genuine, calling the occasion a rehearsal is a comfort offered to the student at the users' expense. If the consequence is managed — bounded traffic share, staff on-call escalation, a guaranteed reversal path, a domain constraint that failure is inconvenient rather than harmful — then the pressure is bounded, the occasion is closer to [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s constructed pressure than the day admits, and objection 1's comparison is between two constructed occasions differing in degree.

The second reading is the correct one and the design knows it: every one of those bounds is real and is described on the [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page.

**The students are not told the bounds at this occasion.** They know the escalation layer exists, because that page requires it to be disclosed. They are not told, in the entrustment statement, what share of traffic their change will touch, or that the reversal path is guaranteed by staff regardless of their design, or that the domain constraint caps the harm. The fix is one paragraph in the entrustment statement.

It is declined, and the reason is the objection. **Telling students the blast radius is capped is precisely what would convert the occasion into an exercise**, and the day's whole claim to differ from [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) rests on the student experiencing the consequence as unbounded. So the design withholds a material fact from the student in order to preserve the pressure that makes the assessment valid.

**That is the same structure this programme repaired for its users and has not repaired for its students.** [WP-035](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-035.md) §8 found that the substrate's users were being told about maintenance and not about deliberate disturbance, established that proportionality is not consent, and required a disclosure. The argument transfers without modification: a student told that a decision is consequential, and not told that its consequence is capped, has been told the true thing that supports the design's purpose and not the true thing that would weaken it. **This objection is not answered.** The design does not know how to have both an honest entrustment statement and an unbounded-feeling decision, it has chosen the second, and it has not written the choice down anywhere a student could find it. Recording it here is not a remedy; it is the minimum.

**Objection 3: item 9 was introduced as an assessed element on an occasion where nothing may be introduced.**

At its strongest: the practical turn added a ninth artefact item and made it a checked element with a named failure condition attached. **A student meets it for the first time at an entrustment occasion.** The programme's rater discipline forms the level from the artefact before the defence, which means an item the student has never written is being read as evidence about their judgement rather than about their familiarity with a form. That is the same defect [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §7 identified in a different register — an instrument that measures acquaintance and is reported as measuring capability — and it is worse here because the occasion carries a level.

The partial rebuttal is that item 9 is not new content. Its four components were each produced and assessed in the preceding four days: the decided-against-accepted settings at [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md) step 5, the sampling rules at [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) step 4, the row reconciliation at [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) step 7, and the enforced-against-agreed distinction at [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) step 8. The parts are rehearsed four times over.

**The synthesis is not, and that is conceded. The fix costs five minutes and is made.** [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s next-morning retrieval now carries a five-minute item requiring the student to write the tooling position for their own arrangement — which is the same composition, in the same form, without stakes, one week before the occasion. It is placed in the between-day protocol rather than in a phase because every phase in this module is full, and it is placed at S069 rather than earlier because the composition requires all four parts to exist.

**What is not fixed is that the fix was found by writing this section.** The element was specified, its failure condition written, and its scope boundary drafted before anybody asked where it had been rehearsed. That is the pattern [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 named — phase 5 and artefact elements are written as tasks and are only later claimed as measurements — arriving one more time, at the design's most consequential occasion, and it is recorded rather than tidied away.

**Objection 4: the practical turn changed how expensive the assessed evidence is to produce, and the design has not asked what that does to the judgement.**

This is the objection the paper cannot answer and it is the most important one in the module.

At its strongest: the entrustment apparatus assumes an artefact is a proxy for a capability. That assumption was always doing work, and it was doing it under a stable relationship — producing a validated gate, a measured gap, a detection design and a governance arrangement took a student four days of effort proportional to their understanding. **Copilot in agent mode changes the proportion, and it does not change it uniformly.** A student who understands the material can now produce more of it; so can a student who does not. The artefact's *length*, its *polish*, its *coverage* and its *internal consistency* have all become cheaper, and every one of those is something an assessor forming a level from an artefact will read.

The design's answers are three and none is sufficient. **The judgement is on the decision, not on the artefact's production**, and the decision — ship, do not ship, under conditions — is not something an agent can supply, because it requires being answerable. **Item 9 exists precisely to surface the production**, and a student relying on unchecked work has to say so in the document the level is formed from. And **the defence perturbs**: the third perturbation question turns the module's own material on the student's attribution, and no artefact answers it in advance.

**None of that measures the thing the objection names.** The programme has no comparison between an artefact produced with agent assistance and one produced without, at this level, on this task. It has three such comparisons in total — [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md), [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) — and none of them is a class-5 release decision, and the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) records that whether three occasions is enough is unknown. **So the design has changed the production conditions of its own highest-stakes evidence and has no instrument pointed at the change.** The fix that would answer it — an unassisted portion of this occasion — is not available, because the decision rests on four days of assisted work and removing the assistance from the last hour would measure nothing. **This objection is not answered, no cheap fix exists, and it is recorded here as the practical turn's largest unpaid cost.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** More than a quarter of students will change their decision between item 1 and the artefact, and the modal direction will be from ship to conditional rather than from ship to no-ship. *Instrument:* item 1 against artefact item 1, coded for direction. **The modal direction is the claim**: a conditional is the move a student makes when re-reading their evidence has made them uncomfortable and the artefact still has to be written.

**Prediction 2.** More than half of artefact item 8 counter-arguments will be weaker than the same student's item 2, written before any evidence was re-read. *Instrument:* item 2 and item 8 paired and rated blind by the assessors against each other, not against a standard. **If this holds, the A4 mechanism does not survive being applied to oneself**, and [WP-069](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md)'s proposed countermeasure is retracted for every contingent reuse under rule 5.

**Prediction 3.** The cohort's ship, no-ship and conditional split will not correlate with the strength of the students' evidence positions and will correlate with the quality of their detection designs. *Instrument:* decisions coded against independent ratings of artefact items 2 and 4. **If the split tracks the evidence instead, phase 6 point 2 is wrong** and the module has taught that a release decision is a quality decision after all.

**Prediction 4.** In the scoped S050 comparison, real-pressure conduct will diverge downward from constructed-pressure level for a majority of students. *Instrument:* the phase 5 part-two field, aggregated by direction. **This is the prediction the programme most needs to be wrong about**, because a systematic downward divergence means every level awarded on a constructed occasion in this programme is an overestimate — and §8 objection 1 states that the instrument is too weak to distinguish that finding from a dose effect, which is why the result is reported with its dose and not as a transfer estimate.

**Prediction 5.** At item 9, fewer than half of students will state that their gate's judge has no published agreement with human judgement, despite the fact being taught at [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md) point 10, restated at that day's phase 8, and retrievable in one line. *Instrument:* item 9 second sentences, coded for whether the uncalibration is named. **If the rate is high, the module's most-repeated fact has landed and the fourth failure condition is redundant; if it is low, the design has evidence that a fact taught four times and never required in an artefact does not survive to the occasion that needs it** — which would be the strongest single result in this module and would bear directly on instrumentation rule 7.

**Prediction 6.** More than a quarter of item 9 third lists — what the student is relying on without having checked — will be empty, and empty third lists will be concentrated among students whose artefacts are longest. *Instrument:* item 9 third lists coded for emptiness, against artefact length and against the four-day intervention-record counts. **The correlation is the claim.** An empty third list on a four-day agent-assisted chain is not credible, and if it correlates with length rather than with intervention count then the artefact's polish is being produced by the tool and read as diligence — which is §8 objection 4 becoming measurable in the one place the design can currently see it.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That four days of good work will not add up to a sufficient case for releasing anything, that the insufficiency is structural rather than your shortfall, and that the response being assessed is the one nobody has taught you: decide what you are willing to be wrong about, say so to the people it will happen to, and state how you will find out.

**What the evidence licenses.** The entrustment apparatus — ordinal levels, narrative substantiation, artefact before defence — is **class 1**, verified here. The platform is inherited and **class 4**: documented defaults, preview capabilities, and the silence the day's fourth failure condition turns on — **no agreement with human judgement is published for any evaluator in the catalogue**. The nine-item artefact and the symmetric treatment of ship and do-not-ship are **class 3**, untested.

**What to do with it.**

- **A condition with no named verifier is a ship with a sentence in front of it.**
- **Sort item 9's three lists by the five classes of conclusion** — see the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). The third list, what you are relying on unchecked, is the one nobody reaches by working harder at the first two; an empty one is not credible.
- **Naming a judge as uncalibrated and shipping anyway is a defensible decision.** The condition bites on silence.
- **Write *nobody owns this* when it is true.** A plausible owner who was never asked is worse than an honest none.

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes, and does not answer, that the day tells you the occasion is both real and a rehearsal while withholding the bounds that would let you judge which — traffic share, guaranteed reversal path, capped harm — because telling you would remove the pressure the assessment depends on. **Hold the design to that.** It is the structure the programme repaired for its users after finding that proportionality is not consent, and has not repaired for you.
