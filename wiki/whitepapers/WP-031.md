## In plain terms

A spike is not a rough first attempt at the system. It is an instrument pointed at one uncertainty, and everything that distinguishes it from a rough first attempt is written down before the work starts.

Six things go in that writing: the question, the decision waiting on it, the bound, what evidence would count, where the code will live and what happens to it, and what the spike will not decide. **None of the six can be reconstructed afterwards.** A question written after the answer is a description; evidence named after it is found is a rationalisation; and a disposal rule written at the end of a hard week, next to something that works, will not be written at all.

The two hard rules come from the openly published playbook and both are prohibitions rather than techniques. **The goal of a spike is fact-finding, not decision-making or recommendation.** And **the code written for a spike is not going to serve as the code for the final solution.** Together they stop a spike becoming an unreviewed prototype in production, which is the failure that makes the whole instrument counterproductive.

The session's real difficulty is that the tooling has made the distinction invisible from the outside. An investigation now routinely produces something that runs, looks finished and could be demonstrated tomorrow. **The difference between that and the beginning of a system is entirely in the charter**, which is why the charter is the assessed artefact and the code is not.

The platform workflow exists to make that difficulty physical rather than described. The learner runs the spike in agent mode on a branch named for its own deletion, and ends the session holding a written finding and no code. Two things are meant to be noticed while that happens. The first is how uncomfortable the deletion is, which is the point. **The second is that the tool's own safety confirmation offers to remove itself permanently** — a reasonable ergonomic choice for an instrument used continuously, and a demonstration that any boundary implemented as a dialog is a boundary implemented as a preference. A disposal rule cannot rest on one.

## Abstract

This paper defends the spike as a chartered instrument rather than an exploratory activity, and locates the whole of its discipline in a document written beforehand. The argument is that a feasibility investigation's value depends on properties — a falsifiable question, a declared bound, pre-specified evidence and a disposal rule — none of which can be established retrospectively, and that generative tooling has removed the physical friction that previously enforced the last of them. The session installs a six-part charter, a bound with a stop rule in both directions, and a peer review that tests whether the charter can distinguish an investigation from the start of a system.

Three literatures do the theoretical work and they are not interchangeable. **Error-management training** supplies the account of why a deliberately disposable investigation teaches anything, and also supplies the boundary condition that constrains which questions may be spiked at all: the task has to answer back. **The planning literature** supplies the division of labour between a generative instrument and an external check, which is the architecture a charter implements. **The measurement literature** supplies the requirement that the analysis precede the run, without which *the investigation found no problem* and *the investigation could not have found this problem* are the same sentence.

The paper concedes that the disposal rule is a prohibition with no measurement behind it; that the playbook supplying the protocol offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices; that the corpus contains a flagship anecdote which the design cannot distinguish from the failure it warns against; and that the error-management warrant is about learning rather than about delivery, from a study pool in which no outcome was measured on the job.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A design has an uncertainty in it and the team knows which one. The available responses are to start building, to argue about it, or to investigate.

Investigation is obviously right and is nonetheless the least stable of the three, because an investigation has no natural boundary. It ends when the person doing it feels finished, it produces whatever it produces, and its output is reported in whatever form the reporter chooses — which is very often a recommendation, because a recommendation is what the room asked for.

**Each of those three degradations is separately fatal and they compound.** An unbounded investigation consumes the schedule the proof was supposed to protect. An investigation reported as a recommendation absorbs its own evidence, so that a reader who disagrees with the conclusion cannot use the observations. And an investigation whose code is retained converts a cheap enquiry into an expensive foundation that nobody reviewed.

The session's response is to move all three decisions to a point before the work, where they can be made cheaply and checked by somebody else.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) is required, and the requirement is structural rather than conventional: a spike's charter must name the decision waiting on it, and until a design exists at the scope of its decision there is no such decision. A learner who spikes before framing produces a competent investigation of something nobody was going to act on, which §5 records as the second-commonest failure.

[S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) is also assumed, for the stopping rule. The prior it works against is that a technical investigation is somehow exempt from the discipline a discovery is held to — that *we will know when we have it* is an acceptable bound for a spike, though it was rejected for a discovery ten sessions earlier. **The two are the same rule and the session says so.**

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate. The charter's six parts are individually simple. The load is evaluative rather than intrinsic: several of the parts require a judgement about what the answer might be, made before there is one, and learners find that uncomfortable rather than difficult.

**3.2 Why pre-specification is not merely tidy.** A question, a bound and an evidence standard written after the investigation are all consistent with any outcome, because they were selected in the presence of it. Written before, each of them can fail. The mechanism is the same one behind [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md)'s stopping rule and behind the falsifiability requirement at [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md), and this session is its third application rather than a new claim.

The measurement literature states the same discipline in a setting where it has been formalised. An evaluation is an experiment; the analysis belongs before the run; and doing it beforehand is what turns *we tested and found nothing* into a statement about the thing rather than about the test ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). **The transferable form for a spike is that an investigation which could not have produced a no has not investigated anything, and that nothing in the finished document reveals which kind it was.** That source is analytical rather than empirical, its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions, and it addresses comparing scores rather than choosing what to score — so it warrants the discipline and not any threshold. **No number from it appears in this paper or in the session.**

**3.3 Why the bound needs a stop rule in both directions.** A bound that only stops on success is a target with a deadline attached, and it will be extended. A bound that stops on exhaustion produces a second finding — that the question is more expensive than it looked — and that finding is frequently more valuable than the answer, because it changes the design rather than confirming it.

**3.4 Why disposal is enforced by location rather than by intention.** The rule survives if the code lives somewhere that would have to be deliberately moved, and does not survive if it lives where it could simply be merged. **This is a claim about mechanism design, not about character**, and it is the only form of the rule this paper is prepared to defend.

The vendor documentation supplies an unusually clean instance of the same principle operating against the design. Agent mode requests confirmation before running a terminal command or a tool that is not built in, and that confirmation is **a dismissible default rather than a boundary**, auto-confirmable per session, per solution or permanently ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md), read 2026-07-30). **A control that offers to remove itself is a preference**, and the session makes the learner exercise that option deliberately so the general point lands on a specific artefact. The documentation is first-party, highly perishable, and was read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches.

**3.5 Why a disposable investigation is a training design and not only a hygiene rule.**

This is the section's substantive addition and it comes from a workplace literature rather than a schooling one. Error management training "involves active exploration as well as explicit encouragement for learners to make errors during training and to learn from them", distinguished by two elements: minimal guidance, and explicit instructions that errors carry information ([research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md)). **A chartered spike whose code is discarded is that arrangement with the last incentive to protect the artefact removed.**

Three of that meta-analysis's results bear on this design and they do not all support it.

The first does. **The advantage is absent during training and present afterwards.** Studies measuring performance within the training itself found no advantage; the advantage appeared on separate post-training transfer tasks, and was largest for **adaptive transfer** — "using one's existing knowledge base to change a learned procedure, or to generate a solution to a completely new problem". This is the strongest available warrant for a module that never marks the spike's code and marks only what the learner can produce afterwards, and it is also a warning about learner satisfaction: **a session designed on this principle will feel less productive than one that is not.**

The second constrains it. Because the method withholds guidance, **"the task itself needs to provide clearly interpretable and informative feedback if EMT is to be effective"**, and where feedback cannot be readily interpreted the method may be worse than instruction that supplies external guidance. **The session converts this into a charter test**: a spike is only worth running on a question whose result the learner will be able to read. Exercise 4 is that test and is the only place in the module where the boundary condition is exercised directly.

The third undercuts both, and the paper states it rather than burying it. **The authors describe this moderator as receiving "only limited support"**, its coding was the least reliable of the four they tested, the overall homogeneity test was not significant so the moderators were theory-driven partitions of a pool that did not statistically require partitioning, **every comparison in the pool was against another training method rather than against no training**, almost all of the studies taught software skills, and **no study measured on-the-job performance.** The transfer this literature establishes is transfer to a novel *task*, not to a job. **No magnitude from it is asserted here or anywhere in this wiki.**

**3.6 The ordering, and its known extrapolation.** The session generates before it instructs, and then requires an explicit comparison against the learner's own answer, which is the assembly condition rather than a decoration on it ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). That warrant comes from school, university and laboratory studies with no adult-professional study in its reference list, and the extrapolation to working professionals is conceded across this programme rather than defended here.

**Two independent traditions agreeing on the ordering is the strongest statement this paper can make about its own method**, and it is a statement about the method rather than about spikes.

**3.7 Why the generative instrument is given the code and not the conclusion.**

The planning literature supplies the architecture the charter implements. Language models are best understood as **"universal approximate knowledge sources"** — broad coverage, no correctness guarantee — and the same position paper argues that auto-regressive models cannot by themselves plan or self-verify, since self-verification "is after all a form of reasoning" ([research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md)). The proposed arrangement pairs a generator with an **external verifier** in a two-way critique loop, with correctness coming from the verifier and flexibility from the generator.

**A spike charter is that arrangement written down.** The agent produces candidate structure; the charter's pre-declared evidence clause is the verifier; and the reason the evidence clause must precede the run is that a verifier chosen after the output is not external to it. This also explains why the session's adversarial step is pointed at a specific target — the inputs the run did not cover, and the sentences in the evidence section that are actually conclusions — rather than at the output in general. **Asking the instrument to check itself is the move the source says does not work.** It is a position paper rather than an experiment, its claims are contested in the subsequent literature, and it addresses auto-regressive models as of early 2024.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**The opening items are the instrument and item 3 is the one the design cares about.** Asked what happens to the code, most learners say it is kept, and they say it without hesitation because the alternative sounds wasteful. The gap between that answer and the playbook's flat statement — that spike code is not going to serve as the final solution's code — is the session's actual content, and it is much more effective as a surprise than as an instruction.

**The charter is assessed and the code is not**, throughout the module. This is a deliberate inversion of what learners expect and it is defensible on one narrow ground: the code is the part that does not survive, and a curriculum that marked it would be teaching that it does.

**The review movement removes the disposal line and asks a peer to classify the charter.** If the other five parts cannot distinguish a spike from the beginning of a system, the charter is decorative — which matters because the charter is the only part of a spike that outlives it.

**The corpus's contradiction is handed over rather than resolved.** It states, as a headline rule, that the tooling is for making each step better and not for skipping steps; its customer-facing material's proudest anecdote is a prototype generated on day one from a reusable starting point with no discovery activity described at all. **Neither document notices.** The session presents both and asks the learner what charter, if any, would have made the second one legitimate. **There is a defensible answer and the design does not supply it.**

**4.1 What the platform workflow is for.**

Not tooling knowledge, which will be stale within the year. It exists to make one claim physically true rather than merely stated: **that a running artefact and an answered question are two different objects, and that the first is now dramatically cheaper than the second.** A learner who has generated something that works in an afternoon and then deleted it has felt the asymmetry. A learner who has only read about it has not.

Three elements of the workflow are load-bearing and each is doing something the prose cannot.

**The branch name carries the disposal.** This is §3.4's mechanism-design claim made concrete: the enforcement lives in an artefact a reviewer can see, not in an intention nobody can check. A learner who cannot name a structural enforcement in their own setup has discovered that their disposal rule is an intention, which is the intended finding of exercise 2.

**The instructions file scopes the agent away from the product.** Its `applyTo` glob and its prohibition on touching build files, CI configuration and dependency manifests are the same control at a second layer, and the requirement to observe the instruction text in the request payload before trusting it is [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s discipline at its second appearance. **An instructions file that has not been seen in a payload is a file, not a specification.**

**The confirmation dialog is used as teaching material rather than as a control.** The learner is told to exercise the auto-confirm option deliberately and record which they took. This is the only place in the module where a published property of the tooling is used to argue against relying on the tooling, and it is included because the alternative — asserting that dialogs are weak controls — is an opinion until somebody watches one offer to disable itself.

**A cost the design accepts.** This workflow requires every learner to hold agent-mode tooling, which is a licensing dependency the earlier stages avoided by using an unauthenticated documentation server. **The module records that as a real access cost and not as a detail**, and a learner without the tooling should substitute any generative assistant capable of multi-step work, with the charter and the disposal unchanged.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The spike that becomes the product.** The likeliest failure, and the one the whole session exists for. *Countermeasure:* the disposal line, its date, and the location rule — the code lives somewhere that would have to be deliberately moved. **This is partial and the paper does not overstate it.** A line in a charter does not prevent a merge under deadline pressure; [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) makes retention an assessed disclosure rather than a prohibition, precisely because a rule enforced by virtue is not enforced.

**The unactionable question.** The learner spikes something interesting. *Countermeasure:* the Monday question in the review movement — what changes if the answer is no — asked by a peer rather than by the learner.

**The recommendation smuggled into the goal.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign, and §4's separation of fact-finding from decision-making, which is reinforced as an assessed element at [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md).

**The generated foundation.** An agent produces something that runs, and running is treated as the finding. *Countermeasure:* the requirement that evidence be specified before it exists. If the charter says the evidence is a comparison over inputs the learner did not choose, an artefact that runs on a chosen input does not satisfy it.

**Refusal to bound.** A learner declines to set a limit on the grounds that the question is unpredictable. *Countermeasure:* none available in one session. The bound is revisited at [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md), where reaching it without an answer is an explicit pass.

**The unreadable question.** A learner charters a spike on something the run cannot report on — whether an output is acceptable, whether a latency is tolerable, whether a summary is good. The investigation produces an artefact and no answer, because nothing in the task tells the learner they have gone wrong. *Countermeasure:* exercise 4's sorting drill and the charter's evidence clause, which together force the question *what in the run would tell me this failed* before the bound is spent. **This is the countermeasure the theory most directly supports and the one the design added last**; it was absent from the session's first version, which is why §9's prediction 6 exists.

**The instrument used as a reviewer.** The learner asks the agent to review the spike and it approves. *Countermeasure:* step 7 points the adversarial prompt at two named targets — uncovered inputs, and evidence sentences that are actually conclusions — rather than at the output in general, because a general request for review is a request for self-verification and the planning literature says that is the move that does not work.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md), [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md), [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md), [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md) and [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md).

The charter is this module's principal export and it is retrieved in two different registers. [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md) uses its evidence clause as the model for a whole question set. [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md) uses its disposal clause, transposed onto an artefact made for a person, where letting go is harder. **The second retrieval is the one the design is least confident about** and §9 predicts against it.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. Three specification items at [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md): that the bound was declared before the work and its outcome reported; that the finding states facts rather than recommendations; and that disposal is reported as an act.

**Never assessed:** whether the answer was yes, the quality of the spike's code, or how many spikes were run.

The reason the code is never marked is not that it is bad. It is that **an assessment marks what it says matters**, and a curriculum that marked the spike's code would be teaching, in the only register learners fully believe, that the code survives. The inversion is deliberate and it is the clearest instance in this module of assessment being used as instruction rather than as measurement.

One consequence follows for [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) and is set out there rather than here: **retention is assessed as a disclosure and not prohibited.** A rule that forbids an act that is cheap, invisible and locally rational will be broken silently. A rule that requires the act to be reported, with a named reviewer and a named authority, converts it into something a supervisor can see — which is the only form of the rule that survives contact with a deadline.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: discarding working code is waste, and a curriculum that teaches it is teaching a superstition that will not survive commercial pressure.**

At its strongest: the code works. It was written by a competent engineer against a real question. Deleting it and rewriting it costs a week that nobody has, and the argument for doing so — that it was not written to production standards — is an argument for reviewing it, not for destroying it. In an environment where generating the code was the cheap part, the retained artefact is nearly free and the rewrite is the expensive one. **A rule that made sense when code was costly to produce has been left standing after its economics were removed.**

**Substantially conceded, and the concession changes the rule's form rather than its content.** The design's rebuttal is narrow: the hazard is not retention, it is *undisclosed* retention. Spike code enters production having skipped the review that production code receives, and it does so at exactly the moment when everybody is relieved. So [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) does not prohibit retention — it makes retention an assessed disclosure with a named reviewer and a named authority. **The declined fix is to drop the disposal line from the charter and rely on the disclosure alone.** It is declined because the line is what makes the disclosure noticeable; without it, retention is the default and there is nothing to report. **Whether that holds under real delivery pressure is untested and the paper does not pretend otherwise.**

**Objection 2: the design cannot distinguish its own recommended practice from the corpus's flagship anecdote, and it says so instead of solving it.**

At its strongest: a spike is a fast investigation producing a working fragment. A day-one prototype from a reusable starting point is a fast investigation producing a working fragment. The session's answer is that one has a charter and the other does not, which makes the entire distinction a piece of paperwork — and a sufficiently motivated team will write the paperwork afterwards. **A distinction that can be manufactured retrospectively is not a distinction.**

**Conceded without qualification.** The charter can be backdated and the design has no defence against a team that chooses to. What it has is the claim that the charter cannot be backdated *honestly*, because its evidence clause and its exclusion clause both become obviously convenient once the answer is known — and that a peer reading a charter can usually tell. **That is an argument about detectability, not about prevention.** The declined fix is to require an external time-stamp or a supervisor's counter-signature on every charter. It is declined because a ceremony that must be witnessed will be run for the smallest possible number of spikes, which produces fewer charters rather than more honest ones. **The reasoning is design reasoning and is not warranted by anything.**

**Objection 3: the session imports its central warrant from a literature about learning software skills in a laboratory, and applies it to engineering delivery.**

At its strongest, and it is the strongest of the three: the error-management meta-analysis this paper leans on in §3.5 is a pool of mostly laboratory studies, mostly teaching people to use software packages, mostly against university students, **in which not one study measured performance on the job.** Its comparisons are all relative to another training method rather than to no training. Its own homogeneity test did not require the moderators to be tested at all. And the specific moderator this session builds a charter test on — that the task must supply interpretable feedback — is the one the authors themselves describe as receiving only limited support, coded with the weakest reliability of the four. **A design that takes its boundary condition from the weakest result in a study whose population is unlike its own is doing exactly what this repository exists to prevent, and doing it while citing the source correctly.**

**Conceded, and the concession is the reason the boundary condition appears as an exercise rather than as a rule.** Exercise 4 asks learners to sort investigations by whether the task answers back; it does not tell them not to spike the ones that do not. The rebuttal is narrow and is about the direction rather than the finding: an investigation whose result the investigator cannot read produces no evidence regardless of what any meta-analysis says, and the literature is being used to name a mechanism the design would hold anyway. **The declined fix is to drop the error-management citation and state the boundary condition as design reasoning.** It is declined because doing so would conceal that a relevant literature exists, disagrees in part, and can be checked — and a reader who thinks this is the design's own idea cannot go and find the limits. **The cost is that a reader who weighs the population mismatch more heavily than this paper does will conclude §3.5 is decorated rather than warranted, and that reader is making a defensible call.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A large majority of learners will answer opening item 3 by saying the spike's code is kept, before instruction. *Instrument:* the written answers, coded *kept* / *discarded* / *conditional* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** In the review movement, peers will be unable to classify charter-versus-system for a substantial share of charters. *Instrument:* the peer's binary judgement against the author's declared intent, with disagreements counted.

**Prediction 3.** Charters whose bound states a stop rule in both directions will more often report a bound reached without an answer at [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) than charters whose bound states only a target. *Instrument:* the charter's bound clause, coded, against the [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) finding's reported outcome.

**Prediction 4.** The disposal discipline will transfer to [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md) less often than to [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) — learners who discarded spike code will nonetheless retain and refine their concept artefact. *Instrument:* the [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md) stripping record, coded for whether the second artefact is an amendment of the first, against the [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) disposal statement. **If this fails, the module's claim that disposal is a transferable discipline rather than a code-specific rule has support it currently lacks.**

**Prediction 5.** A majority of learners will auto-confirm the agent's tool-execution prompt for the session or for the solution during workflow step 5, and a majority of those will not record having done so unless exercise 2 requires it. *Instrument:* the exercise 2 submission against the learner's own session log, compared per learner. **This is a prediction about how quickly a control becomes invisible**, and it is the observation the whole platform anchor exists to produce.

**Prediction 6.** Charters whose evidence clause names something the run itself would produce — a failure, a count, a rejection reason — will report a settled answer at [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) more often than charters whose evidence clause names a judgement somebody would have to make. *Instrument:* the charter's evidence clause, coded *task-readable* / *judgement-dependent* by a second reader, against the [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) finding's reported outcome. **This is the only direct test of §3.5's boundary condition anywhere in this programme**, and a null result would mean the error-management moderator does not transfer to this population and §8's third objection should be upheld rather than partly rebutted.

**Prediction 7.** A substantial minority of spike code will be retained, and the retentions reported honestly at [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) will be outnumbered by retentions detectable in the learner's repository but absent from the finding. *Instrument:* the [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) disposal statement against a repository check for surviving spike paths, per learner. **This predicts the failure of §5's principal countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption. If it holds, the disclosure repair in §8's first objection is not sufficient either, and the module needs a control that does not depend on the learner reporting themselves.

**Prediction 8.** Learners will report the spike itself as the more useful half of the session and the charter as the less useful, and the ordering will reverse at [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) when only the charter is marked. *Instrument:* a two-item preference question at the close of S031 and again at the close of [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md). **This follows directly from §3.5's finding that the advantage of unguided exploration is absent during training and present afterwards**, and if it fails in the first half the session's design is not doing what this paper claims.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a spike's value lives in a document written before it, that its two prohibitions are what keep it cheap, and that the tooling has removed the friction which used to enforce the second one.

**What the evidence licenses.** A protocol, published openly by an organisation that holds itself to it, with **no comparative evidence that it beats an alternative.** Everything about outcomes is unwarranted.

**The concession worth remembering.** The disposal rule survived for decades partly because retaining spike code was inconvenient. That inconvenience is gone, and this paper's response — convert the prohibition into a disclosure — is a repair, not a proof that the original rule was right.

Recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md), **read 2026-07-30 through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches**, with several comparison tables truncated in the excerpts read. **The agent-mode page carried two product version ranges simultaneously at that reading**, and the same note records that the Agent Framework SDK is documented as formerly Semantic Kernel — both signals that product identity in this area is unstable enough that a version-pinned claim ages within a term.

**The silences that matter.** The documentation **publishes no measure of how often agent mode's self-correction loop converges, or how often it reports completion on work that is wrong** — which is precisely the property workflow step 7 depends on, since a learner asking the instrument for uncovered inputs is relying on it to be better at finding gaps than at claiming there are none. It **publishes no measure of output variability across repeated prompts**, so a learner who runs the spike twice and gets different results has no published expectation to compare against, and the module cannot tell them whether one run is a measurement. It **states no retention or telemetry position for code generated on a branch that is subsequently deleted**, so the disposal this session teaches is a disposal from the repository and this paper cannot claim it is a disposal from anywhere else — an honest limit rather than a rhetorical one, and one a learner working on customer material must resolve against their own agreements rather than against this page. And it **offers no guidance at all on the distinction this session exists to teach**: nothing in the documentation distinguishes an investigation from the beginning of a system, because from the tool's position they are the same activity. **That absence is the strongest available argument for the charter**, and it is an argument the documentation does not make.
