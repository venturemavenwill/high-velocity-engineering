## In plain terms

Constraints arrive as facts and are mostly beliefs. Somebody said the format was set by the regulator, and everybody afterwards said it too, and nobody has asked the regulator in years.

The move this session teaches is to write them all down with two extra columns: who told you, and what would settle it. Those columns do almost all the work. A constraint whose only source is a document nobody currently owns is not a constraint; it is a residue. A constraint whose test is "ask the person who told me" has not been tested.

The second half of the session is the part learners resist. Having classified honestly, some things really will be frozen, and the correct response is not to write a paragraph explaining why the engagement is difficult. **In an almost entirely frozen space there is still something that can be changed, and finding it is the job rather than a consolation.**

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching constraint classification before any design work, and defends recording the human source of each constraint rather than its rationale. The argument is that inherited constraints are the commonest silent determinant of an engagement's scope, that they are indistinguishable from real ones without a deliberate act of classification, and that recording a person makes an untested belief visible without requiring anyone to be accused of holding it. The paper concedes that the classification comes from a corpus offering no evidence for it, that the accompanying rule against treating constraint as an excuse is an exhortation, and that the session's own remedy — a named test — can be satisfied by a test the learner has no means of running.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Scope is set by constraints more often than by requirements, and constraints are received rather than established. A team that accepts a frozen list without testing it will design around an obstacle that moved two years ago, and will never find out, because a design that avoids a constraint produces no evidence that the constraint existed.

There is a second and opposite failure, and this session is one of the few places in the programme where both are handled at once. A team that classifies honestly and finds most of the space frozen may conclude that nothing can be done. **The corpus behind this programme is explicit that this conclusion is a professional failure rather than a finding**, and that judgement is the most useful thing it says about constraint.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Either**, plus the problem restatement from [S003](/wiki/seminars/S003.md) and the decision from [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md).

Learners from regulated or long-lived estates arrive with a sharp instinct for what cannot move and a weak one for testing it. Learners from newer environments arrive with the reverse and tend to classify almost everything as fluid. The register form suits both, because it does not ask for a judgement without asking for a source.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is moderate. The register is mechanically simple; the cost is in the honesty of the classification, and in the fact that this is the first session where nobody specifies the method.

**3.2 Why the source is a person.** A constraint attributed to a document can be neither challenged nor confirmed; a constraint attributed to a person can be. The column is also the least confrontational available diagnostic, because writing down who said something is not an accusation and refusing to write it is informative on its own.

**3.3 Why the method is not specified.** This is the programme's first class 2 task. What counts as sufficient evidence that a constraint has moved is exactly the judgement the session exists to develop, and specifying it would replace the judgement with a procedure. **No claim is made that unspecified method produces better learning; the design's reason is that the alternative removes the object of study.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The register has four columns and the two that matter are the ones learners would not have added: source and test.

**The review movement is the session's instrument.** A reader is handed the frozen list and reads back only the sources; every entry whose source is a document, a departed colleague or a consensus is marked untested. What remains is usually a short list, and the reduction is the finding. It is designed to happen in front of a peer rather than privately, because a learner marking their own entries untested will find reasons not to.

The supplied brief carries a purpose-built instance: a record structure that everyone attributes to a regulator and one person attributes to a superseded internal system. That case is invented for this session and it is constructed so that both classifications are defensible on the evidence available, which is the condition under which the test column becomes necessary rather than decorative.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The convenient freeze.** The most likely outcome is a frozen list that coincides with the set of conversations the learner would rather not have. *Countermeasure:* the source column, which does not detect the motive but does expose that the source is a person who has not been asked.

**The unrunnable test.** A learner writes a test that would settle the constraint and that they have no standing or access to run. *Countermeasure:* weak. The register accepts it, and [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md) supplies the mechanism for asking somebody who is not in the room. Between the two sessions the learner holds a test they cannot run, which is honest and unsatisfying.

**Classification as an exercise.** A learner produces a well-formed register and continues designing against the original assumptions. *Countermeasure:* none in this session. The register is an input to [S008](/wiki/seminars/S008.md) and [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md), where an unused reclassification becomes visible, and no earlier detector exists.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S008](/wiki/seminars/S008.md) and [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md). The fluid column is where [S008](/wiki/seminars/S008.md) looks for a simpler sufficient option; the frozen column is where [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) finds the constraints that a refusal may legitimately rest on.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence and no specification element of its own. The register is carried into [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md), where a refusal resting on a constraint the learner marked untested fails element 2 of that specification.

**The register is therefore assessed by consequence rather than directly**, which is a deliberate choice: a register produced to be marked is a register optimised for the marker.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the classification is unfalsifiable, and it flatters whoever performs it.**

At its strongest: any constraint can be argued into either column with sufficient motivation, the register produces no prediction, and the source column encourages a learner to reclassify anything inconvenient as untested. A framework that always finds something movable will always find something movable, whether or not anything is.

**Conceded in part, and the specific concession is that the classification produces no prediction.** The rebuttal is narrower: the register's value does not rest on the classification being correct but on the source and test columns being filled, which are matters of fact and can be checked. A register with honest sources and no reclassification at all is a successful outcome of this session. The declined fix was to require a minimum number of reclassifications, rejected because it would manufacture exactly the flattery the objection describes.

**Objection 2: the rule that constraint is never an excuse is a management sentiment, and it will be used against engineers who correctly report that something cannot be done.**

At its strongest: the sentence is unfalsifiable, it places the burden on the person delivering bad news, and in an organisation with any pressure in it, it becomes an instruction never to say no. That is a real harm, and this programme's own Stage 1 exists to make refusal available, so the tension is internal.

**Conceded without qualification.** The design's mitigation is that the register requires a genuinely frozen entry with its scope consequence stated, which makes reporting an immovable constraint an assessed act rather than a failure to try. **The mitigation does not resolve the tension and the tension is real.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first registers will attribute at least one frozen constraint to a document rather than to a person. *Instrument:* the source column, coded *person* / *document* / *consensus* per entry.

**Prediction 2.** The frozen list will shorten after the review movement in most cases, and the reduction will be larger for learners from long-lived estates. *Instrument:* frozen-entry counts before and after review, split by the learner's declared background.

**Prediction 3.** Learners who write a test they cannot run will more often carry the constraint unresolved into [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) than learners who write a test within their reach. *Instrument:* test feasibility coded at S006 against the constraint's status in the S010 artefact. **If this fails, the test column is recording intention rather than plan** and should require a named person able to answer.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That constraints are beliefs until somebody tests them, and that the cheapest way to make an untested belief visible is to write down who holds it.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The frozen/fluid classification is a practitioner idea recorded from a complete reading of its source, and its source offers no instance of it working.

**The concession worth remembering.** The rule that constraint is never an excuse will be quoted at engineers who are telling the truth. Knowing that in advance is most of the defence available.
