# WP-006 — Constraints You Cannot See Yet

**Seminar day:** [S006](/wiki/seminars/S006.md) · **Module:** [M02](/wiki/modules/M02.md) · **Quarter:** [Q1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 2
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Purview DSPM for AI, Agent 365 tenant readiness and the Foundry Control Plane setup tiers — the governance, licensing, consent and isolation constraints that no brief mentions and no customer volunteers

>

> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Frozen / fluid** | A classification of every element of a problem space by how far it can actually move |
> | **Source of a belief** | The person from whom a claim about a constraint was received, as distinct from the document it appears in |
> | **Class 2 task** | Task specified, method chosen by the learner. This is the first such session in the programme |
> | **Resolution of an instrument** | The scan scope, cadence and detection latency that together bound what a governance control can see |
> | **Inference laundering** | A value inferred at one stage of a pipeline, consumed as given at the next, with its provenance dropped in between |
> | **Oversharing** | Content that is over-permissioned, obsolete or ungoverned, latent until a retrieval system reads it on somebody's behalf |
> | **Data posture / workload posture** | What our information is doing inside AI systems, versus what AI systems the organisation has at all |
> | **Minimum detectable effect** | The smallest true difference an instrument of a given size and design could reliably register |

## In plain terms

Constraints arrive as facts and are mostly beliefs. Somebody said the format was set by the regulator, everybody afterwards said it too, and nobody has asked the regulator in years.

The move this session teaches is to write them all down with two extra columns: who told you, and what would settle it. Those columns do almost all the work. A constraint whose only source is a document nobody currently owns is not a constraint; it is a residue. A constraint whose test is "ask the person who told me" has not been tested.

Then the session does something the earlier version of it did not, and the addition is the reason this paper was rewritten. **It sends the learner to find a constraint that nobody in the engagement has stated, using an instrument rather than a conversation.** The customer's permissions estate is a constraint on what a retrieval system may return. Nobody withholds it. Nobody has looked, because until somebody proposed retrieval there was no reason to look — and the instant retrieval is switched on, every dormant permissions error in the organisation becomes a live one.

The second half is the part learners resist. Having classified honestly, some things really will be frozen, and the correct response is not a paragraph explaining why the engagement is difficult. **In an almost entirely frozen space there is still something that can be changed, and finding it is the job rather than a consolation.**

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching constraint classification before any design work; defends recording the human source of each constraint rather than its rationale; and defends introducing a governance instrument at this point in the programme rather than in the security stage, on the grounds that the constraints it finds bear on whether the engagement should happen at all.

The argument runs in four parts. **First**, inherited constraints are the commonest silent determinant of an engagement's scope, they are indistinguishable from real ones without a deliberate act of classification, and recording a person makes an untested belief visible without requiring anyone to be accused of holding it. **Second**, an entire class of constraint is not merely untested but unstated: the permission, classification and audit posture of the estate a proposed system would read, which no brief contains and no customer volunteers. That class is discoverable this week with tooling, and the discovery is qualification work rather than security work. **Third**, every governance instrument has a scan scope, a cadence and a detection latency, and an engineer who cannot state those three does not know what their controls cover — which makes a null result unreadable, in exactly the way an underpowered evaluation makes a null result unreadable. **Fourth**, inference laundering — a guess entering at one stage and leaving the next as a stated fact with its provenance dropped — manufactures constraints that behave identically to real ones, and it is a design pattern rather than a lapse.

The paper records an amendment made while writing it: the register schema as first designed had no field for what the instrument could not see, which meant a clean scan would have been recorded as a fluid classification rather than as an absence of observation. That is the precise failure the session exists to prevent, appearing inside the session's own artefact. The correction is documented at §4.5 rather than applied silently.

The paper concedes that the classification comes from a corpus offering no evidence for it; that the rule against treating constraint as an excuse is an exhortation that will be used against people telling the truth; that the session's remedy — a named test — can be satisfied by a test the learner has no means of running; and that placing a governance instrument in qualification risks producing learners who believe a scan has discharged an obligation it has only begun.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Scope is set by constraints more often than by requirements, and constraints are received rather than established. A team that accepts a frozen list without testing it will design around an obstacle that moved two years ago and will never find out, **because a design that avoids a constraint produces no evidence that the constraint existed.** The avoidance is self-concealing.

There is a second and opposite failure, and this session is one of the few places in the programme where both are handled at once. A team that classifies honestly and finds most of the space frozen may conclude that nothing can be done. The corpus behind this programme is explicit that this conclusion is a professional failure rather than a finding, and that judgement is the most useful thing it says about constraint.

The third problem is the one this revision of the session exists to address, and it is different in kind from the first two. Both of those concern constraints that were stated and went untested. **The constraints that end engagements are frequently never stated at all.** A brief that proposes grounding a system on an existing document estate is silent on who may read that estate, on whether its sensitivity classification is current, on whether the sharing links inside it point anywhere they should not, and on whether the interactions the system will have can be reconstructed later if somebody asks. None of that silence is evasive. It is the ordinary condition of an organisation that has not yet had a reason to look.

The consequence for qualification is specific. A retrieval system is not a feature launch. It is an event that changes the practical meaning of every permission decision the organisation has made over the preceding decade, and it does so on the day it is enabled. An engineer who cannot see that before the engagement starts is qualifying a different piece of work from the one that will be delivered.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Either**, plus the problem restatement from [S003](/wiki/seminars/S003.md) and the decision from [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md).

Learners from regulated or long-lived estates arrive with a sharp instinct for what cannot move and a weak one for testing it. Learners from newer environments arrive with the reverse and tend to classify almost everything as fluid. The register form suits both, because it does not ask for a judgement without asking for a source.

The platform half of the session has a different entry profile and the difference is worth stating. Learners with security or compliance backgrounds will recognise the tooling and will tend to treat the exercise as a control review, which is the wrong frame: the session is not asking whether the estate is safe but whether the engagement is possible. Learners from build backgrounds will not have seen the tooling and will tend to accept its output as authoritative. **The workflow's third step — write down the instrument's resolution before reading any result — is aimed squarely at the second group and is the step they most reliably skip.**

The session assumes no prior knowledge of the products. It assumes the ability to read documentation for what it does not say, which was rehearsed at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md).

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is moderate and higher than the earlier version of this session carried. The register is mechanically simple; the cost sits in the honesty of the classification, in the fact that this is the first session where nobody specifies the method, and now in an unfamiliar instrument whose output is easy to over-read. The design's mitigation is that the instrument's *reasoning* is assessable without operating it, so a learner without tenant access loses the portal experience and not the lesson. This is a design judgement and carries no external warrant.

**3.2 The opening items are a Productive Failure generation phase, and its conditions bind.**

Kapur's Productive Failure is a two-phase, order-dependent design: learners generate solutions to a task targeting a concept they have not been taught, fail, and are then taught ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). The mechanism this session leans on is the **failed generation effect** — attempting an answer before instruction improves later learning even when the attempt is wrong.

Item 4 is the instrument. It asks what the learner would have to find out before the brief's silence about data access stopped being reassuring, and **most learners answer it as a compliance intention rather than as a discoverable fact.** That gap is the session's content, and it is only legible to the learner who wrote it because items 1 to 3 produced confident answers immediately beside it.

Three of Kapur's stated design conditions bind here and they are conditions rather than preferences. The generation phase must not be scaffolded — his 2011 experiment found that prompts and hints which reduced the failure rate reduced learning, which is why the release rule gates §§4, 5 and 9. The consolidation phase must explicitly prompt comparison — Loibl and Leuders showed that presenting correct and incorrect solutions side by side achieves nothing on its own, and only the condition instructed to compare them learned more, which is why exercise 3 returns the learner to their own item 4 and requires a sentence naming the difference. And the norms are part of the design rather than a preamble to it, which matters more here than it looks: the review movement asks a learner to have their frozen list read back in front of a peer.

**That source's limits are recorded and they are substantial.** It is read in full from the source text, and its evidence is school, university and laboratory studies. Kapur states that the cycle works in corporate training and adult education and cites nothing for that sentence. Applying it to working professionals is an extrapolation, and it is conceded at §8's third objection rather than hidden.

**3.3 Why the source of a constraint is recorded as a person.**

A constraint attributed to a document can be neither challenged nor confirmed; a constraint attributed to a person can be. The column is also the least confrontational available diagnostic, because writing down who said something is not an accusation and declining to write it is informative on its own.

There is a warranted move underneath this, and it is not the design's own. The negotiation literature's instruction when a policy is invoked is to examine the policy rather than the person — its principle, its precedent, its purpose, its exceptions and its reciprocal implications ([research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md)). A rule with a traceable purpose and a documented exception regime is usually a fact, and its exception regime is where the fluid part lives. A rule with neither is usually a position. **That source is a practitioner framework and reports no controlled evaluation of anything**; its own worked cases illustrate mechanism without isolating causes, and the note says so.

**3.4 Why an instrument enters here, and not a conversation.**

The earlier version of this session was entirely conversational: ask people, record who said what, write a test. That is correct and insufficient, for a reason the retrieval-engineering literature states plainly.

The seven-failure-point experience report on retrieval systems reaches, from three deployments in unrelated domains, the conclusion that validation of such a system is only feasible during operation, and that robustness is accumulated rather than designed in ([research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md)). If that is right, then a class of constraint exists which cannot be elicited by asking, because nobody holds it as a belief — it is a property of the estate, discoverable only by looking at the estate.

The platform documentation supplies the sharpest instance. Its argument is that because AI can proactively surface content that is obsolete, over-permissioned or ungoverned, generative AI amplifies the problem of oversharing ([research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md)). The general form, which this repository judges the most durable idea in the platform strand: **deploying a capable retrieval system converts every dormant permissions error in an organisation into a live one.** Oversharing survived for two decades because nobody was reading.

The same family splits governance into a data question and a workload question requiring separate tooling, and names grounding and fine-tuning as the two moments a model touches proprietary data — therefore the two moments it can leak by construction rather than by attack. All of this is **first-party documentation whose authority is definitional.** One page is explicitly the superseded classic version and states that improvements will not be added to it. The other was read through search excerpts rather than fetched in full. **No detection rate, no false-positive rate and no oversharing-prevalence measurement is published, and none is asserted here or in the session.**

**3.5 The resolution of an instrument is what makes a null result readable.**

This is the section that connects the platform half of the session to a literature with better standing than the platform documentation has.

Miller's argument about language-model evaluation is that an eval is an experiment, and that the practically decisive quantity is the **minimum detectable effect**: the smallest true difference the instrument, at its given size and design, could reliably register ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). His practical consequence is that "we ran the eval and saw no difference" is not a finding until you can say what the eval was capable of detecting.

**A governance scan is the same object.** It has a scope — which parts of the estate it looked at. It has a cadence — how often. It has a latency — how long before what it saw is visible to you. And it has exclusions. A scan that reports nothing over a sampled subset of the estate, on a weekly cycle, with a multi-day delay and a service excluded from item-level coverage, has told you something narrow and precise, and it is not "there is no oversharing here."

The parallel is exact and the session exploits it: the sentence a learner must write is *not observed within this scope, at this cadence, as of this date.* Miller's own limits apply and are worth stating — his paper is analytical rather than empirical, its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions, and its framework addresses comparing scores rather than choosing what to score. **No number from it is used here, and the transfer to governance instruments is this design's analogy, not his claim.**

**3.6 Why inference laundering is taught as a design pattern rather than as a mistake.**

The threat-modelling pipeline recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md) — access-controlled internal material, held here as a white-labelled paraphrase and never quoted — contains a stage instructed to infer unstated security properties from best practice, forbidden from marking those inferences in the table it produces, and told to resolve conflicts in the more secure direction. The provenance survives in a separate justification section, which a later assembly stage is explicitly instructed to exclude from the delivered document.

The point for this session is that **none of that is careless.** Every individual instruction is defensible; the composition manufactures constraints. A guess enters as an assumption and leaves as a stated fact, with its provenance removed by design and its bias pointing optimistic — and downstream readers, including the engineers who will inherit the engagement, have no way to distinguish it from something somebody established.

That artefact reports no observed result of any kind: no comparison against a human-authored model, no defect-escape rate, no coverage measurement. It is usable as a specimen showing that the pattern is buildable by competent people, and it establishes nothing about how often the pattern occurs.

**3.7 The counterweight.** Effortful classification is not automatically productive. Macnamara's work on the limits of deliberate practice is the corrective this repository holds against reading difficulty as sufficient ([research/04-professional-formation/macnamara-deliberate-practice-limits.md](/research/04-professional-formation/macnamara-deliberate-practice-limits.md)), and Kapur's own model contains the **Unproductive Failure** quadrant — pure discovery producing low performance and low learning. A session in which learners generate a long register and nothing is assembled from it has landed there. The review movement and exercise 3 are the assembly, and they are the parts most likely to be cut for time.

**3.8 Admissibility is a constraint with a licence attached, and it is the cheapest kind to establish.**

The instrument argument in §3.4 concerns constraints that are properties of the estate. There is a second class that is a property of the *contract*, and it is both cheaper to establish and more often fatal.

Two first-party sources supply it. The agent control-plane material states that the product is licensed per human who manages, sponsors or is served by agents rather than per agent; names an enterprise suite as the prerequisite it "works best" with **without enumerating what degrades in its absence**; requires a global administrator to accept terms and select users before any data flows, and states explicitly that a resource-level enablement property moves nothing until both have happened; and carries availability status that varies capability by capability across general availability, public preview and a separate early-access programme ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-365-and-agent-identity.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-365-and-agent-identity.md)). The control-plane governance material supplies the topology half: three named agent setup tiers, an inbound-versus-outbound isolation asymmetry that a one-line summary of them conceals, a basic-to-standard transition that is a re-provision with three bring-your-own resources rather than a toggle, and connected resources whose private endpoints are documented as not created for you ([research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-control-plane-and-governance.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-control-plane-and-governance.md)).

**The design's claim is that all of this is a §3.5 problem rather than a procurement one.** A licence, a terms acceptance and an availability status each have a resolution in exactly the sense a scan does: they are true for a named capability, within a stated boundary, as of a date. An engineer who records "the tenant is licensed" has written the same defective sentence as one who records "no oversharing found", and the correct form is identical — *not established within this scope, as of this date.* The session's `readiness.yaml` exists to make that form unavoidable, and its `checked_on` fields are the same device as the register's `blind_to`.

**The capacity request is the sharpest instance, and it belongs in the frozen column.** Model capacity is allocated per subscription, per region and per model; an increase is a request with **no published response time, no published approval rate and no published appeal route**; and the stated prioritisation rule gives priority to customers already consuming their allocation, which a new engagement structurally is not. That is an unbounded external dependency whose published policy works against the delivery specifically, and no amount of engineering moves it. It is the clearest example in the platform strand of an entry that is genuinely frozen and whose scope consequence must therefore be stated rather than lamented.

**Both sources are definitional and their limits are the most severe in this paper.** They were read on 2026-07-31. Twelve pages of the first and seven of the second were fetched and read in full; roughly thirty and roughly twenty respectively were read only as search-returned excerpts, so their structures are reconstructed. The announcement supplying the first's licensing and chronology arrived fragmented, with two capability tables reduced to their column headers. **Neither publishes a detection rate, a false-positive rate, a coverage measurement, or any service level for the capacity request the schedule may depend on**, and none is asserted here or in the session.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The movement sequence.** Situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits. The workflow sits after the material rather than inside it, because a learner who meets the instrument before the oversharing argument will read the instrument as a control review.

**4.2 The two columns learners would not have added.** The register has six parts and the two that carry it are source and test. Everything else is bookkeeping. The `settles_it` flag inside the test is a third and smaller addition, and it exists because the commonest defective test in the earlier version of this session was an enquiry wearing a test's clothes — *ask the person who told me* — which cannot settle anything and reads as diligence.

**4.3 The review movement is the session's instrument.** A reader is handed the frozen list and reads back only the sources; every entry whose source is a document, a departed colleague or a consensus is marked untested. What remains is usually a short list, and the reduction is the finding. It happens in front of a peer rather than privately, because a learner marking their own entries untested will find reasons not to.

The supplied brief carries a purpose-built instance: a record structure that everyone attributes to a regulator and one person attributes to a superseded internal system. That case is invented for this session and is constructed so that both classifications are defensible on the evidence available, which is the condition under which the test column becomes necessary rather than decorative.

**4.4 What the platform workflow is for.** It is not a governance tutorial and it is not a control review. It exists to make one durable property physically observable: **that a governance posture has a resolution, and that "we are compliant" is a sentence with a scan scope, a cadence and a latency concealed inside it.**

The workflow's design accepts that most learners will not have tenant access or administrative consent, which the documentation records as a substantial prerequisite. Five of its eight steps are executable against the published documentation alone, and the two that matter most — recording the resolution, and writing the blind spots down as entries — are among them. **A workflow whose lesson depends on a licence is a workflow that teaches licensing.**

**4.5 A defect found in this session's own artefact, and the amendment.**

The register schema as first designed had four fields: statement, classification, source, test. It had no field for what the instrument could not see.

Under that schema, a learner who ran a data risk assessment over a sampled subset of the estate and got no findings would have written an entry classified *fluid*, sourced to the scan, with a test marked as settled. Every field would have been filled correctly. The entry would have been false, and it would have been false in precisely the way this session exists to prevent: **an absence of observation recorded as a finding about the world.**

The amendment adds an `instrument` block carrying scope, cadence, latency and an explicit `blind_to` field, and adds step 7 to the workflow requiring blind spots to be written as entries rather than as caveats. **The amendment is recorded rather than silently applied**, because the defect is instructive: a session can teach the resolution-of-the-instrument argument in its material and then hand learners a schema that cannot express it. Only writing the artefact out in full caught it. Assume there are others this paper has not caught.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The convenient freeze.** The most likely outcome is a frozen list that coincides with the set of conversations the learner would rather not have. *Countermeasure:* the source column, which does not detect the motive but does expose that the source is a person who has not been asked.

**The unrunnable test.** A learner writes a test that would settle the constraint and that they have no standing or access to run. *Countermeasure:* weak. The register accepts it, the `settles_it` flag at least separates a test from an enquiry, and [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md) supplies the mechanism for asking somebody who is not in the room. Between the two sessions the learner holds a test they cannot run, which is honest and unsatisfying.

**Scan-scope blindness.** A learner reads a clean instrument output as a clean estate. *Countermeasure:* the `blind_to` field and workflow step 7, added by the §4.5 amendment. This is the session's newest countermeasure and the one with the least evidence behind it; prediction 5 in §9 predicts its failure.

**Governance theatre.** A learner concludes that having run a scan, the data question is handled. *Countermeasure:* partial, and it is the material rather than the artefact — the session states that the tool gives a posture estimate rather than a control, and that the safest remediation is the most disruptive and needs an owner. A learner determined to file a screenshot as an answer will file a screenshot as an answer.

**Classification as an exercise.** A learner produces a well-formed register and continues designing against the original assumptions. *Countermeasure:* none in this session. The register is an input to [S008](/wiki/seminars/S008.md) and [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md), where an unused reclassification becomes visible, and no earlier detector exists.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S008](/wiki/seminars/S008.md) and [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md). The fluid column is where [S008](/wiki/seminars/S008.md) looks for a simpler sufficient option; the frozen column is where [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) finds the constraints on which a refusal may legitimately rest.

The platform half hands forward to [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md) directly and immediately. This session establishes that a permissions estate is a constraint on what a system may return; the next establishes how that constraint is enforced at retrieval time, and that enforcement lags the source system in a way nobody has published a figure for. **The two sessions are one argument split across a day**, and the split is deliberate: the constraint has to be experienced as a discovery before it is met as an engineering mechanism.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence and no specification element of its own. The register is carried into [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md), where a refusal resting on a constraint the learner marked untested fails element 2 of that specification.

**The register is therefore assessed by consequence rather than directly**, which is a deliberate choice: a register produced to be marked is a register optimised for the marker.

There is a second reason, and it follows from the entrustment framework this programme's assessment architecture rests on ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). An entrustable professional activity is a unit of practice that can be handed over to be performed unsupervised. **Classifying constraints is a plausible component of such an activity and is not one by itself**, because its correctness depends on facts about the engagement that no assessment can hold constant. What can be judged is whether the register's sources and tests are of a kind that could be checked — a property of the artefact rather than of the world — and that judgement is exercised where it has consequences, at S010.

**Nothing else is assessed.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the classification is unfalsifiable, and it flatters whoever performs it.**

At its strongest: any constraint can be argued into either column with sufficient motivation, the register produces no prediction, and the source column encourages a learner to reclassify anything inconvenient as untested. A framework that always finds something movable will always find something movable, whether or not anything is.

**Conceded in part, and the specific concession is that the classification produces no prediction.** The rebuttal is narrower: the register's value does not rest on the classification being correct but on the source and test columns being filled, which are matters of fact and can be checked. A register with honest sources and no reclassification at all is a successful outcome of this session. **The declined fix was to require a minimum number of reclassifications**, rejected because it would manufacture exactly the flattery the objection describes.

**Objection 2: the rule that constraint is never an excuse is a management sentiment, and it will be used against engineers who correctly report that something cannot be done.**

At its strongest: the sentence is unfalsifiable, it places the burden on the person delivering bad news, and in an organisation with any pressure in it, it becomes an instruction never to say no. That is a real harm, and this programme's own Stage 1 exists to make refusal available, so the tension is internal.

**Conceded without qualification.** The design's mitigation is that the register requires a genuinely frozen entry with its scope consequence stated, which makes reporting an immovable constraint an assessed act rather than a failure to try. **The mitigation does not resolve the tension and the tension is real.** The declined fix was to drop the rule entirely, rejected because the failure it addresses — a register that reads as an explanation of why nothing happened — is observable, and because a practice with no answer to a hard constraint produces engineers who treat difficulty as a reason. That is a judgement about which harm is worse and it has no evidence behind it.

**Objection 3: putting a governance instrument in week two is a category error, and it will produce learners who think a scan is a decision.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the session's platform half most deserves: data governance is a discipline with its own professionals, its own law and its own review processes. A qualification session that hands a delivery engineer a posture tool teaches them to form a view about somebody else's domain on the basis of a sampled, delayed, capped instrument they have used once. The likely outcome is not better qualification but confident amateurs, and the specific harm is that a scan reported in a qualification document will be read downstream as due diligence performed.

**Partially conceded, and the concession is that the harm is plausible and unmeasured.** The rebuttal is that the alternative is worse in a way that is easy to demonstrate: an engagement qualified without any view of the permissions estate is qualified on the assumption that the estate is fine, and that assumption is made silently by everyone who does not look. The session's own remedy is to require the blind spots to be written down as entries, which converts a confident finding into a bounded one. **The declined fix was to move this material into the security stage**, rejected on the grounds that a constraint discovered after the engagement has been accepted is no longer a qualification input — it is a change request. **The cost of that decision is that this session hands learners an instrument before it hands them the discipline**, and this paper records that as an accepted cost rather than a solved problem.

**Objection 4: the platform half will be stale before the first cohort finishes.**

At its strongest: one of the two documentation sources behind it is explicitly superseded, the other was read through excerpts rather than fetched, agent-level capability moves to a different licence on a published date, and the portal paths will not survive a year. Teaching from it produces knowledge with a shelf life shorter than the module.

**Conceded, and it is why the session is built the way it is.** Every durable claim in the perishability table is stated without a product name, and the workflow's assessable steps are the ones that read documentation rather than operate a portal. The narrower point is that **pinning does not slow the decay; it makes the decay visible** — a learner who has recorded that they read a page explicitly labelled superseded has learned something about vendor documentation that no stable source could have taught them.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first registers will attribute at least one frozen constraint to a document rather than to a person. *Instrument:* the source column, coded *person* / *document* / *consensus* per entry.

**Prediction 2.** The frozen list will shorten after the review movement in most cases, and the reduction will be larger for learners from long-lived estates. *Instrument:* frozen-entry counts before and after review, split by the learner's declared background.

**Prediction 3.** Learners who write a test they cannot run will more often carry the constraint unresolved into [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) than learners who write a test within their reach. *Instrument:* test feasibility coded at S006 against the constraint's status in the S010 artefact. **If this fails, the test column is recording intention rather than plan** and should require a named person able to answer.

**Prediction 4.** Answers to opening item 4 will be markedly less concrete than answers to items 1, 2 and 3, and will name an intention rather than a discoverable fact. *Instrument:* the five written answers, coded *discoverable fact* / *intention* / *absent* by a second reader. **This is the prediction that the session's generation phase is aimed at the right item**, and if item 4 is answered as well as the others the material should be reordered.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of learners will record at least one instrument-derived entry with an empty or generic `blind_to` field, despite step 7 requiring blind spots to be written as entries. *Instrument:* the register's instrument blocks, coded for whether `blind_to` names a specific unscanned region. **This predicts the failure of the countermeasure added by the §4.5 amendment**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption wearing a fix's clothes.

**Prediction 6.** Learners instructed to compare their item-4 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 3) will produce register entries whose tests a reviewer judges capable of settling the question more often than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* the reviewer's binary judgement across two cohorts, or absent two cohorts, a within-cohort split on whether exercise 3 was completed. **This is the session's only direct test of the mechanism §3.2 rests on**, and a null result would mean the comparison prompt is ceremony.

**Prediction 7.** Learners will report the platform workflow as the more useful half of the session and the register as the less useful, and the reverse will hold at [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) when a refusal has to rest on a constraint that was actually established. *Instrument:* a two-item preference question at the close of S006 and again at the close of S010.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That constraints are beliefs until somebody tests them; that the cheapest way to make an untested belief visible is to write down who holds it; and that an entire class of constraint is never held by anybody at all and has to be found with an instrument.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The frozen/fluid classification is a practitioner idea recorded from a complete reading, and its source offers no instance of it working. The oversharing amplification argument is a mechanism from vendor documentation with no prevalence data behind it. The teaching method is well grounded and its warrant comes from a population unlike you. **Do not let the quality of the pedagogical citations transfer to the substantive thesis; they are about different things.**

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper.** That is a rule of this repository rather than an accident of what was read, and it holds for the platform documentation exactly as for the research literature — including for detection rates and false-positive rates, of which none is published for any product named here.

**The concession worth remembering.** The rule that constraint is never an excuse will be quoted at engineers who are telling the truth. Knowing that in advance is most of the defence available. And note §4.5: this paper caught its own artefact recording an absence of observation as a finding about the world, which is the exact error the session is built to prevent. **Assume there are others.**

**The silences that matter.** The Purview page read is explicitly labelled the classic version, states that it has been replaced, and states that improvements will not be added to it — so some described behaviour may already be historical, and the replacement page was not read. Agent-level discovery and posture are documented as moving to a separate licence on a published future date, which changes what is available under which plan. **The documentation publishes no detection rate, no false-positive rate, no coverage measurement and no estimate of how much oversharing exists in a typical estate** — which is exactly the class of figure a qualification decision would want, and the reason this session teaches learners to write "not observed within this scope" rather than "none found". It does not analyse the privacy implications of capturing prompts and responses, despite recommending capture for compliance purposes. It states operational caps, cadences and latencies without saying what proportion of a typical estate they cover. And it is silent on the question the session actually asks it: whether any of the controls it describes has ever prevented anything.

**Three further silences, from the readiness material, and each one is an absence in the source rather than a gap in the reading.** **No measure of registry coverage exists anywhere** — nothing states what proportion of the agents actually present in a tenant the inventory discovers, and the count of unmanaged agents is defined without any statement of how confidently unmanaged agents are found. A claim that an estate is inventoried therefore carries exactly the concealed resolution that a claim of compliance does, which is why the session's `inventory_coverage` block records the absence as a value rather than omitting the field. **No service level, response time, approval rate or appeal route is published for a model capacity increase**, and the stated prioritisation rule favours existing consumption — placing an unbounded external dependency on the critical path of precisely the engagement that has none. And **no page enumerates what degrades without the enterprise suite the control plane is documented as working "best" with**, so the distance between the supported configuration and the recommended one is not readable and must be asked of a person. All three are the kind of thing a qualification decision needs and cannot obtain from the vendor.
