## In plain terms

Discovery ends with a question that most engineers never ask, and the reason they never ask it is that not asking feels like nothing has gone wrong.

**A commitment is something the stakeholder gives up.** Meaningful time, reputational exposure, or money. Not a warm sentence about the future, not a request for a proposal, not a scheduled follow-up, not an account opened on a free tier that cost nobody anything. If you cannot say what it cost them, you do not have one.

The session's most counter-intuitive claim is that a clear refusal is a better outcome than an enthusiastic deferral. A refusal ends an unresolved position and tells you something about the situation. **A warm deferral tells you nothing and feels better**, which is exactly why it accumulates.

There is one place in this stage where the practice corpus and the interviewing literature agree, and it is here: the corpus insists that a session ending in thanks with no committed next step has failed, and names awareness and enthusiasm as unacceptable outcomes. Two traditions arrived at the same position independently, one from delivery and one from customer conversation. **That is not evidence. It is worth more than one assertion, and the session says exactly that rather than pretending the agreement settles anything.**

The session then makes the abstraction arithmetic. Buying dedicated model capacity turns every idea above into something with a unit, a term and a price — and it supplies one distinction the ledger badly needs. **Quota is permission and costs nothing; capacity is availability and may not be there.** A sponsor entirely entitled to release two engineers may be entirely unable to, and in a meeting those two facts are indistinguishable. The ledger's authority field asks the first question. **Only the ask reaches the second**, which is why this session exists.

## Abstract

This paper defends closing discovery with an explicit commitment ask and defends recording refusal as a positive result. The argument is that a discovery conclusion supported only by expressed enthusiasm is unfalsifiable in the same way as a readiness rubric composed of dispositions; that costly behaviour is the only available observable of seriousness; and that the practitioner's characteristic failure is not a poor ask but an absent one, because an absent ask leaves the pleasant state undisturbed. The session installs a commitment ledger recording, per stakeholder, the smallest ethical ask, its cost, the stakeholder's authority to give it, the outcome, and what a refusal would teach.

It then defends a platform movement in a session whose material is small and whose difficulty is affective. The argument is that dedicated model capacity is the cheapest available place to feel three distinctions the ledger depends on: that **permission is not availability**, that a commitment held has a unit and a term and a price, and that the correct procurement sequence is to verify availability and only then to commit. A learner who fails a deployment against available quota has met the sponsor who is entitled to release an engineer and cannot.

The paper concedes that commitment predicts nothing; that the source establishes no effect; that the framework carries a real ethical hazard when applied to people who bear a system's consequences without controlling its purchase; that marking a rehearsal on obtaining a refusal opens a corruption path the design does not close; and — in its third objection — that the capacity analogy conceals a genuine disanalogy, because quota is free and authority is not.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Discovery ends and everybody is pleased. That state is stable, it feels like success, and nothing in it is checkable.

The mechanism is the one this stage keeps meeting. Enthusiasm is available at no cost to the person expressing it, it is socially rewarded, and it is what a professional produces when a guest has spent two days being helpful. It therefore carries no information about whether anything will happen.

The practitioner's failure is usually not that they asked badly. **It is that they did not ask**, because asking risks converting a pleasant relationship into a refusal, and the refusal will be visible to their own organisation while the deferral will not. The incentive is precisely backwards.

The session's response is to make the ask a required artefact with a required outcome field, and to mark the rehearsal on obtaining a refusal rather than a commitment — which removes the incentive to ask softly.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) supplies the disposition to distrust flattering answers, without which the ledger is filled with warm sentences recorded as advancements. [S018](/wiki/seminars/S018.md) supplies the population reasoning that determines who is worth asking. [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md) supplies the cost-of-inaction framing that makes a refusal interpretable.

The prior it works against is that asking for something is a sales act and therefore not the engineer's job. **The session's move is that the ask is an evidence-gathering act** whose output is information about authority, priority and cost, and that a delivery team which never obtains it is planning against expressed preference.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Low intrinsic, high affective. The material is small — three currencies and a distinction — and the difficulty is entirely in performing the ask and accepting the refusal.

**3.2 Why costly behaviour is the observable.** Time, reputational risk and money are the currencies in which seriousness is expressed, and their evidential strength rises as the cost to that particular stakeholder becomes more real. **A trial is not automatically commitment**: opening an account may cost nothing, while supplying data, changing a workflow or accepting a public case study may cost a great deal. The unit is what this person is actually sacrificing, not the category of the act.

**3.3 Why a refusal is informative.** It ends an unresolved position and supplies negative evidence, which is otherwise almost unobtainable in discovery. **This depends on the refusal being cheap enough for the person to give**, which is why [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md) is retrieved: if declining costs them nothing, their no is as weak as their yes.

**3.4 Why deferrals must be converted.** A vague future offer becomes evidence only when it acquires a person, a condition, a date or a next action. Until then it occupies the space a decision would occupy without being one.

**3.5 Why the enterprise translation is marked as inference.** The mapping from the three currencies onto named engineer time, representative data access, a security reviewer, a pilot cohort, workflow change, procurement engagement and budget sponsorship is **course-design inference recorded as such in this repository's reading**, not a claim the source makes about forward-deployed work.

**3.6 Why a commitment is a capacity decision, and why the platform makes that legible.**

Dedicated model capacity forces every distinction in this session into a form with a price and a term ([research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md)). A provisioned deployment **holds a fixed amount of processing capacity exclusively for your deployment's use, whether or not requests are being made**, and everything else follows: capacity held whether or not it is used must be paid for whether or not it is used, so the billing unit changes from tokens to time, and the deployment stops being a configuration choice and becomes a capacity decision.

The distinction the session imports is **quota against capacity.** Quota is a policy limit with **no associated cost**. Capacity is what is actually available. The documentation's own call-out is that **having quota does not guarantee that capacity is available**, and a request exceeding what the region holds simply fails.

Read against a stakeholder, that is permission against availability. **A person's authority is their quota; their willingness this quarter is their capacity**, and the two are held in different places, change on different schedules and feel identical in a meeting. The ledger's authority field interrogates the first. Only the ask reaches the second, which is the design's justification for requiring an ask at all rather than an assessment of who could in principle agree.

Two further properties transfer without strain and both sharpen the session's existing claims. Capacity **changes throughout the day based on demand**, so *can I have this* is time-dependent rather than a fact about the organisation — which is the honest reading of a refusal that would have been a yes last month. And releasing capacity is a one-way door in practice though it looks reversible: scaling down returns capacity to the pool with **no guarantee the same capacity is available later.** A commitment politely released is not a commitment politely retrievable, and that is the strongest available argument against the practitioner habit of withdrawing an ask to preserve a relationship.

**3.7 Why the sequence is deploy, verify, then commit.**

The documentation's procurement guidance is stated in the correct order and the intuitive order is wrong. **Reservations are a discount applied to the billing meter, not to individual deployments, and they do not guarantee capacity**; the instruction is to create deployments to confirm capacity is available and *then* purchase the reservation.

The transfer is exact and unflattering. A statement of work signed before the named engineer has been released is the same error, and it fails the same way: a commitment locked in against a resource nobody confirmed. **The design's claim is that a discovery engineer who has felt this in a capacity plan will recognise it in a contract**, which is design reasoning and is marked as such.

The warning against using dedicated capacity as an autoscaling mechanism carries a second transfer: capacity may not be available when you scale back up, and sustained hourly billing at high utilisation typically exceeds the committed rate. **Repeatedly giving and withdrawing a commitment costs more than holding one, and may not be available the third time you ask.** Practitioners know this about goodwill and rarely write it down; here it is written down about machines.

**Spillover** completes the set as a graceful-degradation pattern triggered by **an error response rather than by a prediction**. Its ledger equivalent is naming, before the ask, what happens if the answer is no — and the discipline is identical, because a fallback designed after the refusal is an improvisation.

**Its limits.** First-party product documentation; definitional rather than empirical; **highly perishable**, with names, defaults, ratios, minimum sizes and portal paths changing frequently. The concrete figures live on companion pages that were **not fetched for this repository and are not reproduced anywhere.** It compares one vendor's capacity model against nothing, and its choice architecture is the vendor's. The analogy between organisational authority and platform quota is this programme's and not the documentation's.

**3.8 Why the ask must be sized before it is made.**

An evaluation has a minimum effect it is capable of detecting, and doing that analysis before the run is what turns a null result into a statement about the system rather than about the test ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)).

Transposed, it is the operational content of the *smallest ethical ask* field. **If the thing you are about to request would be granted just as readily by an unserious customer as by a serious one, the answer cannot discriminate and the ask is decorative.** A follow-up meeting fails almost always. Named engineer time with a date usually passes. The check is to write what a yes would rule out and what a no would rule out, before asking; identical answers mean the ask should be replaced rather than rephrased.

**Its limits, and they are real.** Power analysis is a procedure for a measurement with a defined variance, and a commitment ask has neither a variance nor a population. **What transfers is the discipline of deciding in advance what an answer could rule out, and nothing else** — no number, no threshold and no method from the source is used here, and its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions in any case.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**The ledger's authority field is what makes the artefact more than a list of asks.** It forces the learner to establish who can actually give the thing, which is discovery in its own right — an enthusiastic person who cannot commit what they are committing has told you something about the organisation.

**The ethical constraint is placed inside the standard rather than in a footnote.** Users, regulators, security reviewers and affected non-buyers may have no legitimate currency to surrender, and the correct ask of them may be nothing more than continued honest participation. **The corpus does not state this constraint and the interviewing literature does**, and the session credits the difference explicitly.

**The live rehearsal is marked on obtaining a refusal.** This is the design's sharpest move and its purpose is incentive removal: a learner marked on obtaining a commitment asks for the smallest thing that will be granted, and a learner marked on obtaining a refusal asks for the thing that would actually matter. The peer is instructed to concede nothing, so the only reachable pass is a clear no.

**The corpus agreement is stated as an agreement and not as evidence.** Two independent arrivals at the same position is a weak signal, it is the strongest one available here, and describing it accurately is itself the lesson.

**What the platform movement is for, and it is not tooling competence.**

This session's material is small and its difficulty is entirely affective. That is a pedagogical problem: there is not much to think about, and the thing that must be learned is the willingness to ask. A movement that gave learners more to read would not help.

The capacity plan solves it by making the session's abstractions arithmetically real. **A commitment acquires a unit, a term and a price**, and the learner discovers that the answer is most sensitive to the inputs they guessed rather than to the ones they argued about — which is the same discovery, one level down, as finding that a discovery finding rests on the informant nobody questioned.

The quota-and-capacity step does the work the ledger's authority field cannot. **A learner will hold a permission they cannot spend**, which is precisely the sponsor who is entitled to release an engineer and unable to. Reading that sentence is unpersuasive; failing a deployment against available quota is not.

And the sequencing step supplies the session's single most transferable procurement lesson in a place where it costs nothing to learn: **verify availability, then commit.** The learner meets it as a documented instruction about billing meters rather than as advice about contracts, which is why it survives.

**One design decision is worth defending explicitly.** Every step of the movement is executable on paper, and the platform steps confirm arithmetic the learner has already done. This is deliberate: a session whose real content is a two-minute conversation must not become a session about a portal, and **no learner should fail this movement for lack of a subscription.**

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The ask never made.** The likeliest failure and the one the whole session targets. *Countermeasure:* the ledger's outcome field includes *not asked* as an explicit option with a required reason, which makes the omission visible rather than absent.

**The costless commitment.** An account opened, a workflow unchanged, a pilot recorded. *Countermeasure:* the standard's cost field, which cannot be completed for a costless act.

**The unauthorised commitment.** *Countermeasure:* the authority field, plus the standard's requirement that the person be able to name who else must agree.

**The extracted commitment.** Pressure applied, concession obtained, concession quietly withdrawn later. *Countermeasure:* partial. The ethical constraint prohibits it and the live rehearsal's marking removes the incentive, but nothing in the session detects it in a real engagement. **A commitment given under pressure looks identical in a ledger to one given freely**, and the paper does not pretend otherwise.

**The affected non-buyer treated as a lead.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's test — would the ask embarrass you if their manager heard it.

**The plan mistaken for the evidence.** A learner produces a capacity plan with a precise-looking requirement and treats it as a finding. *Countermeasure:* the provenance field on every input, and the required sentence naming the input the plan is most sensitive to. **A plan whose least confident input is its most influential one has a precision it has not earned**, and the countermeasure makes that legible rather than preventing it.

**The analogy taken as an argument.** A learner concludes that because platform capacity behaves this way, stakeholders do. *Countermeasure:* the session states repeatedly, and at every point where the mapping is used, that it is this programme's and not the documentation's. **This is a weak countermeasure against a satisfying idea**, and §8's third objection concedes it.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: this is sales method taught to engineers, and it will damage the relationship it is applied to.**

At its strongest: a forward-deployed engineer's standing rests on being the person who is not selling. The moment they begin asking stakeholders for costly demonstrations of seriousness, they have become another part of the commercial apparatus, and the candour that made them useful disappears. The account team owns commitment; the engineer who tries to own it will be resented by both sides.

**Partly conceded.** The role hazard is real and this programme has already conceded a version of it at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md), where qualification authority frequently sits elsewhere. The rebuttal is that the ledger's purpose is evidential rather than commercial: the engineer needs to know whether the data will actually be made available and whether the named engineers will actually be released, and those are engineering facts obtained by asking. **The declined fix is to restrict the ask to technical commitments and route the rest to the account team.** It is declined because the split is not clean — representative data access is technical, and it is also budget, legal authority and somebody's political exposure. **Whether an engineer can hold both roles is untested and this paper does not claim it.**

**Objection 2: refusal is being romanticised, and a design that marks learners on obtaining one teaches them to force a no.**

At its strongest: an engineer trained to prize refusal will ask in a way that produces refusal — too early, too large, or framed so that agreeing is awkward — and will report the resulting no as valuable information when in fact they manufactured it. The scheme rewards an outcome the learner can arrange unilaterally, which is the definition of a corruptible measure.

**This is a strong objection and it is only partly answered.** The design's protection is the *smallest ethical ask* requirement in the standard: an oversized ask that fails teaches you about the ask rather than about the customer, and the ledger must state why the ask was the smallest one that would establish the point. **The declined fix is to mark the rehearsal on the quality of the ask rather than on its outcome.** It is declined because quality is judged by the same person whose discomfort produces the failure this session exists to prevent — but the objection identifies a real corruption path and a cohort showing manufactured refusals would falsify the design's protection.

**Objection 3: the capacity analogy is a conceit, and a conceit is a poor foundation for a session about people.**

At its strongest: a provisioned deployment is a contractual arrangement with a vendor whose behaviour is specified in documentation. A stakeholder is a person inside an organisation with politics, a manager, a workload and a memory. Calling authority *quota* and willingness *capacity* is a pleasing turn of phrase that will encourage learners to reason about colleagues as though their behaviour were documented. And the analogy's most attractive feature — that quota costs nothing and guarantees nothing — is the part most likely to be over-applied: a sponsor's authority is not free, because exercising it spends political credit the documentation has no equivalent for. **The design has borrowed the vocabulary of a system whose failure modes are published, for a system whose failure modes are not, and the borrowing flatters the borrower.**

**Substantially conceded, and the point about political cost is a genuine defect the analogy conceals.** Quota really is free and authority really is not, and a learner reasoning purely from the analogy will under-price what it costs a sponsor to exercise theirs — which is the opposite of what [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md) spent a session establishing. The session states repeatedly that the mapping is this programme's and not the documentation's, and that is a warning rather than a correction.

The rebuttal is narrow. The analogy is not doing inferential work: **no conclusion about any stakeholder is drawn from any property of the capacity model.** What it supplies is a place where the permission-versus-availability distinction can be felt at low cost, and where a learner can be wrong about it in an afternoon rather than in an engagement. **The declined fix is to teach the capacity model as an unconnected platform topic and let learners make the connection or not.** It is declined because an unconnected platform topic in a session about commitment is exactly the ornamental anchor this programme's own review criticised elsewhere — but the objection is right that the connection is asserted rather than demonstrated, and prediction 7 is the only thing that would show whether learners take it as vocabulary or as inference.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** In first-draft ledgers, most rows will record an outcome that is neither a commitment nor a refusal. *Instrument:* the outcome field, coded four ways across submitted ledgers.

**Prediction 2.** Learners will complete the cost field less often than any other required field. *Instrument:* field-presence counts.

**Prediction 3.** In the live rehearsal, most learners will end without either a commitment or a refusal on the first attempt. *Instrument:* the peer's three-way judgement, per attempt.

**Prediction 4.** Ledgers containing a *not asked* row with a stated reason will be rarer than ledgers containing no such row, even where the engagement plainly had unasked stakeholders. *Instrument:* the ledgers against the [S018](/wiki/seminars/S018.md) not-sampled list. **If the two lists never overlap, one of the two artefacts is being completed to satisfy its own session rather than to describe the engagement.**

**Prediction 5.** Most capacity plans will mark the request-count input as observed and the cache rate as inferred, and most learners will nonetheless name the request count as the input their plan is most sensitive to. *Instrument:* the provenance fields against the free-text sensitivity sentence. **A mismatch would mean the sensitivity step is being answered from intuition rather than from the recomputation the workflow requires**, and the step would need to become a comparison of two computed figures rather than a judgement.

**Prediction 6.** Learners who completed the sequencing step will more often record, in their ledger, what they will do if a commitment is refused. *Instrument:* the presence of a fallback per ledger row, split within cohort on whether the capacity plan's spillover section was completed.

**Prediction 7.** In a closed-book item, a substantial share of learners will state a conclusion about stakeholder behaviour supported only by a property of the capacity model. *Instrument:* a single free-text item asking what the quota-and-capacity distinction establishes about a sponsor, coded *vocabulary* / *inference* / *unclear* by a second reader. **This is the direct test of §8's third objection.** If most answers are coded *inference*, the analogy is doing work it cannot support and the movement must be reframed as an unconnected topic or removed.

**Prediction 8.** Learners instructed to compare their item-4 answer against the taught criterion will leave the live rehearsal with a refusal more often than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* the peer's three-way judgement, split within cohort on exercise completion. **The same prediction is made across this module's five papers, so a null across all of them would indict the comparison pattern rather than any one session.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That seriousness is observable only as cost, that a refusal is evidence and a deferral is not, that the characteristic failure is the ask that was never made, and that an ask which cannot discriminate between a serious and an unserious answer is decorative however politely it is phrased.

**What the evidence licenses.** A vocabulary and a set of distinctions. **A commitment is evidence of seriousness and proof of nothing** — it may be given for political, experimental or relationship reasons, and a person with a genuine need may be unable to give one at all. The platform documentation licenses statements about how one vendor's capacity model behaves and nothing about how any person behaves; the mapping between them is this programme's and is an analogy rather than an argument.

**The concession worth remembering.** Two. Marking a rehearsal on obtaining a refusal creates a corruption path the design does not close — if your cohort starts manufacturing refusals, the design is wrong and not the cohort. And the capacity analogy hides a real disanalogy: **quota costs nothing and authority costs political credit**, so a learner reasoning purely from the analogy will under-price what it costs a sponsor to say yes. That is the opposite of what [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md) established, and the session warns rather than corrects.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** That a provisioned deployment holds a fixed amount of processing capacity exclusively for that deployment's use whether or not requests are being made, and that the billing unit is consequently time rather than tokens; that four deployment categories exist and differ across billing, latency guarantee and workload type; that the unit of capacity is model-independent and region-specific, delivers model-dependent throughput, and carries a per-model minimum deployment size; that quota is a policy limit enforced by the platform with no associated cost, scoped per subscription, per region and per deployment type, while capacity is the amount actually available and is allocated at deployment time; that **having quota does not guarantee that capacity is available** and an insufficient region causes deployment failure; that capacity changes through the day with demand and that releasing it offers no guarantee of its return; that sizing takes a request shape, an output-to-input ratio and a cache rate, that output tokens consume more capacity than input tokens and that cached input tokens consume none; that three routing variants trade data residency against availability; that spillover redirects overflow requests to a standard deployment on a non-200 response, is controllable per request by header, and is unsupported for some non-Azure-OpenAI models; that the documentation explicitly advises against scaling provisioned deployments with traffic, for two stated reasons; and that **reservations are a discount applied to the billing meter rather than to deployments, do not guarantee capacity, and should be purchased only after a deployment has confirmed capacity is available** — all recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md), from a full fetch and reading of the page.

**The silences that matter.** **No per-model capacity ratio, minimum deployment size, price, default quota allocation or regional availability figure was verified**, because all of them live on companion pages that were not fetched; none is asserted anywhere in this session, and a learner's capacity plan therefore cannot be completed from this repository alone — which is stated in the workflow rather than concealed. **The documentation states intended behaviour and is not evidence of observed reliability**; the latency targets it references are defined elsewhere and were not read. It offers **no comparison against any other vendor's capacity model**, so nothing here establishes that this is a good way to buy capacity, only that it is this platform's way. **Documentation currency was not independently checked against a dated revision**, and the page is among the most perishable material in this repository: names, defaults, ratios, minimums and portal paths change. Finally, the page has an obvious commercial framing — **the trade-offs it presents are real and the choice architecture is the vendor's** — and nothing in it addresses what a customer should do when the correct answer is to buy no dedicated capacity at all, which is the exact structural silence this session's own corpus exhibits about declining an engagement.
