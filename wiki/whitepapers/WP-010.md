# WP-010 — Being Wrong in Public: The First Error Clinic

**Seminar day:** [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) · **Module:** [M02](/wiki/modules/M02.md) · **Quarter:** [Q1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md)
**Strands:** PE · CF · MS · **Complexity class:** 1
**Platform anchor:** GitHub Copilot agent mode; the module's accumulated repository customization surface · **MCP servers:** Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends spending the programme's first assessment-bearing day establishing a disclosure norm rather than assessing technical content, and defends the specific mechanism used: physically removing the assessor from the clinic phases and demonstrating the firewall in front of the cohort. The argument is that the programme's assessment architecture, its substrate work and its entrustment judgements all depend on accurate self-report over three years, that self-report is governed by beliefs about consequence rather than by stated policy, and that beliefs about consequence are formed by observation of the highest-risk case rather than by the average case. The paper also defends the day's sharpest structural distinction — that disclosure inside the assessed conversation is rewarded while disclosure inside the clinic is not recorded at all — on the grounds that a blanket safety claim is not credible and a bounded one is. It then defends siting the programme's first **unassisted-work occasion** on this day, twenty bounded minutes marked by a non-assessor and excluded from every decision, on the ground that the day is already the programme's demonstration that a costly commitment can be relied on and that a second commitment costs less to establish beside the first than alone. It concedes that the firewall's central commitment is unverifiable by students in principle, that the design's own instrument for measuring belief is contaminated by the setting in which it is administered, that the arrangement imposes a real and uncompensated cost on assessment quality, and — the objection that bites hardest on the practical turn — that the unassisted provision introduces a third category on the one day whose whole force comes from there being two.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The programme has made an unusual bet. Its assessment architecture rests on narrative evidence of judgement rather than on scores; its entrustment decisions require observation of students acting under genuine uncertainty; and its seminar pattern devotes forty minutes of every teaching day to students describing their own failures. All three depend on students reporting accurately what they did, including what they did badly, to an institution that also credentials them.

That dependency is fragile and the ordinary result is well known: people disclose in proportion to their belief about consequence, and stated policies about confidentiality are weak evidence about consequence compared with observed behaviour. A student who has been told that the clinic is safe and has never seen the claim tested will disclose the low-cost items and withhold everything else. The clinic will appear to function — there will be disclosures, they will be discussed — and it will be a performance of a norm rather than the norm.

The belief is therefore formed by the highest-risk case, not the average one. Students will not conclude the firewall holds from ten uneventful clinics; they will conclude it from one clinic held on a day when their assessment was live and the assessor was in the building. That is exactly the case this day constructs.

The second problem is more specific and is the reason the day draws a boundary rather than making a blanket promise. A programme that says *nothing you disclose is ever used against you* is not credible, and students correctly disbelieve it, because they know their assessors are people with memories. The design's alternative is a bounded claim with a physical mechanism: in this room, at this time, the assessor is not present and will not be told. That is a claim students can check, and it is checkable precisely because it is narrow.

The third problem is that the module has to end. M02 produced four artefacts and a stance, and the day's phase 2 and phase 3 are where the stance is tested — not by asking whether the artefacts are good but by asking whether the student can say what they claim and where they failed.

The fourth problem is the programme's newest and least comfortable. **Every artefact this module produced was built with an agent, and the programme cannot currently tell which students could have built them without one.** The risk is not that a student cheated; the module's artefacts require judgements no agent supplies. The risk is subtler and slower: a student who has never worked unaided has no calibration for what they can do unaided, forms no view of it, and finds out in year three, in front of a customer, at the point where the programme's entire claim is that they can say what a system does badly. The [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) states the risk plainly and states that exhortation does not mitigate it. Its structural response is three bounded unassisted occasions across the programme, and this is the first.

Siting the first one here is a deliberate choice and the argument for it is the same argument the rest of the day makes. **The provision requires students to believe a commitment they cannot verify** — that the comparison will not be used against them — and this is the one day in the programme built entirely around demonstrating that such a commitment can be relied on. Establishing a second bounded commitment beside the first, in the same session, with the same physical separation and the same public statement, costs far less than establishing it cold on a day whose subject is something else. The alternative sites were all worse: put it in Q2 and it arrives with no framing; put it at S005 and it precedes the norm it depends on; leave it out of Q1 and the first measurement of a three-year trajectory happens at [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md), halfway through, which is late enough to be a report rather than an intervention.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge assumptions

- **Nine error clinics attended,** all with instructor-first disclosure and none with an assessment stake.
- **A stated firewall,** repeated at S001 and at every clinic, never tested.
- **Four module artefacts** whose specification items are now due.
- **The S009 asymmetry, two days old,** which phase 3's falsification question uses directly.
- **No experience of an assessed oral defence.** This is the first, and its shape recurs at S020, S030, S040 and ultimately at S090.
- **No experience of working unaided on assessed material.** Four builds, four prompt records, four intervention records, and no occasion on which the agent was absent. This is the entry state the unassisted segment exists to make visible, and it is stated here as an assumption rather than a finding because the design has not measured it.
- **Widely varying prior experience of institutional trust,** which is the day's largest uncontrolled variable and is not addressable by design.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Low intrinsic load, deliberately, and high evaluative load, unavoidably.**

No new technical content is introduced. Phase 2 is retrieval of known material; phase 3 defends work already produced; phase 4 is the only instruction and it is conceptual rather than procedural. This is the lowest-interactivity day in Q1 and that is a design decision: the day's demands are affective and social, and those consume capacity that would otherwise be available for content.

The load that matters is **evaluative threat**, which is at its maximum — an assessed oral defence, in the first quarter, on the module's accumulated work. Management:

- **The artefact is already written.** Nothing is produced under time pressure.
- **The defence is one element, chosen by the assessor from work the student knows.** No preparation asymmetry.
- **Groups of four with individual answers,** which reduces the exposure of a solo viva without diluting individual evidence.
- **The five questions are published in advance**, in these words. There is no ambush, and the absence of ambush is stated.
- **The unassisted segment's task and terms are published in advance**, in the same way and for the same reason, and the element it uses is the student's own. Nothing is introduced that the student has not already worked on.
- **The unassisted segment comes first**, before the defence, so that a student who finds it hard does not carry twenty minutes of accumulated evaluative threat into the conversation that is actually assessed.
- **Tokens are available** on the artefact, so a specification failure is recoverable and known to be.
- **Phase 4 and 5 follow the assessment**, not the reverse, so that the clinic is not conducted under anticipation of being assessed.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *An extended cumulative retrieval spanning the whole module,* unblocked, at gaps of two to nine days.
- *The two synthesis items* at phase 2, which require the module to have integrated.
- *Assessed disclosure,* phase 3's last two questions. Genuinely difficult and genuinely assessed.
- *Anonymous belief measurement,* phase 1 items 1 to 3, which asks students to record a possibly unflattering opinion of the institution they are attending.
- *The phase 8 falsification question* — what would have to happen for you to stop believing the firewall — which requires stating the conditions under which one's own trust would break.
- *Twenty minutes of unaided work on one's own artefact,* which for many students is the first sustained unaided technical work of the quarter and is deliberately not softened.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *New content.* Refused entirely.
- *Compulsory clinic disclosure.* No student is called on in phase 5. A norm produced by compulsion is compliance and would invalidate the instrument as well as the norm.
- *Unpublished defence questions.* Refused; surprise would convert the defence into a threat display.
- *An unassisted task the student has not seen.* Refused. The segment measures what a student can do unaided on work they already understand; making it a fresh problem would measure problem novelty and unaided work together and separate neither.
- *A longer or graded unassisted segment.* Refused. Twenty minutes is short enough that failure is recoverable within the day and long enough that a student who cannot start learns something. Grading it would convert a mirror into a threat.
- *Ranking or comparative assessment.* Refused per the programme's assessment position.
- *Any technical difficulty.* The day would not survive it.

**The strong-form scope statement, and where it binds.** The day teaches a simplification: that there are two places, one where disclosure is assessed and one where nothing is recorded, and that knowing which you are in is what makes disclosure safe. **That is safe as a guide to conduct inside this programme, and it is unsafe as a model of institutions in general.** Most organisations have no such boundary, most have no mechanism, and a graduate who assumes a firewall exists because one existed here will disclose into an environment that has not paid for the commitment. The boundary is stated to students at phase 4 point 4, where the firewall's cost is named as the reason it is credible — **a commitment nobody paid for is not one you can rely on** — and it is bound to the artefact at phase 8, where the student must name a specific observable event that would end their belief. A falsification statement that names no observable event is the checkable form of failing to hold the boundary, and it is recorded rather than passed.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day's mechanism is **an institution visibly paying a cost**, and it is the only day in the programme designed around that.

The costs are real and are named to the cohort. The assessor gives up information they would find useful — a student's clinic disclosure would materially improve an assessment judgement, and the firewall forbids using it. The programme director opens phase 5 by describing a decision about this programme that they now believe was wrong, in front of students who are subject to that decision. The board is photographed and destroyed in the room. None of these is a gesture; each has a cost, and the costs are what make the norm credible.

This addresses relatedness in the specific sense the programme needs: not warmth, but the belief that the institution's stated commitments are load-bearing. A student who believes that will disclose; a student who believes the commitments are marketing will not, regardless of how supportive the environment feels.

Competence is supported by phase 3's published questions and by phase 6's named positive example, which establishes that the assessed conversation rewards the disclosure it asks for. Without phase 6, phase 3's last three questions would be experienced as traps.

Autonomy is protected by the voluntary structure of phase 5 and by the anonymity of phase 1.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five anonymous items measuring disclosure intention with and without an assessor present, belief in the firewall, a prediction about the room, and a self-prediction about the unassisted segment. The item 1 / item 2 gap is the day's primary instrument and the cleanest available operationalisation of the norm's status. Anonymity is necessary for validity and is itself a small demonstration. **Item 5 is the module's frontier item turned around**: on four previous days students predicted where the agent would be wrong, and here they predict where they will be, which is the only version of the question that the unassisted segment can settle.

**Phase 2 (30 min).** Extended cumulative retrieval, unblocked, with two synthesis items. Longer than the standard phase 2 because it is the module's integration check and because the day has capacity to spare. The fifth item — what makes an instructions file defective, and what makes it inert — is the module's second register retrieved cold, and it is the item the design expects to be answered best, because it is the only one the cohort learned from a failure rather than from an instructor.

**Phase 3 (75 min).** Twenty minutes unassisted, then the assessment occasion. The five published questions are ordered so that the hard ones come last, after the student has succeeded at the two easy ones, and a fifth has been added on the intervention record because an artefact element nobody is ever asked about is a form. Per rater discipline rule 7, two assessors form a provisional level from the artefact independently and record it before the conversation, and the defence is used to confirm, revise or overturn it; the artefact records completed work rather than a prospective decision, so rule 7's amendment does not bind here. Nothing is carried forward from a prior occasion. **The firewall explicitly does not apply here and this is stated before the phase begins**, which is the day's most important single sentence: an unbounded safety claim would be disbelieved, and a bounded one is checkable.

**Phase 4 (40 min).** The only direct instruction on psychological safety and error management in Q1. Point 2's correction — that safe teams report more errors rather than fewer — is included because the opposite belief is common and makes the whole apparatus look like coddling. Point 4 names the firewall's cost to assessors, because a commitment presented as costless is not a commitment. Point 7 names the dependency risk that the unassisted segment exists for, and does so after the segment rather than before, so that students have the experience before the rationale. Point 8 is the day's perishable content and its named default, and it draws the firewall's boundary in the one register students can check: the tooling keeps local records, the programme does not read them, and that is a policy rather than a mechanism. Assessor absent.

**Phase 5 (60 min).** Double-length clinic, opened by the programme director, ordered by descending seniority. The seniority ordering is the phase's core design decision: disclosure cost is inversely related to standing, so the person with most to lose goes first. The instructor's disclosure is required to be an agent failure that survived review, closing the module's series of four such cases with the only one that carries personal cost to its discloser. The board's destruction is theatre with a purpose — it is the only part of the mechanism students can watch.

**Phase 6 (30 min).** The demonstration. The assessor states what they assessed, states their own ignorance of phases 4 and 5, states that they have not seen and will not see any unassisted segment, and names one persuasive phase 3 disclosure with permission. The fourth element is what prevents students from concluding that all disclosure is risky; it draws the boundary from the positive side. The third element exists because the unassisted comparison would otherwise be the only commitment on the day made in a document and not in the room, and a commitment students read is weaker evidence than one they watch being made.

**Phase 7 (20 min).** Brief, no artefact. The most useful thing learned from someone else's error. This is the argument for disclosure made by its beneficiaries rather than by the institution.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Instruments resolved and shown. **The commitment to re-ask at S020, S030 and S040 is stated,** which converts a one-off measurement into a series and tells students the programme intends to be held to this. The falsification question closes the day.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student from a professional culture with genuine blameless-review practice will find phase 4 familiar and may find the firewall apparatus excessive. Predicted failure: they disclose freely, which is good, and model a level of comfort that less-experienced students read as bravado rather than as an available option. Countermeasure: the seniority ordering means staff disclose first, so the modelling comes from people whose standing makes it legible as institutional rather than personal confidence.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** A student from an educational or professional culture where admitting error has been punished will not disclose, will report low firewall belief at item 3, and will experience phase 5 as a trap being set slowly. This is the day's hardest case and it is not solvable in one day. Countermeasures: no student is called on; the phase 1 instrument is anonymous so that non-belief is expressible without cost; and the S020, S030 and S040 repetitions mean the design expects belief to lag and has said so. The design's honest position is that this student is served by the series, not by the day.

**Phase 2 is too easy.** The five factual items are straightforward for students who kept up. The two synthesis items are not, and the second — name a case where an invariant, a seam and a test are three views of one thing — is answered well by a minority. Fallback: groups that answer it are asked for a case where the three views *conflict*, which is genuinely open and previews S034.

**Affective failure.** The day's characteristic failure is **the demonstration read as manipulation**. A sufficiently sceptical student sees an institution performing trustworthiness — an absent assessor, a destroyed board, a director's confession — and concludes that the performance is the point and the reality is unchanged. This reading is not unreasonable and cannot be argued away, since every counter-argument is more performance. The only available countermeasure is that the mechanism is checkable over time: the assessor's ignorance of phase 5 will either hold or be observed to fail, across ten quarters. The design's position is that scepticism at S010 is appropriate, that item 3's belief rate should be low, and that a high rate at S010 would be more worrying than a low one.

**The unassisted segment lands as a verdict.** The predicted failure is a student who cannot start, concludes in twenty minutes that they have learned nothing all quarter, and carries that into a defence that begins ten minutes later. This is the day's most serious new risk and the countermeasures are structural rather than reassuring: the task is on work the student has already done and understands; it is bounded at twenty minutes and stops; the terms are published in advance and state in plain words that it cannot cause them to fail anything; it precedes the defence rather than following it, so a bad twenty minutes is not the last thing before an assessment; and the result is returned days later by somebody who is not assessing them. The instructor states, before the segment begins, that the design expects this to be hard for most of the room and that the expectation is the reason the occasion exists. **What the design cannot do is make it not feel like a verdict**, and §8 objection 3 is where that is taken seriously.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by S020, S030, S040 (the phase 1 instrument re-administered identically), S032 (errors you cannot reproduce), S080 (blameless forensics on a real incident, which is this day's content at complexity class 6), S085 and S090.

S080 is the long-range target and the relationship is explicit: S010 establishes the conditions under which blameless review is possible; S080 conducts one on a real incident with real consequences. A cohort that does not believe the firewall at S010 cannot do S080 honestly.

The unassisted segment has its own two-point forward schedule, at [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md), and it is not a retrieval schedule. **It is a series, and the series is the instrument** — one occasion produces a number nobody can interpret, three produce a direction the student can act on. Both later occasions carry the artefact element that consumes the previous result, per instrumentation rule 6, and both are named here so that the deferral is checkable rather than assumed.

Retained content: the firewall's terms, the distinction between assessed and unassessed disclosure, and the point that safe teams report more errors. Three items.

Own schedule: the norm is not retrieved on a spaced schedule; it is enacted at every subsequent clinic, which is a denser and more appropriate reinforcement than any retrieval set.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**Assessment-bearing, no entrustment judgement.** Per the [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md), no supervision-level rating is made anywhere in Q1.

Two outputs are assessed. The **module artefact**, pass/fail against specification with tokens. The **phase 3 defence**, a specification decision on whether the student can state what their work claims, what would falsify it, where they were wrong, and what an intervention-record entry would have cost them had they missed it. Eight elements are checked, each binary, and the list ends *nothing else is assessed*; the treatment of that sentence on a pre-[S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md) day is argued at [WP-006](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-006.md) §7.

The defence's third question is the one that carries the programme's intent. A student with a flawless artefact who cannot identify anywhere they were wrong during the module does not pass it, and this is stated in the published questions. That is a defensible position only because the firewall makes the honest answer safe in the one place where it is asked and assessed — and phase 6's named example proves the reward is real.

### The unassisted-work provision, and why it is built this way

**What it is.** Twenty minutes at the start of phase 3. One element of the student's own artefact, one defect introduced into it, no assistance of any kind. The student states what the element claims, names what the defect breaks, and writes the correction by hand. The same element exists in assisted form in the artefact, so the two are comparable on the same content.

**Who sees it.** A member of staff who is not the student's assessor marks it and returns it to the student in writing, separately from the phase 3 outcome and on a different day, so the two never arrive together. The programme receives a cohort aggregate. The assessor sees neither, and says so out loud at phase 6.

**What it decides.** Nothing. The [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) requires that the comparison not be used to award a level. On this day that requirement would be satisfied trivially, because Q1 awards no levels at all, and a commitment that is satisfied by the calendar is not a commitment. **The operative form is therefore the stronger one: the comparison contributes to no specification decision either, here or anywhere.** It cannot cause a student to fail anything. There is no threshold, no pass mark, no record in a file the institution keeps about them.

**Why it is not assessed, stated as an argument rather than as a kindness.** Three reasons, in increasing order of force.

First, it would not be a valid assessment. Twenty minutes on one element, once, is a single observation by a single observer, which rater discipline rule 1 forbids as the basis of any judgement in this programme. Assessing it would require the programme to violate its own stated position about what evidence supports a decision, on the day it is demonstrating that its stated positions are load-bearing.

Second, assessing it would destroy the measurement. A student who believes the segment counts will prepare for it, and preparing for it is the one thing that makes the comparison meaningless — the quantity of interest is what they can do unaided *now*, not what they can do unaided after revising for an unaided test. The instrument is only informative while it is not an occasion.

Third, and decisively: **the segment's purpose is to give the student information, not to give the institution information.** The programme already knows the cohort-level answer will be uncomfortable. The student does not know their own, has no way to find out, and has two and a half years in which the answer is still changeable. An institution that took that information and used it to make a decision would have converted a mirror into a gate, and would have got a worse mirror in exchange, for the reason in the second point.

**What the design does not claim about it.** It does not claim that three occasions across three years detect dependency; whether three is enough is unknown and the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) records it as unknown. It does not claim that twenty minutes on one element generalises to a student's unaided capability. It does not claim the comparison is fair between a student whose element was a recursion argument and one whose element was an instructions file. **It claims exactly one thing: that a student who has never worked unaided cannot have an opinion about working unaided, and that the programme should not let three years pass without giving them one.**

Programme instrumentation: the phase 1 instrument's first four items, re-administered identically at S020, S030 and S040, are the psychological-safety series' primary measure; phase 5 disclosure counts by role are the seniority-ordering check; the phase 8 falsification statements are read in aggregate to identify what students think would break the norm, which is design intelligence the programme cannot obtain any other way; and **the unassisted comparison is the first of three points in a per-student series read only by the student, with the cohort aggregate read by the programme.** Per instrumentation rule 9 the readers are named and are different for the two products. Per instrumentation rule 4 the aggregate has a branch: a cohort whose unassisted work diverges sharply from its assisted work at S010 has the [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md) segment lengthened and its comparison returned with a structured self-review, and S045 carries the artefact element that consumes the result, per rule 6.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the firewall is unverifiable, and the demonstration is therefore theatre.**

At its strongest: students cannot verify that the assessor was not told. They observe an absence from a room and a photograph being destroyed, neither of which constrains a conversation in a staff office the following week. The entire apparatus rests on the institution's word, dressed in observable ritual to make the word feel like evidence. And because the design deliberately stages the ritual on the highest-stakes day, it is optimised for persuasive effect — which is precisely the signature of theatre rather than of mechanism.

The rebuttal concedes the verification point entirely and disputes what follows from it. The firewall is unverifiable in principle: no institutional confidentiality commitment can be verified by those it protects, in any setting, ever. That is true of medical confidentiality, of anonymous reporting systems, and of every professional analogue this programme draws on. The question is not whether it can be verified but whether the arrangement makes violation costly and detectable enough to be unlikely. Physical absence means violation requires a deliberate act by a named person; the destroyed board means there is no artefact to leak; and the assessor's public statement of their own ignorance means a later violation would contradict something they said in front of thirty witnesses.

The concession that remains is real and is not small. Everything above raises the cost of violation and none of it prevents violation. Students who withhold on that basis are behaving rationally, and the design's position — that item 3's belief rate should be *low* at S010 and should rise across the series — is an admission that the day does not accomplish what a single demonstration would have to accomplish to make the norm true.

**Objection 2: the phase 1 instrument is contaminated by the setting in which it is administered.**

At its strongest: students are asked, in a room controlled by the institution, on an assessment-bearing day, using materials the institution supplies, whether they trust the institution. Anonymity is itself an institutional promise of the same kind as the firewall, and a student who disbelieves the firewall has no principled reason to believe the anonymity. The instrument therefore measures belief in anonymity as much as belief in the firewall, and its results will be biased toward whatever students think it is safe to record. Worse, the design intends to read a *rising* belief rate across S020 to S040 as evidence the norm is taking hold, when it is equally consistent with students learning what the institution wants to hear.

This objection the design does not fully answer, and it is the more damaging of the two because it attacks the measurement rather than the mechanism. The countermeasures available are weak. Anonymity is procedural — responses are unsigned and aggregated before any staff member sees them — and it is subject to exactly the objection raised. The item 1 / item 2 *gap* is somewhat more robust than either item alone, since a student managing their impression would plausibly minimise both, and a gap is harder to fake in a consistent direction. But this is an argument that one derived quantity is less contaminated than its components, not that it is clean.

The honest position is that the design has no uncontaminated instrument for the thing it most needs to measure, and that an external measurement — administered by a party with no assessment relationship to the cohort — would be materially better and is not currently arranged. That is a concrete and unimplemented improvement, recorded here rather than resolved.

**Objection 3: the unassisted provision introduces a third category on the one day whose entire force comes from there being two.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn and it is the most damaging of the three, because it attacks the day's central mechanism using the day's own argument. S010 works by drawing a line that a student can see: phase 3 is assessed, phases 4, 5 and 7 are not recorded at all, and knowing which side you are on is what makes honest disclosure possible. The whole apparatus — the physical absence, the destroyed board, the stated exceptions — exists to make that line unambiguous, and phase 4 point 3 tells students explicitly that ambiguity about consequence is what kills disclosure.

**The unassisted segment is on neither side.** It is recorded, marked, returned, and aggregated — and it is not assessed. That is precisely the ambiguous middle the day spends six hours denying exists, introduced in the first twenty minutes of the assessed phase, on the student's own assessed artefact, by the same institution making the other two promises. A student who is sceptical of the firewall has no reason to be less sceptical of this, and now has a concrete instance to point at: *you told me there are two places, and then you made a third one.* Worse, the third one is the only one that produces a document about their individual competence, which is exactly the shape of the thing they are being asked to trust nobody will read. The design has, on its most carefully constructed day, weakened the construction.

**The design concedes this, and concedes it substantially.** The category is real, it is new, and it is not resolvable by explanation — every explanation is another institutional assurance, which is the regress §8 objection 1 already identified. The mitigations are the same two devices used elsewhere and are subject to the same limits: a bounded scope, and a physical separation between the person who sees the work and the person who judges you. Both raise the cost of a violation and neither prevents one.

**The cheap fix, named.** The alternative the design considered and did not take is the cheapest available and would remove the objection almost entirely: **let the student mark it themselves.** Give them the defect, give them the model answer in a sealed envelope, let them do the comparison in the room, and have the programme collect nothing but a show of hands. That is one page of printing, no staff time, no third category, and no document about an individual anywhere.

**Why it was not taken, stated as a trade rather than as a refutation.** Self-marking on the day removes the aggregate the programme needs to know whether the provision is worth running at all, and it removes the only signal that would trigger the S045 branch in §7. It also asks a student who has just discovered they cannot do something unaided to score themselves on it immediately, which the design regards as a worse affective outcome than a marked return three days later from somebody who is not assessing them. The trade is therefore: **one document about an individual, held by a non-assessor, in exchange for an aggregate the programme can act on and a gentler return.** The design believes that is the right trade and records that it is a trade, that the objection is not answered by making it, and that if the phase 1 item 3 belief rate falls at S020 relative to a cohort run without the segment, the self-marking version should replace this one.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At S010, the item 1 / item 2 gap will be large, and item 3's firewall-belief rate will be below half the cohort. *Instrument:* the anonymous phase 1 instrument. **A high belief rate at S010 would indicate acquiescence rather than trust and would be treated as an instrument failure, not a success.**

**Prediction 2.** The item 1 / item 2 gap will narrow across S010, S020, S030 and S040, and will narrow faster than item 3's belief rate rises. *Instrument:* the identical instrument at four points. The differential is the interesting quantity: behaviour changing ahead of stated belief is the expected signature of a norm forming through observation.

**Prediction 3.** Phase 5 disclosure counts by role will show student disclosures rising across the quarter's clinics while staff disclosures remain flat, and the rise will begin after S010 rather than before it. *Instrument:* clinic disclosure counts by role at S006 to S010 and across Q2.

**Prediction 4.** Students whose phase 8 falsification statements name a *specific observable event* will show higher subsequent clinic disclosure than those whose statements are general. *Instrument:* phase 8 statements coded for specificity, against later clinic participation. Weak and correlational, and offered as such.

**Prediction 5.** Phase 1 item 3's firewall-belief rate at S020 will not be lower than it would have been without the unassisted segment. *Instrument:* item 3 belief rates at S010 and S020, compared against the first cohort run before the provision existed, and — where no such cohort is available — against the free-text falsification statements at phase 8 coded for whether they name the unassisted segment as a reason for doubt. **This is the §8 objection 3 test.** If students name the segment when asked what would break their trust, the third category is doing the damage the objection predicts, and the self-marking alternative replaces it.

**Prediction 6.** The gap between assisted and unassisted work will be largest on the element requiring a claim to be *stated*, and smallest on the element requiring a correction to be *written*. *Instrument:* the unassisted segment's three parts scored separately, in aggregate only. **This is the prediction the design most wants to be wrong about**, because the reverse ordering — students who can state claims and cannot write corrections — would mean the module taught a vocabulary and not a practice, and would put the entire M02 design in question rather than only its unassisted provision.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That everything the programme does for three years rests on you reporting accurately what you did badly, that people disclose according to what they believe the consequence is rather than what the policy says, and that the belief is formed by watching the highest-risk case — which is why the firewall is demonstrated on the day your assessment is live and the assessor is in the building.

**What the evidence licenses.** Psychological safety, error management, narrative assessment and the entrustment framework are **class 1**, verified here with notes cited. Evaluative threat, modelling by high-status actors and the contamination of self-report administered by an interested party are **class 2**, direction and mechanism only — including the point that safer teams report more errors, which is a direction and never a rate. The dependency risk behind the unassisted segment is asserted as a risk and not a finding: **no source in this repository measures skill loss from agent-assisted work.** The firewall's design is **class 3**.

**What to do with it.**

- **Know which side of the line you are speaking on.** Phase 3 is assessed; phases 4, 5 and 7 are not recorded at all, and that boundary is the thing that makes honesty affordable.
- **Do not export the firewall.** Most organisations have no such boundary and nobody there has paid for one.
- **Name an observable event that would end your belief.** A falsification statement naming no event is not one, and is recorded rather than passed.
- **The editor's local records are a configured control, not a guarantee.** The programme's undertaking not to read them is a policy rather than a mechanism, and keeping that distinction is exactly what the five classes on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md) are for.

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes that the unassisted segment introduces a **third** category on the one day whose entire force comes from there being two — recorded, marked and returned, yet not assessed — and that it is the only one producing a document about your individual competence. It names the self-marking alternative that would remove the problem, states that it made a trade instead, and accepts that the objection is not answered by making it.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** The psychological-safety position taught at phase 4 — the definition as a *shared* belief that a team is safe for *interpersonal risk taking*, its discriminant separation from team efficacy, and learning behaviour as the mediator that makes the construct observable — and the error-management-versus-error-avoidance distinction, are grounded in [research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md) and [research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md). The assessment posture — narrative evidence, no ranking, dispositions observed within activities — and the assessor-reasoning findings that motivate the firewall's necessity are drawn from [research/08-assessment-epas/govaerts-narrative-assessment-literature.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/govaerts-narrative-assessment-literature.md) and [research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md). Blameless review practice follows [research/05-fde-craft/](/research/05-fde-craft/collected-materials.md).

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** Retrieval practice at expanding intervals and cumulative unblocked retrieval, evaluative threat and its effect on available working memory, vicarious learning and modelling by high-status actors, the influence of observed institutional behaviour over stated policy in forming trust, and the general contamination of self-report administered by an interested party are cited for **direction and mechanism only**. No effect size is asserted for any of them. **Phase 4 point 2's finding — that safer teams report more errors rather than fewer — belongs here and not above.** It is canonical in the psychological-safety literature, and the note this repository holds is the 1999 team-learning paper, which records error discussion as one of the learning behaviours safety enables and **does not report a higher error rate in safer teams**. The point is therefore cited for direction and mechanism only, no magnitude is asserted, and the day's material is worded so that it claims the direction and nothing more. The dependency risk the unassisted provision addresses is asserted as a risk and not as a finding: **no source in this repository measures skill loss from agent-assisted work, and none is cited as doing so.**

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.**

*What the documentation says.* Visual Studio Code exposes an agent debug panel, opened from the command palette or from the Chat view's overflow menu, for inspecting customization discovery and agent behaviour, and a chat history through which previous sessions can be reopened by title, message preview or last-updated time. The chat customization diagnostics view lists all loaded instruction files, prompt files, custom agents and skills with any errors. Both are local surfaces available to the person using the editor.

*Version.* Visual Studio Code agent-customization and troubleshooting pages carrying a page-edit date of 15 July 2026, cited as at that date. No page consulted states a product version.

*What it does not report that a reader would need.* **The documentation describes these records as tools for the developer and says nothing about what an institution can collect from them**, because that is not a question about the product. It is, however, exactly the question a student on this day needs answered, and the programme fills the silence with a policy — it does not collect, read or request them — which is a commitment of the same unverifiable kind as the firewall and is described that way at phase 4 point 8. **What a vendor retains under its own terms is a further silence in this paper**: the programme has not verified it and therefore does not summarise it, and a design that taught students to demand evidence and then paraphrased an unread terms document would be teaching the opposite of its subject.

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The decision to spend the first assessment-bearing day on a disclosure norm; the firewall's exact wording and its stated exceptions; the physical removal of the assessor and the destruction of the board; the sharp phase 3 / phase 5 boundary and the decision to make assessed disclosure explicitly assessable; the five published defence questions and the requirement that a student identify where they were wrong; the descending-seniority disclosure order and the requirement that the instructor's case be an agent failure; phase 6's named positive example and its third statement; the five-item instrument and the repetition of its first four items at S020, S030 and S040; the phase 8 falsification question; **the unassisted segment's length, placement, task, marking arrangement, return path and complete exclusion from every decision, together with the argument in §7 for why it is not assessed**; and all six §9 predictions are this design's own reasoning, untested. The §8 objection-2 concession identifies an unimplemented improvement — externally administered measurement of firewall belief — that the design accepts it needs and has not arranged. The §8 objection-3 concession accepts that the provision weakens the day's central distinction, names the self-marking alternative that would remove the problem, states the trade that was made instead, and names the condition under which the alternative should replace it.
