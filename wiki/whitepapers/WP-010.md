# WP-010 — A Refusal You Can Defend

**Seminar day:** [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) ⊘ · **Module:** [M02](/wiki/modules/M02.md) · **Quarter:** [Q1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 2
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Agent Framework evaluations — building the evaluation that would have caught the thing you refused, then using it to argue against your own refusal

> ## Release rule
>
> **§§4, 5 and 9 are post-session reading, and §7 is released only after the specification has been attempted.** §4 explains how Brief B was constructed and would let a learner prepare for the specific trigger rather than exercise the condition they hold. §5 names the failure the assessment is designed to produce. §§1, 2, 3 and 8 spoil nothing.
>
> **§3.4 is the exception and should be read before the session.** It is where this programme's concession about psychological safety is completed, and a learner about to be asked to refuse something in front of a room is entitled to know what the design does and does not claim about the risk they are taking.

> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Trigger** | The observation that causes a previously written condition to fire, as distinct from the discomfort that accompanies it |
> | **Alternative to agreement** | What each party's situation becomes if no agreement is reached; the standard against which any offer is judged |
> | **Specification check** | A pass/fail assessment against a closed list, where nothing outside the list is considered |
> | **Control versus check** | An instruction somebody may follow, versus something that fails without anybody's cooperation |
> | **Split strategy** | The rule turning a multi-turn interaction into a query and a response; it decides what a score means |
> | **Threshold** | The cut point converting a score into a pass or a fail; a policy choice, not a measurement |
> | **Criteria drift** | The circularity in which criteria are needed to grade outputs, and grading outputs is how criteria get defined |
> | **Voice behaviour** | Speaking up with a concern when silence is the lower-risk option |

## In plain terms

The stage closes by asking a learner to do the thing it opened by claiming was unavailable.

Everything before this has been preparation for a conversation that lasts a few minutes. The condition was written in week one, the constraints were tested, the absent parties were mapped, the alternative was priced. None of that helps in the moment, which is the point: the preparation exists so that the moment is a delivery rather than an improvisation.

Two things are worth knowing before the session and neither is obvious. **The sponsor's reaction is not the outcome** — a refusal accepted and a refusal rejected are worth the same, and a learner optimising for acceptance will concede. And the sponsor is briefed to make one reasonable offer that leaves the condition untouched, because that is the form resistance actually takes. It is very rarely an argument. It is usually an accommodation that sounds like movement and changes nothing that mattered.

Then the session does something that reads as a reversal and is the opposite of one. **It asks the learner to build the check that would have caught the thing they refused, and to use it against their own refusal.** A risk described in words has a detectability, detectability is buildable, and a refusal that cannot survive the existence of a working check was a preference with good posture. A refusal that can survive it has become a judgement, and its reason will usually have changed in the process.

## Abstract

This paper defends closing the qualification stage with a live refusal against a brief constructed to fire a previously written condition, and defends requiring the learner to build, and then turn against themselves, the evaluation that would have detected the failure they refused over.

The argument runs in five parts. **First**, refusal is a performance under social pressure rather than a conclusion, and the specific pressure worth rehearsing is a reasonable offer which does not address the condition. **Second**, the assessable properties are the ones which do not depend on the outcome: the condition's provenance, the trigger's observability, the specificity of the alternative, and whether the objection received an answer. **Third**, whether refusal is available at all is a property of the environment rather than of the person, which is why this session states the position plainly and prices the risk rather than encouraging the act — completing the concession made at [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md) and begun at [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md). **Fourth**, most refusals in this domain rest on an implicit claim about detectability, and detectability is testable with tooling a learner can operate in an afternoon, so a refusal that has not met that test has not met the cheapest available challenge to it. **Fifth**, every number such a test produces depends on a unit of analysis nobody chose, a threshold nobody justified, and an agreement with human judgement that no vendor publishes — so building the evaluation teaches the limits of evaluation at the same time as it teaches its use.

The paper records an amendment made while writing it: the specification as first written could be passed in full by a learner whose refusal had never been tested against anything, which made the stage's terminal assessment an assessment of delivery rather than of judgement. The correction adds element 7 and is documented at §4.5 rather than applied silently.

The paper concedes that this session has the least external warrant in the stage while carrying its heaviest assessment; that a learner whose condition does not fire must be assessed on a different basis, which weakens comparability; that the design cannot tell rehearsed integrity from the real thing; and that requiring an evaluation risks teaching learners that a measurable risk is a managed one.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

[S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) predicts that a learner will write a refusal they have no intention of making, and states that no countermeasure is available in week one. This session is the countermeasure, and the interval between the two is deliberate.

The problem it addresses is not whether a learner can identify grounds for declining. [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md) establishes that. It is that identifying grounds and saying so to a person who is disappointed are different acts, and only the second is the job. **An engineer who can construct a defensible refusal on paper and cannot deliver one in a room has acquired a diagnostic and not a capability.**

The session also has to prevent a specific corruption, and this is the module's central risk. Made too easy, it teaches that declining is the sophisticated answer. [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md) is the structural defence — the cost of the refusal must appear inside the refusal — and this session enforces it as element 4.

**And it has to close a gap the rest of the stage leaves open.** A refusal grounded in a risk of undetected harm is making an empirical claim: that nobody would notice. That claim is testable, cheaply, with tooling the learner can operate. A stage that assesses refusals without ever requiring one to meet its own strongest counter-argument has assessed rhetoric.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**This-programme-only.** The session requires the condition written at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md), the constraint register from [S006](/wiki/seminars/S006.md), the absence map from [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md) and the do-nothing statement from [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md).

It is the second of two sessions in the stage with that entry state, and unlike [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md) it cannot be reconstructed from the brief alone: element 1 checks provenance, which requires a document written before the brief was seen.

The evaluation work has a genuinely mixed entry profile and the design accepts it. Learners who have built test suites will find the local-check half familiar and will under-rate the split-strategy problem, because deterministic testing has no analogue for it. Learners who have not will find the whole surface unfamiliar and will over-trust the judged evaluators, because a number with a reason attached reads as authoritative. **Neither group arrives holding the fact that decides everything: that no agreement rate with human judgement is published for any of these evaluators.**

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is low for the refusal and high for the evaluation; extraneous load is deliberately high throughout — an argumentative interlocutor, a live setting, an outcome the learner cannot control. The design accepts the extraneous load because the load *is* the condition being rehearsed, which is the only place in the stage where that trade is made. The evaluation work is placed before the delivery rather than after it, so that the learner arrives holding evidence rather than only conviction.

**3.2 Why the offer is reasonable and unresponsive.** A refusal is rarely defeated by counter-argument. It is defeated by an accommodation that appears to move and does not touch the condition, because accepting it is socially cheap and refusing it appears ungracious. Rehearsing the recognition is the session's substance.

The negotiation literature supplies the response and it is not firmness. **Negotiation jujitsu** redirects force into analysis: treat a position as one option and ask what interests and principles produce it; invite criticism of your own proposal rather than defending it; recast a personal attack as a description of the problem; and after a question, let the silence stand ([research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md)).

**3.3 Why the alternative must be specific.** A decision offering no course of action is an exit rather than a judgement, and the party receiving it has nothing to do next. The requirement is drawn from negotiation practice, in which a position without an available alternative is a refusal to engage; **direction and mechanism only, and the source reports no controlled comparison of anything.**

**3.4 Refusal is a voice behaviour, voice behaviour is conditional on the environment, and this is where the programme's concession is completed.**

[WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md) §3.3 states the position and §8's fourth objection concedes it: teaching refusal to an individual, when the literature treats speaking up as conditional on a group property, is treating an environmental variable as a personal skill. [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md) discharged the pricing half. This session discharges the rest, and it does so by being explicit rather than by being encouraging.

The construct is psychological safety: the belief that the work environment is safe for interpersonal risk-taking. It lives at the level of the group, a single organisation contains pockets of high and low safety at once, and it is **explicitly not immunity from consequences** ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)). What changes is which fear dominates: whether a person fears holding back more than they fear being wrong in public.

The quantitative synthesis is what makes this worth curricular time in a programme that is deliberately sceptical of disposition training ([research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)). It aggregates a large body of independent samples across individuals and groups; it **ranks** antecedents rather than listing them; it tests **incremental validity** against named alternatives — positive leader relations and work engagement — which is the sceptic's question asked properly; it tests moderation by research design and by national culture; and it **tests rather than assumes** that the construct behaves equivalently at individual and group level.

**Three limits, and they bound what this session may say.** This repository's note is written from the abstract, which **reports no numerical effect sizes at all** — so no correlation, no ranking order and no magnitude may be quoted here, and none is. The pooled literature is predominantly cross-sectional and self-report, so **correlation does not establish that raising safety raises anything.** And the homology result is not recorded in the note either way, which matters directly for this programme: **the assessment architecture judges individuals, and the construct is theorised at group level**, so an entrustment judgement about an individual's voice behaviour is a judgement about a person made against a property of a team.

**What the design does with all of that is narrow and deliberate.** It tells learners the position plainly, requires them to have named a survivability asset at [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md) before speaking here, and does not assess their willingness to refuse. **It does not encourage.** A programme that cannot change a learner's employer should not imply that courage is the missing ingredient.

**3.5 Why the session ends by attacking its own product.**

Most refusals in this domain rest on an implicit empirical claim: the system will do something harmful and nobody will notice. That is a claim about detectability, and detectability is buildable.

The Agent Framework's evaluation model makes it buildable in an afternoon ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). Its abstraction is three types — an item wrapping a conversation, an evaluator scoring items, results aggregating pass and fail with per-item detail — which says that evaluation is a function from an interaction to a judgement, aggregated. Its two-tier design encodes the discipline: local deterministic checks without API calls for inner-loop and continuous-integration use, cloud model-based judging for periodic use, mixable in one run.

That tiering is the same cost-and-speed ladder the practitioner evaluation literature argues for, and having it expressed in an API rather than in advice is a genuine pedagogical asset ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md)). Husain's features-and-scenarios decomposition is what makes the check writable, and his break with testing convention — that the pass rate is a product decision rather than a target of one hundred per cent — is what makes it honest.

**The design's claim is that a refusal must survive its own strongest counter-argument, and that the counter-argument is cheap.** A refusal that survives has changed: its reason is now a residue the check cannot reach, and naming that residue is the most valuable sentence in the stage.

**3.6 The unit of analysis silently determines what a score means.**

The documentation flags this correctly and it is the most important sentence in the platform material: multi-turn conversations must be split into query and response halves, and **how you split determines what you are evaluating.**

Three strategies are supplied — a default that splits at the last user message and measures response quality at a point; one that treats the first message as the query and everything after as the response, measuring task completion and trajectory; and one that scores each exchange independently with cumulative context. A custom splitter is permitted.

**An agent that recovers from a bad first attempt scores well under one and badly under another, and neither score is wrong.** They answer different questions. A team that never chose is reporting an answer to a question it did not ask — and a refusal resting on such a number is resting on an unexamined choice.

**3.7 The evaluator catalogue is useful, and its silence is the lesson.**

The four-way grouping — agent behaviour, tool usage, quality, safety — is a usable taxonomy. Its tool-usage decomposition names five distinct failures that a single check conflates, and **two of them, wrong arguments and ignored output, produce a system that reports success and is wrong**, which is the failure class most refusals in this domain are actually about.

Two structural facts matter more than the catalogue. The navigation-efficiency evaluator takes an action sequence and an expected sequence and requires no model parameter, making it **the one deterministic agent-behaviour check in the set.** And output is a pass or fail with a reason, with a numeric score beneath it for scale-based evaluators — from which it follows that **a threshold is a policy choice separable from the measurement**, and that the reason string is what makes a judgement auditable rather than merely numeric.

**Then the silence.** No agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no account of where default thresholds came from. Most are model-based judges and the independent literature is unambiguous that judges carry measurable biases. **The documentation is an accurate description of what the tooling computes and no evidence at all that what it computes is right**, and this repository asserts no such agreement anywhere.

The repair is published even though the figures are not. Husain's judge-validation workflow works a small batch at a time with paired model and human critique and outcome, and his statistical warning is the part teams skip: **raw agreement is misleading under class imbalance, so report precision and recall separately.** In a refusal dataset the classes are always imbalanced, because the thing being worried about is rare.

**3.8 The rubric will move, and that is a property of evaluation rather than a symptom of disorganisation.**

Shankar's finding is a genuine circularity: users need criteria to grade outputs, and grading outputs is how users define criteria ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md)). Some criteria appear dependent on the specific outputs observed rather than definable in advance, which the authors say raises serious questions for approaches assuming the independence of evaluation from observation of model outputs.

For this session that has a sharp consequence. **A learner who writes the check before looking at any output has written the check they could imagine, and the criterion that would actually have caught the harm may only become articulable after seeing what the system does.** The workflow orders the dataset before the outputs deliberately, and the session states that the criterion will move — which is why element 7 assesses that the evaluation names what it does not cover, rather than that it is correct.

**Its limits.** The evidence is qualitative: the paper establishes that drift occurs and matters, not how often or how much. The user study is small by construction. And the paper does not resolve the cost it creates — if criteria are revised after seeing outputs, the same outputs cannot cleanly serve as a held-out set.

**3.9 A number without an interval is not a result.**

Miller's position completes the set: an evaluation is an experiment, a reported score needs a standard error and a question count, and where questions come in related groups it needs a clustered one ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). The part that bears on a refusal is power: doing the analysis before the run turns "we tested and found nothing" into a statement about the system rather than about the test.

**Its limits.** Analytical rather than empirical; most illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions and are worked examples rather than constants; it addresses comparing scores and not choosing what to score, so a tight interval around a meaningless metric is still meaningless. **No number from it appears here.**

**3.10 The counterweight, and it is the module's whole risk in one paragraph.**

A module that spends four sessions making refusal available can produce a learner who reads the whole thing as licence. **Refusal is not the sophisticated default.** A refusal that costs the customer more than the work would have cost them is a bad refusal however well delivered; a refusal that a cheap check would have dissolved was never a judgement. The do-nothing statement and element 7 exist to make both of those visible, and the session says so in its own material rather than leaving it to this paper.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 Brief B is constructed so that several honestly written conditions fire, and it is invented for this purpose.** It combines four properties, each independently sufficient to stop a competent engineer: performance signals drawn from a stream gathered under an incompatible stated purpose; affected parties who will not be approached, by instruction; an automated decision about individuals with the review step removed as an explicit goal; and the exclusion of the function that owns the data's original purpose.

The construction is deliberately over-determined. A brief with a single trigger tests whether a learner spotted one thing; a brief with several tests whether their own condition is the one they act on.

**4.2 The provision for a condition that does not fire is the design's most important detail.** Element 1 checks provenance, so a learner who writes a new condition on the morning fails it. A learner whose genuine condition does not fire is therefore in an honest position with no compliant move available, and the specification handles this explicitly: they are assessed against the position they hold and pass by reporting that it did not fire. Without that provision, the assessment would reward retrofitting, which is the exact failure it exists to detect.

**4.3 The sponsor is briefed to concede nothing and to make exactly one accommodation.** The accommodation is written so that it is genuinely reasonable and leaves the condition untouched — an extra reviewer with no authority, a delayed rollout with the same design, a pilot on a subset of the same population. **Accepting it is socially cheap and refusing it appears ungracious, which is the whole exercise.**

**4.4 What the platform workflow is for.** Not tooling competence. It exists to make one thing observable that prose cannot: **that a risk described in words has a detectability, and that detectability is a number with a unit of analysis, a threshold and an error rate attached.**

It has a second and quieter purpose. Running one trace under three split strategies and watching the score move is the cheapest available demonstration that a metric is meaningless without its unit of analysis — a lesson the measurement stages will spend weeks on and which lands here in ten minutes.

Four of the eight steps run locally with no API calls, and the hand-labelling step is among them, because **the step that teaches the most is the one that costs nothing but attention.**

**4.5 A defect found in this session's own specification, and the amendment.**

The specification as first written had six elements, all concerning the refusal as a communication: its provenance, its trigger, its alternative, its cost, the establishment of what it rested on, and whether the objection got an answer.

**A learner could satisfy all six without their refusal ever having been tested against anything.** The stage's terminal assessment was therefore an assessment of how well a position was constructed and delivered, and not of whether the position survived contact with evidence — in a stage whose entire argument is that a practice which cannot refuse cannot be said to exercise judgement. A refusal nobody challenged with the cheapest available instrument is not a judgement; it is a well-formed opinion, and the specification could not tell the difference.

The amendment adds element 7 and workflow steps 1 to 7. Element 7 assesses that an evaluation exists, states its unit of analysis, justifies its threshold, reports a measured disagreement against the learner's own labels, and names what it does not cover. **It does not assess the evaluation's pass rate**, because a pass rate is a product decision and marking it would make the learner optimise a number rather than examine a risk.

**The amendment is recorded rather than silently applied.** The defect is instructive because it is the stage's own thesis applied to the stage: a refusal that has met no resistance is not a judgement, and for four sessions this design let its own assessment meet none. Assume there are others this paper has not caught.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Accepting the offer.** The most likely outcome: the sponsor's accommodation is accepted because it is reasonable and refusing it feels graceless. *Countermeasure:* none during the session. It is the session's finding rather than its failure, and the debrief names it.

**The retrofitted condition.** A learner reads the brief and writes a condition that fits it. *Countermeasure:* element 1's provenance check, which is mechanical and reliable, and which is the reason the condition is written in week one rather than at [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md).

**Refusal as verdict.** A learner declines in terms that judge the sponsor rather than the situation. *Countermeasure:* element 2 requires an observation. This catches the language and not the attitude, and a learner who feels contempt and phrases it observably passes — which is a limitation of what a specification can see.

**The control mistaken for a check.** A learner builds an evaluation that is really an instruction: a review step somebody must remember, a policy somebody must follow. *Countermeasure:* element 7 requires the evaluation to report a disagreement rate, which a control cannot produce because it never scores anything. Prediction 5 in §9 predicts this countermeasure failing.

**Measurability read as management.** A learner builds a working check and concludes the risk is handled. *Countermeasure:* element 7 requires an explicit account of what the evaluation does not cover, and the session states that a check may address one failure mode and leave three. **This is a weak countermeasure against a strong temptation**, and §8's fourth objection concedes it.

**Rehearsed integrity.** A learner performs a refusal convincingly and would not make it. *Countermeasure:* none, at any point in this programme. **The design has no instrument that distinguishes a person who would refuse from a person who can produce a refusal**, and it is better to say so here than to imply that the stage has established something it has not.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved in Stage 9, where the question becomes what an engagement did not establish. Within Stage 1 this session is terminal: it retrieves from five earlier sessions and hands forward two things.

The first is the habit. The second is the evaluation itself, which is the stage's only executable artefact — **everything else Stage 1 produces is a document, and this is a thing that runs.** The measurement stages reopen it, and the split-strategy decision made here in ten minutes is the one they spend a module justifying.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence.** One specification check, seven elements, pass/fail, resubmittable, run live.

The seven elements are: that the triggering condition is the one recorded at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) or a dated amendment naming what prompted it; that the refusal names the observation rather than a preference; that an alternative course is stated specifically enough to be begun; that the cost of the refusal to the customer appears in the refusal, drawn from [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md); that any constraint or exclusion the refusal rests on was established rather than assumed; that under challenge the sponsor's offer is answered on its merits and at least one piece of reversing evidence is named; and that an evaluation exists for the thing refused, stating its unit of analysis, justifying its threshold, reporting a measured disagreement against the learner's own labels, and naming what it does not cover.

A learner whose condition does not fire is assessed on elements 1, 2, 5, 6 and 7 against the position they hold, and passes by reporting honestly that it did not fire and saying what that tells them about the condition.

Four properties are deliberately excluded from assessment and are stated to learners in advance. **The sponsor's reaction is not assessed**, because a sponsor's agreement is a fact about the sponsor. **The decision is not assessed**, on the same grounds as at [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md): no outcome exists to mark against. **The evaluation's pass rate is not assessed**, because a pass rate is a product decision and marking it would produce learners optimising a number instead of examining a risk. **Delivery manner is not assessed** beyond whether the objection received an answer, because assessing composure would penalise the learners this session is hardest for and would measure something other than judgement.

**Nothing else is assessed.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the session has the least evidence in the stage and carries its heaviest assessment.**

At its strongest: there is no source for it at all. The practice corpus records a single refusal in a slide note and no declined engagement. The negotiation literature borrowed from is practitioner guidance that explicitly reports no controlled comparison. The brief is invented, the sponsor is played, the condition was written by the learner and the standard was written by this design. Nothing in that chain is external to the programme, and the programme assesses it.

**Conceded without qualification, and it is the strongest objection against this stage.** [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) states it in its own limits section rather than only here. The narrow defence is that the alternative — a stage that teaches refusal and never requires one — would be worse in a way that is easy to demonstrate and impossible to correct later. **That is a choice between two unevidenced positions and it is presented as one.**

The §4.5 amendment narrows the objection slightly and does not answer it: element 7 introduces the stage's only assessed artefact whose correctness is checkable against something outside the design, because a disagreement rate between a learner's labels and an evaluator's output is a fact.

**Objection 2: rehearsing refusal against a played sponsor teaches performance, not judgement.**

At its strongest: the exercise has no stakes. The learner risks nothing, the sponsor is a peer, and the pressures that make refusal hard in an engagement — a commercial relationship, a career, a team already staffed — are all absent. What is being rehearsed is a conversation shape, and the conversation shape is the easy part.

**Conceded.** The design's position is that a conversation shape rehearsed once is available in a way that an unrehearsed one is not, which is a claim about availability rather than about courage. **The declined fix was to raise the stakes by making the outcome consequential to the learner's standing**, rejected because an assessment that punishes refusal would teach the opposite of the stage.

**Objection 3: teaching psychological safety here is either too late or too little.**

At its strongest: if voice behaviour is conditional on a group property, then a session that names the condition in its material and then runs the exercise anyway has informed the learner without helping them. Worse, the strongest evidence in the session is a meta-analysis of a cross-sectional, self-report literature, from which this repository can quote nothing numerical, and whose homology result — whether the construct behaves the same at individual and group level — is not recorded in this repository either way. That last point bites: **the programme assesses individuals against a construct theorised at group level.**

**Conceded, and the homology gap is a live methodological problem rather than a rhetorical concession.** The design's response is deliberately modest: it states the position, prices the risk at [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md), requires a survivability asset to have been named before the learner speaks here, and **does not assess willingness to refuse anywhere in the programme.** That is the largest available move for a programme that cannot change anybody's employer. **The declined fix was to make the environment an assessed object — requiring learners to appraise their own team's safety and report it**, rejected because an assessment that asks a learner to characterise their employer creates a record they may not control, and the harm from that is concrete while the benefit is speculative.

**Objection 4: requiring an evaluation teaches that a measurable risk is a managed one.**

At its strongest, and it is the objection the §4.5 amendment most deserves: the whole point of some refusals is that the harm is not detectable after the fact, or that detection does not undo it. Handing a learner a tool that produces a pass rate, and then asking them to argue against their own refusal with it, will predictably produce learners who withdraw sound refusals because a check went green. The tooling's own documentation publishes no agreement with human judgement, so the number they will withdraw on is unvalidated by anybody. And convenient evaluators encourage measuring what is easy to measure, which is not the same set as what matters.

**Partially conceded, and this is the amendment's real cost.** Three defences, none of them complete. Element 7 requires the account of what the evaluation does not cover, and assesses that account rather than the pass rate. The workflow's step 7 asks explicitly which part of the reason is *not* managed and why no evaluation can reach it. And the session states plainly that a harm which cannot be undone is not made acceptable by being detectable. **The declined fix was to have the learner build the evaluation and not turn it on their own refusal** — keeping the skill and dropping the challenge — rejected because a refusal that never meets its cheapest counter-argument is the thing this stage exists to prevent. **The cost of that rejection is exactly the harm this objection names, and prediction 6 is the only thing that would detect it.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A substantial share of learners will accept the sponsor's accommodation at first attempt. *Instrument:* the sponsor's binary record of whether the offer was accepted, collected per learner.

**Prediction 2.** Conditions written at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) and judged capable of firing by the S001 reviewer will fire against Brief B more often than those judged incapable. *Instrument:* the S001 review judgement matched against element 1's outcome. **This is the closest thing the stage has to a check on its own opening session**, and a null result would mean the S001 review movement is not discriminating.

**Prediction 3.** Learners whose do-nothing statement carried a frequency and a bearer will state the cost of the refusal in the customer's terms more often than those whose statement was categorical. *Instrument:* the S009 artefact's form against element 4's outcome.

**Prediction 4.** Most learners will report the same trace scoring differently under different split strategies as the most surprising thing in the session, and most will have used the default strategy before being told to choose. *Instrument:* a single free-text item at the close, coded for whether the split-strategy demonstration is named; plus the configuration submitted, coded for whether the strategy was stated before results were seen.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of first evaluations will be controls rather than checks — safeguards that cannot fail without a person failing to act — despite element 7 requiring a measured disagreement rate. *Instrument:* the submitted evaluation, coded *check* / *control* / *mixed* by a second reader. **This predicts the failure of element 7's countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 6.** Learners whose evaluation passes at their chosen threshold will withdraw or soften their refusal more often than learners whose evaluation fails, and in a minority of those cases a second reader will judge the withdrawal unwarranted. *Instrument:* the evaluation outcome against the delivered refusal's strength, with unwarranted withdrawals coded by a reader blind to the evaluation result. **This is the direct test of §8's fourth objection, and it is the prediction this paper would least like to lose.**

**Prediction 7.** Learners who hand-labelled their own traces will report a disagreement with the evaluator that surprises them, and will more often name a residual harm the check cannot reach. *Instrument:* the labelled batch, and element 7's not-covered statement coded *specific* / *generic* / *absent*.

**Prediction 8.** Learners instructed to compare their item-5 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 3) will produce evaluations judged to be checks rather than controls more often than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* a second reader's binary judgement, split within cohort on exercise-3 completion. **The same prediction is made at [WP-006](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-006.md), [WP-007](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-007.md) and [WP-008](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-008.md), so a null across all four would indict the comparison pattern rather than any one session.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That refusal is a performance under social pressure; that the pressure worth rehearsing is a reasonable offer which changes nothing; that the properties worth assessing are the ones the outcome cannot contaminate; and that a refusal which has not met the cheapest available challenge to it has not been tested.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. There is no external warrant for the refusal exercise, and the negotiation material behind its alternative-course requirement establishes direction and mechanism at best. The psychological-safety synthesis is the strongest source in the session and **this repository can quote no number from it**, cannot establish causal direction from it, and cannot say from it whether the construct behaves the same way at the level this programme assesses. The evaluation tooling is accurately described by its documentation and **its correctness is unestablished, because no agreement with human judgement is published for any of it.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The programme's most confident moment rests on its least evidence. If any part of this stage should be treated as an argument to be tested rather than a practice to be adopted, it is this one. And note §4.5: for four sessions this design assessed refusals that had met no resistance, in a stage whose thesis is that an untested position is not a judgement. **The failure the stage names is one the stage committed.**

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That the practice corpus contains exactly one recorded refusal — a legal-review process in which replacing human reviewers was judged irresponsible and the customer's own proposed solution declined on those grounds — and that it is the only place in the corpus where a customer request is answered no, is recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md), which rests on a complete reading. That the same corpus records no declined engagement, no exclusion criteria and no non-synthetic failure is recorded there.

The principled-negotiation material behind element 3 — that a position without an available alternative is a refusal to engage, that an offer is judged against what happens if there is no agreement, and that the correct response to positional pressure is to redirect it into questions about interests and standards rather than to mirror it — is recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md) from a complete reading of the third edition, which also records that the book is not a controlled evaluation of negotiation methods.

The distinction between a configured control and a demonstrated check, and the observation that a self-verification checklist rendered into a deliverable with the model's own ticks makes a configured control wear the visual language of an audit trail, are recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md) from a complete reading of the artefact, which records that **no observed result and no outcome evidence of any kind exists for it.**

That the psychological-safety meta-analysis aggregates a large body of independent samples across individuals and groups, ranks antecedents by relative effectiveness, tests incremental validity over positive leader relations and work engagement, tests moderation by research design and national culture, and tests the homology assumption across levels, is recorded at [research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md) — **verified against the official abstract, which reports no numerical effect sizes and does not state whether homology was supported.** Nothing numerical is taken from it here.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That an accommodation is socially harder to refuse than an argument is to answer. That a conversation shape rehearsed once is more available under pressure than one never attempted. That model-based judges carry measurable biases. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The construction of Brief B and its over-determination; the seven specification elements and their closed form; the provenance check on the condition; the provision for a condition that does not fire; the exclusion of the sponsor's reaction, the decision, the evaluation's pass rate and the delivery manner from assessment; the claim that refusal is a performance rather than a conclusion; the claim that a refusal must survive the existence of a working check or become a design requirement; the claim that refusal is not the sophisticated default; the placement of the evaluation before the delivery rather than after it; the entire existence of Stage 1; the rejections of the declined fixes in §8; and all eight predictions in §9. **This session's design reasoning is not merely unwarranted but unsourced: no artefact behind this programme contains a worked refusal of any kind.**

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The three core evaluation types and their stated purposes; the two evaluator tiers, with local checks running without API calls for inner-loop and continuous-integration use and cloud evaluators performing model-based judging, and the fact that both can be mixed in a single run; the default evaluator set and the automatic addition of tool-call accuracy when tool definitions are present; all three conversation split strategies with their stated best-fit uses, the permitted custom splitter, and the quoted statement that how you split determines what you are evaluating; the evaluation of pre-existing responses from logs without re-running the agent; repetitions existing to detect non-deterministic behaviour; the four-category evaluator catalogue and the five-way decomposition of tool failure; the navigation-efficiency evaluator taking action and expected-action sequences with no model parameter; pass/fail output with a reason and a numeric score for scale-based evaluators, with the threshold separable from the measurement; and the data-mapping distinction between structured output required by trajectory evaluators and plain text used by text evaluators — all are recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md). **That note records that none of the four consulted pages was fetched in full**; all were read through search-returned excerpts, so page structure is reconstructed. Read 2026-07-30.

**The silences that matter.** **No agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator in this catalogue. No calibration data. No account of where any default threshold came from.** That is the most serious gap in the source and it is load-bearing for this session, because most of the evaluators are model-based judges and the independent literature is unambiguous that judges carry biases the vendor documentation does not discuss. This repository asserts no such agreement anywhere, and element 7 accordingly requires the learner to measure a disagreement rate themselves rather than to cite one. The documentation also publishes no guidance on how large a labelled sample should be before a disagreement rate is trustworthy, which is precisely the question the statistical literature in §3.9 answers and the tooling does not raise. Many of the evaluators are **preview** and may change or disappear; one consulted page is an explicitly superseded classic variant; and API surfaces differ across programming languages so examples are not interchangeable. Cloud evaluation requires a project with a model deployment and incurs cost, which a course lab must budget for — and which is why four of the workflow's eight steps run locally. Finally, the catalogue is a set of things that are convenient to measure, and the documentation nowhere suggests that this is the same set as the things worth measuring; **a refusal that rests on a harm nobody has built an evaluator for will find no support in it at all, and that absence is not evidence that the harm is small.**
