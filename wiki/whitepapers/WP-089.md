# WP-089 — The Handover Artefact and Its Warranty

**Seminar day:** [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) · **Module:** [M18](wiki/modules/M18.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](wiki/quarters/Q9.md)
**Strands:** PE · SY · SG · **Complexity class:** 6
**Platform anchor:** Azure deployment stacks — `actionOnUnmanage` and deny settings as a warranty with enforceable terms — and Foundry and Entra agent identities · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (write) · Foundry MCP Server (preview) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends assembling three days' artefacts into one document with a warranty, and defends the claim that a warranty is a conditional promise whose conditions fall on the receiver. It defends the day's resolution of a contradiction this programme created and did not flag: [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) fails an owner named as a function, and a named person is exactly what a handover does not preserve. The resolution is that ownership has a slot and an occupant, that [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) named the occupant correctly for a live arrangement in which the slot is implicit, and that a handover must carry both with a date and a mechanism by which the artefact learns of a change. The paper defends putting the contradiction to the room unresolved at phase 2 and leaving it open across phase 3. **It defends adding an enforcement mechanism to the day** — a deployment stack whose deny settings and unmanage action are the same four warranty columns expressed in a system — and defends requiring a reconciliation between what the prose promises and what the stack enforces, on the ground that a promise and its mechanism are two artefacts that drift and only one of them is written for a reader. **Section 7 reports the discharge of the [WP-069](wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) contradiction, the [WP-034](wiki/whitepapers/WP-034.md) discoverability obligation and four rule-6 obligations.** It concedes that it makes an untested form a fail condition, that its resolution silently rescopes an earlier day's specification, and that the enforcement mechanism it has added is more persuasive than the resolution it is supposed to illustrate.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The assembly is not the problem. A student holding a bounded safety case, a residual-risk statement and a measured transfer gap can produce a handover document in an hour, and the three-part contract form has been available to them since [S034](wiki/seminars/S034.md).

Three things are hard, and the third is the programme's own fault.

**A warranty with no conditions on the receiver is the natural thing to write and is unmaintainable.** Asked what they guarantee, an engineer states what the system does. Asked what the receiver must do, most of this cohort will write *nothing*, and phase 1 item 3 is constructed to catch that. The belief underneath is that conditions are a hedge and an unconditional guarantee is generous. It is the reverse: an author cannot guarantee behaviour under conditions they neither control nor observe nor will be present for, so an unconditional guarantee is either a promise they cannot keep or one they privately do not intend, and W1's receiving team escalating for four months to a person who had left is what the first looks like.

**The reader is absent in both directions.** [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) established that the reader cannot be asked. This day adds that the author cannot be reached either, which changes what the artefact must contain: every not-met procedure, every ownership, every escalation is a route that must work without the person who wrote it. A document that reads correctly and whose every route terminates in an unreachable name is the module's most dangerous artefact, and it is W3.

**The programme has told these students to do the thing that fails here.** [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s specification fails an owner named as a function rather than a person, because a function cannot bear a cost and cannot be asked a question. That reasoning is sound. [WP-069](wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §6 recognised at the time that it sets up a contradiction with the handover and assigned the resolution here — **"this day does not flag it, and S089 must."** The cohort will feel the conflict the moment phase 2's bridge is asked, because they have all followed [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s rule and are all about to leave. **A programme that resolves its own contradiction by quietly changing the rule teaches that rules are negotiable; one that resolves it by extending the rule, in public, with the earlier position stated as having been correct for its scope, teaches something else.** Which of those two the day actually achieves is §8 objection 2.

**And the whole of it is written in prose about a system that has its own version of it.** This is the practical turn's contribution and it changes what the day can demonstrate. A deployment stack holds a managed set, a deny setting and an unmanage action — what is guaranteed, under what conditions, and what happens when the guarantee stops applying — so the artefact the students are writing has an enforcement counterpart that already exists and that they have never compared it against. **The comparison is the finding.** In three of the four real handovers phase 3 supplies, the prose and the stack disagree, and in three of the four the disagreement was discovered by a failed operation months later. The slot-and-occupant distinction has the same property: **a project's managed identity is a slot, an agent identity carries an owner assigned at creation, and the tenant registry counts the agents whose owner has gone** — so the day's resolution is a distinction the identity system already makes, and the artefact's job is to add the two things the system does not record, which are the date and the mechanism.

**If the disposable subscription or the stack permissions are unavailable** — the deny-setting operations require a role a department may not hold — phase 3's execution leg runs as a read of the four stacks' declared settings rather than as an attempt against them, and phase 5 step 8's stack is **declared and validated but not deployed.** The day states that the mechanism is **demonstrated rather than operated** in that case, the reconciliation table is produced against the declaration rather than against observed behaviour, and **the specification's reconciliation fail conditions are retained** because they concern the presence of the line rather than the truth of it. What is lost is the phase 3 finding that receiving teams believed the prose, which is the stronger half.

**If the receiving party will not commit to obligations at all** — plausible where the receiver is the next cohort, who have no standing to bind themselves to a year of weekly checks — the negotiation runs with the operating staff member who will hold the component in the interval, and the artefact records that the obligations are provisional pending the receiving cohort's acceptance. That is a weaker occasion and is stated as one; it is not a fallback that changes the task.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Low intrinsic and high combinatorial.** Nothing is computed. What is high is the number of cross-references the artefact must maintain: each guarantee has conditions, the conditions include receiver obligations, each obligation is attached to specific guarantees that lapse if it is not met, each ownership has a slot and an occupant and a date and a record, and every [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md) unowned assumption must appear in exactly one of two places. **A student who loses track of a single cross-reference produces a document that is internally inconsistent in a way no reader can detect** — which is the artefact's characteristic defect and is why the warranty is required as a table rather than as prose.

The interactivity peak is between steps 2, 4 and 6. Step 6's negotiation can withdraw a guarantee, which changes step 2's table, which may orphan an assumption resolved at step 4. The design accepts one round of this and sizes step 6's thirty minutes for it. **Step 8's reconciliation is deliberately placed after the negotiation and not inside the loop**: it reads the finished table against the deployed stack and produces one line per row, so it adds a traversal and not another cycle.

Management:

- **All three input artefacts exist and are in front of the student.**
- **The warranty is a four-column table**, supplied, so the cross-reference structure is visible rather than held.
- **Steps 1 and 6 reuse forms the cohort has used at least twice.**
- **Step 8's stack has a supplied skeleton with every setting blank**, so the declaration is not the work and the settings are.
- **The receiving party is present only for the last thirty minutes**, so the artefact exists before it is negotiated.

Expertise reversal applies to step 1 for anyone who has written a service contract. Nothing is done about it; their difficulty is at step 3, which no professional practice supplies.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *A contradiction put to the room and deliberately left open across a whole phase,* phases 2 and 4.
- *Establishing what four real warranties actually enforce, before learning how their handovers went,* phase 3.
- *Writing what the receiver must do before writing what you guarantee,* for the students whose [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) gap ran over-confident.
- *A sealed prediction about which setting the agent will choose for you,* item 8.
- *A negotiation in which the other party may decline and the cost of declining falls on the guarantee,* step 6.
- *Reconciling your own promise against the mechanism that will keep it,* step 8.
- *A document-only partner test six months in the future,* phase 7.
- *An unsupported ownership form and an unsupported discoverability clause,* steps 3 and 5.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *A commercial or legal contract.* Named as out of scope at point 1 in strong form. The engineering content is the day's; liabilities are not.
- *A hostile receiving party.* Good faith throughout, as everywhere in this programme's assessed occasions.
- *A second negotiation round.* Available and declined: a second round would let students converge on an agreeable obligation set, and the day wants the first draft's obligations exposed rather than optimised.
- *Assessing whether the obligations were accepted.* Excluded. A declined obligation with its guarantee withdrawn is a pass, exactly as [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) treated a declined arrangement.
- *Assessing how much of the warranty the stack enforces.* Excluded and stated in the specification. Most of a good warranty is unenforceable by a stack, and rewarding enforcement would produce warranties written to fit a mechanism — which is the construct-instrument substitution [S026](wiki/seminars/S026.md) exists to prevent, applied to a promise.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The three-part contract with a four-part warranty is **safe as the engineering content of a handover between parties inside one organisation, or between organisations already covered by an agreement, where failure is inconvenient rather than injurious and where a reversal path exists.** It is **unsafe as an inter-organisational contract**, because the same sentences carry liabilities the form does not contemplate, because "guarantee" and "not met" have meanings in that setting that this design does not control, and because a student who writes them without counsel has created an exposure they cannot see; and it is **unsafe where the receiving party lacks the capability to meet the obligations the warranty conditions on** — a receiver who cannot run the weekly check will accept the obligation, fail it, and lose guarantees they believe they hold, which is a worse outcome than an unconditional guarantee honestly scoped. In the second case the correct move is to narrow the guarantee rather than to condition it, and the day does not teach how to tell the two cases apart. This is stated to students at phase 4 points 1 and 4.

**A second strong-form scope statement, on the enforcement mechanism.** A deployment stack is **safe as the enforcement of guarantees about the existence, configuration and deletion of resources you have declared, against principals inside the same tenant, on the control plane.** It is **unsafe as enforcement of anything on the data plane** — secrets, blobs, index contents, model behaviour — which is where most of what a receiving team actually cares about lives; **unsafe over resources a managed service created implicitly**, which is most of what a modern component consists of; and **unsafe as a durable exclusion list**, because the exclusion holds at most five principals and supplying more does not error, so a warranty resting on it degrades without any signal at the moment a sixth person is added. **A student who writes *protected by the stack* against a guarantee about data-plane behaviour has applied the simplification outside its range, and step 8's reconciliation table is where that fails**: the line must say *does not enforce*, and an artefact whose reconciliation claims enforcement the mechanism does not provide is a specification failure of the same kind as an invented magnitude at [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md). This is stated to students at phase 4 point 9.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **W3**.

The room is shown a handover artefact that satisfies every rule the programme has taught, written by people who did nothing wrong, whose every ownership is a named person, and all of those people have left. It looks exactly as authoritative as it did the day it was written. The room has followed the same rule, in an assessed artefact, two quarters ago, and is leaving in eleven weeks. **The recognition is immediate and it is about them.**

That is why phase 2's bridge is left unresolved. A room told the answer at phase 2 reads W3 as an illustration of a rule they hold. A room carrying an open question reads W3 as the reason the question is open, and phase 4 point 3 then lands on a felt problem. The cost is forty-five minutes of an unresolved conflict, which some students will find uncomfortable and which the instructor is briefed to name as deliberate rather than to relieve.

Competence is supported by three weeks of inputs and by nothing on the day being new except two forms.

Autonomy is at steps 2 and 6: which guarantees to make, and what to ask of the receiver, are entirely the student's, and the specification checks the structure rather than the content.

Relatedness is the receiving party at step 6 — in most cases the people who will own the component after the cohort leaves, who are being asked to commit to obligations they will actually have to meet.

The affective risk is **the anticlimax of a declined negotiation.** A student whose receiving party declines most obligations watches their warranty shrink to almost nothing in the last thirty minutes of the day, in the last week of the module. Countermeasures: a declined obligation is a pass and is stated as the expected outcome for a substantial share of the cohort; the withdrawal is recorded as a result rather than as damage; phase 8 reports the cohort's declined-obligation total so nobody's is singular; and the instructor states once that a warranty which shrank under negotiation is a warranty that had been describing a receiver who did not exist.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Eight items. Item 3 is constructed so that most of the room writes *nothing*, which phase 3's payload defeats. Item 4 takes the contradiction cold and its answers are collected verbatim for phase 8's read-back. **Item 8 is the sealed Copilot prediction and is deliberately about a default rather than an error**, because the day's subject is terms nobody stated.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Seven retrievals reaching two years to [S015](wiki/seminars/S015.md), oldest scaffolded. The bridge puts the programme's own contradiction to the room, the split is recorded, and **the instructor explicitly declines to resolve it**, naming point 3.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four real handovers with six-month receiving-team records, **and their four stacks reproduced in a disposable resource group and attacked for twenty minutes** to establish what each actually prevents. **The corpus is found rather than commissioned**, unlike [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md)'s and [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md)'s, and its selection bias — toward handovers that generated record, which is toward the ones that went badly — is disclosed per instrumentation rule 3, as is the disposable subscription's own effect on how a deny setting behaves. Payload W4; **W3 stages the contradiction twice, in prose where every owner has left and in a stack whose seven-name exclusion list silently exceeded a five-name limit**, with no culpable party.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Point 3 is the resolution and is the day's obligatory content; **its platform paragraph turns the distinction from an invention into an observation about an identity system the cohort already uses.** Points 1, 7 and 9 carry strong-form scope statements. Point 5 discharges [S034](wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s terminal demand by converting a reader's skill into an author's requirement. **Points 9 and 10 are the perishable platform content and the named defaults, and point 9 carries the documentation silence: the five-principal cap is stated together with the fact that exceeding it does not error, and nothing states what becomes of the entries beyond the fifth.**

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Eight steps with a live negotiation. Step 4 is [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md)'s rule-6 element and the column-order requirement is [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)'s. Steps 3 and 5 unsupported. **Step 8 deploys the stack and reconciles it against the warranty, and the artefact carries a prompt record, an intervention record and a frontier statement.**

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A warranty met exactly, with the component abandoned within a year. No detectable defect anywhere in the document. **Then the agent case: a stack generated with the documentation's own example flag, which deleted a resource group nine months later when the receiving team removed one resource from the Bicep file** — a term about the end of the relationship, reviewed by somebody thinking about the beginning of it.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Document-only partner test, the seventh in the programme and **not compared against the earlier six** per instrumentation rule 2. The first question is [S034](wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s third with its second half added.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 4 against point 3; item 3 against the cohort's obligation count; **item 8 opened and the distribution of agent-chosen unmanage actions reported**; the W1-over-W4 ranking and the prose-versus-stack disagreements found before the records; the [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md) and [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) instruments closed; **the step 8 reconciliation totals**; the declined-obligation total; the [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) kept sentences returned uncollected.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has written or read service contracts will produce a clean warranty table quickly. Predicted failure: **they will produce W2** — a short guarantee and a long exclusion list — because that is the professional norm and because exclusions are the risk-management move their experience rewards. Countermeasure: phase 3's W2 record shows a receiving team that stopped reading and lost two constraints, so the failure mode is adjudicated rather than asserted; and phase 7's partner is asked which guarantee they believe has lapsed, which a W2-shaped artefact cannot answer because it has almost no guarantees to lapse.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is at step 2's second column: a student who cannot think of anything to require of the receiver. Countermeasures: W4 is in front of them with its column populated; step 4's assumption resolution generates obligations mechanically, since an assumption assigned to the receiver is an obligation; and staff answer questions of fact about what the receiving party is capable of, which is a matter of record.

**A new failure mode belonging to the practical turn: the stack becomes the artefact.** Step 8 is the only step on the day that either works or does not, and a student behind on time will finish the stack and leave the discoverability clause thin. Countermeasures: step 8 is last, deliberately, so that what it displaces is itself; the skeleton is supplied; **the specification does not assess how much the stack enforces**, so there is no reward for enforcing more; and staff are briefed that a student who cannot deploy should record the reconciliation against the declaration and say so, which is the same **demonstrated rather than operated** provision §2 states for the whole cohort when permissions fail.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** A group that ranks W4 first before the records is likely, because the two-way column is visible and the cohort has been trained since [S039](wiki/seminars/S039.md) to notice reciprocity. The difficulty is relocated to the final question — *what does W4 cost, and what happens when a receiving party refuses its obligations* — whose answer is that the attached guarantee is withdrawn rather than the obligation waived, and that obligations nobody agreed to are decoration. **Fallback if a group answers that too:** they are asked to write the W3 entry that would have helped, using only what W3's author knew on the day they wrote it — which is point 3's resolution generated rather than received, and which no group has been observed to reach in full.

**Affective failure.** Two.

The first is **the anticlimax at step 6**, described in §3.3 with its countermeasures.

The second is specific to this day's position and is the more serious: **a cohort that reads point 3 as the programme admitting it was wrong.** These students are one week from an entrustment occasion, and a design that visibly corrects itself in the final module can be read either as intellectually honest or as unreliable, and which one it lands as is not fully in the design's control. Countermeasures: the resolution extends [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) rather than reversing it, and the extension is argued from [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s own reasoning; the correction is stated as the design's, not the students'; [WP-069](wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §6 identified the conflict two quarters before this day, and that is shown to the room, so the correction is visibly planned rather than forced; and the instructor states once that the programme's convention, published since [WP-023](wiki/whitepapers/WP-023.md), is to record its own defects rather than repair them silently, and that the cohort has been reading those records for three years. **§8 objection 2 argues that this framing is more generous to the design than the facts support.**

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md) only, at one week, where the artefact is handed over for real, read by the receiving party in front of the assessors, and EA-2 is formed from it.

**That is the whole retention architecture and it is a real limitation.** Every other day in the programme is retrieved at expanding intervals across quarters; this one is retrieved once, seven days later, and then the programme ends. Nothing about this day's content will be measured at any interval the design's own spacing argument regards as informative, and no cohort evidence about its retention can exist. **The design states this rather than listing notional future retrievals.** It is the structural cost of the module's position and it applies to [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md) and [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) most sharply.

The partial compensation is that the day's content is almost entirely assembly of material retrieved at proper intervals elsewhere: the three-part contract from [S034](wiki/seminars/S034.md), the not-met procedure from [S025](wiki/seminars/S025.md), the negotiation from [S039](wiki/seminars/S039.md), the ownership requirement from [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md). **Two things are genuinely new — slot-and-occupant, and the two-way obligation — and those two are the ones with no retention architecture at all.**

Retained content: the warranty's four parts; slot-and-occupant with a date and a record; obligations running both ways with lapse stated; discoverability as an author's requirement; **the gap between a promise and the mechanism that keeps it**; and completion as a receiver capability. Six items.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, and [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md).

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md), where **this artefact is the evidence EA-2's level is formed from**, per rater discipline rule 7.

One specification item in PE390 with CAP390. Per rule 1, the checked elements are the three-part contract in the receiver's language, the four-column warranty table including receiver obligations, a not-met procedure with route and response, and the [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md) floor restated with whether it survived and what moved it, every ownership in slot-and-occupant form with date and authoritative record or an explicit none, every [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md) assumption assigned or excluded, the discoverability clause, the negotiated obligations with each declined obligation's withdrawn guarantee in three-part form, the completion check with date runner and failure consequence, the [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md) disposition register, **the deployed deployment stack as merged and the reconciliation table with one line per warranty row**, the phase 7 answers with adjudication and revision, **and the prompt record, intervention record and frontier statement**. **Nothing else is assessed** — not length, not whether the obligations were accepted, **and not how much of the warranty the stack enforces.**

Seven hard requirements: **a guarantee missing any warranty column fails**; **an ownership naming only a person or only a function fails**, binary in both directions; **an [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md) assumption neither assigned nor excluded fails**; **no discoverability clause fails**; **a disposition register omitting a recommendation fails**, where *never answered* is an admissible entry and silence is not; **a warranty row with no reconciliation line fails**; and **a reconciliation reporting no *enforces more* entries where the stack carries settings the warranty does not mention fails**, because that column is where the artefact's unstated terms live. An intervention record with no entries fails, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — an excluded-principal list expressed as a group rather than an enumeration, with a comment saying why, is the accepted example. A declined obligation with its guarantee withdrawn is a pass; an obligation recorded as agreed when it was not is a specification failure; **a low enforcement count is a pass**, because most of a good warranty is not enforceable by a stack.

### Discharge of the WP-069 contradiction

**This paragraph is the report [WP-069](wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §6 requires, and the contradiction is resolved.**

[WP-069](wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §6 stated: *"An arrangement whose owner is a named person is exactly what does not survive a handover, and the day that teaches 'name a person, not a function' is setting up a contradiction S089 has to resolve. The design regards that as correct — the contradiction is real and the resolution is that a handover must transfer the ownership explicitly rather than that the ownership should have been anonymous — but this day does not flag it, and S089 must."*

**The element exists and it is a fail condition, not a discussion.** The resolution is delivered in four connected places. **Phase 2's bridge** puts the contradiction to the room as an open question and the instructor declines to answer, so the room carries it through phase 3. **Phase 3's W3** stages it as a real artefact, [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md)-compliant in every respect, whose every named owner had left within fourteen months — **and whose stack carried the same names as excluded principals, seven of them against a documented limit of five that returns no error**, so the room sees the same failure in prose and in enforcement, in one case, with nobody having erred. **Phase 4 point 3** resolves it: ownership has a **slot**, which is the role that bears the cost and persists, and an **occupant**, who can be asked a question and does not; [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) required the occupant and was correct, because it was governing a live arrangement in which the slot is implicit to everyone present; a handover has no such shared context, so **both must be written, with the date on which the occupant was correct and the mechanism by which the artefact learns of a change.** The mechanism is the load-bearing addition: an ownership whose occupant can change with no signal is [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) point 4's failure — a medium that goes stale without failing — and the artefact must therefore name where the current occupant is authoritatively recorded or state that it is not recorded anywhere, which is [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md)'s recoverability condition applied to a name. **Phase 5 step 3** requires the form, unsupported, and an entry naming only one of the two fails the specification.

**Point 3's platform paragraph is what the original resolution lacked.** The distinction is not this design's invention: a Foundry project's managed identity is a slot that holds the data-plane role assignments and never resigns, an Entra agent identity carries an accountable owner assigned at creation, and the tenant's agent registry publishes a count of agents whose owner has gone. **The identity system already separates the two things [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) conflated**, which converts point 3 from a distinction the design needed into one a student can be shown, and which narrows what the artefact must add to the two things the platform does not record — the date and the mechanism. §8 objection 1's concession is correspondingly narrower than it was, and is restated there.

[WP-069](wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md)'s own preferred resolution is adopted rather than replaced, and it is made operational rather than endorsed. **§8 objection 2 examines what the resolution does to [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s specification and argues that the compliance register should carry it.**

**Discharge of the [WP-034](wiki/whitepapers/WP-034.md) obligation.** [WP-034](wiki/whitepapers/WP-034.md) §7 recorded that "the phase 7 third-question quality is the incompleteness-discoverability measure carried to S089." **The element exists in two places and one is a fail condition.** Phase 4 point 5 converts [S034](wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s reader-side demand — *name something the contract does not tell you that you need* — into an author-side requirement, and phase 5 step 5's discoverability clause is one of the day's four hard requirements. Phase 7's first question re-administers [S034](wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s with its second half added, *how did you discover that it does not tell you*, which is what makes the measure a property of the artefact rather than of the reader's ingenuity. The obligation is discharged.

**Rule-6 discharge.** Four obligations land on this day and **all four elements exist.**

- **[S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)** named this day for its prediction-to-reconstruction gap, specifying that over-confident students write the receiver-obligation clause before the guarantee clause. **That is phase 5's column-order requirement**, and the cohort direction is reported at phase 4 and closed at phase 8.
- **[S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md)** named this day for its unowned-assumption total, specifying that every unowned assumption be assigned an owner or entered in the exclusion set, with one in neither failing. **That is phase 5 step 4**, it is a hard requirement, and the cohort total is reported at phase 4 and closed at phase 8.
- **[S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md)** named this day for its item 8 handover — three recommendations with owners, produced for an implementer who was not present — specifying that the handover artefact carry the disposition of each: implemented, declined with a reason, or never answered. **That is phase 5 step 7's disposition register, it is a checked element, and an omitted recommendation fails.** [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md) named this as the only occasion in the programme at which a student's recommendation meets the organisation that received it, and the design makes *never answered* admissible for exactly that reason: it is the commonest real outcome, it is the one an author is most tempted to soften into *deferred*, and a register that punished it would produce a register of softenings. The obligation is discharged.
- **[S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md)** named this day for its element 7 floor-holding rate, specifying that the handover warranty "must restate today's floor and state whether it survived the term and what moved it," and requiring this paper to report whether the element exists. **It exists at phase 5 step 2's fixed row and it is a checked element.** [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md) element 3's warranty is the object this artefact extends. The design records a floor that moved as **a guarantee the student no longer holds** rather than as a negotiation outcome, because the receiving party inherits the guarantee and not the negotiation, and a floor described as having been renegotiated tells them nothing about what they can now rely on. The obligation is discharged.

**Instrumentation under rule 6, with programme-level response occasions.** Two instruments, and **for both the response occasion is programme-level rather than a later seminar day, because after [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md) there is no later seminar day.** The rule permits a named occasion; it does not require that occasion to be a seminar day, and this paper states the substitution explicitly rather than allowing a forward reference to imply a day that does not exist.

- **The slot-and-occupant usability rate** — how often a phase 7 partner, reading as a receiver six months on, names an ownership entry they could not use. Its within-programme leg is [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md) phase 3, where the real receiving party is asked the same question and the two rates are compared. **Beyond that it is consumed by PE390's specification review**, which is required to state whether the form survived contact with the receiving organisation and what replaces it if it did not, **and by the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md)'s amendment process**, since a failed resolution to a programme-level contradiction must be reopened at [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) rather than patched here.
- **The step 6 declined-obligation rate and the guarantees withdrawn.** **No seminar day consumes it and the design says so.** It is consumed by **the substrate's continuity plan** — the withdrawn guarantees are collectively the list of things the substrate will not be guaranteed to do after this cohort leaves, and the [Substrate and Infrastructure](wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page's operating position for the following year is required to carry it — **and by the next cohort's [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)**, whose four handover cases are drawn from the components whose guarantees were withdrawn here. That is the only instrument in the programme whose response occasion is in a different cohort, and it is the mechanism by which this day's outputs become next year's phase 3 corpus.

**Contingent reuses recorded.** Step 6's negotiation reuses [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) step 7's live-negotiation structure, which is a **proposed** remedy under instrumentation rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8; the reuse is **contingent on [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s reported outcome.** Phase 7's document-only test is the seventh administration of a structure in use since [S028](wiki/seminars/S028.md) and its rate is **not compared** across occasions per instrumentation rule 2.

Programme instrumentation: the item 4 contradiction-resolution distribution against point 3; the item 3 *nothing* rate against the cohort's obligation count; the W1-over-W4 ranking; the slot-and-occupant usability rate; and the declined-obligation and withdrawn-guarantee totals.

ABET and CS2023 contribution: PO3 and PO5, and the day is the principal seminar rehearsal of **EA-2**. Dispositions: **reliability**, at step 2, in whether the warranty is one the student could have honoured; and **integrity**, at step 6, where recording an obligation as agreed when it was not is the available shortcut and is a specification failure.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day makes an untested form a fail condition.**

At its strongest: slot-and-occupant is this design's invention. It was written because the day needed a resolution, it has never been used by a receiving team, and no artefact in the phase 3 corpus is in that form — W4 is the payload and its ownership entries are ordinary names. **The design then makes it binary in the specification**, failing an entry that names only one of the two, on the last assessed artefact before the entrustment occasion. That is a heavier commitment than the evidence carries, and it is made on the day whose module teaches that a claim's strength is in its boundary.

Worse, the form's load-bearing clause may be inert. Point 3's requirement is that the artefact name **where the current occupant is authoritatively recorded**, and the substrate may have no such record for most slots. If it does not, every student writes *not recorded anywhere*, the clause degrades to a function name beside a stale personal name, and the day has added a column rather than solved anything.

**Two things have changed since that objection was first written and one of them is a partial answer.** The design has since checked, and **the substrate does have an authoritative record for a specific and important class of slot**: role assignments on a Foundry project's managed identity and on agent identities are queryable, an Entra agent identity carries an owner assigned at creation, and the agent registry maintains a count of agents without owners. For those slots the clause is not inert and the answer is a lookup. **For every other slot — the human ones, which are most of the artefact — the design still does not know, and did not check.** The concession therefore narrows and does not disappear: the form's load-bearing clause is demonstrably live for machine identities and remains untested for the roles that a receiving team actually needs to ring.

**The available fix is cheap and it is declined, and the reason is uncomfortable.** The four phase 3 receiving teams exist and are reachable; showing them the slot-and-occupant form and asking whether they could have used it would take an hour of staff time and would supply the only external evidence available anywhere on the human case. It is declined because it would change what phase 3 does: a fifth artefact in the resolved form, with a receiving team's verdict attached, converts point 3 from something the room reaches through an open question into something the room is shown. **The design prefers an unvalidated resolution the students generate to a validated one they receive.** That is a pedagogical preference outranking an evidential one, taken on the day the module's subject is what evidence licenses, and stating it that way is the only honest description of the trade. The specification's binary requirement is retained, and the honest position is that it is binary on a form the design believes in and has tested only on the half of the problem that a platform happens to solve.

**Objection 2: the resolution rescopes an earlier day's specification without saying so, and the compliance register should carry it.**

At its strongest: point 3 states that [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) "was addressing a live arrangement, where the slot is implicit because everybody in the room knows it." **[S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) does not say that.** Its specification says, flatly and without qualification, that *an owner named as a function rather than a person fails*, and it gives the reason — a function cannot bear a cost and cannot be asked a question — as a general truth rather than a scoped one. The scoping is being supplied here, two quarters later, by a whitepaper that needs it in order to present its resolution as an extension rather than as a correction.

This matters because of the programme's own conventions. The [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) states that earlier days **are not retrospectively amended** and that their concessions stand so the failure remains legible. The [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s specification rule 1 requires a specification to state what is actually checked. If [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s requirement was always scoped to live arrangements, its specification does not say what is actually checked and is defective. If it was not scoped, then this day is amending it. **It cannot be both, and the framing of "extension" lets the design avoid choosing.**

**This objection is conceded.** The resolution is a correction to [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s specification, dressed as a distinction that was always implicit. The distinction is a good one and the design would make the same resolution again; what it should not do is claim the earlier day already contained it.

The consequent claim is straightforward and is made here rather than acted on, because a whitepaper cannot edit the compliance register. **The [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s compliance register should carry an entry**, and under the register's own published criterion it is a **fixed** finding rather than a recorded one: the register fixes outright contradictions and violations of written rules, and records findings requiring a design judgement. This is a contradiction between two specifications in the same programme, and the fix — adding the slot to [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s named-person requirement, with the correction annotated at the point of change — is the register's prescribed treatment. The proposed entry is stated precisely in this paper's closing report so that it can be entered verbatim or disagreed with.

The residual concession is sharper than the fix. **This is the second time a day in this module has resolved a programme-level tension by adding a distinction that makes both prior positions correct** — [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md) point 3 did the same to [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md)'s two conditions, finding them necessary and insufficient rather than wrong. Two instances is below the elevation threshold and is recorded so that a third is recognised as a third. **The pattern to watch for is a design whose corrections never cost it anything**, which is a comfortable shape for a self-assessing system to settle into and is not distinguishable, from inside, from a design that keeps getting things nearly right.

**Objection 3: the enforcement mechanism is more persuasive than the resolution it illustrates, and the day cannot tell which of the two the room takes away.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn.

At its strongest: the day's argument is point 3, and point 3 is a distinction about documents. The day's most memorable twenty minutes are phase 3's stack attacks, and its most memorable single fact is that a seven-name exclusion list silently exceeded a five-name limit. **Those are not the same lesson and the second is far stickier.** A student who leaves able to recite the five-principal cap and unable to say why [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) needed extending has learned a platform fact on the day the programme resolved its own contradiction, and the platform fact will be false within two years while the contradiction will not. **The design has, on its most conceptually important day, supplied a rival attractor for attention and made it concrete, immediate and destructive** — the three properties that determine what a room remembers.

The rebuttal is that the two are not in competition on the evidence. W3 is the case that carries both: it fails in prose because its owners left and in enforcement because its exclusion list overflowed, and **the phase 3 task-after asks which of the two the receiving team believed, whose answer is the prose.** That is the day's own argument for why point 3 is the thing that matters — the mechanism was wrong and nobody noticed, and the document was wrong and everybody acted on it.

**The concession is that the design has no instrument that separates the two.** Phase 8 reports the item 4 resolution distribution and the reconciliation totals, and a room that has internalised point 3 and a room that has internalised the cap produce identical numbers on both. **The cheap fix is one line in the between-day set and it is made: day 3's five-minute exercise asks for the slot-and-occupant entry a supplied artefact is missing, with no stack in front of the student** — a retrieval the platform fact cannot answer. What that does not fix is the balance of the day itself, which the design could rebalance and has not: phase 3's execution leg could run at twelve minutes rather than twenty. **It does not, because the design wanted the five-principal finding and enjoyed having it.** That is the honest reason, it is a bad one, and it is recorded rather than corrected so that the priority is visible.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At item 3, a majority of students will write *nothing* for what the receiving party must do, and at step 2 the same students will produce at least two receiver obligations each. *Instrument:* item 3 coded binary against the per-student obligation count. **If the item 3 rate is low, phase 3's payload is not defeating a belief this cohort holds** and W4's staging is spending forty-five minutes on a point already made by [S039](wiki/seminars/S039.md).

**Prediction 2.** At item 4, the modal cold resolution of the contradiction will be to retreat to a function name, not to name a successor, and students who retreat will be disproportionately those who wrote strong [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) arrangements. *Instrument:* item 4 answers coded into three categories — function, successor, both — and matched against [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) specification outcomes where records permit. **The correlation is the claim**: it would indicate that the students who best internalised the earlier rule are the ones the contradiction damages most, which is what a rule taught without its scope does.

**Prediction 3.** At phase 7, partners will name an unusable ownership entry less often than they name a missing item, and the ownership-entry rate will be lower than it would be for an artefact in plain named-person form. *Instrument:* the phase 7 third answer, with a comparison arm supplied by the four phase 3 artefacts read under the same protocol on day 3 of the between-day set. **This is the design's only test of whether slot-and-occupant helps a reader**, it is weak, and §8 objection 1 states why a better one was declined.

**Prediction 4.** More than a third of receiving parties will decline at least one obligation at step 6, and the most commonly declined class will be recurring checks rather than one-off notifications. *Instrument:* step 6 records coded by obligation class. **If almost nothing is declined, the negotiation is ceremonial** — either the obligations were written to be acceptable, which the assessors can see in the table, or the receiving party does not believe they will be held to them, which is worse and is not visible in the artefact at all.

**Prediction 5 — the practical turn's own, per §8 objection 3.** At step 8, the modal number of *enforces more* entries will be **zero** on first submission and non-zero after the assessors' query, and the settings most often unmentioned in the warranty will be the unmanage action and the excluded-principal list. *Instrument:* reconciliation tables at submission and after adjudication, coded by setting. **The prediction is that students write warranties about what they intend to guarantee and stacks about what they intend to prevent, and never notice the third category** — what the mechanism does that nobody promised. If *enforces more* is populated on first submission, the reconciliation is doing less work than the design claims and step 8 could be shortened to a declaration without a table.

**Prediction 6 — the frontier item.** A large majority of item 8 sealed predictions will name a **deny setting** as the term the agent will choose for them, and a small minority will name the **unmanage action**. *Instrument:* item 8 responses coded by setting against the settings the agent actually supplied at step 8. **The design predicts that the room's attention is on protection and the agent's most consequential choice is about destruction**, which is the phase 6 case's mechanism and would be, if it holds, a frontier finding about where a room looks when it reviews generated infrastructure. Its reader is next year's cohort, through the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md).

Consistent with the folder-wide asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), **the pages state limits, scopes, values and role names throughout and state no accuracy or frequency anywhere**; no claim is made here about how often a stack's scope surprises a receiving team, and none could be.
