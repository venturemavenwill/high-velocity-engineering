# WP-001 — Why This Programme Feels Wrong: The Design You Are Subject To

**Seminar day:** [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) · **Module:** [M01](/wiki/modules/M01.md) · **Quarter:** [Q1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · CF · **Complexity class:** 1
**Platform anchor:** GitHub Copilot agent mode · **MCP servers:** Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends the decision to spend the programme's first six hours teaching students the instructional design they are about to be subject to, rather than teaching computer science. The argument is that a curriculum built on desirable difficulties is behaviourally unstable without learner buy-in, because the difficulties are aversive in the moment and their benefit is not observable at the point of choice. Students who lack a model of the design will substitute their own study strategies, and the strategies they substitute are predictably the ones that maximise the sensation of learning while minimising it. The day therefore treats learner metacognition not as a soft preliminary but as a load-bearing structural component, and installs it using the same mechanisms it is describing — pretesting, productive failure, retrieval, and a delayed calibration confrontation. **The day also installs the programme's primary instrument, and the paper argues that this is one decision rather than two: a student's sense of how well a study session went and an agent's confident paragraph are both instrument readings, and the day's single thesis is that an instrument is characterised before its outputs are trusted.** The paper concedes the design's principal vulnerability: the day asks students to accept an argument about long-run retention on the basis of a single compressed demonstration, and the demonstration is weaker evidence than the argument requires.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The programme's pedagogy is built on effortful retrieval, distributed practice, interleaving and generation before instruction. Each of these shares a property that makes it operationally fragile: **performance during learning is a poor guide to learning, and these techniques specifically depress performance during learning while improving it afterwards.**

This creates a control problem rather than a knowledge problem. A student is not a passive recipient of a schedule. Outside seminar hours they choose how to study, and they choose on the basis of the signal available to them, which is the sensation of fluency. Re-reading produces fluency. Blocked practice produces fluency. Massed study before an assessment produces fluency. All three are, on the evidence, comparatively poor for durable retention, and all three feel excellent.

So the design has a leak. Inside the seminar day the programme controls conditions. Outside it, students will optimise against a misleading signal, and the more effortful the seminar day feels, the more strongly they will optimise against it — seeking relief in exactly the practices the design is built to avoid.

The day must therefore do three things: give students a causal model of why effort and learning dissociate; give them first-hand evidence that their own fluency judgements are unreliable; and give them a concrete alternative plan they authored themselves. Only the third produces compliance, and it depends on the first two.

There is a second problem, less obvious and equally serious. A programme that demands intellectual honesty from students — conceding rather than hedging, stating what a measurement does not license, reporting the result that undermines your own position — cannot begin by asserting its own design without warrant. The first day is where the programme's own epistemic standards are either demonstrated or revealed as rhetoric. Phase 7's requirement that the instructor concede genuine objections is therefore not a courtesy; it is the day's second content.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge assumptions

Students arrive with:

- **No shared technical vocabulary.** The cohort spans self-taught programmers, students with formal preparation, and career-changers. Any technical content on day one would be either trivial or excluding for a substantial fraction.
- **A near-universal folk theory of learning** in which learning is exposure, understanding is the sensation of following an explanation, and forgetting is a personal deficiency rather than an expected property of memory.
- **Substantial prior experience of assessment as measurement** rather than as a learning event, and therefore a defensive stance towards being tested on material not yet taught.
- **High initial motivation and high uncertainty**, which is the most favourable window for installing a norm and the worst window for installing a habit.
- **No shared position on AI assistance.** A minority arrive having used a coding agent daily; a minority arrive having been told at school that using one is cheating; most arrive somewhere between, with no articulated position either way. This variance is *wider* than the technical variance and it is the one the day must not let harden, because a cohort that has silently sorted itself into permitted and forbidden camps cannot run phase 3 honestly. The day's response is that the tool is used by everybody, in the first hour, on a question where its answer can be argued with.
- **No entitlement to assume any licence or configuration.** The programme supplies the Copilot seat; the Learn MCP Server requires no authentication and carries no charge, which is why it and not a provisioned service is week one's server. **Any day that required a subscription or a tenant role in week one would be a day a fraction of the cohort could not run**, and the design has none.

The absence of shared technical knowledge is what makes this day possible at all. It is the only day in the programme where the subject matter — the students' own learning — is one on which every member of the cohort has equal standing and equal data.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

The day's content has **low intrinsic element interactivity**. The mechanisms taught in phase 4 are largely independent: understanding spacing does not require simultaneously holding interleaving. This is unusual and it is exploited.

Because intrinsic load is low, the design can afford unusually high extraneous demand elsewhere — a generation phase with no scaffolding, a full 90-minute whole task on day one, a public error clinic. In a day with high intrinsic interactivity this combination would exceed capacity. Here it does not, and the surplus capacity is spent on affective and metacognitive work that no later day can afford to do at this length.

Phase 4 is nonetheless designed to minimise extraneous load: single integrated representation, no split attention between diagram and legend, no seductive detail. The self-imposed constraint matters, because a phase 4 that overloaded students while asserting that the programme manages load would refute itself in front of the cohort.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Pretesting on untaught material* (phase 1). Chosen because it is the day's thesis in miniature: an experience of failure that is nonetheless productive, felt before it is explained.
- *Generation before instruction* (phase 3). Chosen because the alternative — telling students about productive failure — would be a performative contradiction of some magnitude.
- *A delayed rather than immediate reveal* of the phase 2 re-reading demonstration. Chosen because immediacy destroys it.
- *Public error disclosure* (phase 6). Chosen because the norm must be established before there is anything costly to disclose.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Interleaving within the day.* The day's mechanisms are presented in a coherent block, not mixed. Interleaving them would obscure the structure of the argument at exactly the moment students need to hold it as a whole. Interleaving begins at S003.
- *Spacing within the day.* Same reason. The spacing schedule begins the following morning, not within the session.
- *Withholding the reconciliation rule.* Students are given the raise-difficulty-in-retrieval, lower-it-in-representation formulation directly, because it is the sentence they will need under stress and a generated version of it would be less precise.
- *High-stakes assessment.* Nothing today is assessed for entrustment. Making the first day consequential would recruit exactly the threat response the programme spends three years suppressing.
- *Teaching the tool before using it.* No time is spent explaining what agent mode is. Students configure it, run four contrasting cases with it, and are told what it is in phase 4, after they have seen it behave. The alternative — a tool briefing at the top of the day — would install the description before the procedure and would be the exact error [WP-002](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-002.md) §1 identifies about execution models.

**The scope statement for the platform instance, in strong form.** The day teaches a simplification: that a documentation-grounding server makes an agent's answer accountable to a source. That simplification is **safe** for reasoning about why a claim needs a citation, for deciding whether a question falls inside a named corpus, and for writing an instruction that forces the absence of a source into view. It is **unsafe** in three specific conditions and all three are stated to students in phase 4.

- **Outside the corpus.** The Learn MCP Server carries publicly available Microsoft Learn documentation and nothing else. On the phase 3 question it has no relevant content, and the agent's answer is neither grounded nor marked as ungrounded.
- **When the tool is connected and not called.** The server's own getting-started documentation recommends an instructions file for the case where the agent does not use the tool when expected. A configured server is an available tool, not a used one, and nothing in the output distinguishes the two.
- **On any version other than the one in front of the student.** The endpoint's own developer reference states that it is not an API in the traditional sense and that its interface, including its tools and their request and response formats, may change dynamically. **The unsafe condition is, as it almost always is, any version other than this one**, and here the vendor has said so in writing.

The artefact element that makes the boundary checkable is item 3 of the phase 5 specification: the student must show the instructions in the request payload. A student who cannot produce that evidence has not demonstrated that any of this happened, and fails — which is the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s widened scope rule applied at the first opportunity the programme has had to apply it.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day is designed against three motivational constructs simultaneously.

**Autonomy.** The phase 5 deliverable is the student's own schedule, not the programme's. This is the day's most important motivational move: the design converts an imposed structure into an authored one. Students who author a plan grounded in mechanisms they generated arguments about are in a different relationship to that plan than students handed a timetable.

**Competence.** Phase 1 threatens it — students fail publicly-ish on five items in the first half hour. The threat is bounded deliberately: the items are on untaught material, this is stated, and the failure is framed in advance as the instrument. Phase 3 restores it, because groups genuinely produce partially correct accounts and are told which parts were right before being told which were incomplete.

**Relatedness.** Phase 6 and phase 7 are the mechanisms. The instructor's first-person error disclosure is a status-costly act by the highest-status person in the room, and it is the standard precondition for the cohort to follow. Phase 7's break-it protocol makes disagreement a contribution rather than an affront.

The day also opens the **interest** trajectory. Situational interest is triggered by the phase 2 demonstration — a small, concrete, personally implicating surprise. Whether it develops further depends on the S011 confrontation, which is where the demonstration is finally resolved.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min, pretest and prediction).** Two functions. The pretest establishes that retrieval attempts on unknown material are productive, which is the claim phase 4 will make. The predictions P1 and P2 are the calibration instrument; without them the S011 confrontation has no data and becomes an assertion. P2 is sealed rather than discussed, because discussing it invites social calibration and destroys the individual signal. **P3 — the Copilot prediction — is a third instrument and its two clauses do different work.** The count clause is cheap and is resolved in front of the room. The failure-location clause is the one the programme cares about, because a student who cannot name where a tool will be wrong has no basis for reading its output, and this is the day the programme finds out how many of them can. Instrumentation rule 8 binds P3 to phase 1; asked at phase 8 it would measure recall of phase 3.

**Phase 2 (20 min, replaced demonstration).** The only day on which the cumulative retrieval phase is displaced, and it is displaced because there is nothing to retrieve. The re-read versus recall contrast is run without disclosing that conditions differ, because knowing the manipulation contaminates the confidence measure, which is the interesting measure. Confidence is collected today; performance is collected at S003. The two-day gap is small enough that students remember the manipulation and large enough that the retrieval advantage has room to appear.

**Phase 3 (45 min, contrasting cases).** Three timetables with outcomes and no rationale. The third case is constructed so that the contact-hours account fails. Groups commit in writing before presenting, which prevents the retrospective drift towards whatever the instructor says next. Accounts are left visible and uncorrected through phase 4 so that consolidation can be explicitly indexed to what each group produced. The design point is that groups will not generate the canonical account — the aim is differentiated prior knowledge, not discovery.

**The four executed cases occupy the phase's middle third and are matched on everything but one configuration decision each.** The payload is case B, and it is the payload because it is the case the room's day-one model does not predict: attaching a grounding server to a question outside its corpus changes the answer very little and changes the confidence not at all. The design's reason for putting an unanswerable-by-grounding question in front of the tool on day one, rather than a Microsoft-documentation question it would answer well, is that **the corpus boundary is invisible from the output**, and a student whose first experience of grounding is a case where it worked will not learn that the boundary exists. Case C — the in-sentence citation requirement — is what converts the invisible boundary into an observable absence, and it is also the day's first instance of specification rule 2, delivered as an experience rather than as a rule.

**Phase 4 (50 min, consolidation).** Direct, efficient, indexed to phase 3. The ordering — retrieval, spacing, interleaving, generation, illusion, load — is chosen so that the illusion arrives after the mechanisms it applies to, and load arrives last because it is the one mechanism that runs in the opposite direction and would otherwise be heard as a contradiction of the preceding four. **Points 7 and 8 sit after the reconciliation** because both are claims about instruments and the preceding six points characterised the students' own. The jagged frontier is delivered with direction and mechanism and no magnitude, per the standing rule; the four documented defaults are delivered as an enumeration whose scope statement is that it is not complete and is version-bound.

**Phase 5 (90 min, whole task).** A genuine instance of the programme's target task class: decide under stated constraint, justify against principle, commit to a falsifiable consequence. It is class 1 because the method is given. The four supplied constraints are not decorative — the competing obligation in week three exists specifically to force a spacing-versus-availability trade that has no clean answer. **The first thirty minutes build the instrument the remaining sixty use**, and the ordering is the day's thesis in operational form. The payload-verification step is the element that makes the build a build rather than a configuration exercise: an instructions file that has not been observed in a request payload is a file.

**Phase 6 (40 min, error clinic).** Instructor-first is non-negotiable. The disclosure protection is stated in fixed wording, at every clinic, for three years, because a protection restated is a protection believed and a protection assumed is a protection eroded. **The agent case is placed third and last**, so that the room has established error as safe to examine before being asked to examine an error it made an hour earlier. It is analysed for why review did not catch it, never for what the tool did wrong, because the tool is not in the room and the reviewers are.

**Phase 7 (30 min, articulation).** The break-it framing recruits the interactive tier rather than the constructive tier: the partner is not an audience but a counterparty. The instructor's concession of genuine objections is the day's demonstration of the programme's own standard.

**Phase 8 (20 min, calibration and commitment).** P1 is resolved today because same-day resolution is available and cheap. P2 is not, because it is the one that matters and its resolution requires the delay. **P3 is resolved live**, and the live run is deliberate: a recorded run would be a claim about the tool, and a run in front of the room is a demonstration that the claim is checkable, which is the property the day is selling.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** Some students arrive having read the popular literature on learning science and will find phase 4 familiar. The predicted failure is disengagement in phases 3 and 4 and a conclusion that the programme is remedial. Countermeasure: phase 3's third case is not solvable by the popular account, which stops at spacing and testing and does not address the load reconciliation; and phase 7's break-it protocol gives the well-read student the highest-value role in the room. Expertise reversal is a genuine risk here and the countermeasure is role differentiation rather than content differentiation.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** Some students will experience phase 1 as confirmation that they do not belong. This is the more dangerous failure because it is silent and it compounds. Countermeasures: explicit advance framing of the pretest as untaught; the instructor's phase 6 disclosure normalising error at the highest status level; and phase 3's group structure, which distributes the generation attempt so that no individual is exposed. The residual risk is not eliminated and this is the day's most serious equity concern.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** If a group produces something close to the canonical account, the productive-failure structure collapses into a lecture with a warm-up. Countermeasure: the third contrasting case is held in reserve with a harder variant, and the instructor's fallback is to press the group's account against the load reconciliation, which no group has reached. Note that this failure mode is not costly — a group that generates the account has done the encoding work by another route.

**Affective failure.** The day's genuine risk is that phase 6 does not work — that no student discloses, the silence is read as a norm, and the error clinic is dead for the cohort before it has run once. Countermeasures: the instructor's disclosure is prepared, specific and genuinely costly rather than a token; a planted disclosure by a returning student is permitted; and if silence persists the phase converts to written anonymous disclosure read aloud, which is weaker but recoverable. The design accepts that phase 6 is the highest-variance component of the day.

**Compliance without belief.** Students produce a schedule that satisfies the specification and then study however they were going to anyway. This is the most likely outcome for a meaningful minority, and the design's only real answer is S011 — the confrontation with their own P2 prediction. If S011 does not land, the day has produced an artefact and not a habit.

**The build consumes the day.** The most likely operational failure of the revised phase 5 is that thirty minutes of configuration becomes sixty, the schedule is rushed, and the day's autonomy-supporting artefact — the thing that converts an imposed structure into an authored one — is the part that gets cut. This would be a serious loss and it is the failure the design would least like to trade. Countermeasures: the repository is supplied as a template with the directory structure present and the instructions file empty, so that the student writes rules rather than scaffolding; the MCP configuration is a documented block of four lines; and if the room is behind at the thirty-minute mark, **the instruction is to abandon the configuration and take the supplied working repository, because the schedule is the assessed artefact and the configuration is repeated at every subsequent day.** The design would rather lose the build once than lose the schedule once.

**The tool answers phase 1.** A student who runs the pretest items through the agent before attempting them has removed the retrieval attempt that the phase exists to create, and nothing in the room prevents it. The countermeasure is weak and is stated as weak: the items are on untaught material, no marks are recorded, and the day's entire argument is that the failure is the instrument. A student who defeats phase 1 has defeated an instrument that was never going to be used against them, which is the design's only real protection and is a protection against motive rather than against opportunity.

## 6. Retention architecture

This day is retrieved by S003, S009, S026 and S036 — a deliberately wide and long-gapped set, because the day's content is the programme's operating system rather than a topic.

- **S003** retrieves the phase 2 demonstration, resolving it with performance data. This is the closure of the day's open loop and it must not slip.
- **S009** retrieves the notion of a prediction that could be wrong, transposed from studying to testing. Same deep structure, entirely different surface — the transfer test for the day's core idea.
- **S026** retrieves the illusion material into a measurement context: your sense of how well the system is performing is not evidence either.
- **S036** retrieves the phase 7 concession norm into professional inquiry: the question you should not have asked.

The day's own expanding-interval schedule runs next morning, day 3, then week 2 and week 6. The week 6 repetition is placed at S011, co-located with the P2 confrontation, so that retrieval and calibration land together.

**Two cross-day commitments are created here and both name their discharging occasion**, per instrumentation rule 6.

- **The frontier map opened by P3 is collated at [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md) phase 8** and carried to [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md), where the quarter's statements are consolidated and the cohort's calibration is reported to it. S010 is also the first of the programme's three unassisted-work occasions, which is the earliest point at which the map has anything to be compared against.
- **The repository built at phase 5 is a standing prerequisite from [S002](/wiki/seminars/S002.md) onward.** Every subsequent day in the module assumes it exists and adds to it. A student who did not complete it does not have a missing artefact; they have a missing tool, and the failure surfaces the following week rather than at the end of term.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. The programme deliberately generates none in Q1.

One specification item: the phase 5 artefact, assessed pass/fail in PE101. The schedule and justification are assessed on soundness against the taught mechanisms. **It is not assessed on whether the schedule was followed**, because assessing adherence would convert an autonomy-supporting artefact into a compliance instrument and destroy its motivational function.

Three elements are added to the item and all three are assessed for presence and specificity rather than for quality: the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement. **None of them assesses the agent's output**, and the distinction is load-bearing. A student whose schedule the agent drafted badly, who noticed, who corrected it and who recorded the correction has produced a better artefact than a student who got a good draft first time and recorded nothing. That inversion is stated to students in advance because it is not the inversion they expect.

The repository and its payload verification are the fourth added element and the only one that is a capability check. It is binary and it is not resubmittable-by-argument: either the instructions appear in the request payload or they do not.

The day generates four programme-instrumentation data points: the P1 calibration gap, the P2 prediction for later comparison, the phase 6 disclosure rate, which is the psychological-safety indicator tracked for the full three years, and **the P3 failure-location accuracy, which opens the frontier map**. Instrumentation rule 9 binds the last of these: its reader is next year's cohort, by way of the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), and the programme publishes before each offering whether it intends to analyse it that year.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: this is six hours not spent on computer science, in a programme that already claims to be short of time.**

At its strongest, the objection is that metacognitive instruction has a poor record of transferring to actual study behaviour, that students frequently know what they ought to do and do otherwise, and that the day therefore purchases a belief rather than a behaviour at the cost of a full teaching day.

The rebuttal is partial. The design does not rely on knowledge-to-behaviour transfer alone; it pairs the knowledge with an authored plan, an outstanding personal prediction, and a scheduled confrontation at S011 — three commitment devices rather than an exhortation. But the objection is substantially correct that belief is cheap and habit is expensive, and the honest position is that this day is necessary and not sufficient. It buys the *right to impose* the design, which the programme would otherwise lack.

**Objection 2: the phase 2 demonstration is not evidence.**

This objection the design does not fully answer. A single within-session comparison, with two dozen students, no randomisation worth the name, a two-day retention interval and an outcome measured once, would not survive the standards taught at S028 and S049. The programme will spend three years telling students not to accept exactly this kind of evidence, and it opens by using it.

The partial defence is that the demonstration's function is motivational rather than evidential — it makes a claim *personally salient*, and the claim's actual warrant is the cited literature presented in phase 4. But this is a defence of the demonstration's role, not of its rigour, and the correct handling is to say so to students at S028 and use this day as the worked example of a persuasive-but-weak design. The programme's own first day becomes a case study in its own measurement course. That is the best available resolution and it is not a complete one.

**Objection 3: a first day that installs one vendor's toolchain produces product familiarity, not understanding, and does it before the student can evaluate the choice.**

At its strongest the objection has two edges and the second is sharper. The first is the ordinary one: a graduate whose entire practical formation ran through GitHub Copilot and the Microsoft Learn MCP Server knows a product. Asked to work with a different agent, a different protocol implementation or no agent at all, they may find that what they learned was a set of paths, keys and modes rather than a way of working. The second edge is about consent. The day is *about* students holding a model of the design they are subject to and being able to disagree with it specifically — and it hands them a toolchain in phase 5, on the strength of an argument they are in no position to evaluate in week one, having spent the preceding four hours being persuaded of the programme's honesty.

The first edge is answered structurally and the answer is thin but real. Every phase 4 platform point on this day carries an explicit durable/perishable split, and it is the *durable* half that is assessed: that an instrument is characterised before trusting it, that defaults are opinions, that a rule an executor cannot check is not a rule. None of those is a Microsoft fact. The programme's bet is that a student who holds them can re-acquire a toolchain in a week, and re-acquiring the perishable layer is itself taught at S011, S015 and S086. **The bet is asserted and not demonstrated**, it is recorded as the largest open risk on the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](/wiki/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md), and this paper does not improve on that.

The second edge **is conceded, and the fix is cheap.** The day already has a mechanism for exactly this — phase 7's break-it protocol, where the instructor concedes genuine objections and adds them to the programme's visible open-questions list — and the design simply did not point it at the toolchain. The fix is one sentence in the phase 7 brief: the toolchain decision is named as an available target for the break-it exercise, and an objection to it is added to the open-questions list on the same terms as any other. It costs nothing, it is available in week one, and it converts an imposition into something the cohort has been invited to attack. It is added to the day.

**Objection 4: a student who has only built with an agent may be unable to build without one, and this day is where that begins.**

At its strongest: phase 5 is the first build in the programme and it is agent-directed. Whatever the student would have learned by writing an instructions file unaided, or by reading the configuration documentation and working it out, has been foreclosed on day one. The programme's own [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) states this risk plainly and states that the design cannot currently detect it.

This objection the design does not answer, and the honest position is that this day makes it *worse* rather than better, by front-loading the dependency to the first six hours. Two things are true in mitigation and neither is sufficient. The programme's structural response — bounded unassisted work at [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md), [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md), with the comparison reported to the student — begins five days after this one, which is the shortest gap available. And the artefact this day builds is small enough that a student who cannot rebuild it unaided will discover that at S010 rather than at graduation. **Whether three unassisted occasions across three years is enough is unknown, and the Copilot Practice Standard records it as unknown.** This paper adds only that if the answer is no, this is the day the design will be judged to have started it.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** The P1 calibration gap will be positive for the substantial majority of the cohort — students will predict more correct pretest items than they achieve. *Instrument:* sealed P1 against phase 1 scores, resolved same day.

**Prediction 2.** At S003, the free-recall condition from phase 2 will outperform the re-read condition on the delayed measure, while having reported lower confidence at S001. *Instrument:* phase 2 confidence ratings against S003 performance, analysed by condition. **If this fails, the day's central demonstration has failed and the reveal must be handled as a disconfirmation in front of the cohort rather than quietly dropped.** The protocol for that eventuality is fixed in advance precisely so that it cannot be quietly dropped.

**Prediction 3.** P2 overestimation at S011 will exceed P1 overestimation at S001 — students will be more wrong about their six-week retention than about their immediate performance. *Instrument:* sealed P2 against the S011 delayed measure.

**Prediction 4.** Cohorts with a higher phase 6 disclosure rate will show higher error-clinic participation through Q1 and lower rates of undisclosed specification failure at S010. *Instrument:* disclosure counts per clinic, correlated with S010 outcomes. This one is weakly powered at cohort scale and becomes informative only across several cohorts.

**Prediction 5.** On P3, the substantial majority of the cohort will produce a usable count and no usable failure location — either leaving the second clause blank or filling it with a generic statement about unreliability. *Instrument:* P3 responses coded as *specific location named* / *generic* / *absent*, against the actual phase 8 run. **If most students name a specific location, the design's premise that frontier calibration must be built is wrong for this cohort and phase 4 point 7 is redundant.** The result branches: a cohort scoring high is given the harder S002 item 5 variant, which asks for a detection method rather than a location.

**Prediction 6.** Groups running phase 3 case B will report that the grounded answer was better than case A's, in the absence of any difference an assessor can identify in the two outputs. *Instrument:* a one-line group judgement collected before the four outputs are compared, against a blind pairwise comparison of the outputs by staff. **This is the day's sharpest available test of the fluency illusion in its new register**, and it fails informatively either way: if groups correctly report no difference, the illusion does not transfer from self-assessment to tool-assessment, and phase 4 point 8's framing is over-claimed.

The jagged-frontier material at phase 4 point 7 is grounded in [research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md](/research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md), whose verification status records that the note was written from the official abstract and not from a full-text reading; **that depth is carried forward here, and no figure from it is asserted anywhere on this day.** The programme-architecture decisions this day implements are recorded in [research/01-course-structure/10-gap-analysis-and-course-architecture.md](/research/01-course-structure/10-gap-analysis-and-course-architecture.md). **Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** The testing effect, distributed practice, interleaving, the pretesting effect, metacognitive illusions of fluency, expertise reversal, and cognitive load theory are canonical results in the learning-sciences literature and are cited here for **direction and mechanism only**. No effect size is asserted for any of them. Where this paper says a mechanism improves retention it means the direction of the reported effect, not a magnitude, and the scope conditions summarised in [Learning Science Foundations](/wiki/program/02-Learning-Science-Foundations.md) apply.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.**

*What the documentation says, and where.* Agent mode determines its own steps, makes code edits, runs terminal commands, invokes tools, monitors outcomes such as build results and test failures, and iterates rather than stopping after one response; it requests confirmation before running a terminal command or a tool that is not built in; tools contributed by MCP servers are not automatically enabled and their checkboxes are cleared by default; its file manipulation is bounded to the open solution directory and its subdirectories while its terminal commands carry the editor process's permissions and are not bounded by that restriction ([learn.microsoft.com/visualstudio/ide/copilot-agent-mode](https://learn.microsoft.com/visualstudio/ide/copilot-agent-mode?view=visualstudio)). The default `Auto` model selection lets Copilot choose a model per step dynamically ([learn.microsoft.com/azure/developer/intro/quickstart-agent-assist](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/developer/intro/quickstart-agent-assist)). Instructions files live at `.github/instructions/<name>.instructions.md`, are scoped by an `applyTo` front-matter glob, and are injected into every matching request automatically with no command required; the request payload is inspectable in the editor's Copilot Chat output channel, which the documentation calls the source of truth ([learn.microsoft.com/sql/tools/visual-studio-code-extensions/github-copilot/custom-instructions](https://learn.microsoft.com/sql/tools/visual-studio-code-extensions/github-copilot/custom-instructions?view=sql-server-ver17), [learn.microsoft.com/visualstudio/ide/copilot-chat-context](https://learn.microsoft.com/visualstudio/ide/copilot-chat-context?view=visualstudio)). The Learn MCP Server is a remote streamable-HTTP server at `https://learn.microsoft.com/api/mcp`, requires no authentication, carries no charge, exposes `microsoft_docs_search`, `microsoft_docs_fetch` and `microsoft_code_sample_search`, contains publicly available documentation and not training or profile data, and refreshes incrementally with a full refresh once a day ([learn.microsoft.com/training/support/mcp](https://learn.microsoft.com/training/support/mcp), [/mcp-developer-reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/training/support/mcp-developer-reference)). Its getting-started article recommends an instructions file for the case where the agent does not use the tool when expected ([/mcp-get-started](https://learn.microsoft.com/training/support/mcp-get-started)).

*Version and date.* All of the above was read on **26 July 2026** against the current published articles. The Learn MCP Server's own release notes date the server to 12 June 2025 and its fetch tool to 6 August 2025. The developer reference states explicitly that the interface, including its tools and their request and response formats, **may change dynamically** and that developers should not hardcode tool names or schemas. Agent-mode behaviour is documented against Visual Studio 2022 version 17.14 and later. **Every platform sentence on this day is dated to that reading and is re-verified before each offering.**

*What the documentation does not report that a reader would need.* Four silences, and they are the ones a student on this day will run into first. **No retrieval accuracy is published for `microsoft_docs_search`** — no precision, no recall, no statement of what fraction of Learn is indexed, and **no way to distinguish a search that found nothing from a subject the documentation does not cover.** Phase 3's case B depends on exactly that distinction and the documentation cannot settle it. **No rate limits are quantified**; the FAQ states that limits exist and gives no number. **No instruction-adherence rate is published**, and no precedence rule is stated for two instructions files whose globs both match — so the claim that instructions are *injected* is documented while the claim that they are *followed* is not. And **no measure is published of how often agent mode's self-correction loop converges, or of how often it reports completion on work that is wrong**, which is the property phase 6 is about. This is the asymmetry [research/06-microsoft-platform/](/research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md) records across six independent sources: every one states a latency or a sampling bound, and none states an accuracy. Students are shown it on day one as a property of the genre.

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The eight-phase structure and its minute allocation; the decision to open the programme with this day rather than with technical content; the reconciliation formulation *raise difficulty in retrieval and selection, lower it in representation and attention*; the instructor-first error-clinic protocol; the sealed P2 prediction and its placement at S011; the break-it framing of phase 7; the decision not to assess schedule adherence; the four-configuration construction of phase 3 and the choice of case B as its payload; the claim that a corpus boundary is invisible from an output; the ordering that builds the instrument before using it in phase 5; the placement of the agent case third in phase 6; and all six predictions in §9 are this design's own reasoning. None has been tested. The predictions exist so that they can be.
