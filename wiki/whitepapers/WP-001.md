# WP-001 — Why This Programme Is Shaped Like an Engagement

**Seminar day:** [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) · **Module:** [M01](/wiki/modules/M01.md) · **Quarter:** [Q1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md)

## In plain terms

Most engineering education starts with how to build. This programme starts with whether to.

The reason is that a forward-deployed engineer's most consequential decision is usually made before any code exists, and it is a decision most practices never write down. The corpus of professional practice this programme is built on — a large vendor's internal account of how its customer-embedded engineers work — is detailed, experienced and in places genuinely wise. **It contains no worked example of declining an engagement.** Its case studies all succeed. Its readiness criteria are a list of customer dispositions. Its prepared answer to the objection *your tooling improved and our delivery did not* reattributes the shortfall to the customer.

So the first stage of this programme is written past its source rather than from it, and that is the concession this paper makes up front: **nothing establishes that qualification improves outcomes.** It is an argument that a practice which cannot refuse work cannot be said to exercise judgement. Arguments are not findings, and this one has not been tested.

## Abstract

This paper defends opening a delivery programme with qualification rather than with technique. The argument runs in three parts. **First**, the engagement decision is the highest-leverage act in forward-deployed work, is made under the worst available information, and is systematically absent from the practice literature the programme is built on — a corpus of roughly ninety internal documents containing case studies that all succeed, one synthetic failure narrative, no exclusion criteria and a single recorded refusal. **Second**, the session's structure — unanswerable questions before any instruction — is a Productive Failure generation phase, which is a warranted design rather than a stylistic one, though its warrant comes from school and university studies and its application to working professionals is an extrapolation this paper concedes. **Third**, the platform workflow exists to make one durable property observable in the first hour: grounding renders an answer accurate within a corpus and silent outside it, with nothing in the output marking the boundary.

The paper records a design defect found while writing it. The session cited Kapur's assembly phase approvingly and did not implement its central condition — that consolidation must explicitly prompt comparison against the learner's own failed attempt — and the amendment is documented in §4.3 rather than applied silently.

The paper concedes that the stage is design reasoning with no external warrant; that its source corpus offers no counter-example, no declined engagement and no outcome data of any kind; that its pedagogical warrant is borrowed from a population unlike its learners; and that it teaches a disposition whose organisational precondition it does not address until §8's fourth objection and two stages later.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A practitioner arriving at an engagement is given a solution, a date and a room. Every incentive in that situation favours starting.

The customer has already spent political capital getting the work approved. The delivery organisation has a commercial interest in proceeding. The engineer has been selected for competence and reads hesitation as a failure of it. **None of those pressures is illegitimate, and together they make refusal structurally unavailable** unless somebody has rehearsed it.

The session's response is to make the refusal concrete before it is costly: a written condition, stated in week one, that would stop the work.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

This session assumes **ordinary professional experience** and nothing from this programme. It is one of very few that does.

Learners arrive with a near-universal prior that being asked to build something is the same as being asked whether to. They also arrive with widely varying exposure to AI tooling, which the session equalises by having everyone use an agent in the first hour on a question where its answer can be argued with.

## 3. Theoretical basis

This is the section the first draft of this paper got wrong. It asserted three design choices as reasoning of its own when a substantial research literature bears on all three, is held in this repository, and in one case **contradicts what the session actually does**. The correction is recorded in §4.3.

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is low — the concepts are few and mutually independent. The surplus is spent on the affective work of rehearsing a refusal, which no later session can afford at this length. This is a design judgement and carries no external warrant.

**3.2 The opening items are a Productive Failure generation phase, and that is a warranted design rather than a stylistic one.**

Kapur's Productive Failure is a two-phase, order-dependent design: learners generate multiple solutions to a task targeting a concept they have not been taught, fail, and are then taught ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). The mechanism the session leans on is the **failed generation effect** — attempting an answer before instruction improves later learning *even when the attempt is wrong*. That is precisely the situation item 2 creates: most learners cannot answer it, and the design wants them not to.

Three of Kapur's stated design conditions bind this session, and they are conditions rather than preferences:

- **Do not scaffold the generation phase.** His 2011 experiment manipulated guidance during problem-solving and found that prompts and hints which reduced the failure rate *reduced learning*. A version of this session that helped learners toward a good disqualifying condition during the opening would not be a gentler Productive Failure; it would be a worse one. This is why the release rule above gates §§4, 5 and 9.
- **The consolidation phase must explicitly prompt comparison.** Loibl and Leuders showed that presenting correct and incorrect solutions side by side achieves nothing on its own — only the condition instructed to compare them learned more. **The session as first written violated this.** See §4.3.
- **The norms are part of the design, not a preamble to it.** Kapur makes the dependency on the social surround explicit and directional.

That last condition is where a second literature enters, and it is the one that makes this session's subject matter unusually load-bearing.

**3.3 Refusal is a voice behaviour, and voice behaviour is conditional on safety.**

What the session asks a learner to rehearse — saying *this should not be built* to a room that has already decided to build it — is not primarily a cognitive act. It is speaking up when silence is cheaper, which is the construct Edmondson's psychological-safety literature studies and which Frazier's meta-analysis synthesises ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md); [research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)).

The consequence for this session is sharper than it first appears. **If refusal is conditional on safety, then teaching refusal to an individual is treating an environmental variable as a personal skill.** A graduate who has rehearsed a disqualifying condition and joins a team where dissent is costly has been given a capability they cannot exercise. The session does not solve this. §8's first objection is where it is confronted, and the confrontation is a concession rather than a rebuttal.

Kapur and Edmondson converge here from opposite directions — one saying the learning design needs supportive norms, the other saying the behaviour needs them — and the convergence is the strongest theoretical statement this session can make: **the thing being taught and the method of teaching it have the same precondition.**

**3.4 Independent convergence from error-management training.**

Keith and Frese reach the same ordering conclusion from a different literature: learners should meet the difficulty before they are given the resolution, and the payoff shows on transfer rather than during the session ([research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md)). Two independent research traditions agreeing on ordering is a stronger warrant than either alone, and it is the main reason this programme puts generation first everywhere rather than only here.

**3.5 The counterweight.** Effortful practice is not automatically productive. Macnamara's work on the limits of deliberate practice is the corrective held in this repository against reading difficulty as sufficient ([research/04-professional-formation/macnamara-deliberate-practice-limits.md](/research/04-professional-formation/macnamara-deliberate-practice-limits.md)), and Kapur's own model contains the failure quadrant: **Unproductive Failure** — pure discovery, producing low performance *and* low learning. A session that lets learners flounder without assembling their attempts into anything has landed in that quadrant, not in the intended one.

**3.6 Why the agent is introduced in the first hour.**

The session's second claim is that an instrument which agrees with you has not been used. Two findings make this more than an aphorism.

Capability in these systems is **jagged**: the boundary between competence and failure is uneven and does not track human-perceived difficulty ([research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md](/research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md)). Outside the boundary the failure mode is not refusal but confident error. Separately, experienced developers have been observed to be **slowed** by AI assistance while believing they were sped up ([research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md](/research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md)) — perceived and actual effect diverging in the direction that makes self-assessment unreliable.

There is a third strand the session uses implicitly and which deserves naming, because it is the reason a *feasibility* check is the right first use. Kambhampati's argument that large language models do not plan in the sense the word usually implies ([research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md)) bears directly on what an agent can be trusted to do in qualification. Retrieving and quoting a documented constraint is a task the tooling is well suited to. Judging whether an engagement should proceed is not. **The session uses the agent for the first and reserves the second for the learner**, and that division is the actual lesson of the workflow.

**3.7 Why the platform check is documentation retrieval rather than experiment.**

One further caution shapes the workflow. Published capability claims about these systems are systematically distorted by the conditions under which they are produced — Singh's analysis of leaderboard practice is the repository's record of this ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md)). A qualification check that rested on benchmark standings would inherit that distortion. A check that rests on a vendor's *stated operating envelope* — a scale claim, a networking limitation — is a different kind of evidence: definitional rather than comparative, and falsifiable by reading.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The movement sequence.** Situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**The opening items are the instrument.** Item 2 — what would have to be true for the right answer to be *do not build this* — is the one the design cares about. **Most learners cannot answer it concretely**, and the gap between the confident answers to items 1, 3 and 4 and the empty answer to item 2 is the session's actual content. Items 1, 3 and 4 are not filler; they establish the contrast that makes item 2's emptiness legible to the learner who wrote it.

**4.2 Why the failure modes are warning signs rather than definitions.** A definition is recognised after the fact; a warning sign is noticed during. This format is taken from the source practice corpus, which uses it well, and it is one of the few places where that corpus's form rather than its content is being borrowed.

**4.3 A defect found in this session, and the amendment.**

The first version of this session asked the five opening items and never returned to them. The learner generated, failed, and then read an explanation — but was never instructed to compare the explanation against what they had written.

**That is the exact condition Kapur's assembly phase requires, and Loibl and Leuders tested directly: side-by-side presentation of correct and incorrect solutions produced no benefit; only the explicitly-instructed comparison did.** The session was throwing away the mechanism it was built on, and the paper defending it cited that mechanism approvingly in §3.2 while the session violated it.

**The amendment:** exercise 5 now requires a closed-book restatement of the three selection axes, and exercise 3 requires the learner to return to their own item-2 answer and rewrite it against the criterion. Both are comparison prompts rather than recall prompts.

**The amendment is recorded rather than silently applied**, because the defect is instructive: a design can cite a mechanism correctly, describe it accurately, and fail to implement it, and nothing in the citation catches that. **Only reading the source at the depth of its design conditions catches it** — which is an argument for the research layer of this repository, and the clearest instance of it paying for itself.

**4.4 What the platform workflow is for.** It is not a tooling tutorial. It exists to make one durable claim physically observable in the first hour: **grounding makes an answer accurate within a corpus and silent outside it, and nothing in an ungrounded output tells you which side of that line you are on.** The instructions file forcing in-sentence citation is the apparatus that renders the absence visible. Every later platform session in this programme is a variation on that same observation.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The unfalsifiable condition.** The most likely outcome is a disqualifying condition that could never fire. *Countermeasure:* the review movement, where another person's only job is to say whether it could.

**Qualification as theatre.** A learner writes a refusal they have no intention of making. *Countermeasure:* none available in week one. The condition is revisited at [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) against a brief constructed so that it fires.

**Refusal as reflex.** A learner concludes that declining is the sophisticated answer and declines everything. *Countermeasure:* [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md) requires the cost of doing nothing to be sized, which makes refusal expensive too.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md), [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md), [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md). The position written here is reopened at S005 and tested against a disqualifying brief at S010.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. One specification item at [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md): the engagement position, assessed on whether the disqualifying condition is capable of firing — **never on whether the learner would in fact have declined.**

That distinction is doing more work than it appears to, and it follows from the entrustment literature this programme's assessment architecture is built on ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). An entrustable professional activity is a unit of practice that can be handed over; the judgement is about whether a supervisor would let this person do this unsupervised. **Qualification is a plausible EPA. Refusal is not**, because refusal is an outcome rather than an activity, and its correctness depends on facts about the engagement that the assessment cannot hold constant.

Assessing the *capability of the condition to fire* assesses the reasoning. Assessing whether the learner declined would assess their agreement with the assessor. The first is entrustment; the second is compliance.

A second reason for the same boundary comes from the narrative-assessment literature ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)): judgements of this kind are better recorded as reasoned narrative than as a score, because the useful information is *why* the assessor judged as they did. The specification item accordingly requires a written reason and not a mark.

**Nothing else is assessed.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: qualification is a commercial decision, not an engineering one, and teaching it to engineers is teaching them a job they will not have.**

At its strongest: in most delivery organisations the decision to take work sits with an account team, the engineer is assigned after it is made, and a graduate who arrives with a rehearsed refusal will find nobody willing to receive it.

**This is substantially correct and the design concedes it.** The rebuttal is narrower than the objection: the engineer is frequently the only person who can see the disqualifying condition, and an engineer who has never articulated one will not recognise it when it appears. The session teaches the recognition, not the authority. Whether recognition without authority is useful is untested.

**Objection 2: the stage is built on an absence.**

The source corpus does not refuse work, so this programme has inferred that it should. Inferring a practice from its absence in a document is weak. **Conceded without qualification.** The stage is class 3 throughout and every session in it says so.

One qualification of the concession, offered as mitigation and not as rescue: the corpus's silence is not uniform. It contains one refusal — declining to replace human reviewers in a legal-review process — which establishes that the practice *can* refuse and simply does not document doing so. That is a thinner basis than a stated exclusion criterion and it is not being presented as more.

**Objection 3: the pedagogical warrant is borrowed from schoolchildren.**

At its strongest, and it is strong: every study behind Productive Failure is a school, university or laboratory study. Kapur states that the cycle works in corporate training and adult education and **cites nothing in his reference list for that sentence**. This programme's learners are working professionals doing multi-week engagements. Applying a design validated on short mathematics and science interventions to that population is an extrapolation.

**Conceded, and it is the most serious methodological objection to this programme's entire teaching pattern rather than to this session alone.** The mitigation is that Keith and Frese reach the same ordering conclusion from error-management training, which *is* an adult workplace-training literature, and two independent traditions agreeing raises the prior. The mitigation is partial. **The declined fix was to weaken the design to something the evidence covers outright** — direct instruction with practice afterwards — and it was declined because the convergent warrant is judged strong enough to prefer the better-motivated design while recording the extrapolation. A reader who weighs the extrapolation more heavily than this paper does is making a defensible call.

**Objection 4: teaching refusal without teaching the conditions that permit it is close to negligent.**

If voice behaviour is conditional on psychological safety, a programme that installs the disposition and says nothing about the environment has equipped a learner to take a risk it has not helped them assess. The strongest form of this objection is that it could cause harm — a junior engineer refusing work in an unsafe team pays a real cost.

**Partially conceded.** The session does not address it; [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md) and [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) do, by requiring the cost of the refusal to be sized alongside the cost of the work. That is late. **The declined fix was to move the safety material into S001**, and it was declined on load grounds: the session already carries the affective work of rehearsing a refusal, and adding an analysis of when refusal is survivable would blunt the generation phase that §3.2 requires be left unscaffolded. **The cost of that decision is that S001 through S008 teach a disposition without its precondition**, and this paper records that as an accepted cost rather than a solved problem.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A substantial majority of learners will produce a disqualifying condition that a reviewer judges incapable of firing. *Instrument:* the review movement's binary judgement, collected per learner.

**Prediction 2.** Answers to opening item 2 will be markedly less specific than answers to items 1, 3 and 4. *Instrument:* the five written answers, coded *specific* / *generic* / *absent* by a second reader.

**Prediction 3.** Learners who used the agent to attack their own position will revise it more often than those who used it to elaborate. *Instrument:* the prompt record against the revision count. **If this fails, the session's claim that an agreeing instrument is unused has no support at this scale**, and the movement should be rewritten as an assertion rather than a demonstration.

**Prediction 4.** Learners instructed to compare their item-2 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 3, added by the §4.3 amendment) will produce disqualifying conditions judged capable of firing more often than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* the reviewer's binary judgement across two cohorts, one run before the amendment and one after — or, absent two cohorts, a within-cohort split on whether exercise 3 was completed. **This is the session's only direct test of the mechanism §3.2 rests on**, and a null result would mean the amendment in §4.3 was ceremony.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of learners will accept at least one agent-produced claim about the platform without a citation, despite the instructions file requiring one in every sentence. *Instrument:* the learner's submitted feasibility check, coded for whether each platform claim carries a page reference and a date. **This predicts the failure of the session's own countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 6.** Learners will report the platform check as the more useful half of the session and the opening items as the less useful, and the reverse will hold at [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) when the disqualifying condition is tested against a brief built to fire it. *Instrument:* a two-item preference question at the close of S001 and again at the close of S010. **This is a prediction about the gap between how learning feels and how it works**, and if it fails in the first half the session's opening is not doing what this paper claims.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the engagement decision is the highest-leverage act in this work, and that it is teachable.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The teaching *method* is well grounded — Productive Failure is read here in full, and error-management training converges on the same ordering independently — but that warrant is about the method and comes from a population unlike you. **The claim that qualification improves engagements has no evidence behind it at all.** Do not let the quality of the pedagogical citations transfer to the substantive thesis; they are about different things.

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper.** That is a rule of this repository rather than an accident of what was read, and it holds even where a source states a number in a full text that has been read.

**The concession worth remembering.** A programme that opens by teaching refusal, built on a corpus that never refuses, is making an argument and dressing it as practice. Hold it to that. And note §4.3: this paper caught its own session violating the mechanism this paper defends. **Assume there are others it has not caught.**

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** The account of what the practice corpus does and does not contain — no declined engagement, no non-synthetic failure narrative, no exclusion criteria, a readiness rubric composed of customer dispositions, one recorded refusal — is recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md), which rests on a complete reading. The claim that a stated solution signals thinking done elsewhere is recorded there as a practitioner judgement, not a finding.

The Productive Failure design conditions in §3.2 — that the design is two-phase and order-dependent, that the generation phase must not be scaffolded, that consolidation must explicitly prompt comparison, and that the effect depends on the surrounding norms — are recorded at [research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md), **which rests on a complete reading of the source text.** The same note records the author's own concession that failure is sufficient but not necessary, and that no adult-professional study appears in his reference list.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That psychological safety conditions voice behaviour, and that self-efficacy beliefs condition whether a person attempts an act they expect to be costly. The notes for both are held in this repository but were not re-read for this paper, so the claims are stated at the level of direction and mechanism only. That entrustment decisions are better recorded as reasoned narrative than as a score. That large language models do not plan in the sense the word usually implies, and that published capability comparisons are distorted by the conditions of their production. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted for any of these.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The eight-movement session structure; the decision to open a delivery programme with qualification; the claim that a condition incapable of firing is not a condition; the claim that an instrument which agrees with you has not been used; the division of labour in §3.6 that gives the agent retrieval and reserves judgement for the learner; the judgement in §7 that qualification is a plausible EPA and refusal is not; the entire existence of Stage 1; the load-grounds rejection of the declined fix in §8's fourth objection; and all six predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The three build paths by service model, the selection criteria stated as technical capabilities, timeline and control requirements, the basic setup's lack of network isolation, and the positioning of Copilot Studio for small-scale department-level deployments are recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md), read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches. **That note marks the material highly perishable** and records that the Agent Framework SDK is documented as formerly Semantic Kernel. Agent mode determines its own steps and iterates rather than stopping after one response, and requests confirmation before running a terminal command or a tool that is not built in — with the confirmation being **a dismissible default rather than a boundary**, auto-confirmable per session, per solution or permanently. Read 2026-07-30; the agent-mode page carried two product version ranges simultaneously at that reading.

**The silences that matter.** The documentation publishes no measure of how often the agent's self-correction loop converges, or how often it reports completion on work that is wrong — which is exactly the property this session's review step depends on. It publishes no adoption data, no failure-rate data and no cost comparison for any of the build paths, so the selection guidance is a reasoned framework and not a validated finding. And it states no scale envelope at all for most of the tools it compares; **the Copilot Studio statement is the exception rather than the pattern**, which is why exercise 2 tells learners to record an absence as a finding rather than to infer a limit from one.
