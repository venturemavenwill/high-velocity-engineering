# WP-087 — Engineering Feedback That Goes Somewhere

**Seminar day:** [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) · **Module:** [M18](/wiki/modules/M18.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 6
**Platform anchor:** The Microsoft Learn MCP Server and vendor documentation feedback paths — a defect report that reaches the people who can fix it, and what happens to one that does not

> ## Release rule
>

> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Feedback item** | A structured written report of product friction, with a specified set of fields |
> | **Loop** | Receipt, obligation, ownership, states, closure and notification back to the reporter. **None of it is specified in the source that specifies the item** |
> | **Considered no** | A legitimate state in which a report is read, understood and declined with a reason |
> | **Silence** | A published absence — a quantity or boundary a reader relying on the documentation would need and cannot get. Reportable in the same fields as a defect |
> | **Closure evidence** | Anything externally observable that distinguishes a report which was acted on from one that was never opened |
> | **Voice behaviour** | Speaking up with a concern when silence is the lower-risk option; the organisational-behaviour construct a friction report belongs to |
> | **Definitional versus evidential authority** | A source can establish with total authority what a thing *is* and supply no evidence at all that it *works* |
> | **Golden dataset** | A curated question-and-answer benchmark built to test changes. Described in the anchor's own engineering blog; **no result from it is published** |

## In plain terms

An engineer hits friction in a product while delivering with it. That friction is genuinely valuable information — it comes from someone using the thing under real load, on a real problem, with a real deadline, which is the population product teams find hardest to reach.

There is a published standard for writing it down, and it is a good one. **A title, a summary, a categorisation block naming the product area, the kind of problem, the stage of work it was hit at, its impact and its time frame, an ordinal priority, then steps to reproduce it and any notes.** Those are the right fields, they are specific enough to act on, and a report written to them can be assessed by somebody who was not there.

**And that is where the source stops.** No routing, no triage, no ownership, no service-level expectation and no closure loop appears anywhere in the section. It does not claim to describe one. It teaches an engineer to write a report a product team could act on, which is exactly what it says it does — and a reader who takes it for an institutional mechanism has supplied that reading themselves.

This session's work is the part that is missing, and its honest position is that nobody has published one.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching a documented writing standard exactly as written, then requiring the learner to specify the mechanism the standard omits, and then requiring one report about a published *silence* rather than about a defect. The argument runs in four parts. **First**, a report format without a loop transfers the cost of product improvement onto the person who hit the problem while returning nothing to them, and the reporter's incentive collapses on the second unanswered report. **Second**, the omission is invisible precisely because the standard is complete and well made within its own scope, so a reader supplies the institutional reading themselves. **Third**, whether a report lands is a governance property of the receiving institution rather than a quality property of the report, which is why the session's platform anchor is a documentation family whose external change register attributes nothing to anybody. **Fourth**, the highest-value report an engineer can write is about an absence, because a silence has a named consequence, a cheap fix and no advocate.

The session produces one feedback item written to the standard, one silence found in first-party documentation and written to the same fields, and a six-part routing specification with honest blanks where the learner's organisation has no answer. The paper concedes that the loop is inferred from an omission; that this programme has now made that move in three separate stages; that no outcome is reported anywhere for structured feedback of any kind; and that the engineer taught to specify a loop will in most organisations have no authority to install one.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

**A writing standard with nothing behind it is a request for unpaid work from the person with the least spare time.**

The engineer who hits the friction is mid-delivery, under a date, and already carrying the workaround. Writing the report costs them an hour and returns nothing they can observe. **They will do it once. Whether they do it twice depends entirely on something the standard does not describe.**

Three specific defects follow from the absence.

**The report has no addressee.** A queue is not a person. Where receipt is unowned, triage happens when somebody has capacity, and a report's fate is determined by arrival timing rather than by content.

**There is no legitimate no.** A loop with no state meaning *read, understood and declined, for this reason* forces every report into either action or silence. Silence is cheaper, so silence is what arrives, and the reporter cannot distinguish it from never having been read. **This is the same failure that a compliance regime has when it offers no approvable way to say *we cannot comply yet*: the regime is not resisted, it is quietly ignored.**

**Nothing returns to the reporter.** If the only observable difference between a report that was acted on and a report that was never opened is on the receiving side, then from where the engineer sits **the loop does not exist**, and behaving as though it does not is rational.

And underneath all three, the ordinary attrition: in the supplied situation the third report was never written, because the person who hit the problem left that week. **Knowledge that lives in one person's intention to write something down is knowledge with an expiry date attached to their employment.**

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session assumes the decision-record discipline from [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md), the unexplainable agent behaviour from [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md), the unseen incident from [S063](/wiki/seminars/S063.md), and the duty from [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) to report a result that undermines you.

The [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) retrieval is the important one. **Reporting friction in a product your own organisation sells, or recommended, is a small instance of that duty**, and it is the instance learners are least likely to recognise as one.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile, and why the session opens with obedience.** Intrinsic load is low — the field set is short and unambiguous. The surplus is spent on the discovery in 3.2, which requires the learner to finish something before they can see what is missing from it. Executing a specification exactly is a faster and more reliable route to its gaps than reading an analysis of it. **A learner who is told the gap in advance produces a critique; a learner who hits it produces a specification.** This is a design judgement and carries no external warrant.

**3.2 Reporting friction is a voice behaviour, and voice behaviour is conditional on safety.** This is the strongest theoretical claim the session can make, and it reframes the whole artefact.

Writing up a defect in a product your own organisation sells, or recommended, is speaking up when silence is cheaper — the construct Edmondson studies and Frazier synthesises ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md); [research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)). Edmondson defines it as **"the belief that the work environment is safe for interpersonal risk taking"** and locates it at group level: **"psychological safety seems to 'live' at the level of the group"**, so that even in a strong corporate culture there are pockets of both.

Two consequences bind this session. **The report that was never written was not withheld because the standard was unclear.** And **a loop with no legitimate refusal state fails at both ends simultaneously**: the receiving team obliged to action everything stops reading, and the reporter who learns that stops writing. The considered-no state is not a courtesy; it is the only state that makes the mechanism survivable for the people who have to operate it.

**The limits are real and travel with the claim.** The Edmondson note covers a 2019 trade book read in full and a 1999 paper that was **not read**, with case studies drawn almost entirely from secondary sources. The Frazier note is written from the official abstract of a closed-access meta-analysis. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude from either appears anywhere in this wiki.**

**3.3 Whether a report lands is a governance property, not a quality property.** The evaluation-governance literature supplies the cleanest available demonstration. An audit of the leaderboard that had become the de facto public ranking of frontier models found its scores distorted **by undisclosed platform policy rather than by any technical flaw**, producing **"biased Arena scores due to selective disclosure of performance results"** and, ultimately, **"overfitting to Arena-specific dynamics rather than general model quality"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md)). Its recommendations are policy recommendations: disclose private testing, sample equitably, deprecate transparently.

The transfer is exact. **The determinant of whether information changes an institution is whether the institution's handling of that information is observable from outside.** The audit's authors could see the distortion only because battle records were externally visible. Where the process is not externally visible, no amount of good reporting produces evidence that it works — which is why the session requires the learner to attempt attribution against a public change register and report where it stops.

**Its limits:** allocation figures are external estimates by the paper's own account, findings are specific to one platform at one time, and **nothing in it establishes that models are not improving**. **No figure from it is reproduced here.**

**3.4 Why reproducibility is the report's load-bearing property.** A report that cannot be reproduced is closed as not reproducible — a correct decision on the receiving side that teaches the reporter the mechanism is theatre. Reproducibility is also the property most degraded by delay, which is an argument for writing at the moment of friction rather than at the end of the engagement. The practice-state observation that many teams evaluate by eyeballing results ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md)) is the same defect one layer along: adequate to get something moving, inadequate for anything that must be handed to somebody else. **That source is a practitioner text rather than a study, heavily citational, with much of its best material resting on unattributed interviews.**

**3.5 Why a generic report is worse than none.** The same reasoning applies here as to a generic mitigation: it cannot be assessed, implemented or refuted, so it consumes attention and produces nothing, and it degrades the credibility of the specific reports arriving beside it. **Volume is the enemy of a loop that has no triage.**

**3.6 Why a silence is the highest-value item an engineer can write, and why the anchor proves it.** A defect has an advocate — the person it blocks. **An absence has none**, because everybody who needed the missing quantity worked around it privately and nobody wrote it down.

The session's anchor supplies a set of specimens that are verified, public and cheap to check ([research/06-microsoft-platform/learn-mcp-server-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/learn-mcp-server-docs.md)). Freshness is documented as cadence — **"refreshes incrementally after content updates and performs a full refresh once a day"** — which bounds nothing about indexing latency. Rate limits are confirmed to exist and no value, window, error code or retry guidance is published. Corpus scope is defined only negatively. Deprecated content **"redirect[s] to the next best appropriate content"**, so a fetch of a known-good address may silently return something else. Each of those fills the standard's fields exactly: a stage of work, an impact, a reproduction step that is *read the page and look for the number*.

**And the anchor also supplies the session's sharpest single fact.** A first-party engineering blog describes a **"golden dataset"** of questions, approved answers and best source articles, and before-and-after tests on indexing changes, in detail — **and publishes no result from any of it.** The apparatus of measurement is described and its outputs are withheld. **That is what a closed loop looks like from outside: work was done, something changed, and nothing crossed the boundary that would let anybody tell.**

**The source's limits are severe.** First-party, definitional in authority and evidential nowhere; extremely perishable, with a developer reference reserving the right to change the tool list without notice; collapsed sections on three host pages did not expand and were not read; the Terms of Use is a stated condition of use and was not read. **Its repository's outcome claims are marketing and are prohibited in this wiki in any form, including as paraphrase.**

**3.7 Why the loop must return something observable.** Where a feedback channel is closed, a system's faults present as the person's failure. **Here the direction reverses — where the return channel is closed, the product's faults present as the reporter's futility** — and the consequence is identical: the information stops.

**3.8 Why honest blanks are required.** A learner asked to specify a loop for their own organisation will invent one rather than admit they do not know how it works. **An invented process is worse than a blank, because a blank prompts somebody to find out.** This is the same rule the programme applies to unreachable dimensions at [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md).

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**The standard is used unmodified.** No improvements, no additional fields, no local adaptation, on the first pass. The point is to reach the end of a well-made artefact and find nothing there.

**The six questions are asked in a fixed order** — who receives, what is owed on receipt, who owns it afterwards, what states exist, what closes it, what the reporter sees — because the last is the one that gets dropped and the order puts it where it cannot be.

**The third report is written retrospectively**, by somebody who did not hit the problem, from whatever remains. This is deliberately frustrating and it is the session's clearest demonstration of what the absent loop costs: the report is worse, and the reason it is worse is not the writer's fault.

**The closing observation is about the weakest link, and it is not the writing.** Almost every loop of this kind fails at receipt or at return, and a learner who identifies the writing as the weak point has not yet understood the artefact they just produced.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The report as the loop.** *Countermeasure:* the artefact has two parts and only the second is the session's work.

**Volume as evidence.** Reports raised becomes the measure. *Countermeasure:* the loop specification requires a state model, and a state model makes the fate of a report the thing being described.

**The unreproducible report.** *Countermeasure:* the reproduction steps are read by somebody who was not on the team, in the review movement.

**The report nobody may decline.** *Countermeasure:* the required considered-no state.

**The invented process.** *Countermeasure:* the explicit instruction to write *unknown*, and the assessment position that a blank is a finding.

**Feedback about a preference.** The learner reports something they would have designed differently. *Countermeasure:* partial. The standard's own categorisation forces an impact and a stage of work, which a preference cannot fill honestly — but a determined learner can fill it dishonestly and the session will not catch it.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No specification item and no entrustment level. This session generates observation for the entrustment decision at [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md).

**What assessors record**, as narrative: whether the report was reproducible by somebody outside the team; whether the loop specification named a person or a role at receipt; whether a considered-no state existed; whether the reporter's return was specified; whether blanks were honest; and whether the learner identified the weakest link correctly.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: specifying a loop is not an engineer's job, and a learner who arrives at a large organisation with a designed feedback process will be told so.**

At its strongest: routing, triage, ownership and service levels between a delivery organisation and a product organisation are questions of organisational design, negotiated between functions with budgets, and settled far above the engineer who hit the bug. Teaching an engineer to specify one produces a document nobody asked for, addressed to nobody, and a learner who mistakes their frustration for a mandate.

**This is conceded in substance.** The engineer will not own the loop and will very often not be able to influence it. The rebuttal is narrower than the objection: **an engineer who has specified what a loop would have to contain can tell, in one question, whether the one they have been handed is real** — and can decide honestly whether to keep writing reports into it. That is a smaller claim than the session's framing suggests, and it is the defensible one. **The fix that was considered and declined** was to teach only the writing standard and note the absence in passing. It was rejected because a standard taught without its gap will be taught as a mechanism, which is exactly the misreading the source itself does not invite and readers perform anyway.

**Objection 2: the entire session is built on an omission, and this programme keeps doing that.**

At its strongest: the source specifies a writing standard and says nothing about a loop. It does not claim to describe one. Inferring that a loop ought to exist, and that its absence is a defect, is reading a document as though its scope were an argument. **This programme has now made this move in Stage 1, in Stage 8 and here**, and repetition does not strengthen a weak inference — it establishes a habit.

**Conceded without qualification, including the observation about repetition.** The move is weak, it is the same move three times, and this paper says so rather than defending it. What can be said in mitigation is small: unlike Stage 1's omission, this one has a directly observable consequence in the supplied situation — two reports whose fate nobody can state — and a session built on an absence that produces a visible symptom is on marginally firmer ground than one built on an absence that produces none. **That is a difference of degree and the objection is entitled to say it is not enough.**

**Objection 3: teaching learners to hunt for silences will produce a flood of pedantic reports about missing numbers, and will make the loop problem worse.**

At its strongest: a documentation team that receives a stream of items saying *you did not publish a rate limit* is receiving noise dressed as rigour. Most such absences are deliberate — the anchor's developer reference **explicitly and reasonably declines** to document request and response formats, and says why: the interface may change dynamically, so a client should call `tools/list` rather than rely on a published schema. **A learner trained to treat every absence as a defect will report that decision as a defect**, and the receiving team will correctly conclude that the reporter has not understood the product.

**This is conceded in part and it identifies a real hazard in the session's design.** The mitigation is the standard's own categorisation block, which forces an impact and a stage of work: a deliberate, explained absence has no unfilled impact, because the documentation supplies the alternative in the same sentence. An unexplained absence with a named consequence — *cannot size an agentic loop* — does. **The distinction is the difference between an absence and an unexplained absence**, and the session's fields carry it.

**The fix that was considered and declined** was to require every silence item to be accompanied by a search of the whole documentation family for an explanation of the absence. It was declined on load grounds: the session already asks the learner to write a report, design a loop and attempt an attribution, and adding an exhaustive negative search would push the silence work past the point where anybody finishes it. **The cost of that decision is that some fraction of learner-produced silence items will be reports against deliberate design decisions**, and this paper records that as an accepted cost rather than a solved problem.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most loop specifications will name a queue, a team or a system at receipt rather than a person or a role. *Instrument:* the specifications, coded at the receipt step.

**Prediction 2.** The reporter-return step will be the most frequently omitted of the six, despite being last in a fixed order. *Instrument:* the six steps, checked for completion, counted by step.

**Prediction 3.** Learners will more often invent a process for their own organisation than record a blank, on the first attempt. *Instrument:* the specifications against a follow-up question asking where each answer came from.

**Prediction 4.** Most learners will identify the writing as the loop's weakest link. *Instrument:* the closing statements, classified by which of receipt, obligation, ownership, states, closure, return or writing was named. **If most name receipt or return, the session's premise is already held by this population** and the working movement can be shortened.

**Prediction 5.** A substantial minority of learner-produced silence items will report a deliberate, explained absence — most commonly the developer reference's stated refusal to publish request and response formats — despite the categorisation block that §8's third objection claims will catch it. *Instrument:* the silence items, coded by a second reader as *unexplained absence with a named consequence* or *explained design decision*. **This predicts the failure of the session's own countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 6.** Learners will attempt attribution against a public change register and, on being unable to complete it, will report that as an obstacle rather than as the result — despite exercise 4 stating that the failed check is the work. *Instrument:* the exercise 4 write-ups, coded for whether the incompletion is presented as a finding or as a limitation of the exercise. **This is the same reading error §9 of [WP-068](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-068.md) predicts about the failed traceability check**, and a cohort that makes it in both places has not transferred the move.

**None of these has been measured.** No cohort has run this session, and no instrument named above has been applied to anybody.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a writing standard with no loop behind it is a request for unpaid work; that a loop with no legitimate way to decline will be ignored rather than resisted; and that the report which was never written is usually the one that cost the most.

**What the evidence licenses.** The field set and the absence around it are verified from a public, page-dated source. **Everything about the loop is this design's argument**, and no outcome for structured feedback of any kind is reported anywhere.

**The concession worth remembering.** §8 concedes that this session is built on an omission and that the programme has done this three times. If that reads as a pattern rather than as three independent judgements, you have read it correctly, and [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) is where an objection to it belongs.
