# WP-087 — The Safety Case

**Seminar day:** [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) · **Module:** [M18](/wiki/modules/M18.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md)
**Strands:** SG · ME · SY · **Complexity class:** 6
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Foundry observability — agent tracing into the one Application Insights resource connected to the project · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read) · Foundry MCP Server (preview) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching a safety case as a bounded claim whose strength lies in its boundary, against the widespread and intuitive belief that a safety argument is strengthened by breadth. It defends a contrasting-case set in which the shortest and least ambitious case is the only one still true, and defends bounding that payload on the day so that a cohort does not convert it into a reflex for minimal claims. It defends requiring every evidence item to carry the direction and magnitude of the error that would hurt the claim in the same field, and defends the explicit permission to write *uncharacterised*, on the ground that a mandatory-magnitude rule manufactures numbers. **It defends adding a second column of the same shape — the command that produces the item, with an explicit permission to write *not reproducible* — and a shipped bundle that runs them**, on the ground that an evidence item nobody can produce is an evidence item nobody has checked. It defends treating a detection commitment as a claim rather than a reassurance, and defends the presence of the acceptance owner at the task. **Section 7 reports the discharge of three inbound retention obligations from WP-066, WP-068 and WP-069.** It concedes that the four-case corpus is an artefact of the design's own construction, that the day's expiry claim is asserted rather than demonstrated, and that the reproducibility requirement quietly narrows what a case may rest on in a direction the design has not measured.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The students can already do every constituent act. They can state a claim ([S004](/wiki/seminars/S004.md)), weigh evidence asymmetrically ([S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md)), characterise a judge's error ([S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md)), model a threat ([S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md)), design detection ([S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md)) and say when something cannot be done ([S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md)). The assembly is not obviously hard.

It is hard for one reason, and the reason is dispositional rather than cognitive: **the boundary is what makes the case strong, and stating it makes the author look less capable.**

An engineer asked whether a system is safe, by an organisation about to depend on it, is under a pressure the programme has characterised precisely at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md). The audience wants a broad answer. A narrow answer sounds like hedging, and this cohort has been told at length that unusable hedging is a failure symmetric with overclaiming — which means the day has to install a distinction the cohort will not initially see: **a narrow claim is not a hedge.** A hedge is a claim with its strength removed. A bounded claim is a claim with its scope stated and its strength intact, and it is usable in a way the broad version is not, because a reader can check whether their case falls inside it.

Three further difficulties sit underneath.

**Evidence is treated as a quantity rather than a structure.** A student assembling a case will list what they have. The list is the easy part and phase 1 item 2 gets it in three minutes; item 3 asks for the error direction of each item and most of the list will not survive. C2's defect — an eleven-month gate record cited as evidence, with the gate's own false-pass rate never characterised — is the same defect the cohort built into the substrate themselves at [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md), and [WP-066](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-066.md) §6 named this day as the occasion on which it becomes nameable.

**And evidence is treated as a thing that exists rather than as a thing that is produced.** This is the practical turn's contribution to the day and it is a second problem of the same shape. An engineer writing a case will cite what they remember seeing. Item 3's second column asks what command would put it in front of a sceptical reader, and the list shortens again — not because the evidence was false, but because it lives in a store with a retention policy, behind a role assignment, at a level of detail somebody else's default decided. **A case is a document a stranger will check, and the stranger will check it later.** The day's answer is a shipped bundle that regenerates every reproducible item, and an explicit permission to write *not reproducible*, which is the same construction as *uncharacterised* and exists for the same reason: a mandatory rule manufactures compliance, and compliance here would mean cases built from whatever happens to be queryable.

**Assumptions are invisible in exactly one direction.** Students will produce environment assumptions readily, because the environment is what they have been reasoning about all programme. They will not produce operator assumptions, because the operator is themselves and the self does not appear as a premise; and they will not produce adversary assumptions, because M16 taught the adversary as a subject rather than as an assumption class. C3 exists to make the omission visible as a structural feature of an otherwise excellent case, not as carelessness.

**A case is written to be read once, by someone entitled to disbelieve it, who is not present.** That is [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md)'s condition and it is why this day sits after it rather than before.

**If a student's component has no release gate and no detection design** — possible where the component was inherited late or is not on the release path — the day supplies the substrate's own, and the assessor narrative records which evidence in the case is the student's own work. This costs ten minutes of step 4 and is the stated trade.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Moderate intrinsic, high organisational.** The individual elements are simple. What is not simple is that steps 1, 2, 5 and 7 are mutually constraining: narrowing the claim removes evidence requirements and removes assumptions; adding an assumption may rescue an inference and creates a new defeater; naming a defeater may force the claim narrower. **Students will iterate, and the design expects them to** — the ninety minutes is sized for two or three passes over a one-page artefact rather than one pass over a long one.

**Step 3's bundle adds a fourth term to that loop and it is the reason the day is at its load ceiling.** Running a query returns a result, the result can contradict what step 2 says the item establishes, and that sends the student back to step 1. The design accepts one round of this and no more: the bundle's skeleton is supplied, the store is one the cohort has used for a quarter, and **the specification does not assess how many items reproduced**, so a student under time pressure can mark items *not reproducible* and proceed without penalty. That escape is what keeps the addition from converting a class-6 argumentation task into a debugging session.

The load is managed by giving the artefact a fixed skeleton and leaving only the boundary and the defeaters unsupported. Step 4's inference-step headings are supplied, step 6 reuses S068's four-part target verbatim, step 8 reuses S024's reply form, and step 3's bundle has a supplied skeleton. Steps 1 and 7 are unsupported because a supplied boundary template produces the boundary the template implies, and a supplied defeater list is answered rather than generated.

Expertise reversal applies to step 6 for any student who built a strong detection design at S068: for them the step is transcription. That is accepted; the step's function here is to make the commitment visible inside the argument, not to teach the commitment.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Writing the claim cold at item 1, before any framework,* and comparing it against step 1 at phase 8.
- *An error-direction column and a reproducibility column for every evidence item,* items 3 and step 2, which is where most phase 1 lists collapse twice.
- *Producing the evidence four other authors cited, before knowing how their cases turned out,* phase 3.
- *Committing a ranking before the outcome,* phase 3.
- *A sealed prediction about the query the agent will write that runs and misleads,* item 8.
- *Generating defeaters against your own case,* step 7.
- *An acceptance owner in the room who may decline,* step 8.
- *A partner who reads the case alone and names a use you did not intend to cover,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *A formal argument notation.* Structured notations carry more and cost a day to teach. The four-boundary form is what a one-page case needs and the omission is stated at point 1.
- *A regulated-domain case.* Out of scope and named as unsafe at point 1. A statutory safety argument has a prescribed form this one will not satisfy.
- *A hostile reviewer.* The accountable staff member acts in good faith, as every party in this programme's assessed occasions does. An adversarial review is a different skill and is not in the programme.
- *Quantified risk.* The design does not require probabilities on defeaters. [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md)'s discipline would demand intervals the substrate cannot supply, and requiring them would produce invented numbers — the same reason step 2 permits *uncharacterised* and *not reproducible*.
- *Requiring every evidence item to be reproducible.* Available, obviously attractive, and rejected. It would produce cases assembled out of what the trace store happens to hold, which is the construct-instrument substitution [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) exists to prevent, performed on a safety argument.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The four-boundary case form is **safe as the argument for a component you operate, are handing to a named receiving party inside a known organisation, and whose failure is inconvenient rather than injurious** — which is the substrate's domain constraint and is what makes the whole module runnable. It is **unsafe in a domain with a statutory safety argument**, where the notation, the independence of the assessor and the evidence standard are all prescribed and where a one-page four-boundary case will be rejected on form before its content is read; and it is **unsafe where failure injures somebody**, because the four-boundary form has no mechanism for the asymmetry between a claim that is wrong about throughput and a claim that is wrong about harm, and a student who carries this form into such a domain will produce a confident argument with no place to put the thing that matters most. The substrate's constraint is what makes the form safe here and is not a property of the systems these students will work on next. This is stated at phase 4 point 1.

**A second strong-form scope statement, on the reproducibility requirement.** A reproducible evidence bundle is **safe as a means of making a case checkable by a reader who was not present, for a component whose evidence lives in a store the reader can be given access to, within that store's retention window.** It is **unsafe as a completeness criterion**: nothing in the platform's documentation states a sampling rate for traces, so a bundle that runs and returns rows licenses no claim that the rows are all the rows. It is **unsafe across the retention boundary**, where a query that produced evidence in July returns nothing in November and the case looks falsified when only its store has aged. And it is **unsafe as a substitute for the error-structure column**: a gate record reproduces perfectly and still cannot show a false pass, which is exactly C2's defect surviving the new requirement intact. **The permission to write *not reproducible*, and the specification's refusal to assess how many items reproduced, are where a student applying the requirement outside its range would fail** — a case that dropped an uncharacterisable-but-important item in order to raise its reproducibility count fails the same check that fails an invented magnitude. This is stated at phase 4 points 9 and 10.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **C4's outcome**.

The room ranks four cases and a substantial share will place the one-page case last, on criteria — thoroughness, traceability, evidence volume — that the programme itself has taught them. Then they are told that it is the only one still true, and that its boundary excluded the three failures in advance. The reversal is not rhetorical: it is adjudicated by an outcome the groups committed against before seeing, which is the only construction that makes a belief reversal stick rather than register as a clever framing.

Competence is supported by the artefact being one page and by every constituent skill being two quarters or more established. Nothing on the day is new except the assembly.

Autonomy is at step 1. The boundary is entirely the student's, and there is no answer key: a narrower claim than the evidence requires is not penalised, and neither is a broader one that the evidence and the assumptions actually support. What is penalised is an unbounded axis.

Relatedness comes from the accountable staff member at step 8, who is the person the phrase *acceptably safe* refers to and who may decline. That is the same construction [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) used and it works for the same reason: the acceptance is real, so point 2 is a fact about the room rather than a principle.

The affective risk is a specific and predictable one: **a student whose case cannot be made.** Some components will not support any claim the student is willing to sign — the evidence is thin, the assumptions are unowned, the detection is absent. That is a correct finding and it will feel like a failure at the end of three years on a component the student is attached to. The countermeasures are structural: step 8 is the reduction, which turns *I cannot make the case you want* into the day's assessed professional act rather than an admission; a declined case is a pass and this is written in the specification; and the instructor states once that the programme's own position, from [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md) onward, is that the finding that a claim cannot be made is a result and not an absence of one.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Eight items. Items 2 and 3 are adjacent so that the length difference between the evidence list, the error-rate column and the reproducibility column is the day's opening finding, produced by the student rather than reported to them. Item 5 pretests C2's defect. Item 7 is a numeric self-prediction resolved at phase 8. **Item 8 is the sealed Copilot prediction**, administered here per instrumentation rule 8.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Seven retrievals spanning M16 at one quarter and S009 at two years, the oldest scaffolded per the pattern. The bridge joins the programme's oldest epistemic result to its newest operational one and produces point 4 from the room.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four cases for one change, matched on change, organisation and writing moment, with the corpus's construction disclosed per instrumentation rule 3 — including that it was written as an exercise, that the authors knew they were being compared, and that the change was chosen because its outcome was informative. **Twenty of the forty-five minutes are spent producing the evidence the four cases cite, and the results are recorded before the outcome records appear.** Commit-before-outcome. Payload C4, bounded by the final question. **The evidence-production leg carries its own disclosure**: what a case written more than a quarter ago can still produce is partly a fact about a retention setting, and the room is told so before it draws a conclusion.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Points 1, 5 and 7 carry scope statements, two in strong form; point 6 carries the normative exception and names [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) for its conditions. Point 3 is the [WP-066](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-066.md) discharge and point 4 is the [WP-068](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-068.md) discharge. **Points 9 and 10 are the day's perishable platform content and its named defaults, and point 9 carries the documentation silence — a ninety-day portal window and a configurable retention are both stated, and no sampling rate is stated anywhere, so no case may claim its trace evidence is complete.**

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Eight steps on the student's own component, with the acceptance owner present for the last twenty-five minutes. Steps 1 and 7 unsupported. **Step 3 ships a bundle that regenerates every reproducible evidence item, and the artefact carries a prompt record, an intervention record and a frontier statement. Three inbound rule-6 elements sit inside the artefact rather than beside it** — the [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) selection at step 1, the [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) mute limit at step 6, and the [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) overruled claim at step 8 — because each constrains what the case may say, and a constraint recorded outside the case does not constrain it.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A correct case read as saying more than it said, with the boundary one line below the quoted sentence, and no careless party. **Then the agent case: a query that ran, returned true rows, and was written against a table documented as being deprecated on a date inside the module — an undermining defeater arriving from outside the system, which the reviewer had no reason to look for.**

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Document-only partner test, the sixth in the programme and per instrumentation rule 2 **not compared against the earlier five.** The first question tests the boundary from outside, which the author structurally cannot do.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 1 against step 1; item 3's two columns before and after; item 7 against actual; **item 8 opened and the cohort frontier hit rate recorded**; the C4 ranking distribution and how many rankings moved on the evidence-production result; the unowned-assumption cohort total; the step 8 decline rate.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has written a compliance document professionally will produce a well-formed case quickly. Predicted failure: **they will produce C2** — clean structure, proper nesting, evidence that is institutionally acceptable and epistemically uncharacterised — because that is what the form rewards in practice and nobody has ever asked them for the error direction. Countermeasure: step 2's same-field error requirement is a hard specification requirement and is not satisfiable by structure; **step 3's bundle makes the reproducibility claim testable inside the same ninety minutes**; and phase 7's partner is instructed to ask, of the strongest-looking evidence item, what its false-pass rate is.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is at step 1: a student who cannot bound a claim because they cannot see what the axes would be for their component. Countermeasures: the four axes are named and exemplified in C4, which is in front of them; the task-after at phase 3 has already required them to name the four boundaries for all four cases, so the operation has been performed eight times on somebody else's work before it is performed once on their own; and staff answer questions of fact about the component's use, context and population, which are matters of record rather than judgement.

**A new failure mode belonging to the practical turn: the bundle eats the day.** A student whose first query does not run will debug it, because debugging is familiar and rewarding and writing a boundary is neither. Predicted failure: ninety minutes produce a working bundle and a one-sentence claim. Countermeasures: the bundle's skeleton is supplied; **the specification does not assess how many items reproduced**, and this is stated in writing before the phase; *not reproducible* is an admissible entry that takes four seconds to write; and staff are briefed that the correct intervention is to tell a stuck student to mark the item and move on, which is the only content instruction staff give on this day.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** A group that ranks C4 first before the outcome has, in the design's terms, not failed productively — and it is more likely here than at most days, because [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) trained this cohort to distrust broad claims and C4 is visibly the modest one. The difficulty is relocated to the final question — *what is C4 unable to be used for* — whose answer is a real cost borne by a real party and which the design's own trials of the case set indicate groups do not reach. **Fallback if a group answers that too:** they are asked to write the case C4's author would have to write for the new use, and to say which of C4's five evidence items transfer to it. Most do not, and the reason they do not is the day's point 3 arriving before phase 4.

**Affective failure.** Two, and the second is the serious one.

The first is **the modest-claim reflex**: a cohort that takes C4's lesson as *claim less* will produce cases so narrow that they license nothing, which is [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s unusable-hedging failure in a new costume. Countermeasures: the final question bounds the payload on the day; the specification does not reward narrowness and fails only unbounded axes; and phase 7's first question asks the partner to name a use the claim *covers* that the author did not intend, which finds over-narrow claims by finding the accidental coverage in them.

The second is **the case that cannot be made**, described in §3.3. Its full countermeasure set is there. It is named as a failure mode as well as a mechanism because the design expects it to occur for a real share of the cohort and does not want it to arrive as a surprise to the instructor in the room.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) (one week, where everything the case does not cover becomes something a person must be told, and where the case's uncharacterised evidence becomes a disclosure question), [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) (two weeks, where the unowned assumptions must be owned or excluded and the expiry becomes a warranty term) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) (three weeks, where the case is handed over and read cold by the receiving party).

**[S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) at one week is the immediate consequence and the module's hinge.** A safety case is a statement of what is covered; the residual-risk statement is its complement, and it is written from this artefact rather than from scratch. The two days are adjacent deliberately: a residual-risk statement composed without a bounded case in front of the author is a list of worries.

**[S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) at two weeks consumes two specific outputs.** The unowned-assumption set becomes either an owner in the handover artefact or an entry in the warranty's exclusion set, and an assumption in neither is a fail condition. The expiry condition becomes the warranty's term.

Retained content: the five parts; the four boundaries; acceptability as owned; evidence inheriting its error structure; the three assumption kinds; the three defeaters; and the expiry naming an event. Seven items, which is at the upper end of what the module retains from a single day and is accepted because six of the seven are one-word retrieval cues for material the students already hold.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 1, week 3.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md).

One specification item in SG390 with ME390. Per rule 1, the checked elements are the four-bounded claim with an event-and-date time boundary selected against the [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) record-gap list, the evidence list with error direction and magnitude or explicit *uncharacterised* **and the producing command or explicit *not reproducible***, both in the same field, **the merged evidence bundle and its run output**, the argument in inference steps in the receiver's language, three assumption sets each with a responsible party or explicit none, the detection commitments in four-part form with the signal-failure case and any [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) mute condition stated as a limit, three defeaters of the three named kinds with a would-notice statement, the [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) overruled claim with its record and the overruling grounds, the step 8 reduction sentence, the phase 7 partner answers with adjudication and revision, **and the prompt record, intervention record and frontier statement**. **Nothing else is assessed** — not length, not whether the case was accepted, **and not how many evidence items turned out to be reproducible.**

Five hard requirements: **an unbounded axis fails**; **an evidence magnitude not derivable from the substrate's records fails**, which is the counterpart to the *uncharacterised* permission and exists so that specification rule 2 cannot be satisfied by invention; **a claimed command that the bundle does not run fails**, which is the counterpart to the *not reproducible* permission and exists for the same reason; **an unstated detection commitment fails**; and **a detection commitment resting on an alert whose [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) recipient named a mute condition, where that condition does not appear in the case, fails.** An intervention record with no entries fails, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a bundle that asserts its expected row count and fails when it does not get it is the accepted example. A declined case is a pass; a case recorded as accepted when it was not is a specification failure; **a low reproducibility count is a pass**, because it is a finding about the substrate's records rather than about the student.

**Rule-6 discharge.** Three obligations land on this day and **all three elements exist.**

- **[S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md)** named this day as the response occasion for its step 7 mute answers, specifying that the safety case must not rest on a detection claim whose named recipient stated a condition under which they would mute it, unless that condition appears in the case, and recording the commitment as falling on a day outside its own module. **The element exists at phase 5 step 6's final clause and it is one of the day's five hard requirements.** It is a fail condition rather than a caution because the failure it prevents is the sharpest available instance of point 4: a promise to notice, resting on a signal whose recipient has already stated in writing that they would silence it, is a claim about another person's behaviour that the person has contradicted. **The element is contingent under instrumentation rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8**, on the terms [WP-079](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-079.md) §9 prediction 4 set: if mute conditions turn out to be unrelated to the students' own designs, the mute question was measuring the rota's disposition rather than the artefact, the originating remedy is retracted, and this day's requirement is withdrawn with it. The obligation is discharged and the contingency is recorded.
- **[S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md)** named this day as the response occasion for its pooled phase 1 item 3 list — what the cohort wanted to know and the substrate does not record — specifying that the safety-case claims be selected against it "so that the case is built on quantities the substrate can actually evidence and its gaps are the cohort's own findings." **The element exists at phase 5 step 1's selection clause and it is a checked element.** A claim that survives selection while requiring a listed quantity is admissible only with the gap named, which is the form that keeps the constraint from operating as a silent ban. **Step 3's bundle strengthens the discharge beyond what [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) asked for**: the pooled list is a record of gaps a previous cohort found, and the bundle is the instrument by which this cohort finds its own — an item marked *not reproducible* is a record gap discovered rather than inherited. The obligation is discharged.
- **[S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md)** named this day as the response occasion for its step 6 outcomes, specifying that the safety case include one claim the student raised and was overruled on, with the record of the raising and the overruling party's grounds, and requiring this paper to report whether the element exists. **It exists at phase 5 step 8's first half and it is a checked element.** It is carried as a claim the case does not make, which is the only honest placement: the overruling stands, and what a receiving reader needs is the record that the boundary was contested and by whom. The obligation is discharged.

**Discharge of three inbound retention obligations.** Three prior whitepapers named this day in their §6 as the occasion at which their material becomes a different object, and **all three elements exist.**

- **[WP-066](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-066.md) §6** stated that "a safety case that cites a release gate as evidence has cited an evaluation artefact whose error structure was never characterised, and this day is where that becomes a defect the student can name." **The element exists in four places and two of them are fail conditions:** phase 3's case C2 is exactly that defect presented as an otherwise excellent case; **phase 3's evidence-production leg shows the gate record reproducing perfectly and saying nothing about false passes, which is the defect as an observation rather than as a lesson**; phase 4 point 3 names it and generalises it to every evidence item; and phase 5 step 2 requires the error direction and magnitude in the same field. The obligation is discharged.
- **[WP-068](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-068.md) §6** named this day as "the safety case, where a detection commitment is a claim the case rests on." **The element exists and is assessed:** phase 4 point 4 states it, phase 5 step 6 requires the commitment in S068's four-part form including its own failure case, and an unstated commitment — any unwritten *we would notice* — is a fail condition. The obligation is discharged.
- **[WP-069](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §6** named this day as "the safety case, whose claims require owners." **The element exists in three places:** phase 4 point 2 assigns the acceptance decision to the cost-bearer per S069 point 2, phase 5 step 5 requires a responsible party or an explicit none for every assumption in all three sets, and the accountable staff member is present at step 8 and may decline. The obligation is discharged.

None of the three is a rule-6 obligation in the strict sense — all three were recorded in retention architecture rather than as instruments with named response occasions — and this paper reports them in the same form because the substantive commitment is identical and because a retention claim that no later day honours is a deferral with no instrument, which is exactly what the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s deferral rule exists to prevent.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions. The **unowned-assumption total** responds at [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) phase 5, where every unowned assumption must be assigned an owner or entered in the warranty's exclusion set and one in neither fails, with the cohort total reported at S089 phase 4. The **phase 7 first-answer hit rate** — how often a peer reading the case alone finds a use the author did not intend to cover — responds at [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) phase 3, where the receiving party is asked the same question about the same case, and the two rates are compared and reported at S090 phase 8. **[WP-089](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-089.md) §7 and [WP-090](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the four-case corpus is manufactured, and the day's central reversal rests on it.**

At its strongest: the day's whole argument turns on C4 being the only case still true. That fact is supplied by a corpus the design constructed — four cases written as an exercise, by authors who knew they were being compared, about a change staff selected because its outcome was informative. **Every degree of freedom in that construction points the same way.** A designer who wanted the narrow case to win could produce this corpus without noticing they were doing it: choose a change whose failure modes were breadth-related, ask four authors to write in four styles, and the result is guaranteed. The room commits before seeing the outcome, which makes the reversal feel adjudicated, and what it is adjudicated against is a case set built by the person who holds the conclusion.

This is conceded and the disclosure at phase 3 is not a repair. **Telling students that the corpus was constructed does not make the inference from it valid**; it makes the invalidity visible, which satisfies instrumentation rule 3 and satisfies nothing else. The design is running, on its own materials, the selection effect the same rule exists to catch, and it is doing so on the day whose subject is what evidence licenses.

The available fix is real and is declined for a reason that should be stated plainly. **A genuine corpus would be four independently written cases for one change, found rather than commissioned, with outcomes.** That does not exist on this substrate and the design has no route to obtaining it: safety cases are rare, one system produces one case per change, and four independent cases for one change is not a thing organisations do. The alternative fix — running phase 3 on four cases for four *different* changes — is available and is worse, because the cases would then differ on the change as well as on the argument and the adjudication would be uninterpretable.

So the design keeps the manufactured corpus, discloses it, and **weakens what it claims from it.** Phase 4 point 1 is not presented as an empirical finding about safety cases in general; it is presented as the form this design requires, with its scope statement, and the outcome record is used to make the form's rationale legible rather than to establish it. That is a smaller claim than the phase 3 staging implies, and the gap between the two is this objection's residue.

**Objection 2: the day's expiry claim is asserted, not demonstrated.**

At its strongest: point 8 states that a case with no expiry is a claim about all future states of a system you are about to stop watching, and requires the time boundary to name an event as well as a date. It is a good rule. **Nothing on the day shows it.** C4 carries an expiry clause and C1, C2 and C3 do not, but none of the four failed *because* of an expiry: C1 failed on an unexcluded use, C2 on undermined evidence, C3 on an unmodelled operator. The one boundary axis the day requires and does not adjudicate is the one the room has least reason to believe in, because a date in a document is the most obviously ignorable thing an engineer produces.

This is conceded, and the fix is nearly free. **A fifth case — or a second outcome window on C4 — would do it: C4's own claim expires at the next authentication change, and the substrate has had authentication changes.** Showing what happened to C4's case at the moment its stated event occurred, and what the receiving reader did about it, would convert point 8 from a rule into a demonstration at the cost of one paragraph in an existing case. It is not in the design. It is not declined for cost; it was not seen, and it is recorded here rather than quietly inserted so that the omission is legible.

**This is a further instance of the standing pattern-level observation that this design stages what it expects students to resist and asserts what it finds obvious once stated.** The observation reached the programme's elevation threshold of three instances and **its elevation to a numbered instrumentation rule is pending and not in force**; it is cited here as pending, and this instance is recorded as evidence for it rather than as a new finding. The mechanism is visible in this day with unusual clarity: the design spent forty-five minutes staging a claim it expected the room to fight — narrow beats broad — and gave one sentence to a claim it finds obvious, which the room has every reason to disbelieve. **Obviousness to the designer is not evidence about the room, and this design keeps using it as though it were.**

**Objection 3: the reproducibility requirement narrows what a case may rest on, in a direction the design has not measured and cannot see.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn.

At its strongest: the day adds a column asking what command produces each evidence item, and a bundle that runs them. The stated intent is to make a case checkable by an absent reader. **The effect is to put a cost on every evidence item that is hard to produce, and the items that are hard to produce are not a random sample.** A test result reproduces in seconds. A trace query reproduces if it is inside ninety days and the reader holds Log Analytics Reader. **An operator's account of what actually happened, a design review's reasoning, a prior incident's post-mortem — the evidence that speaks to the operator and organisational assumptions [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) point 5 says are always missing — reproduces never.** The day has therefore added an incentive that runs precisely against the assumption class it spends point 5 arguing for, and it has done so on the day whose subject is what evidence licenses. The design's answer, that *not reproducible* is admissible and the count is not assessed, is the same answer §8 objection 3 of [WP-086](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-086.md) already conceded is insufficient against student behaviour.

The rebuttal is thin and is stated as thin. Phase 3 stages the counter-case: **C3's environment assumptions cannot be produced at all, and C3 is the case whose author did the most careful work.** The room sees that before phase 4. And the specification's fail condition is asymmetric on purpose — it fails a *claimed* command that does not run, and never fails an absent one — so the cheapest safe move for a student is to mark an item *not reproducible*, not to drop it.

**The concession is that the design cannot see whether the incentive bit.** An evidence item that a student never wrote down leaves no trace in the artefact, and the reproducibility rate the day collects is computed over the items that survived selection. **The cheap fix is one instruction and it is made: step 2 requires the enumeration before the columns, so the items that leave the case leave a record of having been considered** — which is the same construction [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) step 5 uses on omissions one week later, imported here rather than invented. What the fix does not do is tell the design whether a student silently declined to think of an operator account at all, and no artefact can. **That residue is real, it is the practical turn's characteristic cost on this day, and §9 prediction 5 is written so that the composition of the surviving evidence is reported rather than the count.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At phase 1, the ratio between the number of evidence items students list at item 2 and the number they can give an error direction for at item 3 will be large, and the items that survive will be disproportionately tests rather than operational records. *Instrument:* items 2 and 3 paired per student, with surviving items coded by evidence kind. **The composition is the claim**: if operational records survive as often as tests, the cohort holds a better model of production evidence than the day assumes and phase 4 point 3 is over-taught.

**Prediction 2.** A majority of groups will rank C1 or C2 above C4 before the outcome is supplied, and the justifications will appeal to completeness or traceability rather than to any of the four boundaries. *Instrument:* phase 3 rankings with justifications coded for the criterion appealed to. **If the justifications appeal to boundaries and the ranking still favours breadth, the day's problem is not the belief it thinks it is**, and point 1 needs rewriting as an argument about evidence supply rather than about claim strength.

**Prediction 3.** At step 5, adversary assumptions will be the least populated of the three sets and operator assumptions the least *owned* — that is, students will write operator assumptions and leave them with no responsible party more often than any other kind. *Instrument:* step 5 assumption sets coded by kind and by ownership, reported as two separate distributions. **The two failures are different and the design predicts different ones for different sets**, which is what makes this falsifiable rather than a general prediction that assumptions are hard.

**Prediction 4.** At phase 7, partners will more often identify a use the claim accidentally covers than an assumption likely to fail first, and the gap will be large. *Instrument:* phase 7's three answers coded for correctness against the author's adjudication. **If assumptions are identified more readily, the boundary is not the hard part for this cohort** and the day's emphasis is misplaced; the design would then move phase 5's unsupported step from 1 to 5.

**Prediction 5 — the practical turn's own instrument, per §8 objection 3.** The evidence surviving into step 2 will be **more heavily weighted toward test and gate artefacts, and less toward operator and organisational accounts, than the same students' phase 1 item 2 lists were** — that is, the composition will shift between the pretest and the artefact, in the direction the reproducibility column rewards. *Instrument:* item 2 lists and step 2 lists coded by evidence kind, paired per student, reported as a composition shift rather than as a count. **If there is no shift, objection 3 is wrong and the admissibility of *not reproducible* was sufficient.** If the shift is large, the day has bought checkability at the price of the assumption class point 5 exists to install, and the response is to move the bundle out of the assessed artefact and into phase 3 only.

**Prediction 6 — the frontier item.** A large majority of item 8 sealed predictions will state that the agent's query will run and mislead, and a small minority will name the mechanism — a table that is being replaced, a filter that silently excludes, a window shorter than the claim. *Instrument:* item 8 responses coded for generic-versus-specific identification against what each student's agent produced at step 3. **The gap between the two rates is the day's frontier-calibration measure**, its reader is next year's cohort, and a high specific rate would mean phase 6's agent case is redundant for this cohort.
