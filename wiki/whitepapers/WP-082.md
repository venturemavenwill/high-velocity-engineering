# WP-082 — Adoption You Cannot Mandate

**Seminar day:** [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) · **Module:** [M17](/wiki/modules/M17.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 6
**Platform anchor:** Copilot Studio at its published department-level scale, with gateway telemetry as the record of whether anyone actually used it

> ## Release rule
>
> **§§4, 5 and 9 are post-session reading.** §4 explains why the supplied situation contains a reverted site, §5 gives countermeasures for the failures the session is designed to produce, and §9 states the predicted cohort behaviour on the opening items. §§1, 2, 3, 6, 7 and 8 spoil nothing.

> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Readiness sequence** | The distinction between being aware of a change, being willing, knowing how, being able, and continuing — from a named practitioner model with a commercial warrant |
> | **Blocking element** | The first element of that sequence that is weak for a given person or group |
> | **Adoption position** | A claim about a group's use of a system, its supporting observation, and the evidence that would overturn it |
> | **Receiving group** | The unit an adoption position is written about — a site, a team, a role — chosen so that its members share an alternative |
> | **Dimension** | A label attached to an emitted metric, without which consumption cannot be attributed to a group |
> | **Deployment envelope** | The scale a vendor publishes as a product's intended operating range |
> | **Self-efficacy** | A person's belief that they can execute a behaviour, distinct from their belief that the behaviour works |
> | **Class 3** | Design reasoning with no external warrant — see [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) |

## In plain terms

A system was delivered and the usage report says it is used. It counts accounts and it counts drafts produced. Both numbers are healthy and neither is about use.

At one site the reviewers stopped weeks ago and went back to doing the work by hand. Their accounts are still provisioned, so the report still counts them. **Nothing in the instrument can tell the difference between a person using a tool and a person having been given one**, and the party that built the instrument is the party that wants the answer to be yes.

The durable claim underneath the session is short. **Adoption cannot be mandated, because use is a choice made repeatedly by people who have an alternative that already works.** A mandate raises the cost of the alternative. It does not remove it, and it does not survive the first week in which the mandated path is slower.

The session's move is to require an observed act with a date, and to require the groups you cannot see to be listed as groups you cannot see. Then it asks for a diagnosis: is this group unaware, unwilling, untaught, unable, or unsupported? Those five states have five different remedies and they are collapsed into one remedy — training — with remarkable consistency, by people who are not stupid.

**The platform is where the argument stops being an argument.** A gateway in front of the model endpoint can attach a dimension to every emitted metric identifying the receiving group, and a metric without that dimension cannot distinguish a site that stopped from a site that never started. **Telemetry is the only honest record available, its absence for a group is itself a finding, and the traffic that arrives unattributed is a measured hole in your map of the estate.** None of that is arguable. It is either configured or it is not.

**And one question underneath all of it, which most learners find genuinely uncomfortable: whose job got worse because this exists?** A system that automates part of somebody's work and made nobody's work worse has either been badly examined or was not worth building. Resistance is frequently a correct reading of an interest, and a plan that treats it as a communication failure is a plan that will not work and will not be able to say why.

## Abstract

This paper defends assessing adoption from observed acts rather than from provisioned capability, defends requiring a diagnosis before an intervention, and defends instrumenting the claim at the gateway rather than arguing it in prose.

The argument runs in four parts. **First**, the instruments organisations use to measure adoption measure availability, because availability is what a platform can see from where it sits — a provisioned account is a fact about an administrative action and a generated output is a fact about a system, and neither is a fact about a person. **Second**, adoption cannot be mandated, because use is a decision remade daily against an alternative that already works; reversion is therefore more informative than absence, and the reflex remedy of training is aimed at one of five distinguishable states and applied to all five. **Third**, a token-metric dimension identifying the receiving group converts an unarguable claim into a checkable one, and the same counter key that makes contention fair makes consumption attributable — so the fairness mechanism and the adoption instrument are the same mechanism. **Fourth**, a tool operated outside its published deployment envelope fails as governance rather than as function, which is why a healthy usage report can sit on top of a system nobody is maintaining.

The session produces an adoption position per receiving group, with its supporting observation, its date and the evidence that would overturn it, plus an instrumentation record, a scale position, and a diagnosis for one group that has stopped.

The paper concedes that the readiness distinctions it borrows come from a commercially interested practitioner model with no independent evaluation cited anywhere; that a claim this repository previously inherited from that model has been withdrawn as unsupported by the book; that gateway telemetry establishes traffic and not use, so the better instrument is still not a good one; and that the most useful conclusion in the session is the one a delivery team is structurally least able to reach about its own work.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Delivery organisations measure adoption with the instruments they have, and the instruments they have measure the things a platform emits.

A provisioned account is a fact about an administrative action. A generated output is a fact about a system's behaviour. Neither is a fact about a person. **The gap matters most in exactly the case where it is hardest to see**: a group that adopted the system and then reverted continues to generate telemetry indistinguishable from a group that is using it, for as long as nobody removes their access.

The second problem is diagnostic. The states behind non-use are distinguishable — the group does not know the system exists or why; the group knows and does not want it; the group wants it and has not been taught; the group has been taught and cannot yet do it under real workload; the group can do it and nothing sustains it — and the interventions differ completely. **The default reflex is to train, and training aimed past a willingness problem does not work.**

The third problem is attributional and is the one the platform addresses directly. Where consumption is emitted without a dimension identifying the receiving group, **no amount of analysis recovers the per-group picture**, and the aggregate is compatible with every story anybody wants to tell. Worse, traffic reaching the model by a path that bypasses the gateway is invisible rather than wrong, so the analysis is quietly incomplete in a direction nobody can estimate. **A metric with no group dimension is not a neutral instrument; it actively conceals the finding.**

The fourth problem is political and is the one the session is least comfortable about. A system that removes part of somebody's work is rationally unwelcome to them. No amount of communication resolves a genuine conflict of interest, and the practice corpus behind this programme is unusually clear that resistance originates in ownership of process rather than in technical incapacity. **A delivery team is structurally unlikely to reach that conclusion about its own system**, because reaching it costs the account of the engagement.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why the evidence must be an act with a date.** A capability is a state of the world that persists without anybody doing anything. An act is a state of the world that requires somebody to have done something. Adoption claims are claims about the second and are routinely evidenced with the first. **The date is not decoration**: adoption is a claim about a period, and an undated observation cannot distinguish current use from historical use.

**3.2 Why reversion is worth more than absence.** A group that never started may have been unaware, unauthorised, unequipped or uninterested, and the evidence is compatible with all of them. A group that used the system and stopped has performed an experiment: they know what it does, and they concluded against it. **The information is in the stopping**, and organisations discard it because it arrives as bad news.

**3.3 Why the diagnosis must precede the intervention.** The readiness model's contribution is that its elements are separable and are usually conflated. The direction of its argument — that instruction aimed past a willingness problem disappoints — is defensible as mechanism. **Its ordering claim is argued from the meanings of its own terms and rests on nothing external**, and this paper asserts no strength for it.

**3.4 What may not be inherited from that model, stated because it is widely repeated.** A common summary holds that effort spent past the blocking element is wasted. **The book does not say that, and this repository withdrew the claim on reading it.** Its own formulations are conditional — that a given effort will not by itself produce the change, or will not be productive until the blocking element moves — with a single absolute restricted to knowledge in the absence of willingness. The conditional form is the only form used here.

**3.5 Why the closed-channel observation is load-bearing.** If a group has no route to report that the system is at fault, a system's faults present as the group's failure. The source states it directly: **"If the feedback channel is not open, then the employee will appear to be failing when the system is actually at fault."** That mechanism is directly reusable, it is cheap to check, and it reverses the direction of most adoption diagnoses when it applies.

**3.6 Why adoption is an efficacy question before it is a preference question.**

There is a state the readiness sequence blurs, and it explains the group that agrees with everything and does nothing. A person's judgement that they can successfully execute a behaviour is distinct from their judgement that the behaviour produces a good outcome ([research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md](/research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md)).

The two come apart routinely in adoption. A reviewer can believe the assistant produces good drafts *and* believe that they personally cannot get a good draft out of it, and **the second belief governs whether they open it.** The design consequence is that *they do not see the value* is the easiest diagnosis to reach and usually the wrong one, because it is the answer people offer when asked — the efficacy answer being the embarrassing one.

**The limits are severe.** The paper that introduced the construct is **paywalled and unread by this repository**; the account rests on the official abstract and on later authors' characterisations. The volume read in full is an edited conference collection in which the originator wrote one chapter, and a claim from a contributed chapter is that contributor's rather than his. **Direction and mechanism only.**

**3.7 Why the instrument is a dimension rather than a survey.**

The gateway material argues that the reverse proxy in front of model endpoints is where cost, quota, authentication and observability belong, and is explicit that this is "not a separate offering" — the AI gateway "extends API Management's existing API gateway" ([research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md)). The token-metric policy attaches custom dimensions to emitted metrics, and the source's own summary of why is exactly the design argument this session needs: **dimensions are what make a metric answerable** — total consumption tells you the bill; consumption per API per user tells you who to talk to.

Two structural consequences follow and neither is obvious.

**The fairness mechanism and the adoption instrument are the same mechanism.** The token limit policy keys on "any counter key, such as subscription key, originating IP address, or an arbitrary key defined through a policy expression". A team that configured per-group fairness has already built per-group attribution and does not know it, which makes the instrument nearly free where contention was ever a problem.

**The absent measurement is a positive finding rather than a gap.** A group with provisioned accounts and no dimensioned traffic in the window is a group that is not using the system, and that statement rests on an observation. It is also falsifiable in a specific way — they may be reaching the model on a path that bypasses the gateway — which is why the workflow requires the unattributed share to be reported as a headline rather than excluded silently.

**The documentation's limits.** Policy names, tier availability and preview status change frequently; the individual policy reference pages were not fetched; the Foundry integration was in preview at the reading. And the page **does not discuss the privacy implications of logging prompts and completions**, in a section that recommends logging them for "billing and auditing". **A learner instrumenting adoption is instrumenting people, and the documentation will not raise that with them.**

**3.8 Why the deployment envelope belongs in an adoption position.**

Vendor comparison guidance positions **Copilot Studio as "designed for small-scale deployments, such as department-level solutions"** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). That is a scale claim published by a vendor about its own product, and its consequence for adoption is indirect and strong.

**A tool used outside its stated envelope does not stop working — it stops being governable.** The symptoms present as ownership and lifecycle questions: who owns this agent, who reviews its instructions when the model behind it changes, who notices when the department that built it reorganises. Those are adoption questions wearing governance clothes, and they predict *which* group stops: the one furthest from whoever built it, because that group has no route to the person who could fix their problem. **The envelope therefore belongs beside the adoption claim rather than in a risk register**, and stating it costs one sentence.

**Its limits:** read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches; product names actively in flux; **no adoption, cost, failure-rate or outcome data supports any recommendation in it**; and a capability boundary recorded today may close tomorrow.

**3.9 Why a visible win is defined operationally rather than motivationally.**

The other change literature this programme holds defines a good short-term win by three properties: **visible**, so that "large numbers of people can see for themselves whether the result is real or just hype"; **unambiguous**, with "little argument over the call"; and **clearly related to the change effort** ([research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md](/research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md)). What does not qualify is enumerated — a good meeting does not, nor a design the engineering manager thinks is terrific, nor a circulated vision statement.

The distinction the session inherits is the next sentence: **"Creating short-term wins is different from hoping for short-term wins. The latter is passive, the former active."** An adoption position built on hoped-for wins is a forecast; one built on visible attributable acts is a record. The same source supplies the mechanism by which a stated commitment is undone behaviourally rather than communicatively — **"Nothing undermines the communication of a change vision more than behavior on the part of key players that seems inconsistent with the vision"** — which is the correct reading of a group told the assistant is now the way of working, whose own manager still asks for the hand-written version.

**Its limits are stated by the author.** The book contains no footnotes, cites no published source except his own writing, and every extended case is pseudonymous or unnamed, so **no case can be traced or checked by a reader.** Direction and mechanism only, and no figure from it appears anywhere in this session.

**3.10 Why the opening items are unscaffolded, and why exercise 3 exists.**

The opening items are a Productive Failure generation phase: learners generate answers to a problem they have not been taught, fail, and are then taught ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Two of the design's stated conditions bind here. **The generation phase must not be scaffolded**, which is why the release rule gates §4 and specifically why the two exits from item 5 are post-session reading. **The consolidation phase must explicitly prompt comparison** — presenting a correct answer beside a learner's wrong one achieves nothing on its own, and only the condition instructed to compare them learned more — which is why exercise 3 requires the learner to return to their item-3 answer and name the difference before rewriting it.

**Its limits are conceded in §8 of [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md) and hold here unchanged:** every study behind the design is a school, university or laboratory study, the source is a trade book, and no adult-professional study appears in its reference list.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**The reverted site is the session's instrument.** It exists so that the usage report and the truth disagree in a way the learner can find, and so that the finding is bad news for the engagement. A situation in which adoption is simply low would teach the same technique and none of the discomfort.

**The learner may not ask the delivery team what happened.** This is the session's only hard constraint, and it forces the observation design rather than permitting a shortcut through the people with the strongest interest in the answer.

**Item 5 of the opening — if nobody's job got worse, what were you delivering — is a trap with two exits and both are findings.** Either the learner has not looked, or the system displaced nothing. The session accepts both answers and requires the learner to say which they are giving.

**What the platform workflow is for.** It is not a gateway tutorial. It exists to make two things physically observable that no amount of prose achieves, and both are properties of the measurement rather than of the estate.

The first is the **unattributed bucket**. Requests arrive without the group header from paths the learner did not know existed, and the size of that bucket is a measured statement about the learner's own map. It appears within minutes and it cannot be argued away.

The second is the **high-then-zero shape**. A group that decided repeatedly and then decided differently is invisible in every total, in every dashboard, and in the usage report that opened the session — and it is unmistakable in a per-group series. **A learner who has once watched a group's line fall to zero while its provisioned-account count stayed flat does not afterwards accept a provisioning number as evidence of anything**, and that single observation is worth more than the argument in §1.

**The three shapes are named in advance deliberately.** Flat-and-low, high-then-zero, one-identity. Naming them before the learner queries is scaffolding of the *interpretation* rather than of the generation phase, and it is permitted for the reason §3.10 gives: the generation phase is the opening items, which are already closed by the time the workflow runs.

**The closing question is fixed:** what would this group say about your diagnosis if they were here? A diagnosis that cannot be said aloud to its subject is a diagnosis of their deficiency, and it will be wrong for that reason before it is wrong for any other.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Provisioning read as adoption.** *Countermeasure:* the artefact's required fields, which have no place to record a capability.

**The undimensioned metric.** The learner instruments consumption and cannot attribute it. *Countermeasure:* the workflow's step 4 supplies the dimension explicitly, and step 5 makes the unattributed share a required headline.

**The unattributed bucket quietly dropped.** Per-group figures are reported and do not sum to the total. *Countermeasure:* partial. The standard names it and the failure-modes list names it, and **neither prevents a learner from reporting the tidy subset**, which is what §9's fifth prediction expects.

**The enthusiast as the population.** One capable, interested person is doing well and the group is recorded as adopting. *Countermeasure:* positions are per group, and a position whose evidence is a single named individual is read as a position about that individual — the same countermeasure used for capability at [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md).

**Training as the universal remedy.** *Countermeasure:* the diagnosis precedes the intervention and the two are assessed together. Identical interventions for differently diagnosed groups fail on their face.

**Value substituted for efficacy.** The group is recorded as unconvinced when in fact they cannot get a usable result and will not say so. *Countermeasure:* §3.6 names the distinction, and the workflow's step 7 asks about the last attempt rather than about the general case — which is the only question shape that reaches an efficacy answer.

**Rational objection recorded as resistance.** *Countermeasure:* the item-4 requirement to name a role whose work got worse, and the closing question.

**Observation designed as a survey.** The learner reaches for a questionnaire, which returns what people say about their own tool use. *Countermeasure:* partial. The session names the failure and relies on the discipline from [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) and [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md), which has by this point been retrieved several times and may have decayed anyway.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md), which asks whether the position will still hold later and what it costs to make it so, and by [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md), where the adoption position becomes evidence for or against a maturity claim.

**The knowledge retrieved is not the model.** It is the requirement that a claim about people rest on something somebody did, which is the same requirement applied to capability at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) and to closure at [S062](/wiki/seminars/S062.md).

**The instrumentation habit retrieves more widely than the module.** A dimension identifying the population a metric is about is a property of every measurement the learner will ever build, and the session installs it against a gateway because that is where the mechanism is cheap and visible rather than because gateways are the point.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No specification item and no entrustment level. This session generates observation for the entrustment decision at [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md).

**What assessors record**, as narrative: whether every position rested on a dated act; whether unobservable groups were listed rather than omitted; whether the unattributed share was reported or quietly excluded; whether the diagnosis and the intervention matched; whether the learner named a role whose work got worse, or said honestly that none had; and whether the diagnosis was one they were willing to say in front of its subject.

Recording this as reasoned narrative rather than as a mark follows the assessment literature this programme's architecture rests on: the useful information is *why* an assessor judged as they did, and assessors reaching the same outcome frequently reach it by different reasoning that a score conceals ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)).

**Nothing else is assessed.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the readiness sequence is a commercial product being taught as though it were knowledge.**

At its strongest: the model is a registered trademark of a consultancy that sells training and certification in it. Its supporting evidence is that firm's own practitioner surveys, published by the firm, with no methodology, no sampling frame and no independent replication. Its ordering and completeness claims are argued from the meanings of the words and are offered without hedge and without evidence. Its own operational instrument produces a blocking element from an anonymous self-rating with a cut-point. **Teaching it inside a programme whose whole subject is the difference between a claim and its warrant is either hypocrisy or a very fine distinction.**

**This is conceded, and the distinction the design relies on is stated rather than hidden.** What is taught is three separations — told from aware, aware from willing, knowing from able — because they are real, are routinely collapsed, and change what a team does when separated. **What is not taught is the model**: no ordering strength, no completeness claim, no rate, no proportion, no instrument, no cut-point, and no suggestion that using it improves anything. **The fix that was considered and declined** was to teach the distinctions with no attribution, sourced to general practice. That was rejected because an unattributed distinction cannot be checked by a learner, and because this programme's rule is that the warrant travels with the claim even when the warrant is embarrassing. The objection is entitled to say that citing a source in order to discount it is still citing it.

**Objection 2: requiring an observed act makes adoption unmeasurable at any scale.**

At its strongest: an engagement may serve dozens of sites and hundreds of users. Requiring a dated observation of an act, per group, is affordable for the site down the road and impossible for the estate. Meanwhile the telemetry that this session dismisses is the only instrument that reaches everywhere, and refusing to use it leaves the learner with a rigorous claim about two groups and nothing at all about the rest.

The rebuttal is narrow and concedes the frame. The session does not forbid telemetry; it forbids reading availability as use, and requires the unobserved population to be named as unobserved. **A position that says two groups are using this, on evidence, and that nothing is known about the other eleven is more useful than one that says all thirteen are users** — because the first supports a decision about where to look next and the second supports a decision about nothing. The platform workflow narrows the objection further than the first draft of this paper could: a dimensioned metric is an observed act at estate scale, cheaply, for every group at once. **Whether an organisation would accept such a position is untested, and no source behind this programme reports one being offered.**

**Objection 3: instrumenting adoption is surveillance, and the session teaches it without saying so.**

At its strongest: the workflow attaches a user identifier to every emitted metric and recommends a group identifier alongside it. That is a record of who used which tool, when, at what volume — built by an external delivery team, deployed into somebody else's organisation, and justified by an engineering argument about measurement. **The vendor documentation that recommends logging prompts and completions for billing and auditing does not analyse the privacy consequences at all**, so a learner following it faithfully will build a monitoring capability without ever meeting the question. And the people being measured are precisely the people the session has just argued have a rational interest in not using the system.

**Conceded without qualification, and only partly addressed.** The session names it — the material states plainly that a learner instrumenting adoption is instrumenting people and that the documentation will not raise it — and the perishability table carries the logging options as a dated instance. **That is naming rather than solving.** The fix that was considered and declined was to require a data-protection assessment as part of the artefact. It was declined on load grounds: the session already carries an observation design, a diagnosis and a conflict-of-interest question, and adding a compliance instrument would displace the diagnosis, which is the part with no substitute elsewhere in the programme. **The cost is that a learner may leave this session able to build a monitoring capability and unpractised at arguing about whether they should**, and this paper records that as an accepted cost rather than a solved problem. The group-level dimension is the mitigation actually available: **a position about a group needs no per-person identifier, and a learner who drops the user dimension loses nothing this session assesses.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will produce at least one position whose supporting evidence is a capability rather than an act, on the first attempt. *Instrument:* the positions, coded per row for evidence type by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Answers to opening item 4 will more often be *nobody* than any named role, and learners answering *nobody* will more often also have produced a capability-evidenced position. *Instrument:* the item-4 answers coded as *named role* / *nobody* / *absent*, cross-tabulated against prediction 1's coding.

**Prediction 3.** Learners will more often name a role whose work got worse when their diagnosis concerned the reverted site than when it concerned a group that never started. *Instrument:* the item-4 answers cross-tabulated against the group diagnosed.

**Prediction 4.** Where the diagnosis is a willingness problem, the proposed intervention will more often be instructional than where the diagnosis is a knowledge problem. *Instrument:* diagnosis and intervention coded independently and paired. **If this does not hold, the training reflex this session is built on is not present in this population** and §1 should be rewritten as a caution rather than as a premise.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of learners will configure the group dimension correctly and then report per-group figures without stating the unattributed share. *Instrument:* the submitted instrumentation records, checked for whether an unattributed figure appears at all and whether it appears before the per-group table. **This predicts the failure of the session's own principal countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption. If it holds, the unattributed share should become a required field of the artefact rather than a step of the workflow.

**Prediction 6.** Learners who list unobservable groups explicitly will more often name the managed-service supplier than those who do not list any. *Instrument:* the unobserved list, checked for the supplier.

**Prediction 7.** Learners instructed to compare their item-3 answer against the criterion (exercise 3) will more often produce a diagnosis naming a discriminating observation than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* a within-cohort split on whether exercise 3 was completed, against the diagnoses coded for whether a discriminating observation is named. **This is the session's only direct test of the mechanism §3.10 rests on**, and a null result would mean the comparison prompt is ceremony.

**None of these has been measured.** No cohort has run this session, and this repository's prediction register is machinery rather than a result — which is [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s finding applied to this page.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a provisioned account is a capability and not a use; that adoption cannot be mandated because use is a decision remade daily against a working alternative; that a group which stopped has told you more than one which never started; that five distinguishable states are routinely treated as one; and that a metric without a group dimension conceals the finding rather than merely omitting it.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about how often adoption fails, where it fails, or whether any intervention works. The readiness distinctions are direction and mechanism from a commercially interested source. The gateway capabilities are verified from first-party documentation that publishes no adoption or outcome data of any kind. Everything about the artefact is this design's argument.

**The instrument's remaining limit, stated so it is not forgotten.** Gateway telemetry establishes that a request was made under an identity. It does not establish that a person read the output, kept it, or was helped by it. **The gateway is a better instrument than the usage report and it is not a good instrument**, and a position treating a token count as evidence of value has repeated the opening error one layer down.

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper.** That is a rule of this repository rather than an accident of what was read.

**The concession worth remembering.** The most useful thing in this session — that resistance is often a correct reading of an interest — is the thing a delivery team is structurally least able to conclude about its own work, and no instrument in this session fixes that.

**What the documentation does not say, recorded because it is the more important half.** **No vendor documentation known to this programme defines adoption as sustained use, publishes a definition of abandonment or reversion, or distinguishes a provisioned identity from an active one in any adoption or usage surface it ships.** What such surfaces publish is availability and volume — identities assigned, requests served, outputs produced — because those are what a platform can see from where it sits, and no product is in a position to observe somebody quietly returning to doing the work by hand. **The instrument's limit is structural rather than an omission**, which is precisely why it must be stated: a reader who mistakes it for a measurement of adoption is not misreading the documentation, they are reading it exactly as it is written.

Three further silences bear on this session. The gateway page **does not discuss the privacy implications of logging prompts and completions**, in the same section that recommends logging them for billing and auditing. The comparison guidance publishes a scale envelope as design intent and **publishes no adoption, cost, failure-rate or outcome data** to support it, so a deployment inside the envelope is not thereby a deployment that will be used. And **no accuracy, detection or effectiveness figure appears anywhere in either source**, which is consistent across every platform note this programme holds: each states a latency or a bound and none states an accuracy. **None is asserted here.** If any vendor publishes a sustained-use or reversion definition, this session's opening is due for revision and this paper should record its version and what it still omits.
