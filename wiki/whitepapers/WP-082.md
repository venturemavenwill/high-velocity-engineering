# WP-082 — Stakeholders, Power and the Person Not in the Room

**Seminar day:** [S082](wiki/seminars/S082.md) · **Module:** [M17](wiki/modules/M17.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](wiki/quarters/Q9.md)
**Strands:** PE · SG · all · **Complexity class:** 6
**Platform anchor:** Azure AI Search document-level access control over the substrate's corpus, and Purview's data risk assessment as an instrument with a published sampling frame · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends a day whose central claim is that absence is produced rather than accidental, and whose payload is a four-entry stakeholder map that beat a thirty-four-entry one because it asked a power question instead of running a consultation. The target competence is placing parties on two axes — can they refuse, can they be harmed without recourse — stating the sampling frame of one's own analysis, and taking a finding to the person whose decision it contradicts. The mechanism the design leans on hardest is a contrasting-case set in which the most thorough method is systematically blind, adjudicated by a real harm. **A fifth map is added and is the only one the students produce: a party set assembled from the substrate's own tenant in four minutes, whose frame — possession of an identity — is legible in the tool where the human maps' frames are not.** The principal risk is cynicism: a cohort taught to see unstated interests will find them everywhere and stop being falsifiable. **Section 7 reports the discharge of the deferral [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 4 placed here, of the second half of [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5, and of the rule-6 instrument [S078](wiki/seminars/S078.md) placed here — the only inbound obligation that named its material rather than its theme.** Section 8 concedes that six days deferred material to this one, that only two receive artefact elements, that the design has been satisfying the deferral rule from the wrong end, and that the day's new build risks teaching the opposite of the day's claim.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The material is easy to agree with and that is the whole problem. Every student in the room will assent to "some people are affected and not consulted" in under a minute, and the assent is worth nothing, because the belief it produces is that the remedy is a longer list.

**The difficulty is that thoroughness is the failure mode.** A stakeholder analysis conducted properly — identify the parties, contact them, run a comment period, address the objections, record it — has a sampling frame, and the frame is *reachability*. Every party it finds is a party with a channel into the institution. The parties who most need representing are, by construction, the ones without one. This is not a defect that better execution repairs; it is a property of the method, and it is invisible from inside the method because the method's own quality measure is people contacted.

That is the same structure the programme has taught three times already and never at organisational scale: [S028](wiki/seminars/S028.md)'s population question, [S049](wiki/seminars/S049.md)'s dependency structure, and instrumentation rule 3, which was added after this design ran a selection effect it teaches students to detect. **The day is therefore not introducing a difficulty source; it is applying one the cohort holds to an object where they have never applied it, and where the object is people rather than data.**

The second difficulty is different in kind and is the reason the day carries a scope statement that matters. [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 4 gave three escalation conditions and conceded that they fail where the only authority is the person whose decision the finding contradicts. [WP-069](wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §8 argued, correctly, that this is the normal case for a graduate two years into a career rather than an edge case. **The honest answer to it is not a procedure and this day must resist supplying one**, because a procedure for this case would be a lie with the programme's authority behind it. What the day can supply is a map of four moves with their costs, and the statement that the choice is the student's.

The third difficulty is affective and is the module's worst failure mode arriving on its second day. A room that learns to look for unstated interests will find them everywhere, including where there are none, and the resulting position is unfalsifiable and professionally corrosive. **The design's countermeasure is not a warning; it is phase 7**, which requires the partner to build the strongest good-faith account of the interest the student has just hypothesised.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Intrinsic load is low and the day's cost is emotional rather than cognitive.** Two axes, three absence mechanisms, four moves. Nothing interacts arithmetically.

What does interact is steps 1, 2 and 3. The placement at step 1 determines what the sampling frame at step 2 has to explain; the frame determines whether the student can say who it cannot reach; and step 3 asks them to find one such party in a corpus, which is the only step where the abstraction becomes checkable against a system. A student who writes a frame at step 2 that predicts nothing at step 3 has written a paragraph rather than a frame, and the adjacency is what makes that visible to them.

Load reductions:

- **The party set is the cohort's own service and last week's boundary**, so nothing is acquired.
- **The two-axis grid is supplied** at step 1 and the escalation preparation reuses [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 4's three conditions and [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s actionable-language standard.
- **Point 4 restates [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5 in full** rather than assuming a three-quarter-old open question.
- **The four maps are the four that were produced**, not a constructed set, which removes the search for a designed distinction.

Expertise reversal applies to students with organisational experience and applies at points 1 to 3, which such a student holds. Point 7 is where their attention is wanted and is where their experience is least likely to have produced a usable position, because the situation it describes is one most people meet once and resolve by leaving.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *A pretest enumeration that will be produced competently and is the wrong shape,* item 1.
- *A power question about your own list before any apparatus exists,* item 2.
- *The unanswered case taken cold,* item 3, four weeks after the procedure that does not cover it.
- *A payload that is the shortest and least thorough artefact,* M4.
- *A task-after demanding a mechanism rather than a name.*
- *Stating what your own method cannot reach, unsupported,* step 2.
- *Naming a party you believe exists and cannot name,* step 2.
- *Writing an interest hypothesis you would let be repeated to its holder,* step 5.
- *Contradicting, in person, a decision made by someone with the authority to make it,* step 6.
- *Being required to argue the good-faith case for the interest you just hypothesised,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Bad faith.* No party in any case, and nobody in the room, is acting in bad faith. The design has no material on deliberate concealment and would produce a worse professional by inventing some.
- *A quantified power analysis.* Placement with a reason, not a score. Scoring parties would make the artefact comparable and would teach that the axes are measurements.
- *Multi-party negotiation.* Two parties who disagree is [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md)'s, four days later, and separating them is what lets today's encounter be about authority rather than about trade.
- *The regulatory route.* Point 7's second move names it and the day does not teach it. Disclosure ethics are SG390's and [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md)'s.
- *A second escalation encounter.* One encounter, six minutes, one staff member. A second would double the day's most expensive resource and would teach that the move is repeatable.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** Point 5's hypothesis move — naming an unstated interest aloud, as a hypothesis inviting correction — is **safe where the student holds a working relationship with the constraint's owner, where the hypothesis can be stated without accusation, and where being wrong costs the student standing rather than costing somebody else their position.** It is **unsafe where the interest concerns a person's employment, conduct, or a protected characteristic**, because in those cases a hypothesis stated aloud is an allegation regardless of the framing, the framing does not travel, and the cost of being wrong falls on the subject rather than on the speaker. The correct move there is to state the constraint's effect and not its cause, and **this day does not teach that move and has no home for it.** Point 7 carries a second and blunter statement: the four moves are safe as a map of what is available and **unsafe as a theory of professional courage**, which the programme does not have and should not simulate. Both statements are in the day.

**The platform instance carries a third, at point 9, and it is bound to the artefact.** The corpus-coverage method is **safe for establishing who can see what within a corpus you indexed, at the freshness your indexer runs at, and for distinguishing a party the system refused from a party the system has no record of.** It is **unsafe for any claim about a party's existence.** The failure is precise and is the one the day is most likely to produce: a zero-result query establishes that nothing of a party's is in the index, and a student who reads that as *this party has no material* has confused the corpus with the world — which is M3's error with a machine in front of it. **The artefact element that binds it is step 8's one-sentence frame requirement** — *what must a party have in order to appear in this output at all* — together with step 2's unsupported demand for a party the student believes exists and cannot name. A student who writes the frame correctly and then treats the query as an existence test has contradicted their own artefact, which is what makes the boundary checkable rather than exhortative.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **step 6.**

A student who has spent seventy minutes producing a party map and a finding, and then walks into a room with the person whose decision that finding contradicts, is in the only situation the module can construct that is genuinely uncomfortable in the professional register rather than the academic one. It is real: the staff member made the decision, the finding comes from the cohort's own [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) records or [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md) incident record, and the staff member is briefed to decline where the merits warrant it rather than to decline on cue.

Autonomy is at step 6's second clause: the student states, in advance, which of the four moves they would take if declined, and the design accepts all four including *accept the decision and record it*. **Recording acceptance is a legitimate professional answer and the specification treats it as one**, which is what prevents the step from becoming a test of who is willing to perform defiance.

Competence is supported by the preparation being scaffolded — the three conditions, the actionable-language standard — while the judgement is not.

Relatedness is supplied by phase 6's disclosure, which puts every student in the absent party's position before the analysis hardens into a technique, and by the second question, *what would it have cost the decider to have asked you*, whose answer is almost always *very little*.

**The affective risk is that step 6 rewards confrontation.** A cohort that reads the day as being about courage will produce students who escalate everything, which [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 3 already named as the same failure wearing better clothes. Countermeasures: the four moves are presented with costs and without a ranking; a recorded acceptance is a pass; the staff member's declines are reported at phase 8 with their grounds, in the room, so that a decline is visibly a judgement rather than an obstruction; and point 7 states outright that anyone claiming there is a correct answer has not been in the position.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items. Item 2 is the day's apparatus administered before it exists; item 3 is [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 4's unanswered case, distinct from that day's item 3 and reported beside it without comparison. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction and asks for the party the tool structurally cannot return.**

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Three retrievals at two years, one quarter and one week, closing with a bridge that converts last week's boundary into a political fact. The good-faith half of the answer is required from the room.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** **Six minutes executed, then thirty-nine demonstrated.** The executed part is M5, the party set the room assembles from the substrate's own tenant. The demonstrated part is four real maps of one deployment, sampling disclosed, adjudicated by the harm that occurred; they cannot be run and are **not assessed**. Payload M4. The task-after demands the mechanism of each absence, M5 included — and M5's is the only one written down in a product rather than inferred from an outcome.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Point 2 names M4's question; point 3 carries its own omission; points 4 to 8 each discharge a deferral from an earlier day; point 7 carries the two scope statements the day most needs. **Points 9 and 10 are the platform instance, placed after the argument per this module's convention; point 9 carries the third strong-form statement and point 10 names two defaults whose documented cost is a named person's inconvenience.**

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Eight steps on the cohort's own service, with the contradicted decision-owner present for thirty minutes. Steps 2 and 5 unsupported. Steps 3 and 6 are the two discharging artefact elements. **Step 8 is the build, timeboxed to thirty of the ninety, performed first and listed last, with the decision-owner's thirty minutes timetabled ahead of it.**

**Phase 6 (40 min).** An exemplary consultation with a harm its own method produced, **an agent-written access review that was right about every row and had no row for the documents the question was about**, and a disclosure that places the student among the absent.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Bilateral good-faith reconstruction, recorded as a contingent reuse of the mechanism [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) phase 3 item 8 first uses.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Party-set movement; the axis overlap; item 3 against the four moves with [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s responses beside and uncompared; **item 5's two rates reported separately**; the coverage checks' absent-versus-denied outcomes; the step 6 outcomes reported by the staff member in person.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has worked in a large organisation will produce a good map at item 1 and will rank M4 first at phase 3, because they have seen a short map beat a long one. The predicted failure is **premature closure at step 2**: they will state a sampling frame confidently and it will be the frame of the organisation they worked in rather than of the method they used today. Countermeasure: step 2's second clause requires them to name a party they believe exists and cannot name, and step 3 requires them to find one in the corpus. A frame that predicts no such party is checkable against an index.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is that the two axes are applied as a diagram-filling exercise, with every party placed and no placement evidenced. Countermeasures: step 1 requires evidence per placement, not a quadrant; the hard specification requirement is that the cannot-refuse-and-can-be-harmed quadrant is occupied or its emptiness is argued; and phase 3's task-after has already required a mechanism rather than a name four times.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Some groups will rank M4 first before the outcome is supplied, because the room contains students who have read the day's title. That is not a defect and the phase does not depend on the ranking being wrong. The difficulty is relocated to the task-after: **name a party affected and absent from each map, and say what produced the absence.** M4 is the hard one — a four-entry map omits nearly everybody, and the question is which omission mattered and why it did not. Fallback if a group answers that: ask what M4's author would have missed had the harm fallen on somebody who could complain, which has no clean answer and is the honest limit of the two-axis method.

**Affective failure.** Two.

The first is **cynicism**, described in §1 and countered by phase 7. It will not be fully prevented and the countermeasure is measurable: the phase 7 hypothesis survival rate is reported, and a cohort whose hypotheses almost never survive the good-faith account has learned to generate suspicion rather than analysis.

The second is **futility at point 7.** Four moves, all costly, none of them a remedy, delivered to twenty-year-olds about a situation the paper describes as normal. A room can leave this believing that professional integrity is a matter of choosing which harm to accept, and some of them will be right. Countermeasures: the encounter at step 6 frequently succeeds — the design's own §9 prediction is that a substantial share of findings are accepted — and the outcomes are reported in the room by the staff member, so the base rate is visible rather than assumed; and point 7's framing is that the moves have costs rather than that the situation is hopeless. **This is a countermeasure to the tone and not to the fact, and the fact is the one the design believes.**

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md).

One specification item, in PE390 with SG390. Per rule 1 the checked elements are the placements with evidence, the sampling frame with its unreachable set and one named unfindable category, the party absent from the corpus with the system's behaviour toward them, the message to a party who cannot refuse with a named teller, the interest hypothesis or the statement of what stopped it, the escalation encounter with the pre-stated move and its cost, the outcome in three-part form, the phase 7 result, **the shipped coverage check with its absent-versus-denied distinction and its one-sentence frame, the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

Four hard requirements: **an empty cannot-refuse-and-can-be-harmed quadrant fails unless its emptiness is argued and accepted**; **a move requiring an authority the student cannot name fails**; **a step 6 outcome recorded as accepted when the staff member's record says otherwise fails**; and **a coverage check that cannot distinguish absent from denied fails, as does a step 3 entry evidenced only by a zero-result query.** A recorded refusal is a pass. The fourth requirement is the §3.2 platform scope statement bound to the artefact, which is the widening [WP-055](wiki/whitepapers/WP-055.md) §8 showed to be cheaper than the register assumed and [WP-083](wiki/whitepapers/WP-083.md) §8 argues at length.

**What this day does not license anyone to conclude.** It generates evidence that a student can produce a party analysis and conduct one difficult conversation. It generates none about whether they would conduct it where the cost was their employment rather than six minutes of discomfort, and the design has no way to generate that and should not claim to. The assessors are told so in the brief, because a narrative that reads a good step 6 as evidence of courage has made exactly the inference point 7's scope statement forbids.

### Discharge of the deferral S069 point 4 placed on this day

**This paragraph is the report [WP-069](wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §8 required and could not verify.**

[S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 4 stated the three escalation conditions and their scope: "**It is unsafe where the path terminates in the person whose decision your finding contradicts, which is common, is not a procedural problem, and is [S082](wiki/seminars/S082.md)'s material; there the question is not how to escalate but what you are prepared to do, and this day does not answer it.**" [WP-069](wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §8 objection 2 then recorded, under the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s fourth register, that it "**cannot check that S082 carries an artefact element for it because S082 is not written**", and named that as exactly the failure the register was widened to catch.

**The element exists and it is an artefact element, not a discussion.** Phase 4 point 7 supplies the four moves with their costs and states that none of them is a procedure. **Phase 5 step 6 requires the student to take a real finding, from the cohort's own [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) or [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md) records, to the staff member who made the decision it contradicts, in person, with that person briefed to decline where the merits warrant it — and to state before the encounter which of the four moves they would take if refused and what it costs them.** Step 7 requires the outcome recorded in [S034](wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s three-part form including a refusal. The pre-stated move and its cost are checked elements; a move requiring an authority the student cannot name is a fail condition; and a recorded refusal is a pass. **The obligation is discharged, and WP-069's specific concern — that the receiving day might carry the material as content with nothing checkable — is answered.**

What is not answered is the substance of WP-069's objection, which was that the day teaching escalation supplied a procedure for the wrong case. This day supplies no procedure for the right one, because it does not believe there is one. That is a different answer from the one WP-069 wanted and it is the only honest one available.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: this day is the programme's disposal site for organisational difficulty, and it cannot carry what has been sent here.**

At its strongest: six earlier days deferred material to S082, and every one of the deferrals is correct. [S036](wiki/seminars/S036.md) point 2 sent organisational incentives and the compromise request. [S038](wiki/seminars/S038.md) point 4 sent two expert communities with rival vocabularies. [S039](wiki/seminars/S039.md) point 3 sent constraints protecting unstated interests — which [WP-039](wiki/whitepapers/WP-039.md) §8 called the constraints that actually block work in organisations. [S046](wiki/seminars/S046.md) point 2 sent criteria built to make a system look successful. [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5 sent the unindexed person. [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 4 sent the terminal escalation. Separately, [WP-017](wiki/whitepapers/WP-017.md) §6 named this day as the target of a stakeholder-representation measure taken two years earlier, and [WP-062](wiki/whitepapers/WP-062.md) §5 named the just distribution of latency as this day's subject.

**Two of the six get artefact elements. Four get a paragraph in phase 4 and nothing checkable. Neither of the two carried instruments is consumed by any element of this day at all.** A day with fifty minutes of consolidation cannot do more, and the design did not decide to do less; it accumulated the obligations one at a time over two years, each time correctly, and never checked the total.

**The objection is conceded and it is not repairable inside this day.** The [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s deferral register requires a whitepaper deferring material to "name the instrument by which that day will show the deferral was discharged — and must check that the named day can carry it." **Every one of these six deferrals was made to a day that did not exist, so the check was unperformable in every case, and each paper satisfied the rule's first clause and could not satisfy its second.** [WP-069](wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §8 is the only one that noticed and said so.

**There is now one counter-instance and it is instructive.** [S078](wiki/seminars/S078.md)'s rule-6 instrument, reported at §7, is the seventh inbound obligation on this day and the only one that named **the material** rather than the theme: not *S082 handles the person not in the room* but *S082 must use this cohort's per-class recall figures and its point 5 recall rate, and must state what it does differently if the recall rate is low.* **It is also the only inbound obligation this day discharges without argument**, because there was nothing left to decide. That is direct evidence for the cheap fix below rather than against it.

This is a violation of a written rule rather than a gap, so it belongs in the compliance register rather than in a whitepaper, and the entry is argued here in the register's own terms:

> \| [WP-082](wiki/whitepapers/WP-082.md) §8 — six prior days deferred omitted material to [S082](wiki/seminars/S082.md); two are discharged as artefact elements and four as taught content, and in every case the deferral rule's requirement to check that the named day can carry it was unperformable because the day was unwritten \| Scope rule, deferral register \| **Recorded** \| Which deferrals receive checkable elements is a design judgement the whitepaper is arguing for, not a correction; and the register's own criterion assigns judgements to *recorded* \|

**The cheap fix exists and is declined.** A deferral could be required to name the *element* it expects rather than the day — "S082 must carry an artefact element in which the student states which department's vocabulary the interface adopted" — which costs one clause at the deferring day and gives the receiving day an inventory rather than a theme. It is declined because it would have to be applied to every deferral in the programme to be worth anything, and applying it at one site is what [WP-064](wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 declined to do for the parallel binding-surface question, for a reason this paper accepts: a rule enforced at one day and not at the other eighty-nine makes the underlying question harder to see rather than easier. **The decline is now weaker than it was when this paragraph was first written, and the reason is above:** [S078](wiki/seminars/S078.md)'s obligation took the fix's form, cost one clause, and produced the only clean discharge on this day. **One working instance is not a warrant for a programme-wide rule and it is more than the design had.** **The honest summary is that the deferral rule has been operating in one direction for two years — deferring days name a receiver and the receiver finds out later what it has agreed to — that this day is where the accumulated bill arrives, and that the one deferral which specified its element is the one that arrived payable.**

**Objection 2: the day's most important element depends on one person's willingness, and the design has no account of what happens when they decline for the wrong reason.**

At its strongest: step 6 requires a staff member who made a real decision to sit for thirty minutes and be told, by twenty-year-olds, that their decision was wrong — and to decline on the merits where the merits warrant it. That is a considerable ask of a colleague, it recurs every cohort, and it is the second consecutive day in this module resting on a named individual's attendance after [S081](wiki/seminars/S081.md)'s maintainers.

The failure the design has no answer to is not absence, which is handled: without the staff member the day runs with a prepared escalation and no encounter, weaker and honest. **It is the staff member who attends and declines everything**, because declining is briefed as legitimate, because thirty minutes across a whole cohort is six minutes each, and because a person being contradicted twenty times in an afternoon will become defensive whatever their intentions. If that happens, the day's headline finding — the base rate of accepted findings, reported in the room to counter the futility risk — inverts, and a cohort is sent into [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md) having learned that raising a finding does nothing.

**The design's mitigation is thin and is stated as such.** The staff member is briefed that a decline must carry grounds, and the grounds are reported at phase 8 in the room, which makes a blanket decline visible. That is detection, not prevention. **The fix is to use two or three decision-owners rather than one, on different decisions**, which distributes the load, prevents any single person's fatigue determining the cohort's finding, and costs nothing but scheduling. **It is not in the design**, because the module already asks the institution for maintainers at S081, a decision-owner here, a decision-maker at [S083](wiki/seminars/S083.md), a user at [S084](wiki/seminars/S084.md) and two counterparties at [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md), and the paper judged that adding two more requests would push the module past what the substrate's institution will supply. **That is a resourcing judgement made without asking, which is precisely the move point 5 of this day tells students not to make**, and it is recorded rather than resolved.

**Objection 3: the day's new build teaches the opposite of the day's claim.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn and it is the sharpest of the three.

At its strongest: the day's whole argument is that a party with no channel into your method is invisible to it, that no amount of thoroughness closes the gap, and that the remedy is an inference rather than a longer list. **Then step 8 hands students a query that finds an absent party in four minutes.** A student who runs it, gets a name, and puts that name in step 3 has had the day's difficulty removed by a tool, and the lesson available to them is the opposite of the one intended: *the absent party is findable, you just have to query properly.* Worse, the build is seductive in exactly the direction the day warns against — it is fast, it is complete within its frame, and it produces a list. **M4 beat M3 because it was not a list. Step 8 produces a list.**

The rebuttal has one real part. The check does not find absent parties; it finds parties absent *from the corpus*, which is a different and much smaller claim, and it finds them only because the student already suspected a specific person and went looking. **It cannot enumerate.** That is why step 8 requires the one-sentence frame and why step 2 — unsupported, and prior — requires the student to name a category of person they cannot name a person in.

**The objection is conceded on the point that matters, which is what a student will take away rather than what the artefact requires.** A frame written correctly at step 8 and a conclusion drawn incorrectly at step 3 are entirely compatible, the design has no instrument that separates them, and §9 prediction 5 exists to detect the failure after the fact rather than to prevent it.

**The cheap fix is named and is taken in part.** The strongest version would be to require the coverage check to be run **twice — once for a party the student can name and once for the category they cannot** — so that the tool's silence on the second is part of the artefact rather than an absence from it. That costs one clause and would bind the boundary tightly. **It is not taken**, because a query for an unnameable category is not a query, and requiring students to construct one would produce a ritual failure with no information in it. **What is taken instead is weaker and is stated so it can be judged: the frame sentence is a checked element, a zero-result query alone cannot evidence step 3, and phase 6's agent case is chosen to be exactly this failure** — an access review that was right about every row and had no row for what the question was about. **A demonstration is not a fail condition and the design knows the difference.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At item 2, the set of parties who can stop the deployment and the set who cannot complain about it will be near-disjoint for a large majority of students, and a majority of item 1 lists will contain nobody in the second set at all. *Instrument:* item 2 responses coded for set membership and overlap. **The second clause is the claim**: a disjoint pair of non-empty sets means the cohort can see both and has not connected them, and an empty second set means they cannot see one, which are different findings requiring different days.

**Prediction 2.** At phase 3, groups will rank M3 above M4 before the outcome is supplied, and the reason given will refer to coverage rather than to method. *Instrument:* rankings with free-text justification, coded for whether the justification names a property of the method. **If groups rank M4 first for the right reason, the phase is over-built** and the day should open at the task-after.

**Prediction 3.** At step 6, more than a third of findings will be accepted by the decision-owner, and the modal ground for declining will be information the student did not have rather than disagreement about the finding. *Instrument:* step 6 outcomes and grounds, recorded by the staff member and coded. **The modal ground is the claim, and it is the prediction the day most needs to hold**, because a cohort whose findings are declined on the merits has learned something different from a cohort whose findings are declined because they were wrong about the facts — and only the second is a lesson about their own analysis.

**Prediction 4.** At phase 7, fewer than half of hypothesised unstated interests will survive the partner's good-faith account. *Instrument:* phase 7 outcomes coded for survival, with the distinguishing evidence recorded where the hypothesis survived. **If nearly all survive, the phase is not working as a countermeasure** and the cohort is constructing accounts designed to lose, which is the same defect [WP-070](wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §9 prediction 2 anticipates in the mechanism this structure reuses — and a failure there retracts this reuse under instrumentation rule 5.

**Prediction 5.** At item 5, a large majority will predict that M5 is longer than their own item 1 list, and **fewer than a third will name possession of an identity as its frame.** Separately, **more than a quarter of step 3 entries will treat a zero-result query as evidence that a party has no material, despite a correctly written frame sentence at step 8.** *Instrument:* item 5 coded on two axes; step 3 entries coded for whether the inference drawn exceeds the frame the same student wrote, cross-tabulated against the frame sentence's correctness. **The cross-tabulation is the claim and it is §8 objection 3's test.** If frame-correct students draw corpus-to-world inferences at the same rate as frame-incorrect ones, the checked element is a formality and the build is teaching what the objection says it teaches. **If the rate is near zero, the objection is answered and the paper was wrong to concede it.**
