# WP-079 — Proving They Can Run It

> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Rehearsal** | A period in which the recipient operates the system against real work while the delivery team observes and does not assist |
> | **Intervention condition** | A single observable condition, written before the rehearsal, under which the observer stops the run |
> | **Observed incapacity** | A specific act the recipient attempted and could not complete, recorded with what it obliges |

## In plain terms

Everything before this session is intention. A manifest is intention, a capability statement is intention, and a teaching plan is a very well-organised intention. This session is the only one in the stage that produces evidence.

The instrument is simple and socially expensive: the recipient operates the system against real work, and the person who built it watches and does not help. Then there is an interval in which the delivery team is reachable and is not used.

Three design decisions carry the session. The rehearsal must be run by **whoever will actually hold the responsibility**, not whoever is most interested, because the enthusiast produces a reassuring result and the person who will carry the consequence produces an informative one. The intervention condition must be **written before the rehearsal and phrased in terms of consequence**, not of discomfort, because *I will step in if it is going badly* is not a condition, it is permission. And the rehearsal must be run at a difficulty that produces a failure, because **a rehearsal in which everything went well was run at the wrong difficulty**, and the useful record is the one that names which difficulty was avoided.

The last movement is about the learner rather than the recipient. If they intervened outside their own written condition, that is the finding, and it is more useful than anything the recipient did.

## Abstract

This paper defends direct observation as the only instrument in this stage that produces evidence rather than intention. The argument is that a capability claim made without watching somebody act is a claim about their willingness, and that willingness and ability are separately observable and routinely conflated. The session produces a rehearsal record: what was attempted, what was observed act by act, what the recipient could not do, every intervention and whether it fell inside a pre-written condition, and what the observed incapacity obliges. The paper concedes that the programme can only run a simulation over a short interval with a recipient who cannot be genuinely harmed, that a short silent unsupervised interval proves very little, and that the session takes the opposite position from its source corpus on an argument rather than on evidence — the corpus contains no observation of anyone doing anything, anywhere.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A delivery team leaves on the strength of a belief about the recipient, and the belief is usually formed from the wrong evidence.

The evidence available in the room is attendance, agreement, questions and demeanour. All of it is social, all of it is about willingness, and all of it feels like knowledge because it is the only thing on offer. The delivery team is not credulous; it is reading the only signal present.

There is a second, quieter problem. Where a recipient does struggle, the struggle is usually resolved before it becomes information. Somebody helps, because the alternative — sitting still while a colleague founders on something you could fix in a sentence — is genuinely unpleasant and looks like unkindness. **The help is the failure**, and it costs nothing visible.

The session's job is to arrange one occasion where neither substitution is available.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why observation.** A stated understanding and a demonstrated performance are different observables, and the second cannot be inferred from the first. The distinction is borrowed, and its warrant is stated in full at [WP-076](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-076.md): a commercially interested practitioner model with no independent evaluation, licensing **direction and mechanism only.** The same source supplies the point that some people, for some changes, do not acquire the ability — which is why the session requires an incapacity to be recorded rather than resolved.

**3.2 Why the condition is written first.** A condition written during a rehearsal is a rationalisation of an intervention already made. Writing it beforehand converts *should I help?* from a live judgement under social pressure into a check against a stated fact. This is the same move as the reclaim rule at [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) and the evidence requirement at [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md), and like both it is **an argument from auditability with no external warrant.**

**3.3 Why consequence rather than discomfort.** A condition phrased around difficulty fires whenever the observer is uncomfortable, which is continuously. A condition phrased around consequence — an act about to affect something outside the exercise — fires rarely and identifiably, and the distinction between stopping and coaching becomes checkable.

**3.4 Why an unsupervised interval.** The supervised period observes acts. The unsupervised period observes whether the recipient reaches for the delivery team, which is a different fact and is the one that bears on the taper. **Its evidential value here is low and the session says so**, because the interval is short and its silence is as consistent with nothing happening as with competence.

**3.5 Why the source's position is rejected.** The practice corpus's discovery method is entirely self-report: nobody is asked to demonstrate a deployment, share a screen during a pipeline run, or set up an environment while being watched. That is a defect this programme names elsewhere, and this session inverts it. **The inversion is argued, not evidenced** — the corpus reports no observation, so there is nothing to compare against.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**The session expects the learner to intervene, and is arranged so that the intervention is recorded rather than hidden.** The commonest self-report afterwards is that the intervention was necessary; the written condition is what makes that claim checkable, and the movement that reports it is framed as the session's most useful finding rather than as a confession. **A programme that penalises this report will stop receiving it**, which is why the rule excluding it from the entrustment judgement at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) exists and is published to learners in advance.

**Operator selection is the session's second instrument.** The supplied situation offers an enthusiastic under-authorised team leader and a capable unwilling function, and the person who will actually carry the responsibility after handover is not obviously either. Learners who choose the enthusiast get a smooth rehearsal and a weak record, and the choice is visible in the record itself.

**The difficulty requirement is stated as a standard rather than enforced**, because a session cannot manufacture a real incident on demand. The compromise is that a rehearsal with no failure must name the difficulty it avoided, which converts an uninformative result into a statement about scope.

**The obligation clause is what stops the rehearsal being ceremonial.** An observed incapacity must move something: an owner, a dependency, a date, or the decision to leave. A rehearsal scheduled after the handover is signed cannot move anything, and the sequencing of [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) before [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) is the design's answer to that.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The rescued rehearsal.** *Countermeasure:* the written condition, the intervention count, and the reporting movement. **All three are self-report** and a learner who does not record an intervention cannot be detected.

**The wrong operator.** *Countermeasure:* the standard names the criterion — whoever holds the responsibility — and the record must state who was chosen and why.

**The easy day.** *Countermeasure:* the requirement to name the avoided difficulty.

**Willingness recorded as ability.** Observation notes full of adjectives about the person. *Countermeasure:* the record is structured by act, and an act either completed or did not.

**The finding with no consequence.** *Countermeasure:* the obligation clause and the sequencing before [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md).

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No specification item. The rehearsal record is the principal evidence entering the entrustment decision at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), where what is read is the fit between the learner's account of readiness and what the record shows.

**Nothing the learner surfaces about their own error in a rehearsal debrief may be used against them at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md).** This is a firewall, it is absolute, and it is published to learners before the rehearsal. If it is breached once, the mechanism is dead for that cohort.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the rehearsal is a simulation, and the paper's own limits section concedes so much that the exercise's evidential value approaches zero.**

At its strongest: the recipient is a role-player who cannot be harmed, the system carries no consequence into next month, the interval is short, the work is real only in the sense that the brief says it is, and everybody knows they are being watched. Observation under those conditions produces a performance. A learner who concludes from it that their recipient can or cannot operate a system has drawn an inference the setting cannot support, and the session's structure encourages exactly that inference by calling the output evidence.

**This is conceded without qualification.** The word *evidence* is used in this paper for what the rehearsal produces relative to the intention-shaped artefacts around it, not relative to a real observation, and that comparative sense is the only one available. **The fix that was considered and declined** was to run the rehearsal against a live system with a genuine operator outside the programme, which was rejected because the programme cannot place a learner's fictional handover in front of a real organisation, and a partial version — a real system with a role-played operator — would import the same defect with more logistics. The rejection is a constraint rather than a judgement, and the objection is right about what the constraint costs.

**Objection 2: watching somebody fail in front of their supplier is humiliating, and the session institutionalises it.**

At its strongest: the recipient is being observed by the people who built the thing, on their own site, at their own work, with their competence at issue and their employer's investment in the background. The instruction to say nothing while they struggle is not neutral — it is a power display, and a recipient who experiences it once will not agree to a second. The technique may be sound and unusable.

**This is substantially correct and the design's response is partial.** The rehearsal must be agreed in advance with its purpose stated as testing the handover rather than the person, the intervention condition exists so that the observer's silence has a stated boundary, and the record is structured by act precisely so that it does not become a character assessment. **None of that removes the asymmetry.** The programme's position is that the alternative — leaving without knowing — moves the humiliation to a night when nobody is there to see it, and that this is a worse trade. That position is a judgement about relative harms and has nothing behind it.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will intervene at least once, and most interventions will fall outside the written condition. *Instrument:* the intervention count and the condition text, coded *inside* / *outside* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Learners who select the enthusiastic operator will record fewer incapacities than those who select the responsibility-holder. *Instrument:* operator identity against the incapacity count.

**Prediction 3.** Rehearsals with no recorded incapacity will more often lack a named avoided difficulty than rehearsals with one. *Instrument:* the two record fields, cross-tabulated.

**Prediction 4.** A substantial share of observed incapacities will produce a note rather than a change to an owner, a dependency, a date or the decision. *Instrument:* the obligation field, coded *changed something* / *note only*. **If most produce notes, the obligation clause is inert** and the session should require the change to be made in the record before the session closes rather than asserted.

**None of these has been measured.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a capability claim made without observation is a claim about willingness; that a rehearsal producing no failure was run at the wrong difficulty; and that an intervention condition written before the rehearsal is a decision while one written during it is a rescue.

**What the evidence licenses.** The willingness-versus-ability distinction is direction and mechanism from a commercially interested source. The instrument, the conditions and the obligation clause are this design's arguments. **The rehearsal itself, in this programme, is a simulation and licenses nothing about a real recipient.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The most defensible output of this session is not a judgement about the recipient. It is a record of what the learner did when somebody in front of them could not do something — which is a fact about the learner, obtained cheaply, and the only one in the stage.

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The rehearsal instrument; the pre-written intervention condition and its phrasing in terms of consequence; the operator-selection criterion; the claim that a rehearsal without failure was run at the wrong difficulty; the requirement to name an avoided difficulty; the obligation clause; the unsupervised interval; the debrief firewall; and all four predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** **This session has no platform anchor and relies on no vendor documentation.** The subject is an observation protocol between two organisations, which no product describes. **The silence worth recording:** no vendor documentation known to this programme describes how a delivery organisation establishes that a customer can operate a delivered system, publishes an acceptance test for operational capability, or reports any observation of a customer operating one. Readiness material published across the industry describes checklists completed by the delivering party. The one thing nobody publishes is somebody watching the recipient work, which is what this session is. If such material appears, this session acquires an anchor and this paper records its version and its omissions.
