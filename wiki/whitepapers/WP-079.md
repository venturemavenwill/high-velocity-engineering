> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Rehearsal** | A period in which the recipient operates the system against real work while the delivery team observes and does not assist |
> | **Intervention condition** | A single observable condition, written before the rehearsal, under which the observer stops the run |
> | **Observed incapacity** | A specific act the recipient attempted and could not complete, recorded with what it obliges |
> | **Mastery experience** | Direct experience of succeeding at the task. The one source of efficacy belief Bandura ranks first, and the one this session either produces or destroys |
> | **Ad-hoc versus summative entrustment** | A continuous informal delegation judgement, versus a certification-like decision requiring multiple observers and multiple observations |
> | **Split strategy** | The rule turning a multi-turn conversation into a query and a response for scoring. It determines what the score is about |
> | **Access finding** | A permission, credential, project membership or quota the recipient discovered they did not hold. A result of the rehearsal, not a delay to it |
> | **Attribution** | What a person names as the cause of their own failure — effort, acquirable skill, the situation, or ability. Observable in a debrief |

## In plain terms

Everything before this session is intention. A manifest is intention, a capability statement is intention, and a teaching plan is a very well-organised intention. This session is the only one in the stage that produces evidence.

The instrument is simple and socially expensive: the recipient operates the system against real work, **on their own credentials**, and the person who built it watches and does not help. Then there is an interval in which the delivery team is reachable and is not used.

**Hands off the keyboard is not a refinement of the method; it is the method.** The mechanism the session runs on is a mastery experience, and a mastery experience is evidence for the person who had it. The moment the author types, the experience transfers to the author and the recipient is left with the weaker second source — watching somebody who is not much like them succeed. **There is no partial version of this that survives a small helpful correction.**

Four design decisions carry the session. The rehearsal must be run by **whoever will actually hold the responsibility**, not whoever is most interested. It must run on **the recipient's own credentials**, because a rehearsal on the author's sign-in has measured the author's access. The intervention condition must be **written before the rehearsal and phrased in terms of consequence**, not of discomfort, because *I will step in if it is going badly* is permission rather than a condition. And it must run at a difficulty that produces a failure, because **a rehearsal in which everything went well was run at the wrong difficulty.**

The last movement is about the learner rather than the recipient. If they intervened outside their own written condition, that is the finding, and it is more useful than anything the recipient did.

## Abstract

This paper defends direct observation as the only instrument in this stage that produces evidence rather than intention, and defends the absolute form of its central rule.

**The first argument is that a capability claim made without watching somebody act is a claim about their willingness.** Willingness and ability are separately observable and routinely conflated, and the evidence available in a handover meeting — attendance, agreement, questions, demeanour — is entirely of the first kind while being read as the second.

**The second argument is why the keyboard rule admits no exceptions**, and here the paper has its best warrant. Of the four sources from which a person forms a belief about their own capability, only mastery experience is explicitly ranked first, and it is ranked because it "provides the most authentic evidence of whether one can muster whatever it takes to succeed". **Evidence, and evidence belonging to whoever had the experience.** An author who takes the keyboard has not diluted the rehearsal; they have relocated it. The error-management literature reaches the same protocol from workplace training, and states it operationally: deliver the framing that errors are expected, then provide *no further assistance when an error occurs*.

**The third argument concerns what makes the rehearsal possible at all.** A rehearsal is a situation engineered so that somebody's inadequacy becomes visible to their supplier. That is an interpersonal risk before it is a technical exercise, and a recipient who cannot afford to fail will attempt only what they already know they can do — producing a record of successes that is then read as capability. **The session cannot manufacture psychological safety in an afternoon and does not claim to**; it removes the cheapest threats and states the residual as a limit.

The platform anchor makes the exercise concrete and adds one result the session did not originally seek. Running a Microsoft Agent Framework evaluation on the recipient's own credentials requires a project, a deployment, permission to invoke it and permission to read traces, and **most rehearsals stop at the sign-in.** Those access findings are results rather than delays: they are the commonest way a delivered system turns out to be unoperable, and they are invisible from the delivery side because the delivery team's credentials worked.

The paper concedes that the programme can only run a simulation over a short interval with a recipient who cannot be genuinely harmed; that a short silent unsupervised interval proves very little; that one observer on one occasion is an ad-hoc judgement and not the summative standard the entrustment literature specifies; that the safety literature it relies on is read at two removes; and that the session takes the opposite position from its source corpus on an argument rather than on evidence — the corpus contains no observation of anyone doing anything, anywhere.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A delivery team leaves on the strength of a belief about the recipient, and the belief is usually formed from the wrong evidence.

The evidence available in the room is attendance, agreement, questions and demeanour. All of it is social, all of it is about willingness, and all of it feels like knowledge because it is the only thing on offer. The delivery team is not credulous; it is reading the only signal present.

There is a second, quieter problem. Where a recipient does struggle, the struggle is usually resolved before it becomes information. Somebody helps, because the alternative — sitting still while a colleague founders on something you could fix in a sentence — is genuinely unpleasant and looks like unkindness. **The help is the failure**, and it costs nothing visible.

And there is a third, which the platform anchor exists to expose. **A system that a delivery team can operate is not thereby a system anybody else can operate**, because operation requires access, and access was granted to the delivery team early, invisibly, and often through a route that will not be available again. A team that never tests the system on somebody else's principal has never tested the system.

The session's job is to arrange one occasion where none of the three substitutions is available.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why the keyboard rule is absolute rather than strong.**

This is the section that decides whether the session's central instruction is a preference or a mechanism, and the answer is available in one ranked claim.

Of the four sources from which a person forms an efficacy belief, **only mastery experience is explicitly ranked**: "The most effective way of creating a strong sense of efficacy is through mastery experiences. **They provide the most authentic evidence of whether one can muster whatever it takes to succeed**" ([research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md](/research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md)).

**The operative word is *evidence*, and evidence has an owner.** The rehearsal is not a teaching device that happens to work better when the recipient does the typing; it is the production of evidence for a specific person about a specific capability. **When the author takes the keyboard, the evidence is produced about the author.** What the recipient receives instead is vicarious experience, the second named source, which is conditional on perceived similarity — "If people see the models as very different from themselves their beliefs of personal efficacy are not much influenced by the models' behavior" — and which, in the case of an expert on their own system, is the least similar model available.

Two further passages from the same source govern the session's difficulty setting and its debrief. **"If people experience only easy successes they come to expect quick results and are easily discouraged by failure. A resilient sense of efficacy requires experience in overcoming obstacles through perseverant effort."** That forecloses the reassuring rehearsal, and it corrects an earlier reading in this repository that would have licensed engineering the difficulty away. And the attributional split — efficacious people attribute failure to effort, acquirable skill or the situation; inefficacious people to ability — is described as observable, which makes the debrief a data-collection step rather than a courtesy.

The source also states, in terms, what a handover meeting achieves without any of this: **"The multiple benefits of a resilient sense of personal efficacy do not arise simply from the incantation of capability. Saying something should not be confused with believing it to be so."**

**Its limits.** A conference collection edited by the field's founder, reporting no search strategy, no inclusion criteria and, across the editor's own chapter, no study whose result went the wrong way; several load-bearing results cited as unpublished manuscripts. Empirical settings in schools, families, clinics, migration and addiction treatment, with **no study of adult professionals learning a technical craft**, so this application is an inference. Self-report measurement throughout, immediately before a performance that is itself often self-reported. Directionality conceded as unsettled in field settings. The foundational 1977 paper unread in this repository. **Its magnitudes are recorded in the research layer and prohibited here.**

**3.2 The independent convergence, and the protocol stated operationally.**

The error-management literature arrives at the same instruction from workplace training rather than from developmental psychology ([research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md)). Its two distinguishing elements are **minimal guidance** — participants receive only basic information and then work independently on difficult tasks, "a procedure that almost inevitably leads to many errors" — and **error-management instructions**, brief framing that errors are expected and informative. And then the operational clause this session lifts wholesale: the trainer repeats the framing and reminds participants to reflect, **"but provides no further assistance when an error occurs."**

**The framing is delivered; the help is not.** That is the rehearsal protocol in eight words, and two independent traditions agreeing on it is a stronger warrant than either alone.

The same source names the condition under which withholding help is unwise, and it governs which acts are worth observing: because the method withholds guidance, **"the task itself needs to provide clearly interpretable and informative feedback"**, and where feedback cannot be readily interpreted the approach may be worse than instruction supplying external guidance. **The authors state that this hypothesis received only limited support**, so this paper treats it as a caution about act selection rather than as a rule.

**Its structural limits.** Every study in the pool compared error-management training against another training method rather than against no training, so all effects are relative. **No study in the pool measured on-the-job performance** — every outcome is a post-training laboratory task. And the pooled homogeneity test was not significant, so the moderators are theory-driven partitions of a pool that did not statistically require partitioning; the authors declare this and proceed on stated grounds.

**3.3 Why the recipient must be able to fail, and why the session cannot guarantee it.**

A rehearsal engineers a situation in which somebody's inadequacy becomes visible to their supplier. **That is an interpersonal risk before it is a technical exercise**, and the construct that describes what people do about such risks is psychological safety ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)). Its account of the avoidance is exact: "No one wakes up in the morning excited to go to work and look ignorant, incompetent, or disruptive. These are called interpersonal risks, and they are what nearly everyone seeks to avoid, not always consciously." The learned response is automatic — "Don't want to look incompetent? Don't admit to mistakes or weaknesses."

**The consequence for this session is an inversion the design must guard against.** A recipient who cannot afford to fail will attempt only acts they already know they can perform. The record then shows a series of successes, and the absence of failure is read as capability — **which is precisely the error the session exists to prevent, produced by the session itself.**

The quantitative synthesis of the literature is retained here for a specific reason: it tests whether the construct explains anything over adjacent ones such as positive leader relations and work engagement, and it ranks antecedents rather than merely listing them ([research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)). **That incremental-validity question is the sceptic's question and it is the right one**; a construct that turned out to be a proxy for having a good manager would not warrant separate attention in a curriculum.

**The limits on both are real and are why this paper's claim here is modest.** The book consulted is a trade press statement by the construct's originator whose case material comes almost entirely from secondary sources — teaching cases, journalism, accident reports, a documentary — and whose review is narrative rather than systematic. **The 1999 paper that defined the construct empirically is unread in this repository, and the book does not verify it.** The meta-analysis note rests on the official abstract. **Direction and mechanism only.**

**What the design can and cannot do follows directly.** It cannot manufacture safety in an afternoon, and a session claiming to would be selling an environmental variable as a technique — the same objection this programme concedes about teaching refusal at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md). What it can do is remove the cheapest threats: state in advance what is being observed and what is not, keep the recipient's line manager out of the room unless the manager is the operator, and say plainly that an incapacity found today changes the plan rather than the person's record. **The residual is stated as a limit rather than solved.**

**3.4 What an observation licenses, and what this session's record may not claim.**

The entrustment literature supplies the standard, and the session does not meet it ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). Four sources of information support an entrustment decision: direct observations, longitudinal observations, case-based discussions and product evaluations. **This session produces the first and touches the third.**

It also separates two decision types. Ad-hoc entrustment is continuous and informal, taken when a supervisor judges that the learner's skills match the complexity and that the risks are acceptable. **Summative entrustment "has the nature of certification"** and **"should include an agreement between multiple observers that this entrustment is justified, to avoid subjective bias, and multiple observations".**

**One observer on one occasion is ad-hoc, and the rehearsal record must say so on its face.** Calling it more is the commonest route from an honest observation to an overclaim, and it is the route [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md)'s challenger is instructed to test.

The supervision sub-levels are the second reusable item and the one that gives a handover its resolution: level 3 splits into all findings double-checked, key findings double-checked, and a distant supervisor with prompt review; level 4 into remote monitoring and none. **A record reporting only *supervised* or *unsupervised* has discarded exactly the distinctions a handover consists of.** And the primer records that entrustment expires with disuse, which gives today's observation a shelf life nobody writes down.

**Its limits:** a clinical model with patients, legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint, so a course must construct its stakes artificially and the construction is the hardest part of the transfer; guidance from the concept's originator reporting adoption rather than effectiveness; and a full evidence standard expensive enough that **any course adopts a reduced version and should say honestly that it is reduced.** This one is reduced.

**3.5 Why the credentials matter, and why access findings are results.**

Running an evaluation is not one act ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). The framework's two evaluator tiers make the dependency structure explicit: a **`LocalEvaluator`** "runs checks locally without API calls — ideal for inner-loop development, CI smoke tests, and fast iteration", while **`FoundryEvals`** connects to a cloud service "for cloud-based LLM-as-judge evaluation" and requires a project with a model deployment, incurring cost.

**So the recipient needs an identity, a project membership, an invocation permission, a trace-read permission and a budget before any of this produces a number.** Most rehearsals stop somewhere in that chain, and stopping there is the finding rather than a failure of the exercise: it is the commonest way a delivered system turns out to be unoperable, and it is invisible from the delivery side because the delivery team's access was arranged early and often informally.

Three properties then decide whether the recipient's run means anything, and each is a thing they must be able to say rather than a thing they must do.

**The split strategy.** "Multi-turn conversations must be split into query and response halves for evaluation. **How you split determines *what you're evaluating*.**" Three are supplied — last turn, full, per-turn — plus a custom splitter. An agent that recovers from a bad first attempt scores well under one and badly under another, and neither is wrong. **A recipient who cannot name the strategy ran a command.**

**The threshold.** Output is pass or fail with a `reason`, plus a numeric score for scale-based evaluators; the documented example shows a tool-call-accuracy score of 2 failing against a threshold of 3. **The threshold is policy, separable from the measurement**, and moving it changes the pass rate without changing the system.

**Repetitions.** Supplied "to detect non-deterministic behavior" — the acknowledgement that one sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement.

The framework also permits evaluating **pre-existing responses** "from logs or previous runs… without re-running the agent", which is the act that distinguishes diagnosis from execution and is therefore the act worth observing at step 6.

**And the silence that travels with all of it: no agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold.** Most agent evaluators are model-judged. **Assert no agreement figure for any of them, here or anywhere.**

**Its limits:** first-party documentation, definitional rather than empirical; read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches, with structure reconstructed; most agent evaluators marked preview; one consulted page an explicitly superseded variant; API surfaces differing across languages so examples are not interchangeable; and cloud evaluation carrying a cost a course lab must budget for.

**3.6 Why the condition is written first, and phrased around consequence.** A condition written during a rehearsal is a rationalisation of an intervention already made. Writing it beforehand converts *should I help?* from a live judgement under social pressure into a check against a stated fact. And a condition phrased around difficulty fires whenever the observer is uncomfortable, which is continuously; one phrased around consequence — an act about to affect something outside the exercise — fires rarely and identifiably. **Both are arguments from auditability with no external warrant**, the same move as the reclaim rule at [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) and the evidence requirement at [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md).

**3.7 Why an unsupervised interval.** The supervised period observes acts. The unsupervised period observes whether the recipient reaches for the delivery team, which is a different fact and is the one that bears on the taper. **Its evidential value here is low and the session says so**, because the interval is short and its silence is as consistent with nothing happening as with competence.

**3.8 Why the source's position is rejected.** The practice corpus's discovery method is entirely self-report: nobody is asked to demonstrate a deployment, share a screen during a pipeline run, or set up an environment while being watched ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). That is a defect this programme names elsewhere, and this session inverts it. **The inversion is argued, not evidenced** — the corpus reports no observation, so there is nothing to compare against. **Access-controlled material, paraphrased and never quoted, carrying no citation of any kind.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The session expects the learner to intervene, and is arranged so that the intervention is recorded rather than hidden.** The commonest self-report afterwards is that the intervention was necessary; the written condition is what makes that claim checkable, and the movement that reports it is framed as the session's most useful finding rather than as a confession. **A programme that penalises this report will stop receiving it**, which is why the rule excluding it from the entrustment judgement at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) exists and is published to learners in advance.

**4.2 Operator selection is the session's second instrument.** The supplied situation offers an enthusiastic under-authorised team leader and a capable unwilling function, and the person who will actually carry the responsibility after handover is not obviously either. Learners who choose the enthusiast get a smooth rehearsal and a weak record, and the choice is visible in the record itself.

**4.3 The clock starts at the sign-in, not at the evaluation.** This is the design decision that most changes what the session finds, and it was added because the alternative hides the commonest failure. **A rehearsal that begins after access has been arranged has silently excluded access from the system**, and access is the part of a delivered system that most reliably does not transfer.

**4.4 The recipient is asked to *read* the configuration before running it.** Two questions — which split strategy, and where the threshold came from — separate an operator from an executor at negligible cost, and they are asked before the run rather than after so that the answer is not reconstructed from the result. **This is the only movement in the session that tests understanding rather than performance**, and it is included because §3.4's evidence standard names case-based discussion as an evidence source and the session would otherwise produce none.

**4.5 The difficulty requirement is stated as a standard rather than enforced**, because a session cannot manufacture a real incident on demand. The compromise is that a rehearsal with no failure must name the difficulty it avoided, which converts an uninformative result into a statement about scope.

**4.6 The obligation clause is what stops the rehearsal being ceremonial.** An observed incapacity must move something: an owner, a dependency, a date, or the decision to leave. A rehearsal scheduled after the handover is signed cannot move anything, and the sequencing of [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) before [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) is the design's answer to that.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The rescued rehearsal.** *Countermeasure:* the written condition, the intervention count, and the reporting movement. **All three are self-report** and a learner who does not record an intervention cannot be detected — and §3.1's own literature on perception suggests the undercount will be systematic rather than random.

**The borrowed credential.** The recipient works from the author's session, or from an account the delivery team created that morning. *Countermeasure:* the record asks what was refused, and a rehearsal with no refusals is flagged. **Weak**, because a competent delivery team can pre-provision everything and produce a clean run that proves nothing.

**The wrong operator.** *Countermeasure:* the standard names the criterion — whoever holds the responsibility — and the record must state who was chosen and why.

**The easy day.** *Countermeasure:* the requirement to name the avoided difficulty.

**Willingness recorded as ability.** Observation notes full of adjectives about the person. *Countermeasure:* the record is structured by act, and an act either completed or did not. The debrief records the attribution verbatim rather than the observer's impression.

**The command mistaken for the evaluation.** *Countermeasure:* the two questions at step 4, asked before the run.

**The finding with no consequence.** *Countermeasure:* the obligation clause and the sequencing before [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md).

**The recipient who will not risk failing.** *Countermeasure:* the cheapest threat-removals in §3.3 — stated scope, no line manager present, an explicit statement that an incapacity changes the plan rather than the record. **This is the countermeasure least likely to work**, because the thing being addressed is a property of the recipient's organisation rather than of the session, and a one-afternoon intervention against an environmental variable is what §3.3 says cannot be done. See prediction 6.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No specification item. The rehearsal record is the principal evidence entering the entrustment decision at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), where what is read is the fit between the learner's account of readiness and what the record shows.

**Nothing the learner surfaces about their own error in a rehearsal debrief may be used against them at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md).** This is a firewall, it is absolute, and it is published to learners before the rehearsal. If it is breached once, the mechanism is dead for that cohort.

**The record's own standard is part of what is assessed at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), and it is the part learners get wrong.** A record that describes one observer on one occasion as though it established capability has overclaimed, and the challenger is instructed to find it. **The correct form is available and costs nothing: state the observation, state that it is ad-hoc, and state what a summative judgement would have required.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the rehearsal is a simulation, and the paper's own limits section concedes so much that the exercise's evidential value approaches zero.**

At its strongest: the recipient is a role-player who cannot be harmed, the system carries no consequence into next month, the interval is short, the work is real only in the sense that the brief says it is, and everybody knows they are being watched. Observation under those conditions produces a performance. A learner who concludes from it that their recipient can or cannot operate a system has drawn an inference the setting cannot support, and the session's structure encourages exactly that inference by calling the output evidence.

**This is conceded without qualification.** The word *evidence* is used in this paper for what the rehearsal produces relative to the intention-shaped artefacts around it, not relative to a real observation, and that comparative sense is the only one available. **The fix that was considered and declined** was to run the rehearsal against a live system with a genuine operator outside the programme, which was rejected because the programme cannot place a learner's fictional handover in front of a real organisation, and a partial version — a real system with a role-played operator — would import the same defect with more logistics. The rejection is a constraint rather than a judgement, and the objection is right about what the constraint costs.

**One thing survives the concession, and it is worth naming.** The access findings are not simulated. A recipient's credentials either reach the project or do not, and that result is as real inside the exercise as outside it. **It is the only part of the rehearsal whose validity does not depend on the role-play**, which is a reason to weight it and not a reason to trust the rest.

**Objection 2: watching somebody fail in front of their supplier is humiliating, and the session institutionalises it.**

At its strongest: the recipient is being observed by the people who built the thing, on their own site, at their own work, with their competence at issue and their employer's investment in the background. The instruction to say nothing while they struggle is not neutral — it is a power display, and a recipient who experiences it once will not agree to a second. The technique may be sound and unusable.

**This is substantially correct and the design's response is partial.** The rehearsal must be agreed in advance with its purpose stated as testing the handover rather than the person, the intervention condition exists so that the observer's silence has a stated boundary, and the record is structured by act precisely so that it does not become a character assessment. **None of that removes the asymmetry**, and §3.3's literature says plainly that what is being asked for — willingness to be seen failing — is conditional on an environment the session does not control. **The declined fix was to make the rehearsal self-administered and self-reported**, which was rejected because it reinstates exactly the self-report method the session exists to replace. The programme's position is that the alternative — leaving without knowing — moves the humiliation to a night when nobody is there to see it, and that this is a worse trade. **That position is a judgement about relative harms and has nothing behind it.**

**Objection 3: the platform half turns an observation protocol into a product exam, and rewards learners whose recipients happen to have good tooling.**

At its strongest: whether a recipient can run an evaluation depends on which framework the delivery team chose, how the project was scoped, whether the organisation's identity model permits guest access, and what the tenant's policy allows. None of that is about the recipient's capability, all of it varies enormously between organisations, and a session that records an access refusal as a finding about the handover has confounded four things. Worse, it teaches a specific vendor's evaluator vocabulary — split strategies, thresholds, tiered evaluators — which will be renamed, and which a learner may carry into an environment where none of it applies.

**Partially conceded, and the confounding is real.** The design's answer is that the confounding is the point rather than an artefact: **a recipient who cannot run the harness because of a tenant policy still cannot run the harness**, and the handover is defective in a way that is invisible to any document. The session asks what was refused rather than whose fault it was, and the obligation clause requires the refusal to move something regardless of where the cause sits. **The declined fix was to have the recipient run only local checks, which need no project, no deployment and no permission** — declined because it removes the access wall from the exercise, and the access wall is the finding with the highest ratio of consequence to cost in the entire stage. **The cost of that decision is that this session's platform half will need re-verification before every offering**, and the perishability register says so.

**Objection 4: the paper leans on an efficacy literature that has never studied anybody like the recipient, and uses it to justify an absolute rule.**

At its strongest, and it is the objection with the best paperwork: the ranking of mastery experience comes from a conference volume edited by the theory's originator, containing no disconfirming result in his own chapter, drawing on schools, families, clinics, migration and addiction treatment, measured almost entirely by self-report, with the foundational paper unread by this repository. **An absolute prohibition on touching a keyboard is a strong operational rule to derive from a source that has never observed an engineer, a handover, or a piece of software.**

**Conceded, and the concession changes the rule's status rather than the rule.** The keyboard rule is not licensed by the efficacy literature; it is *motivated* by it and independently converged on by a workplace-training synthesis whose own limits are stated in §3.2 and which also never measured anybody doing a job. **What makes the rule absolute is not the strength of the evidence but the structure of the failure**: a partial rule — help only a little, only when it is really needed — is unenforceable, uncountable and indistinguishable from no rule, because every intervention feels necessary at the moment it is made. **The declined fix was a graduated rule with permitted assistance levels**, rejected on exactly those grounds. A reader who weighs the population mismatch more heavily than this paper does should read the rule as a design convention with a psychological motivation rather than as a finding, and the session survives that reading.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will intervene at least once, and most interventions will fall outside the written condition. *Instrument:* the intervention count and the condition text, coded *inside* / *outside* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Learners who select the enthusiastic operator will record fewer incapacities than those who select the responsibility-holder. *Instrument:* operator identity against the incapacity count.

**Prediction 3.** Rehearsals with no recorded incapacity will more often lack a named avoided difficulty than rehearsals with one. *Instrument:* the two record fields, cross-tabulated.

**Prediction 4.** A substantial share of observed incapacities will produce a note rather than a change to an owner, a dependency, a date or the decision. *Instrument:* the obligation field, coded *changed something* / *note only*. **If most produce notes, the obligation clause is inert** and the session should require the change to be made in the record before the session closes rather than asserted.

**Prediction 5.** More rehearsals will halt at an access refusal than at any act in the capability statement, and most learners will record the refusal as a setup problem rather than as a result. *Instrument:* the rehearsal record's stop reason, and the placement of access items in the findings section versus the preamble.

**Prediction 6.** Recipients told in advance that an incapacity changes the plan rather than their record will attempt more acts they are unsure of than those not told. *Instrument:* a within-cohort split on whether the statement was made, against the count of attempted-and-failed acts. **This predicts the failure of the session's own weakest countermeasure and is the prediction most likely to come out null**, because §3.3 holds that the variable being addressed belongs to the recipient's organisation rather than to the session — and a null result would mean the threat-removal movement is ceremony and should be replaced by an honest statement that the session cannot secure the condition it depends on.

**Prediction 7.** Most recipients will be unable to name the split strategy in force before running the suite, and most learners will not have known it either. *Instrument:* the step-4 answers, recorded for both parties. **If the learner cannot answer, the harness was inherited rather than built**, and the finding belongs at [S063](/wiki/seminars/S063.md) rather than here.

**None of these has been measured.** The register is machinery and not a result about this session.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a capability claim made without observation is a claim about willingness; that a mastery experience is evidence belonging to whoever had it, so the keyboard rule is a mechanism rather than a courtesy; that a rehearsal producing no failure was run at the wrong difficulty; and that access is part of the system and is only discovered by somebody trying.

**What the evidence licenses.** The keyboard rule is *motivated* by a well-read source and independently converged on by a second, and neither has studied anybody like your recipient. The observation standard is well specified and this session's version of it is reduced. The platform properties are definitional and checkable. **The rehearsal itself, in this programme, is a simulation and licenses nothing about a real recipient** — except the access findings, which are as real inside the exercise as outside it.

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper**, including from the two sources read in full whose effect sizes are recorded in the research layer, and including any agreement figure for any evaluator, because none is published.

**The concession worth remembering.** The most defensible output of this session is not a judgement about the recipient. It is a record of what the learner did when somebody in front of them could not do something — which is a fact about the learner, obtained cheaply, and the only one in the stage.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That the practice corpus's discovery method is entirely self-report about the engineering system, with nobody asked to demonstrate a deployment, share a screen during a pipeline run, or set up an environment while being watched; that it contains no consolidation across informants and an artefact that actively prevents it; and that it proposes substituting a model's simulation of a user for a user without any validity discussion, in a collection that opens by warning against building for an imagined user. Recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md). Also verified: the knowledge-versus-ability distinction, its named barriers, and the closed-feedback-channel mechanism, recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md), together with the warrant limits stated there.

The efficacy claims in §3.1 — the ranking of mastery experience and its description as the most authentic evidence, the similarity condition on vicarious experience, the only-easy-successes failure mode, the saying-versus-believing warning, and the attributional split as an observable — are recorded at [research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md](/research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md), **which rests on a complete reading of the 1995 volume.** That note also records that the 1977 paper is unread, that the volume is a conference proceedings with no disconfirming result in its lead chapter, and that it contains no study of adult professionals.

The protocol in §3.2 — the two elements of error-management training, the framing statements, and the instruction that the trainer provides no further assistance when an error occurs, together with the feedback-clarity moderator and the authors' statement that it received only limited support — is recorded at [research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md), **which rests on a complete reading of the article**, and which also records that all effects are relative to another training method and that no study in the pool measured on-the-job performance.

The evidence standard in §3.4 — the four information sources, the ad-hoc versus summative distinction with the multiple-observer requirement quoted, the expanded supervision sub-levels, and expiry through disuse — is verified against the open-access primer at [research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md).

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That a demonstrated performance and a stated understanding are different observables; that performance under observation differs from unobserved performance; that assistance offered during an observation removes the observation; and that people avoid interpersonal risks such as appearing incompetent, so that a person who cannot afford to fail will attempt less. The psychological-safety notes at [research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md) and [research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md) are held in this repository, but the first is a trade press restatement whose defining empirical paper is unread here, and the second rests on its abstract. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted for any of these.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The rehearsal instrument; the absolute rather than graduated form of the keyboard rule; the pre-written intervention condition and its phrasing in terms of consequence; the operator-selection criterion; the decision to start the clock at the sign-in rather than at the evaluation; the treatment of access refusals as results rather than as setup; the requirement that the recipient read the configuration and answer two questions before running it; the claim that a rehearsal without failure was run at the wrong difficulty; the requirement to name an avoided difficulty; the obligation clause; the unsupervised interval; the debrief firewall; and all seven predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The two evaluator tiers and their differing access and cost requirements — a local evaluator running checks without API calls, and a cloud evaluator requiring a project with a model deployment and incurring cost; the three conversation split strategies together with the statement that how you split determines what you are evaluating, and the custom-splitter signature; the pass-or-fail output format with `label`, `reason`, `threshold` and `passed`, the numeric score for scale-based evaluators, and the documented example of a tool-call-accuracy score of 2 failing against a threshold of 3; repetitions supplied to detect non-deterministic behaviour; the evaluation of pre-existing responses from logs without re-running the agent; and the data-mapping expressions distinguishing structured output from plain text — are recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md). **That note records that the material was read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches, with structure reconstructed; that most agent evaluators are marked preview; and that one consulted page is an explicitly superseded variant.** First-party documentation, definitional rather than empirical: it is an accurate description of what the tooling computes and no evidence that what it computes is right.

**The silences that matter, and there are four.** First, and most consequential for this session: **no agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold** — so a recipient who runs the suite competently and reads the result correctly still has no basis for believing the verdict corresponds to what a competent human would say. Second, **the documentation states the permissions required to invoke a deployment and read traces nowhere in the evaluation pages themselves**, so the access chain this session tests must be assembled from separate identity documentation; there is no published account of what a minimally-permissioned operator can and cannot do. Third, **no vendor documentation known to this programme describes how a delivery organisation establishes that a customer can operate a delivered system**, publishes an acceptance test for operational capability, or reports any observation of a customer operating one. Readiness material published across the industry describes checklists completed by the delivering party. **The one thing nobody publishes is somebody watching the recipient work, which is what this session is.** Fourth, the documentation publishes no measure of how often an evaluation run is misconfigured in a way that produces a plausible number — the failure mode a rehearsal is most likely to observe and least likely to recognise.
