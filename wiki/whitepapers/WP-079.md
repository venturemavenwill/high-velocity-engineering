# WP-079 — Drift, Alerting and the Incident You Did Not See

**Seminar day:** [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md) · **Module:** [M16](wiki/modules/M16.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](wiki/quarters/Q8.md)
**Strands:** ME · SG · SY · **Complexity class:** 6
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Foundry observability — OpenTelemetry tracing into Application Insights, the Agent Monitoring Dashboard, continuous evaluation on sampled responses, and alerts · Defender for Cloud AI alerts · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server · Azure MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends the day on which a promise made nine weeks earlier is checked against what actually happened, and defends checking it without assessing the result. The design's target is that a condition arriving gradually has no start, so the detection form the cohort holds cannot be filled in; that a stopped signal is the modal long-horizon failure rather than an edge case; and that an alerting threshold is a public budget an adversary reads, which is the module's thesis in operational form. The mechanism leaned on hardest is four alerting designs over one component's six-month record, configured and re-run rather than described, with the payload constructed as a function of whichever monitor failure the cohort omitted at [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md). The platform turn adds a fifth configuration that is not a failure at all: a sampled, rate-capped quality monitor that stops producing results for documented and entirely correct reasons while its dashboard stays green. **Section 7 reports the discharge of the two obligations [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) placed on this day and the ones [S074](wiki/seminars/S074.md) and [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md) placed on it.** Section 8 concedes that the day's central instrument requires something to have gone wrong, that the quarter may not oblige, and that the fallback is weaker than the design would like to admit.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The cohort holds a detection form that works. [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 2 supplied it — what condition, within what time, observed by whom, at what false-alarm rate — they built one, they defended it at [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md), and it has been running for nine weeks. **The form is correct and it does not fit today's material**, and the difficulty is that partial fit is harder to teach than no fit.

Four difficulties operate.

**The first slot cannot be filled.** *What condition* presupposes a condition: something that is true after a moment and false before it. Drift has no such moment. A student reaching for the form finds it works for slots two, three and four and fails at slot one, and the available responses are to invent a threshold (which converts the drift into a failure that starts, incorrectly) or to conclude the form is wrong (which discards a good form). The correct response — that the first slot expands into three, a reference, a distance and a window — is not reachable by inspection, which is why the phase 2 bridge exists.

**A reference is a decision made before there is anything to detect.** This is the day's genuinely counterintuitive result and it is a temporal inversion of everything the students hold about measurement. Every measurement discipline in [M06](wiki/modules/M06.md) and [M10](wiki/modules/M10.md) begins with a question and then chooses an instrument. Here the instrument's most important parameter — what "normal" is — has to be fixed before the question exists, by somebody who has no reason to think carefully about it, and the choice is usually made by whoever set up the dashboard.

**The long horizon changes which failure is modal.** [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 8 named the third outcome and treated a liveness property as a design refinement. Over nine minutes it is. Over nine weeks, deployments happen, exporters change, collection paths move, and **a signal that stopped is more likely than a system that broke.** The students will not have internalised this because their [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) commitments were written with an incident in mind. **And the platform supplies a version of it the design did not previously have available: a monitor that stops producing results because it was configured to sample and capped to a run rate, both correctly, with nothing anywhere reporting red.** That case is harder than a dead exporter, because there is no fault to find.

**And the adversarial difficulty, which is why this day is in this module.** [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md) established that an adversary aims at whichever commitment is weakest. A threshold is a commitment; it is written in the design document; it is visible to everyone inside the organisation. **Detection tuned on observed variation tells an adversary exactly how much is free.** The difficulty is not that students will disagree — they will accept it instantly — but that the obvious remedy, a lower threshold, is paid for at point 6 and the room will propose it anyway.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

The day carries **two structures with a deliberate seam between them**, and the seam is where the load is.

The first structure is the extended detection form: reference, distance, window, time, observer, false-alarm rate. Six elements, all interacting, and the interaction is real — the window constrains the achievable detection time, the distance constrains the false-alarm rate, and the reference determines whether the distance means anything. That is high element interactivity and it is managed by supplying the form and by working step 3 with the room.

The second structure is the human half of an alert: told what, when, expected to do what, and what happens if they do not. Four elements with almost no interaction between them, which is why it can be introduced in one phase 4 point and produced in one artefact step. **The seam matters because students will try to derive the second from the first**, and it does not derive: what a person is expected to do at three in the morning is not a function of the false-alarm rate.

Management:

- **The six-month record is supplied whole**, in writing, so that the series can be re-inspected. A drift argument made from a transient display would be a representation-side difficulty the programme's reconciliation rule forbids.
- **The [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) commitments are retrieved rather than recalled**, and are physically sealed at phase 1 so that item 2 measures belief and step 1 measures fact.
- **Step 3's test selection is worked with the room**, because the failure mode is defaulting to whichever detector the library exposes and that failure is invisible in a written artefact.
- **The statistical methods themselves are not taught**; the day teaches the choice and defers the methods to ME302 with an assessed join. This is a substantial load reduction and §8 objection 2 argues it may be too substantial.
- **One detector is built and it runs on a series that already exists**, and the four phase 3 designs are configured on telemetry the cohort already emits. Nothing on the day requires new instrumentation to be stood up.

Expertise reversal applies to the whole cohort at point 6, which is [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 6 with evidence attached rather than new content, and it is delivered in two minutes as a re-expression rather than as instruction.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Stating whether your own commitment fired, from memory, before opening the record*, item 2.
- *Predicting your own latency error, signed*, item 3.
- *Estimating your own dead-signal latency before the day's payload*, item 4.
- *Being asked for a start date for a series that has no start*, item 1.
- *Committing an alerting ranking before six months of record*, phase 3.
- *A payload constructed from the cohort's own earlier omission*, A4.
- *Being shown a monitor that stopped for a documented and correct reason*, the fifth configuration, which is harder to accept than a fault.
- *Naming a recording change that would separate attack from accident, unsupported*, step 5.
- *Writing a contract that promises less than the student wanted to promise*, step 6's unpublished-accuracy clause.
- *Hearing a peer say what they would do at three in the morning and discovering they supplied it themselves*, phase 7.
- *Asking a professional what would make them mute your alert*, step 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Deriving or implementing the statistical tests.* ME302's, with a join. The day teaches the decision the parameter encodes.
- *Building a monitoring platform.* SY301's, and there is no concurrent course; the day operates on the substrate's existing collection path.
- *Root-cause analysis of the drifts observed.* [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md)'s, one week later, and deliberately withheld so that this day stays about detection rather than about explanation.
- *Assessing whether the commitment held.* Excluded on the [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §7 argument, which this design accepts unchanged: an assessed prediction check is a strategy.
- *Anomaly detection as a machine-learning problem.* IS302's; the day treats a detector as a decision rule with a parameter.

**Items 2, 3 and 4 are placed at phase 1 as a deliberate selection and it is recorded as one.** The design has twice observed that **it places its phase 5 instruments downstream of its own instruction** — opened at [WP-054](wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 and recorded as a second instance at [WP-066](wiki/whitepapers/WP-066.md) §8 — with the rule it would become already stated: *an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.* The natural design for this day asks the dead-signal question at step 4 and reports it at phase 8. **That would be a third instance and would elevate the pattern.** Item 4 exists so that the day has a pre-instruction reading, and item 2 exists so that the commitment check measures whether the student had been watching rather than whether they can read a log. **Item 6 is under the same constraint and for the same reason.** The pattern remains at two and is not elevated by this paper; it is cited as a constraint that changed four phase 1 items, which is what a pattern-level observation is for while it is still an observation.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The day's apparatus — the extended target, the four drift kinds, the statistical parameter as an error preference, the alert's human half — is **safe for a component whose output can be summarised as a distribution over a series you own, with a rota that will actually receive the alert.** It is **unsafe in three conditions.** It is unsafe for a system whose output is a trajectory rather than a value: an agent's behaviour drifts in a space with no series to plot, point 3 has nothing to attach to, and a student who applies this apparatus to their [M15](wiki/modules/M15.md) agent will monitor its tool-call latency and call that behavioural monitoring. It is unsafe as a substitute for the tests' assumptions: every detector in the frame assumes something about independence or stationarity that production series violate, the frame will still produce a well-formed detector, and the assumption failure is silent — which is why step 3's ME302 join is a checked element rather than a suggestion. And it is unsafe as a security control: point 7's remedy, keeping a mechanism out of the design document, is a threshold with a secret, and [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md) point 3's analysis applies to it unchanged. **All three are stated to students in the points that carry them.**

**The platform instance carries a scope statement of its own.** Point 8 teaches one vendor's observability stack as the concrete form of the day's apparatus. That is **safe as a reason to know your monitor's sample rate, its run caps, its retention and its published accuracy before you promise anything on top of it, and as a worked instance of an instrument having a resolution.** It is **unsafe in three directions.** It is unsafe as coverage — an instrumented component is not a monitored one, and two of point 3's four drift kinds have no series regardless of how good the tracing is. It is unsafe as a source of thresholds: the documentation's guidance values are reasonable and are somebody else's decision, and a student who adopts them has performed exactly the failure point 2 names. And it is unsafe as evidence of detection: **no accuracy is published for the security alerts, the content filters or the quality evaluators in this stack**, so a contract may commit to a routing and a latency and may not commit to a detection rate. **The third condition is a fail condition in the specification** rather than a caveat in this paper, which is the strong form's binding-surface widening applied voluntarily ahead of the date it takes effect.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **the sealed [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) commitment on the desk.**

The mechanism is answerability across time, which [M14](wiki/modules/M14.md) identified as the class-5 acquisition and which this day converts into a measurement. A student who wrote *I will know within four hours, observed by the rota, at fewer than one false alarm a week, and if the signal itself fails I will know within a day* made a promise in public. Nine weeks later the promise has an answer, and the answer is not the instructor's opinion.

**The decoupling from assessment is what makes it work rather than what softens it.** The specification checks that the student stated what happened, not what happened. This is not generosity: an assessed commitment check produces commitments written to be safe, which is [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §7's argument and this design accepts it unchanged. The framing is stated before phase 1 and was stated at [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) as well.

The second mechanism is **step 7's mute question**. Asking a professional what would make them stop responding to your alert is an uncomfortable question and it produces the most useful sentence in the artefact, because the answer is always specific — *if it fires twice in a night with nothing behind it*, *if I can't do anything about it from home* — and it names a condition the student's own design creates.

The third is **phase 7's asymmetry**. The peer cannot fill the gap in a badly written contract from experience; the rota member can and will. Running the same test with both readers gives the student a direct measurement of what their document supplies versus what the reader supplies, which is the class-6 discrimination in its cheapest form.

The affective risk is specific and serious: **a student whose commitment failed, publicly, nine weeks after defending it at an entrustment occasion.** The countermeasures are the decoupling; the cohort-level reporting of the four commitment outcomes without names; the phase 6 case in which nobody was wrong; and the instructor's statement, made once, that a commitment that failed and was noticed is worth more than one that was never checked. **The design's position is that this is the correct discomfort and that it must not be removed** — a cohort whose promises are never checked will make promises accordingly, which is the condition [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 6 describes for alerts and which applies equally to commitments.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Six sealed items on the cohort's own six-month record, with each student's [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) commitment sealed on the desk. Items 2, 3, 4 and 6 are the pre-instruction readings whose placement is argued at §3.2. Item 1 asks for a date the series cannot supply. **Item 6 asks what an agent will omit from a monitoring configuration, and the design's expected answer is the day's own subject.**

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals at four quarters, three quarters, two quarters and one week. The bridge produces point 1 and the room answers it. [S075](wiki/seminars/S075.md) is retrieved for the case point 3 cannot cover.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four alerting designs, six months each, **configured and re-run over the recorded window rather than summarised.** A1 is credibility decay with evidence; A2 is the undocumented parameter; A3 defeats the four-part form; **A4 is the payload and is constructed as a function of the [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) phase 1 item 3 result.** **The fifth configuration is the platform's own sampled evaluator and is A4 without a fault.** Task 1 consumes [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s recording gaps; task 2 makes the finding the student's own.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Nine points. Point 1 generated by the bridge; point 4 by A4; point 6 delivered as a re-expression under expertise reversal. Points 2, 3 and 7 carry strong-form scope statements. Point 2 carries the ME302 cross-course deferral with its join named in the same point. **Point 5 is the day's new object** — the alert's human half, which [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) deliberately did not build. **Point 8 is the platform-specific content and consolidates the fifth configuration rather than introducing it**; point 9 is the class-6 close.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps producing an alerting contract for a named receiver **and a shipped detector left running**. Step 3 worked; **step 5 unsupported**. Step 4 is [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s rule-6 element; step 5(a) is [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s and step 5(b) is [S074](wiki/seminars/S074.md)'s. Step 7 uses an external observer per rule 1 in the narrate-not-approve form, contingent under rule 5; **the mute question is new and is recorded as a proposed remedy.** **The artefact carries the prompt, intervention and frontier records.**

**Phase 6 (40 min).** The eleven-week dead exporter. A clean gate, a clean deploy, a green dashboard and four daily readers. Nobody was wrong. **Then the agent case: a good monitoring configuration with no signal reporting on the signals.**

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Construct a drift inside the window; then read the contract and say what you would do, and whether the contract told you.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 2's commitment result in four categories; **item 3's signed latency comparison, which is the day's most quoted result**; item 1's confidently-given dates for series with no start; item 4 against step 4, signed; **item 6 opened, with the predicted-a-failure and named-the-failure rates separate**; the dead-signal distribution shown alongside [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s **with instrumentation rule 2's three-occasion threshold stated**; the pooled mute answers; **and the count of contracts that named an unpublished-accuracy dependency against the count that promised a detection anyway.**

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has run production monitoring will produce a good detector and a fluent contract. Predicted failure: **the liveness property on their own new detector.** They will add liveness to the signals they inherited, because that is the lesson they came in with, and omit it on the detector they just built, because it is new and working. Countermeasure: step 4's clause names the detector explicitly, the hard specification requirement covers every signal in the design, and the phase 7 partner is asked to construct a drift the detector will not see — which surfaces the detector as an object with failure modes rather than as the solution. **Step 4's stopped-versus-sampled clause catches the same student a second time**, because a practised operator's liveness check is *is data arriving*, and a sampled evaluator that has hit its run cap is still arriving, less often.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is at step 2: a student who has never chosen a reference will use the first month of the series, because it is there, and will not record that as a choice. Countermeasures: the date-and-provenance-in-the-same-field requirement makes the choice visible in the artefact; the specification fails a reference with no date; and phase 4 point 1 states that the reference is a decision with a provenance before the step asks for one.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** If a group identifies A4 as a dead signal before the records are supplied — plausible, since [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 8 said exactly this and the cohort defended a liveness clause at [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) — the payload has failed and the retention has succeeded. That is a good outcome and it removes the phase's engine. Fallback: the group receives the reserved fifth case — **a signal that is alive, correct, and monitoring a component that was decommissioned five weeks into the period**, so that it reports healthy about something that no longer exists — and is asked which of [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s three detection failures it is. It is none: the signal is live, somebody is watching, and it is distinguishable from normal. It is a fourth failure the earlier day's list did not contain, and finding it is a better outcome than the payload. The reserve is not used generally because it requires the group to hold the deploy log as well as the series, which the phase does not have room for.

**Affective failure.** Two, and they are opposite.

The first is **public commitment failure**, described at §3.3 and countered by the decoupling, the anonymised cohort reporting and the phase 6 case.

The second is the more insidious: **relief in students whose commitment fired correctly.** A student whose alert worked will conclude that their detection design is sound, and the day's whole content is that it was tuned on nine weeks in which nobody was choosing. Countermeasure: point 7 is delivered to the whole room rather than to the students who failed, and the phase 8 mute answers are pooled — **a contract whose recipient has named a condition under which they would stop responding is not a working detection design regardless of what happened in the last nine weeks**, and that constraint is carried forward to [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md) as an instrument rather than left as a remark.

Seven items.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment judgement.** One specification item in ME302 with SG302. The module's judgement is at [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md).

Per specification rule 1 the checked elements are enumerated on the day and end with *nothing else is assessed*. Six hard requirements: a signal without a liveness property fails; a detector whose parameter has no stated cost in the other error fails; an alerting contract omitting what happens if the recipient does not act fails; **a contract that promises a detection rate fails**; a reference with no date fails; an intervention record with no entries fails unless the artefact shows the design could have caught an intervention-worthy error.

**The promise-no-detection-rate condition is new to this day and it is the platform turn's only addition to the specification.** It is defensible because it is a prohibition rather than a demand: it asks the student to write less, it is checkable by reading one sentence, and its honest answer is always available. It is also the module's most transferable single sentence, because the condition it describes — a control whose accuracy the supplier has not published — is the ordinary case rather than the exception.

**The commitment result is not assessed and the specification says so in writing**, on [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §7's argument, which [WP-070](wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §7 accepted unchanged and which this paper accepts unchanged again. What is assessed is that the student stated what happened.

**The cross-course join is a checked element.** Point 2 defers the statistical methods to ME302; step 3 requires the student to name the material used to establish the test's assumptions.

### Discharge of the two obligations S070 placed on this day

**This paragraph is the report [WP-070](wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §7 requires, and both obligations are discharged.**

**Obligation one — the alerting case in which the signal itself stopped.** [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s instrumentation block and [WP-070](wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §7 named this day's phase 3 as the response occasion for the phase 3 item 4 detection-commitment quality, specifying that *the alerting cases must include one in which the signal itself stopped, selected against whichever monitor failure mode this cohort omitted.* **The element exists as phase 3 case A4, and its construction is a function of the [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) phase 1 item 3 result rather than a fixed case**: where the cohort could not say how it would know a signal had stopped, A4 is the dead exporter; where the cohort's omission was the third detection failure, A4 is a signal that continued reporting a plausible constant. **The response does not end at the case.** Step 4 requires a liveness property on every signal in the student's design including the detector they build on the day, and **it is one of the day's six hard specification requirements** rather than a discussion point. It is not a mention; a case selection plus a fail condition is the strongest response form available to a non-assessment-bearing day. The obligation is discharged.

**Obligation two — the shipped changes' behaviour against each student's stated detection commitment.** [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s between-day protocol committed that *at [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md) the shipped changes' behaviour is reported against each student's detection commitment, and the time-to-detection of anything that went wrong is compared against the latency committed today.* **Both halves exist and they are three separate elements.** The behaviour is reported at phase 1, where each student's commitment is retrieved sealed and item 2 asks whether it fired before the record is opened; at phase 5 step 1, where the answer becomes a checked artefact element with the realised latency where it fired; and at phase 8, where the cohort result is stated in four categories — fired as committed, fired late, condition occurred and nothing fired, condition did not occur. **The latency comparison is item 3 against the [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) commitment against the realised time, reported signed**, and it is the day's most quoted result. The obligation is discharged, and it is discharged without assessing the outcome, which is the condition [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s own specification attached to it.

**Discharge of the obligation S077 placed on this day.** [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md) named this day's phase 5 as the response occasion for its pooled step 3 recording gaps, specifying that each alerting design name one recording change that would make an attack-versus-accident distinction possible on that component, or record that none would. **The element exists as step 5(a), it takes the student's own [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md) gap list as its input, it is unsupported, and it is a checked element that permits an explicit none** — the permission matters, because a student forced to invent a recording change would invent one and the measurement would be destroyed. Phase 3's task 1 supplies a second consumption of the same material at the cohort level. The obligation is discharged.

**Discharge of the obligation S074 placed on this day.** [S074](wiki/seminars/S074.md) named this day's phase 5 as the response occasion for its pooled step 6 undetected-shape list and its summed expected cost, specifying that each student's drift and alerting design carry a discrimination for the shape the cohort declined, **or record that it still does not and assign the cost an owner.** **The element exists as step 5(b) and it is a checked element.** The owner clause is what makes it a response rather than an acknowledgement: an undetected shape carried forward with a named cost-bearer is [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 8's form applied to a discrimination rather than to a signal, and without it the artefact would restate the same undetected list one quarter later, which is the outcome instrumentation rule 4 was written to prevent. The obligation is discharged. **It is placed inside step 5 rather than in a step of its own because it is structurally identical to [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s** — supply a discrimination or record its absence with an owner — and separating two instances of one demand would teach that they are two demands.

**A proposed remedy is introduced here and is recorded as proposed.** Step 7's second question — *what would make you mute this?* — is a new structure. Under instrumentation rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 it is **proposed** rather than validated, it is used at the day proposing it, and any reuse elsewhere is **contingent** on this day's reported outcome. The narrate-rather-than-approve form it sits inside is itself a contingent reuse from [S038](wiki/seminars/S038.md) via [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md), [S076](wiki/seminars/S076.md) and [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md), and a reported failure at any of those retracts all of them at once.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments, one inside the module and one outside.

- **The item 3 signed latency error** responds at [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md) phase 3 item 2, where the forensic account must establish the incident's detection latency and compare it against the detection commitment in force at the time, and at [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md) phase 5, where both readings are reported together. **That will be the third administration of the same latency comparison across [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md), this day and [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md), and instrumentation rule 2 applies at three**: the figures are reported side by side and their rates are not compared across occasions. [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md) phase 5 states the constraint to the cohort.
- **The step 7 mute answers** respond at [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md), whose safety case must not rest on a detection claim whose named recipient stated a condition under which they would mute it, unless that condition appears in the case. **This is a cross-day commitment on a day outside this module.**

**[WP-080](wiki/whitepapers/WP-080.md) §7 and [WP-087](wiki/whitepapers/WP-087.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

Programme instrumentation: the four-category commitment result; the signed latency error; the item 1 confident-date rate on series with no start; the item 4 against step 4 dead-signal error; **the item 6 frontier pair**; the pooled mute conditions; the phase 7 contract-versus-reader difference; **and the count of contracts naming an unpublished-accuracy dependency against the count promising a detection anyway.**

ABET and CS2023 contribution: PO2 and PO7. Dispositions: **reliability**, which this day measures directly and uniquely — a commitment made nine weeks ago against what happened, reported by the student themselves and not assessed; **integrity**, at step 1, where the honest answer for some students is that the condition occurred and nothing fired.

**What this day does not license anyone to conclude.** It does not license a claim that a student's component is monitored, and the commitment result licenses nothing about the student at all — it is a measurement of the programme's own [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) design as much as of anybody's promise. It licenses a claim that the student can state a reference with its provenance, choose a detector and say what its parameter costs, put liveness on their own signals, and write an operational commitment a rota member can act on. **Four statements about an artefact.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day's central instrument requires something to have gone wrong, and nothing may have.**

At its strongest: phase 1 items 2 and 3, step 1 and the entire phase 8 headline depend on there having been a condition for the commitments to detect. **A quiet nine weeks produces a cohort of students whose commitments did not fire because there was nothing to fire about**, and the four-category result collapses into one category. The signed latency comparison — the day's most quoted result and [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s named discharge — has no data. The day still runs, because the six-month series contain drift whether or not there were incidents, but the personal stake evaporates and what remains is an exercise on supplied cases.

The partial rebuttal is real. Drift is not an incident: a six-month series on a live service will contain population change, input change and at least one deployment that moved something, whether or not anyone was paged. The substrate's own record over nine weeks reliably contains the conditions point 3 describes even in a quarter with no incidents, and step 1's four categories include *condition occurred and nothing fired*, which is the informative case and is more likely in a quiet quarter rather than less.

**What is conceded is that the design cannot tell the two situations apart in advance and has no fallback that preserves the stake.** A quiet quarter and a well-detected quarter produce the same phase 8 slide. The available fix is to inject a condition — the substrate has a disturbance-window mechanism and could produce one — and **it is declined**, because an injected condition is announced, is bounded, and is drawn from the designer's distribution rather than from the world's, which is the exact substitution the [Substrate and Infrastructure](wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) withdrawal commitment refuses for this material: *a simulated incident produces a student who can perform incident response.* The design would rather have a weak day in a quiet quarter than a strong day built on an injection.

**Objection 2: the day teaches a decision about statistical methods to students who will not have the methods.**

At its strongest: point 2 says that a change detector's parameter is an error preference and that choosing it is a decision, not a default. **That is the right lesson and it is unusable without the methods.** A student who can say *this parameter trades false alarms against detection time* and cannot state what a sequential test assumes about independence will select a test, set a parameter, defend the choice in the terms this day supplied, and deploy a detector whose assumptions the production series violates. The day has given them the vocabulary of a defensible choice without the basis for one.

The deferral to ME302 is the right structural answer and the join is built: step 3 requires the student to name the material they used for the test's assumptions, which is the artefact element the cross-course deferral rule requires and which [WP-041](wiki/whitepapers/WP-041.md) §8 and [WP-044](wiki/whitepapers/WP-044.md) §8 both established the design gets wrong when it is not enforced. So the deferral is verifiable.

**Verifiable is not the same as sufficient**, and this is the concession. The join proves the student consulted the course; it does not prove the course reached the assumptions before this day needed them, and the seminar strand has no visibility into ME302's ordering. **The cheap fix is to require step 3 to state the assumption the chosen test makes that the student's own series most plausibly violates** — one clause, checkable, and it converts the join from an attendance record into a demonstration. **It is declined**, and the reason is weak: phase 5 is already carrying seven steps including two rule-6 elements and the step would need worked support the phase cannot afford. **That is a timing argument against a validity objection**, which [WP-064](wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 already identified as this design's characteristic bad answer, and it is recorded as the same shape.

**This objection is not fully answered.** The day teaches a choice, defers its basis, verifies the deferral, and does not verify that the basis arrived.

**Objection 3: the day's systems content has no concurrent course and is retrieved from one a quarter old.**

At its strongest: exporters, collection paths, deployment mechanics and rota structure are the substance of A4, of step 4, and of the phase 6 case. [Q8](wiki/quarters/Q8.md)'s four courses are IS302, SG302, ME302 and PE301. **There is no SY course**, so the day cannot use the cross-course deferral form for its systems material — the form requires a concurrent course to defer *to*. It retrieves from SY301 instead, one quarter behind, which is a weaker relationship in a specific way: a deferral is verifiable through an artefact join, and a retrieval is verifiable only by asking the student to recall.

This is the mirror image of the gap [M14](wiki/modules/M14.md) recorded for PE at [Q7](wiki/quarters/Q7.md), where [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) had to carry its governance content at seminar length because there was no concurrent PE course. **Two of nine quarters have a structural strand gap at exactly the point where a module needs it**, which is what a four-course constraint produces when seven strands must be covered, and this paper records the second instance so that it is legible as a pattern rather than as two independent scheduling accidents.

The rebuttal is that the exposure is smaller here than at [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md): this day does not *teach* systems material, it operates on a collection path the students have run for a quarter, and the artefact elements that touch it — liveness, dead-signal latency — are properties of a signal rather than of an infrastructure. That is true and it does not answer the objection, which is that the day has no mechanism to check the assumption. **The finding is recorded rather than fixed**, since the fix is a course-allocation change and the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s criterion records findings that require a design judgement.

**Objection 4: the day teaches students to instrument with tools whose accuracy nobody has published, and then congratulates itself for saying so.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the practical turn creates. The day's platform content is a monitoring stack that is sampled by design, capped by default, partly in preview, and accompanied by a security alert catalogue with **no published detection rate, no false-positive rate and no confidence scale anywhere.** The day's response is to add a specification clause requiring students to say so. **That converts an absence of evidence into a teachable moment**, which is comfortable, cheap, and not obviously distinguishable from making a virtue of a limitation the design cannot remove. A sceptical reader may reasonably say that a programme whose measurement strand spends two quarters teaching instrument characterisation has, at the point where it finally puts students in front of a production instrument, characterised nothing and required the students to write a disclaimer instead.

The rebuttal has two parts and the first is the stronger. **The day does characterise the instrument, on the axes where characterisation is possible.** Sample rate, run cap, retention, the guidance thresholds and their provenance, and the preview status of each capability are all observable, all read from the settings by the group at phase 3, and all convert directly into artefact elements. That is [S029](wiki/seminars/S029.md)'s discipline performed rather than described, and it is more than most engineers ever do to a dashboard. Second, **the accuracy axis is not characterisable by anyone in the room**, because it requires labelled ground truth over adversarial traffic on a live service, and constructing that is neither available nor honest.

**What is conceded is that the day cannot distinguish between two students it should distinguish between.** A student who writes *this rests on a control whose miss rate I cannot state* because they understood the argument, and a student who writes it because the specification told them to, produce identical artefacts. Every other element on this day is checkable against evidence and this one is checkable only against itself.

**The cheap fix exists and is declined.** It is to require the student to state **what they would have to observe to estimate the miss rate for their own component, and why they cannot** — one clause, unfakeable, and it would separate the two students immediately, because the second cannot produce the design and the first can. **It is declined for a scheduling reason and the paper records that this is the same bad answer [WP-064](wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 named and [WP-079](wiki/whitepapers/WP-079.md) §8 objection 2 has already committed once in this same section.** Phase 5 carries seven steps, two rule-6 elements and a proposed remedy, and the design will not put a ninth demand into ninety minutes. Committing the same class of error twice in one paper is worse than committing it once, and it is recorded here rather than argued away.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At item 2, students will over-report that their [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) commitment fired, and the largest single category at step 1 will be *condition occurred and nothing fired*. *Instrument:* item 2 against the alert history, and step 1's four categories. **The second half is the claim.** If the largest category is *condition did not occur*, the quarter was quiet and §8 objection 1's failure case has occurred, which the design must report as such rather than as a result about detection.

**Prediction 2.** Item 3's signed latency errors will run in one direction: students will predict detection faster than they committed, and both will be faster than realised. *Instrument:* the three-way comparison reported signed, per student. **The sign is the claim, not the magnitude**, and a symmetric distribution would mean latency estimation is noisy rather than optimistic — which is a different defect with a different remedy and would move the response from point 8 to step 2's reference discipline.

**Prediction 3.** At item 1, more than two thirds of students will give a confident start date for at least one of the series that has no start. *Instrument:* item 1 dates coded against which series have an identifiable onset. **If most students decline to give a date for the drift series, point 1 is not the difficulty this design treats it as** and the phase 2 bridge is answering a question the room does not have.

**Prediction 4.** At step 7, a majority of rota members will name a mute condition that the student's own design makes likely — a false-alarm rate the design permits, or an action the recipient cannot take from home. *Instrument:* mute answers coded against the student's own stated false-alarm rate and the contract's third part. **If mute conditions are unrelated to the design, the mute question is measuring the rota's disposition rather than the artefact**, the proposed remedy is retracted under rule 5, and the [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md) commitment built on it is withdrawn.

**Prediction 5.** At item 6, a large majority will predict the agent's monitoring configuration will be wrong and fewer than a fifth will name the absent liveness property; and at step 4, a majority of first-draft designs will carry liveness on inherited signals and not on the detector built that morning. *Instrument:* item 6 responses coded for generic-versus-specific failure identification, and step 4 entries coded by which signals carry liveness before the phase 7 challenge. **The two rates should move together and the claim is that they will.** If students name the agent's omission and then commit it themselves, the day has taught a fact about a tool and not a property of monitoring design, and point 4 should be delivered against the cohort's own step 4 drafts rather than against A4.
