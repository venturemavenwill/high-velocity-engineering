## In plain terms

This is the module's assessment and its test is not the specification. It is the build.

A specification is a claim about a reader: that somebody who was not present, and who cannot ask you anything, could act on it. **That claim can only be checked by finding such a reader and letting them act.** Every other check — reviewing it, walking through it, presenting it — is performed by somebody who has your explanations available, and the explanations are the thing under test.

So the assessment hands the packet to two readers who are given nothing else. **If they ask a question, the question is recorded and not answered**, because each question marks a place where the specification required the author to be present.

One of the readers is a generative instrument and one is a person, and they fail differently. **The instrument is literal.** It will not fill a gap out of courtesy, will not decide you must have meant something sensible, and will build exactly what your words permit — which is the property the assessment wants. **The person is slower and supplies the thing the instrument cannot:** they will tell you which parts felt ambiguous rather than silently choosing an interpretation.

**The assessment marks the change the author made, not the build.** A build that went badly and produced a specification revision is the successful case. A build that went well and produced no revision has established only that the specification was clear enough for one reader on one occasion.

## Abstract

This paper defends assessing a requirement specification by an unassisted build performed by two readers — one generative, one human — who receive the specification and nothing else, whose questions are recorded and not answered, and whose divergences from authorial intent are treated as findings about the document. It defends marking seven published items and nothing else, including that the unverifiable list be non-empty or its emptiness defended, and that the build record name something the builder got wrong and something the author changed. The paper concedes that the scheme cannot distinguish a specification that is complete from one whose reader happened to guess well, that the generative reader's behaviour is neither stable nor characterised in any published measure, and that the practice corpus from which the module's discipline derives records no engagement in which a specification was found defective at build time, because it records no engagement that went badly.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A specification is written by somebody who knows what it means. That is the defect, it is unavoidable, and no amount of care removes it.

**The author's knowledge is not in the document and cannot be inspected from inside it.** Reading your own specification, you supply the missing constraint automatically, exactly as you supply the missing word in a familiar sentence. Reading it with a colleague, they supply it too, because they were in the same meetings. The gap is invisible to everybody who could conveniently be asked.

The conventional remedy is review, and review does not work for this. **A reviewer's job is to find fault with what is written, and the defect here is what is not.** A reviewer who cannot see the absence has no procedure for detecting it, and a reviewer who can see it usually knows the answer and mentally inserts it before noticing they have done so.

The assessment's response is to replace review with use. Not a discussion of the specification — **an attempt to act on it, by somebody with no access to the author**, and a record of what they did.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only, and the whole module is load-bearing.** The packet is assembled from [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md), [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md), [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md) and [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md), and a candidate missing any of them submits a packet with a hole in a named place.

The prior it works against is that a specification is finished when it is complete. **In this assessment it is finished when a stranger's build has changed it**, which reverses the direction of the last step most candidates expect to take.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Assembly and evaluation rather than intrinsic. The contents were produced in earlier sessions. The load is in accepting an unwelcome reading of your own document without explaining it away, which is affective and is the reason the assessment specifies how the build record must be written.

**3.2 Why two readers rather than one.** They detect different defects. The instrument detects what the words permit; the person detects what the words fail to determine. **A specification can be permissive and determinate, or restrictive and ambiguous, and one reader will miss whichever failure it is not sensitive to.**

**3.3 Why questions are recorded and not answered.** A question answered becomes context the reader had and the document did not. **The whole assessment depends on the readers having only the document**, and answering one question destroys the instrument for the rest of the build.

**3.4 Why the change rather than the build is marked.** The build's quality depends on the builder, on the difficulty of the problem, and on how much of the system a reader could plausibly produce in the time available. The revision depends only on whether the author read the result honestly. **The second is the thing the assessment can attribute to the candidate**, and it is also the behaviour the design wants.

**3.5 Why the defence movement asks for a concession and a refusal.** A specification is a set of positions, and the interest behind a position is what is actually being defended. Requiring the candidate to say what they would concede and what they would refuse, and to say what would have to be true for the refusal to change, tests whether they can distinguish the two. **The additional requirement — that the other party could take the answer back and justify it to people who were not in the room — is taken from the negotiation literature and carries that source's limit: it establishes no effect.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**Seven specification items, published in [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) and repeated in §7 here.** Each checked present and adequate, or not.

**The readers receive the packet and nothing else, and this is enforced rather than encouraged.** The candidate is not present during the builds. The instruction given to each reader is identical and is a single sentence.

**Opening item 2 is the assessment's real question** — *what in this specification is not written down and exists only in your head?* — and it is asked before the build so that the candidate's own answer can be compared against what the build actually exposed. **The comparison is not marked**, because a candidate who correctly predicts their own gap and does not write it down has demonstrated something the design does not want to reward.

**The build record's required content is a divergence and a change.** Not a summary, not an assessment of the builder. A named thing the builder got wrong, and a named thing the author changed because of it. **A record naming a divergence and no change is a record of an argument the author won against an instrument**, which is not the exercise.

**The specification refuses to assess the build's success**, and this is stated in the session so that candidates do not optimise for it. A candidate who selects an easy fragment of the system so that the build will succeed has satisfied nothing.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The builder blamed.** The likeliest failure. The build goes badly, and the record explains why the builder was inadequate. *Countermeasure:* the specification requires a change, and the standard states plainly that an instrument producing an unwelcome reading has been used correctly.

**The assisted build.** The candidate cannot resist supplying context, in the prompt or in the room. *Countermeasure:* partial. The prompt record is required for the generative reader and the human reader is asked to note any conversation. **A candidate determined to assist can still do so and the design has no reliable detection.**

**The safe fragment.** The candidate hands over the part of the specification least likely to be misread. *Countermeasure:* opening item 1 requires them to name the requirement they are least willing to have misread, submitted before the build, which raises the cost of quietly omitting it.

**The empty unverifiable list.** *Countermeasure:* the specification permits emptiness with a defence, which converts an omission into a claim somebody can disagree with.

**The undisputed glossary.** *Countermeasure:* none beyond [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md)'s. The item requires a date and a role, which raises the cost of fabrication and does not prevent it. **This is the weakest item in the specification and this paper says so rather than implying otherwise.**

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**Seven specification items, published in [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) and repeated here.** Each checked present and adequate, or not.

1. Every requirement names a measurement and a verification method, both executable by somebody who is not the author. An instrument that does not exist yet is acceptable when named as work with an owner.
2. No requirement names a technology unless the technology is an imposed constraint, and where it is, the constraint's owner is named.
3. Every threshold has a stated origin; *borrowed from a catalogue that carries no source* is admissible when declared.
4. The unverifiable list is present and not empty, or its emptiness is defended in a sentence.
5. At least one decision record is present whose context does not reveal which way the decision went.
6. The glossary records at least one word the customer and the team meant differently, says which meaning was adopted, and names who agreed.
7. The build record is present and names something the builder got wrong and something the author changed because of it.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Not the number of requirements, not whether the design implied by them is any good, not whether the customer agreed, not the presentation of the packet, not whether the build worked, and not whether the assessor would have written the same requirements.

**Entrustment evidence generated: none.** This is a specification check.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the assessment cannot distinguish a complete specification from a lucky reader.**

At its strongest: a build that succeeds tells you one reader, on one occasion, with one interpretation, produced something acceptable. It does not establish that the specification determines the outcome — a reader with adjacent domain experience may reconstruct the missing constraint correctly and the document gets the credit. **Symmetrically, a build that fails may reflect a reader's limitation rather than the document's**, and the scheme's response to that — mark the change, not the build — means a candidate with a perfectly good specification and an incompetent reader must invent a revision to pass item 7.

**Conceded, and the second half is the sharper part.** The rebuttal is partial: two readers of different kinds reduce the chance that a single reader's competence dominates, and item 7 is satisfiable by a small change, which is proportionate to a small finding. **The declined fix is to supply a standard reader — a fixed instrument, a fixed prompt, a fixed environment — so that builds are comparable across candidates.** It is declined because a fixed reader becomes a specification the candidates write for, and writing for a known reader is precisely the failure the assessment exists to detect. **It is declined at the cost of comparability, and comparability is a real thing to lose in an assessment.**

**Objection 2: the module's central discipline has no precedent in the practice it claims to teach.**

At its strongest: this assessment's whole design assumes that deliberately generating a bad build and learning from it is a professional move. **Nothing in the practice corpus behind this programme does anything of the sort.** That corpus reports no engagement in which a specification was found defective at build time, no engagement that went badly at all, and its single failure narrative is a synthetic composite that is fictional by its own admission. A programme that teaches deliberate failure-seeking, on the authority of a body of material in which nothing ever fails, is inventing a practice and attributing it.

**Conceded without qualification.** The assessment is design reasoning and the paper says so in its closing section. The rebuttal is only that the alternative — assessing a specification by review, in a programme whose source corpus has never recorded a specification failing — would inherit the corpus's blindness rather than correcting it. **The declined fix is to assess against the playbook's own review workflow instead**, which is a real, published, well-specified process. It is declined because that workflow reviews design documents among people who share the engagement's context, which is the reader the assessment is trying to avoid.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** The generative reader will diverge from authorial intent more often than the human reader, and the human reader will raise more questions. *Instrument:* divergence counts and question counts per reader, recorded per candidate.

**Prediction 2.** Most candidates' answers to opening item 2 will not match the gap the build actually exposed. *Instrument:* the submitted item 2 answer against the build record, coded *matched* / *partially matched* / *unmatched* by a second reader.

**Prediction 3.** Item 7 will be the most frequently failed specification item, and item 1 the least. *Instrument:* per-item pass rates, reported without aggregation.

**Prediction 4.** Build records naming a divergence and no change will be more common than records naming neither. *Instrument:* the records, coded *divergence and change* / *divergence only* / *neither*. **If records naming neither dominate, the assessment is being satisfied by builds that were never genuinely unassisted, and the enforcement of reader isolation has failed.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a specification's only real test is an attempt to act on it by somebody who cannot ask you anything; that a question asked is a gap found; and that the change is the finding, not the build.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The artefact specifications come from a publication read on 2026-07-30 that offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices, states no measured compliance, and in its own worked examples does not always follow its own specifications. The assessment design is this repository's.

**The concession worth remembering.** This assessment teaches a failure-seeking discipline on the authority of a corpus in which nothing ever fails. That is an argument dressed as a practice, and it should be held as one.
