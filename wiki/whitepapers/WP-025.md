## In plain terms

This is the module's assessment and its test is not the specification. It is the build.

A specification is a claim about a reader: that somebody who was not present, and who cannot ask you anything, could act on it. **That claim can only be checked by finding such a reader and letting them act.** Every other check — reviewing it, walking through it, presenting it — is performed by somebody who has your explanations available, and the explanations are the thing under test.

So the assessment hands the packet to two readers who are given nothing else. **If they ask a question, the question is recorded and not answered**, because each question marks a place where the specification required the author to be present.

One of the readers is a generative instrument and one is a person, and they fail differently. **The instrument is literal.** It will not fill a gap out of courtesy, will not decide you must have meant something sensible, and will build exactly what your words permit — which is the property the assessment wants. **The person is slower and supplies the thing the instrument cannot:** they will tell you which parts felt ambiguous rather than silently choosing an interpretation.

**The assessment marks the change the author made, not the build.** A build that went badly and produced a specification revision is the successful case. A build that went well and produced no revision has established only that the specification was clear enough for one reader on one occasion.

## Abstract

This paper defends assessing a requirement specification by an unassisted build performed by two readers — one generative, one human — who receive the specification and nothing else, whose questions are recorded and not answered, and whose divergences from authorial intent are treated as findings about the document. It defends marking seven published items and nothing else, including that the unverifiable list be non-empty or its emptiness defended, and that the build record name something the builder got wrong and something the author changed. The paper concedes that the scheme cannot distinguish a specification that is complete from one whose reader happened to guess well, that the generative reader's behaviour is neither stable nor characterised in any published measure, and that the practice corpus from which the module's discipline derives records no engagement in which a specification was found defective at build time, because it records no engagement that went badly.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A specification is written by somebody who knows what it means. That is the defect, it is unavoidable, and no amount of care removes it.

**The author's knowledge is not in the document and cannot be inspected from inside it.** Reading your own specification, you supply the missing constraint automatically, exactly as you supply the missing word in a familiar sentence. Reading it with a colleague, they supply it too, because they were in the same meetings. The gap is invisible to everybody who could conveniently be asked.

The conventional remedy is review, and review does not work for this. **A reviewer's job is to find fault with what is written, and the defect here is what is not.** A reviewer who cannot see the absence has no procedure for detecting it, and a reviewer who can see it usually knows the answer and mentally inserts it before noticing they have done so.

The assessment's response is to replace review with use. Not a discussion of the specification — **an attempt to act on it, by somebody with no access to the author**, and a record of what they did.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only, and the whole module is load-bearing.** The packet is assembled from [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md), [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md), [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md) and [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md), and a candidate missing any of them submits a packet with a hole in a named place.

The prior it works against is that a specification is finished when it is complete. **In this assessment it is finished when a stranger's build has changed it**, which reverses the direction of the last step most candidates expect to take.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Assembly and evaluation rather than intrinsic. The contents were produced in earlier sessions. The load is in accepting an unwelcome reading of your own document without explaining it away, which is affective and is the reason the assessment specifies how the build record must be written.

**3.2 Why two readers rather than one.** They detect different defects. The instrument detects what the words permit; the person detects what the words fail to determine. **A specification can be permissive and determinate, or restrictive and ambiguous, and one reader will miss whichever failure it is not sensitive to.**

**3.3 Why questions are recorded and not answered.** A question answered becomes context the reader had and the document did not. **The whole assessment depends on the readers having only the document**, and answering one question destroys the instrument for the rest of the build.

There is a stronger reason than information leakage and it has a warrant. Studies of assessors judging complex written evidence, working aloud, find a cyclical process of acquiring, organising and integrating evidence, followed by a return to the remaining material — at which point **"assessors were inclined to stick to their initial judgments even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence"**, with final judgements more elaborate than preliminary ones but not substantially different ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). **A single answer therefore does not merely add information; it fixes an interpretation that the rest of the reading will elaborate rather than test.**

The same literature supports §3.2 from a direction the design did not originally have. Assessors reaching the same outcome frequently reach it by different reasoning, and **the divergence is invisible in the outcome and visible only in what each of them says.** Two readers who both produce something acceptable, for incompatible reasons, have told the author more about the document than one reader who succeeded.

**Its limits.** Clinical-education research using think-aloud protocols on mock portfolios, in a single institution and one portfolio type; the method cannot capture fast or automatic processing and carries a risk that participants confabulate explanations for the researcher. **No magnitude from it is reproduced here**, and its application to specification readers is this paper's inference rather than the source's claim.

**3.4 Why the change rather than the build is marked.** The build's quality depends on the builder, on the difficulty of the problem, and on how much of the system a reader could plausibly produce in the time available. The revision depends only on whether the author read the result honestly. **The second is the thing the assessment can attribute to the candidate**, and it is also the behaviour the design wants.

That is also the entrustment literature's own boundary. The unit of assessment is a bounded activity with a beginning and an end that a supervisor could hand over whole ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). *Producing a specification a stranger can build from, and revising it on what they produced* satisfies that test. *Producing a good system* does not: it is an outcome, its correctness depends on facts the assessment cannot hold constant, and marking it would assess the candidate's agreement with the assessor.

Two further things from that source shape the occasion. The description template's title must carry no adjectives, because an adjective smuggles a quality standard into the name of the activity — quality standards belong in the specification and its stated limitations, which is why §7's items are properties rather than gradings. And the oral component is an **entrustment-based discussion**, which "should probe the student's deep understanding of what was done, but should include 'what-if' questions". **Retrospective explanation is evidence about the case the candidate met; counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases they did not.**

**Its limits.** A clinical model, in a primer by the concept's originator; it is guidance rather than evaluation and reports adoption rather than effectiveness. Medicine has patients, legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint. **A course must construct its stakes artificially, and the construction is the weakest part of the transfer.**

**3.5 Why the defence movement asks for a concession and a refusal.** A specification is a set of positions, and the interest behind a position is what is actually being defended. Requiring the candidate to say what they would concede and what they would refuse, and to say what would have to be true for the refusal to change, tests whether they can distinguish the two. **The additional requirement — that the other party could take the answer back and justify it to people who were not in the room — is taken from the negotiation literature and carries that source's limit: it establishes no effect.**

**3.6 Why the specification must name a build path.**

A specification that names no construction path is not neutral. It assumes one, and the assumption is discovered by whoever implements first.

The published guidance for this platform makes the fork explicit and — importantly — makes it a **hosting** distinction rather than a capability one ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). Prompt agents use declarative configuration and are managed with no agent compute; hosted agents run your containerised code on managed container compute. **The line between them is whether you are running code, and therefore whether you are paying for and operating compute** — which decides who owns the runtime, what it costs to run, what can change without a deployment, and who is on call. Those are requirements facts.

Three further published facts convert the fork into consequences a specification must accept. **A build choice made at the start binds distribution at the end**, because publication to the commercial store through the toolkit is available only for certain build routes — so a silent specification has made a distribution decision without noticing. **Prototyping topology and production topology are different things**: the rapid setup is documented for speed and ease of access and "lacks network isolation", while the controlled setup provides fine-grained control over data, security and networking, and the move between them is a deliberate act. And **the options are not mutually exclusive**: the guidance says so, and gives the example of one runtime exposing tools that agents built elsewhere consume, which means a build-path statement is about a *component* rather than a system.

**Its limits, and they bound §7.** First-party product documentation with a selection-guidance purpose; **its authority is definitional and it offers no adoption data, no failure-rate data and no cost comparison for any path**, so its recommendations are a reasoned framework and not validated findings. Read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches, so page structure is reconstructed. Highly perishable: product names and feature boundaries are actively changing. And it publishes a scale envelope for only one of the tools it compares — **the absence of one for the others is not evidence that they have none.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**Eight specification items, published in [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) and repeated in §7 here.** Each checked present and adequate, or not.

**The readers receive the packet and nothing else, and this is enforced rather than encouraged.** The candidate is not present during the builds. The instruction given to each reader is identical and is a single sentence.

**Opening item 2 is the assessment's real question** — *what in this specification is not written down and exists only in your head?* — and it is asked before the build so that the candidate's own answer can be compared against what the build actually exposed. **The comparison is not marked**, because a candidate who correctly predicts their own gap and does not write it down has demonstrated something the design does not want to reward.

**The build-path statement is written before either reader sees the packet, and the ordering is the point.** Written afterwards, it would record what the readers assumed rather than what the author decided, and a specification whose construction path was set by its first implementer is exactly the artefact item 8 exists to prevent.

**The build record's required content is a divergence and a change.** Not a summary, not an assessment of the builder. A named thing the builder got wrong, and a named thing the author changed because of it. **A record naming a divergence and no change is a record of an argument the author won against an instrument**, which is not the exercise.

**The specification refuses to assess the build's success**, and this is stated in the session so that candidates do not optimise for it. A candidate who selects an easy fragment of the system so that the build will succeed has satisfied nothing. **It also refuses to assess which build path was chosen**, for the same reason: item 8 marks whether the choice was made and its cost accepted, never whether it was the right choice, which depends on facts the assessment cannot hold constant.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The builder blamed.** The likeliest failure. The build goes badly, and the record explains why the builder was inadequate. *Countermeasure:* the specification requires a change, and the standard states plainly that an instrument producing an unwelcome reading has been used correctly.

**The assisted build.** The candidate cannot resist supplying context, in the prompt or in the room. *Countermeasure:* partial. The prompt record is required for the generative reader and the human reader is asked to note any conversation. **A candidate determined to assist can still do so and the design has no reliable detection.**

**The safe fragment.** The candidate hands over the part of the specification least likely to be misread. *Countermeasure:* opening item 1 requires them to name the requirement they are least willing to have misread, submitted before the build, which raises the cost of quietly omitting it.

**The empty unverifiable list.** *Countermeasure:* the specification permits emptiness with a defence, which converts an omission into a claim somebody can disagree with.

**The undisputed glossary.** *Countermeasure:* none beyond [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md)'s. The item requires a date and a role, which raises the cost of fabrication and does not prevent it. **This is the weakest item in the specification and this paper says so rather than implying otherwise.**

**The inherited build path.** The candidate names a path because it is the one their team already uses, and writes an accepted consequence that would be true of any path. *Countermeasure:* item 8 requires a named role who agreed, and exercise 3 requires the candidate to write the statement that would have followed from the opposite choice. **Neither prevents inheritance; both make it visible as a pair of documents rather than a silence.** Prediction 6 in §9 predicts this countermeasure failing.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**Eight specification items, published in [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) and repeated here.** Each checked present and adequate, or not.

1. Every requirement names a measurement and a verification method, both executable by somebody who is not the author. An instrument that does not exist yet is acceptable when named as work with an owner.
2. No requirement names a technology unless the technology is an imposed constraint, and where it is, the constraint's owner is named.
3. Every threshold has a stated origin; *borrowed from a catalogue that carries no source* is admissible when declared.
4. The unverifiable list is present and not empty, or its emptiness is defended in a sentence.
5. At least one decision record is present whose context does not reveal which way the decision went.
6. The glossary records at least one word the customer and the team meant differently, says which meaning was adopted, and names who agreed.
7. The build record is present and names something the builder got wrong and something the author changed because of it.
8. The build-path statement is present, names the path, names one accepted consequence, and names a role inside the customer's organisation who agreed to it — or states that the path is deliberately open and says what that costs.

The checks are pass or fail per item, resubmittable, and recorded as a written reason rather than as a mark. **That form is deliberate**: the assessment literature this programme's architecture rests on finds that narrative is what carries a judgement and that scores are used only to confirm impressions already formed from the prose ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). A number would record the conclusion and discard the information.

The oral component is an entrustment-based discussion and **must include what-if questions**, on the reasoning in §3.4. It is not separately marked; it is the means by which items 5, 6 and 8 are probed.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Not the number of requirements, not whether the design implied by them is any good, not whether the customer agreed, not the presentation of the packet, not whether the build worked, not which build path was chosen, and not whether the assessor would have written the same requirements.

**Entrustment evidence generated: none.** This is a specification check. The activity it checks is a plausible entrustable unit — bounded, with a beginning and an end, handed over whole — but a single occasion judged by one assessor is an ad-hoc judgement rather than a summative one by that literature's own standard, which requires multiple observers and multiple observations. **The programme records it as a specification check rather than promoting it, and that is a deliberate under-claim.**

The eight items above are the closed list. The build's success, the correctness of the design, the customer's agreement, the presentation of the packet, the identity of the chosen build path, and the assessor's own preferences are all outside it.

**Nothing else is assessed.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the assessment cannot distinguish a complete specification from a lucky reader.**

At its strongest: a build that succeeds tells you one reader, on one occasion, with one interpretation, produced something acceptable. It does not establish that the specification determines the outcome — a reader with adjacent domain experience may reconstruct the missing constraint correctly and the document gets the credit. **Symmetrically, a build that fails may reflect a reader's limitation rather than the document's**, and the scheme's response to that — mark the change, not the build — means a candidate with a perfectly good specification and an incompetent reader must invent a revision to pass item 7.

**Conceded, and the second half is the sharper part.** The rebuttal is partial: two readers of different kinds reduce the chance that a single reader's competence dominates, and item 7 is satisfiable by a small change, which is proportionate to a small finding. **The declined fix is to supply a standard reader — a fixed instrument, a fixed prompt, a fixed environment — so that builds are comparable across candidates.** It is declined because a fixed reader becomes a specification the candidates write for, and writing for a known reader is precisely the failure the assessment exists to detect. **It is declined at the cost of comparability, and comparability is a real thing to lose in an assessment.**

**Objection 2: the module's central discipline has no precedent in the practice it claims to teach.**

At its strongest: this assessment's whole design assumes that deliberately generating a bad build and learning from it is a professional move. **Nothing in the practice corpus behind this programme does anything of the sort.** That corpus reports no engagement in which a specification was found defective at build time, no engagement that went badly at all, and its single failure narrative is a synthetic composite that is fictional by its own admission. A programme that teaches deliberate failure-seeking, on the authority of a body of material in which nothing ever fails, is inventing a practice and attributing it.

**Conceded without qualification.** The assessment is design reasoning and the paper says so in its closing section. The rebuttal is only that the alternative — assessing a specification by review, in a programme whose source corpus has never recorded a specification failing — would inherit the corpus's blindness rather than correcting it. **The declined fix is to assess against the playbook's own review workflow instead**, which is a real, published, well-specified process. It is declined because that workflow reviews design documents among people who share the engagement's context, which is the reader the assessment is trying to avoid.

**Objection 3: requiring a build path turns a requirements assessment into a design assessment, one stage early.**

At its strongest: the whole module has insisted that a requirement naming a technology is a design smuggled in as an obligation, and item 2 checks exactly that. **Item 8 then requires the candidate to name a construction path.** A reader could be forgiven for concluding that the module forbids technology in the requirements and mandates it in the packet, and a candidate under time pressure will resolve the apparent contradiction in the easy direction — by letting the named path leak back into the requirement sentences, which is the failure item 2 exists to catch. The stage question is *what can be built, against what that cannot move*; **naming how it will be built is the next stage's work, and doing it here risks fixing an architecture before the constraints are registered.**

**Partially conceded, and the tension is real rather than apparent.** Three defences. The build-path statement is a separate artefact from the requirement set and is assessed separately, so a path named there and absent from the requirements satisfies both items — and a path that leaks into a requirement sentence fails item 2, which is the discrimination the pair is designed to produce. The item marks the *acceptance of a consequence* rather than the choice, so it is a statement about cost and ownership rather than about architecture. And a specification that names no path has not avoided the decision; it has delegated it to whoever implements first, at which point the deployment topology and the distribution route are fixed by somebody who never saw the requirements. **The declined fix was to defer the build path to [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md), where the design is assessed** — which is where it conventionally belongs. It is declined because the consequence that matters most, who owns and pays for the runtime, is a fact about the customer's organisation rather than about the software, and it must be agreed by somebody on their side while the requirements are still open. **The cost of that decision is that this module now contains a decision that is arguably the next module's**, and prediction 7 is the only thing that would detect the leakage it risks.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** The generative reader will diverge from authorial intent more often than the human reader, and the human reader will raise more questions. *Instrument:* divergence counts and question counts per reader, recorded per candidate.

**Prediction 2.** Most candidates' answers to opening item 2 will not match the gap the build actually exposed. *Instrument:* the submitted item 2 answer against the build record, coded *matched* / *partially matched* / *unmatched* by a second reader.

**Prediction 3.** Item 7 will be the most frequently failed specification item, and item 1 the least. *Instrument:* per-item pass rates, reported without aggregation.

**Prediction 4.** Build records naming a divergence and no change will be more common than records naming neither. *Instrument:* the records, coded *divergence and change* / *divergence only* / *neither*. **If records naming neither dominate, the assessment is being satisfied by builds that were never genuinely unassisted, and the enforcement of reader isolation has failed.**

**Prediction 5.** Candidates will nominate a different sentence as their packet's load-bearing one than the instrument does, and the instrument's nomination will more often be a short clause about scope or ordering. *Instrument:* the two nominations from exercise 2, compared per candidate and coded by clause type.

**Prediction 6.** A majority of build-path statements will name the path the candidate's team already uses, and the accepted consequence will be one that would have been true of either path. *Instrument:* the statement and its exercise-3 counterfactual, coded *path-specific consequence* / *generic consequence* by a second reader. **This predicts the failure of §5's countermeasure for the inherited build path.**

**Prediction 7.** Packets whose build-path statement names a specific product will fail item 2 more often than packets whose statement names a hosting property. *Instrument:* item 2 outcomes cross-tabulated against the build-path statement's phrasing. **This is the direct test of §8's third objection**, and a positive result would mean item 8 is contaminating item 2 and the build path should move to [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md).

**Prediction 8.** Candidates asked what-if questions in the defence will more often name a reversing condition for their refusal than candidates asked only to explain what they did. *Instrument:* the defence records across two assessor briefings, coded for the presence of a reversing condition. **If there is no difference, the entrustment-based discussion's counterfactual requirement is ceremony and the defence can be shortened.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a specification's only real test is an attempt to act on it by somebody who cannot ask you anything; that a question asked is a gap found; that the change is the finding, not the build; and that a specification naming no build path has delegated the decision to whoever implements first.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The artefact specifications come from a publication read on 2026-07-30 that offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices, states no measured compliance, and in its own worked examples does not always follow its own specifications. The anchoring result behind the no-answers rule is clinical-education research, and **the transfer to specification readers is this repository's.** The entrustment frame is a clinical model reporting adoption rather than effectiveness, and a course must construct its stakes artificially. **The build-path guidance is definitional and unvalidated: it publishes no adoption data, no failure-rate data and no cost comparison for any path.** The assessment design is this repository's.

**The concession worth remembering.** This assessment teaches a failure-seeking discipline on the authority of a corpus in which nothing ever fails. That is an argument dressed as a practice, and it should be held as one. And it now asks a requirements module to name a construction path, which is arguably the next module's work — §8's third objection is where that is argued, and prediction 7 is the only thing that would show it was a mistake.

**The silences that matter.** **The build-path guidance publishes no adoption data, no failure-rate data and no cost comparison for any of the paths it compares**, so its selection framework is reasoned and not validated — which means item 8 can ask a candidate to accept a consequence and cannot tell them what the consequence typically costs. It publishes a scale envelope for only one of the tools it compares, and **that absence is not evidence that the others have none**; a candidate who infers a limit from silence has done the thing [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) told them not to. It says nothing about how a build path should be recorded, by whom it should be agreed, or what happens when the decision is revisited after work has started — **the entire content of item 8 sits in a gap the documentation does not acknowledge exists.** And the agent-mode documentation publishes no measure of how often the self-correction loop converges, or how often the instrument reports completion on work that is wrong; **the second silence bears directly on this assessment**, because a build reported as finished and quietly wrong produces a divergence the candidate may not detect, and no specification item would catch it.
