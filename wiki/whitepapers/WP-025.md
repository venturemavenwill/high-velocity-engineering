# WP-025 — Approximation, Heuristics and Stated Guarantees

**Seminar day:** [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) · **Module:** [M05](/wiki/modules/M05.md) · **Quarter:** [Q3](/wiki/quarters/Q3.md)
**Strands:** CF · MS · PE · **Complexity class:** 2
**Platform anchor:** Azure AI Search recall against an exhaustive ground truth · Foundry `document_retrieval` and the LLM-judged `retrieval` evaluator · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends making checkability rather than performance the criterion by which an approximation is selected, and defends constructing the day's contrasting-cases phase so that the best-performing option is the one students should reject. The first argument is that a guarantee the caller cannot verify is a claim about the engineer rather than about the system, that such claims are the normal form in which heuristics are actually shipped, and that the substitution of trust for verification is the mechanism by which quality failures become invisible to the people who bear them. The second argument is that a criterion revealed after a ranking has been committed produces a re-ranking whose reasons the students must articulate, which is the only construction that makes performance's insufficiency a finding rather than an assertion. The paper additionally defends running the day's main task as a continuation of the previous day's artefact; and defends adding a fifth approach whose evidence is produced by an instrument with no published agreement with human judgement, on the ground that distinguishing *no measurement* from *an unvalidated measurement* is the distinction the entire measurement strand rests on and is cheapest to install here. It concedes that the checkability criterion is stated more absolutely than the design can defend and that the repair is deliberately still withheld; that the day's terminal integration combines two disciplines whose interaction it has not tested; and that requiring the word *uncalibrated* in an assessed artefact converts a disposition into a checkable string.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

S024 taught students to say that something cannot be done. The immediate professional consequence is that they must say what will be done instead, and the module's four preceding days have supplied everything needed to construct an alternative except the criterion for choosing one.

The natural criterion is performance, and it is wrong. Not wrong in the sense of unimportant — a heuristic that performs badly is useless — but wrong in the sense of insufficient, and insufficient in a way that is invisible from inside the engineering team. A heuristic that performs best on the instances you have, with no proof and no measurement protocol, gives a caller nothing they can check. They must take the engineer's word for it. That is a workable arrangement while the engineer is present, the instances remain representative, and nothing changes; it fails silently when any of those stops holding, and the failure surfaces as degraded results that nobody attributes to the heuristic.

The day's target is therefore the substitution: **checkability, not performance, decides what you ship.** Phase 3 is built to produce this as a finding. Five approaches, ranked on the supplied queries, and approach E — no proof, no measurement, best performance — usually wins. Then the caller's requirement is revealed: they must be able to tell their own customer what the system promises. E becomes unshippable, and the reason has nothing to do with how well it works.

The day's second problem is what counts as a promise. Students who accept the checkability requirement will produce promises that sound checkable and are not — "usually fast," "close to optimal," "works well in practice." Phase 1 establishes that this is the normal register of real documentation by supplying three genuine descriptions, none of which makes a guarantee, and asking students which ones do. Most believe at least one does.

**The day's third problem is new with the platform anchor and the design considers it the most important thing this revision adds anywhere in the module.**

Until now, the alternative to *no evidence* has been *evidence*. That dichotomy is comfortable, it is what the module's four preceding days have implicitly taught, and it is false in every system these students will build. Foundry offers two evaluators for the same construct. One computes its metrics arithmetically against human relevance labels and requires no model at all. The other asks a language model to judge, returns a score from one to five, applies a default pass threshold of three, and attaches a written reason — and **no agreement between it, or any other evaluator in the stack, and human judgement is published anywhere.**

The second is not *no evidence*. It has a number, a threshold, a pass label and an explanation; it has every visible feature of a measurement; and nothing is known about what it tracks. **A student who cannot separate that from approach E's absence of evidence will accept either**, and will accept the first far more readily because it argues for itself. Approach D exists in phase 3 to make the separation happen in a room where both are running, and point 7 exists to give the room the sentence: *this score is uncalibrated.*

The day's fourth problem is the module's closure. S021 through S024 built a chain: recognise the structure, know what you can guarantee, prove the problem is hard, say so. The chain has no terminus until something ships. Step 6 requires the day's promise to be checked against the alternative the student *built and demonstrated* in yesterday's reply, which closes the loop inside the artefact rather than in the instruction — and which is a stronger check than the paper version could make, because yesterday's alternative now exists.

**The platform also supplies the day's best case, and it is worth naming beside the worst.** An approximation's promise usually has no adjudicator a student can reach. Here it does: the same field the approximate search runs on will answer an exhaustive query, and the vendor's own documentation names that exhaustive query as the way to build a ground-truth set for evaluating an approximation's recall. **A promise the student can actually establish is one command away**, which is rare in this programme and is the reason the day can require step 2's evidence to be run rather than described.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Moderate, and deliberately the module's lowest.** This is the module's fifth consecutive demanding day and its terminal one, and the design reduces intrinsic load in favour of integration.

The technical work at step 2 is a choice among four established methods, each with a template. The genuinely interactive part is step 4's sentence, which must be simultaneously true, checkable, short and addressed to a non-specialist — four constraints on one sentence, which is why it takes most students several attempts.

Management:

- **Continuation from S024.** The problem, the requester, the analysis and the built alternative are all a day old, so comprehension load is zero.
- **Templates for the ratio proof and the measurement protocol,** and a supplied sentence form.
- **Phase 3 supplies all five approaches** configured and ready to run, with their evaluators already wired.
- **The module's single index and single problem domain,** fifth consecutive day.
- **One sentence,** bounded.
- **The evaluators are used and not built.** Students read outputs and reason about them; the data mapping and the run configuration are supplied. **Approach D's judge model is fixed for the room**, so that the score's variability is not confounded with a choice nobody was asked to make.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *A criterion revealed after commitment,* phase 3, which forces a re-ranking with stated reasons.
- *Separating an absent measurement from an unvalidated one,* phase 3's second task, which is the hardest thing the room does in the module.
- *Reading real guarantee language,* phase 1, where the answer is that none of it guarantees anything.
- *Caller-side detection,* step 3 — how would *they* know, not how would you.
- *The consistency check against yesterday's built alternative,* step 6, in both directions.
- *The combined break-and-detect test, executed,* phase 7, where the unacceptable outcome is a miss rather than a failure.
- *Peer judgement of checkability,* phase 8, which is the specification's adjudicator.
- *The sealed Copilot prediction at item 5,* which asks what a number will not be a measure of.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Approximation theory.* One ratio is proved; the theory is omitted.
- *Constructing novel approximations.* Students select and establish; they do not invent.
- *Hardness of approximation.* A genuine and important topic, entirely omitted, which means students may propose approximations that are themselves intractable to achieve. Named at point 3 as a limit and not pursued.
- *Characterising the judge.* The day requires the word *uncalibrated* and the sentence naming what would remove it. **It does not ask students to measure agreement**, which is S048's whole content and requires machinery ME101 has not yet supplied.
- *The preview minimum-score threshold.* Demonstrated once and not operated, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), and excluded from the specification.
- *Withholding the promise forms.* Point 1 states them.

**Enumeration scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md).** Point 1's four promise forms are a selection. They omit guarantees conditional on input parameters, probabilistic approximation schemes, and asymptotic-only guarantees. The selection criterion is stated in the seminar page and is deliberate: these four are the promises a second-year student can establish and a non-specialist caller can check, which are the two constraints the day is built around. The omitted forms fail the second constraint even when a student could establish them.

**The platform instance, scoped in the strong form.** The day teaches an exhaustive scan as an adjudicator and two evaluators as promise-evidence, and both simplifications must carry their conditions.

*Safe.* Reasoning about what a retrieval promise can be; about why an exhaustive comparison is a legitimate ground truth for an approximation's recall; about why a metric counting unjudged retrieved documents is a provenance statement; and about why a judged score with a threshold is not thereby a validated measurement. In all four the correspondence is sufficient and correct, and the fourth is the day's payload.

*Unsafe.* Reasoning about **how good any configuration measured today actually is.** One corpus, one embedding, one label set, one judge model, one service version. Also unsafe: treating the exhaustive scan as a universal adjudicator — it adjudicates *which documents are nearest in this embedding space*, which is a different question from *which documents are relevant*, and a student who conflates them has proved a promise about geometry and made a promise about usefulness. **The unsafe condition is, as it nearly always is, any version other than the one in front of you**, and it applies with unusual force to the evaluator catalogue, whose names, metrics, thresholds and preview boundaries are all product decisions.

The boundary is stated to students at point 11 rather than left to this paper, and **step 7's coverage clause is the artefact element under which a student applying the simplification outside its safe range would fail.**

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **the best option being the wrong one**.

Phase 3's approach E is genuinely the best performer on the supplied queries, and groups rank it first. The late-revealed caller requirement does not change its performance; it changes what performance is worth. The re-ranking that follows is the day's central instructional event and it is entirely student-produced — the instructor supplies a requirement, not a conclusion.

This construction is unusual and is worth naming. Most contrasting-cases phases in this programme reveal that a group's reasoning was incomplete. This one reveals that the reasoning was correct and the *criterion* was wrong, which is a different and more uncomfortable finding, and it is the appropriate one for the module's terminal day.

**Approach D supplies a second and sharper discomfort.** Groups rank it high, for a reason they can state: it is the only approach that explains itself. Then they are told that nobody has published what its explanations agree with. The design's observation is that this lands differently from every other limitation the module has taught, because the other limitations were about systems and this one is about *knowing* — the number is not wrong, it is unexamined, and there is no experiment in the room that would settle it. That is ME101's subject arriving one day early, produced by an artefact rather than by a syllabus.

Phase 1 supplies the day's third engagement, and its effectiveness depends on the descriptions being genuine. They are taken from real documentation and their vagueness is not exaggerated, which students detect. Discovering that language they have read and accepted for years makes no checkable claim is a durable realisation.

Phase 8's module-closing paragraph — what do you now believe about limits — mirrors S005's and is kept for S055, which is the programme's second longest artefact loop.

The competence risk is low. The day asks students to select and establish rather than to invent, and every option at phase 3 is legitimate.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Three real descriptions, five items. Item 2 is the day's move — what could a caller check — asked before checkability is named as the criterion. Item 4's answer, that none of the three guarantees anything, is the day's opening finding. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction** and is administered here per instrumentation rule 8; it is the quarter's bridge into M06, because a student who cannot yet name what a judged score lacks is a student for whom S026 through S029 have work to do, and phase 8's rate is that cohort's baseline.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** The module's cumulative set with a synthesis item asking which day produced something you could put in a contract. The item is answered variously and the variety is the point: students who say S023 have misunderstood what a hardness result is for, and students who say S025 are anticipating correctly.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Five approaches, one ranking, a late criterion, a re-ranking, and a second and harder task. All five are executed. Approach E's construction remains the phase's most important design decision and it must genuinely win on the supplied queries or the phase collapses into a morality play. **Approach D is the phase's second payload and is new.** It is ranked high for a stateable reason, it falls for a reason that is not E's reason, and the second task exists because groups reliably collapse the two. The design's position is that a room which can say *E has no evidence and D has evidence of unknown quality* has acquired the measurement strand's founding distinction four days before the strand begins.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Eleven points. Point 1 carries the enumeration-scope statement. **Point 2 deliberately does not carry the criterion scope statement the widened rule now requires** — see §8 objection 1. Point 3 connects the approximation ratio to S022's population-versus-individual distinction, which is where a ratio's meaning is most often overstated. Point 4's detection obligation is what makes restricted exactness a legitimate promise rather than a hidden condition, and it now has a vendor sentence to work on. **Point 6 gains a mechanical instance**: the metric that counts unjudged retrieved documents is sample provenance asked by the tool rather than by a conscientious engineer, which is the first time in the programme a discipline the design teaches has appeared as a field in somebody's output. **Point 7 is the day's principal gain from the platform anchor** and is the sentence the quarter is built to install. Point 8 is the terminal claim and now carries D alongside E. Point 9 names three defaults; point 11 carries the strong-form scope statement.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps continuing yesterday's artefact. Step 3's caller-side framing is the specification's weighted element alongside step 4. **Step 2's *run, not described* clause and step 6's second question are the day's structural additions.** Step 6's consistency check now tests a stronger claim than it could before: yesterday's alternative was built and demonstrated, so a student who cannot deliver today's promise with it has a real inconsistency rather than a rhetorical one — and the failure is still recorded rather than penalised, because it is a valuable finding about S024's step 5 and not about the student. Step 7's coverage clause is the artefact element enforcing §3.2's scope boundary.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A promise understood differently by the two parties, then an agent failure. Wording failures are the day's characteristic professional error and they are not competence failures, which the instructor disclosure makes plain. **The agent case is the module's last and the design considers it the most consequential**: an evaluation that was correct in every particular, produced a number with a threshold and a label and a reason, and was merged as evidence of quality by reviewers who had no way to see the missing thing, because the missing thing is not in the output and is not a field anyone knows to look for. It is the exact failure the next two years of this programme are most likely to reproduce.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Break the promise, then check detection, with the breaking instance executed rather than argued. Combining S006's construction move with S018's detectability pair is the module's terminal integration and students are told so, because the design wants the combination recognised as a combination.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Peer judgement of checkability, which is the specification's adjudicator and is deliberately not an instructor judgement — if a promise is checkable only by someone who knows the analysis, it is not checkable. Then item 5's sealed predictions, whose second rate is the quarter's M06 baseline. Then the module-closing paragraph.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with optimisation or operations-research background will select and establish a guarantee fluently. Predicted failure: their step 4 sentence will be precise and unreadable — it will contain the ratio, the conditions and the qualifications, and no non-specialist could check it. Countermeasure: phase 8's peer judgement is made by the whole class, and a technically impeccable unreadable promise fails it. This is the second consecutive day where technical fluency makes the assessed element harder.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** A student who cannot prove a ratio has three other promise forms available, and the measured-distribution route is accessible to anyone who completed S022 — **and is now cheaper still, because the adjudicator is a query parameter rather than an apparatus.** Countermeasures: templates for both, and the day's lowest intrinsic load. Residual risk: they may default to the measured distribution when a stronger guarantee was available, which is a real loss and is only partially caught by step 2's *strongest you can establish* framing.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** The initial ranking is straightforward and is meant to be. The re-ranking is where the difficulty is, and its difficulty is in articulating the reason rather than in reaching the conclusion — once the caller requirement is revealed, most groups know E is out and struggle to say what principle excluded it. Fallback: ask the group whether approach C, with labelled measurement but no proof, is excluded by the same principle. It is not, and the distinction is the day's content. **A group that also separates D from E without prompting is asked what evidence would move D into C's position and who would have to produce it**, which is S048's question and is genuinely hard.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **the checkability requirement read as bureaucratic**. A student who has built a heuristic that works, and is told it cannot ship because the promise is not verifiable by a non-specialist, may conclude the programme prefers paperwork to results. Countermeasures: phase 6's incident, where the wording failure had a real cost; phase 3's construction, where the exclusion of E is reached by the students rather than imposed; and point 8's framing as a consequence rather than a rule. The design's honest position is that this risk is highest for the strongest students and that the countermeasures are argumentative rather than structural.

**The word without the understanding.** New with the platform anchor and predicted to be the day's most common shallow pass. The specification requires a judged score to be reported as *uncalibrated*; the cheapest route to that requirement is to type the word. Countermeasure: the specification requires a second sentence naming **what evidence would remove the word and who would have to produce it**, which cannot be typed without a model of what calibration is. Whether that is sufficient is §8 objection 3 and the design does not claim it is.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by S029 (designing an instrument for a question, where the measured-distribution promise becomes an instrument design problem and where item 5's rate is re-probed), S034 (consistency models as a contract with the caller — the same checkability requirement with a formal contract), S048 (the judge is biased, which is where *uncalibrated* stops being a word and becomes a measurement), S050 (saying what the measurement does not license), S055 (what these systems cannot do, where the module's closing paragraphs are returned), S059 (zero-shot generalisation and the benchmark trap) and S088 (residual risk, stated plainly).

**S048 at roughly two quarters is the target this revision creates and it is the most important one.** Today the student says the score is uncalibrated and names what would calibrate it. At S048 they characterise a judge, which is that sentence discharged. **The deferral names its day and its instrument**, per the deferral rule: S048's artefact requires an agreement measurement against human judgement on the student's own task, and a student who wrote the naming sentence today and cannot produce the measurement there has not carried anything.

S059 at roughly three quarters is the other interesting target: a benchmark score is a measured distribution offered as a promise, its provenance and horizon are usually unstated, and point 6's legitimacy conditions are exactly what it fails.

S088 at roughly two years is where the promise-with-a-not-met-procedure becomes a residual-risk statement, and step 5 is its earliest form.

Retained content: the four promise forms, the checkability requirement, the detection obligation, the provenance conditions, and the sentence that a judged score from an uncharacterised instrument is uncalibrated. Five items, no evaluator names.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, then the module cumulative set at S030.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement.

One specification item, jointly in CF103 and MS103, the module's terminal artefact, **plus the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement**.

**A promise the class cannot judge checkable at phase 8 fails.** Making a peer cohort the adjudicator of a specification element is unusual and is defended on the ground that checkability is a property of a reader rather than of a text — a promise only an expert can verify has failed the requirement by definition, so an expert assessor is the wrong instrument.

**A promise resting on a judged score that is not reported as uncalibrated, with the evidence that would remove the word and the party who would have to produce it, fails.** This is the day's newest clause and it is the one the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](/wiki/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md)'s standing prohibition requires of every day that touches a Foundry evaluator. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a coverage assertion that fails the run below a stated level being the accepted form, per the escape [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) established and [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 corrected.

Programme instrumentation: the phase 1 item 4 rate is the cohort's guarantee-language literacy baseline, re-probed at S059; the phase 3 re-ranking articulations are the criterion-substitution measure; **the phase 3 second-task rate — how many groups separate D from E without prompting — is the quarter's measure of whether the unvalidated-instrument distinction landed, and its consumer is S029, whose phase 1 opens on it**, per instrumentation rules 4 and 6; the phase 8 checkability judgement distribution is the cohort's first peer-assessed quantity; step 6's consistency-check failures are recorded as a design signal about S024's step 5. **Item 5's second rate is read by the following year's cohort** through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), per instrumentation rule 9.

The day's phase 3 evidence is a sample and the day says so, per instrumentation rule 3: five approaches configured by staff, one corpus, one label set produced by staff for a query set staff selected, and one judge model fixed for the room. **The label set's coverage of the corpus is itself reported to students by approach C's own metric**, which is the only place in the module where the day's sampling disclosure is produced by the apparatus rather than by the instructor.

Dispositions: **integrity**, at step 2, where the incentive is to claim the strongest-sounding guarantee rather than the strongest establishable one.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: checkability is stated as an absolute and cannot be.**

At its strongest: point 2 says a guarantee the caller cannot verify is a claim about your competence rather than about the system. Taken literally this excludes almost everything engineers legitimately ship. A caller cannot verify a proved approximation ratio without following the proof. They cannot verify a measured distribution without access to the measurement apparatus and the ability to assess its protocol. Nearly all engineering guarantees are ultimately backed by expertise the recipient does not have, which is what professional accountability structures exist to compensate for. The day sets a standard that its own approach A fails.

The rebuttal distinguishes verifying the guarantee's *derivation* from checking its *satisfaction*. A caller who receives "this returns a solution within twice the optimal cost" cannot check the proof and can check, on any instance where the optimum is known or boundable, whether the claim held. That is the checkability the day requires: the promise must be falsifiable by its recipient in operation, not derivable by them from first principles. Approach A passes on this reading and approach E does not, because E's claim — it works well — has no operational falsification condition.

The concession is that the day does not draw this distinction anywhere. Point 2's wording asserts the absolute version, phase 8's peer judgement will be made against whatever reading the class adopts, and the specification fails artefacts on that basis. A criterion that is applied to assessment while being stated more strongly than the design can defend is a genuine defect, and it is the same failure the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s rule addresses for enumerations, in a third register — after taxonomies at S023 and normative claims at S024. Three registers in three consecutive days is enough to say that the rule as written is too narrow.

**The concession stands and, at this revision, standing is a decision.** The rule *was* widened to criteria, and its provenance cites this paragraph as one of the two findings that prompted it. The same page states that each widening applies from the following seminar day and that **the days listed are not retrospectively amended, so that the failure remains legible.** The platform revision reopened phase 4 entirely and made adding the falsifiability-in-operation sentence nearly free; the design declined, for the reason [WP-024](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-024.md) §8 sets out at length — a rule whose provenance is a recorded failure loses its provenance when the failure is tidied. What is added instead is a clause in the phase 4 warrant on the seminar page stating that the omission is deliberate and why. **Two consecutive days have now declined the same repair for the same reason**, which is either a discipline or a rationalisation, and the design records that it cannot tell which from the inside.

**Objection 2: phase 7 combines two disciplines whose interaction has never been tested.**

At its strongest: the phase asks a partner to break the promise and then to check whether the detection method would have caught it. Breaking a promise is S006's construction move; detection assessment is S018's pair. Both have been practised separately. Their combination introduces a failure the design has not anticipated — a partner who cannot break the promise learns nothing about detection, and a partner who breaks it trivially tests detection against an unrealistic case. The phase's stated acceptable outcomes assume a well-calibrated break, and nothing in the design calibrates it.

This objection the design does not fully answer. The phase is the module's terminal integration and it is the least-designed thirty minutes in the module: the two constituent moves are well specified and their conjunction is not. Specifically, there is no instruction about what constitutes a fair break, no fallback for the partner who cannot construct one, and no guidance on what to conclude when detection catches a break that could never occur in practice.

The available repair is straightforward — require the partner to state, before testing detection, whether the substrate could produce their breaking instance, which is step 7's own reachability question applied to the attack. That single addition would calibrate the break and connect the two halves. It is not in the design, and its absence means the module ends on its least-specified phase.

**The platform revision touches this and does not repair it, and the difference is worth stating.** The breaking instance is now executed rather than argued, which removes the phase's weakest outcome — an attack nobody tested — and makes the *existence* of a break a fact instead of a claim. It does nothing about calibration: an executed break can be as unrealistic as a described one, and is now more persuasive for having run. **The repair therefore became more valuable and was still not made**, which is a worse position than the one this paper recorded before, and the design states it as such rather than presenting the execution requirement as progress on this point.

**Objection 3: requiring the word *uncalibrated* converts a disposition into a checkable string.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn.

At its strongest: the day's most important addition is point 7 and the specification clause that enforces it. The clause says that a promise resting on a judged score must report the score as uncalibrated. **That is a requirement on a token.** The cheapest path to satisfying it is to type the word, and a student who types it passes; a student who understands the point and phrases it differently — *nobody has published what this evaluator agrees with* — is, on a literal reading, at risk of failing. The design has taken the one genuinely epistemic thing the day teaches and made it the most mechanically checkable element in the specification, which inverts exactly the relationship between substance and form that the whole programme exists to defend. Worse, the failure is self-concealing: a cohort in which every artefact carries the word looks like a cohort that learned it, and the design has no instrument that distinguishes the two.

The objection is conceded. The rebuttal available — that a shared term is worth installing because it must survive two years of retrieval to S048 — is true, is why the requirement exists, and does not answer the point.

**The cheap fix is one clause and it is made.** The specification now requires the word **and one sentence naming what evidence would remove it and who would have to produce it.** That sentence cannot be written without a model of what calibration would consist of, it is the question S048 answers, and it makes the requirement a claim rather than a token. §6 records S048 as the deferral's named day and its agreement measurement as the named instrument, per the deferral rule.

What remains unfixed is recorded. **The design still cannot distinguish a student who understands the point from one who has learned the two-sentence shape**, and the phase 3 second-task rate is the only signal it has, which measures groups rather than students and measures them before the artefact is written. The obvious instrument — ask the partner at phase 7 to state what would calibrate the writer's score, rather than asking the writer — is instrumentation rule 1 applied exactly as written, costs nothing, and is **not added, because phase 7 already carries two demands and §8 objection 2 has just conceded that it cannot carry the one it has.** That is a real conflict between two of this paper's own findings and it is left standing.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A majority will identify at least one of the three phase 1 descriptions as making a guarantee, and the most-selected will be the one containing a number. *Instrument:* item 4 responses by description.

**Prediction 2.** Approach E will be ranked first or second by a majority of groups before the caller requirement is revealed. *Instrument:* committed pre-revelation rankings. **If E does not win, its construction has failed and the phase's argument does not land.**

**Prediction 3.** Students with optimisation backgrounds will produce step 4 sentences rated more precise and less checkable by peers than students without. *Instrument:* blind precision ratings and phase 8 peer checkability judgements, split by the admission background survey.

**Prediction 4.** In at least a fifth of artefacts, step 6's consistency check will reveal that the S024 alternative cannot be delivered as promised. *Instrument:* step 6 outcomes. **A very low rate would suggest S024's step 5 is producing safe rather than substantive alternatives — and now that S024's alternative must be built and demonstrated, a very low rate is more informative than it was, because the safe-alternative route is more expensive to take.**

**Prediction 5.** Fewer than a third of groups will separate approach D from approach E without prompting at phase 3's second task. *Instrument:* group artefacts coded for whether the two exclusions are given distinct reasons. **This is the platform anchor's own test on this day. A high rate would mean the unvalidated-instrument distinction is available to students earlier than the measurement strand assumes, and S029's phase 1 opens on a room that already holds it.**

**Prediction 6.** A large majority of item 5 sealed predictions will describe a number and a pass, and fewer than a tenth will name the absence of published agreement, or any other specific missing property, as what the number is not a measure of. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded for generic-versus-specific identification. **The second rate is this quarter's baseline for M06 and is re-probed at S029. A rate near zero is the expected result and is the design's justification for spending a whole quarter on measurement; a rate above a quarter would mean ME101 can start further along than the [Course Catalogue](/wiki/program/06-Course-Catalogue.md) assumes.**
