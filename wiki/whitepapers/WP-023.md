# WP-023 — Non-Functional Requirements That Can Be Verified

**Seminar day:** [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md) · **Module:** [M05](/wiki/modules/M05.md) · **Quarter:** [Q3](/wiki/quarters/Q3.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 2
**Platform anchor:** Azure API Management AI gateway and the Microsoft 365 Agent Store validation criteria — non-functional requirements with an enforcement point you own, beside published thresholds enforced by a party who is not the customer and measured by an instrument nobody names

## In plain terms

Functional requirements are volunteered. Somebody wants the system to do something and says so.

**The requirements that end engagements are not volunteered by anybody.** Nobody asks for availability until it is absent, for maintainability until the author has left, for portability until they are moving, or for data-handling discipline until somebody has looked at production data they should not have. These properties have no advocate in the room, which means they arrive by one of two routes: from an enumeration, or from an incident.

This session uses an enumeration, and then does something the enumeration does not expect. **It requires every number to carry its origin.**

The reason is a specific and instructive defect in the source. The playbook that publishes the enumeration also publishes a large catalogue of example thresholds — response times, availability figures, coverage targets, complexity limits — and **that catalogue carries no citation of any kind.** The numbers are illustrations by the authors' own framing. They sit on the page in the same flat declarative register as the publication's best-sourced material, and nothing distinguishes them.

An engineer who lifts a number from that catalogue has not done anything wrong. **An engineer who lifts it and strips the provenance has manufactured a specification**, and the only difference between the two is a clause.

## Abstract

This paper defends producing non-functional requirements by exhaustive walk of a published enumeration, in the same capture form and against the same two marked fields as [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md), with one addition: every threshold carries its origin, and *borrowed from an uncited catalogue* is an admissible origin when declared. The argument is that these properties have no advocate in the room and therefore arrive either from a list or from an incident; that the list is a list rather than a validated taxonomy; and that an unsourced number acquires the authority of a specification by repetition alone. The session closes by requiring one category the enumeration does not contain, on the grounds that a navigation implying completeness is not a completeness claim. The paper concedes that the origin requirement is unenforceable against a determined learner, and that the enumeration's own structural defects — an absent index, categories of a few lines, and no shared internal shape across them — undermine the exhaustive-walk method it is being used for.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

The properties in question share one structural feature: **nobody in the discovery raised them, because nobody in the discovery is accountable for them.**

The sponsor is accountable for outcomes and speaks about outcomes. The operational manager is accountable for a process and speaks about the process. The people accountable for availability, for data handling, for what happens when the author leaves, are usually in different parts of the organisation, were not in the room, and — as [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md) established — may not have been invited.

**The consequence is that these requirements enter the engagement late, from a review function, after the design is fixed.** At that point they are experienced as obstruction, they are argued about rather than engineered, and the argument is conducted in the vocabulary of compliance rather than of design.

The session's response is to bring them in early by walking a list, and to accept that the list is a poor instrument. **A poor instrument used early beats a good one used late**, and that is an argument rather than a finding.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** The session works from the [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) form where a learner has one and from any requirement set where they do not.

It retrieves [S006](/wiki/seminars/S006.md), which asked what constraints you cannot see yet. **This session is the answer to that question in one specific area** — you cannot see them because their owners were not present, and here is a list of the places to go and look.

The prior it works against is that non-functional requirements are a compliance activity performed by somebody else. **The countermeasure is the ownership field**, which requires a role inside the customer's organisation and therefore requires the learner to find out who that is.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The heaviest session in the module. The load is intrinsic and it comes from the absence of an informant: everywhere else in this programme a learner can ask somebody. Here they must reason from a list to a system they only partly understand, and then defend a number.

**3.2 Why an enumeration rather than a method.** There is no method for discovering that a property matters to a system before the property has failed. **An enumeration is a memory aid and this paper makes no larger claim for it.** Its value is that omitting a category becomes visible, which is a property of lists in general and not evidence about this list.

**3.3 Why origin rather than justification.** Requiring every threshold to be justified would force learners to invent justifications, which is worse than borrowing. Requiring the origin is cheaper, checkable, and preserves the information a later reader needs. **A borrowed number with its provenance attached is a usable engineering artefact; the same number with the provenance stripped is indistinguishable from a fabricated specification.**

**3.4 Why the enumeration is treated as incomplete on purpose.** The published section this enumeration comes from has structural defects that a reader navigating by menu will not see: **its index page does not resolve at all** — the single such address in the whole publication — several of its category pages are a few lines long, and most but not all follow the structure the section itself proposes. Read 2026-07-30. **The session uses those defects as content**, because a learner who has once found a menu overstating a document's completeness will check the next one.

**3.5 Why some properties are absolute and the form is dangerous for them.**

The capture form has a measurement field, and a form with a field invites a value. But the same publication states an operating rule for engagements that admits no threshold at all: production data is not accessed directly ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md)). **There is no percentage version of that sentence**, and a learner who writes one has downgraded a policy into a target without noticing.

The mechanism is worth naming because it will recur wherever a template meets a constraint: **a form propagates its own shape into the content it collects.** The eight-field form is a good instrument and it silently assumes every property is scalar. The session's standard states the exception explicitly rather than trusting learners to notice it, which is design reasoning and carries no external warrant.

**3.6 Why latency and throughput are architectural consequences rather than settings.**

This is the session's technical substance and it is the reason a number chosen because it sounds serious is worse than no number.

Inference decomposes into two workloads with different bottlenecks. Processing the prompt handles many tokens at once and can keep the accelerator busy; generating the response produces tokens one at a time and is dominated by moving weights and cached attention state through memory rather than by arithmetic ([research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md)). Two consequences follow that a requirement author must hold. **Raw compute capability is a poor predictor of the latency a user experiences.** And the cached attention state rather than the parameter count is what binds at long context, so a context-length increase can cost more than it appears to.

Throughput has the same character from the other direction. Batching is the throughput lever; what limits how many requests fit is memory management of that cached state; and the named waste mechanisms — fragmentation and duplication — cap the batch, which caps the throughput ([research/02-technical-foundations/kwon-pagedattention-vllm.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/kwon-pagedattention-vllm.md)).

**What the design takes from both is a shape and not a number, and this is a hard boundary.** The first source reports operating points tied to one accelerator topology, one model and one context length, and it predates several now-standard serving techniques. The second reports a comparison against named 2023-era baselines, stated at matched latency and explicitly conditional on sequence length, model size and decoding strategy. **No figure from either appears anywhere in this session or this paper.** What transfers is that latency and throughput trade against one another, so an honest performance claim is a claim about a curve rather than a point — and therefore **a requirement naming one without the other has not specified an operating point.**

**3.7 Why a single number is not a requirement.**

The position that closes the argument is that an evaluation is an experiment, and a reported figure needs an interval and a count ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). Applied here: **a non-functional requirement stated as a bare number, with no distribution and no observation window, can be neither missed nor met.** The practitioner treatment reaches the same place operationally — latency is a distribution, so the average misleads and percentiles are the reportable form ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md)).

**Their limits.** The first is analytical rather than empirical; its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions and none is reproduced; and it addresses comparing scores rather than choosing what to score, so a tight interval around the wrong metric is still wrong — which is §8's second objection in a different register. The second is a practitioner book whose figures are largely reported second-hand from other people's studies.

**3.8 Why the session ends at an enforcement point rather than at a document.**

A non-functional requirement with no enforcement point is an intention. The published argument for an AI gateway is that the reverse proxy in front of model endpoints is the correct place to enforce cost control, quota fairness, authentication, resilience and observability, and it is candid that this is "not a separate offering" but an extension of an existing API gateway ([research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md)). **That framing makes the intellectual claim visible: AI traffic is API traffic with different resource semantics, not a new category of thing.**

Three of its mechanisms carry durable lessons that survive the product.

**The noisy-neighbour argument.** Tokens are the scarce resource, quota is assigned as a rate, and that quota must be distributed across consuming applications and teams. **A shared finite resource with no per-consumer accounting will be consumed by whichever consumer is least careful.** The token limit policy's counter key — subscription, address, or an arbitrary expression — is therefore the requirement's unit of fairness, and choosing it is a requirements decision that most documents never state.

**Attribution is what makes governance possible.** Metric dimensions convert total consumption, which tells you the bill, into per-consumer consumption, which tells you who to talk to. A cost requirement with no attribution dimension cannot be enforced against anybody.

**A failing dependency should state its own recovery time.** The circuit breaker takes its trip duration from the backend's `Retry-After` header rather than a fixed interval, which is better design than guessing — and it is the clearest small example in the platform material of a requirement expressed as a protocol rather than as a constant.

**And one mechanism is a trap, which is why the workflow ends where it does.** Semantic caching reuses prior completions by vector proximity of the prompt. It buys cost and latency and **spends correctness**, with the similarity threshold as the dial; set loosely, the system answers a question that was not asked. **The documentation does not discuss this at all.** A performance requirement met by a mechanism that silently weakens an unwritten correctness requirement is this session's thesis in miniature, and it is the reason workflow step 5 exists.

**Its limits, and they bound §7.** First-party product documentation with a commercial frame; it argues for a gateway and does not discuss the latency, cost or operational burden the gateway itself adds. **It publishes no performance data, no cost-saving measurement and no comparison against any other product.** Capability varies by service tier; several features are preview; policy names and attributes drift between revisions; and the privacy consequences of logging prompts and completions are absent from a page that recommends the logging for billing and auditing.

**3.9 Why a second, externally owned enforcement point is taught beside the first.**

§3.8's enforcement point is chosen by the learner and operated by the learner, which leaves one failure mode entirely unaddressed. **A requirement whose enforcement you own can be relaxed by you**, silently, at the moment it becomes inconvenient — and nothing in the artefact records that it was. The session's whole apparatus of origins and owners is a defence against a number arriving without provenance; it is not a defence against a number leaving without notice.

The store's admission criteria supply the missing case, and they supply it in the only form that makes the point unarguable: **a set of non-functional requirements published by a party who can refuse the product** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agents-toolkit-and-sdk-family.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agents-toolkit-and-sdk-family.md), read 2026-07-31). Most are architectural — an AI label, citations and a feedback control on every response, streamed responses, a declared scope in two manifest locations, a bounded range of sample prompts per command, name identity across three files, and explicit user permission before any API or Model Context Protocol server call. Two are numeric: a set of response-time percentile obligations and an availability figure with a worked gloss.

**Neither number appears in this paper or in the session, and the omission is the design decision rather than an oversight.** They are contractual admission thresholds set by one party for its own catalogue. Under [09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) they are perishable policy values, and this paper's own §3.4 forbids a learner from adopting an uncited catalogue figure as a benchmark. A design that then reproduced a vendor's admission thresholds in teaching material would have broken its own rule in the most visible place available.

**What is taught instead is the shape, and the shape is the session's thesis in an unusually clean specimen.** The criteria are published, specific, enforced, and consequential. **And no instrument, no population, no observation window and no sampling rule accompanies any of the numeric ones.** It is not stated whether the enforcer measures them, whether the publisher is expected to, or what evidence is accepted in place of a measurement. So a team can satisfy every architectural criterion, read the numeric ones exactly, and still not know what would count as failing them.

That produces the design's third assessable move, after the origin requirement and the refusal requirement: **supply the four missing fields yourself, and mark them as yours.** The workflow's `published_by_enforcer` block exists for one purpose, which is to stop the learner absorbing an inherited obligation as though they had specified it. A gate criterion restated without its enforcer and without its silence becomes, on the next page of the next document, a specification the team appears to have chosen.

**A second silence is carried into §5 rather than §3, because it is a planning failure rather than a measurement one.** No submission pass rate, rejection rate, rejection-reason distribution, median time to approval or typical resubmission count is published. **An external dependency on the critical path, with a published requirement and no published throughput, is a schedule risk that cannot be sized from anything a team can read** — which makes it a register entry at [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) with an authority and a date, and not a step in a plan.

**Its limits, and they are heavier than §3.8's.** **The validation guidelines and the marketplace certification policies were not fetched at all**, so the criteria the session works from are the subset returned by search excerpts and **are not the complete gate.** Page structure for most of the surrounding material is reconstructed rather than confirmed. **Every absence recorded above was found by searching and not stated by any source**, and absence at one reading is not proof of absence in the product. The ergonomics claims in the surrounding documentation carry no measurement and are marketing register. **No measure exists anywhere of how much any of this changes time to a working agent**, and the standing prohibition on duration claims applies to this material without exception.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

Eight movements: situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**Opening item 5 is the session.** *What is your evidence that the number you just wrote down is the right number?* It is placed last because items 1 to 4 produce the number, and a question about provenance is only sharp once the thing has been written.

**The walk is exhaustive and the refusals are required in writing.** A category declared out of scope with a reason is a decision; a category never mentioned is the same decision taken invisibly. **The refusals are the harder writing and the design intends that**, because a refusal must say why and a requirement can be copied.

**The closing exercise asks for a category the enumeration does not have.** This is the movement that converts the session from list-completion into judgement. The candidates that recur in forward-deployed work — what happens to the system when the engagement ends, what the customer must be able to observe without you, what evidence the system must produce about its own behaviour, what it costs to run at the volume actually expected — **are this design's, are not in the source, and are offered as prompts rather than as a second enumeration.**

**The workflow's order is the argument, and its middle step is the one that does the teaching.** Counter key before limit; policy before result; and then, before anything else, **the sentence naming the property the enforcement spent.** Every mechanism the gateway offers buys one property at the cost of another, and a learner who can name the trade has understood that a non-functional requirement set is a system of exchanges rather than a list of targets.

**The review question is a single one asked repeatedly.** *Where did that come from?* Answers of *it's standard* are struck. The movement is deliberately mechanical because the failure it catches is mechanical: numbers that arrived without provenance and acquired authority by sitting on a page.

**The adversarial step asks two questions rather than one, and the second is the more useful.** *Build a system that satisfies all of these and that the author would refuse to operate* finds the unstated constraint. *Which of these numbers could I move without anybody noticing?* finds the ones with no consequence attached — and **a threshold with no consequence is a preference that has been formatted.**

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The borrowed catalogue.** The likeliest failure and the one the session is built around. *Countermeasure:* the origin requirement and the review movement, which together make the borrowing visible rather than preventing it. **Borrowing is not the failure; concealed borrowing is.**

**The un-owned property.** The requirement is well written and the owner field contains the learner's own team. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires a role inside the customer's organisation. **This is weakly enforced** — a role can be named without being asked, and the session has no way to detect that.

**The list-shaped set.** The learner produces exactly the categories the list had, in the list's order, and stops. *Countermeasure:* the closing exercise, which requires one addition and a note saying it was added.

**The absolute written as a target.** A data-handling constraint that admits no threshold is written with one, because the form has a measurement field and the learner filled it. *Countermeasure:* the standard states that some properties are absolute and that the form must not be allowed to soften them.

**The refusal that is silence.** The learner walks the list, skips the categories that seem irrelevant, and produces a shorter set. *Countermeasure:* the specification requires a written refusal per skipped category. **This is the countermeasure most likely to be satisfied formulaically**, and the design has no better one.

**The bare number.** A threshold arrives with no distribution, no workload and no observation window, and is defended because it is precise. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires the operating point rather than the point estimate, and the workflow's step 3 forces the concurrency question. **This countermeasure is weak against a learner who has nobody to ask**, which is most of them, and the honest outcome is an origin recorded as an open question rather than a better number.

**The enforcement that spent something.** A learner configures a mechanism that satisfies the stated requirement and quietly weakens an unstated one — most often a cache that buys latency and spends correctness. *Countermeasure:* workflow step 5 requires the trade to be named and attributed. Prediction 6 in §9 predicts this countermeasure failing.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence.** One specification item at [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md): that every threshold has a stated origin, with *borrowed from a catalogue that carries no source* admissible when declared.

**The ownership requirement is not separately assessed**, and that is a known gap. It is the item most likely to be satisfied by naming a role nobody has spoken to, and the design has no check that distinguishes a named owner from an agreed one.

**Two further things are deliberately excluded from assessment.** The numbers themselves are not assessed — a threshold is marked on whether it carries an origin, never on whether it is the right value, because no source behind this programme supplies a standard for rightness and the one that supplies the catalogue supplies numbers with no citation. And the gateway policy is not assessed at all: it is an instrument for making a decision visible, not a deliverable, and marking it would produce learners optimising a configuration in a module about requirements.

**Nothing else is assessed.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the enumeration is not good enough to be walked exhaustively, and the session's method depends on it being good enough.**

At its strongest: the section has no index page, several of its categories are a few lines, one has broken rendering, and most but not all use the structure the section proposes for itself. **A walk of that list is not a systematic review of a system's qualities; it is a walk of one organisation's incompletely maintained wiki.** Presenting it to learners as the instrument for finding requirements nobody volunteered gives an unearned impression of coverage — which is exactly the failure the session warns about, committed by the session.

**Conceded, and the concession is the sharper form of the session's own lesson.** The rebuttal is that the alternative is worse: the realistic alternative to walking a flawed list is walking no list, and the properties in question then arrive from an incident. The session's response to the objection is structural rather than rhetorical — **it requires the learner to find a category the list does not have**, which is a way of teaching the list's incompleteness through the list. **The declined fix is to construct a better enumeration for this programme.** It is declined because this design has no warrant for a taxonomy of system qualities that the published one lacks, and inventing one would be asserting completeness with less evidence than the source has, while looking more authoritative.

**Objection 2: the origin requirement is unenforceable and therefore decorative.**

At its strongest: a learner who wants a number to look authoritative will write an origin that sounds authoritative. *Derived from the customer's existing service commitments* is a sentence, not a fact, and nothing in the assessment can check it. **The requirement therefore filters out only the learners who are honest and careless**, which is a strange population to target.

**Partly conceded.** It cannot be enforced and the assessment does not claim to enforce it. The rebuttal is about the default rather than the ceiling: the observed failure is not fabrication but omission — numbers arrive without anybody having thought about where they came from, and a field that requires an answer makes the thought happen once. **The declined fix is to require documentary evidence for each origin.** It is declined on cost grounds, which is a weaker reason than this paper would like to give, and it means the item marks whether a learner considered provenance rather than whether the provenance is real.

**Objection 3: anchoring a requirements session to a gateway teaches learners that a non-functional requirement is a configuration.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the platform anchor most deserves: the session's most concrete, most memorable artefact is a policy fragment. A learner will come away believing that availability, cost and fairness are things you set, because the session's climax is setting them. The document work — the enumeration walk, the refusals, the origins — is slower, harder and less satisfying, and it is where the actual engineering judgement lives. **Worse, the mechanisms are seductive precisely because they work:** a quota really does stop a noisy consumer, and a learner who has watched it work will under-rate the requirement that could not be enforced at any gateway, which is most of the ones that end engagements.

**Conceded, and the design's response is a matter of ordering rather than of emphasis.** The policy is written after the enumeration and after the origins, never before; it is not assessed; and workflow step 5 exists to make the *cost* of enforcement the last thing a learner does rather than the first. **The declined fix was to place the gateway material in the design stage, where an enforcement point properly belongs, and leave this session entirely documentary** — which is where the previous version of this session sat. It is declined because a non-functional requirement whose author has never seen an enforcement point is written without any model of what enforcement costs, and the numbers that result are exactly the aspirational figures §5 names as a failure mode. **The cost of that decision is that a requirements session now contains a product, and the product will be wrong before the argument is**, which is what the perishability table exists to record.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first-pass thresholds will have no origin recorded until the review movement forces one. *Instrument:* the sets before and after review, counted by presence of an origin per threshold.

**Prediction 2.** Where an origin is eventually recorded, *borrowed from a catalogue* will be the most common one. *Instrument:* origins coded into categories by a second reader, reported without aggregation across cohorts.

**Prediction 3.** Most learners will produce a set whose categories match the published enumeration exactly, including its order, before the closing exercise. *Instrument:* the category list compared against the source's order.

**Prediction 4.** The added category will most often concern what happens after the engagement ends. *Instrument:* the added categories, coded by a second reader. **If they cluster instead on performance or security, the design's claim that forward-deployed work has a characteristic blind spot at handover is not supported at this scale.**

**Prediction 5.** Most latency and availability requirements will be written as a single figure with no distribution, no workload and no observation window, and most will remain so after the material is taught. *Instrument:* the requirements before and after, coded *operating point* / *bare number* by a second reader.

**Prediction 6.** A majority of learners who configure an enforcement mechanism will not name the property it spent, and among those who do, the cache-versus-correctness trade will be named less often than the quota-versus-availability trade. *Instrument:* the step-5 statements, coded for presence and for which trade is named. **This predicts the failure of §5's countermeasure for the enforcement failure mode**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 7.** Asked which of their numbers could move without anybody noticing, most learners will name at least one, and the ones they name will be disproportionately those whose origin is the uncited catalogue. *Instrument:* the adversarial-step record cross-tabulated against the recorded origins. **This is the session's only direct test of the claim that an unprovenanced number carries no consequence.**

**Prediction 8.** Learners instructed to compare their item-5 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 4) will record an admissible origin for every threshold more often than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* a second reader's completeness count, split within cohort on exercise-4 completion. **The same prediction is made at [WP-021](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-021.md), [WP-022](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-022.md) and [WP-024](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-024.md), so a null across all four would indict the comparison pattern rather than this session.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the requirements which end engagements have no advocate in the room; that a threshold without an origin becomes a specification by repetition; that a menu implying completeness is not a completeness claim; and that a property with no enforcement point is an intention.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The enumeration is a list from a publication read on 2026-07-30 that offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices. Its threshold catalogue carries no citation of any kind, which this paper records as a property of the source and this session treats as material. The inference literature establishes mechanism and no magnitude, and its concrete figures are tied to hardware, models and baselines that have moved. **The gateway documentation publishes no performance, latency or cost data at all** — the benefits it describes are plausible mechanisms rather than measured outcomes. The claim that an unstated non-functional requirement surfaces later at higher cost is **mechanically plausible and unmeasured**; direction only.

**The concession worth remembering.** This session teaches list discipline using a list it has just finished criticising, and it teaches requirements using a product that will be obsolete before the argument is. Both are uncomfortable, both are deliberate, and both are the honest position available: the alternatives are a better-looking list with less behind it, and a requirements session in which nobody has ever seen what enforcement costs.

**The silences that matter.** **The documentation publishes no performance, latency or cost-saving data of any kind**, so every benefit in it is a plausible mechanism rather than a measured outcome — which is precisely the standard this session applies to a learner's own thresholds and must therefore apply to its own platform anchor. It does not discuss the latency, cost or operational burden that the gateway itself adds, so a requirement set that adopts one has acquired an unpriced component. **It says nothing at all about the correctness risk of semantic caching**, despite the similarity threshold being the dial that decides whether the system answers a question that was not asked; that analysis is this repository's and is marked as such. It does not address the privacy consequences of logging prompts and completions, on a page that recommends the logging. And it publishes no guidance on choosing a counter key — **the single decision this session treats as the requirement** — which is a gap the documentation does not acknowledge exists.

**The externally enforced gate is a second and differently shaped silence, and it is load-bearing for §3.9.** **No measurement method is published for any numeric store criterion — no instrument, no population, no observation window, no sampling rule** — and it is not stated whether the enforcer measures the property itself, whether the publisher must, or what evidence would be accepted instead. **No outcome data accompanies the criteria either: no submission pass rate, no rejection rate, no distribution of rejection reasons, no median time to approval, and no statement of how many resubmissions are typical.** A team can read exactly what is required of it and cannot learn how often anyone meets it. **No calibration is published for the responsible-AI manifest check** — it is not stated whether it is deterministic or model-judged, no false-positive or false-negative rate is given, no appeal path exists beyond resubmitting, and **no evidence is offered that a manifest passing it yields an agent that behaves acceptably.** **Nothing is published about what happens to an admitted agent when the toolchain that built it is renamed or superseded again** — whether the listing persists, whether resubmission is triggered, or what the publisher's obligation becomes. And the Government-tenant publishing restriction is stated **once**, on one page, with no alternative offered and no rationale. **Every one of these absences was found by searching for the thing and not finding it, not by any source stating that it does not exist**, and the note behind them records that the validation guidelines and certification policies were never fetched — so the criteria this session teaches are a subset and must not be presented as the complete gate.
