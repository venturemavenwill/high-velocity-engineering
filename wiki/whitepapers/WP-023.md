## In plain terms

Functional requirements are volunteered. Somebody wants the system to do something and says so.

**The requirements that end engagements are not volunteered by anybody.** Nobody asks for availability until it is absent, for maintainability until the author has left, for portability until they are moving, or for data-handling discipline until somebody has looked at production data they should not have. These properties have no advocate in the room, which means they arrive by one of two routes: from an enumeration, or from an incident.

This session uses an enumeration, and then does something the enumeration does not expect. **It requires every number to carry its origin.**

The reason is a specific and instructive defect in the source. The playbook that publishes the enumeration also publishes a large catalogue of example thresholds — response times, availability figures, coverage targets, complexity limits — and **that catalogue carries no citation of any kind.** The numbers are illustrations by the authors' own framing. They sit on the page in the same flat declarative register as the publication's best-sourced material, and nothing distinguishes them.

An engineer who lifts a number from that catalogue has not done anything wrong. **An engineer who lifts it and strips the provenance has manufactured a specification**, and the only difference between the two is a clause.

## Abstract

This paper defends producing non-functional requirements by exhaustive walk of a published enumeration, in the same capture form and against the same two marked fields as [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md), with one addition: every threshold carries its origin, and *borrowed from an uncited catalogue* is an admissible origin when declared. The argument is that these properties have no advocate in the room and therefore arrive either from a list or from an incident; that the list is a list rather than a validated taxonomy; and that an unsourced number acquires the authority of a specification by repetition alone. The session closes by requiring one category the enumeration does not contain, on the grounds that a navigation implying completeness is not a completeness claim. The paper concedes that the origin requirement is unenforceable against a determined learner, and that the enumeration's own structural defects — an absent index, categories of a few lines, and no shared internal shape across them — undermine the exhaustive-walk method it is being used for.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

The properties in question share one structural feature: **nobody in the discovery raised them, because nobody in the discovery is accountable for them.**

The sponsor is accountable for outcomes and speaks about outcomes. The operational manager is accountable for a process and speaks about the process. The people accountable for availability, for data handling, for what happens when the author leaves, are usually in different parts of the organisation, were not in the room, and — as [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md) established — may not have been invited.

**The consequence is that these requirements enter the engagement late, from a review function, after the design is fixed.** At that point they are experienced as obstruction, they are argued about rather than engineered, and the argument is conducted in the vocabulary of compliance rather than of design.

The session's response is to bring them in early by walking a list, and to accept that the list is a poor instrument. **A poor instrument used early beats a good one used late**, and that is an argument rather than a finding.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** The session works from the [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) form where a learner has one and from any requirement set where they do not.

It retrieves [S006](/wiki/seminars/S006.md), which asked what constraints you cannot see yet. **This session is the answer to that question in one specific area** — you cannot see them because their owners were not present, and here is a list of the places to go and look.

The prior it works against is that non-functional requirements are a compliance activity performed by somebody else. **The countermeasure is the ownership field**, which requires a role inside the customer's organisation and therefore requires the learner to find out who that is.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The heaviest session in the module. The load is intrinsic and it comes from the absence of an informant: everywhere else in this programme a learner can ask somebody. Here they must reason from a list to a system they only partly understand, and then defend a number.

**3.2 Why an enumeration rather than a method.** There is no method for discovering that a property matters to a system before the property has failed. **An enumeration is a memory aid and this paper makes no larger claim for it.** Its value is that omitting a category becomes visible, which is a property of lists in general and not evidence about this list.

**3.3 Why origin rather than justification.** Requiring every threshold to be justified would force learners to invent justifications, which is worse than borrowing. Requiring the origin is cheaper, checkable, and preserves the information a later reader needs. **A borrowed number with its provenance attached is a usable engineering artefact; the same number with the provenance stripped is indistinguishable from a fabricated specification.**

**3.4 Why the enumeration is treated as incomplete on purpose.** The published section this enumeration comes from has structural defects that a reader navigating by menu will not see: **its index page does not resolve at all** — the single such address in the whole publication — several of its category pages are a few lines long, and most but not all follow the structure the section itself proposes. Read 2026-07-30. **The session uses those defects as content**, because a learner who has once found a menu overstating a document's completeness will check the next one.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

Seven movements: situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 5 is the session.** *What is your evidence that the number you just wrote down is the right number?* It is placed last because items 1 to 4 produce the number, and a question about provenance is only sharp once the thing has been written.

**The walk is exhaustive and the refusals are required in writing.** A category declared out of scope with a reason is a decision; a category never mentioned is the same decision taken invisibly. **The refusals are the harder writing and the design intends that**, because a refusal must say why and a requirement can be copied.

**The closing exercise asks for a category the enumeration does not have.** This is the movement that converts the session from list-completion into judgement. The candidates that recur in forward-deployed work — what happens to the system when the engagement ends, what the customer must be able to observe without you, what evidence the system must produce about its own behaviour, what it costs to run at the volume actually expected — **are this design's, are not in the source, and are offered as prompts rather than as a second enumeration.**

**The review movement is a single question asked repeatedly.** *Where did that come from?* Answers of *it's standard* are struck. The movement is deliberately mechanical because the failure it catches is mechanical: numbers that arrived without provenance and acquired authority by sitting on a page.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The borrowed catalogue.** The likeliest failure and the one the session is built around. *Countermeasure:* the origin requirement and the review movement, which together make the borrowing visible rather than preventing it. **Borrowing is not the failure; concealed borrowing is.**

**The un-owned property.** The requirement is well written and the owner field contains the learner's own team. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires a role inside the customer's organisation. **This is weakly enforced** — a role can be named without being asked, and the session has no way to detect that.

**The list-shaped set.** The learner produces exactly the categories the list had, in the list's order, and stops. *Countermeasure:* the closing exercise, which requires one addition and a note saying it was added.

**The absolute written as a target.** A data-handling constraint that admits no threshold is written with one, because the form has a measurement field and the learner filled it. *Countermeasure:* the standard states that some properties are absolute and that the form must not be allowed to soften them.

**The refusal that is silence.** The learner walks the list, skips the categories that seem irrelevant, and produces a shorter set. *Countermeasure:* the specification requires a written refusal per skipped category. **This is the countermeasure most likely to be satisfied formulaically**, and the design has no better one.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence.** One specification item at [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md): that every threshold has a stated origin, with *borrowed from a catalogue that carries no source* admissible when declared.

**The ownership requirement is not separately assessed**, and that is a known gap. It is the item most likely to be satisfied by naming a role nobody has spoken to, and the design has no check that distinguishes a named owner from an agreed one.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the enumeration is not good enough to be walked exhaustively, and the session's method depends on it being good enough.**

At its strongest: the section has no index page, several of its categories are a few lines, one has broken rendering, and most but not all use the structure the section proposes for itself. **A walk of that list is not a systematic review of a system's qualities; it is a walk of one organisation's incompletely maintained wiki.** Presenting it to learners as the instrument for finding requirements nobody volunteered gives an unearned impression of coverage — which is exactly the failure the session warns about, committed by the session.

**Conceded, and the concession is the sharper form of the session's own lesson.** The rebuttal is that the alternative is worse: the realistic alternative to walking a flawed list is walking no list, and the properties in question then arrive from an incident. The session's response to the objection is structural rather than rhetorical — **it requires the learner to find a category the list does not have**, which is a way of teaching the list's incompleteness through the list. **The declined fix is to construct a better enumeration for this programme.** It is declined because this design has no warrant for a taxonomy of system qualities that the published one lacks, and inventing one would be asserting completeness with less evidence than the source has, while looking more authoritative.

**Objection 2: the origin requirement is unenforceable and therefore decorative.**

At its strongest: a learner who wants a number to look authoritative will write an origin that sounds authoritative. *Derived from the customer's existing service commitments* is a sentence, not a fact, and nothing in the assessment can check it. **The requirement therefore filters out only the learners who are honest and careless**, which is a strange population to target.

**Partly conceded.** It cannot be enforced and the assessment does not claim to enforce it. The rebuttal is about the default rather than the ceiling: the observed failure is not fabrication but omission — numbers arrive without anybody having thought about where they came from, and a field that requires an answer makes the thought happen once. **The declined fix is to require documentary evidence for each origin.** It is declined on cost grounds, which is a weaker reason than this paper would like to give, and it means the item marks whether a learner considered provenance rather than whether the provenance is real.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first-pass thresholds will have no origin recorded until the review movement forces one. *Instrument:* the sets before and after review, counted by presence of an origin per threshold.

**Prediction 2.** Where an origin is eventually recorded, *borrowed from a catalogue* will be the most common one. *Instrument:* origins coded into categories by a second reader, reported without aggregation across cohorts.

**Prediction 3.** Most learners will produce a set whose categories match the published enumeration exactly, including its order, before the closing exercise. *Instrument:* the category list compared against the source's order.

**Prediction 4.** The added category will most often concern what happens after the engagement ends. *Instrument:* the added categories, coded by a second reader. **If they cluster instead on performance or security, the design's claim that forward-deployed work has a characteristic blind spot at handover is not supported at this scale.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the requirements which end engagements have no advocate in the room; that a threshold without an origin becomes a specification by repetition; and that a menu implying completeness is not a completeness claim.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The enumeration is a list from a publication read on 2026-07-30 that offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices. Its threshold catalogue carries no citation of any kind, which this paper records as a property of the source and this session treats as material. The claim that an unstated non-functional requirement surfaces later at higher cost is **mechanically plausible and unmeasured**; direction only.

**The concession worth remembering.** This session teaches list discipline using a list it has just finished criticising. That is uncomfortable, it is deliberate, and it is the honest position available: the alternative is a better-looking list with less behind it.
