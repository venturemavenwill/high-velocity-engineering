## In plain terms

This module is written past its source, and the honest version of that sentence is worth stating before anything else.

The aspiration is present in the practice corpus: capable people are named as one of the things that must hold together for accelerated delivery to produce anything, and the corpus observes once — inside a narrative about business value, not inside any procedure — that the customer should be left holding the cost-modelling capability because they will need it after the team disengages. **That is the whole of it.** No mechanism for producing capability in a customer's engineers, no measure of whether it was produced, no evidence that it ever was, and no place in any exit procedure where somebody is asked.

An organisation that mechanised its own teardown down to a register state change left the transfer of capability as an unrecorded intention. This module fills that gap with an argument, and the argument has no external warrant.

The session's own content is smaller and more useful, and it comes in two halves.

The first half is diagnostic. There are four different states a receiving group can be in — nobody has told them it is theirs; they have been told and do not want it; they want it and do not know how; they know how and cannot yet do it — and each calls for a different response. Almost everybody proposes instruction for all four, because instruction is the available response and the other three do not look like engineering problems. Separating them is what the session does.

The second half is constructive, and it is the platform half. **A document describes a capability; a convention enacts one.** A written standard is discharged by a person remembering it and choosing to comply; the same standard expressed as configuration the tooling loads is discharged by the tooling. The session has learners take one convention out of the handover folder, express it as an instructions file, and prove on the recipient's own machine that it arrived — because a configuration corpus that is not loaded behaves exactly like one that is, and nothing in the output tells you which you have.

## Abstract

This paper defends opening a capability module with a diagnostic distinction and an executable artefact rather than with a training design. The argument runs in three parts.

**First**, capability gaps have several distinct causes, the responses to them differ, and a delivery team's default response addresses only one of them — which is why the session's opening item asks for four responses and is arranged so that most learners produce four versions of the same one.

**Second**, the module's central psychological claim is that information is the weakest available lever, and here the paper has a better warrant than it does anywhere else. Bandura's change model puts information first and immediately discounts it, ranks mastery experience above the other three sources of efficacy belief, and states that saying one is capable is not the same as believing it. That literature also supplies the reason a recipient's silence is not agreement: efficacy beliefs preempt cost–benefit reasoning, so an option in a low-efficacy domain is excluded before it is weighed. **Its settings are schools, families and clinics, and it contains no study of adult professionals learning a technical craft**, which this paper concedes as an extrapolation rather than a finding.

**Third**, the platform workflow exists to make one durable claim physically observable inside an afternoon: a convention the tooling reads survives the person who wrote it, and a convention only a person reads survives exactly as long as that person's attention. The apparatus is an instructions file, a matching rule, and a captured request payload — and the failure mode it renders visible is that an unloaded specification and an absent specification are indistinguishable from the output.

The paper concedes that the entire module is inferred from an omission in its source; that no source behind this programme reports a customer's engineers becoming more capable by any route, or reports an engagement in which capability transfer failed; that the four-state vocabulary is a commercially interested practitioner model with no independent evaluation and one widely repeated claim withdrawn here on reading; and that the session's central artefact is a statement of intent whose only test arrives three sessions later.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A delivery team ending an engagement will be asked whether the customer can run the system, and will answer from the wrong evidence.

The available evidence is social. The receiving group attended the sessions, asked reasonable questions, nodded at the right moments and said they were comfortable. None of that bears on whether anybody can perform an act at a standard under load, and all of it feels like evidence because it is what the room supplied.

The team's default response to a gap, when one is admitted, is instruction. Instruction addresses one of the four states and is aimed at the other three routinely: at a group that has not been told the responsibility is theirs, at a group that does not want it, and at a group that has been instructed thoroughly and cannot yet act. **The characteristic outcome is more training and no change**, which is then read as a deficiency in the recipient.

There is a second problem underneath the first, and it is the one the platform anchor attacks. Even where the team correctly diagnoses a knowledge gap and correctly writes the standard down, **the artefact they leave behind is prose whose enforcement mechanism is a person's memory.** Nothing in a folder of runbooks and conventions pages is self-executing, so the whole apparatus degrades at the rate at which attention degrades — silently, unevenly, and invisibly to anybody who checks by reading the folder.

The session's job is to make the four states separately nameable before any intervention is proposed, and to leave at least one convention in a form that does not depend on anybody remembering it.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session assumes a handover with named owners from [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md), because a capability statement is written against what somebody has agreed to hold, and assumes the runbook and harness work from [S072](/wiki/seminars/S072.md) and [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md), which supply the acts.

It also assumes [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md), where the equivalence between a specification and a configuration file was established from the delivery side, and where the failure mode of a specification the executor never received was first observed. **This session is that claim arriving from the transfer side**, and a learner who has not held it will read the workflow as a tooling exercise.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 The four-state model, and its warrant, stated in full because it governs every use.**

The four-state distinction comes from a **practitioner change model published by the consultancy that owns its trademark and sells the training and certification in it** ([research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md)). The author is candid that it is not a breakthrough and offers it as a lens over existing practice rather than as a method. Its supporting evidence is of four kinds and no others: that firm's own benchmarking surveys of project teams, which are practitioner self-report with no methodology reproduced, no sampling frame and no independent replication; roughly twenty case narratives, all anonymised or drawn from public events; the author's own informal polling by show of hands; and pages of practitioner testimonial, which carry no evidential weight. **No independent evaluation of the model is cited anywhere in it.**

Its two structural claims — that the states occur in a fixed order and that all of them must be present — are argued from the meanings of the words, without hedge and without evidence.

**This licenses direction and mechanism only.** No ordering strength, no rate, no proportion, no magnitude, no timescale, and no claim that using the model improves outcomes.

**3.2 The correction this repository carries.** The model is widely summarised as holding that effort spent past the blocking element is wasted. **The book does not say that**, and this repository withdrew the claim on reading the text. The author's formulations are conditional — that a given effort will not by itself produce the change, or will not be productive until the blocking element moves — with a single absolute confined to instruction in the absence of willingness. Any use of the model in this programme carries the conditional form, and a submission that cites the model as evidence-based fails the evidence criterion regardless of the quality of its plan.

**3.3 One asymmetry inside the source, worth knowing before quoting it.** Its case narratives repeatedly land on unwillingness as the blocking state; the one systematic survey finding it reports on why people resist names not having been told. The book does not reconcile them. **Any claim about where changes most often fail must be sourced to one or the other and labelled**, and this programme makes no such claim at all.

**3.4 The better-warranted claim underneath the model: information is the weakest lever.**

This is where the module stops borrowing and starts standing on something. Bandura's change model, set out in the health chapter of the 1995 volume and stated generally, has four components in order — information, self-regulatory skills, efficacy, social support — and it discounts the first as it names it: **"factual information alone, much of which is usually redundant with what people already know, usually produces little change"** ([research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md](/research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md)). The efficacy component is built by "repeated opportunities for guided practice in applying the skills successfully in situations simulating those they are likely to encounter in their everyday life", and the stated failure mode when it is omitted is precise: "If people are not fully convinced of their personal efficacy they undermine their efforts in difficult situations and readily abandon the skills they have been taught when they suffer reverses or fail to get quick results."

That sentence describes a handover recipient in month two exactly.

Of the four sources of efficacy belief, **only one is explicitly ranked** — "The most effective way of creating a strong sense of efficacy is through mastery experiences. They provide the most authentic evidence of whether one can muster whatever it takes to succeed." The remaining three are enumerated rather than ordered, and this paper does not rank them. It also does not repeat the common summary that verbal persuasion is the weakest source; the volume places physiological and affective states fourth.

Two further passages bear directly on the session's artefact. **"The multiple benefits of a resilient sense of personal efficacy do not arise simply from the incantation of capability. Saying something should not be confused with believing it to be so."** And, more sharply: **"Efficacy beliefs preempt expectancy-valence analyses"** — options in domains of low perceived efficacy are excluded wholesale, "with little thought to costs and benefits". The consequence for the handover meeting is that **an absence of objection is not evidence of agreement**, because the recipient may never have treated the responsibility as a thing they could hold.

**The limits are load-bearing and are stated wherever this source is used.** The volume read is a conference proceedings edited by the field's founder, with no search strategy, no inclusion criteria and, across the editor's own forty-five-page chapter, no study whose result went the wrong way; several load-bearing results in it are cited as unpublished manuscripts. Its empirical settings are schools, families, clinics, migration and addiction treatment. **It contains no study of adult professionals learning a technical craft**, so this application is an inference. The construct is almost always measured by self-report immediately before a performance that is itself often self-reported. Directionality is conceded as unsettled in field settings by the editor and by one contributor. And the foundational 1977 paper is unread in this repository, so any claim resting on it alone is declared unverified. **This paper asserts no magnitude from the volume, and the repository's namespace rule prohibits one even though the volume was read in full.**

**3.5 Why the act must be written as an activity rather than as an objective.**

The entrustment literature supplies a writing discipline that is directly reusable and is not a theory ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). An entrustable professional activity is a bounded unit of practice with a beginning and an end that a supervisor could delegate whole. Its description template requires that a title **"should not contain an adjective"** and **"should not be phrased as an educational objective—it is just an activity"**, and that a specification-and-limitations section capture "no more and no less than what is exactly meant".

The adjective prohibition is the rule doing the most work here. An adjective smuggles a quality standard into the name of the thing, where it cannot be observed; the standard belongs in the specification and in the supervision level, where it can. *Understands evaluation* fails on both counts — it is an objective and it is unobservable. *Interprets a failing harness run and decides whether to pause the rollout* passes.

The same source records that **entrustment expires** where the activity has not been practised for a long time, which is the property of a handover that nobody writes down and which [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) is where this programme returns to it. **Its limits:** a clinical model with legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint; guidance from an advocate reporting adoption rather than effectiveness; and a full evidence standard — multiple trained observers producing documented, deliberated judgements — expensive enough that any course adopts a reduced version and must say so.

**3.6 Why the opening items are not scaffolded, and why exercise 3 is mandatory.**

The session's opening is a Productive Failure generation phase, and two of its author's stated design conditions bind here ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). **Do not scaffold the generation phase** — an experiment manipulating guidance during problem solving found that prompts and hints which reduced the failure rate reduced learning, which is why the release rule above gates §§4, 5 and 9. And **the consolidation phase must explicitly prompt comparison** — presenting correct and incorrect solutions side by side achieved nothing on its own, and only the condition instructed to compare them learned more. Exercise 3 exists to satisfy the second condition and would be ceremony without the instruction to name the difference.

**Its limits:** a trade book; school, university and laboratory evidence; the author's own concession that failure is sufficient but not necessary for the awareness mechanism; and no adult-professional study in the reference list. Applying it to working professionals is an extrapolation this programme concedes everywhere it uses the pattern.

**3.7 Why the convention is more durable than the document, and what warrants it.**

The claim is not derived from a study; there is no study. It is derived from an inspectable instance and an argument about enforcement.

The instance is an openly published agent configuration corpus read at a pinned commit ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)). Its organising claim is that a coding agent's behaviour is an engineering artefact to be authored, versioned, reviewed, packaged and distributed; it separates four artefact kinds by application rule, keeps authoring source apart from generated output, and — the property that makes it a teaching object rather than an example — **runs conformance checks against the standards themselves.** The corpus is also candid about the limits of its own gates: its behavioural graders are documented as testing output shape rather than correctness, its behaviour conformance is advisory by default and lacks calibration, and a check can be advisory at one tier while a scheduled run treats it as authoritative.

The argument is that a rule's survival depends on who discharges it. A rule discharged by a person surviving requires that person to read it, retain it, and choose it under deadline; a rule discharged by tooling requires only that the tooling load it. **That is an argument about enforcement mechanisms, not a finding about outcomes, and it is class 3.** Nothing measured anywhere establishes that teams with executable conventions outlive teams with well-written ones.

**The corpus's limits are extreme and travel with every use.** Perishability is total on paths, counts, artefact names and package versions, all of which moved during the reading. **Host and model dependence is absolute**, and the corpus says so in its own transparency note: every artefact is inert without a host that loads it, and a result on one client and one model is not a result about the corpus. Scale will overwhelm a small team. And a single vendor's opinion about agent configuration is not the field. **Nothing in it licenses a claim about learning; it was not built for a cohort and no learner study exists.**

**3.8 Why the convention has to be in place before the team leaves.**

The change literature supplies the ordering claim and the self-implicating one. Culture moves last rather than first: **"Culture changes only after you have successfully altered people's actions, after the new behavior produces some group benefit for a period of time, and after people see the connection between the new actions and the performance improvement"** ([research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md](/research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md)). And **"Behavior from important people that is inconsistent with the vision overwhelms other forms of communication"** — which is why exercise 6 turns the convention on the learner's own last fortnight.

**Its warrant is practitioner counsel and the author states it as such**: the book carries no footnotes or endnotes by design, draws no major idea from any published source except his own writing, and cites no evidence to bolster its conclusions. Every extended case is pseudonymous and untraceable. Its numbers, including the multiplier in one of its own chapter headings, are constructed illustrations rather than measurements, and none is reproduced anywhere in this wiki.

**3.9 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is moderate and split awkwardly: the four-state diagnosis is conceptually light, and the platform half carries real extraneous load in the form of host-specific loading rules that vary by editor and version. The design accepts that unevenness because the observation the workflow produces — an unloaded specification is indistinguishable from an absent one — cannot be obtained by argument. **This is a design judgement and carries no external warrant.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 Opening item 4 is the instrument.** *What would you do about each of those four, and are the four answers different?* The session is arranged so that most learners answer with four variations of instruction, notice it while writing, and have nowhere to hide it. The supplied situation then makes the point unavoidable by putting a capable, unwilling group in the room whose unwillingness is legitimate: they did not ask for the system and were not consulted, and no training plan addresses that.

Items 1, 2, 3 and 5 are not filler. They establish the contrast that makes item 4's collapse legible to the learner who wrote it — the same structural role the four surrounding items play at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md).

**4.2 The third group is not asked for and is required.** The managed-service supplier will hold the credentials, has attended nothing, and appears in nobody's stakeholder map because they are a contract rather than a person. **A capability statement that omits the party holding the credentials is a capability statement about the people who were pleasant to work with.**

**4.3 The standard must name the workload**, because almost everybody can perform almost anything when it is the only thing they are doing, and every act in this stage will be performed on a day when it is not. This is also where the efficacy literature's least-quoted finding applies: under taxing demand, those with a low sense of efficacy become "more and more erratic in their analytic thinking". The degradation is cognitive, not merely motivational, which is why a standard stated without a workload is stated about a different person.

**4.4 At least one act the learner believes will not be reached is required to be written down.** Some people, for some changes, do not acquire the ability — the borrowed model says so in terms, and it is one of the few places where a practitioner source is willing to be unflattering. A plan in which everybody succeeds has no failure state and therefore no decision attached to it.

**4.5 What the platform workflow is for, and what it is not.** It is not a tooling tutorial and it is not an endorsement of any configuration corpus. It exists to make one thing physically observable in one afternoon: **a specification that has not been loaded and a specification that does not exist produce the same output, and nothing in that output distinguishes them.** Step 5 breaks the convention deliberately for exactly this reason, and step 4's payload capture is the only evidence in the whole session that is not somebody's report.

**4.6 Step 6 hands the keyboard over, and that placement is deliberate.** The recipient writes the second instructions file while the learner watches. This is the module's first mastery experience in the technical sense — direct experience of succeeding at the task rather than of watching somebody succeed — and putting it here rather than at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) means the rehearsal three sessions later is not the recipient's first unaided act. **If the recipient cannot get the file loaded, the session has produced a real capability finding**, which is worth more than the artefact would have been and is recorded as such.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Instruction as the universal response.** *Countermeasure:* the statement must name the blocking element before proposing anything, and a willingness problem answered with a training plan fails the statement rather than the plan.

**The demonstration mistaken for capability.** Evidence of current state is an event the learner ran. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires evidence to be something observed of the recipient acting, and the review movement asks who would have to be watched doing what.

**The convention that never arrived.** The file is at the right path, the host does not load it, and every subsequent output looks correct. *Countermeasure:* step 4 requires a captured payload, and step 5 requires a deliberate breach to observe the difference. **This is the countermeasure most likely to fail in practice**, because payload inspection is host-specific and some environments do not expose it — see prediction 5.

**Capability located in one person.** *Countermeasure:* per-group assessment, and the requirement at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) that the rehearsal be run by whoever will hold the responsibility. **Partial**: a group of one is still recorded as a group, and the session has no mechanism for forcing depth where the customer has none.

**Willingness inferred from agreement.** Nobody objected, so everybody agreed. *Countermeasure:* the supplied situation makes the unwilling group articulate, which teaches recognition in the easy case. **Real unwillingness is usually silent and this session does not teach the silent case** — and the efficacy literature suggests the silence may not even be unwillingness, but an option never brought into consideration.

**Acts written as topics.** *Countermeasure:* the review movement's single question, which cannot be answered for a topic; and the no-adjectives rule, which is mechanical enough to apply without judgement.

**The learner's own non-conformance.** The convention being handed over was abandoned by its author under deadline. *Countermeasure:* exercise 6, which is mechanical and self-administered. **No countermeasure exists for the underlying incentive**, which is that the author's sponsor rewards delivery and not conformance.

Retrieved by [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md), which binds each named capability to real backlog items; by [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), where the acts become the observed acts; and by [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), where the gap between the stated capability and the observed capability is the evidence the entrustment decision rests on. It is retrieved again in Stage 9 at [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md), where adoption that cannot be mandated is the subject, and at [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md), where reinforcement and decay are — which is also where entrustment expiry, named in §3.5, becomes the topic rather than a footnote.

It retrieves [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) in the other direction. The specification-equals-configuration claim was made there with an agent as the executor; here the executor is a team, and the learner is asked to notice that the claim did not change.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No specification item. The capability statement contributes observation towards the entrustment decision at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), where what is read is not the statement's quality but whether the learner's account of readiness matched what was later observable.

**The statement is deliberately not marked**, because marking it would reward ambition. A learner who names a demanding act and later observes that it was not reached has produced better evidence than one who named an easy act and observed success.

**The captured payload is not assessed either**, and the reason is worth stating because it looks like the one objective artefact in the session. Assessing it would convert a diagnostic into a compliance item, and would reward learners whose recipients happened to use an editor that exposes request payloads. **What is carried forward is whether the learner knows which of the two situations they are in**, not which one they got.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the module is inferred from an omission, which is the weakest available form of argument, and this programme has now done it twice.**

At its strongest: a document's silence about capability transfer is evidence about the document. It may be silent because the practice happens and is not written down, because it is covered by a commercial agreement outside the corpus, or because it is somebody else's job in that organisation. Building an entire module on the inference, and then teaching learners that the organisation left capability as an unrecorded aspiration, states more than the evidence supports.

**This is conceded without qualification.** The module is class 3 throughout and every page says so. The rebuttal is confined to the standard of proof required for a design decision as opposed to a factual claim: the programme is not asserting what that organisation does, it is deciding what to teach, and the corpus supplies no mechanism for a learner to adopt. **The fix that was considered and declined** was to teach the capability question as an open one without proposing any method — no capability statement, no rehearsal, no disposal record — which was rejected because a stage that raises a question and supplies nothing produces agreement and no change, exactly as the corpus's own aspiration did. The rejection is itself an argument from the same absence, and the objection reaches it too.

One mitigation is offered and is not a rescue. The corpus's silence is not uniform: it contains one reflection that the customer should be left holding the cost-modelling capability because they will need it after the team disengages. **That establishes that the concern is felt and not procedurally held**, which is a thinner basis than a stated method and is not being presented as more.

**Objection 2: the four-state model is a consultancy product, and importing it into an engineering programme launders a commercial framework as pedagogy.**

At its strongest: the model is a registered trademark of a firm that sells certification in it. Its evidence is that firm's own surveys. Its ordering and completeness claims are argued from word meanings. Teaching it, even hedged, puts the vocabulary in learners' heads and they will meet it again in a sales context and recognise it as something their programme endorsed.

**Substantially correct, and the design's response is to make the laundering impossible rather than to deny it.** The warrant is stated in full in §3.1, the withdrawn claim is named in §3.2, the internal asymmetry is named in §3.3, and a citation of the model as evidence-based fails on the evidence criterion. The programme also has a positive reason for the import: learners will spend careers being handed frameworks by consultancies, and one worked example of reading such a framework fairly — taking the distinctions that hold, declining the numbers, locating the interest — is worth a session. **This model is a good specimen precisely because its author is honest about what it is.**

**Objection 3: the executable-convention claim is a preference dressed as an engineering finding, and the session's own platform anchor is the best evidence against it.**

At its strongest, and it is strong: the corpus held up as the exemplar is one vendor's opinion, read at one commit, on one host, with one model, with every path in it already stale; it contains substantial evaluation machinery and **no committed results whatever**; and its own documentation concedes that its graders test output shape rather than correctness and that its conformance checks are advisory and uncalibrated. A paper that cites that corpus as the demonstration that conventions outlast documents is citing an apparatus and calling it a result — which is the exact error [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) exists to prevent, committed four sessions later by the same programme.

**This is conceded, and the concession changes what the claim is allowed to be.** The corpus is not evidence that executable conventions produce better outcomes; nothing establishes that, here or anywhere, and §3.7 marks the claim class 3. What the corpus supplies is an existence proof that the equivalence can be taken seriously at scale, plus a candid account of its own limits that a learner can read. The argument for the convention is about **enforcement mechanism** — who has to remember for the rule to hold — and that argument is available without any outcome evidence at all.

**The declined fix was to drop the platform half and teach the four states alone**, which would have made the session's evidence position cleaner and is the option a strict reading of the objection recommends. It was declined because the diagnostic half produces a document, and a module whose thesis is that documents are not capability would then have produced only a document. **The cost of that decision is that the session's most memorable artefact rests on its weakest warrant**, and this paper records that as an accepted cost rather than a solved problem.

**Objection 4: an executable convention is a control transferred to people who cannot amend it, which is worse than a document they can ignore.**

At its strongest: a recipient team inherits a rule enforced by tooling they did not configure, whose loading behaviour they cannot debug, whose model they do not choose, and whose failure mode is silence. When the convention becomes wrong — and it will, because the system changes — they cannot tell whether it is misfiring or whether they are misusing the system. A document, for all its weaknesses, can be read, argued with and amended by anybody.

**Partially conceded, and it is the reason the session requires prose to ship beside the convention.** The document carries the *why*, which is what a recipient needs in order to amend the rule safely, and the session's standard states which artefact is doing which job. **The declined fix was to require the recipient to author every convention themselves**, which was rejected on time grounds — the last fortnight of an engagement does not contain it — and replaced with the weaker step 6, where the recipient authors one. **The residual risk is real:** every convention the learner authored is a control the recipient holds and did not build, and this session does not close that.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will propose an instruction-shaped intervention for at least three of the four states at opening item 4. *Instrument:* the written answers, coded per state as *instruction* / *authority* / *engagement* / *practice* / *other* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most first-draft capability statements will express at least one act as a topic, and the commonest tell will be an adjective in the name of the act. *Instrument:* the review movement's single question, recorded as answerable or not per act, together with a mechanical count of adjectives in act titles.

**Prediction 3.** The credential-holding third party will be omitted from first drafts more often than either named group. *Instrument:* presence of each of the three groups in the pre-review draft.

**Prediction 4.** Learners will record the willing group's current state more optimistically than the unwilling group's, at equal observed evidence. *Instrument:* current-state ratings against the evidence cited for each, read by an assessor blind to which group is which. **If this fails, the session's warning about mistaking willingness for ability is unnecessary at this stage** and should move to [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), where it costs less.

**Prediction 5.** A substantial share of learners will complete step 4 without obtaining a payload, and of those, most will record the convention as in force anyway rather than recording the absence as a finding. *Instrument:* the submitted workflow record, coded for whether a payload was captured and, where it was not, whether the omission is stated. **This predicts the failure of the session's own principal countermeasure**, and it is included because a verification step whose completion rate is unmeasured is an assumption. If it holds, step 4 needs a fallback that does not depend on host instrumentation.

**Prediction 6.** Learners who complete exercise 6 — checking their own last fortnight against the convention they are handing over — will revise the convention more often than those who do not. *Instrument:* the convention as submitted before and after the exercise, with a within-cohort split on completion. **This is the session's only direct test of the mechanism §3.8 rests on**, and a null result would mean the self-audit is decoration.

**None of these has been measured.** The register is machinery, not evidence, and a reader who mistakes its existence for a result about this session is making the error [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) is about.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That capability gaps have several distinct causes; that a delivery team's default response addresses one of them; that a capability claim with no named observation cannot be wrong and is therefore not a claim; and that a rule's survival depends on who has to remember it.

**What the evidence licenses.** The four distinctions are direction and mechanism from a commercially interested practitioner source with no independent evaluation. The claim that information is the weakest lever is well warranted and comes from a population unlike yours. The claim that conventions outlast documents is this design's argument about enforcement, with an existence proof and no outcome evidence. **Do not let the quality of the efficacy citations transfer to the platform claim; they are about different things.**

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper.** That is a rule of this repository rather than an accident of access, and it holds for the efficacy volume, which was read in full and whose effect sizes are recorded in the research layer precisely so that the prohibition is legible as a choice.

**The concession worth remembering.** This session produces a statement of intent and one file. Its only test is the rehearsal at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), and a learner who leaves here feeling that the capability problem has been addressed has mistaken a plan for an outcome — which is the precise error the module is about, committed on the material teaching it.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That being informed and being able to perform are different observables; that willingness is not produced by information; that a closed feedback channel makes a system's faults present as a person's failures; that performance under competing load differs from performance in isolation; and that culture follows altered behaviour rather than preceding it. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude, rate or ordering strength is asserted for any of these.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The existence of the module; the capability statement and its seven fields; the requirement that the standard name a workload; the requirement to include the credential-holding third party; the requirement to write down at least one act believed unreachable; the decision not to mark the statement or the payload; the placement of the recipient's first unaided authoring act at step 6 rather than at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md); **the entire claim that a convention the tooling reads is more durable than a document a person reads**, which is an argument about enforcement mechanisms and is supported by no measurement anywhere; and all six predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The artefact-kind separation into instructions, prompts, agents and skills; the separation of authoring source, packaging manifest and generated output, with the generated tree declared non-canonical; the presence of conformance checks applied to the standards themselves; and the corpus's own documented limits — that its behavioural graders are regex-based and test output shape rather than correctness, that behaviour conformance is advisory by default and lacks established calibration, and that a check can be advisory at one tier while a scheduled run treats it as authoritative — are recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md), read at commit `cf29fb45` with its published package version, on `main`, in a machine-assisted full-tree pass. **That note marks the material extremely perishable**: file counts, paths, artefact names and package version all moved during the reading. The revision read is not all-green; one check failed and the note records it rather than rounding it up.

**The silences that matter, and there are three.** First, **the corpus publishes no committed result of any kind** — no controlled study, no calibrated baseline, no outcome dataset, no human-rated sample — while its own prose uses the vocabulary of demonstrated benefit. It therefore establishes that the equivalence can be built and establishes nothing about whether building it helps. Second, **no vendor documentation known to this programme states how often an instructions file fails to be applied**, or under what host and model conditions, which is exactly the property step 4 of the workflow depends on; the loading rule is documented and its reliability is not. Third, and more broadly, **vendor enablement material across this industry publishes learning paths, certifications and completions, and publishes no measure of whether anybody could subsequently perform an act at a standard under load.** Completion is the published instrument and completion is a record of attendance. That is the same substitution this session exists to name, occurring at industry scale. If a vendor publishes an instrument that closes it, this paper records its version and what it still does not measure.
