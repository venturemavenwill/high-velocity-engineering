## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

The problem is not that students have never heard of prompt injection. Most have, and one or two can name evasions the substrate does not handle. **The problem is that what they hold is a catalogue of techniques**, and a catalogue is the wrong shape of knowledge for something that changes every time the day is taught.

So the day redefines the word. **An adversary is anyone holding a write path into a source your system reads.** That sounds so broad as to be useless until you notice what it catches: most of your adversaries turn out to be your own users, your own colleagues and your own components. Three of the day's human cases contain no attacker at all — the author acted correctly, the vendor acted correctly, the staff writer acted correctly — and all three behave exactly like one. **The discomfort is the content**, and the day is careful never to say anything about anybody's intentions.

Underneath that sits the claim students resist hardest: **in one channel, data and instruction cannot be told apart.** Everyone's first response is that a better delimiter, a better tag or a better system prompt would fix it, and that belief has a direct engineering consequence — it produces defences at the one place that does not survive a legitimate write path. So the day does not assert the claim. It makes the room try to recover where four pieces of content came from after the markers are stripped, and lets the error rate make the argument.

What follows is the only defence that holds. **Bound the capability, not the string.** You cannot filter your way out of a channel property, so you remove an action — and the discriminating question, the one a control cannot answer, is *who can no longer do the thing that action enabled?*

**What this paper concedes, and it is a real concession.** The day says the trust boundary is a property of the *production* assembler — where the concatenation happens, what provenance survives, which filters run — and then builds and defends against on a **copy**. Running against production would mean students causing a live service to do something its operator did not intend, which exceeds what the substrate's users have been told. Running on a copy **simulates the exact property the day says cannot be simulated.** Neither place is honest. The fix that removes the problem entirely — staff-recorded demonstrations against production — is declined because it also removes the day's only measurement of the belief the day exists to correct, and the paper says plainly that it does not claim the trade is obviously right.

This paper defends a day that redefines *adversary* from a person with intent to anyone holding a write path into a source the system reads, and defends the redefinition against the charge that it is so broad as to be useless. The design leans hardest on one mechanism: four pieces of content reaching one agent by four routes, printed both at source and as the model received them, executed against a disposable copy, with the room required to reconstruct provenance from the stripped versions before anything is named. That reconstruction generates the day's central claim — that data and instruction are indistinguishable in one channel — rather than asserting it, and it does so because the design applied to itself a rule that is pending and not in force. The platform turn adds a second generation the design did not previously have: a vendor's own injection control is split into a user-prompt shield and a document shield, which is a taxonomy of the assembler's choices dressed as a taxonomy of content. The principal risk is unchanged and is now sharper. The day's constructions are an attack and a bound on a system the programme owes a duty of care to, and §8 concedes that neither available option is honest: a production demonstration exceeds the substrate's published disclosure, and a copy is a simulation of the exact property the day says cannot be simulated.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The problem is not that students are unaware of prompt injection. Most will have seen it, and several will be able to name evasions the substrate does not defend against. The problem is that what they know is a **technique catalogue**, and a technique catalogue is the wrong shape of knowledge for a property that changes every delivery.

Three difficulties sit underneath, and only the first is cognitive.

**The perimeter model is a schema, not a gap.** Every student in the room holds a working model of security in which there is an inside and an outside, and controls are placed where they meet. That model is correct for most of what they have built and it is the reason phase 1 item 3 will produce filters. It is not wrong so much as inapplicable: an assembled context has no inside, because the sources that feed it were each authorised individually and the concatenation authorises none of them. Replacing a working schema is harder than filling a gap, which is why the day spends forty-five minutes producing a counterexample instead of five minutes stating one.

**The claim that data and instruction are indistinguishable in one channel is easy to say and hard to believe.** It sounds like a limitation of current models, and every student's first response is that a better delimiter, a better tag or a better system prompt would fix it. The belief has a direct engineering consequence — it produces defences at point 6's first place, which is the place that does not survive — and the design's own analysis says a belief with an engineering consequence, held confidently, is not dislodged by being told. This is the reason phase 3 carries a reconstruction task rather than a discussion.

**The definition of adversary is affectively uncomfortable in a way the day must not resolve.** If the adversary is anyone with a write path, then most of the day's adversaries are the substrate's own users, the students' own colleagues, and the students' own component. Students will experience the definition as either paranoid or as an accusation, and the design's answer is not reassurance: it is phase 3's C2 and C4 and the error clinic's staff-authored document, all three of which contain no attacker and behave exactly like one. The discomfort is the content.

Underneath all three is the module's structural condition, argued in [M16](/wiki/modules/M16.md): the cohort has had **one** prior seminar day of adversarial reasoning, at [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md), thirty-two days ago. The day is therefore doing a strand's foundational work — re-establishing that a threat has an actor — while also discharging a deferral and teaching a class-6 handover. §8 objection 2 examines whether it can.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

The day's element interactivity is concentrated in one place and it is the whole reason for the phase ordering. **Write path, provenance survival and capability bound interact and cannot be evaluated separately.** A path with a hostile writer and no capability is uninteresting; a path with a trusted writer, no surviving provenance and an unbounded capability is phase 3's C2. Students must hold all three per path, across five to twelve paths, which is where the load sits.

Management:

- **The context is printed rather than described**, so that phase 1's marking is done on an artefact that can be re-inspected. Transient presentation of a structure students must reason over would be a representation-side difficulty, which the programme's reconciliation rule forbids.
- **The four phase 3 cases are printed twice**, source and stripped, so that the reconstruction task is a comparison rather than a memory exercise.
- **Step 1's enumeration is worked with the room**, because the systematic omissions — inherited paths, vendor-controlled strings, the system's own output — are not discoverable by a student who does not yet know they are paths.
- **The boundary statement's form is supplied**; only its content is the student's.
- **Two things are built and both are small** — the step 3 demonstration and the step 5 bound, both on a copy with the harness supplied, and the second is a subtraction from a schema rather than a construction.

Expertise reversal begins at step 1 for the two or three students who have done security work professionally: for them the enumeration is a checklist and the guidance is redundant. The day's answer is step 6, which is where their fluency stops helping, and §5 records the specific failure that produces.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Marking a real context before any framework exists*, phase 1 item 1, with the answer physically present and unread.
- *Predicting your own marking*, item 4, which produces a calibration gap rather than a score.
- *Predicting what an agent will produce when asked to make your component injection-resistant*, item 5, against a question the room has just answered itself at item 3.
- *Committing a ranking before the agent's behaviour is shown*, phase 3.
- *Reconstructing provenance from stripped strings*, phase 3 task 1, which is the generation the day's central claim depends on.
- *Being asked which of a vendor's two shields could have applied to each case*, phase 3 task 3, where the honest answer for the payload is neither.
- *A payload with no outsider in it*, C4.
- *Being required to ship a subtraction*, step 5, where the obvious and well-documented move is an addition.
- *Naming an action you removed*, step 7, which forces the same discipline into the statement.
- *Handing the statement to somebody who must narrate a three-a.m. response*, step 8.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *A live adversarial exercise against production.* Excluded on duty-of-care grounds and examined at §8 objection 1, where the exclusion is shown to have its own cost.
- *A red-team-versus-blue-team format.* It produces engagement and measures the wrong thing: the winner is whoever is more inventive, and the day's claim is that inventiveness is not the variable.
- *Model-level defences and their evaluation.* Alignment training, refusal behaviour and safety tuning are [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md)'s and SG302's; this day treats the model as a component with a channel property.
- *Poisoning, extraction and exhaustion.* [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s, and named in point 9 rather than left implicit, because a day about adversarial input that did not say what it excluded would produce students who believe injection is the attack surface.
- *A generalised taxonomy of injection techniques.* Excluded on perishability grounds, with the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md)'s reasoning read to the room rather than applied silently.
- *Evaluating a content filter's accuracy.* Not available: no vendor on this stack publishes a detection or false-positive rate for any shield or filter, so an evaluation the students ran would be a measurement of the day's own attack set. Point 8 says so, step 4 requires the student to say so, and **the specification checks the saying rather than the measuring.**

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The day's frame — three exposure properties, three places for a defence, bound the capability rather than the string — is **safe for a single-principal agent whose sources you can enumerate and whose actions you can list, operated by a team that owns the assembler.** It is **unsafe in three specific conditions and each produces a confident-looking wrong answer.** Where the retriever's corpus is not controlled by the operator, the enumeration at step 1 is the hard part and the frame assumes it is done. Where two principals' content is assembled into one context, the capability bound is a per-principal question the frame has no expression for, and the artefact will be written as though there were one requester — that case is [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md)'s and the day says so. Where the component's actions cannot be enumerated because they are composed at run time by another agent, step 7's removed action is not well defined, and a student who writes one anyway has described a bound that does not hold. **In all three the day's repertoire produces an artefact that passes its own specification and does not describe the system.** This is stated to students at point 3 and point 6 rather than confined to this paper, and step 1's enumeration explicitly requires paths the student does not control, so that the first condition is at least visible in the artefact.

**The platform simplification carries its own scope statement and it is narrower than the frame's.** The day teaches a vendor's two-shield split as an instance of point 2, and the correspondence is **safe for reasoning about what a filter has been told about your content, and for the general claim that a control's correctness can depend on a decision its supplier did not make.** It is **unsafe in two directions.** It is unsafe as evidence for point 4: the product's inability to separate content is a product's inability, and a student who cites it as proof that models cannot has confused an implementation with a property, which is the same error [WP-016](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-016.md) §8 conceded about a prompt cache. And it is unsafe as any statement about how well the shields work: **nothing published states a detection or false-positive rate for either shield, so a request that passes one is a request that passed one.** The specification accordingly checks the capability bound and never the filter configuration, and step 4 requires any rate the student quotes from adversarial tooling to travel with the attack set that produced it.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **phase 3's C4**: the agent's own output, returning as untrusted content through the orchestration layer the student built two weeks ago.

The mechanism is not surprise for its own sake. It is that C4 is the only one of the four cases where the student is the writer. Every other case invites the response *we would filter that*; C4 invites no response at all, because there is nobody to filter and the component doing the laundering is the student's own. Competence is supported rather than threatened, because the component is good — the summariser works, and that is why it launders.

The second mechanism is **step 7's removed action**, which is the day's only genuinely costly demand. Removing a capability means telling somebody they can no longer do something, and phase 8 pools the removed actions with who lost each one. Autonomy is complete — the student chooses which action — and the choice has a visible consequence, which is what makes it a decision rather than an exercise. **Step 5 supplies its enabling condition**: a student who has shipped the subtraction and watched their own step 3 attempt stop working is in a materially different position from one who wrote the bound down.

Relatedness is supplied at step 8 by the rota member, who is present because they will receive the incident and not because the seminar needs an audience. The narration form matters affectively as well as instrumentally: a student who has heard a professional say *I would check this, and then I would stop* has received a vicarious mastery experience rather than an evaluation.

The affective risk is the definition itself, and it is real. A cohort told that their users and colleagues are adversaries will either reject the frame or adopt a posture. The countermeasure is that **every one of the day's three human cases contains no attacker** — C2's author, C3's vendor, and the error clinic's staff writer all acted correctly — so that the definition arrives as a description of write paths rather than as a claim about people.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items on a printed real context. Item 1 marks influence; item 2 enforces [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md)'s actor requirement; item 3 elicits the perimeter model in the student's own words; item 4 produces the calibration gap; **item 5 asks the same question as item 3 about a different respondent**, so that the day can report the room's model of enforcement beside the agent's. Items 3 and 5 are the two the day is measured on and both are administered before any instruction, per instrumentation rule 8.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals across two quarters, opening with actor D so that the day's broad definition arrives after the room has re-derived why the actor with no intent outranks the one with a plan. The bridge is answered by the room and produces the day's structural claim.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four contents, four routes, matched on target and varied on origin, **executed against a disposable copy rather than supplied as results**. Ranking committed before behaviour is shown. C4 is the payload, and **C2's requested action is a function of the [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) schema-reduction result**, which is this day's rule-6 discharge. **Task 1 — reconstructing provenance from stripped strings — is the generation that produces point 4**, and it exists because the design applied to itself the rule proposed at [WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 and argued again at [WP-057](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-057.md) §8 and [WP-067](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-067.md) §8. **That rule is pending and not in force**; the day complies with it voluntarily and the compliance is what turns point 4 from an assertion into a measured error rate. **Task 3 runs the same argument on the vendor's product**, and is the cheapest generation in the day: the shield taxonomy is on a documentation page the students fetch in ninety seconds and cannot be applied to C4 at all.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Nine points. Point 4 is generated, point 6 supplies the sorting frame for item 3, points 3 and 6 carry strong-form scope statements, **point 8 is the platform-specific content and carries a perishability marking, a named default and its own scope statement**, and point 9 discharges [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md)'s deferral while bounding the day's own claim.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Eight steps producing a handover with a warranty clause **and a shipped bound**. Step 1 worked; **step 6 unsupported**. Steps 3 and 5 are the constructions and both run on the copy. Step 8 uses an external observer per rule 1 and imports [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md)'s narrate-rather-than-approve form per rule 5, contingent on that day's reported outcome under the [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 amendment. **The artefact carries the prompt, intervention and frontier records** required by the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A staff-authored document that behaved as an injection. No attacker, no error, and the document was in the corpus because it was the most trusted source available. **Then the agent case: three real controls, correctly implemented, correctly cited, with the tool schema untouched.**

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Dual demand: construct an uncovered path, then read the partner's own capability bound back against it. A covered consequence is not a defeat, which is point 7 demonstrated on the student's artefact.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 1 against the true influenceable fraction; item 4 against item 1; **item 3 sorted into point 6's three places, with the cohort's agent outputs sorted beside it**; the phase 3 reconstruction error rate and the task 3 shield attributions; **the step 5 re-run outcomes**; the cohort's removed actions pooled with who lost each; **item 5 opened, with the predicted-a-failure and named-the-failure rates reported separately.**

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** Two or three will have done security work and will produce a fluent write-path map in fifteen minutes. Predicted failure: **fluency at step 7.** A practised engineer's repertoire is controls, and controls are additions; asked for a removed action they will supply a control with the word *removed* in front of it — a required confirmation, a rate limit, an approval step. Countermeasure: the step's clause requires the student to name **who now cannot do the thing the action enabled**, and a control has no such person. **Step 5's must-be-a-subtraction clause catches the same failure one step earlier and in a form the student cannot argue with**, because a filter that blocks their own step 3 string is trivially demonstrable as a filter. The rota member at step 8 is briefed to ask it directly, and the assessors treat an unanswerable version as a failed element rather than as a stylistic issue.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is that step 1's enumeration is not merely incomplete but structurally wrong — the student lists the user message and the documents and stops, because the tool outputs and the orchestration summary are not experienced as inputs. Countermeasures: step 1 is worked with the room precisely for this; phase 3's C3 and C4 are the two cases that name those paths; and the enumeration is scored on the presence of paths the student does not control rather than on count, so a short list containing an inherited path passes and a long list of user-facing surfaces does not.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** If a group ranks C4 highest before the behaviour is shown, the payload has failed and the design must not pretend otherwise. This is plausible for a group containing one of the professionally experienced students. Fallback: the group is given the fifth case held in reserve — **a retrieved document whose injected sentence is in the metadata rather than the body, and which the assembler includes and the group's own [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md) retrieval evaluation never scored** — and is asked to state which of their own evaluation harnesses would have seen it. None will have. The reserve case is not used generally because it requires the group to hold the retrieval evaluation in mind as well, which is a load the phase does not have room for.

**Affective failure.** Two, in opposite directions.

The first is **posture**: a student who leaves treating colleagues and users as suspects, which is unpleasant to work with and produces bad engineering. Countermeasure: all three human cases in the day contain no attacker, and phase 6 is explicitly about a trusted document. The instructor states once that the day has defined a write path and has said nothing about anybody's intentions.

The second is **fatalism**, and it is the more likely. A student who accepts point 4 — that the channel cannot separate data from instruction — can reasonably conclude that nothing works. **Point 8's second bullet makes this worse before it makes it better**, because the honest statement that no shield publishes an accuracy reads, to a twenty-year-old, as *the controls do not work.* The countermeasure is structural rather than reassuring: point 6 names three places, point 7 names the one that holds, and **step 5 requires the student to ship it and watch their own attack stop.** A student who has removed a capability and re-run their own demonstration has a defence they can describe and has seen it hold, and the day is arranged so that this happens after the fatalism rather than before it. **That ordering is the single strongest argument for making phase 5 a build**, and it is the reason the subtraction is required rather than encouraged.

## 6. Retention architecture

**Retrieves from** [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) at eight months, [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md) and [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) at days. The long retrieval is the load-bearing one: [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md)'s actor requirement is the only prior instance of adversarial framing in the programme, and the gap is the widest functional gap the schedule contains for a concept the day depends on rather than merely references. The design compensates with a scaffolded prompt at phase 2 — actor D is named in the item rather than left to recall — per the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](/wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md)'s constraint on retrievals older than two quarters.

**Retrieved by** [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) at one week (where the write path becomes a poisoning path and the day's exposure-estimate error selects the cases), [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md) at two weeks (where the capability bound meets authorisation and step 6's output is consumed directly), [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) at three weeks (where point 6's third place is shown to be tuned on the wrong distribution), [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) at four weeks (where a real incident's content path is reconstructed from a record that did not preserve provenance) and [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) at two quarters (where the boundary statement becomes a claim the safety case rests on, and the [Seminar Day Index](/wiki/program/08-Seminar-Day-Index.md) records S087 as retrieving S076–S079 as a block).

**[S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) at four weeks is the consequential retrieval.** Everything in this day is a prediction about what a record will contain; [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) is where the record is read.

Retained content is short and is a set of questions rather than facts: who can write here; how many hops; does provenance survive; what may this do while holding that; where is the enforcement and what happens when it is bypassed. Five questions.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 4.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment judgement.** One specification item in SG302 with IS302. The module's judgement is at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md).

Per specification rule 1 the checked elements are enumerated in the day and end with *nothing else is assessed*. Four hard requirements: a boundary statement without a named enforcing component fails; a capability bound with no removed action fails; a demonstration without a statement of what it does not establish fails, and a quoted attack-success rate without the attack set that produced it fails; an intervention record with no entries fails unless the artefact shows the design could have caught an intervention-worthy error. **Whether the demonstration succeeded is not assessed**, because a student whose component resisted their best attempt has produced evidence about their attempt and the specification must not reward the reverse. **Which platform controls are configured is not assessed either**, for the reason §3.2's platform scope statement gives: no accuracy is published for any of them, so a configuration is not a claim.

**The cross-course join is a checked element.** Point 9 defers infrastructure write paths to SG302 and step 1 requires the student to name the material they used. This is the form the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s cross-course deferral rule requires, and it is applied here without prompting because [WP-044](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-044.md) §8 recorded the identical failure on this material's own predecessor day: [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) deferred infrastructure security to SG201 with no join, that omission is where most real incidents originate, and the register records the finding as **Fixed**. Instrumentation rule 5 requires the remedy to be applied wherever the same problem recurs, and it recurs here.

**Rule-6 discharge, as required by [WP-073](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-073.md) §7.** [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) named this day's phase 3 as the response occasion for its step 4 schema-reduction cost, specifying that the injection cases include one that the cohort's narrowest schema still admits, selected against the reduction this cohort was least willing to make. **The element exists as phase 3's C2, and it is a content-selection function rather than an acknowledgement: the action C2's injected sentence asks for is derived from the [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) result, so the printed case differs with what the cohort declined to give up.** It is not a mention; it is the case that makes point 7 necessary, because a room whose accepted restriction does not stop C2 has been shown the limit of restriction on its own evidence. The obligation is discharged.

**Older forward link discharged.** [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) point 3 named this day as the home for attacks specific to generative components. The obligation predates instrumentation rule 6 and is discharged in the rule's spirit: the deferral's subject is this day's entire phase 4, and **point 9 re-bounds the claim in the same paragraph that discharges it**, so that the discharge does not silently become a completeness assertion — which is the failure mode the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s strong form was written to prevent.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments, both responding inside this module.

- **The item 1 exposure-estimate error** responds at [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) phase 3, whose four traces are selected against whichever write-path class this cohort most systematically failed to mark. The response is a case-selection function rather than an acknowledgement, which is the property that makes it a response under rule 4.
- **The step 7 capability-bound quality** responds at [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md) phase 5 step 4, where the authorisation position must state which actions are unavailable to a request carrying untrusted content. A student who removed nothing here arrives there with nothing to state, and the element is checked.

**[WP-077](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-077.md) §7 and [WP-078](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-078.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

Programme instrumentation: the item 1 marking error; the item 4 calibration gap; the item 3 distribution across point 6's three places; the phase 3 provenance reconstruction error rate and the task 3 shield attributions; the step 5 re-run outcomes; the pooled removed-action list; **and the item 5 frontier pair — how many students predicted the agent would err, and how many named the error.** The frontier data are a sample and the day says so, per instrumentation rule 3: one cohort, one substrate, one component class, on a prompt chosen by staff because a documented failure is available for it.

ABET and CS2023 contribution: PO2 and PO4. Dispositions: **humility**, at step 4, where the honest statement is that one demonstration establishes one path; **integrity**, at step 7, where naming who lost a capability is the costly half of the answer.

**What this day does not license anyone to conclude.** It does not license a claim that a student's component is defended. It licenses a claim that the student can state where their component's trust boundary is, who can write across it, what the component may do while holding what crosses it, and what happens when the enforcement fails. **Those are four statements about an artefact and none of them is a security property of a running system**, which is [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md)'s object and is not established here.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day's constructions are an attack and a defence, and neither place to run them is honest.**

At its strongest: the day claims that the untrusted-content boundary is a property of the *production* assembler — where the concatenation happens, what provenance survives, which filters run. Steps 3 and 5 then demonstrate an injection against **a copy** and ship a bound to **a copy**. A copy has a different corpus, a different tool set, a different traffic mix and, most importantly, a boundary the students configured for the exercise. **The day has therefore simulated the exact property it says cannot be simulated**, and a student who succeeds at step 3 has established something about a system nobody operates.

**The practical turn makes this worse in one specific way and the paper says so.** Step 5's re-run gives the student the experience of watching their bound hold. On a copy, the bound holds against the attack the student wrote, in an environment the student configured, with a corpus the student knows. **That is the most persuasive moment in the day and it is the least warranted**, which is the same shape as the concession [WP-016](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-016.md) §8 made about a demonstration whose rhetorical force exceeded its evidential force.

The alternative is worse. Running the demonstration against production would mean students deliberately causing the live service to do something its operator did not intend. The [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page's disclosure — added after [WP-035](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-035.md) §8 found the programme running an experiment on people who had not been told — covers *deliberate disturbance during announced windows* and instrument adoption. **An injection demonstration is neither.** It is not degradation; it is unintended behaviour, potentially visible to a user, potentially producing an output attributable to the service. The disclosure would have to be extended before the demonstration could run, and extending it is a programme-level decision this paper cannot make.

So the day runs on a copy and accepts the loss. **The rebuttal is partial and the concession is specific:** steps 3 and 5 establish that the student can identify an exposed path, construct content that exploits it, and remove the capability that makes it consequential — three real competences and the three the specification checks; they establish nothing about the production boundary, and step 4 requires the student to say so, which converts the limitation into an assessed element rather than a caveat.

**The cheap fix is to drop the demonstration entirely** and supply staff-run recordings of successful injections against the production system, taken under staff authority where the duty-of-care question is answerable. It would cost nothing, it would remove the honesty problem completely, and **it is declined.** The reason is that a recorded demonstration cannot produce the item 1 exposure-estimate error against a path the student chose, and that error is the day's only measurement of the belief the day exists to correct. A student watching a recording learns that injection works. A student who picks the path they judged most exposed, fails, and discovers the exposure was elsewhere has learned something about their own model of their own system. **The design has traded an honesty problem for a measurement, has recorded the trade, and does not claim the trade is obviously correct.**

**Objection 2: the day is doing a strand's foundational work and its own work at once, and it has five days to do both.**

At its strongest: the [Outcomes and Accreditation Map](/wiki/program/01-Outcomes-and-Accreditation-Map.md) concedes that security is not distributed throughout the programme and first appears at Q5. The concrete consequence lands here. **The cohort has had one seminar day of adversarial reasoning**, eight months ago, and this day assumes that the actor requirement is a reflex, that thinking about who benefits is habitual, and that a room asked to enumerate write paths will produce actors rather than hazards. **None of those assumptions has evidence.** A programme with security distributed from Q2 would arrive here with a cohort for whom phase 1 item 2 is trivial, and phase 1 could spend its twenty-five minutes on the channel property instead.

The rebuttal is that the design has done what is available to it: phase 2 opens on actor D rather than on a definition; the §2 contingency branches the day if item 2 shows the requirement is not functioning; and SG201 and SG302 carry the material at course length with an assessed join. That is not nothing.

**It is also not a rebuttal to the objection as stated**, which is about curriculum structure and not about this day. The honest position is that **this day would be a better day in a programme that had introduced adversarial framing in year one**, that the design knows this, that the [Module Map](/wiki/program/07-Module-Map.md) names the strand's density as the design's thinnest area, and that no change available inside M16 fixes it. What can be said is that the cost is now specific rather than general: it is one phase 1 item on every day of the module, a contingency branch that would not otherwise be needed, and a phase 4 that must bound its own claim because the room has no other day to bound it against.

**Objection 3: the reconstruction task proves less than the day claims.**

At its strongest: phase 3 task 1 asks students to reconstruct provenance from stripped strings, and a high error rate is presented as evidence for point 4 — that data and instruction are indistinguishable in one channel. **Those are different claims.** Task 1 measures whether *humans* can recover origin from text with markers removed. Point 4 is about what a model conditions on. A student could reasonably say that the model has more context than the group did, that the group had ninety seconds, and that the inference does not follow.

The rebuttal is that the task is not offered as proof of point 4 but as a demonstration that **provenance is a property of the assembler and not of the content** — which is the part of point 4 that students resist and the part with the engineering consequence. If origin cannot be recovered from the string, then any separation the system enforces must have been recorded outside the string, by a component that can be named. That is exactly what step 6 then demands. The stronger version of point 4, about model conditioning, is asserted rather than demonstrated, and this paper does not claim otherwise.

**Objection 4: teaching a vendor's control catalogue on a day whose thesis is that the control catalogue is the wrong shape of knowledge.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the practical turn creates. §1 argues that the students' problem is that what they know about injection is a *technique catalogue*, and that a technique catalogue is the wrong shape of knowledge for a property that changes every delivery. Point 8 then spends eight minutes on one vendor's shields, their split, their defaults and their alert taxonomy. **That is a control catalogue**, it is exactly as perishable as the technique catalogue the day rejects, and a student will reasonably conclude that the shape of knowledge the day wanted was *catalogue of defences* rather than *catalogue of attacks*.

The rebuttal is that point 8's content is selected to be self-undermining rather than to be learned. Its first bullet says the shield split is a decision your assembler makes; its second says the alert taxonomy publishes no rate; its third says the useful sub-feature is off by default. **None of the three is a fact about how to configure anything**, and the specification checks none of them: no product name, no configuration and no filter appears on the checked-element list, and step 4 requires any rate quoted from platform tooling to travel with the attack set that produced it. What is assessed is the boundary statement, the removed action and the shipped subtraction — all three of which survive the vendor.

**That is a real answer and it is not a complete one, and the incompleteness is conceded.** A room shown a named product for eight minutes will retain the product. The design's own [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) exists because retention does not respect a lecturer's framing, and asserting that this instance is different because the framing is unusually careful is the assertion the register was written to distrust.

**The cheap fix is one clause in the perishability register and it is made.** The register's durable column is now written as **boundary reasoning and measurement obligations rather than as facts about named attacks or named products** — *state the default you did not choose*, *a control whose accuracy is unpublished licenses no claim about what its silence means* — so that what the student is told to retain is a question rather than a feature, and the feature sits visibly in the perishable column beside it. What remains unfixed is that the design has no way to measure which column a student actually retained, and §9 prediction 5 is the closest available proxy.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At phase 1, students will mark under half of the externally influenceable portion of the printed context, and the systematic omissions will be the tool outputs and the orchestration summary rather than the retrieved documents. *Instrument:* item 1 markings scored against the context's true influence map, reported **by source class rather than in aggregate**. The class breakdown is the claim; an aggregate under-marking rate would be consistent with several accounts, and only the class pattern distinguishes the perimeter model from ordinary incompleteness.

**Prediction 2.** More than two thirds of item 3's enforcement answers will fall in point 6's first place — restricting what reaches the assembler. *Instrument:* item 3 free-text answers coded into the three places by two raters blind to each other. **If the answers are already distributed across the three, the perimeter model is not the entry state the day is built on**, and phase 3's ordering is solving a problem the cohort does not have.

**Prediction 3.** Groups will rank C1 highest and C4 lowest before the behaviour is shown, and C4's provenance reconstruction error will be the highest of the four. *Instrument:* committed rankings against the agent's measured behaviour, and task 1's per-case error rate. **If C4 is ranked accurately, the payload has failed** and the reserve case in §5 is the fallback rather than the design.

**Prediction 4.** At step 7, more students will produce an addition described as a removal than will produce a removal, and the discriminating question — who now cannot do this — will be unanswerable for the majority of first attempts. *Instrument:* step 7 entries coded for whether a person or role lost a capability, with the rota member's phase 5 challenge recorded. **If most students produce genuine removals unprompted, point 7 is not the difficulty this design treats it as**, and phase 5's fading should move to step 7 instead of step 6.

**Prediction 5.** At item 5, a large majority will predict that the agent's injection hardening will be wrong, and a small minority will name that it will add a filter and leave the tool schema untouched. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded for generic-versus-specific failure identification, against what each student's agent actually produced, and against the point 6 place its output falls in. **The gap between the two rates is the day's frontier measure.** If the specific rate is high, the room's model of the agent is better than the design assumes and the phase 6 agent case is redundant; **if the room's own item 3 answers and the agent's outputs land in the same place in the same proportions, the day has a finding it did not design for** — that the tool's failure mode and the room's are the same failure mode — and it should be reported as the module's most useful result rather than as a coincidence.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That an adversary is anyone holding a write path into a source your system reads — which makes most of them your own users, colleagues and components. And that data and instruction are indistinguishable in one channel, so the defence that survives is a bound on the capability rather than a filter on the string.

**What the evidence licenses.** The retrieval and corpus apparatus the day attacks, and its licensing discipline, are **class 1**. The shields are **class 4**: two detectors are documented, and the sub-feature that makes a model treat document content as less trustworthy is off by default — while **no detection rate, false-positive rate or confidence scale is published for either shield**. That such systems act on instructions arriving in retrieved content is **class 2**, direction and mechanism only. The definition of adversary, the three exposure properties and the three places a defence can sit are **class 3**.

**What to do with it.**

- **Enumerate write paths, including the ones you do not control** — tool outputs and your own system's summaries are inputs.
- **Bound the capability, not the string.** Name the action you removed and who lost it.
- **Pin the revision, host and model** — see the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). A declarative boundary acts only through a host, so a result observed against an unpinned configuration is an anecdote about an unknown object.
- **A quoted attack-success figure travels with the attack set that produced it, or not at all.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes, unresolved, that neither place to run the day's constructions is honest: a demonstration against production would exceed the substrate's published disclosure, and a copy simulates the exact property the day says cannot be simulated. **Hold the design to that.** Watching your own bound hold on a copy you configured is the day's most persuasive moment and its least warranted, and the fix that removes the honesty problem is declined because it removes the measurement.
