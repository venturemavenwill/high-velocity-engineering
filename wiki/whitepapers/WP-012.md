## In plain terms

A gate that can only ever find fault with somebody else is not a gate.

The corpus behind this stage has a readiness rubric for its engagements. Almost every condition in it describes the customer: their engagement, their openness, their willingness to invest. Alongside it sits a prepared answer to the objection the organisation expects most — *your tooling improved and our delivery did not speed up* — and that answer reattributes the shortfall to the customer's own organisational constraints.

Put those two artefacts side by side and something becomes visible that neither shows alone. **The model has arranged its evidence so that a disappointing result confirms the thesis instead of challenging it.** If things go well, the model works. If things go badly, the customer was not ready. There is no observation left that counts against it.

That is the single most teachable structure in the whole collection, and this session is where it is taught, because a readiness gate is the smallest artefact in which the whole pattern is visible.

The session then adds the column the corpus does not have and nobody writes: the platform premises. A charter's decision always rests on claims about the product — what can be deployed where, what can be published how, what a connector supports — and those claims are the ones a practitioner carries from the last engagement rather than checks for this one. **They are also, uniquely among the gate's contents, checkable in advance at almost no cost**, which is why a gate without them is not merely incomplete but lazy.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching readiness through its failure mode rather than through its checklist, and defends adding a class of condition the source material does not contain.

The argument runs in four parts. **First**, a gate composed of the assessed party's dispositions is unfalsifiable in advance and infinitely available as an explanation afterwards, and pairing such a gate with a prepared objection brief that reattributes disappointment to the same party produces a model no observation can disconfirm. **Second**, the corrective is not a better attitude but a formal property: a condition must be checkable by somebody who was not there, must state its own limits, must name the evidence that would settle it, and must expire. **Third**, at least one condition must point inward, because a gate that cannot implicate its holder cannot change its holder's behaviour. **Fourth**, the premises most likely to be wrong are the platform ones, they are the cheapest to check, and an instrument now exists that makes checking them a step in drafting rather than a separate task — which changes readiness from a judgement into a dated record.

The paper records an amendment made while writing it: the gate as first designed had two scopes, customer and us, and therefore encoded the same blind spot as the specimen it criticises — a practitioner could satisfy it in full while holding an unchecked platform assumption that would collapse the session's central demonstration. The correction adds the platform-premise scope and is documented at §4.5 rather than applied silently.

The paper concedes that customer readiness is a real determinant of outcome and that the session's complaint is about the form of the criterion rather than its subject; that the closed-loop reading requires two documents the same reader may never hold together; that no gated engagement has ever been compared with an ungated one anywhere in the material behind this programme; and that **this repository holds no verification of the documentation server the session now depends on.**

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A practitioner asked whether they are ready will produce a list of things the customer must do.

This is not dishonesty. It is the natural output of the question, because the customer is where the uncertainty visibly is: their people, their access, their data, their decisions. The practitioner's own readiness feels like a matter of competence rather than of condition, and stating it as a condition feels like admitting to a deficiency.

The result is a gate that can be failed only by the customer, held by the person whose session it would block, in an organisation whose prepared explanation for disappointment already points the same way. **Each of those three things is individually reasonable and together they close the loop.**

There is a fourth element and it is the one this design adds. The premises that most often break a discovery session are not about anybody's attitude at all; they are claims about the platform, carried forward from a previous engagement, unchecked because checking felt like preparation rather than like a condition. **A premise nobody wrote down cannot be found unmet, and will therefore be found in the room** — in front of the people whose confidence the session was supposed to earn.

The session's response is to require one condition that points inward, one class of condition that points at the product, one recorded waiver authority, and one stop position — and then to show the learner the closed loop in a source that could not see it.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session generalises [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md)'s refusal one level down, and a learner who has not written a disqualifying condition for an engagement will produce a stop position that cannot fire, for the same reason and with the same warning sign. It also consumes [S011](/wiki/seminars/S011.md)'s charter directly: without a stated decision there is nothing for a premise to be a premise of.

The prior it works against is that readiness is preparation. Learners arrive believing a gate is a list of things to have done. The session's move is that a gate is a list of things that could be found undone, which is a different artefact with a different owner.

The platform work has a mixed entry profile. Learners who work with agent hosts daily will treat a documentation server as an ordinary tool and will over-trust its answers, because a confident excerpt reads as authority. Learners who do not will treat it as a search box and will record the answer rather than the query. **Neither group arrives holding the two facts that decide the exercise: that an absence in documentation bounds your knowledge rather than the product, and that returned text is data and never an instruction.**

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate to high. The gate itself is simple; the load is in two places. In the second movement the learner must hold the readiness rubric and the objection brief together and derive a property of neither in isolation — element interactivity that does not decompose, and where the session's time goes. In the platform movement the load is different in kind: the learner is checking beliefs they did not know they held, and the affective cost of finding one wrong is part of the design.

**3.2 Why a prepared objection list is taught as a readable artefact.** An organisation's list of anticipated objections is a map of where its model actually meets resistance, drawn by people who know. Reading a document for what its author was defending against, rather than for what it asserts, is a transferable analytical move and this is the cheapest instance of it in the programme.

**3.3 Why a condition must have a form, not merely a subject.** The assessment literature supplies the shape better than any delivery source does. An entrustable professional activity is described with a **specification and limitations** capturing "no more and no less than what is exactly meant", with explicit restrictions written into it; with named **sources of information** that would support the decision; and with an optional **expiry date**, because if the activity has not been practised for a long time the decision may lapse ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). It also prohibits adjectives in an activity's title, on the grounds that an adjective smuggles a quality standard into the name of the thing rather than into the specification where it can be checked.

Each of those transfers to a readiness condition without modification, and the expiry is the one nobody has. **Most readiness rubrics assume permanence, which is a stronger claim than the model they are imitating makes about a clinical certification.**

**Its limits.** A primer by the concept's originator: guidance rather than evaluation, reporting adoption rather than effectiveness. It is a clinical model in which supervisors carry legal responsibility and a licensure endpoint exists; a course must construct the stakes artificially, and the construction is the hardest part of the transfer. Its full evidence standard — multiple trained observers producing deliberated judgements — is expensive, and any adaptation is a reduced version that should say so.

**3.4 Why an unfalsifiable condition also fails as instruction.** There is a second argument for checkability that does not depend on epistemics at all.

The error-management training literature identifies a boundary on learning by exploration: where the task supplies clearly interpretable feedback, exploratory training with error encouragement outperforms guided instruction, and **where the task's feedback cannot be readily interpreted the advantage is not observed** ([research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md)). Take direction only and the analogy is exact: a gate made of conditions that cannot answer back generates no feedback at any point in its life, so nothing in it can ever be found wrong and it cannot improve.

The same source supplies a second and sharper caution about self-evaluation, which this design has taken seriously elsewhere: the advantage of exploratory designs is **absent when measured during training and present afterwards**, so a programme that evaluates itself inside the session will see nothing and will conclude, wrongly, that its design is not working.

**Its limits, which are substantial.** Every effect in that pool is relative to another training method; **no study used a no-training control**, so nothing speaks to absolute effectiveness. **No study measured on-the-job performance** — every outcome is a laboratory transfer task, almost all of them software training, mostly with students. The feedback-clarity moderator did not reach conventional significance, its coding was the least reliable in the paper, and the authors state explicitly that it cannot be concluded the method works only under clear feedback. The transfer to readiness gates is this design's inference and is not the paper's claim.

**3.5 Why the waiver must carry a name.** An unrecorded decision cannot be reviewed. This is a property of records, not a claim about behaviour, and the paper does not assert that named waivers occur less often.

**3.6 Why the gate acquires a platform column, and what the instrument is.**

The premises a charter rests on are not all of one kind. Some are about people and are genuinely uncertain until the day. Some are about the product and are, right now, written down somewhere by the vendor.

The published comparison material is dense with premises worth checking before anybody stands in front of anybody ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). The rapid setup topology "lacks network isolation" where the controlled one gives "fine-grained control over data, security, and networking", so a demonstration on the fast path is not a demonstration of the thing anybody would run. Distribution is constrained by a decision taken at the start: "Only agents built with the Teams SDK, Microsoft 365 Agents SDK, or Foundry can be published to the Microsoft Commercial Store with the Agents Toolkit." Rich card rendering has a version ceiling in one of the tools. And the whole family states that the options "aren't mutually exclusive" and compose through MCP, so the correct unit of the question is the component rather than the system.

A documentation MCP server changes the economics of checking these. The durable idea is that the check happens inside the act of drafting rather than as a separate task an hour later, which is the difference between a premise that gets checked and one that does not.

**The hazard arrives with the capability and must be taught in the same breath.** Returned documentation sits in a model's context looking exactly like an instruction. The strongest available statement of the correct posture treats fetched pages, uploaded documents, source repositories, tool output and issue bodies as **data rather than authority**, and requires embedded instructions to be reported rather than obeyed ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)). The same corpus supplies this session's thesis in one line: **the existence of a control is not evidence that the control works.** Its own untrusted-content boundary is a declaration whose effectiveness depends on the host attaching it and the model complying, neither of which the file can guarantee — and its evaluation lanes are candid that a check may be advisory at one tier and authoritative at another, which looks identical in a green summary.

**Its limits.** Read at a pinned commit at which one of its own checks was failing, recorded rather than rounded up. Perishability extreme — counts, paths and versions moved while it was being read. It contains benefit vocabulary its own evidence does not support, and **it commits no results at all: a grader exists, no result corpus does.** Nothing in it licenses a claim about learning.

**And the absence that bounds the whole movement.** **This repository holds no research note on the Microsoft Learn MCP Server.** Its coverage, freshness, ranking behaviour and failure modes are unverified here. The design's response is to require the learner to record the query and the read depth rather than the answer, so that the artefact remains checkable even if the instrument is not.

**3.7 Why the gate is written before the session rather than after.** A criterion produced after the outcome is known cannot discriminate. The same argument returns at [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) about stopping rules and at [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md) about baselines. **It is one claim appearing three times and the design says so each time.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 Opening item 2 is the instrument.** Learners mark their own conditions customer, us or platform, and count. The count is the finding, and it is produced by the learner about their own work before anything is taught, which is why it lands.

**4.2 The specimen is the session's centre.** The rubric and the objection brief are given together, and the learner derives the closed loop themselves rather than being told it. The design's judgement is that a learner who has assembled the argument will recognise the shape elsewhere — in a value framework at [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md), in a maturity claim at [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md) — and a learner who has been shown it will not.

**4.3 The stop position is checked against [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) deliberately.** A practitioner who can refuse an engagement but not a session has learned an instance rather than a skill, and the check is cheap.

**4.4 What the platform workflow is for.** Not tooling competence. It makes one thing observable that prose cannot: **that a premise has a date.**

Three properties of the exercise carry the weight, and the third is the one that transfers furthest. The learner records the **query** rather than the answer, so the check is reproducible by somebody who distrusts the instrument. The learner records the **read depth** — excerpt or full page — because an excerpt is a weaker read and the difference belongs in the record rather than in the practitioner's memory of how confident they felt. And the learner records the **silence**: what the documentation does not say about the premise. The silence field is the one learners skip and the one that pays, because a page describing a capability without stating its limits has told them less than it appears to, and because **an absence bounds the reader's knowledge rather than the product** — a distinction most practitioners collapse in the direction that suits them.

**4.5 A defect found in this session's own design, and the amendment.**

The gate as first specified had three parts and two scopes: conditions marked customer or us, a waiver rule, a stop position.

**A learner could satisfy it completely while holding an unchecked belief about the platform that would collapse their session's central demonstration** — and the gate would have no place to put that belief, no requirement to state it, and no mechanism to find it unmet. The session's whole argument is that a criterion which cannot be found unmet cannot implicate anybody, and the design had built a gate with a category of premise that could not be found unmet by construction, in a stage whose subject is instruments that cannot fail.

The defect is instructive because it is the specimen's defect in a different direction. The corpus's rubric points only at the customer; this design's first gate pointed at the customer and at the practitioner's preparation and at nothing else, and therefore treated the product as a fixed background rather than as a set of dated claims. **A model that treats the platform as stable is making a perishability error that this repository exists to prevent**, and it made it in the session about readiness.

The amendment adds the platform-premise scope, produce-item 2, workflow steps 1 to 5, and the expiry convention. It does not add an assessed item at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md), because assessing the premises would produce learners generating premises rather than examining assumptions.

**The amendment is recorded rather than silently applied.** Assume there are others this paper has not caught.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The outward-only gate.** The most likely outcome is a gate with no inward condition, or one so trivial it cannot be unmet. *Countermeasure:* the two-sentence exercise — write the customer-caused and the us-caused failure explanation, then check the gate would have caught the second.

**The waivable stop position.** A learner writes a stop position and then, under the peer question, discovers they would proceed anyway. *Countermeasure:* the peer review's single question, which is deliberately about the reviewer's willingness to stop the session rather than the author's.

**Readiness theatre.** The gate is produced because it is required and will never come back unmet. *Countermeasure:* none available here. It is tested at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md), where an unmet condition is introduced on the morning of the session and the learner must decide and record.

**The specimen read as an indictment.** A learner concludes the source organisation is dishonest. *Countermeasure:* the paper's §8, and the repeated insistence that the material is thoughtful, experienced and in places genuinely wise. **A structure that cannot be disconfirmed does not require anybody to have intended it.**

**Absence read as denial.** A learner records that the documentation does not support a premise when what they observed is that it does not mention it. *Countermeasure:* the silence field is a separate column from the finding, and the failure-mode list names this case. **This is a weak countermeasure against a strong temptation**, because the learner is under time pressure and a negative finding is more useful to them than an unresolved one.

**The oracle.** A learner treats the documentation server's answer as the check rather than as a pointer to a page. *Countermeasure:* the recorded query and read depth, which make the shortcut visible in the artefact. Prediction 5 in §9 predicts this countermeasure failing.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md), [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md), [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) and [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md).

The closed-loop argument is the stage's longest-range export. It is installed here about a readiness rubric, applied at [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) to a question bank, and applied much later to a value framework that generates numbers while asserting none.

The platform column exports two habits with different ranges. The **expiry** returns wherever this programme records a verified claim, which is everywhere. The **silence** field is the direct ancestor of the evidence-status discipline every whitepaper in this programme closes with — the requirement to record what a source does not say, not only what it does — and a learner who has written one for a product page has performed the move this repository is built on.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence.** Two specification items at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md): that the gate contains at least one condition about the learner's own organisation and that it was checked, and that every waiver carries a named person.

**Never assessed:** whether the learner would in fact have stopped the session; whether any platform premise was correct; and the number or coverage of the premises recorded.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: customer readiness is a real determinant of outcome, and calling it unfalsifiable is a debating trick.**

At its strongest: whether the customer has engineers who will attend, data they can share and a sponsor who will decide genuinely does determine whether a session is worth running. Those are facts about the customer. A design that treats every customer-facing criterion as suspect will produce practitioners who run sessions they should have declined, in the name of methodological purity.

**Substantially correct, and conceded.** The objection defeats a strong reading of the session and the session does not hold the strong reading. The rebuttal is about form rather than subject: *the customer is engaged* is a disposition and cannot be checked; *the four named engineers have accepted* is a fact about the customer and can be. **The declined fix is to drop the inward-condition requirement and simply require checkability.** It is declined because checkable outward conditions still leave the practitioner unassessed, and the loop closes on assessment rather than on checkability.

**Objection 2: the closed-loop reading requires two documents the same reader may never hold together.**

At its strongest: the readiness rubric and the objection brief were written by different people for different audiences at different times. Deriving a property of the model from their conjunction attributes to an organisation a coherence it does not have, and the corpus is on its own account a stratum that contradicts itself in many places.

**This is the strongest objection available and it is not fully answered.** The design's position is that a model's evidential structure is a property of the artefacts practitioners actually receive, whoever wrote them and whenever — a practitioner holding both is in the closed loop regardless of anybody's intent. **Whether that position is right has not been tested and the learner should be told it is contestable.** **The declined fix is to present the two artefacts separately, in different sessions**, declined because separating them would reproduce the exact condition under which nobody notices.

**Objection 3: the platform column makes readiness a documentation exercise, and documentation is the least reliable thing in the room.**

At its strongest, and it is the objection the §4.5 amendment most deserves: vendor documentation lags the product, is written by people with a commercial interest, publishes no adoption or failure data, and — in the family this session uses — was read here through search excerpts rather than full pages, with several comparison tables truncated. Worse, the checking instrument is a documentation server this repository has never evaluated, whose retrieval behaviour is unknown, and whose output arrives inside a model's context where it is indistinguishable from instruction. A gate that says a premise was checked will be trusted more than one that says nothing — and the confidence will have been manufactured by a tool nobody characterised.

**Partially conceded, and this is the amendment's real cost.** Three defences, none complete. The artefact records the **query and the read depth**, not the answer, so a reader who distrusts the instrument can re-run the check rather than inherit the conclusion. The **silence** field forces the learner to state the boundary of what they found, which is the discipline that stops a retrieval result becoming a fact. And the untrusted-content posture is taught in the same movement rather than later, so the learner meets the hazard and the capability together. **The declined fix was to have learners check premises by reading documentation directly and never register the server** — keeping the discipline and dropping the instrument — rejected because the check that is not adjacent to the drafting is the check that does not happen, which is the whole reason platform premises go unwritten in the first place. **The cost of that rejection is exactly the manufactured confidence this objection names, and prediction 5 is the only thing that would detect it.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** On opening item 1, most learners will produce no condition marked *us*, and a larger majority will produce none marked *platform*. *Instrument:* the marked list, counted by scope per learner before instruction.

**Prediction 2.** Stop positions written before the [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) comparison will be judged incapable of firing more often than those written after it. *Instrument:* the peer's binary judgement, collected at both points in the session.

**Prediction 3.** Learners who derived the closed loop themselves will identify the same structure at [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) more often than those who were shown it. *Instrument:* unprompted identification at [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md), against a record of which route the learner took here. **This requires a deliberate split and the design does not currently create one, so the prediction is unmeasurable as the session stands.** That is recorded rather than fixed.

**Prediction 4.** Waivers recorded during [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md) will more often lack a name than lack a reason. *Instrument:* the submitted gates, coded for presence of name and presence of reason.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of submitted gates will record a finding without a reproducible query, a read depth, or both — despite all three being required fields. *Instrument:* the platform-premise rows, coded for completeness by a second reader. **This predicts the failure of §8's third countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 6.** The silence field will be the most frequently empty field in the gate, and where completed will more often record a denial than an absence. *Instrument:* the silence entries, coded *absent* / *records an absence* / *records a denial* by a second reader. **If denials dominate, the session has taught learners to over-read documentation and the movement should be rewritten around a page that omits something obvious.**

**Prediction 7.** Learners instructed to compare their item-2 count against the taught criterion (exercise 5) will submit gates containing a fixable inward condition more often than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* a second reader's binary judgement, split within cohort on exercise-5 completion. **The same prediction is made at [WP-010](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-010.md) and [WP-011](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-011.md), so a null across all three would indict the comparison pattern rather than any one session.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a gate composed of the assessed party's dispositions cannot fail informatively; that a prepared objection brief pointing the same way closes the loop; that a well-formed condition has a specification, a named evidence source and an expiry; and that the premises most worth checking in advance are the ones about the product, because they are the only ones somebody has already written down.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The argument is structural: it says what the rubric cannot do, not what a better rubric achieves. **No gated engagement has been compared with an ungated one anywhere in the material behind this programme.** The error-management boundary condition is carried at the level of direction only and its own authors decline to state it firmly. The assessment framework reports adoption rather than effectiveness. The platform documentation describes what is offered and evidences no outcome. **And the documentation server the workflow depends on has never been evaluated by this repository at all.**

**The concession worth remembering.** Customer readiness is real. The session's complaint is about the form of the criterion and the direction of the assessment, and a learner who hears it as *the customer is never the problem* has heard it wrong. And note §4.5: the first version of this gate treated the platform as a fixed background in a programme whose central methodological commitment is that the platform decays in months. **The failure the session names is one the session committed.**

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That the corpus's readiness rubric is composed almost entirely of customer dispositions; that its most heavily prepared objection is that tooling improved without delivery accelerating, and that the prepared answer reattributes the shortfall to the customer's organisational constraints; that its stated prerequisites are followed by an offer to proceed regardless; that it contains no exclusion criteria and no declined session beyond a single recorded refusal on responsibility grounds; and that its antipattern warning signs are observable in advance. All recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md), resting on a complete reading of access-controlled material. **No public reader can check that note against its source.**

That a well-formed activity description carries a specification and limitations capturing no more and no less than is meant, names the sources of information supporting a decision, prohibits adjectives in its title, and may carry an expiry because entrustment lapses through disuse, is recorded at [research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md) from a complete reading of the open-access primer — **which also records that the primer reports adoption rather than effectiveness and makes no empirical claim that the model outperforms what it replaced.**

That the error-management literature finds no advantage where task-generated feedback cannot be readily interpreted, and that the advantage is absent when measured during training and present on later transfer, is recorded at [research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md) from a complete reading of the paper — **which records that the feedback moderator did not reach conventional significance, that the authors decline to state the boundary firmly, that no study used a no-training control, and that no study measured on-the-job performance.** No magnitude from it appears here.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That a criterion formed after an outcome is known cannot discriminate between outcomes; that a decision without a recorded owner cannot be reviewed; that a check adjacent to the work is performed more often than one requiring a separate task. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The requirement for an inward-pointing condition; the addition of the platform-premise scope and its expiry convention; the requirement that a waiver name a person; the requirement to record query, read depth and silence rather than the answer; the claim that an absence in documentation bounds the reader rather than the product; the claim that a practitioner who derives the closed loop will recognise it elsewhere; the consistency check against [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md); the §4.5 amendment and its scope; the rejections of the declined fixes in §8; and all seven predictions in §9, one of which is explicitly unmeasurable as the session currently stands.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** That the basic setup is for rapid prototyping and "lacks network isolation" while the standard setup provides "fine-grained control over data, security, and networking" in public and private networking variants; that "Only agents built with the Teams SDK, Microsoft 365 Agents SDK, or Foundry can be published to the Microsoft Commercial Store with the Agents Toolkit"; that Adaptive Cards in one of the compared tools are "limited to schema 1.6 and earlier"; that the product family is "designed for small-scale deployments, such as department-level solutions"; and that the build options "aren't mutually exclusive" and compose through MCP — all recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md). That fetched pages, uploaded documents, source repositories, tool output and issue bodies are to be treated as data rather than authority with embedded instructions reported rather than obeyed, that a gate may be advisory at one tier and authoritative at another, and that the maintainers state their own behavioural checks test output shape rather than correctness, are recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md), read at commit `cf29fb457b0fe62745bb71592ec9394b834957f4`.

**The silences that matter.** **None of the consulted platform pages was fetched in full**; all were read through search-returned excerpts, so page structure is reconstructed and several comparison tables were truncated in the excerpts read. **No adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no outcome evidence accompanies any recommendation in that family**, and no non-Microsoft alternative is compared anywhere. The documentation states that the fast topology lacks network isolation and says nothing at all about **migrating a workload from it to the controlled one**, which is the transition a discovery workshop's proposal almost always implies; it states the publishing constraint and says nothing about what happens to an agent already built on a path that cannot publish. The agent-configuration corpus was read at a single pinned commit at which one job was failing, **and absence of a result corpus at one commit is not proof of absence in the project.** Its untrusted-content boundary is a declaration and not a demonstration; whether it holds depends on the host and the model, and the repository says so. **Finally, and most consequentially for this session: the Microsoft Learn MCP Server has no note in this repository at all.** Its retrieval behaviour, its index freshness and its failure modes are unknown here, and every claim in this session about the instrument is a claim about what the learner records rather than about what the server returns.
