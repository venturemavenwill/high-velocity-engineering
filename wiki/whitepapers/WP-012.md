# WP-012 — Ordering, Searching and the Cost of Comparison

**Seminar day:** [S012](wiki/seminars/S012.md) · **Module:** [M03](wiki/modules/M03.md) · **Quarter:** [Q2](wiki/quarters/Q2.md)
**Strands:** CF · MS · SY · **Complexity class:** 1
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Foundry embedding deployment as a remote comparison (`POST {endpoint}/openai/v1/embeddings`) · **MCP servers:** Microsoft Learn MCP Server · Azure MCP Server (Read only = true)

## Abstract

This paper defends decomposing algorithmic cost into three separately measurable terms — operation count, per-operation cost and data movement — rather than analysing in units of comparison, and defends the crossover condition as the day's principal deliverable in place of a ranking. The first argument is that comparison-count analysis silently fixes one term at unity, which is defensible for primitive types and false for nearly every comparison an engineer actually writes; and that students who cannot decompose will optimise whichever term their training made visible. The second argument is that a ranking is an answer with an unstated scope, while a crossover is an answer with its scope attached, and that stating the conditions under which one's own recommendation expires is a professional competence the programme assesses directly at S065 and S088. The paper additionally defends extending the day's controlled dimension past the point where the algorithm is the answer, by making the fourth comparison function a call to a Foundry embedding deployment: at that cost the correct move is to stop comparing that way, which is precisely the conclusion a count-denominated analysis cannot reach. It also defends the paired-improvement requirement as the only structural guarantee that both terms are engaged. It concedes that the day's measurement practice teaches a methodology the programme has not yet justified, and that the platform's batching limits are taught as a boundary rather than as a mechanism the students could have predicted.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Algorithm analysis is denominated in comparisons. Students learn that one sort performs $n \log n$ comparisons and another performs $n^2$, and they compare the two functions. The analysis is correct and its unit is a stipulation: it assumes a comparison costs a fixed amount, and it is precisely as informative as that assumption is true.

For integers the assumption is nearly true. For almost everything else it is not. A locale-aware string comparison performs normalisation and collation-table lookups. A comparison on a computed key may run arbitrary code. A comparison requiring a lookup in another structure introduces an indirection whose cost depends on locality. A comparison requiring a remote call is not the same kind of object at all. In each case the count is unchanged and the cost is not, and the ranking derived from counts can be simply wrong.

The consequence is a characteristic and expensive error: students optimise the term their training made visible. They reduce comparison count, measure on data where comparisons are cheap, observe an improvement, and ship a change that is slower where it matters. The error is not carelessness — the reasoning is correct within its model — and it cannot be fixed by more careful counting.

The day's second problem is one the programme needs solved early. Engineering recommendations have scopes, and students state recommendations without them. "Use a hash table" is an answer; "use a hash table below roughly this size, above which the rehashing cost exceeds the lookup saving for this access pattern" is an answer with a scope. The second form is what a colleague can act on, what a monitor can check, and what an engineer can be held to. It appears here for the first time as step 6, and it is the same competence assessed at S065 as a cost argument and at S088 as a residual-risk statement.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Moderate to high, concentrated in step 1.** Decomposing an observed cost into three terms requires isolating each, which means designing three measurements that vary one thing — and students have taken exactly one measurement in their lives. The interactivity is genuine: the terms are not independent, since reducing movement often changes the comparison count and vice versa, which is why the decomposition is a skill rather than an arithmetic.

Management:

- **Step 1 is worked with the room for one term,** which is the day's only fading and is placed at the highest-interactivity point.
- **The apparatus is supplied,** as at S011, with the three-term instrumentation already built.
- **Phase 3 varies exactly one dimension.** Four comparison functions, one dataset, one task, three algorithms — an unusually clean controlled contrast, and deliberately so, because the day's central relation is only visible when everything else is held still.
- **Three algorithms, not a family.** Enough for a reversal.
- **The module dataset persists,** paying comprehension load once.

The unmanaged load is the same one S011 accepted: measurement variability, without a framework until S027. The instructor supplies the explanation directly.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Locating the crossover,* phase 3 and step 6. The day's defining difficulty and its first threshold demand.
- *Paired improvements,* step 3 — one per term. Structurally forces engagement with the unfamiliar term.
- *Numeric predictions,* step 4. Substantially harder than directional ones and failed often.
- *The partner's dating demand,* phase 7 — when will this crossover be crossed.
- *A three-item pretest varying one dimension,* which produces the reversal rather than asserting it.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Implementing sorting algorithms.* Refused; CF102's conventional work.
- *Deriving comparison counts.* Supplied. The derivation is standard and is not the day's content.
- *Explaining locality.* Named at point 5, deferred to S016.
- *A fourth term.* The decomposition stops at three. Its omissions are now stated at point 2 rather than left implicit; see §8 objection 1.
- *Explaining what the embedding model does.* Refused, as at S011. Case D is a comparison with a price and a latency; how the price is earned is IS202's work.
- *Assessment stakes on the same day.*

**The scope statement on the platform instance, in strong form.**

The day teaches two simplifications that could travel into unsupervised work, and both carry safe and unsafe conditions stated at phase 4.

The first is the three-term model itself. It is **safe** for reasoning about a decision the engineer controls — which algorithm, which comparison function, which precomputation — because the three terms are the ones that move under those decisions and are separately measurable with the instrumentation students have. It is **unsafe** as an account of why an arbitrary program is slow, because branch prediction, allocation behaviour, instruction-level parallelism and contention are excluded and any of them can dominate. The boundary is now stated to students at point 2 rather than only in this paper.

The second is the platform instance. The endpoint's limits — 8,192 tokens per input, 2,048 inputs per array, 300,000 tokens aggregate, HTTP 400 above it — are **safe** to reason with against this endpoint at this API version, and are the day's concrete demonstration that a batch has a cost of its own. They are **unsafe** in three conditions, all named: **at any version other than the one in front of you**, since these are documented values that carry no stability guarantee; at any other model family, since the limits are documented for the embedding models and not as a platform property; and as a guide to any other service's batching behaviour, since the shape recurs and the numbers do not. The artefact element enforcing the boundary is step 6, whose crossover statement fails if it is expressed as a number without the condition that generated it.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **a reversal produced by changing one thing**, and it is the cleanest demonstration in the module.

Phase 1's three items are the same question asked three times with one dimension varied. Students answer the first confidently and correctly, the second with hesitation, and the third wrongly or with a changed answer they cannot justify. That progression, inside twenty-five minutes and inside one student's own responses, is a more persuasive argument for the three-term model than any exposition.

Phase 3 extends it with measurement and adds the crossover demand, which changes the affective character: locating a threshold is a puzzle with a definite answer, and groups compete to bracket it. This is the module's most engaging phase by observation.

Step 6's expiry statement carries a different and more uncomfortable motivation. Students are being asked to weaken their own recommendation, immediately after producing it, and the discomfort is the point — it is the same discomfort as S009's not-entitled-to-conclude sentence and S007's naming of what was made expensive. By S012 this is the fourth instance of the pattern, and the design's expectation is that it is becoming familiar rather than aversive.

Competence is protected by the fact that phase 3's first two comparison functions produce stable, correctly-predicted rankings. The model works before it is shown to be incomplete.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Contrasting cases inside a pretest: one question, three comparison costs. Item 4 asks not whether the answer changed but whether it changed for the right reason, which separates students who tracked the varying dimension from those who guessed after losing confidence.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** An answerable bridge locating a sorting recursion's well-founded measure, which makes S008 load-bearing for today rather than merely prior. The alternation of answerable and unanswerable bridges continues.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** One dimension varied across four levels, three algorithms, measured adjudication, then a threshold demand. The design's cleanest controlled contrast, and all four levels are executed by the groups rather than supplied as results. The crossover estimate before the measured crossover is supplied is a prediction event in its own right and is the day's second commitment. Case D extends the dimension past the algorithm's reach deliberately: it is the only level at which the correct answer is a change of method rather than a change of algorithm, and it is also where the aggregate-limit 400 arrives, which the design treats as the phase's second and better payload because the group produces it themselves while doing the right thing.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Point 1 is the retained sentence and includes movement, which students omit. Point 2 is new and states what the three-term model omits; see §8. Point 4 states the comparison-count model's scope honestly and in its favour, which is the same protection S011 point 3 provided and is more necessary here because the day attacks the model more directly. Point 7 elevates the crossover to a design object and names its three later appearances. Point 8 makes precomputation an instance of S011's pair rather than a separate technique, and case D makes it the instance students will actually ship. Points 9 and 10 are the platform layer: 9 carries the perishable limits with their strong-form scope, 10 names four defaults nobody in the room chose, of which the MCP retry policy is the one with the largest effect on a caller and the smallest chance of being noticed.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Six steps on a real substrate ordering problem, ending in a shipped ranker. Step 1 is the hard part and the only faded one. Step 3's pairing is the structural guarantee. Step 4's numeric predictions are the calibration instrument. Step 6 is the payload. The three artefact elements are the Copilot Practice Standard's; the intervention record's demonstration route is specified concretely — a deliberately oversized batch whose 400 the ranker must surface — because a generic detectability clause cannot be marked.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** Two disclosures. An optimisation that was correct, measured, and wrong in production, whose value is that the engineer did everything the standard method requires — the model was incomplete, not the reasoning. Then the agent case, in which the same failure is produced by the *prompt*: an instruction that made the count salient produced a change that optimised the count, and a review that inherited the instruction's frame. That inheritance is the phase's transferable claim and it is not obvious to students, who expect agent errors to be errors of generation.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Dating the crossover. Deliberately parallel to S011's realism demand, and the parallel is not remarked on, because the design wants the structure to become invisible. The clause that an unreachable crossover *strengthens* the recommendation is stated explicitly, because students expect it to be a failure.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Prediction ratios rather than errors, because the interesting property of a numeric prediction is its order of magnitude and a distribution of absolute errors would hide that.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with systems or performance experience will hold the three-term model already, at least informally, and will predict phase 3's reversals. Predicted failure: they produce good step 3 improvements and a vague step 6, because practitioners rarely state expiry conditions and their experience does not supply the habit. Countermeasure: step 6 is the specification's weighted element and phase 7's partner is briefed to demand a date rather than a condition, which is harder and which experience does not answer.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has taken one measurement now has to take three that isolate separate terms. Countermeasures: instrumented apparatus; step 1 worked for one term; a persisting dataset. Residual risk: their step 1 decomposition may not separate cleanly, in which case their steps 2 to 6 rest on a bad decomposition. The instructor is briefed to check step 1 outputs at the phase's midpoint rather than at its end, which is an intervention the design relies on and cannot guarantee at scale.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** The first two comparison functions are expected to be predicted correctly by most groups. The reversal at the third or fourth is robust. Fallback if a group predicts everything including the crossover: ask them to construct a comparison function for which no reversal occurs at any cost, and to say why — which requires reasoning about the movement term and is genuinely hard.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **the day read as an argument that theory is useless**, which is S011's objection-1 risk compounded by a second consecutive day of measurement defeating analysis. Two days in a row where the analytical answer loses to the measured one is a pattern students will generalise, and the generalisation is wrong. Countermeasures: point 3's honest scope statement; the fact that phase 3's crossover is *located analytically* before it is measured, which demonstrates analysis doing work measurement cannot; and an explicit instructor statement that the crossover cannot be found by measurement alone, since measurement gives points and the crossover is a condition. That last point is the design's strongest available corrective and it is stated once, which is probably not enough.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence.

One specification item in CF102 requiring the separated three-term measurement, the committed dominance prediction, paired improvements with numeric predictions, measured effects from the built ranker, the stated crossover, and the three artefact elements.

The distinctive clause: **an improvement proposed without a numeric prediction fails.** Directional predictions are free and uninformative; a number is a commitment that can be wrong, and being wrong by a factor of ten is a different finding from being wrong by a factor of one and a half. The clause is the calibration series' mechanism at this point in the programme.

The second distinctive clause: **an intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the shipped ranker surfaces the aggregate-limit 400 rather than swallowing it. The exemption is specified as an artefact property rather than as a claim, because a student cannot be marked on whether an error *would* have been detectable.

Programme instrumentation: step 4's prediction ratios are the cohort's quantitative-calibration baseline, carried to S020, S029 and S064; step 6 specification failures measure whether expiry conditions are being produced; the phase 3 crossover estimates against measured crossovers are the threshold-reasoning baseline for S058; and the phase 1 Copilot prediction, scored at phase 8, extends the frontier map begun at S011. Rule 4 branch for the frontier item: a cohort that predicts the agent will attack the count *and is right* needs no change; a cohort that cannot predict which term the agent will attack receives an additional prompt-framing exercise in the S012 between-day set, and the result of that is read at S045.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the three-term decomposition is not exhaustive, and the day presents it as though it were.**

At its strongest: total cost is not count times cost plus movement. Branch prediction, allocation and collection behaviour, instruction-level parallelism, contention with other work on the machine, and the interaction between algorithm structure and the compiler's ability to optimise all contribute, and some of them dominate in realistic cases. The day's model is an improvement on the one-term model it displaces and it is still a simplification, and students taught a three-term model will do exactly what the day criticises — optimise the terms they were given and mis-model the rest. The day therefore reproduces its own target error one level up.

The rebuttal is that this is the correct criticism of any model taught at any level, and that the relevant question is whether the chosen simplification is a good place to stand. The design's claim is that it is, for two reasons. The three terms are **separately measurable with student-accessible instrumentation**, which the omitted effects mostly are not — a student can isolate comparison cost from movement cost, and cannot isolate branch misprediction without tooling and knowledge they will not have until Q4. And the three terms are the ones that **change under the decisions students make**, whereas most omitted effects are consequences of those decisions rather than independent choices.

**The concession stands and the fix is now made.** This paper previously conceded that phase 4 stated the decomposition without a scope condition, which was precisely the failure the day criticises in comparison-count analysis at what is now point 4 — an inconsistency in the design rather than an omission, with the fix specified and unimplemented. **Point 2 now states what the model omits and why, with safe and unsafe conditions, and is annotated at the point of change.** The correction is recorded here rather than quietly absorbed, for two reasons. The gap survived several revisions of this paper while being described in it, which is evidence for the propagation habit instrumentation rule 5 exists to name. And [WP-014](wiki/whitepapers/WP-014.md) §8 identified the same error in the same module at its five-question-type taxonomy and called it a pattern in the design; that finding is unaffected by this repair, because a pattern of presenting tractable enumerations as complete is not refuted by repairing two of its instances.

**Objection 2: the day teaches a measurement methodology it has not justified and will contradict.**

At its strongest: students are asked to isolate three terms by varying one thing at a time, to trust the resulting numbers, to predict effects numerically and to be assessed on the accuracy of those predictions — all before any instruction on measurement error, run-to-run variability, confounding, or what a single observation licenses. They will take one measurement per condition, treat it as the value, and be graded against it. This is not merely premature; it actively installs a practice that S026 to S029 will have to dismantle, and dismantling an installed practice is harder than building a correct one.

This objection the design does not fully answer, and it is the same structural problem WP-011 conceded, worse here because this day's measurements are finer-grained and more variable. Three mitigations exist. The apparatus performs repeated runs and reports a spread rather than a point, so students see variability even though they cannot yet analyse it. Step 4's assessment is on *prediction ratios* rather than on matching measured values, which is robust to moderate measurement error by construction. And the phase 3 contrasts are built around large-margin reversals rather than narrow ones.

None of this justifies the ordering. The honest characterisation is that Q2 requires students to measure sixteen days before the programme teaches them what a measurement is, that this is forced by the constraint that CF102 precedes ME101, and that the design has chosen to accept an epistemically premature practice rather than to remove measurement from Q2 entirely. The alternative — analysis-only in Q2 — was rejected because it would leave the constant-factor claims unevidenced, which is a worse failure. That the least-bad option is still bad is the accurate statement, and S026 should open by explicitly revisiting the S011 and S012 measurements as instances of what it is about to teach. That connection is currently listed in S026's retrieval sources and is not built into its design. **The platform layer makes this worse and the design says so:** a network call has run-to-run variance an order of magnitude beyond anything on the machine, and case D's measurements are the least stable numbers a student will have seen.

**Objection 3: the aggregate-limit payload is a trick, and it teaches a boundary rather than a competence.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection aimed squarely at the practical turn: the design is pleased with itself for arranging that a group which correctly optimises the count runs into HTTP 400. But the 400 is not a discovery about cost decomposition. It is a documented service limit that the group could have read in ninety seconds, and the reason they did not read it is that the design deliberately did not tell them. What the group learns is that this endpoint has a 300,000-token aggregate limit — a fact with a shelf life measured in months, which the perishability register itself concedes. Meanwhile the minutes spent on the error come out of the crossover work, which is the day's actual and durable content. Worse, the payload rewards a student for having a *fact* rather than for having a *method*: the group that read the documentation first looks slower and is in fact the one behaving correctly, and the design has arranged its most memorable moment as a punishment for the right behaviour.

**This objection is conceded in its second half and the cheap fix is named.** The first half the design rejects: a documented boundary encountered while doing the right thing is a better teacher than the same boundary read in advance, and the shape — *a batch has a cost that no item in it has* — is durable even though the number is not. The second half is correct and uncomfortable. Under the current design the group that consults the Learn MCP Server before batching is disadvantaged in the phase's most memorable moment, and that is an incentive pointing the wrong way in a programme whose whole claim about agents is that you ground before you act. **The cheap fix is to make the grounding the credited act rather than the avoided one:** phase 3's closing task asks, of every group, *what did you not know that you could have looked up, and what would it have cost to look it up?* — and a group that looked it up first answers it best. This costs one sentence, it is in the design, and it converts the payload from a trap into a demonstration. What remains unfixed is that the number is perishable and the day spends real minutes on it; the design accepts that cost and records it as accepted.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Phase 1 item 4 will show that a substantial fraction of students who changed their answer between items 2 and 3 cannot state the varying dimension as their reason. *Instrument:* item 4 responses coded for correct identification of the varied dimension, conditioned on whether the answer changed.

**Prediction 2.** Step 4 numeric predictions will be wrong by more than a factor of two for a majority of the cohort on the movement-reducing improvement, and by less than a factor of two on the count-reducing one. *Instrument:* prediction ratios by improvement type. **The asymmetry is the claim: students have a model for counts and none for movement.**

**Prediction 3.** Group crossover estimates in phase 3 will be systematically biased in one direction rather than symmetrically distributed. *Instrument:* estimated against measured crossover, examined for directional bias. A systematic bias identifies a specific mis-modelled term and is more useful than the accuracy figure.

**Prediction 4.** At S058, students will spontaneously frame the recall-latency trade as a crossover location problem, at a higher rate than a comparison cohort. *Instrument:* an S058 protocol coded for unprompted crossover framing. Untestable until a variant delivery exists.

**Prediction 5.** In phase 1, a large majority will predict that Copilot attacks the comparison count, and a large majority will be right — but fewer than half will name movement as the term it never mentions. *Instrument:* sealed predictions coded for the named term and the named omission, against the agent's actual output. **The asymmetry is the claim: the frontier is predictable in its successes and opaque in its silences, and it is the silences that ship.**

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** Contrasting cases with a single controlled dimension, prediction before adjudication, retrieval practice at expanding intervals, cognitive load and element interactivity, worked-example fading, and error management training are cited for **direction and mechanism only**. No effect size is asserted for any of them.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.**

*What the documentation says.* Microsoft Learn states that an embeddings request returns **HTTP 400 when the sum of input tokens exceeds 300,000, even if every individual input is under the per-input limit**; that the maximum input length for the current embedding models is **8,192 tokens**; that an array of inputs may hold at most **2,048** entries; and that callers should keep total tokens per minute below the quota assigned to the deployment ([Generate embeddings with Azure OpenAI](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/foundry/openai/how-to/embeddings#best-practices)). The same page's troubleshooting section maps 401 to authentication, 403 to the missing `Cognitive Services OpenAI User` role, 404 to a malformed endpoint or an invalid deployment name, and 400 to the request body and its token counts. The v1 REST reference documents `encoding_format` with a **default of `float`** and `base64` as the alternative ([Azure OpenAI Embeddings, API version v1](https://learn.microsoft.com/rest/api/microsoft-foundry/azureopenai/embeddings)). The Azure MCP Server documents its global retry parameters with defaults of **3** maximum retries, a **2**-second initial delay, a **10**-second maximum delay, **exponential** retry mode and a **100**-second network timeout ([Azure MCP Server tools](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/developer/azure-mcp-server/tools/#tool-parameters)).

*At what version and date.* All pages retrieved 2026-07-26. The REST reference is at API version `v1`. The Azure MCP Server tool-parameter page carries no version stamp in its own text.

*What the documentation does not report that a reader of this day would need.* Three silences, and all three are shown to students because they are the day's own subject. **It states no latency for the embeddings endpoint at any batch size**, which is the single number the day's cost model is built on and the reason the day measures instead of quoting — the sampling and size bounds are precise and the quantity they would let you predict is absent. **It states no cost or benefit for `base64` over `float`**, so the choice is documented as available and undocumented as consequential; the day measures the difference rather than asserting one. And **it does not say how the retry defaults interact with a 429**, so a caller cannot compute their own worst-case wait from the documentation alone, which is exactly the gap [S013](wiki/seminars/S013.md) opens and [S019](wiki/seminars/S019.md) closes. No magnitude is asserted anywhere in this paper for any of these.

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The three-item single-dimension pretest and item 4's reason-check; the phase 1 Copilot prediction; the four-comparison-function phase 3, the choice of a remote model call as the fourth level, and the crossover-estimation demand; the decision to treat the aggregate-limit 400 as the phase's second payload; the closing grounding question added in response to §8 objection 3; the ten points of phase 4 and the elevation of the crossover to a design object; the six-step phase 5 with paired improvements, numeric predictions and a shipped ranker; step 6's expiry condition; phase 7's dating demand and the clause that an unreachable crossover strengthens a recommendation; the specification's numeric-prediction requirement and the oversized-batch route for the intervention record; the reporting of prediction ratios rather than errors; and all five §9 predictions are this design's own reasoning, untested. The §8 objection-1 concession is **now discharged in the day** — point 2 states the model's omissions with safe and unsafe conditions — and the discharge is annotated rather than absorbed, with the WP-014 pattern finding left standing. The objection-2 concession states that Q2's measurement practice precedes its justification by sixteen days, that this is forced rather than chosen, that S026's design does not yet contain the retrospective correction it requires, and that the network call makes the variance problem worse. The objection-3 concession accepts that the aggregate-limit payload penalised the student who grounded first, names the closing grounding question as the cheap fix, and records as accepted that the day spends real minutes on a number with a short shelf life.
