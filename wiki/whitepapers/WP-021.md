## In plain terms

A discovery tells you about a situation. A requirement commits somebody to something. The step between them is where most of the discovery is lost, and the loss is silent because a badly written requirement looks exactly like a well written one.

Two things go wrong at this step, and they are different failures with different causes.

**The first is that the requirement restates the solution.** The customer arrived with an answer, the discovery examined the problem behind it, and the requirement document quietly returns to the answer, now phrased as an obligation. This is not laziness; it is gravity. The answer is the thing everybody in the room can already picture.

**The second is that the requirement is written in the wrong language.** Not the wrong jargon — the wrong *ownership* of language. A requirement written in the engineer's vocabulary can only be checked by the engineer. A requirement written in the customer's unaltered vocabulary inherits every ambiguity that vocabulary carries, and specialist jargons carry a great many. **The working answer is neither: the customer's language, sharpened, with the customer holding a veto over the sharpening.**

That pairing is taken from the domain-modelling literature, where it is argued from the cost of translation rather than from tidiness. **A team where one or two people are bilingual has a bottleneck, and the bottleneck's translations are inexact.** That is the mechanism, it is legible, and it is not measured anywhere.

## Abstract

This paper defends opening the framing stage with language rather than with structure. The argument is that a requirement is a commitment expressed in somebody's vocabulary, that the choice of whose vocabulary determines who can check the commitment, and that the working answer is the customer's terms sharpened under the customer's veto. It defends the requirement that at least one false cognate be found and recorded, on the grounds that the words which cause damage are ordinary rather than technical and are therefore never checked. It adopts the corpus's rule that engineering artefacts belong as text in the repository, and records that the corpus asserts it without evidence. The paper concedes that its principal source declares two prerequisites — iterative development and continuing access to domain experts — which many engagements do not satisfy, and that the session's discipline is therefore frequently applied outside the conditions its source claims for it.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A practitioner who has just finished a good discovery is asked for requirements, and the request arrives with a shape attached: a numbered list, due Friday, one line each.

**That shape is the adversary.** A numbered one-line list cannot hold a contradiction between informants, cannot hold an observation that was not reported, and cannot hold the sentence *we do not yet know*. Everything the previous stage worked to preserve is inexpressible in the format the next stage asks for, and so it is dropped — not decided against, dropped.

The session's response is to change what the artefact is before changing what it says. A requirement set that comes with a glossary, that names who would notice each requirement's absence, and that lives as reviewable text in the repository, has somewhere to put the things a numbered list has nowhere to put.

There is a second problem underneath. **A requirement is the point at which the engagement acquires a vocabulary, and the vocabulary is acquired by accident.** Whoever writes first decides. If that person writes in the engineer's terms, the customer becomes a reviewer of a document they cannot check; if they write in the customer's terms unaltered, the document inherits ambiguities that will surface as defects.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** A practitioner arriving from industry has written requirements before and can do this session; a learner arriving from [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) brings a discovery packet and can do it better.

The prior it works against is universal and rarely examined: that a requirement is a thing you write down after you know what to build. **In this programme it is the instrument by which you find out whether you know**, and the session is designed so that the finding-out is uncomfortable.

Learners also arrive with an unexamined belief that vocabulary problems are technical. They are not. The terms that cause damage — *order*, *complete*, *approved*, *customer*, *site*, *shift* — are ones everybody is confident about, which is precisely why nobody checks them.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is low. The session's difficulty is social rather than conceptual: asking somebody what they meant by a word they have used for twenty years is awkward, and the awkwardness is the work. The surplus is spent on the repeated-why dialogue, which cannot be hurried.

**3.2 Why the opening items precede instruction.** Attempting an answer before instruction improves later retention of the tested material and makes the distinctions noticeable. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted here or anywhere in this wiki.**

**3.3 Why translation is treated as the cost rather than ambiguity.** The domain-modelling source frames the problem as translation rather than as vocabulary hygiene: where two languages exist, a small number of people become bilingual, they become bottlenecks of information flow, and their translations are inexact. **The failure is structural rather than lexical**, which is why the remedy is a shared language rather than a bigger dictionary.

**3.4 Why the customer keeps a veto.** The same source is explicit that the language derives from the experts' own jargon but is cleaned up — sharper and narrower — and equally explicit that the experts should object if the cleaning diverges from what the term means in their field. **Both halves are needed and dropping either produces a different, worse practice.** Dropping the sharpening gives you their ambiguity; dropping the veto gives you a model they will not use, which the same source names as the thing you must never adopt however elegant it seems.

**3.5 Why the repeated-why dialogue is taught as a dialogue.** The corpus teaches it as a worked conversation rather than as a principle, and that is the right way to teach it: the move is a sequence, its value comes from the second and third repetition rather than the first, and a principle stated once does not convey that. **The corpus's own worked example turns a request about lighting into a requirement about what happens when an alert fires**, which is the shape the move produces and is not obvious from a description of it.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

Seven movements: situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**The opening items are the instrument and item 4 is the one the design cares about.** Naming a word you and the customer both use and mean differently is a request most learners cannot satisfy, and the inability is the session's opening content. It is placed fourth rather than first because items 1 to 3 establish enough context for the question to be answerable in principle.

**Two artefacts are required rather than one, and the glossary is not a supporting document.** A requirement set without a glossary has resolved its ambiguities silently, in the author's head, which is where they will stay. The requirement that the glossary contain at least one genuine disagreement is a forcing function: a glossary of undisputed terms can be produced without talking to anybody.

**The review movement uses speech deliberately.** The source's argument is that speech is the cheapest modelling instrument available and that rough edges are easier to hear than to see. The session's version is narrower and more checkable: **a requirement you cannot say without inserting an explanatory clause has a missing definition, and the clause you inserted is that definition.** That converts a general claim about modelling out loud into a mechanical test.

**The repository requirement is adopted from the corpus and its warrant is thin.** The corpus states that engineering artefacts should move out of documents and slides into text in the repository because that is what makes them actionable, and evidences it nowhere. The mechanism is legible — text can be diffed, reviewed and referenced from a decision record, and a slide cannot — and the mechanism is all there is.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The restated solution.** The likeliest outcome. *Countermeasure:* the repeated-why dialogue, applied to the learner's own sentences rather than to the customer's, which is the only place in the session where the move can be practised without a customer present.

**The undisputed glossary.** A learner produces a glossary of terms nobody ever disagreed about and satisfies the letter of the requirement. *Countermeasure:* partial. The specification asks for a resolved disagreement with a date and a name attached, which raises the cost of fabrication without preventing it. **[S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) checks the same item and cannot distinguish either.**

**The imposed vocabulary.** The learner invents a term because it is tidier, the customer defers to their technical authority, and the model silently stops matching the business. *Countermeasure:* the veto requirement, plus the review movement, where reading the requirement aloud to somebody who did not write it exposes terms that only work on the page.

**The comfortable review.** The learner reads their requirements to a colleague who agrees with them. *Countermeasure:* none available at this session. **[S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) supplies it by making the reviewer's job adversarial**, which is why the two sessions are adjacent.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence.** Two specification items at [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md): that no requirement names a technology unless the technology is an owned constraint, and that the glossary records at least one word the two sides meant differently, with the adopted meaning and the person who agreed.

**Neither item marks whether the requirements are good.** They mark whether the requirement is about the business and whether the vocabulary was negotiated. That is a deliberately narrow claim about what the session can establish.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the language discipline is being lifted out of the conditions its source declares for it.**

At its strongest: the source states two prerequisites and states them flatly — development is iterative, and developers have a close continuing relationship with domain experts. It says the book makes no claim without them. **Forward-deployed engagements frequently satisfy neither.** Access to the people who actually understand the domain is often mediated, rationed, or supplied through a project manager who is not one; and delivery is often a fixed scope against a fixed date with one demonstration at the end. Teaching a language discipline whose author disclaims it outside those conditions, to a cohort who will mostly work outside them, is teaching a method that will not hold.

**This is conceded, and it is the stronger of the two objections.** The rebuttal is narrow: the discipline degrades rather than fails, and it degrades informatively. A team that cannot get access to a domain expert to negotiate a term has learned something specific about the engagement — and the glossary entry that records *nobody available to arbitrate this* is a more useful artefact than a definition invented in its place. **The declined fix is to gate the session on the prerequisites and skip it where they are absent.** It is declined because absence of access is the common case, and a programme that teaches nothing about language in the common case has surrendered the field to whoever writes first.

**Objection 2: requiring a false cognate manufactures one.**

At its strongest: a specification item that requires a disagreement will produce a disagreement, and the disagreements produced to satisfy an assessment will be small, safe and discovered by asking a colleague rather than a customer. **The item measures compliance and calls it discovery.**

**Partly conceded.** The item cannot distinguish a found disagreement from a manufactured one and nothing in this design can. The rebuttal is that the base rate is favourable: in any engagement of real size the ordinary words genuinely are overloaded, so a learner who looks will find one, and a learner who fabricates has still had to think about which word would plausibly be contested. **The declined fix is to require the disagreement be attested by the customer.** It is declined because it makes the assessment depend on a customer's availability, which the programme cannot guarantee, and an assessment that fails on unavailability teaches learners to avoid the item.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will be unable to answer opening item 4 before instruction, and most of those who can will name a technical term rather than an ordinary one. *Instrument:* the written answers to item 4, coded *absent* / *technical term* / *ordinary term* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** A substantial number of first-draft requirements will be satisfiable by exactly one named product, and their authors will be able to name it when asked. *Instrument:* the first draft against a single question put to the author, recorded before revision.

**Prediction 3.** Requirements revised after the repeated-why dialogue will be stated at a different level of the problem than their first drafts, and the difference will be visible to a reader who has not seen the dialogue. *Instrument:* paired first and revised drafts, presented unlabelled to a second reader who is asked which of each pair is more general. **If the reader cannot tell, the dialogue movement is decorative and should be rewritten as a checklist.**

**Prediction 4.** Glossaries whose disagreement is dated and attributed to a named role will be rarer than glossaries whose disagreement is undated. *Instrument:* the glossaries, counted by presence of date and role.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a requirement is a commitment in somebody's language; that whose language it is determines who can check it; and that the words which cause damage are the ordinary ones.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The language discipline's source makes **no quantitative claim of any kind** and declares prerequisites many engagements do not meet. The repository rule is the corpus's assertion with no evidence behind it. The learning mechanism behind the pre-instruction items is direction and mechanism only.

**The concession worth remembering.** This session teaches a discipline outside the conditions its author claims for it, deliberately, because the alternative is teaching nothing. That is a defensible choice and it is a choice, not a finding.
