# WP-032 — Synchronisation and Errors You Cannot Reproduce

**Seminar day:** [S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md) · **Module:** [M07](/wiki/modules/M07.md) · **Quarter:** [Q4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md)
**Strands:** SY · ME · PE · **Complexity class:** 3
**Platform anchor:** API Management semantic caching — `llm-semantic-cache-lookup` and `llm-semantic-cache-store` · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (write) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends treating an irreproducible concurrency defect as a measurement problem rather than a debugging problem, and defends assessing the day on an evidence argument and a report rather than on a fix. The first argument is that reproduction is the sampling of a rare event, that the standard debugging loop is unavailable when the event cannot be sampled on demand, and that students without a substitute either stall or declare victory on the disappearance of a symptom — both of which are failures of evidential discipline rather than of systems knowledge. The second argument is that the professional act in this situation is to decide without confirmation while describing the diagnosis accurately, which is a distinction students collapse and which no amount of technical instruction supplies. The paper additionally defends re-anchoring the day on a semantic cache, whose wrong answers are irreproducible by construction because they depend on what else was cached and the cache empties itself on a timer, and whose controlling parameter has a direction opposite to what its name implies. It records that this day implements the S031 framing diagnostic identified as missing in [WP-031](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-031.md) §8. It concedes that the day's central artefact is unverifiable within the programme's timescale, that its most instructive case is one the design can construct and cannot honestly resolve, and that the day now permits an agent to deploy a correctness-critical parameter that neither the agent nor a typical reviewer reads correctly.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

A defect has occurred four times in six weeks. Users saw it. It cannot be reproduced.

Every debugging skill a student holds assumes reproduction. Change something, observe whether the symptom persists, conclude. Remove reproduction and the loop does not degrade — it stops, because there is nothing to observe. What students do next divides cleanly into two failures. Some do nothing, on the reasonable-sounding ground that you cannot fix what you cannot see. Others change something plausible, wait, observe no recurrence, and close the ticket.

The second failure is the dangerous one and it is nearly universal, because it feels like success. A defect occurring in a small fraction of executions will not recur in a short observation window whether or not it was fixed, so the evidence for the fix and the evidence for no fix are identical.

The reframing that unlocks this is at point 1: **reproduction is a sampling problem.** A defect occurring four times in six weeks is not concealed; it is rare, and rarity is a property the student's evidence must account for rather than a barrier to investigation. Once framed that way, M06's apparatus applies directly — what does this observation license, what would falsify this hypothesis, what instrument would distinguish these explanations — and the student has a method again.

**The semantic cache is the cleanest instance of this the design has found, and it was not constructed for teaching.** A wrong answer served from a cache is irreproducible for three independent reasons at once: whether the lookup matches depends on what else happens to be stored, which depends on the traffic that preceded it; the entry is removed on a timer, so the state that produced the fault is destroyed by the passage of time rather than by anything anyone did; and whether a given response came from the cache at all is invisible unless tracing was already enabled. The vendor states, in the policy reference, that because responses are selected by similarity rather than exact match the feature can surface responses that are incorrect, outdated or unsafe for the current request. **The day's defect is therefore documented behaviour operating as designed**, which is a stronger version of the module's standing claim that its failures have no villain.

The day's second problem is professional and is the one point 6 addresses. You may act on an unconfirmed diagnosis; you may not describe it as confirmed. Both halves matter. A student who will not act without confirmation is useless in this situation, and a student who acts and then reports confirmation has created the phase 6 failure: a defensible action on a wrong diagnosis, producing a wrong conclusion invisible for months.

The third problem belongs to the module. [WP-031](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-031.md) §8 conceded that S031's no-implementation design was untested, that a mis-framed opening would corrupt the module's remaining four days, and that no mechanism existed to detect this before S032 began. Phase 0 is that mechanism.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**High, but lower than S031's.** The day sits in the module's first load trough by design, after a peak opening.

The intrinsic difficulty is in step 4 and phase 7 — constructing a rival hypothesis that explains the same observations — which requires holding the observation set and two competing accounts simultaneously and comparing their coverage. That is genuinely interactive and does not decompose.

Management:

- **The defect is supplied with its evidence** at phase 3 and at step 1, so no capacity goes to gathering.
- **The licensing form and the instrument-design sequence are both reused,** not re-taught, which is the design's standing method for making a prior day's apparatus free.
- **Four investigative situations with a single fixed cause,** so all variation is evidential.
- **No application code is written on this day either.** The module's second consecutive implementation-free day, which is a deliberate accumulation and is discussed in §8.
- **The same gateway instance throughout the module,** now on its second day, with the cache and its prior entries prepared by staff before the room arrives.
- **All policy syntax supplied or agent-authored,** per the module's standing decision. The vector-similarity mechanism is presented at the level the day needs — a threshold admits or rejects — and not derived.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Item 3's falsification question,* asked before any technique is supplied.
- *Situation D,* where the defect stopped without intervention.
- *Constructing the alternative hypothesis,* step 4 — the day's discriminator.
- *Deciding what to do before the instrumentation returns,* step 6.
- *Supplying the separating observation,* phase 7, which escalates S027's competing-explanation demand.
- *Phase 0's unassessed self-report,* which is uncomfortable to answer honestly.
- *Item 5's direction question,* which has a correct answer the cohort does not hold and which cannot be reasoned to from the parameter's name.
- *The evidential asymmetry made physical,* since groups in situations C and D watch other groups obtain evidence they are forbidden.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Fixing the defect.* Not attempted. The day's output is an argument, a deployed instrument and a report.
- *Memory-model reasoning.* Point 2 treats visibility as a black box and says so.
- *Lock-free programming.* Excluded; the black-box treatment would be unsafe for it, which the scope statement names.
- *Race detection tooling.* Named at point 8 as instrumentation and not taught, because a tool that finds races would remove the day's evidential problem rather than solve it.
- *Withholding the evidence-argument structure.* Point 5 states it.
- *Tuning the threshold to find its failure boundary.* Genuinely instructive and refused: it would convert an evidential day into an empirical one, and the resulting number would be a sample of one corpus presented as a property.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md).** Point 2's three synchronisation promises omit the memory-model guarantees that make visibility precise. The scope statement in the seminar page does something the standard has not previously required and which [WP-031](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-031.md) §8 raised as a gap: **it states the conditions under which the simplification is safe and the conditions under which it is not** — safe for reasoning about specifications, unsafe for lock-free code, which the day does not write. This is a stronger form of scope statement than naming an omission, and it is offered here as a candidate for the standard rather than as compliance with it. It has since been adopted as the standard's strong form.

**The platform instance, scoped in the strong form.** The day treats the semantic cache as an instance of an unreproducible concurrency defect, and the analogy must carry its safe and unsafe conditions.

*Safe.* Reasoning about what an observation licenses when the state that produced a fault no longer exists; about why a disappearance is not a fix; about which rival hypothesis explains the same evidence; and about what to instrument when the present evidence cannot settle the question. In all of those the cache is a faithful instance and the reasoning transfers unchanged.

*Unsafe.* **As a model of a synchronisation mechanism.** There is no lock here, no memory model, no instruction reordering and no thread. The shared mutable state is a cache in an external store and the concurrency is between requests, not between threads sharing an address space. A student who carries the day's evidential discipline into lock-free work has transferred the right thing; one who carries the cache as a mental picture of a mutex has acquired a wrong mechanism, which point 2's objection-1 argument at [WP-016](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-016.md) established is worse than no mechanism. It is equally unsafe as a claim about any other caching product's threshold semantics, since the direction of this one is a product decision and is documented as such.

Step 4's rival hypothesis is where the difference shows, and the seminar page carries the statement so that the boundary reaches students rather than only this paper.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **a situation with no available move**.

Phase 1 supplies a real defect that cannot be reproduced and asks what students would do first. Nearly every answer is an attempt to reproduce it, and item 4 asks students to predict that this is what the room will say. When the distribution is shown, the reflex is visible as a cohort property rather than as an individual limitation, which is the condition under which it can be examined rather than defended.

Situation D supplies the day's most durable moment. A defect that stopped occurring, with no change made, is a situation students recognise immediately from their own experience and have never had a framework for. The closing task — what would you tell the person asking whether it is fixed — is uncomfortable in exactly the way S024's duty was, and the discomfort is the content.

Phase 0 has an unusual motivational property the design did not anticipate when adding it. Asking students to characterise the previous day in two sentences, telling them the responses will be read by staff and reported back, and then actually reporting them at phase 8, signals that the design treats its own instruction as subject to evidence. Students notice this more than the design expected.

The competence risk is that the day produces no fix and therefore feels unresolved. Step 6's act-now decision is the constructive output and is deliberately placed before the report.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 0 (10 min).** Two sentences on what S031 was about. Unassessed, collected, read before phase 4, reported at phase 8. If a substantial fraction describe S031 as being about races, bugs or rate limiting, point 1 is expanded and the module's framing is re-established. **The third failure category is new and is a consequence of the platform anchoring**: a cohort that describes S031 as "about rate limits" has taken the vehicle for the argument, which is the specific risk the practical turn introduces and which this instrument now detects. This is a design instrument and the seminar page says so.

**Phase 1 (20 min).** Five items on a real irreproducible defect. Item 3 — what would convince you that you had *not* found the cause — is the day's move and is answered by very few. Item 4 converts the reflex into a measurement. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction** and is administered here per instrumentation rule 8. It is unusual among the module's sealed items in having a checkable factual answer the cohort does not hold, which makes it a joint measure of the agent's frontier and the room's.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** The bridge asks which S027 error source most resembles a defect occurring in four executions out of millions. The answer is a selection effect, and stating it reframes the day before any instruction: the executions you observe are not a fair sample of the executions that occur.

**Phase 3 (40 min).** One cause, four evidential situations, **executed against a live cache with prepared contents.** Situation D is the payload and is the design's most difficult construction, because it must be genuinely unresolvable rather than merely hard. The platform improves it in a way that increases rather than reduces the difficulty: the disappearance now has a discoverable mechanism — the stored entry's time-to-live expired — and **a disappearance whose mechanism you understand is harder to report honestly than one you cannot explain**, because the temptation to present the mechanism as a resolution is much stronger. The closing task is a communication demand, not an analytical one.

**Phase 4 (45 min).** Eleven points. Point 1 is the reframing, and its third case — you cannot sample the right conditions because the condition is a cache you do not control — is the platform's contribution. Point 3's fourth source, correct primitives composed incorrectly, is the one that survives review and is given weight; today's defect is an instance of it. Point 4 applies S028 directly. Point 6 is the day's professional core. Point 7 resolves situation D and names its mechanism. Point 8 names instrumentation as the reliable output when evidence is insufficient. **Points 9 to 11 are the platform content: the threshold and its inverted direction, three defaults of which one is an access-control boundary, and the documented silence that no accuracy or false-match rate is published for this feature at any threshold.**

**Phase 5 (85 min).** Seven steps at class 3. Step 4 is the discriminator. Step 6 is the professional act. Step 7's report is assessed against point 6's distinction. **Step 5's promotion from designing an instrument to deploying one is the practical turn's contribution and it changes what can be assessed**: a designed instrument can be judged only on its description, and a running one can be checked for whether it actually emits the distinguishing observation. That is [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md)'s discipline completed rather than described.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A defect declared fixed by a correct change that did not address the cause, then the agent case. The detail that the instructor's change was correct is essential: this is not a story about carelessness, and a story about carelessness would let students exempt themselves. **The agent case is chosen because the reviewer independently made the agent's error**, both inferring a parameter's direction from its name. It is the module's answer to the belief that human review is the control for agent output: a review conducted by someone who shares the author's misconception is a second vote, and the available defence was one lookup rather than more diligence.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Rival hypothesis plus separating observation. The escalation over S027 is the second half — supplying the observation that distinguishes — which is experimental design applied under adversarial conditions and is the module's clearest instance of M06 doing work outside measurement.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** The reflex rate, situation D's answers, the sealed item 5 opened as two rates, and the phase 0 report. The phase 0 report is the design telling the cohort what it learned about its own previous day, which is the S001 self-instrumentation commitment on its fourth occasion.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with production on-call experience will recognise the situation and may have working heuristics. Predicted failure: they produce a plausible step 1 hypothesis quickly and a weak step 4 alternative, because operational practice rewards a single actionable theory and penalises deliberation. Countermeasure: phase 7's partner must construct the rival, and an experienced student's hypothesis is usually the easiest to find a rival for precisely because it was reached quickly.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The day requires applying M06's apparatus in an unfamiliar domain with no worked bridge except phase 2's retrieval item. Countermeasures: the licensing form is supplied, the instrument sequence is reused verbatim, the defect and its evidence are given, and the policy syntax is agent-authored. Residual risk: step 5's instrumentation design may be generic, and the fallback — state which observation would distinguish, without designing the instrument — preserves the reasoning and loses the deployment.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Situations A and B are handled by most groups. C is harder and D is the difficulty. Fallback if a group resolves D: ask them what they would say if the person asking has budget for exactly one more week of investigation, which forces a decision under a resource constraint and previews S065.

**The platform fails on the day.** The cache requires an external store and an embeddings backend, and either can be unavailable. Countermeasure: the cache and its prepared entries are stood up and verified by staff before the room arrives; a recorded trace set from a prior cohort is held so that situations A and B remain adjudicable from data. Where that fallback is used, **the day states that the cases were demonstrated rather than operated and step 5's deployment is not assessed**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md). Situations C and D are unaffected, since their defining property is the absence of evidence.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **the day experienced as an argument for inaction**. A student who absorbs that they cannot confirm the diagnosis may conclude that the responsible thing is to do nothing until they can. Point 6's first half exists precisely against this — you may act on an unconfirmed diagnosis — and step 6 requires an action to be chosen and justified before the report is written. The ordering is deliberate: a student who writes the report first will describe a situation, and a student who decides first will describe a decision.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by S033 (partial failure, where the unobservable event becomes a distributed one), S035 (breaking a system that appears to work), S068 (designing for detectability, which formalises point 8), S074 (tool failure has four shapes), S079 (drift, alerting and the incident you did not see) and S080 (blameless forensics on a real incident).

S080 at roughly four quarters is the principal target and the correspondence is direct: an incident review is an evidence argument about an event that will not recur on demand, conducted under the firewall this day's phase 6 relies on. S032 is S080's technical rehearsal and S010 is its social one.

S068 gains a second connection from the platform anchoring: today's defect is undiagnosable in situations C and D **because tracing was not enabled beforehand**, which is the detectability argument arriving as a felt cost rather than as a principle. The seminar page names it at point 8 and does not develop it.

Retained content: reproduction-as-sampling, the four defect sources, the evidence-argument structure, and the act-without-confirming distinction. Four items, no numbers.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 6.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement.

One specification item, jointly in SY201 and ME101's successor. The joint assessment is the module's structural claim that this is a measurement problem in a systems setting.

**A report describing an unconfirmed diagnosis as confirmed fails.** So does an unfalsifiable step 1 hypothesis. The second clause is the S009 discipline applied to diagnosis and is the more commonly failed. The artefact additionally carries the **prompt record, intervention record and frontier statement**, and **an intervention record with no entries fails** unless the deployed instrumentation demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**The seminar page now states its checked elements and closes with *nothing else is assessed*.** This is the repair [WP-028](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-028.md) §8 specified and which §8 below previously recorded as having been made nowhere. It is now made at S031 and at S032. **It is not made retrospectively at S028**, per the standard's non-retroactivity boundary, and §8's concession is amended rather than deleted so that the two-cycle delay remains legible.

Programme instrumentation: **phase 0's S031 characterisations are the module's framing check** and are the direct implementation of [WP-031](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-031.md) §8's identified gap, now with a third coded category for cohorts that mistake the platform vehicle for the argument; item 4's reflex rate is the cohort baseline, re-probed at S079; the step 4 alternative-hypothesis quality is the rival-construction measure carried to S080; **item 5's two rates — predicted value and correct direction — are this day's frontier datum** and are read by next year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), per instrumentation rule 9.

The frontier statements are a sample and the day says so, per instrumentation rule 3: one cohort, one cache configuration, one prepared corpus, on a defect staff selected because its mechanism is documented.

Dispositions: **integrity**, in step 7's report, where overstating confidence is easy, invisible and immediately rewarding.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day's central artefact cannot be verified, and the specification therefore assesses plausibility.**

At its strongest: the student produces a hypothesis about an irreproducible defect. Nobody knows whether it is right — that is the situation's defining feature — and nobody will know within the programme's timescale, since the instrumentation deployed at step 5 will not return usable evidence for months if ever. So the assessed artefact is judged on internal coherence and rhetorical quality. The specification's two clauses catch overclaiming and unfalsifiability; everything else is an assessor deciding whether an argument reads well, which is exactly the drift mechanism the programme's own assessment material warns about and which [WP-028](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-028.md) §8 already conceded for a structurally identical artefact.

The rebuttal is partial and rests on what is being assessed. The competence is *constructing and bounding an evidence argument*, not being right about the defect. Several elements are checkable independently of the truth of the hypothesis: whether the observations at step 2 are correctly licensed, whether the falsifier at step 3 would in fact falsify, whether the alternative at step 4 genuinely explains the same observations, and whether the instrument at step 5 distinguishes them. All four are verifiable by inspection, **and the fifth is now verifiable by execution**, since the instrument is deployed and either emits the distinguishing observation or does not.

**The concession is amended rather than withdrawn.** When this paper was first written the design did not state the checkable elements, and the same omission had been conceded at S028 and left unrepaired — two artefacts assessed against implied quality where the design was in fact applying a small number of binary checks. **The repair is now made, at this day and at S031, and the record of its delay stands**: it was specified at [WP-028](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-028.md) §8, recorded again here without action, and made only when a third day required it. That is the design's follow-through failing twice before succeeding, and it is left legible rather than tidied, because the pattern is the finding. S028 is not retrospectively amended.

**Objection 2: situation D is unresolvable by construction, and the day resolves it anyway.**

At its strongest: phase 3's situation D presents a defect that stopped occurring with no intervention, and phase 4 point 7 resolves it — a defect that stopped is not evidence that it is fixed. But that is only half an answer. The question the closing task actually asks is what to *tell* someone who wants to close the ticket, and the honest answer depends on facts the day does not supply: how costly the defect is, how costly further investigation is, and what the organisation's tolerance is. Point 7 supplies a principle where a judgement is required, and a student who applies the principle without the judgement will tell a stakeholder to keep an open ticket indefinitely, which is not defensible either.

This objection the design does not fully answer. Point 7 is stated as an epistemic claim — the disappearance is not evidence — and the epistemic claim is correct and complete. What follows from it operationally is genuinely underdetermined, and the day presents an epistemic resolution as though it settled a professional question.

The §5 fallback for a group that resolves situation D asks precisely the right follow-up — what if there is budget for one more week — and it appears only as a fallback for advanced groups rather than as part of the phase. Moving it into phase 3 for everyone would convert the closing task from a principle-application into a decision under constraint, which is what class 3 requires and what S065 will assess. That change is small, obviously right, and not made; the reason is that phase 3 is compressed to forty minutes by phase 0's addition, which is a scheduling consequence of a repair rather than a pedagogical decision.

**The platform anchoring makes this objection worse and the design accepts that.** Situation D now has a discoverable mechanism, so the student is no longer merely unable to confirm a fix — they can explain precisely why the symptom stopped and why it will return. The professional question that follows is therefore sharper and less answerable by principle, not more.

**Objection 3: the day lets an agent deploy a correctness-critical parameter that neither the agent nor the reviewer reads correctly, and treats that as a teaching opportunity rather than as a hazard.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn.

At its strongest: the day's most memorable content is that `score-threshold` runs in the opposite direction to its name, that the agent gets it wrong, and that the reviewer gets it wrong the same way. The design presents this as an argument for instrument characterisation. It can equally be read as an argument the design has not made and cannot easily answer: **if a single misread parameter can silently serve wrong answers to real users, and the two available controls — the agent's competence and a human review — both fail for the same reason, then the responsible conclusion is not "characterise your instruments" but "do not let second-year students deploy this."** The day's response is a disposable environment, which prevents the harm and thereby removes the only thing that would make the lesson stick. And the deeper version bites harder: the students most persuaded by phase 6 will be the ones who conclude that careful reading is the answer, which is precisely the conclusion phase 6 was constructed to refute.

The objection is conceded in part.

What is defensible: the day's assessed competence is building and bounding an evidence argument, and no part of that is delegable. The threshold error is content, not assessment — a student who reproduces it and reports it accurately has done the assessed work.

What is conceded: **the design has no evidence that phase 6 produces the conclusion it intends.** A cohort that leaves believing "read the documentation more carefully" has learned the wrong lesson from the right story, and the day contains no instrument that would distinguish that cohort from one that learned the intended lesson. This is the same shape as the §8 objection [WP-016](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-016.md) conceded about its platform demonstration — the rhetorical force running ahead of the argument — and it is the second instance in the design.

**The cheap fix is available and it is made.** Phase 6's student turn now asks for *a place today where you accepted the agent's explanation rather than its output*, which is a different question from asking what they got wrong, and it elicits the specific behaviour the case is about. **A second fix is available and is not made**: phase 8 could ask the room, in one line, what would have caught the error, and code the answers for whether they name a procedure or a virtue. It would cost two minutes, it would supply the missing instrument, and it is not in the design because phase 8 already carries the sealed item, the phase 0 report and the situation D distribution, and the design judges it full. That is a scheduling reason for omitting a measurement, which is the same reason objection 2 records, and **two omissions in one paper attributable to phase compression is a finding about the day's budget rather than about either measurement.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A large majority of phase 1 item 1 answers will be reproduction attempts, and item 4 predictions will underestimate this. *Instrument:* item 1 responses coded, against item 4 predictions.

**Prediction 2.** Phase 0 responses describing S031 as being about races, bugs or rate limits will be below a third. *Instrument:* phase 0 coding, in three categories. **Above a third triggers the expanded point 1 and is recorded as a partial failure of S031's design, which is the check [WP-031](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-031.md) §8 requested. A cohort whose errors concentrate in the rate-limit category indicates that the platform vehicle has displaced the argument, which is a failure of the practical turn specifically and is reported as such.**

**Prediction 3.** Students with on-call experience will produce step 1 hypotheses rated more plausible and step 4 alternatives rated weaker than students without. *Instrument:* blind ratings of both, split by the admission background survey.

**Prediction 4.** In phase 7, partners will fail to find a separating observation for at least a third of hypothesis pairs, and those pairs' step 5 instruments will be rated weaker. *Instrument:* phase 7 outcomes against blind-rated step 5 designs.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of item 5 responses will state that a higher `score-threshold` makes matching stricter, and the agent's own output will state the same. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded for direction, against the agent's produced policy and its stated justification. **If the cohort's direction accuracy is high, phase 6's agent case loses its force for that cohort and the fallback is to run it as a near-miss rather than as a demonstration. If the agent's justification is correct while the cohort's is not, the day has found a case where the agent's frontier exceeds the room's, which the programme has not yet recorded anywhere and which would be reported to the frontier map as such.**

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The caching behaviour used at phases 3 to 5 is taken from Microsoft Learn's `llm-semantic-cache-lookup` and `llm-semantic-cache-store` policy references, the semantic-caching how-to for Azure API Management, and the API Management policy reference, all read 2026-07-26: lookup by vector proximity of the prompt to previous requests against a required `score-threshold` between 0.0 and 1.0, with the documentation's own statement that **lower values require higher semantic similarity for a match**, that a value above 0.2 may lead to cache mismatch, and that a low starting value near 0.05 should be tuned by the ratio of hits to misses; `ignore-system-messages` defaulting to `false` while the same table recommends `true`; `embeddings-backend-id` and an `embeddings-backend-auth` that must be `system-assigned`; an optional `vary-by` element documented as the means of controlling cross-user access to cache entries; a required time-to-live on the store policy; the statement that **if the cache lookup fails the API call does not raise an error and the cache operation completes successfully**; the recommendation to place a rate limit immediately after a cache lookup in case the cache is unavailable; the requirement for an external RediSearch-compatible cache; the policy's availability on classic, v2, consumption and self-hosted gateways and **not on the workspace gateway**; and the vendor's own caution that **because semantic caching returns responses based on similarity rather than exact match, it can surface responses that are incorrect, outdated, or unsafe for the current request.**

**One silence is recorded and it is taught.** The documentation states that a threshold above 0.2 *may* lead to mismatch and **publishes no rate, dataset, baseline or measurement for it — there is no accuracy, hit-precision or false-match figure for this feature at any threshold, and none is asserted here.** That is the silence point 11 names to students, and it is the day's operative fact rather than a complaint: a correctness parameter shipped without a characterisation of its effect on correctness is [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md)'s unvalidated instrument, and the only party positioned to validate it is the operator. Consistent with the folder-wide asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](/research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), the pages state thresholds, tiers, defaults and requirements throughout and state no accuracy anywhere. The pages carry no version number and are dated by their content; every number the day uses is marked perishable at points 9 to 11.
