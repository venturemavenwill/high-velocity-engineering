## In plain terms

A pre-mortem is a post-mortem run before the work, on a failure that has not happened.

The trick is grammatical and it is the whole device. Asked *what could go wrong*, people produce hedged possibilities, because forecasting is uncomfortable and hedging is the socially safe form of it. Told *it failed — why*, they produce accounts, because explaining a stated outcome is a different cognitive act and a much easier social one. **You are not asking anybody to predict a disaster. You are asking them to explain one that has already been supplied.**

The session's second device is that everybody writes before anybody speaks. A discussion-first pre-mortem produces the most confident person's list, with the room's names attached, and the causes that most needed saying are exactly the ones that do not survive being said aloud first.

Its third is that a cause is not finished until it has been routed. Most causes are settled by asking somebody, looking at something, or checking a constraint that was recorded as a belief rather than as a fact. **Routing everything to an investigation is how a pre-mortem becomes a quarter of work**, and it is the commonest way the device destroys its own value.

The last column is the uncomfortable one. Some causes will be accepted rather than acted on, and an accepted cause with no owner has not been accepted — it has been set down.

The platform workflow carries the device one step past a record. **The learner takes three causes and writes the evaluation that would catch them, before the system that could pass it exists.** The evaluation fails, because there is nothing to run it against, and that is the intended result: an evaluation written after a system is shaped by what the system happens to do, and one written before it is shaped by what somebody was afraid of. **A check written while nobody has a stake in it passing is the check that will never be written later.** That last claim is the paper's own and nothing warrants it.

## Abstract

This paper defends running a structured failure analysis before a feasibility investigation rather than after a delivery, and locates its mechanism in the difference between explaining a stated outcome and forecasting an unstated one. The argument is that the causes which matter most are socially expensive to raise, that anonymous generation before discussion lowers that cost, and that a cause is only useful once it has been assigned to the cheapest instrument capable of settling it. The session installs a four-column record with an owner on every row including the accepted ones.

Its platform workflow extends the device from a record into an artefact: three causes become an executable evaluation, written and run before any system exists to pass it. That workflow makes two things observable which prose cannot. **The first is that the unit of analysis silently determines what a score means** — the learner writes a cause that the default conversation split cannot see, and watches it pass. **The second is that a model-judged evaluator is an uncalibrated instrument**, because no agreement rate with human judgement is published for any of them, and this wiki accordingly asserts none.

The paper concedes that the practice corpus behind it records no failure of any kind and therefore cannot supply a case in which a pre-mortem's causes were checked against a real outcome; that the anonymous-generation claim has no external warrant; that the exercise's own most likely failure is producing a list nobody routes; and — most seriously — that the evaluation literature contains a direct argument against writing criteria before observing outputs, which the design accepts rather than answers.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

The causes of a failed engagement are usually available to somebody on the first day, and they do not get said.

The reasons are structural rather than personal. The cause that implicates the plan implicates the person who wrote it. The cause that implicates the sponsor is career-adjacent. The cause that implicates the team's own capability is the one nobody can raise without appearing to volunteer. And the cause that is merely embarrassing — we do not actually know how their system works — is suppressed by the same mechanism that makes people nod in a briefing.

**A risk register does not solve this, because a risk register is a list of things it is safe to say.** Its items are conditional, hedged and generic, and they are generic precisely because a specific risk names somebody.

The session's response is to change the grammar, remove attribution during generation, and require every cause to acquire either an instrument or an owner. **A list with neither is a document, not a control.**

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** This is one of very few sessions in the stage a learner can enter from ordinary professional experience. Anybody who has been in a risk workshop has the raw material, and the device's mechanism does not depend on anything this programme has taught.

The two programme retrievals sharpen it rather than enable it. [S006](/wiki/seminars/S006.md) supplies constraints that were recorded as beliefs and marked as untested — the cheapest routing target in the exercise, because those rows are settled by asking one person. [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md) supplies the header: who was in the room, who was invited and did not come, and who was not invited. **A learner arriving without those substitutes their own team's equivalents and loses nothing structural.**

The prior the session works against is that a pre-mortem is a mood. Learners arrive expecting an exercise in pessimism and produce one unless the record's routing and ownership columns force the output to be actionable.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Low to moderate, and deliberately so. The session is placed second in the module because [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md) is cognitively heavier and because the pre-mortem's output is what makes [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md)'s question set possible.

**3.2 Why the past tense.** Prospective hindsight — stating an outcome and asking for its causes — changes the task from prediction to explanation. Explanation draws on a different and more fluent process, and it removes the social cost of appearing to forecast disaster. **The direction is well attested; no magnitude is asserted here or anywhere in this wiki.**

**3.3 Why generation precedes discussion.** A group discussion converges early on the first plausible account offered, and the first account is disproportionately likely to come from the most senior or most confident person present. Independent generation followed by anonymous collection preserves the tail of the distribution, and the tail is where the useful causes are. **This is a mechanism argument with no measurement behind it in this repository.**

**3.4 Why the routing column exists.** A cause without an instrument is an anxiety. The routing discipline is [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md)'s, applied to the team's own plan rather than to the customer's problem, and the cheapest instrument is nearly always a question to a named person rather than an investigation.

**3.5 Why acceptance needs an owner and a reopening condition.** A risk accepted by a group is accepted by nobody, and an acceptance with no condition attached is indistinguishable from having forgotten about it. The two fields together are what make the acceptance auditable later, at [S063](/wiki/seminars/S063.md) and [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md).

**3.6 Why a cause becomes a check, and what the evaluation literature contributes.**

A cause with an observable is a scenario, and a scenario is the unit that evaluation practice is built on. The clearest practitioner statement of this decomposes a system's scope into **features** — what it can do — and **scenarios** — the situations each feature must handle — and makes each scenario an assertion ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md)). The same essay supplies the rule that makes a pre-mortem-derived check liveable: unlike traditional unit tests, a full pass rate is explicitly not required, because **"Your pass rate is a product decision."** A check derived from a fear is allowed to fail; that is its function.

Its diagnosis of a stalled system is also the diagnosis of an unrouted pre-mortem. The named symptoms are whack-a-mole failure fixing, no visibility beyond informal quality judgement, and prompts grown into long and unwieldy forms — all three downstream of the same missing infrastructure. **The transferable claim is that the ability to change a system without the ability to tell whether the change helped produces motion and not progress**, which is what a pre-mortem list with no instruments produces too.

**That source is a practitioner essay resting on a single company's case study**, with no comparison group, no measured improvement and no released data. Its recommendations are well-specified hypotheses backed by consulting experience, and its tooling names are dated. It is used here for the decomposition and the pass-rate rule, both of which are design claims rather than findings.

**3.7 The literature's argument against this session, stated at full strength.**

The honest complication is that the evaluation research contains a direct challenge to writing criteria before observing outputs, and a paper that omitted it would be defending a workflow while suppressing its best objection.

Using a model to grade a model relocates rather than solves the trust problem, because **"LLM-generated evaluators simply inherit all the problems of the LLMs they evaluate, requiring further human validation"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md)). The repair is alignment against the practitioner's own grades on a labelled subset, which is exercise 5.

The deeper finding is **criteria drift**: **"users need criteria to grade outputs, but grading outputs helps users define criteria."** And the sharper form of it is that some criteria appear **dependent on the specific outputs observed**, which the authors say "raises serious questions for approaches that assume the independence of evaluation from observation of model outputs".

**Read plainly, that undercuts the session's central instruction.** If some criteria are only articulable after looking, an evaluation written before the system exists cannot be complete, and a design that presented it as complete would be teaching a workflow the literature says does not survive contact with outputs.

**The design concedes the objection and retreats to a narrower claim.** The pre-mortem-derived evaluation is not the rubric; it is the part of the rubric that fear can supply, and it is the only part that can be written while nobody has a stake in it passing. The parts that arrive from looking are welcome and expected, and exercise 6 asks the learner to predict which of their criteria will survive and to seal the prediction. **What the design claims cannot be recovered later is the check nobody had a reason to write once the system was working.** That claim is design reasoning with no external warrant, and §9's prediction 7 is its only test.

The drift evidence is qualitative, from a small interface study; it establishes that the phenomenon occurs and matters, **not how often or how much.**

**3.8 Why the platform makes the unit of analysis visible.**

The Agent Framework's evaluation model is small — an item, an evaluator, a result set — and its two evaluator tiers express the cost gradient the routing column already teaches: local deterministic checks in the inner loop and in continuous integration, model-judged checks less often ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). **The architecture carries the discipline**, which is unusual and is the reason this anchor was chosen over a general test framework.

One design decision in it does more teaching than the rest combined. **"Multi-turn conversations must be split into query and response halves for evaluation. How you split determines *what you're evaluating*."** Three strategies are supplied — last turn as the default, full, and per-turn — and an agent that recovers from a bad first attempt scores well under one and badly under another. **Neither score is wrong; they answer different questions, and a team that never chose has reported an answer to a question it did not ask.** The session engineers a collision with this deliberately: the learner writes a cause about a first response, and the default strategy passes it.

Two further published facts are used. **Repetitions** exist "to detect non-deterministic behavior", which is the platform conceding that one sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement. And **stored responses can be evaluated without re-running the agent**, which is what makes evaluation of real traffic possible rather than only of the queries somebody thought to ask.

**The limits are heavy.** First-party documentation, definitional rather than empirical, **read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches.** Many evaluators are **preview**; one consulted page is a **classic** variant explicitly superseded. And the gap that matters most: **no agreement rate with human judgement, no calibration data and no threshold rationale is published for any evaluator.** Most are model-judged. **This wiki asserts no evaluator's agreement with human judgement, because none is published**, and §5 treats an inherited threshold as an unexamined policy rather than a setting.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 1 is the device, administered before it is explained.** The learner is told the engagement failed and asked why. Item 3 then asks which of their own causes somebody would have been reluctant to say out loud, which is the session's actual subject arriving as a self-report rather than as a lecture.

**The instructor puts a cause implicating the design on the list first.** This is a re-norming move and it is the only countermeasure the session has for the suppression problem that does not depend on anonymity holding. The learning-design source behind this programme's ordering makes the same point about its own generation phase: the norms are a layer of the design rather than a preamble to it, and educators sharing their own failures is one of the named operations.

**Agent use is bounded to extension.** The learner produces five causes alone, then asks a model for causes they did not name, biased towards the non-technical, and records what was kept against what was discarded. **The discard rate is the point.** A model's list of project risks is long, fluent and mostly about any project, and the session is the first place in the programme where a learner is asked to quantify how much of an assistant's output was generic — a habit [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) will need.

**The review movement targets the accepted rows only**, because they are the ones that will otherwise never be looked at again. A reader who cannot state the reopening condition for an accepted cause has found a row that was set down rather than accepted.

**4.1 What the platform workflow is for.**

Not evaluation tooling, which will be renamed. It exists to convert one abstract instruction — write the check before the system — into an artefact that exists, runs and fails on the day the fear was articulated. **A learner who has done that has an object; a learner who has been told to do it has an intention.**

Three elements are load-bearing.

**The dataset is written before the system.** Each row encodes a cause in the past tense, with an expected outcome that a working system would meet and the feared one would not. This is the pre-mortem's grammar carried into a file, and it is the only place in the module where a cause is forced to become executable rather than merely specific.

**The split-strategy collision is engineered.** The learner is steered towards a cause about an agent's first response, which the default strategy cannot see. **The intended experience is watching a check pass and knowing it should not have**, because that is the only way a reader believes that a number without its unit of analysis is uninterpretable. The session does not warn them first; the material states the general principle and the workflow lets them meet the instance.

**The threshold must be defended in one sentence, and "it was the default" is an accepted answer.** Accepting it is deliberate. A learner who writes that sentence has recorded a policy they did not choose, in a document somebody can read, which is a better outcome than a fabricated justification and a strictly better one than silence.

**A cost the design accepts.** Cloud evaluation requires a project with a model deployment and incurs charge, which a cohort must budget for and an individual learner may not hold. **The local evaluator tier is sufficient for the whole of steps 3 to 5** and for two of the three worked causes, and a learner without cloud access should run those and record the omission rather than substituting a judgement of their own for a judged evaluator's.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The risk register in fancy dress.** The likeliest failure. Causes come back hedged and generic. *Countermeasure:* the past-tense requirement, enforced in the record's own column heading, and the standard's warning sign — the words *may*, *could*, *potentially*.

**The polite pre-mortem.** The real cause is known and unsaid. *Countermeasure:* anonymous collection, plus the instructor's own uncomfortable cause going first. **Both are partial and the paper does not overstate them.** Anonymity in a small cohort is nominal, and a learner who fears attribution will still self-censor.

**The all-technical list.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that at least one cause be organisational, political or commercial, and the corpus's own observation that resistance originates in ownership of process rather than in technical incapacity.

**The ratified plan.** The exercise is run after the decision. *Countermeasure:* placement before [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md) and before any investigation, and the standard's requirement that the record changed something nameable.

**The unrouted list.** Forty causes, no instruments, filed. *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — the record took longer to produce than the cheapest test on it would have taken to run — and the review movement, which is about outputs rather than about causes.

**The check that cannot see its own cause.** The learner writes a row about a first response and scores it under the default split, where it passes. *Countermeasure:* none, deliberately. **The design lets this failure occur and then requires it to be recorded**, because the general principle is not believed until the instance is met. Exercise 2 is the recording, and §9's prediction 5 measures whether the lesson survives to the next session.

**The inherited threshold.** A model-judged evaluator's default cut point is accepted, and the resulting pass rate is reported as a property of the system. *Countermeasure:* the one-sentence justification in workflow step 6, which permits "it was the default" as an answer. **This is the weakest countermeasure in the session**, because it converts an unexamined policy into a recorded one and does nothing to make it a better policy. It is included because the platform publishes no threshold rationale at all, so no better countermeasure is available from the source.

**The uncalibrated judge trusted.** A model-judged verdict is treated as ground truth. *Countermeasure:* exercise 5, which requires the learner to hand-label a set and compare. **It surfaces disagreement and cannot resolve it**, since nothing in the output says which party is wrong — and the documentation publishes no agreement data that would help.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: this is a ritual with an appealing story attached, and the story is doing all the work.**

At its strongest: nobody has shown that a pre-mortem improves any outcome in any setting resembling this one. The mechanism is plausible, the exercise is enjoyable, and the output is a list — and lists are the least effective control in engineering practice, which is why the discipline invented tests. A curriculum that spends a session on a facilitation device and then admits it has no evidence for it has taught a superstition with unusually good manners.

**Substantially conceded.** No source behind this session measures its effect, and the corpus that supplies the practice context records no failure at all, so it cannot supply a single case in which a pre-mortem's causes were later checked against an outcome. The rebuttal is narrow and is about cost rather than efficacy: the exercise is short, it is run before anything has been spent, and its output is required to be routed to instruments that are themselves cheap. **A cheap control with a plausible mechanism and no evidence is worth running; it is not worth believing in**, and the session's limits movement says exactly that. **The declined fix is to remove the session and fold the failure question into [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md)'s question set.** It is declined because the question set is written by somebody who already believes the design works, and the grammatical device is the only thing in the module that reliably produces a cause its author would not have volunteered.

**Objection 2: anonymity is theatre in a cohort of this size, and pretending otherwise teaches learners a false safety.**

At its strongest: in a room of people who know each other's projects and writing, an anonymous cause about the design is attributable within seconds. Telling learners that anonymous collection protects them is worse than not offering it, because they will rely on it in a workplace where the same reasoning fails and the consequences are real.

**Conceded without qualification, and the design's response is a substitution rather than a defence.** The session does not claim anonymity works; it claims that the instructor putting an uncomfortable cause first does more than anonymity does, and it makes that the primary mechanism with anonymity as a secondary. **The declined fix is to run the generation phase through an anonymous digital channel with genuine unlinkability.** It is declined because the mechanism the design actually wants is a norm that survives into a workplace, and a technical guarantee teaches learners to depend on infrastructure that will not be there. **That is design reasoning and nothing warrants it.**

**Objection 3: the session's own sources say that criteria cannot be fixed before outputs are observed, and the session asks learners to fix criteria before outputs exist.**

At its strongest: the evaluation research this paper cites approvingly in §3.7 reports that some evaluation criteria are dependent on the specific outputs observed, and states that this "raises serious questions for approaches that assume the independence of evaluation from observation of model outputs". **The workflow is precisely such an approach**, in its purest possible form — the outputs do not merely go unobserved, they do not exist. A learner who follows this session will write a rubric, discover on contact with real outputs that most of it was wrong, and reasonably conclude that the exercise was a waste. Worse, some will not discover it, and will keep a rubric that measures what they feared instead of what matters.

**Conceded, and it is the most serious methodological objection to this session.** The design's response is to retreat to a narrower claim and to instrument it. The narrower claim is that the pre-mortem-derived evaluation is a *part* of the rubric — the part fear can supply — and that it has one property no later part can have: it was written while nobody had a stake in it passing. Exercise 6 requires the learner to predict which of their criteria will survive, seal the prediction, and reopen it at [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md), which makes the drift an observation rather than a surprise.

**The declined fix is to move the whole evaluation activity to [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md), after the spike has produced outputs to look at.** It is declined because by then the learner has a working artefact and a stake in it, and the check that would embarrass the artefact is the one that does not get written. **The cost of declining is real and is exactly what the objection names: a proportion of the criteria written here will be wrong, and some learners will keep them.** The design prefers a rubric that is wrong and revisable to one that is complete and never written, and that preference is a judgement rather than a result.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Answers to opening item 1 will be markedly more specific than the same learners' answers to a conventionally worded *what could go wrong* prompt administered as a control. *Instrument:* the two sets of written causes, coded *specific* / *generic* by a second reader who does not know which prompt produced which.

**Prediction 2.** Most learners' pre-instruction lists will be entirely technical. *Instrument:* the five written causes, coded by domain.

**Prediction 3.** The cause a learner nominates at item 3 as hardest to say will more often be organisational than technical. *Instrument:* the item 3 nomination, coded by domain, against the same learner's item 2 list.

**Prediction 4.** A substantial share of causes will be routed to a spike where a single question to a named person would have settled them. *Instrument:* the routing column, with each row independently re-routed by a second reader and the disagreements counted. **If this fails, the module's claim that over-routing is the device's characteristic failure is unsupported**, and the session's emphasis should move to generation instead.

**Prediction 5.** Learners who met the split-strategy collision in workflow step 4 will state a split strategy alongside any score they report at [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) more often than learners who read the material and did not run the workflow. *Instrument:* the [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) finding, coded for whether reported scores carry a stated unit of analysis, split on workflow completion. **This is the only test of §4.1's claim that an engineered collision teaches what a stated principle does not.**

**Prediction 6.** A majority of learners will accept the default threshold on at least one model-judged evaluator and will write "it was the default" as their justification when that answer is permitted. *Instrument:* the step 6 justifications, coded *reasoned* / *inherited* / *fabricated*. **A high fabricated rate would mean permitting the honest answer failed to elicit it**, and the countermeasure should be replaced rather than reworded.

**Prediction 7.** Most criteria written in this session will be revised or abandoned by [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md), and learners will nonetheless report the pre-mortem-derived checks as having changed something. *Instrument:* the sealed exercise 6 prediction against the [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) submission, coded for criterion survival, alongside the learner's own statement of what the record changed. **This is the direct test of §8's third objection.** If criteria survive at a high rate, the criteria-drift concern does not transfer to pre-system checks; if they do not survive and the learner still reports the exercise as consequential, the design's narrower claim holds; **if they do not survive and the learner reports the exercise as wasted, the objection is upheld and this session should move to [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md).**

**Prediction 8.** Learners will disagree with a model-judged evaluator on a substantial share of hand-labelled items in exercise 5, and will resolve the disagreement in the evaluator's favour more often than in their own. *Instrument:* the exercise 5 submission, coded for disagreement and for which verdict the learner adopted. **This predicts the failure of §5's weakest countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption. **It also predicts the specific direction of the failure**: deference to an instrument whose calibration is unpublished.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That stating the failure produces better causes than asking for risks, that the valuable cause is usually the suppressed one, and that a cause is not finished until it has an instrument or an owner.

**What the evidence licenses.** A device and its mechanism, plus one organisation's published decision to place it at the opening of a feasibility investigation. **Nothing about outcomes.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The corpus this stage draws on has never recorded a failure. Everything this session teaches about anticipating one is therefore reasoning, not experience, and the reasoning is cheap enough to be worth running anyway.

**The silences that matter.** **No agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no account of where the default thresholds came from.** Most of these evaluators are model-judged, and the independent literature this paper cites is unambiguous that judges carry measurable biases — which the vendor documentation does not discuss. **This wiki therefore asserts no Foundry evaluator's agreement with human judgement anywhere**, and §5 records the resulting countermeasure as its weakest. The documentation also **publishes no guidance on which split strategy suits which question** beyond a short "best for" phrase per strategy, so the decision the session treats as consequential is one the source names and does not help with. It **states no expected variance across repetitions**, so a learner who runs the same query twice and gets different verdicts has no published expectation to compare against.
