> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Manufactured dependency** | A reliance on the delivery team created by the engagement itself, as distinct from one inherent to the problem |
> | **Disposal** | One of four decisions about a dependency — transfer, simplify away, document and accept, remove — each with a different decay profile |
> | **Comprehension debt** | Code that exists, passes its tests, and is understood by nobody. The obligation to understand it is a separate transferable object from the code |

## In plain terms

Almost every dependency on a delivery team was created without a decision, by somebody being helpful.

The script that only one person can run was written to save an afternoon. The threshold with no recorded reason was set correctly under time pressure. The escalation path ends at a personal contact because that was the fastest thing to write in the runbook, and it worked. None of these was a design choice, and that is what makes them invisible: nobody remembers deciding, so nobody thinks of them as decisions that could have gone another way.

They are also pleasant. Being needed is agreeable, and where the delivery organisation is paid to remain involved it is more than agreeable — it is aligned with somebody's revenue. **A dependency that is both invisible and rewarded will not be found by looking harder.** The session's approach is to make the learner name the one they are slightly pleased about, and then ask what they would have to give up to remove it.

The session's strongest position is unwarranted and is stated as such: **prefer removal to documentation.** A document goes stale silently, and its staleness is discovered by somebody following it during an incident. A capability that no longer exists cannot decay, needs no runbook, no owner and no rehearsal. That is an argument from decay profiles, and nobody has tested it.

## Abstract

This paper defends an inventory-and-disposal exercise over the dependencies an engagement manufactures. The argument is that manufactured dependencies are created without decisions, are invisible to their creators, and are frequently rewarded, so they require a deliberate enumeration with a forced disposal rather than a review. The session produces a dependency disposal record with four permitted decisions — transfer, simplify away, document and accept, remove — and requires at least one non-documentary disposal. It extends the inventory to organisational dependencies and to comprehension debt, treating the obligation to understand generated code as a separate transferable object from the code. The paper concedes that its preference for removal over documentation is an untested argument from decay, that the commercial-incentive failure mode has no countermeasure, and that the source material for comprehension debt is practitioner judgement with no incidence, consequence or measurement behind it.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

At the end of an engagement, the set of things only the delivery team can do is larger than the delivery team believes and smaller than the customer fears, and neither party can enumerate it.

The team cannot see it because the dependencies are made of their own competence, which does not present itself as an artefact. The customer cannot see it because they only discover a dependency by hitting it, which happens after the team has gone.

There is a third party in the room whose interest is not neutral. Where the delivery organisation is paid for continued involvement, every dependency is a commercial asset, and the person best placed to remove it is the person least rewarded for doing so. **That is not a hypothetical and it is not usually corrupt** — it is an ordinary alignment problem that operates through what nobody thinks to raise.

The session's job is to force enumeration, force a disposal decision per line, and require the commercial position to be written on the record rather than left as the reason nothing was removed.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why four disposals rather than a review.** A review produces observations. A forced choice among four options produces decisions, and the four are chosen because they have different decay profiles: a transferred dependency decays with the transferee's attention, a documented one decays silently, a simplified-away one cannot decay, and a removed one takes a capability with it. **Making the learner choose is what surfaces that they had been choosing "document" by default.**

**3.2 Why removal is preferred, and why the preference is unwarranted.** The argument is that a document's staleness is discovered by a person following it under pressure, whereas an absent capability announces itself immediately and cheaply. That is a claim about when errors surface, it is plausible, and **no source behind this programme tests it.** It is stated as the session's strongest position and marked as its least supported.

**3.3 Why organisational dependencies count.** An escalation path ending at a personal contact, a credential in an individual's name, a review only one person has performed, a meeting that happens because one person convenes it — each is a single point of failure with the same properties as a technical one and none of the visibility, because none of them is in a repository.

**3.4 Why comprehension is a separate object from code.** Generated code that exists, passes and is understood by nobody transfers as a file and does not transfer as an understanding. **The obligation to understand it is a distinct thing somebody must accept**, and a handover that treats the two as one object has transferred the smaller half. The phenomenon is recorded in the practice corpus in practitioners' own voice; **it is a judgement offered by experienced people, not a finding**, with no incidence, no consequence data and no measurement, and it is used here for direction only.

**3.5 Why the commercial position goes on the record.** Not as an accusation. The record's purpose is that a disposal declined for commercial reasons should be visible as such to whoever reads it later, rather than appearing as an option nobody considered.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**Opening item 3 is the instrument.** *Which of the three is the one you are slightly pleased about?* The question works because it is answerable instantly and because the answer is uncomfortable in a mild, non-defensive way — mild enough that learners answer honestly, uncomfortable enough that the answer is remembered. A question asking which dependency is most harmful produces analysis; this one produces disclosure.

**The requirement of at least one non-documentary disposal is the session's forcing function.** Without it, a competent learner produces a complete inventory and disposes of nothing, which is indistinguishable from thoroughness.

**The simplify-away requirement asks what capability was given up**, because a simplification that costs nothing was not a trade and probably was not a simplification. A system with fewer things that can be tuned is a system with fewer things to explain, and it is also a system that does less; making the learner state the second half is what stops the disposal record becoming a list of things that were never needed.

**The review movement asks a single question about the pleasing dependency:** what would you have to give up to remove it? *Nothing* means it should already be gone. *I cannot say* means the learner does not understand the dependency they are defending.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Documentation as disposal.** Every line documented, nothing disposed. *Countermeasure:* the requirement of at least one non-documentary disposal, which is a floor rather than a standard.

**Dependency read as expertise.** A short inventory the learner is comfortable with. *Countermeasure:* opening item 3 and the review movement. **Both are self-report and a learner who does not experience the discomfort will produce nothing.**

**The dependency the learner is.** Their judgement about when something is wrong does not appear on any inventory because it is not an artefact. *Countermeasure:* partial — the standard names it, and the observable proxy is the answer to how the recipient will know something has gone wrong. A learner who answers *they will see it* has located the dependency without recognising it.

**The deliberate dependency.** A cheap simplification is available and not taken, because taking it would end the relationship. *Countermeasure:* **none.** The session requires the commercial position on the record and cannot compel a truthful one. This is named rather than solved, in the session and here.

**The generated inheritance.** Code and comprehension transferred as one object. *Countermeasure:* the record requires the obligation to understand to have its own line and its own acceptor.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: preferring removal to documentation optimises for the delivery team's exit at the customer's expense.**

At its strongest: the capability being removed was built because somebody wanted it. Deleting it so that it need not be explained transfers the cost of the handover from the departing team to the customer, who loses a function they paid for and may not discover the loss until they need it. Framed as engineering hygiene, this is a supplier reducing its own liability and calling it simplification.

**This is conceded and it is the sharpest objection to the session.** The mitigation is procedural and thin: the disposal record requires the given-up capability to be stated, and a removal whose cost is not stated is not a disposal. **The fix that was considered and declined** was to require the recipient's agreement to every removal, which was rejected because a recipient asked whether they want to keep a capability will always say yes, at no cost to themselves, and the requirement would convert every removal into a documentation line. That rejection assumes the recipient's stated preference is uninformative — which is a convenient assumption for the departing party, and the objection is entitled to point that out.

**Objection 2: comprehension debt is being treated as an established phenomenon when its entire warrant is a practitioner's remark.**

At its strongest: the claim that generated code exists, passes and is understood by nobody appears in the source corpus as a line of experienced judgement, with no incidence, no consequence data, no comparison against hand-written code of similar age, and no measurement of any kind. Building a transferable object called *the obligation to understand* on top of it gives a specific shape to something that has only been gestured at. Hand-written code is also frequently understood by nobody, and the session implies a distinction it has not established.

The rebuttal is narrow and does not dispute the evidence problem. The session's claim is only that code and comprehension are separable objects for the purposes of a handover, and that treating them as one transfers the smaller half. **That is a claim about what a manifest should contain, not about generated code specifically**, and it survives if the phenomenon turns out to be no worse for generated code than for any other. The corpus's remark supplies the motivation and not the warrant, and the paper should not be read as claiming more.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** First-draft inventories will contain more technical than organisational dependencies. *Instrument:* inventory lines coded *technical* / *organisational* / *comprehension* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Absent the forcing requirement, most disposal records would be entirely documentary. *Instrument:* the disposal column on the pre-requirement draft where one is collected, tallied by decision type.

**Prediction 3.** The dependency named at opening item 3 will more often be disposed as *transfer* or *document* than as *simplify away* or *remove*. *Instrument:* the item-3 answer matched to its disposal decision. **If this fails, the pleasing-dependency diagnostic is not detecting what the session claims** and item 3 should be replaced.

**Prediction 4.** Few learners will write a commercial position when their situation implies one. *Instrument:* presence or absence of the commercial paragraph, against whether the supplied situation makes continued involvement plausible.

**None of these has been measured.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That manufactured dependencies are created without decisions and are therefore invisible to their creators; that documenting one is not disposing of it; and that code and the obligation to understand it are separate transferable objects.

**What the evidence licenses.** Very little. Comprehension debt is practitioner judgement with no measurement. The preference for removal is an argument from decay profiles that nobody has tested. The four disposals are a design device.

**The concession worth remembering.** The session has no countermeasure for the case where remaining needed is rewarded, which is the ordinary case in commercial delivery. It names the problem and stops there, and a learner should not mistake the naming for a solution.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That the practice corpus records comprehension debt in practitioners' own voice — generated code that exists, passes and is understood by nobody — and offers it as judgement with no incidence, consequence or measurement; that the corpus's model rests on expert human validation and nowhere addresses what happens when generation accelerates and validation does not; and that no cost side of any kind appears in the corpus, with staffing cost, compute cost, discovery cost and opportunity cost all absent. Recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md).

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That documentation decays silently while an absent capability announces itself; that a single point of failure in an organisational route has the same properties as one in a technical route; and that an incentive to remain involved operates through what nobody raises rather than through explicit decisions. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The four disposal decisions and their decay-profile justification; the preference for removal over documentation; the requirement of at least one non-documentary disposal; the requirement that a simplification state the capability given up; the treatment of the obligation to understand as a separately transferable object with its own acceptor; the requirement that the commercial position appear on the record; the pleasing-dependency diagnostic at opening item 3; and all four predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** **This session has no platform anchor and relies on no vendor documentation.** Its subject is what an engagement did to a customer's ability to operate without it, which no product describes. **The silence worth recording is one a learner will meet directly:** code-generation tooling publishes guidance on producing and reviewing generated output, and publishes nothing about who is subsequently obliged to understand it, how a team establishes that anybody does, or what happens to that obligation at handover. The gap between generation and comprehension is created at the tooling layer and addressed nowhere in the tooling layer's documentation. If a vendor publishes any account of comprehension ownership, this session acquires an anchor and this paper records its version and what it still omits.
