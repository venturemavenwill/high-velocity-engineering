# WP-078 — The Dependency You Created

**Seminar day:** [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md) · **Module:** [M16](/wiki/modules/M16.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 5
**Platform anchor:** Foundry hosted agents and their operating burden — the container, the capacity commitment and the evaluation cadence somebody inherits

> ## Release rule
>

> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Manufactured dependency** | A reliance on the delivery team created by the engagement itself, as distinct from one inherent to the problem |
> | **Disposal** | One of four decisions about a dependency — transfer, simplify away, document and accept, remove — each with a different decay profile |
> | **Comprehension debt** | Code that exists, passes its tests, and is understood by nobody. The obligation to understand it is a separate transferable object from the code |
> | **Operating burden** | What a running system obliges somebody to do on a cadence, as distinct from what it does |
> | **Prompt agent / hosted agent** | The platform's own split: declarative configuration with no agent compute, versus containerised code on managed container compute |
> | **Quota / capacity** | A policy limit that costs nothing and guarantees nothing, versus the amount actually deployable right now |
> | **One-way door** | An operation that appears reversible and is not, because what it releases may not be available on return |
> | **Split strategy** | The rule turning a multi-turn conversation into a query and a response for evaluation. It determines what a score means |

## In plain terms

Almost every dependency on a delivery team was created without a decision, by somebody being helpful.

The script that only one person can run was written to save an afternoon. The threshold with no recorded reason was set correctly under time pressure. The escalation path ends at a personal contact because that was the fastest thing to write in the runbook, and it worked. None of these was a design choice, and that is what makes them invisible: nobody remembers deciding, so nobody thinks of them as decisions that could have gone another way.

**And the largest one is not a habit or a script. It is the system.** A running agent is not an asset a recipient receives; it is an obligation they assume. If it is containerised code rather than configuration, somebody must rebuild it when its base image is patched. If it sits on dedicated capacity, it bills whether or not anybody uses it, and the commitment has a term. If it carries an evaluation harness, somebody must run it, read it and act on it — on a cadence, against thresholds nobody justified, using a split strategy nobody recorded. **On the day it ships, all of that looks like success.**

Dependencies are also pleasant. Being needed is agreeable, and where the delivery organisation is paid to remain involved it is more than agreeable — it is aligned with somebody's revenue. **A dependency that is both invisible and rewarded will not be found by looking harder.** The session's approach is to make the learner name the one they are slightly pleased about, and then ask what they would have to give up to remove it.

The session's strongest position is unwarranted and is stated as such: **prefer removal to documentation.** A document goes stale silently, and its staleness is discovered by somebody following it during an incident. A capability that no longer exists cannot decay, needs no runbook, no owner and no rehearsal. That is an argument from decay profiles, and nobody has tested it.

## Abstract

This paper defends an inventory-and-disposal exercise over the dependencies an engagement manufactures, extended to cover the running system itself.

**The first argument is about visibility.** Manufactured dependencies are created without decisions, are invisible to their creators because they are made of the creators' own competence, and are frequently rewarded. They therefore require a deliberate enumeration with a forced disposal rather than a review. The session produces a dependency disposal record with four permitted decisions — transfer, simplify away, document and accept, remove — and requires at least one non-documentary disposal, because without a forcing function a competent learner produces a complete inventory and disposes of nothing.

**The second argument is that the heaviest dependency is the thing the team is proudest of**, and the platform anchor is chosen to make that concrete rather than rhetorical. The vendor documentation draws the line itself: a declarative agent is configuration managed with no agent compute, and a hosted agent is containerised code on managed container compute. **That boundary is the boundary between something a recipient can read and something they must operate.** The capacity documentation then supplies three properties that turn a running system into a commitment: dedicated capacity is held whether or not requests are made, so billing changes from usage to time; having quota does not guarantee capacity is available; and releasing capacity offers no guarantee it can be reacquired — a one-way door wearing the costume of a toggle. The evaluation documentation supplies a fourth: a score's meaning depends on a split strategy somebody chose and nobody recorded, thresholds are policy rather than measurement, and **no agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator.**

**The third argument concerns what a heavy inheritance does to the group receiving it.** The collective-efficacy literature catalogues underminers of a group's belief in its joint capability, and three of them — dependence on complex technologies most people neither understand nor believe they can influence, diffuse accountability, and long delays between action and visible result — describe a containerised agent on a capacity commitment with a model-judged evaluation cadence. That is not a metaphor; it is the design decision, read back.

The paper concedes that its preference for removal over documentation is an untested argument from decay; that the commercial-incentive failure mode has no countermeasure; that the source material for comprehension debt is practitioner judgement with no incidence, consequence or measurement behind it; and that every platform fact it uses is definitional, single-vendor and perishable, with no adoption data, no failure-rate data and no evidence that the recommended mappings produce better outcomes.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

At the end of an engagement, the set of things only the delivery team can do is larger than the delivery team believes and smaller than the customer fears, and neither party can enumerate it.

The team cannot see it because the dependencies are made of their own competence, which does not present itself as an artefact. The customer cannot see it because they only discover a dependency by hitting it, which happens after the team has gone.

**There is a second class the session exists to add, and it is the class nobody counts because it does not feel like a dependency at all.** The running system obliges work. Somebody must patch what it runs on, renew what it is committed to, and execute the cadence that tells anybody whether it still works. None of those obligations is in a repository, none appears in a manifest of components, and all of them arrive on a date the departing team chose without noticing they were choosing.

There is a third party in the room whose interest is not neutral. Where the delivery organisation is paid for continued involvement, every dependency is a commercial asset, and the person best placed to remove it is the person least rewarded for doing so. **That is not a hypothetical and it is not usually corrupt** — it is an ordinary alignment problem that operates through what nobody thinks to raise.

The session's job is to force enumeration across all three classes, force a disposal decision per line, and require the commercial position to be written on the record rather than left as the reason nothing was removed.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why four disposals rather than a review.** A review produces observations. A forced choice among four options produces decisions, and the four are chosen because they have different decay profiles: a transferred dependency decays with the transferee's attention, a documented one decays silently, a simplified-away one cannot decay, and a removed one takes a capability with it. **Making the learner choose is what surfaces that they had been choosing "document" by default.**

**3.2 Why removal is preferred, and why the preference is unwarranted.** The argument is that a document's staleness is discovered by a person following it under pressure, whereas an absent capability announces itself immediately and cheaply. That is a claim about when errors surface, it is plausible, and **no source behind this programme tests it.** It is stated as the session's strongest position and marked as its least supported.

**3.3 Why organisational dependencies count.** An escalation path ending at a personal contact, a credential in an individual's name, a review only one person has performed, a meeting that happens because one person convenes it — each is a single point of failure with the same properties as a technical one and none of the visibility, because none of them is in a repository.

**3.4 Why comprehension is a separate object from code.** Generated code that exists, passes and is understood by nobody transfers as a file and does not transfer as an understanding. **The obligation to understand it is a distinct thing somebody must accept**, and a handover that treats the two as one object has transferred the smaller half. The phenomenon is recorded in the practice corpus in practitioners' own voice ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)); **it is a judgement offered by experienced people, not a finding**, with no incidence, no consequence data and no measurement, and it is used here for direction only. **Access-controlled material, paraphrased and never quoted.**

**3.5 The hosting boundary is the handover boundary.**

The platform draws the line itself, and it is worth taking literally ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). **Prompt agents** use "declarative config in portal or code", are "managed by Foundry with no agent compute", and suit "agents that don't include custom code or require custom hosting". **Hosted agents** run "your code (containerized)", are "managed by Foundry (container compute)", and suit "agents with custom code running in a managed container".

**The distinction is hosting rather than capability, and the consequence is entirely about who operates what.** Configuration is a thing a recipient reads and edits. A container is a build, a registry, a base image with a patch cadence, a dependency set that drifts, and a compute allocation that bills. **The engineering decision that crosses that line is a handover decision, and it is almost never recognised as one at the time it is made** — which is why the session asks for it to be recorded per component rather than discovered per incident.

The same family records that the **basic setup** is "for rapid prototyping and individual development when speed and ease of access are priorities" and **"lacks network isolation"**, while the standard setup provides "fine-grained control over data, security, and networking". **Prototyping topology and production topology are different things and the transition is a deliberate act** — an act somebody must perform, frequently left undone at handover because it is not a feature and nobody's plan contains it.

**Its limits:** first-party product documentation with a selection-guidance purpose; definitional authority only; **no adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no evidence that its recommended mappings produce better outcomes**; highly perishable, with product names and feature boundaries actively changing; read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches.

**3.6 A capacity commitment is a bequest with a term, and it has three traps.**

This is the section with the sharpest practical consequence, and every claim in it is a quoted property of the platform rather than an inference ([research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md)).

The defining property is one clause: a provisioned deployment **"holds a fixed amount of processing capacity exclusively for your deployment's use, whether or not requests are being made."** Everything else follows. Capacity held whether used or not must be paid for whether used or not, so the billing unit changes from tokens to time — the hourly meter runs **"regardless of the number of tokens consumed"**, from deployment creation to deletion. **A recipient's cost therefore does not fall when nobody uses the system**, and they will discover that on a statement rather than in a runbook.

**Trap one: permission is not availability.** Quota is "a policy limit enforced by Azure, and it has no associated cost". Capacity is "the actual amount of PTUs per model version that's available to be deployed". The documentation's own call-out: **"Having PTU quota doesn't guarantee that capacity is available. If capacity in the region is insufficient for the requested PTU count, the deployment fails."**

**Trap two: releasing is a one-way door.** Availability "changes throughout the day based on customer demand", and releasing capacity carries **"no guarantee the same capacity is available if you re-create or scale the deployment up later."** The documentation then advises directly against the economy a cost-conscious recipient will attempt first: **"don't plan to scale provisioned deployments up and down with traffic to stay on hourly billing"**, for two stated reasons.

**Trap three: a financial commitment reserves nothing.** Reservations are "a financial discount applied to the PTU billing meter… not to individual deployments", and **"Reservations don't guarantee capacity. First create deployments to confirm that capacity is available, then purchase the reservation to lock in the discounted rate."** Deploy, verify, then commit — and the intuitive sequence is exactly backwards.

**These are the clearest available instances of a general property this programme cares about: a permission-versus-availability distinction, and an operation that looks reversible and is not.** Both generalise well beyond this product, which is why the session teaches them as classes with an instance rather than as product facts.

**Its limits:** first-party documentation, definitional rather than empirical, single-vendor, with no comparison against alternatives and no evidence of observed reliability; **perishable on every specific** — names, defaults, ratios, minimum deployment sizes and portal paths all change; and the concrete per-model figures live on companion pages that were not fetched and **are not reproduced here or anywhere in this wiki**.

**3.7 An evaluation cadence is a dependency with a rota, and its numbers carry undeclared choices.**

An evaluation harness is not a thing that runs; it is a thing somebody must run, read and act on ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). Three decisions inside it are made casually by a departing team and cannot be safely revisited by a recipient.

**The split strategy.** "Multi-turn conversations must be split into query and response halves for evaluation. **How you split determines *what you're evaluating*.**" An agent that recovers from a bad first attempt scores well under one strategy and badly under another; neither score is wrong, and they answer different questions. **A number inherited without its strategy is an answer to a question nobody was told had been asked.**

**The thresholds.** Output is pass or fail with a `reason`, plus a numeric score for scale-based evaluators. The threshold is a policy choice separable from the measurement, and moving it changes the pass rate without changing the system. **An inherited threshold is a decision the recipient now defends and did not make.**

**Repetitions.** Supplied "to detect non-deterministic behavior" — the acknowledgement that one sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement.

**And the silence that matters most: no agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold.** Most agent evaluators are model-judged. **Assert no agreement figure for any of them, here or anywhere.** A recipient inheriting a dashboard has inherited an apparatus whose correspondence to competent human judgement is undetermined — which is [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s machinery-is-not-a-result arriving as somebody else's operating burden.

**Its limits:** read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, with structure reconstructed; most agent evaluators marked preview; one consulted page explicitly superseded; cloud evaluation requires a project with a model deployment and incurs cost, which is a second bill on the same inheritance.

**3.8 Why interdependence makes the inventory longer than expected, and deletion the strongest disposal.**

The change literature's extended image is of an office where every piece of furniture is joined to every other by ropes, rubber bands and steel cables, concluding that **"changing highly interdependent settings is extremely difficult because, ultimately, you have to change nearly everything"** ([research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md](/research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md)). The recommendation this session builds on is the corollary: **"the purging of unnecessary interconnections can ultimately make a transformation much easier."** A document about a connection is one more thing joined to everything else; a deleted connection is not.

**Its warrant is practitioner counsel and its author states it as such** — no footnotes or endnotes by design, no major idea from any published source except his own writing, no cited evidence, all cases pseudonymous and untraceable. **Direction and mechanism only; none of its numbers appears anywhere in this wiki.**

**3.9 Why a cadence needs an accountability system rather than a calendar.**

The change model this module borrows contributes reinforcement, and its useful claim is that accountability systems are **"one of the strongest forms of reinforcement"** ([research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md)). Its illustrative failure is a bank that deployed a quality programme well, disbanded the implementation team, and lost the programme within a year, with the diagnosis stated as the transferable part: **"The first mistake the implementation team made was to assume that managing change is essentially completing activities in a project plan."**

**Its warrant is thin and is stated wherever it appears:** a trademarked commercial model published by the firm that sells the training and certification in it, whose quantitative claims come entirely from that firm's own practitioner self-report surveys, and which cites **no independent evaluation of itself anywhere.** Its reinforcement list does not include removing the old way as a still-available option; that item was invented by an earlier reading in this repository and withdrawn.

**3.10 What a heavy inheritance does to the group that receives it.**

This is the section that converts the platform material from operational detail into a design argument ([research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md](/research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md)).

Collective efficacy is a group's belief in its **joint** capability — "to forge divergent self-interests into a shared agenda, to enlist supporters and resources for collective action, to devise effective strategies and to execute them successfully, and to withstand forcible opposition and discouraging setbacks". Its catalogued underminers include dependence on **"complex physical technologies that most people neither understand nor believe they can do much to influence"**; the diffusion of accountability, where efforts are "blunted by mazy organizational mechanisms that diffuse and obscure responsibility"; and long delays between action and noticeable result, which "discourage many advocates along the way".

**A containerised agent on a capacity commitment with a model-judged evaluation cadence installs three of those by construction.** It is a technology most of the receiving team does not understand; its responsibility is diffused across a platform team, a supplier and a business owner; and its evaluation signal arrives long after the change that caused it. The conclusion the module carries is the one that makes the disposal record a decision rather than an audit: **"The psychological barriers created by beliefs of collective powerlessness are more demoralizing and debilitating than are external impediments."**

**Its limits:** a conference collection edited by the field's founder, with no search strategy, no inclusion criteria and no disconfirming study in the editor's own chapter; several load-bearing results cited as unpublished manuscripts; settings in schools, families, clinics, migration and addiction treatment; **no study of adult professionals learning a technical craft**, so applying it to a receiving engineering team is an inference; self-report measurement throughout; and the foundational 1977 paper unread in this repository. **Its magnitudes are recorded in the research layer and prohibited here.**

**3.11 Why the commercial position goes on the record.** Not as an accusation. The record's purpose is that a disposal declined for commercial reasons should be visible as such to whoever reads it later, rather than appearing as an option nobody considered.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 Opening item 3 is the instrument.** *Which of the three is the one you are slightly pleased about?* The question works because it is answerable instantly and because the answer is uncomfortable in a mild, non-defensive way — mild enough that learners answer honestly, uncomfortable enough that the answer is remembered. A question asking which dependency is most harmful produces analysis; this one produces disclosure.

**4.2 The requirement of at least one non-documentary disposal is the session's forcing function.** Without it, a competent learner produces a complete inventory and disposes of nothing, which is indistinguishable from thoroughness.

**4.3 The simplify-away requirement asks what capability was given up**, because a simplification that costs nothing was not a trade and probably was not a simplification. A system with fewer things that can be tuned is a system with fewer things to explain, and it is also a system that does less; making the learner state the second half is what stops the disposal record becoming a list of things that were never needed.

**4.4 The record is written where a machine can read it, and that is a deliberate departure from the session's own preference for prose.** An unowned line in a document is a line nobody notices. An unowned line in a validated file fails a check. **The session is applying [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md)'s own claim to its own artefact**, which is the strongest available consistency test of that claim and is not framed as evidence for it.

**4.5 The platform half is chosen to make one inventory line indefensible.** A learner can argue about whether an escalation route is a dependency. **Nobody can argue that a meter running from creation to deletion is not an obligation**, and the capacity material is in the session because it is the one line on the record that cannot be talked away. It is also the line most likely to be missing from a first draft, because it is nobody's component.

**4.6 The review movement asks a single question about the pleasing dependency:** what would you have to give up to remove it? *Nothing* means it should already be gone. *I cannot say* means the learner does not understand the dependency they are defending.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Documentation as disposal.** Every line documented, nothing disposed. *Countermeasure:* the requirement of at least one non-documentary disposal, which is a floor rather than a standard.

**Dependency read as expertise.** A short inventory the learner is comfortable with. *Countermeasure:* opening item 3 and the review movement. **Both are self-report and a learner who does not experience the discomfort will produce nothing.**

**The operating burden that is nobody's component.** The base-image patch cadence, the capacity renewal, the evaluation rota — none belongs to a named part of the system, so none appears in a component inventory. *Countermeasure:* the record's separate section for commitments with a term and for recurring obligations, each requiring a name and a date. **Partial**: the learner still has to think of the obligation before the record can require an owner for it.

**Quota recorded as an entitlement.** *Countermeasure:* the record asks for a successful deployment against the allocation rather than the allocation figure. **Weak**, because a deployment that succeeded on the audit date says nothing about the day the recipient needs one.

**The dependency the learner is.** Their judgement about when something is wrong does not appear on any inventory because it is not an artefact. *Countermeasure:* partial — the standard names it, and the observable proxy is the answer to how the recipient will know something has gone wrong. A learner who answers *they will see it* has located the dependency without recognising it.

**The deliberate dependency.** A cheap simplification is available and not taken, because taking it would end the relationship. *Countermeasure:* **none.** The session requires the commercial position on the record and cannot compel a truthful one. This is named rather than solved, in the session and here.

**The generated inheritance.** Code and comprehension transferred as one object. *Countermeasure:* the record requires the obligation to understand to have its own line and its own acceptor.

**The orphaned cadence.** The harness transfers, nobody owns running it, and it stays green because nothing runs. *Countermeasure:* the rota with named people and asking dates. **This is the countermeasure most likely to fail after the session ends** — see prediction 6 — because a name written at handover is not a name that survives a reorganisation.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No specification item. The disposal record contributes observation towards [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md).

**What the entrustment evidence draws from it is not the number of disposals** but whether the learner's account of what the recipient depends on matched what the rehearsal at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) revealed. A learner who inventoried thoroughly and was surprised at the rehearsal has learned something visible; a learner who was not surprised may have inventoried well or may have run an undemanding rehearsal, and the two are separated by reading both records together.

**The platform lines are not assessed for accuracy**, and the reason matters. Every one of them is a fact about one configuration on one day, on a documentation surface that is actively changing. **Assessing them would teach learners to memorise a deployment taxonomy that will be wrong within a year**, which is the failure the perishability register exists to prevent. What carries forward is whether the learner distinguished permission from availability and marked the one-way doors.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: preferring removal to documentation optimises for the delivery team's exit at the customer's expense.**

At its strongest: the capability being removed was built because somebody wanted it. Deleting it so that it need not be explained transfers the cost of the handover from the departing team to the customer, who loses a function they paid for and may not discover the loss until they need it. Framed as engineering hygiene, this is a supplier reducing its own liability and calling it simplification.

**This is conceded and it is the sharpest objection to the session.** The mitigation is procedural and thin: the disposal record requires the given-up capability to be stated, and a removal whose cost is not stated is not a disposal. **The fix that was considered and declined** was to require the recipient's agreement to every removal, which was rejected because a recipient asked whether they want to keep a capability will always say yes, at no cost to themselves, and the requirement would convert every removal into a documentation line. That rejection assumes the recipient's stated preference is uninformative — which is a convenient assumption for the departing party, and the objection is entitled to point that out.

**Objection 2: comprehension debt is being treated as an established phenomenon when its entire warrant is a practitioner's remark.**

At its strongest: the claim that generated code exists, passes and is understood by nobody appears in the source corpus as a line of experienced judgement, with no incidence, no consequence data, no comparison against hand-written code of similar age, and no measurement of any kind. Building a transferable object called *the obligation to understand* on top of it gives a specific shape to something that has only been gestured at. Hand-written code is also frequently understood by nobody, and the session implies a distinction it has not established.

The rebuttal is narrow and does not dispute the evidence problem. The session's claim is only that code and comprehension are separable objects for the purposes of a handover, and that treating them as one transfers the smaller half. **That is a claim about what a manifest should contain, not about generated code specifically**, and it survives if the phenomenon turns out to be no worse for generated code than for any other. The corpus's remark supplies the motivation and not the warrant, and the paper should not be read as claiming more.

**Objection 3: the platform half teaches one vendor's product taxonomy and calls it engineering judgement.**

At its strongest: the hosting split, the capacity model, the quota mechanics and the evaluator catalogue are one company's design decisions at one moment. They are documented by the company that sells them, in material whose purpose is selection guidance, with no adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no evidence that its recommended mappings produce better outcomes. Several of the evaluators are marked preview and one consulted page is explicitly superseded. Teaching a learner that hosted agents are heavier than prompt agents installs a product distinction that may not survive a rename, and dressing it as a durable claim about configuration versus compute is exactly the platform-familiarity-instead-of-judgement failure this programme warns about elsewhere.

**Partially conceded, and the design's answer is structural rather than argumentative.** Every platform claim in this session appears in the perishability register with its durable counterpart beside it, none of the platform lines is assessed, and the session's standard asks for the distinction rather than the taxonomy. **The positive reason for keeping it is that the alternative was tried and failed:** an earlier draft of this module made the dependency argument in the abstract, and a dependency argument in the abstract produces agreement and no inventory line, because nothing in it is uncomfortable enough to write down. **A meter that runs from creation to deletion is uncomfortable enough.**

**The declined fix was to state the properties generically — "some capacity models bill for reserved capacity" — without naming the product.** It was declined because the generic form is unfalsifiable and unlookuppable: a learner cannot check it, cannot date it, and cannot discover that it has changed. **The cost of that decision is that this session will need re-verification before every offering**, and the register says so.

**Objection 4: the record's own machine-readable form is a dependency the session creates while teaching against dependencies.**

At its strongest, and it is uncomfortable: the session hands the recipient a file format nobody else uses, validated by a check the departing team wrote, describing obligations in a vocabulary the departing team invented. That is a new manufactured dependency, created in the act of enumerating manufactured dependencies, and the session does not put it on its own record.

**Conceded, and it should be on the record.** The mitigation is that the format is a shape rather than a schema and the session says so in the artefact itself, and that a plain table would work if the team prefers one. **The declined fix was to require the record in whatever format the recipient already uses**, which was rejected because most recipients have no format and the requirement would produce a document. **The residual position is that the session creates one small dependency to make several larger ones visible**, which is a trade rather than a solution, and a learner who adds it as line one of their own record has understood the session better than one who does not.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** First-draft inventories will contain more technical than organisational dependencies, and fewer operating-burden lines than either. *Instrument:* inventory lines coded *technical* / *organisational* / *comprehension* / *operating-burden* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Absent the forcing requirement, most disposal records would be entirely documentary. *Instrument:* the disposal column on the pre-requirement draft where one is collected, tallied by decision type.

**Prediction 3.** The dependency named at opening item 3 will more often be disposed as *transfer* or *document* than as *simplify away* or *remove*. *Instrument:* the item-3 answer matched to its disposal decision. **If this fails, the pleasing-dependency diagnostic is not detecting what the session claims** and item 3 should be replaced.

**Prediction 4.** Few learners will write a commercial position when their situation implies one. *Instrument:* presence or absence of the commercial paragraph, against whether the supplied situation makes continued involvement plausible.

**Prediction 5.** Most first-draft records will omit the idle cost of held capacity and will treat a quota allocation as an entitlement. *Instrument:* the record's commercial section, coded for whether idle billing is stated and whether the quota line is accompanied by evidence of a successful deployment.

**Prediction 6.** Of the recurring obligations recorded with a named owner, most will have no owner in place at the first review after handover. *Instrument:* the rota as written against the same rota re-read one quarter later. **This predicts the failure of the session's own principal countermeasure for the orphaned cadence**, and it is included because a name written at handover is the cheapest possible control and is treated as though it were a strong one.

**Prediction 7.** Learners will classify at least one line as *transfer* that a second reader classifies as *document and accept*, and the disagreement will concentrate on lines with no named cadence. *Instrument:* independent re-coding of the disposal column by a reader who did not write it. **If the disagreement does not concentrate there, the record's cadence field is not doing the work §4.4 claims for it.**

**None of these has been measured.** The register is machinery and not a result about this session — a point [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) makes about this repository as a whole.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That manufactured dependencies are created without decisions and are therefore invisible to their creators; that the heaviest one is the running system itself; that documenting a dependency is not disposing of it; and that code and the obligation to understand it are separate transferable objects.

**What the evidence licenses.** The platform properties are definitional and checkable — you can look them up, and you should, on the date you need them. **Everything else is thin.** Comprehension debt is practitioner judgement with no measurement. The preference for removal is an argument from decay profiles that nobody has tested. The four disposals are a design device. The collective-efficacy material is about a population unlike a receiving engineering team.

**Do not let the checkability of the platform facts transfer to the design argument.** That a meter runs from creation to deletion is verifiable. That enumerating it improves anything is not.

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper**, including the per-model capacity figures, which live on companion pages that were not fetched and would be prohibited here even if they had been.

**The concession worth remembering.** The session has no countermeasure for the case where remaining needed is rewarded, which is the ordinary case in commercial delivery. It names the problem and stops there, and a learner should not mistake the naming for a solution.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That the practice corpus records comprehension debt in practitioners' own voice — generated code that exists, passes and is understood by nobody — and offers it as judgement with no incidence, consequence or measurement; that the corpus's model rests on expert human validation and nowhere addresses what happens when generation accelerates and validation does not; that it contains no handover procedure of any kind and no evidentiary requirement that teardown was performed; and that no cost side appears in it, with staffing, compute, discovery and opportunity cost all absent. Recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md).

The collective-efficacy claims in §3.10 — the definition as joint capability, the three underminers reproduced, and the statement that beliefs of collective powerlessness are more debilitating than external impediments — are recorded at [research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md](/research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md), **which rests on a complete reading of the 1995 volume**, and which also records that the volume is a conference proceedings with no disconfirming result in its lead chapter and contains no study of adult professionals.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That documentation decays silently while an absent capability announces itself; that a single point of failure in an organisational route has the same properties as one in a technical route; that an incentive to remain involved operates through what nobody raises rather than through explicit decisions; that highly interdependent systems are harder to change than loosely coupled ones; and that an accountability system outlasts a calendar entry. The interdependence and reinforcement notes at [research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md](/research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md) are held in this repository and are practitioner counsel by their authors' own accounts, one deliberately unfootnoted and one commercially interested with no independent evaluation cited. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted for any of these.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The four disposal decisions and their decay-profile justification; the preference for removal over documentation; the requirement of at least one non-documentary disposal; the requirement that a simplification state the capability given up; the treatment of the obligation to understand as a separately transferable object with its own acceptor; the extension of the inventory to the running system's operating burden; the requirement that commitments with a term carry a decision date earlier than the expiry; the requirement that one-way doors be marked; the requirement that the commercial position appear on the record; the machine-readable form of the record; the pleasing-dependency diagnostic at opening item 3; and all seven predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The hosting split — prompt agents as declarative configuration with no agent compute, hosted agents as containerised code on managed container compute — and the basic setup's stated lack of network isolation are recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md), **read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches**, and marked highly perishable with product names and feature boundaries actively changing.

The capacity properties in §3.6 — that a provisioned deployment holds capacity whether or not requests are being made; that the hourly meter runs regardless of tokens consumed, from creation to deletion; that quota is a policy limit with no cost and does not guarantee capacity; that capacity availability changes through the day and offers no guarantee of return after release; that reservations discount a meter and do not guarantee capacity, so deployment must precede purchase; and that the documentation explicitly advises against scaling provisioned deployments with traffic, for two stated reasons — are recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md), **fetched and read in full**. The evaluation properties in §3.7 — the three split strategies and the statement that how you split determines what you are evaluating, the pass-or-fail output with a threshold and a reason, and repetitions for detecting non-deterministic behaviour — are recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md), read through search excerpts, with most agent evaluators marked preview and one consulted page an explicitly superseded variant.

**All three sources are first-party product documentation whose authority is definitional.** They state how the platform behaves. **None offers adoption data, failure-rate data, cost comparison, or any evidence that its recommended mappings produce better outcomes.**

**The silences that matter, and there are four.** First, **no agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold** — which is the most consequential gap in the whole session, because most of those evaluators are model-judged and a recipient inherits their verdicts as fact. Second, **the capacity documentation states no probability, frequency or historical record for the case it warns about**: it says capacity may be unavailable on return and never says how often, so the one-way door is documented as a risk with no size. Third, **no per-model capacity figure, minimum deployment size or price appears in the note behind §3.6**, because those live on companion pages that were not fetched; nothing quantitative from that family is asserted anywhere in this wiki. Fourth, and the one a learner meets directly: **code-generation tooling publishes guidance on producing and reviewing generated output, and publishes nothing about who is subsequently obliged to understand it**, how a team establishes that anybody does, or what happens to that obligation at handover. The gap between generation and comprehension is created at the tooling layer and addressed nowhere in the tooling layer's documentation. If a vendor publishes any account of comprehension ownership, this paper records its version and what it still omits.
