# WP-078 — Authorisation at Retrieval Time

**Seminar day:** [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md) · **Module:** [M16](/wiki/modules/M16.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md)
**Strands:** SG · IS · PE · **Complexity class:** 6
**Platform anchor:** Azure AI Search document-level access control — the four approaches, the two-stage check, and index projections · Microsoft Purview DSPM for AI · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server · Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends a day whose subject is a placement decision — where in a retrieval pipeline an authorisation check runs — and defends the claim that the placement students rank safest is the one that is not a check at all. The design leans hardest on four placements of one decision over one corpus, configured and queried on a disposable index and adjudicated against the substrate's own retrieval records, with the payload being answer-time redaction: nothing unauthorised appears in the output and the answer was conditioned on documents the principal could not see. A second payload was added when the day became executable and is now the more instructive of the two: **the student chunks a document, the permission metadata stays on the parent row, and the chunks come back unfiltered while every visible control remains correct.** The day's technical payload is distributive rather than architectural — a filtered approximate search has a recall that falls with the selectivity of the filter, so the least-privileged principal is served worst and is told nothing. **Section 7 discharges the authorisation half of the open question [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5 deferred here** and names what it does not take. Section 8 concedes that the half taken is the tractable half, that the half with ethical weight went elsewhere, that the day's central measurement may have no statistical power on this substrate, and that a day teaching four preview-dependent approaches has anchored its most concrete content to its least stable surface.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The students have owned a production retrieval system for two quarters. They can state what their relevance criterion selects for, they can measure recall per class, and they have a licensing discipline for what a retrieval evaluation supports. **Every one of those competences was developed while running as the service**, with access to everything, and none of them has a coordinate for *who is asking*.

Four difficulties operate and they are of three different kinds.

**A conceptual difficulty that is easy to state and easy to under-rate.** Relevance is a property of a document and a query; authorisation is a property of a document and a principal. The index holds the first. Nothing in [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)'s geometry, [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md)'s approximation or [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md)'s benchmark discipline touches the second. Students will accept this immediately and then design as though the check were a post-processing step, because that is what it looks like from inside the retrieval abstraction.

**A schema difficulty that is not easy.** Filtering is experienced as a *narrowing* — the same search, fewer results — and under that model a filtered search cannot be *worse*, only *smaller*. It is worse. An approximate index searched under a selective predicate visits fewer viable candidates on the way to its budget, so the restricted principal gets a lower-recall search rather than a truncated one. The students hold [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md)'s recall-latency trade and have never applied it to a predicate, and [WP-058](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-058.md) §3.2 excluded the case explicitly and flagged it as the one this day needs.

**A distributive difficulty that is affective as much as cognitive.** The consequence of the previous point is that the system is worst for the person with the least access. Students will not resist the claim; they will fail to feel it, because the worst-served principal is not in the room and, on this substrate, may be three people. The day's answer is to make each student measure it on their own system and then face a partner playing that class — which is a construction that works and, §8 argues, may have nothing to measure.

**And a difficulty that is not technical at all.** What a user is told about what was withheld is a decision with three options, none of which leaks nothing, and it is not the engineer's to make. Students will make it anyway, by default, in an interface string, and the default is silent omission — which phase 6 shows producing harm with no defect anywhere in the system.

**A fifth difficulty exists only because the day is executed, and it is the one that stays with students.** A permission check can be present, correct, tested and enforcing over the wrong rows. The chunking case is the cleanest instance available anywhere in the programme of a control that passes every review a reviewer knows how to perform: the filter is configured, the token is attached, the enforcement runs, and the data it enforces against was left behind by a transformation two steps upstream. **Nothing about it is discoverable by inspection**, which is why the day makes the student produce it rather than describing it.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

Three elements interact and must be held simultaneously: **the placement, the principal, and the evidence the principal has about what they did not receive.** The interaction is what makes point 4 hard — changing the placement changes which principal is worst served *and* changes whether they can tell — and it is why the phase 3 task-after asks both questions about all four placements rather than one question about the payload.

A fourth element, staleness, is deliberately held separate. It interacts with the placement (an index-time check has a re-index latency; a query-time check has a cache expiry) and the design suppresses that interaction by fixing the form: step 5 asks for the latency in [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s four-part shape regardless of placement, so the student computes one number rather than reasoning about a coupling.

Management:

- **The corpus, the query set and the relevance criterion are held constant across all four placements**, so that the only varying dimension is the one the day is about. This is the contrasting-case constraint and violating it here would let groups discriminate on retrieval quality.
- **The permission model is printed**, so phase 1 item 1 is a derivation and not a memory task.
- **Step 3's measurement form is supplied**, since the per-class apparatus is [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md)'s and re-deriving it would consume the phase.
- **Step 1 is worked with the room**, because separating *who asks* from *whose authority* is the step students skip and it invalidates everything after it.
- **Nothing is built from scratch.** The retrieval path exists; the day stands up a disposable copy of its index, adds a check to it, measures, breaks one thing deliberately and fixes it.

Expertise reversal applies at step 3 for students who did substantial evaluation work at [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md): the supplied form is redundant for them and they should be told to ignore it. It does not apply at step 6, which is where the day's judgement is and where nobody in the room has experience.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Deriving the correct result set from a printed permission model before any framework*, item 1, with over-return and under-return scored separately.
- *Predicting whose answers get worse, before measuring it*, item 4 — placed at phase 1 rather than phase 5, for the reason given below.
- *Committing a leakage ranking before the records are supplied*, phase 3.
- *A payload that wins on the criterion the room chose*, P4.
- *Producing a leak on your own index and watching every visible control stay correct*, the chunk-projection run.
- *Being asked how the worst-served principal would find out*, the task-after, in a design where three of four answers are "they would not".
- *Measuring a degradation on your own production system, per class, and naming the class it is worst for*, step 3.
- *Measuring a revocation window rather than estimating one*, step 5, where the documentation supplies no figure to fall back on.
- *Deciding what a user is told about what they did not get, unsupported*, step 7.
- *Facing a partner playing the class you just named as worst-served*, phase 7.
- *Predicting which of four documented approaches an agent will produce, and what it will not say about it*, item 5.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Implementing an authorisation system.* The placement decision is the content; building a policy engine is SG301's and SY301's.
- *Cryptographic access control and encrypted search.* A different problem with different trade-offs, excluded on scope.
- *Multi-tenant isolation at the infrastructure layer.* SG302's and SY301's.
- *Inference from aggregates.* Named in point 2 as an omission and explicitly **not** deferred, because no day in this programme carries it and a deferral to nowhere is what the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) added the deferral rule to prevent. It is a gap, it is stated as one, and §7 records it.
- *The corpus half of [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5.* [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md)'s, with an instrument named. §8 objection 1 examines whether taking the other half was the right division.

**Item 4's placement is itself a selection and is recorded as one.** The design has twice observed that **it places its phase 5 instruments downstream of its own instruction** — opened at [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 and recorded as a second instance at [WP-066](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-066.md) §8 — with the rule it would become already stated: *an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.* The natural design for this day puts the whose-answers-get-worse question at step 3 and reports it at phase 8, and that would be a third instance. **Item 4 exists so that it is not one.** The pattern remains at two instances and is not elevated here; it is cited as a design constraint that changed a phase, which is what a pattern-level observation is for before it becomes a rule.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The four-placement frame is **safe for document-level permissions that are stored, enumerable, and attached to documents that exist in the corpus.** It is **unsafe in three conditions, each of which produces a conforming artefact describing a system that is not the one running.** Where authorisation is computed rather than stored — attribute-based, relationship-based, or derived from a graph the retriever would have to join at query time — the placement question has a fifth answer the frame has no name for, and a student will write "query time" for an architecture in which the predicate cannot be pushed anywhere. Where the sensitive object is a *fact* rather than a *document*, no placement helps: a principal permitted each of five documents may combine them into something they may not have, and every architecture on this list returns all five correctly. And where the corpus does not contain the document at all, the permission model is silent by construction — which is the half of [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5 this day does not take, and is the reason it does not take it. **The first two are stated to students at point 2; the third is stated at point 7 with its home named.**

**The platform instance carries a scope statement of its own and it is the sharpest in the module.** Point 8 teaches one vendor's four documented approaches as the concrete form of the placement question. That is **safe for choosing where a check runs, for predicting what each choice costs and to whom, for knowing that granularity is a property of the source rather than of your intent, and for knowing what to measure** — all of which transfer to any system that copies content out of a permissioned store. It is **unsafe in three directions.** It is unsafe as a security comparison: the documentation compares the four on fit and **not on strength**, and a student who concludes that the native approach is *correct* has quoted a selection table. It is unsafe as stability: **three of the four are preview surfaces**, most of the page describing them is preview API, and the day's most concrete content is therefore its least durable. And it is unsafe as a claim about enforcement quality — **no accuracy figure appears anywhere on the page, and the permission-synchronisation lag is stated and never quantified**, which is why step 5 requires a measurement rather than a citation and why the specification fails an asserted latency. Point 8's own bullets state the first and third to students; the second is stated in the perishability register and in the day's opening.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **step 3 into phase 7**: the student measures which class of principal their own check serves worst, names it, and twenty minutes later a partner plays that class and asks one question.

The mechanism is that the affected party stops being hypothetical. [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) phase 7 used the same structure with a stakeholder the student had reasoned about; here the partner represents a class the student produced a number for, from their own system, in the same phase. **There is no available response that the class is speculative.** The question — *what did I not get, and how would I know?* — is answerable only by a position that has already decided point 6, which is why step 7 is placed before phase 7 and is unsupported.

The second mechanism is P4. The room ranks it safest on the criterion they were asked to rank by, which is leakage, and it is safest by that criterion in the sense that matters least. Discovering that a control can be simultaneously correct and irrelevant is the same discovery [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) point 6 produced one week earlier in a different register, and the design intends the recognition: the two days are one argument about where a control has to sit relative to the content.

**The third mechanism is the chunk-projection run and it is the day's cheapest source of situational interest.** It takes five minutes, it is produced by the student rather than shown, and its result is a leak in a system the student configured correctly. There is no version of this that lands as a slide. The design's expectation is that it is the thing students describe to somebody else that evening, and that expectation is the reason it sits in phase 3 rather than in phase 4, where it would be a fact about a product.

Autonomy is supported by step 2 and step 7 — the placement and the disclosure position are both the student's, and the specification checks neither for correctness. Competence is supported by step 3, which is measurement the cohort is good at. Relatedness is supplied by the data owner at step 8, who has authority and is not obliged to agree.

The affective risk is **discouragement at step 3**, and it is specific: a student who measures a large degradation on the least-privileged class has discovered that their production system, which they defended at [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) and have operated for two quarters, is worst for the people with the least power in it. That is an unpleasant finding about one's own work. The countermeasure is the framing at step 3 and it is stated before the phase: **the degradation is a property of filtered approximate search and not of the student's design**, every placement on the list has a version of it, and the assessed element is that the class is named rather than that the number is small. The specification says so.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items against a printed permission model. Item 1 is scored for over- and under-return separately because the errors are not symmetric. Item 3 asks for a duration and will receive a mechanism. **Item 4 is the payload administered pre-instruction, and its placement is argued at §3.2. Item 5 is the day's Copilot prediction and is the only one in the module with a documented right answer available before the day runs**, which makes it the cohort's cleanest frontier datum.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals at three quarters, two quarters and two weeks. **The [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) item asks for *both* selections explicitly**, which makes it the first available check on [WP-060](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-060.md) §9 prediction 2. The bridge produces the day's structural claim and the room answers it.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four placements, one corpus, one query set, one relevance criterion, **configured and queried on a disposable index carrying synthetic principals**. Ranking committed before running. P4 is the payload. **The task-after produces point 4 and produces the day's distributive finding before phase 4 states it**, which is the arrangement the pending rule proposed at [WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 would require and which this design applies while the rule remains pending. **The chunk-projection run is a second, non-placement configuration and produces point 8's sixth bullet the same way.**

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Nine points. Point 2 carries the enumeration scope statement in strong form; point 4 is the technical payload and is generated; point 6 carries the three-option enumeration with its owner; **point 7 discharges [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5's authorisation half and names the half it does not take, its home, and the instrument**; **point 8 is the platform-specific content, is marked perishable, names the default and carries its own scope statement**; point 9 is the class-6 close.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Eight steps producing an authorisation position for a named receiver **and a working check on a copy**. Step 1 worked, step 3's form supplied, **step 7 unsupported**. Step 4 is [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md)'s rule-6 element. **Step 5 measures a latency the documentation does not publish and step 6 converts the chunking trap into an obligation.** Step 8 reuses [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s one-question structure, contingent under rule 5 as amended. **The artefact carries the prompt, intervention and frontier records**, and the intervention record's accepted demonstration — a build-breaking query as the least-privileged principal — is the cheapest in the module.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A correctly enforced permission that harmed a user, with no defect at any layer and an absent decision at the centre. **Then the agent case: a correct implementation of the approach the documentation calls a workaround, described in the pull request as document-level access control, with no chunk projection.**

**Phase 7 (30 min).** The partner plays the student's own measured worst-served class. One question.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 4 against step 3 first; item 1 scored by error direction; item 2 sorted against point 3; item 3's durations against measured latencies, signed; **item 5 opened and sorted three ways**; **the cohort's chunk-projection results**; **the [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5 recall result reported with what it implies for [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md)**; the disclosure positions with the unowned count.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has implemented access control will produce a correct placement in minutes and will treat step 3 as a formality. Predicted failure: **a measurement reported as a mean.** Their instinct is that the check is correct, and a correct check has no per-class story. Countermeasure: the hard specification requirement is that the least-privileged class be *named*, not that the degradation be small, and a mean fails the element outright. The phase 7 partner is assigned from the student's own measurement, so a student who did not produce one has no partner role to assign.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is at step 1: a student who cannot separate *who asks* from *whose authority the retrieval runs under* will produce a principal model with one entity in it, and everything downstream will be well-formed and meaningless. Countermeasures: step 1 is worked with the room; phase 2's [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md) retrieval is chosen for exactly this, since a ubiquitous-language failure where two roles share one word is the case that day taught; and phase 1 item 1's four named principals make the distinction concrete before the abstraction is needed.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** If a group ranks P4 worst before the records are supplied — plausible where a group contains a student who remembers [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) point 6 vividly, which is the design's own hope one week earlier — the payload has failed and the transfer has succeeded, which is a good outcome badly placed. Fallback: the group receives the reserved fifth placement — **a query-time check whose predicate is correct and whose index was built before the current permission structure existed**, so that the filter is applied against stale metadata and returns a correct-looking result set that is wrong in both directions — and is asked which of the four failures on the list it is. It is none of them; it is point 5 arriving as an architecture rather than as a latency. The reserve is not used generally because staleness is already a step in phase 5 and phase 3 has no room for a fifth record.

**Affective failure.** Two.

The first is the discouragement at step 3 described at §3.3, countered by the framing and by the specification's choice of assessed element.

The second is **futility at step 7**, and it is the more likely. A student who works through the three disclosure options correctly reaches the conclusion that none leaks nothing, and the available response is to decide that the choice does not matter. It does: the three leak *different* things to different parties, and the phase 6 case shows the default option producing harm. Countermeasure: point 6 names the owner rather than the answer, step 7 requires a *person*, and the phase 8 unowned count is reported as a cohort figure so that a student who could not find an owner sees that most of the room could not either. **The honest professional act here is escalation, not resolution**, which is [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 4's three conditions applied to a disclosure decision, and the instructor says so once.

## 6. Retention architecture

**Retrieves from** [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md) at three quarters, [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md) at two quarters, [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) at four weeks and [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) at two weeks. **Retrieved by** [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) at two weeks (where the disclosure entitlement is an element of a forensic account on a real incident), [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) at four weeks (where the corpus half is taken up and this day's per-class results are the material), [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) at two quarters (the safety case, which the [Seminar Day Index](/wiki/program/08-Seminar-Day-Index.md) records as retrieving S076–S079 as a block) and [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) at two quarters (residual risk, where an accepted disclosure position is a residual risk statement in a different register).

**[S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) at four weeks is the consequential retrieval and it is instrumented in both directions.** This day supplies it with per-class recall results and with the point 5 recall rate; that day must state what it does differently if the cohort could not name the corpus selection. The two-way link exists because [WP-060](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-060.md) §9 warned that if the corpus selection is unreachable at class 4, [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md) and [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) are carrying more than the deferral credits them with — and this is the first day able to measure whether that is true.

Retained content is four questions, one number and one obligation: where does the check run; who is served worst; how long until a revocation takes effect and how do you know; who decides what the user is told; the per-class recall gap on your own system; and **carry the permissions across every transformation that makes new rows.**

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 4.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment judgement.** One specification item in SG302 with IS302, drawing on SG301.

Per specification rule 1 the checked elements are enumerated on the day and end with *nothing else is assessed*. Five hard requirements: a per-class measurement that does not name the least-privileged class fails; **a revocation latency asserted rather than measured fails, and a measured one that does not name the synchronisation path it exercised fails**; a disclosure position with no named person and no explicit unowned fails, and a function named as owner fails; a placement stated without its accepted failure fails; an intervention record with no entries fails unless the artefact shows the design could have caught an intervention-worthy error.

**The measured-rather-than-asserted requirement is the platform's contribution to the specification and is defensible only because the documentation is silent.** Where a vendor publishes a latency, requiring a student to measure it teaches diligence. Where a vendor states that a lag exists and publishes nothing, **a student who supplies a figure has either measured it or invented it, and there is no third possibility** — which makes the fail condition unusually clean and is the reason it was worth adding.

**The function-is-not-an-owner rule is a rule-5 reuse**, imported from [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md), where it was established on the ground that a function cannot bear a cost and cannot be asked a question. Under rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 the reuse is **contingent** on [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s reported outcome.

### Discharge of the open question S060 point 5 deferred to this day

**This paragraph is the report [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5 and [WP-060](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-060.md) §3.2 require, and the authorisation half is discharged.**

[S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5 stated that every retrieval system selects for something nobody asked for; that the relevance criterion selects for a kind of answer and the corpus selects for **whose documents are findable at all**; that the second is the one with ethical weight and the one users cannot see; that it is not answered there; and that **it returns at [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md), where a relevant document may be one this user may not have, and at [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md), where the person whose documents were never indexed is the person not in the room.**

**The half assigned here is taken in full and is the day's subject.** Point 1 states that relevance and authorisation are two questions and only one is in the index. Phase 3 stages four answers to where the second is asked. Point 3 rules out the placement that is not a check. Point 4 supplies the distributive consequence. Step 2, step 3 and step 6 make the position, the measured cost and the disclosure decision assessed artefact elements, and step 7 hands the position to the data owner.

**The half assigned to [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) is explicitly not taken**, and point 7 says so to the room: the person whose documents were never indexed is not affected by any placement on the list, does not appear in any per-class recall measurement, and is not in the permission model, because a permission model describes documents that exist. **The instrument by which [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) will show the deferral was discharged is named in this day's specification** — that day must use this cohort's per-class recall results and its phase 2 point 5 recall rate as its material — which is what the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s deferral rule requires and which [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) itself could not supply.

**One thing is conceded in the discharge.** [WP-060](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-060.md) §9 prediction 2 warned that if almost nobody names the corpus selection, the second half is not reachable at class 4 and the two receiving days are carrying more than the deferral credits them with. **This day measures that at phase 2 and cannot act on it**, because the half it would have to absorb is not this day's material: no placement, no measurement and no disclosure position on this day touches a document that is not in the corpus. If the phase 2 result is bad, **the response is entirely [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md)'s**, and this day's contribution is to have measured it early enough for that day to change.

**Older forward links discharged.** [WP-058](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-058.md) §3.2 excluded filtered and hybrid search over metadata predicates and flagged it as the case this day needs; **it is phase 3's P2 and phase 4 point 4, and it is the day's technical payload rather than a mention.** [WP-056](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-056.md) §6 and [WP-057](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-057.md) §6 named this day as where a relevant document may not be returnable; **that is point 1.**

**Two deferrals to this day are recorded as mis-addressed rather than discharged.** [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §3.2 excluded *safety-specific alignment* and named it "[S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md)'s", and [WP-055](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-055.md) §3.2 excluded *safety-relevant capability limits* and named them "[S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md)'s and [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md)'s". **The capability half is genuinely here** — step 4 states which actions are unavailable to a request carrying untrusted content, and it is a checked element. **The alignment half is not this day's material at any reading.** This day is about where an authorisation check runs over a corpus; alignment training and refusal behaviour are properties of a model and belong to SG302 and to [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md)'s safety case. The deferral is recorded here so that it is not silently dropped, and the correct home is named. **This is a finding for the compliance register and not a fix**, because re-homing another whitepaper's deferral is a design judgement rather than a correction and the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s criterion says such findings are recorded and left standing.

**One gap is stated rather than deferred.** Point 2 names inference from aggregates — a set of individually permitted documents jointly revealing an unpermitted fact — as an omission of the four-placement list. **No day in this programme carries it.** It is not deferred, because the deferral rule requires naming a day that can verify the discharge and there is none; it is recorded as a gap in the programme's coverage and stated to students in the point that omits it.

**Rule-6 discharge, as required by [WP-076](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-076.md) §7.** [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) named this day's phase 5 step 4 as the response occasion for its step 7 capability-bound quality, specifying that the authorisation position state which actions are unavailable to a request whose context carries content from an untrusted path. **The element exists as step 4, it is a checked element, and it is constructed so that a student who removed no capability at [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) produces a visibly empty statement here rather than a silently absent one.** It is not a mention; it makes the earlier day's result legible in this day's artefacts. The obligation is discharged.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments.

- **The item 4 prediction against the step 3 measurement**, together with **the phase 2 [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5 recall result**, responds at [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md), which must use this cohort's per-class recall results and its point 5 recall rate as the material for the person not in the room, and must state what it does differently if the cohort could not name the corpus selection. **This is a cross-day commitment on a day outside this module and is recorded as such.**
- **The unowned disclosure-position count** responds at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) phase 3 item 7, where the forensic account must state who was entitled to decide what users were told about the incident and whether anyone did.

**[WP-080](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-080.md) §7 and [WP-082](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-082.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

Programme instrumentation: the item 4 prediction error, signed and by magnitude; the item 1 over- and under-return rates; the item 2 distribution across the four placements; the item 3 duration-versus-mechanism split against the measured latencies; **the item 5 frontier pair, sorted by which approach the agent chose, whether it reported the documentation's own term for it, and whether it projected permissions onto chunks**; **the cohort's chunk-projection results in three categories**; the phase 2 point 5 recall rate; and the disclosure-position distribution with its unowned count.

ABET and CS2023 contribution: PO2 and PO4, with PO3 touched at step 7. Dispositions: **humility**, at step 3, where the honest finding is about the student's own production system; **integrity**, at step 7, where the honest answer is frequently that nobody in the organisation is entitled to make the decision the student is about to make by default.

**What this day does not license anyone to conclude.** It does not license a claim that a student's retrieval path is correctly authorised — no element of the specification checks correctness. It licenses a claim that the student can say where their check runs, what that placement costs and to whom, how long a revocation takes to bite, and who is entitled to decide what a user is told about what was withheld. **The fourth of those is a professional judgement and the first three are measurements**, and none is a security property of a running system.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day takes the tractable half of [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5 and leaves the half that day called the one with ethical weight to a module that is not security's.**

At its strongest: [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5 was explicit about which half mattered — *the second is the one with ethical weight, it is the one your users cannot see.* The second half is the corpus: whose documents are findable at all. **This day takes the first half**, which is document-level authorisation, which has an architecture literature, four clean placements, a measurable cost and a specification. It hands the weighty half to [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md), one module later, in [M17](/wiki/modules/M17.md), where the strand is professional formation and the day's subject is stakeholders and power.

The division looks like a subject-matter split and reads like an avoidance. A security module that says *a relevant document may be one this user may not have* has taken the version of the problem with an engineering answer, and has left the version with no engineering answer to a day whose method is stakeholder analysis. **A sceptical reader is entitled to conclude that the programme put the tractable half where the assessment is and the intractable half where the discussion is.**

The rebuttal has two parts and neither is complete. First, **the split is not arbitrary**: the two halves have genuinely different objects. This day's half concerns a document in the corpus and a principal in the permission model, and every question about it is answerable by a measurement. The other half concerns a document that is not in the corpus and a person who is not in the model, and there is nothing to measure — the correct method really is stakeholder analysis, and [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) really is where the programme does that. Second, **the deferral is instrumented in a way [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) could not manage**: this day measures whether the cohort can even name the corpus selection, hands that number and its own per-class results forward, and requires [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) to state what it does differently on a bad result.

What is conceded is the shape of the outcome regardless of the reasoning: **the half with an assessed artefact is the half with the tractable engineering, and the half [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) called weighty is assessed nowhere in this programme.** Whether [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) assesses it is [WP-082](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-082.md)'s to answer, and this paper is putting that day on notice.

**Objection 2: the day's central measurement may have no power, and the design has no answer that is not a simulation.**

At its strongest: step 3 requires a per-class recall measurement naming the least-privileged class, phase 7 assigns a partner to play that class, and phase 8 reports the item 4 prediction against it. **All three depend on the substrate having a principal population with meaningful permission variation.** It may not. The [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page's domain constraint — a service whose failure is inconvenient rather than harmful — selects against exactly the domains where document-level permissions are rich, and it says so: *this constraint is absolute and it eliminates most of the interesting problems.* A substrate with three restricted principals gives a measurement over three people, and a null result is indistinguishable from a well-designed control.

**The day requires the class size to be stated in the same field as the result**, per specification rule 2, which makes the weakness visible rather than hidden — a measurement over three people is labelled as one. That is the right discipline and it is not a fix: the artefact is honest and the finding is still absent, and a student whose measurement has no power has performed the activity and learned nothing from it.

The available fix is to construct synthetic principals with synthetic permission structure over the real corpus, which would guarantee a measurable effect. **It is declined**, and the reason is that the effect would then be a property of the construction: the degradation's size is determined by the selectivity of the filter, so a designer choosing the permission structure is choosing the answer. The measurement would be a demonstration of point 4 rather than a test of the student's system, and the day already has a demonstration of point 4 at phase 3.

**This objection is not fully answered.** The design has chosen a real measurement that may be empty over a constructed one that cannot fail, has made the emptiness legible in the artefact, and has no third option. §9 prediction 4 is where the frequency of the empty case would first be visible, and if it is common the honest response is to move step 3 to the substrate's whole corpus for the whole cohort and lose the ownership that makes the finding land.

**Objection 3: the day teaches that the disclosure decision is not the engineer's and then has the engineer make it.**

At its strongest: point 6 states that the disclosure choice belongs to whoever bears the cost of a wrong disclosure, and that it is not the engineer. Step 7 then requires the student to *decide and record* a position. The escape clause — record it as unowned if you cannot obtain the owner — will be used by most of the cohort, and the artefact will contain a decision the student made with a note saying they were not entitled to make it. **That is precisely the behaviour point 6 names as the failure**, performed under the specification's instruction.

The rebuttal is that the alternative is worse and the design says so at [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 3, which named two symmetric errors: an engineer who decides something that was not theirs, and an engineer who withholds a finding because the decision was not theirs to make. A student who writes *unowned* and stops has committed the second. The artefact's form — a position, plus who was entitled to set it, plus an explicit unowned where nobody could be reached — is the record of a decision made under an ownership gap, which is the honest artefact for the situation the students will actually be in.

That is a real answer and it is narrower than the objection. It defends the artefact and not the framing: the day would be better if step 7 required the student to state what they would do *until* an owner is found, rather than to state a position. The change costs one clause and is not in this design.

**Objection 4: the day produces a real leak on a real index, and its most concrete content is anchored to a preview API.**

Two halves, and the first is the one that bites on the practical turn.

**On producing the leak.** Phase 3's chunk-projection run and step 6 require students to create a condition in which documents are returned to a principal not entitled to them. It is done on a disposable copy with synthetic principals and no real users' documents, which is the correct arrangement and is the same one [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) uses. **But the exercise trains a procedure for producing an access-control failure**, and a graduate who performs it on a system they do not own has committed an offence rather than a lab. The day says the words *disposable copy* and does not say why anywhere the student will remember.

**That is conceded and the fix is made.** The day's opening now states the constraint with its reason — the substrate's disclosure covers announced disturbance and not unintended behaviour affecting real users — and [M16](/wiki/modules/M16.md) states it once for the whole module. What is not fixed, and is recorded, is that **the constraint is taught as a rule of this programme rather than as a professional obligation with a name**, because the material that would give it a name is disclosure ethics, which is SG390's and [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md)'s. A deferral that does not name an instrument is what the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) forbids, and this paper is not able to name one; it is therefore recorded as a gap rather than deferred.

**On the preview anchoring.** Three of the four approaches point 8 teaches are preview surfaces, most of the documentation describing them is preview API, and the preview terms state that behaviour may change and that data may be processed outside the compliance boundary. **The day's most concrete, most memorable and most demonstrable content is therefore attached to the least stable thing in the module**, and a re-anchoring is not hypothetical: it is expected within the cohort's own three years, which risk 8 on the [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page says will fire at least once.

The rebuttal is that this is the situation rather than a design error, and the design's response is the one it uses everywhere: the perishability register's durable column is written as reasoning and obligations — *an index is a copy*, *where the trust boundary sits is the choice*, *carry the permissions across every transformation that makes new rows* — and **not one product name, approach name or API version is on the specification.** A re-anchoring changes the right-hand column and nothing that is assessed.

**What is conceded is that this defence has never been tested and this is the day where it will be.** The programme's central hedge against vendor perishability is that durable extractions survive re-anchoring, and the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](/wiki/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md) records that hedge as asserted rather than demonstrated. **This day is the strongest case the hedge has and also its hardest one**, because the demonstration that makes the durable claim memorable is itself a product behaviour. If a re-anchoring costs this day its chunking run, the durable claim survives on paper and loses the thing that taught it.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At item 4, a majority of students will predict that adding the check degrades all principals roughly equally, and the measured effect at step 3 will be concentrated on the most restricted class. *Instrument:* item 4 free-text predictions coded for whether they identify a class at all, joined to the step 3 measurement's shape. **The claim is about the coding of the prediction, not its magnitude**: a student who predicts a uniform effect has a different model from one who predicts the wrong class, and only the first is the schema failure §1 describes.

**Prediction 2.** At item 3, more than half of students will answer with a mechanism rather than a duration, and their durations, where given, will be shorter than the measured staleness latency at step 5. *Instrument:* item 3 answers coded as duration or mechanism, and the durations compared signed against step 5. **If the durations are accurate, staleness is already held as a latency** and point 5 is a formalisation rather than an introduction, which would move it out of phase 4 and into the artefact form.

**Prediction 3.** Groups will rank P4 as leaking least before the records are supplied, and will rank P3 as leaking least *after* them. *Instrument:* committed rankings against post-record rankings, per placement. **The second half is the interesting one**: P3 silently truncates, which is a leak of a different kind, and a room that moves from P4 to P3 has learned that a control can be correct and irrelevant without yet learning that absence is informative — which is what phase 6 is for and would be evidence that the clinic is doing work.

**Prediction 4.** For at least a quarter of the cohort, the least-privileged principal class at step 3 will contain fewer than ten principals. *Instrument:* step 3 class sizes, which specification rule 2 requires in the same field as the result. **This is the direct test of §8 objection 2**, and if it holds for more than half the cohort, the per-class measurement is not a measurement on this substrate and the design must either change the substrate's permission structure or stop claiming the finding is the student's own.

**Prediction 5.** At item 5, a large majority of agents will produce the string-filter approach; fewer than one student in five will have predicted that specifically; and **fewer than one in ten will have predicted the missing chunk projection.** At step 6, a majority of pipelines that chunk will be found not to project permissions. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded against what each agent produced, and step 6 results in three categories. **The step 6 rate is the claim that matters.** If most pipelines already project correctly, the chunking run is a demonstration of a solved problem for this cohort and its phase 3 slot should go to the reserve placement in §5; if the rate is high, the day has found a live defect in the cohort's own production retrieval paths, and that finding belongs to the substrate's data owner within the week rather than to a phase 8 slide.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That where an authorisation check runs is the decision, not whether one exists; that the placement a room ranks safest — redaction at answer time — is not a check at all; and that a filtered approximate search serves the least-privileged principal worst and tells them nothing.

**What the evidence licenses.** The retrieval geometry is **class 1**, grounded in [research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md) and [research/02-technical-foundations/subramanya-diskann.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/subramanya-diskann.md). That a selective predicate lowers recall is **class 2** — direction only, and **no figure is asserted for the size of the degradation.** Point 8's four approaches are **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026 against a preview API, compared on fit rather than on strength, with the synchronisation lag acknowledged and never quantified. **The distributive claim — least privileged, worst served, unaware — is class 3.**

**What to do with it.**

- Carry the permissions across every transformation that makes new rows. Chunking is the one that catches you, and nothing about it is discoverable by inspection.
- Measure the revocation window rather than citing one. No figure is published, so a figure you did not measure is one you invented.
- Report per class, name the worst-served class, and put its size in the same field as the result.
- **A gate's authority is a property of the tier it runs in rather than of the check** — see the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). A correct filter sitting off the retrieval path is a configured control and nothing more.

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes that it took the tractable half of the question [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) raised — the document in the corpus, the principal in the model, everything measurable — and left the half that day called the one with ethical weight, the person whose documents were never indexed, to a later day where it is discussed and **assessed nowhere in this programme.** Hold the design to that division, whatever the reasoning behind it was.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** Point 8 is taken from Microsoft Learn's document-level access control overview for Azure AI Search, **read 2026-07-26**, which describes the **2026-05-01-preview REST API**. Verified: four approaches — **security filters, generally available and described as "simple string matching"; POSIX-like ACL and RBAC scopes, preview; Purview sensitivity labels, preview; SharePoint in Microsoft 365 ACLs, preview**. The documentation's own contrast is that permission-filter metadata "is recognized as Microsoft Entra ID authentication, whereas the security trimming **workaround** is simple string comparison". The native pattern requires permission filters enabled on the index, a permission filter on a string field, ACL metadata loaded with documents, and the **`x-ms-query-source-authorization`** header at query time. The **two-stage check** is verbatim: first **Search Index Data Reader** permission for the client application, then, given the extra token, per-document user or group permissions excluding non-matches. **Group access is recommended over individual user access.** RBAC scope is preserved **at document level for Azure Data Lake Storage Gen2 and at container level for Azure blobs.** SharePoint requires Graph **`Sites.FullControl.All`**, plus **`User.Read.All`** for list items and ASPX pages; **changes on items with unique permissions refresh on each successful indexer run, while changes inherited from parent scopes require an explicit `/resync` with `options: ["permissions"]` or `/resetdocs`**; and **SharePoint Information Management policies "aren't evaluated, ingested, or honored at query time."** Purview labels require a **system-assigned** managed identity, administrator-granted access, labels applied before indexing, single-tenant scope, RBAC authentication, and **Autocomplete and Suggest are unavailable on Purview-enabled indexes.** Query-time enforcement "evaluates the caller's Microsoft Entra claims against the permission metadata that's already stored in the index", and source-side changes take effect only after synchronisation. The chunking trap is verbatim: where a skillset chunks documents "the permission metadata fields move from indexer field mappings to **index projections**", and **"Without this projection, chunk-level references aren't filtered."** The governance framing at point 8's seventh bullet is from the Purview DSPM for AI page, read the same day, which is **explicitly labelled classic and states that it is replaced by a new version**: the default data risk assessment runs **weekly over the top 100 SharePoint sites by usage**, with a four-day delay before first display and multi-day waits for custom results.

**Four silences are recorded and three are taught.** **No latency figure is published for permission synchronisation on any of the four approaches**; the preview notice states only that "a timing lag occurs", which is the silence step 5 converts into a measurement obligation and the specification enforces. **The four approaches are compared on fit and not on security strength**, which the page states by omission and which point 8's scope statement names to students. **No accuracy, detection rate or false-positive rate appears anywhere on either page**, consistent with the folder-wide asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](/research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), and no such figure is asserted here or taught. And the SharePoint permission matrix, the per-version SDK support and the sensitivity-label sync prerequisites are on companion pages that were **not fetched**; where the day depends on them it says *demonstrated rather than operated*, and the specification does not assess them.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That an approximate index searched under a selective predicate visits fewer viable candidates and returns lower recall; that generated answers can carry content from documents the requester is not permitted; and the pretesting, contrasting-case, absent-party articulation and error-management mechanisms the phases rest on are cited for **direction and mechanism only. No effect size is asserted for any of them.** **No figure is asserted for the size of the recall degradation under filtering, for any index type or any selectivity**; the only figures in the day are measured by students on their own systems and are registered as perishable. **No effect size is asserted for AI-assisted productivity anywhere in this paper.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The four-placement decomposition and its four failures; the claim that a check off the retrieval path is not a check; **the distributive claim that the least-privileged principal is served worst and told nothing**; the revocation latency stated in [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s four-part form and required to be measured; the chunk-projection run's promotion from a documentation fact to a produced result and to an artefact obligation; the three disclosure options and the claim that none leaks nothing; the named-person requirement and the explicit-unowned escape; the placement of item 4 at phase 1 to avoid a third instance of the downstream-instrument pattern; the two-way instrumentation with [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md); and all five §9 predictions are this design's own reasoning, untested. Predictions about this programme's own outcomes have no external warrant and are stated as such. **The §8 objection 1 concession stands**: the half of [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5 with an assessed artefact is the tractable half, and the half that day called weighty is assessed nowhere in this programme. **The §8 objection 2 concession is not resolved**: the day's central measurement may be empty on this substrate, the design has made the emptiness legible rather than fixing it, and it has declined the synthetic construction that would guarantee an effect because the effect would then be the designer's. **The §8 objection 4 concession is in two parts**: the reason for the disposable-copy constraint is now stated to students and its professional name is a gap the programme cannot currently defer anywhere; and the day's most memorable content is anchored to a preview surface, which makes it the strongest and the hardest test the programme's perishability hedge will get.
