## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

The problem is not that students are missing a concept. **They hold a correct one, and its correctness is the problem.** Every module since the second has taught boundary-drawing as the central engineering act, and taught it well. Draw the boundary at the interface and you can test what is inside it, own it, measure it, gate it and ship it. Everything this programme has certified about these students is certified inside that line.

**The failure class this day is about is defined by being outside it.** A deployment that is used exactly as designed, produces exactly the improvement its team measured, and raises the organisation's net cost is invisible to every instrument the cohort owns — because every one of those instruments takes the component as its unit. They will not reason their way there, and being told will produce agreement and no change, because *the organisation matters* is a sentence they already believe and have already been rewarded for saying.

So the day holds the software constant. Four deployments, identical code, identical evaluation, different organisations — because if the cases differed in their software the room would discriminate on the software, which is the dimension two years of training has made salient. And the payload is a **success**, which is the only one in the programme; every other contrasting case is something that went wrong.

Then it gets specific. Name the non-software components of the delivered outcome. Name their maintainers — **people, not functions**, because a function cannot be unavailable for a fortnight and cannot be asked what it does. Record where each name came from, since *observed*, *asked* and *org chart* are three different claims and only the last is free. And then two of those maintainers sit in the room for half an hour, entitled to say *that is not what I do.*

**What this paper concedes, and it is a real concession.** The day redraws the boundary to include three components the student does not control, maintained by people who do not report to them, and asks whether they are willing to be responsible. **Willingness is not authority**, and the day supplies no next move — the moves are the following four days and it names none of them. The one-sentence fix was declined so that the discomfort would carry the module. **The cost of that falls unevenly**: a confident student gets a week of unresolved tension, and a student whose self-efficacy is fragile gets a week of having been told that what they cannot do is their responsibility. The paper says outright that it does not know how to separate the two and has not tried anywhere in the day.

This paper defends a day whose subject is a boundary the programme has spent two years teaching students to draw well, and whose claim is that drawing it correctly makes a whole class of failure invisible. The target competence is identifying the non-software components of a delivered outcome, naming their maintainers from observation, and deciding where the system's boundary goes in the knowledge that the decision determines which failures are yours. The mechanism the design leans on hardest is a contrasting-case set holding software and evaluation identical across four organisations, with a payload deployment that succeeds on every criterion the cohort has been taught and raises the organisation's cost. **The day's platform instance is unusual and load-bearing: the agent produces a complete component inventory of the substrate in ninety seconds, and it contains no people — so the day's central omission arrives as a property of a tool the students ran rather than as an accusation about their thinking.** The principal risk is that the material is agreed with instantly and changes nothing. **Section 7 records a proposed remedy — the prior-course artefact join [WP-069](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §8 named and declined — with its first use here.** Section 8 concedes that the day's own instrument was originally placed downstream of its own instruction, records this as the third instance of a pattern, argues for a rule, and faces the objection that requiring a build on a day about people is a displacement.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The difficulty is not that students lack a concept. It is that they hold a correct one whose correctness is the problem.

Every module since [M02](/wiki/modules/M02.md) has taught boundary-drawing as the central engineering act, and taught it well: the seam at [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md), the caller's contract at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md), the consistency guarantee at [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md), the trust boundary at [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md). A student who draws a boundary at the interface can test what is inside it, own it, measure it, gate it and ship it. Everything the programme has certified about them is certified inside that boundary.

**The failure class this day is about is defined by being outside it.** A deployment that is used as designed, produces the improvement its team measured, and raises the organisation's net cost is not detectable by any instrument the cohort possesses, because every one of those instruments takes the component as its unit. The students are not going to reason their way to this, and being told it will produce assent without change, because the sentence "the organisation matters" is one they already believe and have already been rewarded for saying.

The difficulty source is therefore **a distinction invisible without contrast**, and specifically without a contrast that holds the software constant. That is the design constraint the whole day is built around: if the four cases differ in their software, the room will discriminate on the software, because that is the dimension two years of training has made salient.

A second and independent difficulty is affective and arrives at phase 5 step 6. Redrawing the boundary to include three components you do not control makes you responsible for things you cannot change. That is not a comfortable act and it is not obviously correct — the position that a boundary should track authority is defensible and is held by competent engineers. **The day does not resolve it, and says so at point 7.** What it refuses is holding a boundary without knowing it was chosen, which is the actual state of every student in the room at phase 1.

The third difficulty is that the day is running the selection effect it teaches. Its four cases were chosen by staff who knew the outcomes. Instrumentation rule 3 requires the disclosure and the day makes it, but disclosure does not remove the effect; it only makes the day's evidence the same kind of evidence it asks students to produce.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Nothing on this day is computed and the intrinsic load is low.** The concepts are three carrying modes, a unit conversion and a boundary. A student could hold all of them after ten minutes of exposition, which is exactly why exposition is not the design.

The interactivity that exists is between steps 3, 4 and 6. The three named maintainers determine what the two-week question can be answered about; the two-week answers determine what redrawing the boundary actually commits the student to; and step 6 is where a student discovers that the component they named most confidently is the one whose absence they cannot describe. Those three steps are adjacent and in that order for that reason.

Load reductions:

- **The object is the cohort's own service**, so no time is spent acquiring a domain.
- **Phase 3's cases hold the software and the evaluation identical**, which removes every dimension the cohort would otherwise search.
- **The boundary notation is supplied** at step 2 and the unit conversion is worked once with the room at step 5.
- **The categories at phase 4 point 3 arrive after groups have produced their own** at the phase 3 task-after, so the trichotomy consolidates rather than instructs.

Expertise reversal applies to any student with prior deployment experience, and it applies at phase 4 rather than phase 3. Such a student holds points 1 to 3 already and will experience fifty minutes of consolidation as review. The design's answer is that point 7 is not held by that student either — professional experience produces the boundary reflex faster, not weaker — and the phase's second half is where their attention is wanted.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *A pretest whose expected answer is professionally correct and is the omission the day is about,* item 1.
- *A numeric prediction about one's own omission,* item 4, checked an hour later.
- *Cases that hold the software constant,* phase 3, so that the only available discrimination is the one the room has never had to make.
- *A payload that is a success,* O4. Every other contrasting-case payload in the programme is a case that went wrong.
- *Producing the categories before receiving them,* the phase 3 task-after.
- *Naming a person and then being asked what happens if they are away for two weeks,* step 4.
- *Accepting or declining responsibility in writing,* step 6.
- *The named maintainer in the room, entitled to disagree with your description of their work,* phase 5.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *A hostile organisation.* Every party in the four cases and in the room acts in good faith. Deliberate obstruction is a different subject and is not in the programme.
- *Quantifying the organisational cost.* Step 5 requires the unit and one instance, not a figure. A cost model of an organisation's process is a term's work and would displace the day.
- *Choosing between boundaries under a deadline.* The negotiation of a boundary against somebody who disagrees is [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md)'s and [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md)'s.
- *A second observation session.* The day uses the [S037](/wiki/seminars/S037.md) record and asking. Running a fresh contextual inquiry would be a better artefact and would consume the whole day.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The three-carrying-mode frame is **safe as a way of finding the people a deployment depends on within the current operating period, on a service you can observe, in an organisation whose members you can ask.** It is **unsafe as a model of how a system survives across years**, because it omits *sustaining* — the training, hiring and succession that keeps the other three staffed — and *shielding*, where somebody absorbs the political cost of the system's existence. The failure mode is specific and is not a matter of incompleteness: a student who maps absorbing, routing and licensing, names three maintainers and hands over will produce a system that works for one staffing cycle and degrades on the second, and the frame will not show them why, because at any single moment all three modes are staffed. The scope statement is carried in the day at point 3 and both omitted modes are given homes, at [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) for shielding and [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) for sustaining.

**The platform instance carries its own strong-form statement, at point 10.** Purview's default data risk assessment is **safe as a way of finding an institution's largest exposures this week, on the sites that are in use, within the documented caps.** It is **unsafe as an inventory of what an AI system can reach, and unsafe as evidence that a party's material is absent rather than unscanned.** The failure is exact: the default assessment covers the top hundred SharePoint sites *by usage*, item-level scanning is capped at ten sites and unsupported on OneDrive, and locations are bounded at two hundred thousand items with the vendor's own accuracy caveat above one hundred thousand files. **A student who reads a clean assessment as an all-clear has drawn a conclusion about the estate from an instrument that sampled the busy part of it** — which is the same error as O4's deploying team, in the register the students are most likely to be trusted with after graduation. The boundary is stated to students at point 10 and the artefact element that binds it is step 8's maintainer rows, whose provenance field forces the student to say where each piece of the inventory came from.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **the two maintainers at phase 5.**

A student who has written that a named person performs a routing step, and then has thirty minutes in which that person can say *that is not what I do*, is in a position no exposition creates. Competence support is direct: the student has something specific to ask and a record to check it against. Relatedness is supplied by the fact that the maintainers are colleagues in the institution the cohort's service belongs to, not role-players. Autonomy is at step 6, where the student decides what they are willing to be responsible for and the design accepts either answer.

The second mechanism is item 4 against step 3. The student predicts, in writing, how many of their listed components are people; an hour later they are required to name three. Phase 8 shows them both. **The gap is the day's evidence and the student sees it without being told what it means**, which is the construction the design uses whenever a claim would otherwise be an accusation about the cohort's competence.

The situational-interest trigger is O4. A deployment that succeeded and cost money is a puzzle rather than a lesson, and the room will work on it because the cost outcome contradicts the report they have just read and both are true.

The competence risk is at phase 7, where a student's own record of somebody else's work is read back to them by a partner who identifies which parts came from the org chart. That is an exposure of sloppiness rather than of ignorance, and it lands after ninety minutes in which the student has been cataloguing other people's undocumented work. **The countermeasure is the phase 6 disclosure prompt, which puts every student in the category before phase 7 puts them in the position** — and it is placed before phase 7 for that reason rather than for retrieval-scheduling ones.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items. Item 1's expected answer is complete, competent and entirely software. Item 3 is unanswerable now and is scored only for attempt. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction and its expected result inverts the usual one: the agent will out-perform the room on item 1 and will be no help at all with the day.** It is administered here and not later, per instrumentation rule 8.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Three retrievals at gaps of two years, two years and four weeks, closing with a bridge that promotes [S037](/wiki/seminars/S037.md)'s finding to department scale. The room supplies both halves, including the second — that the divergence is larger at scale because nobody holds the whole *done*.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** **Six minutes executed, then thirty-nine demonstrated.** The executed part is the agent's inventory of the substrate, produced by every group against a real service. The demonstrated part is four deployments, software and evaluation identical, organisation varied, which cannot be run and are **not assessed**, per the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](/wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md). Sampling disclosed per instrumentation rule 3. Payload O4. The task-after takes the category reading before any vocabulary exists, and its third item turns the room's own machine artefact into the day's evidence.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Points 1 and 3 name what groups produced. Points 5 and 7 are the two claims with engineering consequences and both are reachable from O4. Point 3 carries the strong-form scope statement. Point 6 discharges the organisational half of [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md) point 2. **Points 9 and 10 are the platform instance and are placed after point 8's open question deliberately, which is this module's convention and is an argument about what the platform is for here: the vehicle follows the claim because the claim does not depend on it.** Point 10 names the defaults and carries the second strong-form statement.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Eight steps on the cohort's own service, with two maintainers present for the last thirty minutes. Steps 3 and 6 unsupported. Step 7 is the prior-course join. **Step 8 is the build, timeboxed to thirty of the ninety, and the maintainers' thirty minutes are timetabled first so that the build is what compresses.**

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A withdrawn capability containing no individual error, **an agent-produced inventory whose owner fields were populated and whose owners had left**, and a disclosure prompt that puts the student in the category.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Bilateral record audit, deliberately not the absent-party one-question form, which has run twice and would begin measuring familiarity on a third administration.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 1 against step 3; item 4's distribution; **item 5's two rates, reported separately**; the phase 3 category coverage that determines [S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md)'s branch, stated to the room; the person-row count on the machine inventory; the maintainers' corrections.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has deployed professionally will produce points 1 to 3 at phase 1 and will find the phase 3 cases familiar. The predicted failure is **fluency at step 3**: they will name three maintainers quickly and from the org chart, because in professional practice that is what is available and it is usually good enough. Countermeasure: step 3 requires the provenance of the name to be recorded, step 4 asks the two-week question that an org-chart name cannot answer, and **the maintainers are in the room at phase 5 and are not obliged to agree.** A confidently wrong description delivered to its subject is the correction, and it costs the design nothing to arrange.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is that the day reads as sociology. A student with no deployment experience has no felt experience of the boundary and therefore nothing for point 7 to disturb. Countermeasures: the object is the cohort's own service, which every student has operated on rota; step 2 asks them to describe the boundary they have actually been using rather than one in the abstract; and O4's cost outcome is arithmetic rather than interpretive.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** A group that identifies O4's mechanism before the cost outcomes are supplied has done the day's hardest cognitive work in twenty minutes, and this will happen where a group contains someone who has watched work move. The difficulty is relocated to the task-after's second item: **name the measurement that would have detected it, and say who would have had to take it.** That has no clean answer — the measurement is in the receiving team's unit, the receiving team did not know a deployment was happening, and nobody in the deploying organisation had standing to ask them. Fallback if a group answers that too: ask what they would have had to agree, before the deployment, with a team that did not yet know it was affected — which is [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md)'s problem arriving four days early and has no answer at this point in the module.

**Affective failure.** Two, and the second is the serious one.

The first is **guilt about the maintainers**. A room that has just discovered its service depends on three undocumented people, one of whom is in front of them, can turn the finding into an apology. Countermeasure: point 2 states that nobody is concealing anything and nobody could report it, and the phase 6 case is chosen so that the departing person wrote an honest handover.

The second is **the responsibility refusal at step 6 read as a failure of character.** A student who redraws the boundary and then writes that they are not willing to be responsible for a licensing decision made by a director has given a defensible professional answer, and the design must be able to receive it as one. Countermeasures: point 7 states that both positions are defensible and neither is free; step 6's checked element is the statement rather than its direction; and the specification does not score the answer. **If this fails, the day produces students who write that they accept responsibility for things they cannot affect, which is the failure the whole module is designed to prevent one level down.**

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) at one week (where today's named people are sorted by what they can refuse and what can be done to them, and where the boundary becomes contested), [S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md) at three weeks (where the carrying modes become adoption obstacles and where this day's category-coverage reading selects the cases), [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md) at four weeks (where a carrying mode becomes a term in a scope the receiving organisation must supply), [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) at five weeks (where the three components are things that must transfer, and where the moved-work instrument responds), [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) (the warranty, which is a claim about an organisation) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md).

**The [S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md) retrieval is the instrumented one** and is a branch rather than a review: the cases that cohort meets are a function of what this cohort could not see today.

**The [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) retrieval at five weeks is the consequential one.** A transfer inventory is a list of things the receiving organisation must be able to do, and three of them are the components named here. If those names are wrong, the handover is wrong, and S086 is where that becomes visible.

Retained content is short and procedural: the three carrying modes; the maintainer-must-be-a-person rule; the two-week question; the organisation's unit; and the boundary as a chosen commitment. Five items.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 1, week 4.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md).

One specification item, in PE390 with CAP390. Per rule 1 the checked elements are the capability and outcome in the institution's words, the current boundary and its contents, three non-software components with named persons and name provenance, a two-week consequence or do-not-know for each, the outcome in the organisation's unit with one moved-work instance or a do-not-know with the evidence that would settle it, the redrawn boundary with the new responsibilities and the acceptance statement, the named PE301 material, the phase 7 outcome, **the shipped inventory with its three maintainer rows and their provenance, the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

One hard requirement: **a maintainer named as a function fails.** This is [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s owner rule applied to a component, and the justification transfers unchanged — a function cannot be unavailable for two weeks and cannot be asked what it does. **The same rule binds the build**: an inventory with no maintainer rows, or with maintainer rows whose provenance field is empty, fails, because it is the tool's output rather than the student's artefact.

**On the three build records.** They are assessed exactly as the [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) specifies and not more: three binary checks on the prompt record, two on the intervention record with its sensitivity escape, two on the frontier statement. **Whether the frontier judgement is correct is not assessed and cannot be**, because the programme holds no ground truth. The architecture is explicit that the build record is its weakest evidence class and is never used alone to form a level; on this day it forms no level at all, and its function is to make the phase 8 frontier reading possible. **This day's frontier statement has an unusual property worth recording: for most students the second half will not be a mistake but a silence**, and the day accepts a statement naming what the agent could not have known as stronger than one naming a small error, which is a judgement the design is making without evidence.

**What this day does not license anyone to conclude.** It generates no evidence about whether the student can act on an organisational finding, only about whether they can produce one. The acting is [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md)'s and [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md)'s. It also generates no evidence about the accuracy of the students' organisational maps beyond the two maintainers who attended, and the day's own sampling disclosure applies to itself: two maintainers out of the substrate's whole operating organisation is a convenience sample and the assessors are told to treat corroboration by them as confirmation of one description rather than of a method.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day's own instrument was placed downstream of its own instruction, which is the third instance of a pattern this design has not named.**

At its strongest: the reading that selects [S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md)'s cases is *which of the three carrying modes this cohort could not see*. As the day was first written, that reading was taken at phase 5 step 3, where students are required to produce one component per mode. **But the three modes are supplied at phase 4 point 3, fifty minutes earlier.** A student who under-populates *licensing* at step 3 may have failed to perceive a licensing dependency, or may simply have failed to retain a term they met an hour ago and have never used. Those are different findings with different remedies, the instrument cannot distinguish them, and the branch it drives — which cases a later day supplies — would then be selected against a recall failure while being described as a perception failure.

**The objection is conceded and the fix is taken.** The phase 3 task-after already asks groups to name the non-software parts and their maintainers, unprompted and before any vocabulary exists. That reading costs nothing additional, is taken before instruction, and is the one the day now uses for the branch. Phase 5 step 3's coverage is still recorded and is reported at phase 8 as a retention figure rather than a perception one.

**Taking the fix does not dispose of the finding, which is about the design and not about this day.** [WP-053](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §8 and [WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 each recorded an instrument whose result the day's own instruction had already supplied, and in both cases the paper noticed it only while writing the objections section. **This is the third.** The rule form is short and the design should adopt it:

> **An instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.** Where a day takes a reading of what its cohort can perceive, produce or classify, and the categories, vocabulary or procedure required to produce that reading were supplied earlier in the same day, the reading measures retention of the day's own instruction and must not be reported as a measure of the underlying capability — and must not drive a branch described in those terms. Where both readings are wanted, the pre-instruction reading is the instrument and the post-instruction reading is a retention figure. Identified at [WP-053](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §8, [WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 and [WP-081](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-081.md) §8.

**This paper does not assign the rule a number.** [WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 argued for a rule 7 — that a claim the day expects students to resist must be staged rather than asserted — on three instances, and that elevation is pending and not in force. Numbering a second rule before the first is ratified would produce two rule 7s in the programme's own materials, which is precisely the class of defect the compliance register exists to prevent. The rule is argued here in the form above and the numbering is left to whoever ratifies the first. **Per the hard prohibition on editing programme pages, neither is written there.**

It is worth recording that this day complies with the pending rule 7 as well: points 5 and 7 are the claims the day predicts students will resist, item 3 records the resistance in advance, and both are reachable from O4 rather than delivered by exposition. That is compliance with an unratified rule and is not evidence for it.

**Objection 2: the day teaches that the organisation is part of the system, and gives the student no way to change any part of it.**

At its strongest: phase 5 produces a redrawn boundary containing three components the student does not control, maintained by people who do not report to them, in an institution whose processes were set before the cohort existed. Step 6 asks whether they are willing to be responsible for that. **Willingness is not authority.** A student who says yes has accepted accountability for a routing decision made by somebody with the standing to make it and no obligation to consult them, and the day supplies no move that follows. The professional consequence is not neutral: an engineer who believes they are responsible for organisational components they cannot affect is a candidate for exactly the demoralisation the [Programme Charter](/wiki/program/00-Program-Charter.md)'s self-efficacy commitments exist to prevent, and "the organisation is part of your system" is a sentence that, delivered without a next move, is indistinguishable from "all of it is your fault".

**This objection is conceded in substance and answered only in sequence.** The moves exist and they are the next four days: [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) supplies who can be asked and what to do when the path terminates badly, [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) supplies how to put a finding in front of somebody who can act, [S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md) supplies what is actually within an engineer's control, and [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md) supplies the negotiation in which a carrying mode becomes a term the receiving organisation must supply. The day names none of them at point 8 except the boundary-dispute question, and **it should name all four.**

The cheap fix is one sentence at point 8 and is declined. Point 8's function is to leave a question open, and a point 8 that lists the next four days' remedies converts the module's opening day into a syllabus, removing exactly the discomfort at step 6 that makes S082 to S085 land. **The design has chosen to leave students for one week in a position it has told them is theirs and given them no instrument for**, on the argument that the discomfort is the entry condition for the module. That argument is real and it is not sufficient, because the cost falls unevenly: a confident student experiences a week of unresolved tension and a student whose self-efficacy is fragile experiences a week of having been told that the thing they cannot do is their responsibility. **The design does not know how to preserve the first and prevent the second, and it has not distinguished them anywhere in the day.** Recording it here is the minimum and is not a remedy.

**Objection 3: this is a day about people, and the design has put a build in the middle of it.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn and the paper takes it at full strength rather than as a scheduling quibble.

At its strongest: the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) requires every phase 5 to produce a build, and the requirement was written for days whose subject is a system. **This day's subject is that a system is not only a system.** Its irreplaceable resource is thirty minutes with two colleagues who agreed to sit in a room and be told what a student thinks they do, and that resource is unrepeatable, unbuyable and unrecoverable if a group is still debugging a query at the hour. **A design that has spent two years arguing that the deployment's real constraints are organisational has, at the first day where that claim is examined, allocated a third of the whole-task phase to writing software.** Worse, the requirement was imposed by a standard written elsewhere in the wiki, which is precisely the mechanism [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md) point 6 teaches students to notice: a decision made by whoever was available rather than by the party with the evidence.

**The objection is conceded, and two mitigations are in the day rather than in this paragraph.** The maintainers' thirty minutes are timetabled first and the build is what compresses; and the specification states that a group arriving at the maintainers with an unfinished inventory has made the correct trade. **The cheap fix beyond that is also named and is partially taken: the build could run entirely in the between-day protocol**, where it would cost the day nothing at all. It is not taken in full because the build's whole instructional value is that the room sees what the tool returns *before* phase 4 tells them what is missing from it, and a build completed overnight arrives after the answer — instrumentation rule 8's problem in a new place. **What is taken is the split: the six-minute executed opening at phase 3 carries the instructional load, and step 8's remaining work may be finished after the day and is not time-bounded in the specification.**

**What that does not fix is the thing the objection is actually about.** The design cannot detect, from the artefact, whether a group's maintainer conversation was thinner because the build existed. It has committed to reporting at [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) whether any build displaced any encounter minute, which is a self-report from the party with an interest in the answer, and instrumentation rule 1 says not to use self-report where an external observer is present. **The maintainers are the external observer and they are not asked.** That is a rule-1 departure taken knowingly, on the ground that asking two colleagues to grade the cohort's attention would poison the encounter the whole day exists for, and it is recorded rather than resolved.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At item 1, more than three-quarters of students will list no people at all, and at step 3 nearly all will name three. *Instrument:* item 1 component lists coded for person-entries, paired with step 3. **The pairing is the claim, not the item 1 figure**: a high item 1 rate would mean the cohort already holds the day's premise and phase 3 is over-built, and a low step 3 rate would mean the frame is not usable on a real service.

**Prediction 2.** At the phase 3 task-after, groups will produce *absorbing* and *routing* unprompted and will not produce *licensing*. *Instrument:* task-after categories coded against the trichotomy, by group. **This is the prediction the day's branch depends on**, and if licensing is produced freely while routing is not, [S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md)'s case selection inverts and the design's account of which dependency is hardest to see is wrong.

**Prediction 3.** More than half of students will mark do-not-know at step 5's moved-work question, and among those who do not, the majority of identified instances will be inside the students' own service rather than in a receiving team. *Instrument:* step 5 entries coded for do-not-know and, where present, for the location of the moved work relative to the student's boundary. **The second half is the informative one**: work that moves inside your own boundary is work you can see, and finding only those means the frame has been applied and O4's actual mechanism has not been.

**Prediction 4.** At phase 5, the two maintainers will materially correct more than a third of the descriptions given to them, and the corrections will concentrate on students whose step 3 provenance is recorded as *org chart* rather than *observed* or *asked*. *Instrument:* maintainer corrections recorded and coded against the provenance field. **If corrections are distributed evenly across provenance**, the provenance field is not measuring anything and the checked element should be removed rather than kept as a formality.

**Prediction 5.** At item 5, a large majority will predict that the agent produces a good inventory, and **fewer than a quarter will name that a resource inventory has no field for a person.** *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded on two axes — whether the quality prediction was made, and whether the structural absence was named — reported as two rates rather than one. **The gap between the rates is the day's frontier-calibration measure and the two-axis coding is the claim.** A cohort that predicts the agent will do well has predicted nothing, because they have used it for five quarters; a cohort that names what it cannot be about has located a boundary. **If the second rate is high, this module's frontier argument is already held and phase 3's executed opening is redundant. If both rates are low, the cohort is not yet forming predictions about the agent at all**, which is a finding about the whole [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) and not about this day.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That the boundary you draw so well is what makes a whole class of failure invisible: a deployment can be used as designed, produce the improvement its team measured, and raise the organisation's cost, and no instrument you hold will show it. The remedy is to name the non-software components and their maintainers from observation rather than from an org chart.
**What the evidence licenses.** Contextual inquiry and the divergence between reported and observed work are **class 1**, grounded in [research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md](/research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md). The platform material is **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026: a default assessment bounded by usage, item counts and site counts, with **no detection rate and no false-positive rate published anywhere.** **The three carrying modes, the boundary-as-chosen-commitment framing and the maintainer-must-be-a-person rule are class 3** — this design's own, with two modes it knows it omits.

**What to do with it.**

- Record where a name came from. *Observed*, *asked* and *org chart* are three different claims and only the last is free.
- Ask the two-week question. A name that cannot answer it is a function wearing a person's clothes.
- Read a clean assessment as covering the busy part of an estate rather than the estate.
- Apply the five classes on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md) to any generated inventory. **Provenance is not correctness**: a populated owner field is a configured control, and the owner may have left.

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes that it tells you the organisation is inside your boundary and gives you no move for a week; that the one-sentence fix was declined so the discomfort would carry the module; and that the cost falls unevenly — a confident student gets a week of unresolved tension, and a student whose self-efficacy is fragile gets a week of being told that what they cannot do is their responsibility. **The design does not know how to separate the two and has not tried anywhere in the day.**
