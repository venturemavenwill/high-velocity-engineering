## In plain terms

A documented comparison of options is one of the most respected artefacts in engineering and one of the easiest to fake.

The published specification this session uses is good, and one requirement carries almost all of its value: **each option carries an experiment and the evidence that experiment produced.** Not an assessment of the option. Not a paragraph about its characteristics. An experiment, and what came back.

That single requirement is what separates a comparison from an adjective table — options down the side, criteria across the top, weighted scores that are opinions with arithmetic performed on them, and no contact with reality anywhere in it. **An option you did not try is an opinion about an option**, however carefully the opinion is tabulated.

The specification also carries a negative rule that is more useful than the template: **a comparison should be avoided where the choice is already clear.** That rule is broken constantly, usually by people citing the template, because a comparison run to ratify a decision produces a document that looks like evidence and is not. This session therefore accepts two outputs — a study, or a recorded refusal to run one — and marks them equally.

**The specification is also internally inconsistent, and its own worked example does not follow it**, which is taught here as material rather than smoothed over. Its two pages disagree about how many options to carry forward, nothing reconciles them, and the only published comparison alongside it omits several of the sections the template requires and renames another. **A learner who copies the example fails the specification.**

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching a comparison as a specification whose central requirement is a per-option experiment, and defends accepting a recorded refusal as an equally valid output. It argues that the characteristic failure is the justification study — a comparison run to ratify a decision already taken, producing an artefact with the authority of evidence and none of the content — and that the failure is invisible in the artefact because a justification study and an honest one have the same shape. It requires criteria to be written before options are scored and the incumbent system to appear as an option. It uses a generative instrument twice, both times against the learner's own option set, and neither time to decide anything. The paper concedes that the specification it teaches disagrees with itself, that its own exemplar fails it, that the negative rule is unenforceable because clarity is judged by the person with the incentive to find the choice unclear, and that requiring an experiment per option will produce token experiments.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

There are two situations and they look identical from outside.

**In the first, a genuine choice exists and nobody knows the answer.** A comparison is the right instrument, the experiments are worth running, and the document produced is the reasoning.

**In the second, the answer is known and somebody wants it justified.** This is not always illegitimate — a stakeholder who must defend a choice to people who were not present has a real need — but a comparison is a poor way to meet it, because the resulting document carries the authority of evidence while its conclusion was fixed before it started. **Worse, it teaches everyone who reads it that comparisons look like this**, and the next one will be produced the same way whether or not the choice was open.

The difficulty is that the two are indistinguishable in the artefact. Both have options, criteria, a table and a conclusion. **The only observable difference is whether the criteria preceded the scoring and whether the options were actually tried**, and those are exactly the two things the specification requires and the two things most comparisons omit.

There is a third situation the session must also handle. **The choice is clear and nobody has said so.** A team can spend weeks comparing options where one was always going to be selected, because comparing feels like diligence and deciding feels like exposure.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** Anyone who has produced or read a technology comparison can do this session.

It retrieves [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md), because the refusal is filed as a decision record and because the specification's own parent page instructs that a data-driven decision should refer to its comparison — an instruction neither of the publication's worked records follows. It retrieves [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) and [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md), because the criteria come from the constraint register and the incumbent system is one of the options.

**This is a class 3 session**: nobody supplies the criteria, nobody supplies the number of options, and nobody says whether a comparison is warranted. The learner decides all three, and deciding that no comparison is warranted is a correct answer.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate for the document and high for the experiments, which are the part learners under-plan. The session's realistic constraint is that a meaningful experiment per option is more work than the time available, which is why [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md) sits after [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md) — the boundary work supplies most of what the experiments need.

**3.2 Why the experiment is the load-bearing requirement.** Every other field in the specification can be completed from a reading. **The experiment cannot**, and its presence is the only structural difference between a comparison and a well-formatted preference.

**3.3 Why criteria must precede scoring.** A criterion introduced after a result can be selected to produce the result. **This is the same claim as [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md)'s about stopping rules and [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md)'s about decision records — a criterion formed after the outcome is known cannot discriminate between outcomes.** Its fourth appearance in this programme, and the design says so each time.

**3.4 Why the refusal is a first-class output.** The specification's own negative rule says a comparison should be avoided where the choice is clear. **A session that only accepts comparisons teaches learners to find every choice unclear**, which is the exact failure the rule names.

**3.5 Why the agent is used twice and decides nothing.** The first use — ask for an option nobody considered, given the criteria and constraints rather than the shortlist — attacks the option set, which is where a comparison is most often quietly decided. The second — argue for the option about to be rejected — attacks the conclusion. **Neither use produces evidence. The experiments produce evidence, and the instrument has not run any.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

Seven movements: situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 1 asks whether the answer is already clear, and it is asked first.** Every later movement is conditional on it. A learner who answers yes proceeds to the refusal and is assessed as favourably as one who proceeds to the study.

**The template is filled in full, including the accessibility section.** That section is the one everybody drops and it is the one that catches an option unusable by some of the people who will have to use it. **Requiring the whole template is also how the session sets up its own critique**, because the published exemplar omits several sections and the learner will have just produced them all.

**The agent instruction for the first use withholds the shortlist deliberately.** Given the shortlist, the instrument elaborates it; given the criteria and the constraints, it may not. **An agent that returned only the options already held was pointed at the learner's own list**, which is [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s claim about an instrument that agrees with you, at its fourth appearance.

**The review movement removes the criteria and asks a reader to reconstruct them.** If they can, the criteria were doing the work. If they cannot, the decision rested on something unwritten, and finding out what is the remainder of the session. **This is the session's only mechanical check.**

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The justification study.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* opening item 1, the refusal as an accepted output, and the review movement's criteria-reconstruction test. **None of these prevents it; all three raise its cost.**

**The adjective table.** No experiment anywhere. *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement of an experiment and its evidence per option, which is the specification's own requirement rather than this design's.

**The token experiment.** An experiment exists, is trivial, and would have produced the same result for every option. *Countermeasure:* item 5's requirement to name the criterion whose removal changes the answer, which is indirect. **This is the weakest countermeasure in the session and §8 concedes it.**

**The straw option.** One real candidate and one present to satisfy the floor. *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that each option carry an experiment, which a straw option will visibly fail to have.

**The excluded incumbent.** The customer's existing system is not among the options. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires it, and [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md) supplies the argument for why.

**The criterion added after the result.** *Countermeasure:* the requirement that criteria be traceable to the decision record that opened the study, which makes the addition visible rather than preventing it.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) and [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md).

The chosen option becomes the design, and the study or the refusal is the record the design refers to. **The session also serves [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md) backwards**: the comparison in which the unavailable design is one option and the fallback is another, each with an experiment, is the artefact that makes an unfreezing argument evidential rather than rhetorical. That is the strongest practical use of the specification anywhere in this stage and it is not the use the source describes.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence, and no specification item at [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) marks the study.**

What [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) marks is downstream: that the boundary decision is named with its standing cost, and that open questions name what would close them. **A comparison that reached a decision supplies both; one that did not, does not.** The session's output is therefore assessed indirectly and incompletely, and this paper records that rather than claiming otherwise.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the specification being taught disagrees with itself and its own exemplar fails it, so the session is teaching compliance with an incoherent standard.**

At its strongest: the template page and the page introducing it give different answers about how many options to carry forward, and nothing reconciles them. The only published worked comparison omits several required sections and renames another. **A learner asked to comply is complying with which page?** Teaching a standard whose own author cannot follow it, and marking learners against it, is teaching deference to documents rather than judgement.

**Conceded, and the session's response is to make the incoherence the lesson rather than to pick a page.** The learner produces the full template, then reads the exemplar against it. **The declined fix is to publish a reconciled version of the specification for this programme.** It is declined because a reconciled version would remove the only thing about the source that cannot be got from a better-written template elsewhere — the experience of holding a real standard that contradicts itself, which is the condition of every internal standard a learner will meet. **The cost is real: some learners will conclude the specification is arbitrary and comply with it mechanically.**

**Objection 2: the negative rule is unenforceable, because the person judging whether the choice is clear is the person who benefits from finding it unclear.**

At its strongest: a team that wants to run a comparison — for cover, for time, or because comparing is more comfortable than deciding — will find the choice unclear. A team under time pressure will find every choice clear and record a refusal. **The rule delegates the judgement to exactly the party whose incentives it is meant to constrain**, and the session compounds this by marking the refusal as favourably as the study, which makes the cheap option also the safe one.

**Substantially conceded, and the second half is the part this paper cannot answer.** Marking the refusal equally is intended to remove the incentive to fabricate studies, and it plainly introduces the opposite incentive. The rebuttal is that a refusal must state why the choice was clear and what would have made a comparison worthwhile, which is a claim somebody can disagree with, whereas an unwritten decision is not. **The declined fix is to mark the refusal below the study.** It is declined because it restores the incentive to run justification studies, which is the worse failure — but the choice is between two incentive distortions and not between a distortion and a clean design.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first-pass comparisons will contain no experiment for at least one option. *Instrument:* the studies, counted by options with and without an experiment.

**Prediction 2.** Refusals will be rarer than studies, including among learners whose opening item 1 answered that the choice was clear. *Instrument:* opening item 1 answers against the output produced, compared per learner. **If learners who said the choice was clear go on to produce studies, the negative rule is not transferring and the session's central instruction has failed.**

**Prediction 3.** The agent's first use will produce at least one option not on the learner's shortlist more often when it is given criteria and constraints than when it is given the shortlist. *Instrument:* a cohort split on the prompt form, counting novel options returned.

**Prediction 4.** In the review movement, readers will reconstruct the criteria of studies containing experiments more often than the criteria of studies without them. *Instrument:* reconstruction success against experiment presence, per study.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That an option you did not try is an opinion about an option; that a comparison run to ratify a decision produces a document with the authority of evidence and none of the content; and that refusing to run a comparison is a result.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The specification comes from pages read on 2026-07-30, from a publication offering no comparative evidence for any of its practices and claiming none, whose two pages on this topic disagree with each other and whose own worked example fails its own template. Nothing measures whether documented comparisons produce better choices.

**The concession worth remembering.** This session marks a refusal as favourably as a study, which removes one incentive to fabricate and introduces another. There is no arrangement of incentives here that is clean, and the paper prefers the distortion it can see.
