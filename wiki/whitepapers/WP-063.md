# WP-063 — Memory Management for Serving

**Seminar day:** [S063](wiki/seminars/S063.md) · **Module:** [M13](wiki/modules/M13.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](wiki/quarters/Q7.md)
**Strands:** SY · IS · ME · **Complexity class:** 5
**Platform anchor:** `max_tokens` as a capacity reservation; the deployment utilisation accounting and its refusal · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (write) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends building the day around the inversion of S018's closing sentence — the working set is the one number an operator did not choose, and in a serving system it is the one number they do — and defends delivering that inversion as a phase 2 bridge answered by the room rather than as instruction. It defends locating the reservation, on this platform, in a single field of the request body, and defends the discipline of declaring an output limit as the day's operable content, because the vendor's own guidance for maximum concurrency is to declare it close to the true generation size and the substrate's code declares nothing. It defends writing the day's assessed content as four measurement obligations rather than as an allocation technique, because serving memory management is semi-durable under the programme's own register and the technique will not survive the cohort. It defends five sampled concurrency levels chosen so the refusal threshold must be inferred, reusing S018's construction, and defends the declare-nothing case as payload on the ground that abstention is the largest reservation in the room. **It abandons a manufactured server-side collapse in favour of the documented behaviour, which is a fast refusal, and relocates the non-linear saturation class to the client that retries it.** It reports discharge of two rule-6 obligations placed on this day by S061 and S062. It concedes that the day previously priced the reservation without pricing the guarantee — **a concession now repaired, with the earlier reason for declining the repair recorded as wrong** — that its sketch item is one day downstream of the instruction that answers it, and that the lever it hands students is narrower than the claim it is meant to carry.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Two beliefs are in the room and both are reasonable.

**The first is that concurrency is a hardware property.** Students have operated the substrate for a quarter and have never chosen how many requests are in flight; the number was set by whoever configured the serving path, and it presents as a fact about the machine. It is not. It is the output of an allocation policy, and two policies over identical capacity differ in achieved concurrency by a large factor. Displacing this belief is the day's first job and it is easy — one measurement does it.

**The second is harder and is the day's real target: that reserving against the worst case is prudent.** It is what every student would do, it is what most systems do, and it is defensible. The problem is that the reservation is invisible: nobody measures capacity that was reserved and not touched, because no dashboard reports it, and so the cost of prudence is never priced. The capacity is already bought. Recovering it requires no purchase, no approval and no capacity request — which makes it the cheapest capacity in any serving system and the one nobody looks for first.

**On this platform the invisibility has a specific and instructive form.** A managed deployment prices an in-flight request by estimating what it will cost — prompt tokens, less any served from cache, plus the output limit the caller declared — and the documentation states that if no limit is declared the service estimates one, which can produce lower concurrency than expected when the true output is short. **So the reservation is made whether or not the engineer makes it, it is made larger when they decline to, and the field that makes it is one line of a request body that most code never sets.** A belief about prudence is hard to dislodge by argument. A belief about prudence that is implemented by an omission can be dislodged by a measurement, and that is what phase 3 does.

**The third problem is the cliff, and the honest version of it is not the one this day was originally written around.** [S018](wiki/seminars/S018.md) established that allocation under pressure collapses rather than degrading. [S019](wiki/seminars/S019.md) established the same shape from queueing and named non-linear saturation as a class. A managed deployment does not join that class: at its ceiling it refuses immediately and states how long to wait, which is a fast failure by design and is cheaper than the work. **The collapse is still available, and it is now the client's.** A caller that retries the refusal instead of honouring it has rebuilt the positive feedback [S062](wiki/seminars/S062.md) point 6 described, on its own side of the wire, in code the serving team may not own. That is the third member of the class and it is the first whose position is set by somebody else's software. It is a stronger finding than the one the day previously made, and §8 argues that it also costs the phase most of its difficulty.

The fourth problem is clerical and is the one that costs most in practice. A concurrency ceiling is a measurement, measurements age, and the traffic mix moves the threshold underneath a ceiling that was correct when taken. That is not a sophisticated failure and it is the modal one.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**High, and the module's peak.** Three quantities interact and must be held simultaneously: the length distribution, the reservation discipline, and the offered concurrency. Changing any one moves the refusal threshold, and the threshold's position is what every downstream claim depends on. This is the highest genuine element interactivity in M13 and it is why the day carries the heaviest scaffolding in the module.

Management:

- **All four phase 3 disciplines are supplied as configurations to run**, at five concurrency levels, with per-class results.
- **The controlled-load procedure at step 4 is supplied**, and the wait for it is designed into the period rather than out of it.
- **Step 3's per-class accounting is worked with the room.**
- **The deployment, the workload and the length distribution are fixed across all four disciplines.**
- **The three memory terms are retrieved from S061, not re-derived.**
- **The build is a configuration change rather than a new component**, so the agent-mode work at phase 5 adds no comprehension load of its own — it is the same change the room made four times at phase 3, applied to their own service.

**Step 7 is unsupported.** Expertise reversal applies at points 1 to 3 for any student who has operated a serving system under load; for them the day's content is point 5's second over-reservation form, point 6's relocation, and point 7's date discipline, and the §5 fallback is written accordingly.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Estimating your own system's waste before measuring it,* item 2.
- *Sketching a curve you have drawn wrong before,* item 3.
- *A five-point sample that requires the cliff's position to be inferred,* phase 3.
- *Answering S018's closing question again, with the opposite answer,* phase 3's closing task.
- *Showing the arithmetic for a binding-resource claim rather than asserting it,* step 1.
- *Locating a boundary by crossing it under load,* step 4.
- *A dual defeat requiring both the ceiling and its signal to fail,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Allocator implementation and the internals of the deployment's accounting.* SY301's, and in the second case nobody's outside the vendor. The day needs the two forms of over-reservation and takes the mechanisms as given.
- *Multi-model and multi-tenant memory sharing.* Named at point 8 as the principal unsafe extension and excluded; it belongs with SY301's platform material and returns at [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md) where the sharing is contractual.
- *Compression and quantisation of retained state.* Named at point 4 as members of the same family and not developed, because the catalogue is perishable and the day already carries more perishable material than any other in the module.
- *Adversarial memory exhaustion.* [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The four measurement obligations and the two-waste account are **safe** for a single model on capacity you control, with a stable and known request-length distribution — the substrate's condition and the condition of most first deployments a graduate will meet. They are **unsafe** in three specific settings. Where several models share capacity, the fixed term is not fixed and the ceiling is a joint property. Where the length distribution shifts, a prediction-based discipline degrades in a way none of the four numbers exposes until the threshold has already moved beneath the ceiling. Where capacity is shared with tenants whose behaviour is unobservable, the ceiling is not the operator's to state at all. **The dangerous case is the third, and on this platform it is also the ordinary one:** a standard deployment's throughput is documented as varying with other customers' demand, most students will only ever hold a standard deployment, and a student who quotes a four-number characterisation of one has handed a planner a ceiling that somebody else can move without telling them.

**Scope of the platform instance, in the same form.** The reservation account — that a declared output limit is what the deployment charges against on arrival, that an undeclared one is estimated on your behalf, and that cached input is free of the accounting — is **safe** for reasoning about why concurrency is lower than expected, for recovering already-purchased capacity, and for explaining why a prompt-layout decision shows up as throughput. It is **unsafe** in three conditions.

- **It is unsafe at any date other than the one on which the accounting was verified.** The estimation rule, the burst allowance and the correction behaviour are product decisions on an undated page. The day's response is the specification's: every accounting rule used in an artefact carries the date it was checked.
- **It is unsafe as a free saving.** A declared limit is a limit, and recovering reservation converts a guarantee into a probability for exactly the long-output requests [S062](wiki/seminars/S062.md) established are already the ones that wait. This was §8 objection 1 and it is now the second half of measurement obligation 2, which is the artefact element under which a student who takes the saving and does not price it fails.
- **It is unsafe outside this API family.** The parameter, the accounting and the refusal semantics are one vendor's. The durable claim underneath — that a reservation costs concurrency in proportion to the variance of what it reserves for — is not, and §8 objection 3 argues that the day makes the first much easier to remember than the second.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **the bridge and its answer**.

Five quarters ago the room accepted a sentence — the number that decides which policy you want is a number you do not choose — and it was true then. The bridge asks what that number is here, and the answer reverses it. A reversal of a claim the students themselves hold is a stronger opening than any new claim, because it makes the earlier day's material live rather than archival, and it takes ninety seconds.

The second mechanism is **item 2 against measurement**. Students estimate the waste in their own service, in a fraction, before being told anything, and the measured fraction is reported at phase 8 as the day's most quoted number. Nothing about it is abstract: it is capacity the cohort has been paying for, and it is being wasted by a line of code nobody wrote.

The third is **phase 3's D case**, and §8 objection 2 argues it is the design's weakest bet — though for a different reason than it once was.

The competence risk is at step 4, where students deliberately push a live serving path past the point where it refuses. The instructor states before phase 5 that the controlled-load procedure is bounded, that staff escalation is in place per [Substrate and Infrastructure](wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md), and that a student who declines to cross the boundary on a user-facing path and characterises the threshold on a replica has made a defensible engineering choice which the specification accepts. **That accommodation is stated in advance rather than granted on request**, because a student who has to ask has already learned the wrong thing about what the programme rewards. **The platform lowers this risk materially and the day says so**: at its ceiling the deployment refuses rather than degrading, so the experiment's blast radius is a set of refused requests rather than a damaged service — which is the day's own point 6 arriving as a fact about the students' safety rather than as content.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items. **Item 2 is a rule-6 instrument sited before instruction**, following the placement S061 established and S062 partially failed to carry. Item 3 repeats S018's sketch demand deliberately and the repeat is named at phase 8. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction**, and its expected answer — that the agent will not reach for the request body — is the day's frontier datum.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals and the inversion bridge, whose third clause is left unanswered so that phase 3 answers it.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four reservation disciplines, five sampled concurrency levels, threshold inferred rather than read, **executed against a real deployment**. D is the payload. The closing tasks are S018's, unchanged, with a changed answer, plus a new one locating the cliff's present owner. The second run — two client behaviours across the threshold — is where the relocation becomes a thing the room did rather than a thing it was told.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Point 3 is the retained sentence. Point 5 names the second over-reservation form. Point 6 relocates the saturation class. **Point 7 is the day's durable content written as measurement obligations rather than as technique**, which is the register's requirement rather than a stylistic choice, and its second obligation now carries the truncation rate as the price of the saving. **Point 8 is the perishable platform point and point 9 the defaults point.** Point 10 is the scope statement in strong form.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps, **shipping a reservation discipline into the calling code and the gateway policy**. **Step 1 is S061's rule-6 receiving element; step 6 is S062's.** Step 4 is the only place in the module where waiting for a measurement is part of the design, and its second half is the client experiment. The artefact additionally carries the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A correct measurement, correctly reviewed, undated — then **an agent change composed entirely of recognised good practices that made the service worse under the conditions it was written for.**

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Dual defeat on the ceiling and the signal, contingent under rule 5 as amended.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 1's fixed-term explanation; item 2 against measurement; item 3 against the five samples and both client curves; **item 5 opened**; **and the two received rule-6 reports made to the room.**

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has run a capacity-constrained serving path will predict the waste fraction accurately, will know what an output limit does to the accounting, and will find points 1 to 4 familiar. Predicted failure: they treat step 7's signal as routine because they have alerts already. Countermeasure: phase 7 requires the partner to defeat the signal with a *mix* change rather than a volume change, which alerting practice generally does not cover, and point 5's second over-reservation form is not visible in any utilisation-based alert they will have configured.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is step 1's arithmetic, which requires composing the S061 model with a capacity budget. Countermeasures: step 3's accounting is worked with the room and produces most of the terms step 1 needs; the four supplied disciplines expose the arithmetic as four worked instances before the student attempts their own; and a student who cannot complete step 1's ratio may report the two terms separately with the ratio omitted, recorded as omitted, which is the honest fallback and is stated to them.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** This remains the day's most likely failure and it is examined at §8 objection 2 rather than dismissed here. Fallback if a group predicts D's result and explains it: **give them discipline B's prediction-error distribution and ask which of the two wastes it converts into which, at what point B becomes worse than A, and what the truncation rate would have to be for the answer to reverse.** That question is point 5's territory joined to obligation 2's new second half, has no supplied answer, and is the payload the day would have used had it been designed the other way round.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **fear of the live system.** Step 4 asks students to push a path serving real users past the point where it refuses, and a student who has internalised the programme's duty-of-care framing may reasonably freeze. This is not irrational and treating it as timidity would be a serious error.

Countermeasures: the replica option is offered in advance and is specification-conforming; the controlled-load procedure is bounded and supplied rather than improvised; staff escalation is present and its use is not held against anyone, per the substrate protocol; phase 6 opens with a staff rollback, which establishes that crossing a boundary and being wrong is a recorded and survivable event in this programme; and **the platform's own behaviour is the strongest reassurance available** — what lies past the boundary is a refusal, not a damaged service, and the students find that out by reading the documentation before they run the experiment rather than by being told.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md).

One specification item in SY301 with IS301. Per rule 1, the checked elements are the binding resource with arithmetic, achieved and achievable concurrency and the gap, reserved-and-unused capacity per class with its truncation rate and cache-served fraction, predicted and measured threshold positions, the two client behaviours and where the collapse was, the discipline with its ceiling and headroom, the per-class tail effect, the committed threshold-movement signal, the phase 7 outcome, **the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

Four hard requirements: **a reserved-and-unused figure reported without its truncation rate fails**; **an admission discipline reported without its distance to a measured refusal threshold fails**; **a concurrency figure reported without the date of the measurement that established it fails**; and **an intervention record with no entries fails** unless the artefact demonstrates the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error. The first is new and is §8 objection 1's repair — the artefact element under which a student who takes the cheapest capacity without pricing what it was buying fails.

**No named technique, policy attribute or accounting rule is assessed.** This is the register's semi-durable rule applied literally, and it is worth stating because it is unusual: a student who selects the least fashionable of the four disciplines and characterises it completely passes, and a student who selects the best available and cannot date its ceiling does not.

**Demonstrated rather than operated, and therefore not assessed:** the deployment utilisation figure, its burst allowance and its post-completion correction, all of which are properties of dedicated capacity the programme does not hold. The room assembles the equivalent comparison itself from what requests declared and what they returned. That substitution is more work and is arguably the better lesson, and the design does not claim it was chosen for that reason.

**Rule-6 discharges, both reported as required by [WP-051](wiki/whitepapers/WP-051.md) §8.**

- [S061](wiki/seminars/S061.md) named this day as the response occasion for its item 2 binding-resource error rate, specifying an artefact element in which the student states the binding resource for their admission decision and shows the arithmetic. **The element exists as phase 5 step 1, it is an assessed checked element, and the arithmetic requirement is what makes it a claim rather than a recollection. The cohort's S061 error rate is reported against it at phase 8.** The obligation is discharged.
- [S062](wiki/seminars/S062.md) named this day as the response occasion for its item 2 median-and-tail prediction error, specifying an artefact element reporting the tail effect of the student's own policy per request class. **The element exists as phase 5 step 6, it is an assessed checked element, and it is placed after the policy choice so that the tail effect is a consequence the student owns. The S062 predictions are reported against it at phase 8 under both load conditions.** The obligation is discharged.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions: the item 3 cliff-shape and threshold-position error responds at [S064](wiki/seminars/S064.md) phase 5 step 4, where headroom must be expressed as distance to the measured threshold in the units of the demand change rather than as a utilisation percentage; and the step 3 reserved-and-unused figure with its paired truncation rate responds at [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md) phase 3, where stating the cheapest available capacity before requesting spend is a required element and asking for money for capacity already held is a published failure condition. **WP-064 §7 and WP-065 §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day prices the reservation and never prices the guarantee it was buying. — Conceded, and repaired.**

At its strongest: point 3 is the day's most portable sentence — the capacity you reserved and did not use is the cheapest capacity in the system. It is true only if the reservation was purchasing nothing. It was not. **Reserving against the worst case buys a guarantee: that an admitted request can produce as much output as it needs.** Recovering the reservation converts that guarantee into a probability, and the probability is worst exactly for the longest requests, which are the ones [S062](wiki/seminars/S062.md) established are already the ones that wait.

The platform makes the objection sharper rather than softer. The recovery is one field, the vendor's own guidance is to declare it close to the true generation size, and **a declared limit is a limit** — a request whose real output exceeds it does not get the rest. So the day's headline sentence, applied literally, truncates answers for the users least able to say what they lost.

A student who left with point 3 and applied it would recover the capacity, improve every number they were watching, and meet the cost at the tail — which is the same failure the previous day's error clinic describes, one week later, caused by this day's headline sentence.

**The fix was one number and this paper previously declined it. It is now made.** Measurement obligation 2 carries a second figure: the fraction of requests in each class that reached the declared limit. It comes out of the same run that produces the other four, it is a checked element, and **a reserved-and-unused figure reported without it fails.** Point 3 is thereby converted from free money into a trade, which is what it is.

**The reason previously given for declining it is recorded and is wrong.** It was that a list of four is within the design's retention ceiling and a list of five is not. The retention ceiling governs what students are expected to carry; a measurement obligation is checked in an artefact and the register imposes no such limit on those. The repair keeps four obligations and gives the second two numbers, which is what the original argument should have produced. **The interval between identifying the fix and making it was one revision**, and the design has no account of why it took that long other than that nobody re-read the objection.

**Objection 2: the day's productive-failure phase now depends on the cohort not knowing one parameter, and its sketch item is one day downstream of the instruction that answers it.**

The original form of this objection was that phase 3 required the cohort to fail at non-linear saturation, which [S018](wiki/seminars/S018.md) and [S019](wiki/seminars/S019.md) had already taught twice. **The practical turn has not removed the problem; it has split it into two smaller ones and made the second worse.**

On the first: D is the payload because declaring nothing is the largest reservation, and the room does not expect that. It is a genuine surprise, it is documented, and **a group that has read one paragraph of the accounting page predicts it exactly.** So the phase's difficulty rests on ignorance of a parameter rather than on absence of a concept, which is a thinner thing to build a generative phase on and is the same shape [WP-062](wiki/whitepapers/WP-062.md) §8 objection 3 records for its own day. Two days in three, and the design notes it as a pattern rather than an incident: **the practical turn has repeatedly converted conceptual difficulty into documentation difficulty, and the two are not equivalent.**

On the second, which is newer and worse: **item 3 asks students to sketch what happens when a client retries a refusal, and [S062](wiki/seminars/S062.md) point 6 taught retry as positive feedback on a queue one day earlier.** The item is therefore a recall test wearing a prediction's clothes, and it is instrumentation rule 8's failure at a pretest rather than at a learning task — which is a place the design believed itself safe.

The partial rebuttal is that S062 taught the mechanism in a queueing register and item 3 asks for a curve in a throughput register, and transfer between the two is exactly what the programme claims to build. That rebuttal is available and it is also an admission: **if the transfer succeeds, item 3 measures nothing the design did not already know; if it fails, the programme's one-day-gap retrieval has failed.**

The honest statement is that item 3 is now a transfer probe rather than a prediction, that it should be reported as one, and that **§9 prediction 2 has been rewritten accordingly.** The cheap fix — move item 3 to a mechanism S062 did not name — was considered and declined, because the client-retry curve is the day's own content and removing it from the pretest would leave the relocation unmeasured at entry.

**Objection 3: the day hands students a lever that will not transfer and a claim that will not be remembered.**

This is the objection that bites hardest on the practical turn.

At its strongest: what a student takes away from this day, if the day works, is *set the output limit*. That is concrete, actionable, immediately useful, and **specific to one API family's accounting.** The durable claim underneath it — that a reservation costs concurrency in proportion to the variance of what it reserves for, and that recovering it converts a guarantee into a probability — is abstract, is two clauses long, and has no memorable instance attached that is not this vendor's. **The design has arranged for the perishable half to be the memorable half**, which is the exact inversion of what the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) exists to produce.

The rebuttal available is the specification: the field is not assessed, the obligations are, and a student who states four numbers with dates has demonstrated the durable content whatever they remember. **That rebuttal is about assessment and the objection is about memory**, and this paper cannot claim the two coincide any more than [WP-061](wiki/whitepapers/WP-061.md) §8 could.

**The objection is conceded. The cheap fix is named and declined.** The fix is to require, at step 5, one sentence stating the reservation trade in terms that do not mention the field, the platform or the parameter — the same discipline [S063](wiki/seminars/S063.md) point 7 applies to technique, applied to the day's own headline. It would cost a line in the specification.

It is declined for a reason the paper states and does not fully believe: **the module already requires eleven checked elements at this day, and the design's judgement is that a twelfth would be produced without thought.** That is a claim about student behaviour with no evidence behind it, made by a design that has just spent a page arguing that untested reasons for declining cheap fixes are how this document's own defects accumulate. It is recorded as such.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Item 2's estimate of reserved-and-unused capacity will fall well below the measured fraction for a large majority. *Instrument:* item 2 estimates against measurement, per request class, **reported signed**. The direction is the claim: students under-estimate waste because nothing they have used reports it, and on this substrate the waste is produced by an omission nobody made deliberately.

**Prediction 2.** Fewer than half the cohort will draw a collapse rather than a plateau for the retrying client at item 3, **and among those who do, a majority will have named S062 point 6 as the reason when asked at phase 8.** *Instrument:* item 3 sketches coded for a discontinuity, with the phase 8 attribution recorded separately. **This is a transfer probe rather than a prediction and §8 objection 2 says why.** A high sketch rate with high attribution is a one-day transfer success and means phase 3's second run is confirmatory; a low sketch rate means the programme's shortest-gap transfer failed on material taught the previous morning, which is a more serious finding than anything about this day.

**Prediction 3.** Fewer than a third of step 7 signals will be genuinely leading rather than coincident with the threshold. *Instrument:* step 7 signals coded leading against coincident, using the rubric [S019](wiki/seminars/S019.md) step 7 established and [S018](wiki/seminars/S018.md) step 7 first required. **This is the third measurement of the same competence at the same rubric and is the programme's clearest longitudinal signal-design series.**

**Prediction 4.** Phase 7 partners will defeat the ceiling more often than the signal, and the modal successful defeat will use a shift in the length distribution rather than an increase in volume. *Instrument:* phase 7 outcomes coded for which demand succeeded and for the mechanism of the constructed change. **The mechanism is the claim** — volume changes are what students expect and mix changes are what actually moved the substrate's threshold in the phase 6 case.

**Prediction 5.** Fewer than one in five item 5 sealed predictions will name the request body as the thing the agent will not change. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded for whether the reservation field is named, against what the agent actually changed. **The prediction is deliberately harsh and its failure would be good news.** A cohort that expects an agent to reach for concurrency settings and retries rather than for the accounting has a working model of where an agent's attention goes, and that model is what [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md) will require four quarters later.

**Prediction 6.** Across the cohort, the truncation rate reported at step 3 will be near zero under the current discipline and will rise materially under whichever discipline each student chooses at step 5. *Instrument:* paired truncation rates before and after, per class. **This is §8 objection 1's repair reporting on itself.** If the rate does not rise, the recovery was free and the objection was wrong; if it rises and nobody mentions it in the step 5 justification, the fail condition is doing the work the objection said was needed.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The admission accounting used at phase 3 and phase 4 points 6, 8 and 9 is taken from Microsoft Learn's guidance on operating provisioned deployments in production and from the token limit policy reference for the API Management AI gateway, **read on 2026-07-26**. What the documentation says: that a deployment's utilisation is the ratio of capacity consumed to capacity deployed over a one-minute window; that it is maintained by a variation of a leaky-bucket scheme which permits brief bursts above the ceiling and caps traffic over time; that each request is charged an estimate on arrival formed from its prompt tokens, less cached tokens, plus the `max_tokens` value specified in the call; that **cached tokens receive a full discount and do not contribute to utilisation**; that **if `max_tokens` is not specified the service estimates a value, and that this can lead to lower concurrency than expected when the number of actual generated tokens is small**; that the guidance for highest concurrency is to set `max_tokens` as close as possible to the true generation size; that the estimate is corrected up or down when the request completes and the real cost is known; that at the ceiling the service returns a rate-limit response with `retry-after` and `retry-after-ms` headers; and that **a refusal from such a deployment is a traffic-management signal rather than a service error.** From the gateway reference: that prompt-token estimation is a required attribute with no default, that it is applied unconditionally when a response streams, and that an image is counted at a documented worst case when estimation is in force. **What version it says it at:** none of these pages carries a revision date or a version number, and the utilisation guidance exists in two live variants for two portal generations.

**Four silences are recorded and two are taught.** **When `max_tokens` is unspecified the service "estimates a value" and the estimate is not published** — so the largest reservation in the room is one whose size the operator cannot look up, which is the silence point 8 names to students and the reason phase 3 measures it rather than computing it. **The burst allowance above the ceiling is described as brief and is not quantified.** **The additional latency introduced by prioritising a dedicated deployment before overflow is stated to exist and is not measured.** And consistent with the folder-wide asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), **the material states thresholds, windows and headers throughout and states no accuracy anywhere** — there is no published figure for how often the arrival estimate is close to the true cost, and none is asserted here.
