## In plain terms

Two incidents happened and you know about neither. One produced no signal. The other produced a signal, sent it to somebody, and that somebody has a mail rule.

**Alert fatigue is real**, and the openly published playbook says so in those words. The discipline that answers it is deletion, and deletion is uncomfortable because every deletion is a decision to stop seeing a class of event. **The session's position is that the decision is legitimate and the silence about it is not** — so every deletion is recorded with what will now be missed.

The instrument that makes deletion defensible is the service level, and the definition the session takes is unusual and better than the common one. **A service level is the minimum viable service quality acceptable to the customer. It is not what you intend to deliver.**

That inversion does real work. An objective set at what the team hopes for produces no budget to spend, so every ordinary variation is a breach and the objective is quietly abandoned. An objective set at what the customer would actually accept produces a budget that can be spent deliberately — on change, on risk, on the thing you were going to do anyway. And it is a number you can defend, because somebody outside your organisation agreed to it.

The session's third artefact comes from a failure the source demonstrates rather than describes. **In the pipeline this stage learns from, three auxiliary analysers produce good standalone reports about code and infrastructure. None emits an identifier the rest of the chain uses. No stage consumes their output.** A real vulnerability found by the code analyser never becomes a threat, never becomes a work item, and never reaches anybody's backlog.

**A finding that reaches no queue was not found by the organisation, only by the instrument.** The unconsumed-signal register is where that stops being invisible.

And then the session's hardest claim, which is not about alerting at all. **The absence of an alert is not evidence of the absence of an incident**, and a monitoring system's silence is informative only in proportion to what that system is capable of detecting. If you know an instrument reliably catches a class of event, its silence is strong evidence. If you do not, its silence is evidence of nothing — and it *feels* like evidence, because from the operator's chair a quiet dashboard and a healthy system are indistinguishable.

**The vendor publishes no detection rate and no false-positive rate for its posture tooling.** Not a poor figure; no figure. **So the strength of the inference from no alert to no incident is genuinely unknown rather than merely unstated**, and that silence — not the tool — is what this session teaches.

What the documentation does publish is better than most and is the session's practical gift: the **scan scope**, the **cadence** and the **detection latency** of its assessments. Read those three together as the resolution of the instrument. **An engineer who cannot state all three does not know what their controls cover**, and for most tooling in this class the three are not published at all.

## Abstract

This paper defends a session organised around an epistemological claim and assessed on an operational one. The argument runs in five steps. **First**, alert sets grow monotonically and are pruned by mail rules rather than by decisions, so the only honest response is to do the triage explicitly, in writing, with the losses recorded. **Second**, an objective set at the team's intention leaves no budget and is therefore abandoned, whereas one set at the customer's floor is defensible because somebody outside the organisation agreed to it. **Third**, a system output that no process consumes is not a signal, however good the report containing it. **Fourth**, and centrally, the absence of an alert licenses a belief about the world only in proportion to the detecting instrument's capability — and for the posture tooling this session anchors on, **no detection rate and no false-positive rate is published**, so that capability is unknown rather than unstated. **Fifth**, an incident count measures the reporting climate as well as the system, because silence is invisible and its calculus is asymmetric.

The session installs a customer-obtained service level with an explicit disclaimer of intention; an alert set in which every entry names an expected action and actor; a recorded deletion list priced with what will now be missed; an instrument-resolution line stating scan scope, cadence and latency for every tool relied on; a detection-capability statement naming at least one incident class the instruments cannot see at all; an unconsumed-signal register; and one incident account naming the detection that was missing.

The paper concedes that no source available says where the detection line falls and that this session does not either; that the register is an invention from a verified observation; that the interview step is not always runnable; and that **a design built on a documented absence is fragile in a specific direction — if a detection rate is ever published, the session's central argument must be rewritten rather than defended.**

## 1. The problem this session must solve

An alert set is pruned by the recipient, silently, and nobody finds out.

**The mechanism is well known and rarely written down.** Alerts accumulate the way telemetry does — every incident adds one and nothing removes one — until the receiving engineer reaches a volume they cannot act on. They then do what any competent person does under an unmanageable queue: they triage. **The triage is invisible, undocumented, personal to that engineer, and lost when they leave.**

The organisation believes it is alerting on a long list of things. It is alerting on a handful, and the handful was chosen by somebody who never told anybody and has since moved teams.

**This is not a discipline problem and it cannot be solved by insisting people read their alerts.** It is a capacity problem, and the only honest response is to do the triage explicitly, at the front, in writing, with the losses recorded.

There is a second problem the first one hides. **A system can produce excellent findings that reach nothing.** A scanner runs, a report is generated, the report is correct, and no process anywhere consumes it. That is not alert fatigue; it is a missing edge in a pipeline, and it is invisible because the report exists and looks like an output.

And there is a third, which is the one the session is named after and the only one that cannot be engineered away. **An incident occurred, the posture tooling was running, and it produced nothing** — and the team has no way to establish whether that is because nothing happened, because the tool cannot see that class of thing, or because what it saw fell below a threshold nobody has examined. **The vendor publishes no detection rate and no false-positive rate**, so the inference from silence to safety has an unknown strength. That is not a gap the team can close by working harder, and a design implying otherwise would be teaching learners to manufacture reassurance out of an absence.

The session's response is a service level that licenses the pruning, a deletion record that prices it, a register that finds the missing edges, and a statement of what each instrument could and could not have seen.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is low. The affective load is high and concentrated in one act: writing down what a deletion means you will now miss. **Deleting an alert is easy and pricing the deletion is not**, and the session spends its surplus there.

**3.2 Why a floor rather than an intention.** An objective set at the team's intention has two properties that guarantee its abandonment. It is breached by ordinary variation, so breaches carry no information. And it leaves nothing to spend, so every change is a risk against a budget of zero. **An objective set at the customer's minimum has neither property**, and the difference is structural rather than motivational.

**3.3 Why the customer must supply it.** A service level agreed inside the delivery team is an estimate of what the customer would accept, produced by people with an interest in the answer. **The estimate is systematically optimistic in the direction that flatters the team** — and it is also usually too high rather than too low, because a delivery team's professional pride sets a floor above the customer's tolerance and then treats every dip below it as a failure.

**3.4 Why deletion must be priced.** An unpriced deletion is indistinguishable from an oversight, and both look like a shorter list. **Writing what will now be missed converts the deletion into a decision that can be reviewed**, which is the same move [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) made for the withheld tail: **the difference between a professional withholding and a concealment is entirely a property of the record.**

**3.5 Why the unconsumed-signal register is a register rather than an instruction.** Telling a team to connect their outputs to consumers produces agreement and no artefact. **Requiring an entry per output, with a consumer or a recorded decision, produces a list whose gaps are visible** — and the gaps are the point, because a missing edge is invisible from either end.

**3.6 Why the account is about the system.** The source's reliability material states the operating stance directly: when a user makes a mistake, the question is how it is prevented in future rather than how somebody could possibly have done that. **A remedy of "be more careful" is a decision not to change anything**, dressed as a corrective action.

**3.7 Why the absence of an alert is the session's central object, and why the argument is epistemological.**

The claim is narrow and it is not a claim about any product. **A monitoring system's silence is informative in proportion to what that system can detect.** Where the detection capability is known, silence is evidence; where it is unknown, silence is not weak evidence but no evidence — and it is experienced as strong evidence, because nothing distinguishes a quiet instrument from a quiet world at the point of observation.

The posture tooling the session anchors on is candid about what it covers and silent about how well. It publishes its **scan scope** — assessments run over a sample of the estate's most-used locations, with item-level scanning restricted to a small number of sites and one major storage service excluded from it altogether — its **cadence**, which is weekly by default, and its **latency**, with policies taking time to produce data, first assessments taking days to display, and custom results expiring ([research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md)). **Those three together are the resolution of the instrument, and stating them is the most transferable act in the session**, because it applies to every scanner a learner will ever be handed.

**And it publishes no effectiveness data of any kind: no detection rates, no false-positive rates, no coverage measurements.** That absence is not carelessness, and the design is deliberate about how it frames it: almost no product in this class publishes such figures, so **the absence is a norm — and a norm is precisely what a curriculum should make visible**, because an engineer who believes their tools arrive with characterised error rates will make worse decisions than one who knows they do not.

Three further published facts do real teaching work. The documentation's own statement that **"generative AI amplifies the problem of oversharing data"** yields the most portable idea in the source: **deploying capable retrieval converts every dormant permissions error in an organisation into a live one**, because over-permissioned estates were survivable only while nobody read them. Its **attack path analysis** exists because individually acceptable configurations compose into breaches — "toxic combinations" — and names grounding and fine-tuning as the two moments a model touches proprietary data, and therefore the two moments it can leak by construction rather than by attack. And its threat list names the **wallet attack**, a cost-driving rather than availability-driving attack that follows directly from usage-based billing and that classical security models do not cover.

**The source's limits are extreme and travel with every use.** One of the two pages consulted is explicitly the **classic** version and states improvements will not be backported; the other was read through search excerpts rather than a full fetch, so its structure is reconstructed. Agent-level discovery and posture move to a separate licence on a stated date. Substantial licensing and tenant-administrator prerequisites apply. **No effectiveness figure of any kind is asserted anywhere in this paper or in the session.**

**3.8 Why an absence is treated as a measurement with power.**

The discipline that makes the session's opening item answerable is imported from the statistics of evaluation, and the transfer is exact. **An evaluation is an experiment**, and the analytical treatment supplies the apparatus: standard errors reported beside means, item counts, cluster counts where observations arrive in related groups, and — the load-bearing part — **power analysis, which establishes in advance the smallest true difference an instrument could reliably detect** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). The paper's own target is the practice of reporting a result without testing whether it is distinguishable from noise, which is exactly what "no alert fired" is.

Two of its results matter here. **Clustering**: where observations are drawn in related groups rather than independently, treating them as independent overstates precision, so an instrument is usually less discriminating than it appears. And **the Minimum Detectable Effect is computable before the run**, which converts "we saw nothing" from a conclusion into a specification. **The design's requirement follows without embellishment: an absence is reported as a property of the instrument, never as a property of the system.**

**Its limits:** analytical rather than empirical; most illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions and **none is reproduced here**; and statistical validity says nothing about construct validity — a well-quantified null result about an irrelevant class is still irrelevant.

**3.9 Why some incidents produce no signal because there is nothing to signal.**

The production machine-learning literature supplies the mechanism, and it is the sharpest available example of a failure with no error attached. **Training–serving skew** — preprocessing performed differently at training and at serving — is described as a common production failure whose errors are hard to find **because the model continues to return plausible answers** ([research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md)). The same source separates **drift**, comparing one source over time, from **skew**, comparing two sources such as training against serving; and splits drift into data drift, a change in the inputs, and concept drift, a change in the relationship between inputs and labels, **which invalidates the mapping the model learned rather than merely moving the inputs.**

The operationally decisive detail is about detection rather than failure. **Drift detection splits into supervised techniques requiring fresh labels and unsupervised techniques that do not**, and the unsupervised family is the practical default because labelling is slow and expensive. **So the instrument actually running is the weaker one, and the stronger one is unavailable for the reason that makes the problem hard in the first place** — there is no ground truth for what is happening now. That is why the session's detection-capability statement expects at least one row whose honest confidence in silence is *none*.

**Its limits:** practitioner guidance from a commercial publisher, not research; no study design, no comparison group; a single-vendor default toolchain; some foundational claims rest on **anonymous practitioner testimony**; every quantity is a property of a particular system, and **no figure from it appears here.**

**3.10 Why an incident count measures the reporting climate.**

A further class of incident is one the instruments could have caught and a person chose not to raise. The organisational-behaviour literature is precise about the mechanism and precise about why it is invisible. **"Silence — when voice was possible — rarely announces itself"**: the moment passes and nobody is the wiser except the person who held back ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)). The cause is a calculus rather than a character trait, and the calculus is asymmetric — **voice benefits the organisation, later, with low certainty; silence benefits oneself, immediately, with high certainty** — which yields the observation that carries the argument: **"no one was ever fired for silence."** The same work reconstructs the *implicit theories of voice* that operate as taken-for-granted rules, including that one does not criticise something the boss may have helped create and does not speak without solid data.

The constructive half is what the session's incident account rests on. The failure taxonomy separates **preventable** failures, deviations from a known process; **complex** failures, novel combinations of events and actions; and **intelligent** failures, forays into new territory — and insists the response must differ by type. **Applying the preventable-failure response to a novel failure is the specific act that teaches people to stop reporting**, because it reads as blame for something nobody could have known. The same work records that the proportion of failures managers say *should* be treated as blameworthy and the proportion they concede *are* treated that way differ substantially, **and the gap runs in the direction that suppresses reporting.** Sanction remains inside the model: a fair, considered response to genuinely dangerous behaviour is described as reinforcing the climate rather than harming it.

**Its limits:** the work read in full is a trade book by the construct's originator, with case studies drawn almost entirely from secondary sources — teaching cases, journalism, official investigation reports — and **the foundational empirical paper was not read for this repository**, so claims traceable only to it are held at direction and mechanism. **No magnitude from any of it is reproduced here**, and nothing in it establishes that a better climate produces fewer incidents; it establishes a mechanism by which incidents go unreported, which is narrower.

**3.11 Why the gateway is the second half of the anchor.**

The two instruments look at different things and the seam between them is where incidents live. The gateway sits in the request path, can log prompts, completions and token usage **"for billing and auditing"**, can emit token metrics with **custom dimensions** that make consumption attributable per interface and per consumer, and can break the circuit on an unhealthy backend using a **dynamic trip duration taken from the backend's own `Retry-After` header** rather than a guessed interval ([research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md)).

Two consequences. **The wallet attack named on the posture surface is a traffic pattern before it is a bill**, and is attributable only if the dimensions were emitted before the attack — which is [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md)'s argument arriving as a security property rather than a cost one. And **the gateway's own logging is a privacy surface the documentation recommends and never discusses**, so a register that treats prompt capture as free has an undeclared exposure of its own. **Its limits:** first-party documentation, fetched and read in full, commercially framed; tier-dependent availability; preview components; **no performance or cost data of any kind.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The eight movements run situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**Opening item 4 — no alert fired last month; write down exactly what that licenses you to believe — is the instrument.** It is asked before instruction and the answers divide cleanly into two kinds: sentences about the system, and sentences about the instrument. **Only the second kind is defensible, and most learners write the first.** Item 1 remains the second instrument, and its error has a direction: the service level given is the one the team would like to be held to, which sits above the customer's floor.

**4.1 The opening items are an unscaffolded generation phase.** The design is Productive Failure — learners generate answers to a problem they have not been taught, fail, and are then taught — and the binding condition is that the generation phase must not be helped, because guidance that lowers the failure rate lowers the learning ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). This is why the release rule gates §§4, 5 and 9 rather than merely suggesting an order. **Its limits are recorded: a trade book resting on school, university and laboratory evidence, with no adult-professional study in its reference list.**

**4.2 Why exercise 3 is a comparison rather than a rewrite.** The same research tested exactly this: presenting a correct answer beside a learner's wrong one produced no benefit, and only the condition *instructed to compare them* learned more. Exercise 3 therefore forbids rewriting the item-4 answer until the learner has written one sentence naming the difference between it and the criterion.

**4.3 The workflow obtains the service level before touching the alert set**, and the ordering is the design. An alert set pruned without a floor is pruned against the pruner's judgement, which is what the mail rule already did.

**4.4 The resolution line precedes the silence register**, because a register written first would record confidences the learner has no basis for. Stating scan scope, cadence and latency is a prerequisite for stating what an instrument could have seen, and the session makes the dependency explicit rather than trusting the learner to notice it.

**4.5 Why the silence register carries a confidence column with *none* as a permitted value.** A register that only records what the team can detect reads as coverage. **Forcing a verdict on the classes the instruments cannot see puts the gap in the same table as the coverage**, which is the whole of the artefact's contribution — and §3.9 guarantees that at least one row will honestly read *none*, because supervised drift detection needs labels almost nobody has.

**4.6 The interview step is placed fourth rather than first deliberately.** A learner who asks the receiving engineer before deriving the alert set gets a list of annoyances; a learner who asks after has something to compare the annoyances with. **The step carries a prohibition that is the step**: the learner may not defend any alert while it is being marked. Without it the exercise becomes a negotiation and the marking stops being honest. The second question — is there anything you have stopped raising — is the one §3.10 exists to justify, and it is the one learners flinch from, because its honest answer is a statement about the team rather than the tooling.

**4.7 Why the agent is turned on the register rather than used to build it.** Enumeration is what the tooling is good at; deciding what an absence licenses is what it is not. The session gives it the adversarial half — argue that each claimed confidence is unwarranted — with rejections recorded, and with the standing reminder that an instrument sharing a family with the systems under discussion is not independent of them.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Silence read as safety.** The failure the session exists against, and the one that survives longest because it is the most natural thing to write. *Countermeasure:* the prohibition on the sentence "no incidents occurred", the permitted form that names class, instrument and resolution, and the confidence column. **§9's prediction 5 predicts these failing.**

**The uncharacterised instrument.** A posture tool trusted without its scan scope, cadence or latency being known. *Countermeasure:* the resolution line as a required part of the artefact, and exercise 2, which sends the learner to find all three for a tool that probably does not publish them.

**The aspirational floor.** The service level is what the team hopes to deliver, set without the customer. *Countermeasure:* the explicit disclaimer sentence, which is an assessed element at [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) and is hard to write untruthfully.

**The unpriced deletion.** *Countermeasure:* the deletion record, and the standard's insistence that a deletion is only defensible in writing.

**The undeleted alert.** The learner agrees an alert expects no action and keeps it anyway. *Countermeasure:* the warning sign — your deletion record is empty and your alert count went up — and the interview step, which produces evidence the learner did not generate.

**The convenient register.** Every unconsumed signal is one the learner was going to connect anyway. *Countermeasure:* handing the register to a second reader who finds one more.

**The blameful account.** *Countermeasure:* named in the failure list with its warning sign, the standard's requirement that the account be about the system, and exercise 5, which classifies the failure by type before examining the response.

**No person to ask.** A learner working on a system nobody is paged for cannot run the interview step. *Countermeasure:* none available, and §8's second objection concedes it.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: pruning an alert set against a floor optimises for the failures you already understand, and the incident that hurts is the one nobody modelled.**

At its strongest: a service level is expressed in terms somebody thought of. The outage that damages a customer relationship is usually a mode nobody had in mind — a dependency behaving legally and unexpectedly, a data condition outside the evaluation set, a downstream consumer nobody knew existed. **Alerts derived strictly from a stated floor will not cover any of those**, and the surplus alerting the session deletes is precisely the undirected coverage that occasionally catches one. **The session therefore trades a real, if low-yield, safety net for a tidier list, and calls the tidiness discipline.**

**Conceded in part, and the rebuttal does not fully answer it.** The narrow response is that undirected alerting does not in fact catch the unmodelled failure, because the alert it produces goes to a queue that has already been triaged into silence — **the safety net the objection defends is the net that alert fatigue has already cut.** The broader response is that the unconsumed-signal register and the incident account exist for exactly this class, and require the learner to name a detection that was missing rather than to rely on volume. **The declined fix is a required floor on retained alerting breadth.** Declined because nothing available sets it, and a threshold set by a marker is an opinion in a specification's clothing. **The residual risk is real and the session states it: a learner following this will fail to detect something, and the design's only answer is that they wrote down which class.**

**Objection 2: the session's best evidence step requires a person who is woken up, and many learners have no such person.**

At its strongest: this is a programme with student projects and simulated engagements. The instruction to go and ask the on-call engineer what they have stopped reading assumes an operational context most learners do not have and cannot manufacture. **A requirement that cannot be met becomes a requirement that is performed**, and a performed version of this step — imagining what a receiving engineer would say — is worse than not running it, because it produces a fabricated finding with the authority of an interview.

**Conceded without qualification.** The step is not always runnable and the session does not currently say so. **The rebuttal is only about what to do instead**, and it is thin: where no such person exists, the honest substitute is to record that the step could not be run and to leave the deletion decisions explicitly unvalidated, rather than to simulate the interview. **The declined fix is to replace the interview with an agent playing the receiving engineer.** It is declined on the same grounds this programme declines persona substitution generally: **using a model's simulation of a person in place of a person is proposed in the source corpus with no validity discussion at all**, in the same collection that warns against building for an imagined user, and this design will not import that move to patch a gap in its own runnability. **The gap stays open.**

**Objection 3: building a session on a documented absence is unfalsifiable, selective, and will age badly the moment the absence is filled.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the design finds hardest. A missing detection rate is not evidence of anything. **The session takes the fact that a vendor has not published one and converts it into a disposition — heightened distrust of silence — which no observation could disconfirm**, since an incident confirms the caution and the absence of incidents confirms that the caution was warranted. That is the structure of an unfalsifiable belief, and this repository is normally hostile to it. It is also selective: **no security vendor in this class publishes detection or false-positive rates**, no static analyser ships with a measured miss rate, and singling out this documentation implies a standard nobody meets while making a norm look like a defect. And it is fragile in an embarrassing direction — **if the measure is published next term, the session's central claim evaporates**, and a design that can be deleted by a documentation update was resting on something thinner than it appeared.

**Partly conceded, and the third part conceded outright.** On unfalsifiability: the session's operational content is not a disposition but a prohibition with an instrument — **do not write "no incidents occurred", write the permitted form, and here is the check for whether you did** — and prediction 5 is written to detect its failure, which is precisely what an unfalsifiable design cannot offer. The confidence column is likewise checkable: a register with no *none* row on a system running unsupervised drift detection is wrong on its face. On selectivity: the objection is right that the absence is a norm, and **the design's answer is that a norm is exactly what a curriculum should make visible.** The session states the absence as a working condition rather than as an accusation, and the wording is deliberate. On fragility: **conceded without qualification.** If a detection rate is published, this session's opening must be rewritten, and the honest response is that a design tied to a documented state of the world should be revisable when the world changes rather than insulated from it — which is why the absence is recorded with a date.

**The declined fix is to replace the absent rate with a cohort-generated estimate**: have every learner log every alert against whether an incident in fact occurred, and pool the results. Declined on three grounds, and the third settles it. The denominator is unavailable, because a learner cannot establish that no incident occurred any more reliably than the tool could. The pooled sample would span different estates, tools, licences and threat exposures, so the aggregate would answer no question anybody asked. **And a number produced that way would be repeated** — this repository exists partly because well-formed unsupported figures travel further than the caveats attached to them, and manufacturing one to fill the exact gap the session teaches learners to respect would be the most direct self-contradiction available to it. **The cost of declining is that the session's central fact remains an absence, which is less satisfying to teach and, prediction 5 suggests, harder to retain.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners' first answer to opening item 1 will describe an intended quality rather than a customer floor. *Instrument:* the submitted opening answers, coded *customer floor* / *team intention* / *cannot tell* by a second reader, before any instruction.

**Prediction 2.** Deletion records will more often name the alert removed than name what will now be missed. *Instrument:* per-entry presence check for the missed-event clause.

**Prediction 3.** Where the interview step is run, the receiving engineer will mark alerts the learner expected to be read. *Instrument:* the learner's pre-interview prediction of which alerts are ignored, compared with the engineer's marking.

**Prediction 4.** Unconsumed-signal registers will contain fewer entries before a second reader examines them than after. *Instrument:* entry count recorded at both points. **If the counts are equal, either the register is being written well or the second reader is not being given enough of the system to find anything — and the session cannot currently distinguish those, which is a defect in the instrument rather than in the prediction.**

**Prediction 5.** Despite the prohibition, a majority of submitted artefacts will contain at least one sentence asserting that no incident occurred, or reporting a period as clean, without naming an instrument or a resolution. *Instrument:* the artefacts, scanned for absolute absence claims and coded against whether the permitted form appears elsewhere in the same document. **This predicts the failure of §5's most important countermeasure and of §8's third objection's rebuttal**, and it is the prediction this paper most expects to be confirmed.

**Prediction 6.** Most detection-capability statements will contain no row whose confidence in silence is recorded as *none*, on systems that run unsupervised drift detection only. *Instrument:* the statements, cross-checked against the learner's own description of their drift detection. **A confirmation means learners are treating the confidence column as a scale with a floor above zero**, and the material should state the *none* case as the expected outcome rather than as a permitted one.

**Prediction 7.** Learners will be able to state scan scope, cadence and latency for the anchored posture tooling and for almost nothing else in their estate. *Instrument:* the resolution line, coded per instrument as *all three stated* / *partial* / *absent*, with the source of each recorded. **If this holds, the transferable lesson is the unavailability rather than the triple**, and the session should say so directly instead of implying that most vendors publish it.

**Prediction 8.** Where the interview step is run, the second question — is there anything you have stopped raising — will be omitted or answered with "nothing" far more often than the first. *Instrument:* the interview record, coded for whether both questions were asked and what each returned. **This is the instrument attached to §3.10**, and a confirmation is consistent with either a healthy climate or an unsafe one, which is why the record must be verbatim rather than summarised.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That an alert set is pruned by its recipient whether or not anybody decides to prune it; that a service level set at the team's intention is abandoned rather than met; that a finding reaching no queue was not found by the organisation; and — centrally — **that the absence of an alert licenses a belief about the world only in proportion to what the alerting instrument can detect.**

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The two borrowed operational rules are class 1 — verified from a complete reading of a public document — **and that document supplies no comparative evidence for either, and the page carrying its service-level definition is among the oldest on its site.** The orphan-analyser observation is class 1 from access-controlled material. The posture material is class 4: **its scan scope, cadence and latency are published; its detection rate and false-positive rate are not, and none is asserted anywhere.** The statistical discipline is class 1 and concerns whether a reported absence means anything, not whether the class you watched was worth watching. Everything built on top is class 3.

**Where the design is thinnest.** §8's third objection. A curriculum built on a documented absence is fragile by construction, and this one says so with a date attached rather than pretending otherwise.

**The concession worth remembering.** You are going to stop seeing something, and this session cannot tell you whether it was the thing that mattered. **What it can tell you is that the alternative — a mail rule nobody wrote down, and a quiet dashboard read as a healthy system — is the same decision made worse.**

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That the openly published playbook states in those words that alert fatigue is real, and defines a service-level objective as minimum viable service quality — explicitly not what the team intends to deliver, but what is acceptable for the customer; that the same document states a system cannot target perfect availability; and that its reliability stance is to ask how a user's mistake is prevented in future rather than how somebody could possibly have made it. Recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md), from a complete reading of a public document. **The page carrying the service-level definition is among the four oldest on that site, dated 7 May 2021**, and the site has no single date.

That the pipeline's three auxiliary analysers produce good standalone reports, that none emits an identifier the chain uses, that no stage consumes their output, and that a real vulnerability found by the code analyser therefore never becomes a threat, a work item or a backlog entry; and the practitioner judgements that false positives destroy reviewer trust, that destroyed trust costs more than the findings were worth, and that teams given every finding at once fix none of them. Recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md), from a complete reading of **access-controlled material that no public reader can check.**

That the same corpus proposes substituting a model's simulation of a user for a user with no validity discussion, in material that elsewhere warns against building for an imagined user — recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md), and load-bearing for §8's declined fix.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That a queue exceeding a recipient's capacity is triaged by the recipient rather than left unread; that a target breached by ordinary variation ceases to carry information; that an estimate produced by a party with an interest in the answer tends to move in the direction of that interest; and that an operator cannot distinguish a quiet instrument from a quiet world at the point of observation. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The requirement that the service level be obtained from the customer before the alert set is touched; the requirement that every alert name an expected action and actor; the deletion record and the claim that an unpriced deletion is indistinguishable from an oversight; the instrument-resolution line as a required artefact; the detection-capability statement, the confidence column and the requirement that at least one row read *none*; the prohibition on the sentence "no incidents occurred" and the permitted form that replaces it; the unconsumed-signal register; the incident account and its reconstruction from a pre-mortem; the prohibition on defending alerts while they are marked; the decision to produce the register here and assess it nowhere; and all eight predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The posture documentation states that **"generative AI amplifies the problem of oversharing data"**; that its default data risk assessment runs weekly over a sample of the estate's most-used locations; that item-level scanning is limited to a small number of sites and excludes one major storage service; that a cap applies to items per location, with counts becoming unreliable above a stated size; that policies, custom assessments and first displays each carry their own multi-day latencies and that custom results expire; that without an explicit policy some first-party AI surfaces audit the event and not the prompts and responses; that its bill of materials covers application components, data and AI artefacts from code to cloud; that attack path analysis targets **"toxic combinations"** with grounding and fine-tuning named as the data-exposure moments; and that the named AI-native attacks include prompt injection, **wallet attacks**, model theft and data poisoning. Recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md). **One of the two pages was fetched and read in full and is explicitly the classic version, stating that improvements will not be added to it; the other was read through search-returned excerpts rather than a full fetch, so its structure is reconstructed. Agent-level discovery and posture move to a separate licence on a stated date.**

The gateway documentation states that logging captures prompts, completions and token usage **"for billing and auditing"**; that token metrics can carry **custom dimensions**; and that its circuit breaker applies a **dynamic trip duration** taken from the backend's `Retry-After` header rather than a fixed interval. Recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md), **from a page fetched and read in full**, with policy names, attributes and tier boundaries recorded as drifting between revisions and parts of the surface marked preview.

The openly published handbook is a vendor engineering document and **cannot be dated as a whole**: page footers span more than four years, so the alerting and service-level material is cited by its own page date and the site by none. Read complete on 2026-07-30.

**The silences that matter, and they are this paper's subject rather than its footnote.** **No detection rate and no false-positive rate is published for the posture tooling — and no coverage measurement of any kind.** The documentation bounds the instrument without characterising it, so the strength of the inference from silence to safety is unknown rather than unstated, and **no effectiveness figure is asserted anywhere in this paper, in the session, or in this repository.** The documentation does not analyse the privacy or legal consequences of capturing prompts and responses, despite recommending the capture. The gateway documentation **publishes no performance, latency or cost figure of any kind** and does not discuss the privacy surface its own logging recommendation creates. The handbook **publishes no measure of alert volume against incident detection, no comparison of service levels set as floors against service levels set as intentions, and no evidence that any team following its alerting guidance detected anything it would otherwise have missed**; its observability section is the most rotted on the site, and no tool it recommends is named here. **Nothing in this session or paper is legal, regulatory or contractual guidance**, and the detection and reporting duties that will actually bind a learner belong to their organisation, their customer and their jurisdiction.
