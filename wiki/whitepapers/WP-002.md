# WP-002 — What a Forward-Deployed Engineer Is For

## In plain terms

The role this programme prepares people for has a public definition, and it comes from the company that invented the title. The definition is one line: a product engineer builds one capability for many customers, and a forward-deployed engineer builds many capabilities for one customer.

That line does more work than it looks like it does. It explains why the role is broad rather than deep, why the same person moves between unrelated domains, and why a decision about what to generalise turns up every week rather than every year. It also explains a fact that surprises people: in the organisation that named the role, **the forward-deployed engineer does not sit in the engineering division.** The role is commercial as well as technical, with a reporting line and a negotiated boundary against product.

The same source has one more thing to say that a curriculum ought to find uncomfortable. Asked what skills the role requires, the company declines to answer, describing its practitioners as a population of individuals whose value lies in each being different. This programme specifies a competency set anyway. It has to. But it specifies it as its own design decision, and this paper is where that is admitted.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching the role's boundary before teaching any of its techniques. The argument runs in four parts. **First**, a forward-deployed engineer is defined by an unusual accountability — for an outcome experienced by a customer, achieved largely through systems the engineer does not own, inside an organisation the engineer does not belong to — and an engineer who has not separated what they decide from what they may only propose will either claim authority they do not have or surrender judgement they do. **Second**, the artefact expressing that separation is not free-form: the entrustable-professional-activity description template supplies a tested shape, including a prohibition on adjectives in a title, and this paper adopts it. **Third**, the session's opening items are a Productive Failure generation phase whose central item is deliberately unanswerable, and the paper records a defect found while rebuilding the session — the first version generated learner answers and never instructed anyone to compare them against the taught criterion, which is the one condition the cited mechanism requires. **Fourth**, the platform workflow exists to make a single durable property observable: a specification has a delivery step, the delivery step fails silently, and a declared constraint and an enforced constraint are different objects.

The paper concedes that its primary sources are recruiting artefacts; that no outcome of any kind is measured in them; that the organisation which invented the role publicly refuses to specify its competencies while this programme specifies them anyway; that its staged refusal sits in direct tension with the self-efficacy literature it cites; and that teaching a boundary to an individual whose organisation will not honour it treats an environmental variable as a personal skill.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

[S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) asks a learner to write a position on whether an engagement should proceed. The obvious objection, raised by learners themselves within a day, is that nobody asked them.

The objection is largely correct and the programme concedes it at S001. But conceding it leaves a hole: if the engineer does not decide, what exactly are they for? A learner without an answer will settle on one of two defaults. Either they treat themselves as an implementer whose judgement begins after the requirements arrive, which forfeits the only vantage point from which some disqualifying conditions are visible at all. Or they treat themselves as the responsible adult in the room, which produces an engineer who blocks work they have no standing to block and is removed from the engagement.

There is a third default, less discussed and more common: the engineer who holds no position on the question, discovers the boundary by crossing it, and learns it as a reprimand. That is the outcome this session exists to pre-empt, and it is the one for which the published record gives the clearest warning. The primary source states that contributions back to a shared platform are frequent but conditional, that engineers must "coordinate with the product team", and that "Larger feature requests need to be validated against their roadmap and incorporated into it" ([research/05-fde-craft/palantir-fde-practitioner-writing.md](/research/05-fde-craft/palantir-fde-practitioner-writing.md)). An engineer who has never articulated that gate will meet it as a personal setback rather than as a structural fact.

**The session's response is to make the boundary an artefact rather than an attitude**, because an attitude adjusts under pressure and a written list can be checked, contradicted and dated.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Ordinary professional experience**, plus [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md).

Learners arrive with a strong prior that accountability follows authorship: you built it, therefore you decide about it. In an organisation where the thing you built for one customer may or may not belong in a product used by many, that prior is not merely wrong but expensively wrong, and it is worth disturbing before any technique is taught on top of it.

They also arrive with a second prior that the platform workflow is designed to disturb: that writing something down is the same as communicating it. That prior is nearly universal among engineers who have written a design document nobody read, and it is unusually cheap to falsify with an agent, because the agent's input can be inspected.

No prior exposure to agent configuration is assumed. A learner who has never created an instructions file can complete the workflow; a learner who has will find the verification step unfamiliar anyway, because inspecting the payload is not a normal part of using the tool.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is low. The concepts are few and mutually independent, and the difficulty is affective rather than cognitive: the work is giving something away and finding that the page still says something. The surplus is spent on two things a later session could not afford — the emotional cost of a staged refusal, and a first exposure to the idea that a specification has a delivery step. **This paragraph is a design judgement and carries no external warrant.**

**3.2 The opening items are a Productive Failure generation phase, and its conditions bind this session.**

Productive Failure is a two-phase, order-dependent design: learners generate solutions to a task targeting a concept they have not been taught, fail, and are then taught ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). The mechanism this session leans on is the **failed generation effect** — attempting an answer before instruction improves later learning even when the attempt is wrong. Item 3 is built to produce exactly that: most learners cannot say who is allowed to decide that a customer-specific build becomes part of the product, and the design wants them not to.

Three of the design's stated conditions bind here, and they are conditions rather than preferences.

- **Do not scaffold the generation phase.** Guidance during problem-solving that reduces the failure rate reduces learning. This is why the release rule above gates §§4, 5 and 9, and why the session states which item matters *after* the items rather than before.
- **The consolidation phase must explicitly prompt comparison.** Presenting a correct solution beside an incorrect one achieves nothing on its own; only the condition instructed to compare them learned more. **The session as first written violated this.** See §4.3.
- **The norms are part of the design.** The dependency on the surrounding social conditions is explicit and directional, which matters more than usual here because the session's staged refusal is a social event.

**The extrapolation is conceded up front.** The evidence behind Productive Failure is school, university and laboratory work; its author states that the cycle works in adult and corporate settings and cites nothing for that sentence. This programme's learners are working professionals. §8 returns to this.

**3.3 The staged refusal, and its tension with the self-efficacy literature.**

The session asks a peer to decline the learner's most useful proposal for a roadmap reason. The design argument is that a boundary is learned by meeting it rather than by being told where it is — which is the ordering conclusion error-management training reaches independently: learners should meet the difficulty before they are given the resolution, and the payoff shows on transfer ([research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md)).

**But the self-efficacy literature pushes the other way, and this paper does not intend to hide it.** Bandura's own account of persuasion is asymmetric: it is harder to build a strong sense of efficacy by persuasion than to undermine one, and "people who have been persuaded that they lack capabilities tend to avoid challenging activities" ([research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md](/research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md)). His prescription for anyone building efficacy is explicit that they "structure situations for them in ways that bring success and avoid placing people in situations prematurely where they are likely to fail often". A refusal staged in week one is, on its face, the premature failure he warns against.

Three things reconcile the two, none completely. The refusal is **impersonal by construction** — the peer's script gives a roadmap reason, which is a fact about somebody else's plan and not a judgement of the learner's work, and the same source is clear that what a model transmits is often the coping rather than the skill. The failure is **recoverable within the session**, because the required response is a rewrite rather than a defeat. And Bandura is equally explicit in the other direction: "If people experience only easy successes they come to expect quick results and are easily discouraged by failure. A resilient sense of efficacy requires experience in overcoming obstacles through perseverant effort."

**That is a reconciliation and not a resolution**, and the risk is real: a learner who reads the refusal as a verdict on their competence has been harmed by a design decision this paper made. §5 carries the countermeasure and §9 carries the prediction that would detect it. **Note also that the 1977 paper in which self-efficacy was introduced has not been read for this repository**; the claims above come from Bandura's 1995 restatement, read in full, of which only the first chapter is his own.

**3.4 The artefact borrows the shape of an entrustable-activity description, and the borrowing does real work.**

The boundary statement is not free prose. It follows the description template for an entrustable professional activity, which specifies, in order: a title; a specification-and-limitations section capturing "no more and no less than what is exactly meant"; a link to the prevailing competency framework; the knowledge, skills and experience required; the sources of information supporting an entrustment decision; a targeted level of supervision; and optionally an expiry date ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)).

Three of those seven transfer with unusual force. The rule that a title "should not contain an adjective" is not stylistic fussiness — **an adjective smuggles a quality standard into the name of a thing, where a limit belongs instead**, which is precisely how "responsible for good architecture" evades every question worth asking. The specification-and-limitations section is what stops an activity from silently expanding to cover cases nobody intended to certify, which is the failure mode of every role description ever written. And the expiry date acknowledges that a delegation decays if the activity is not practised, which almost no engineering role description admits.

The framework's granularity test is also the session's: could a supervisor delegate this whole thing and walk away, and would the learner know when they had finished it? A boundary line that fails either half is the wrong size. **The primer is design guidance from medical education, explicitly guidance rather than evaluation, and its transfer to engineering is this programme's inference.**

**3.5 Dispositions are curricular objects, and the warrant for saying so is disciplinary rather than empirical.**

The programme specifies a competency set that its primary source refuses to specify. The licence it uses is the 2023 curricular guidelines' statement that "Computer Science is a professional discipline where skills and professional dispositions are just as important as the underlying knowledge" ([research/07-accreditation-exemplars/cs2023-competency-and-disposition-materials.md](/research/07-accreditation-exemplars/cs2023-competency-and-disposition-materials.md)).

That document is unusually candid about the weakness of its own position. It records that the computing education community "has not yet widely accepted the benefits of professional dispositions to the same extent" as skills, and that its competency model was "yet-to-be-designed" at the time of writing, with the knowledge model shipped as the operational artefact precisely because adoption depends on continuity. **It confers legitimacy, not compliance, and offers no validation of the model against graduate outcomes.** A reader who treats it as evidence that disposition-based curricula work has read it wrongly, and so has a reader who dismisses it for not being that; it is doing a different job.

**3.6 Why the artefact is written as a file an executor loads.**

The session could have stopped at prose. It does not, because prose has no observable delivery step, and the whole claim of the artefact is that a boundary must be received to exist.

The strongest available worked example is a Microsoft repository that treats agent behaviour as an authored engineering artefact and separates instructions, prompts, agents and skills into distinct trees with distinct application rules, versioned and packaged like code ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)). One of its declarations is a role boundary in the exact sense this session means: an agent may not record a human's approval, and a workflow must stop when a required human review is absent. **Human approval is a stop condition, not a field a workflow can fill.**

The corpus is also the source of the caution the session ends on, and that is the more important half. Its own transparency note states that the project ships no model and no safety classifier, that host behaviour varies, and that human oversight remains necessary — so whether a declared boundary holds depends on a host loading it and a model complying, neither of which the file can guarantee. And at the pinned commit the note was written from, its evaluation machinery had graders and no committed results. **The existence of a control is not evidence that the control works**, and the session's step 5 exists so that a learner sees a declared constraint fail rather than being told it might.

The same note supplies the reason the payload check and the compliance check are separate steps: the corpus's own documentation records that its regex-graded behavioural checks test output shape and not correctness. Verifying arrival is a shape test. Verifying that the boundary changed the answer is not, and the session asks for both.

**3.7 The counterweight: an agreeing executor has not been used.**

The workflow's last step asks the learner to have the agent attack the boundary rather than improve it. The warrant is thin and stated as such. Capability in these systems is jagged — the boundary between competence and failure is uneven and does not track human-perceived difficulty, and outside it the failure mode is confident error rather than refusal ([research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md](/research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md)). **That note rests on an abstract; its shape is usable and no number in it is.** The design inference — that a model asked to attack must locate a weakness, which is a harder operation than adding — is this programme's own and is untested.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The movement sequence.** Situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

The artefact has two lists and the second is the one being assessed. A learner who produces a rich accountability list and an empty proposal list has not done the exercise; they have written a job description.

**4.2 Item 3 is the instrument.** It asks who decides that a customer-specific build becomes part of the product, and it is placed third so that items 1 and 2 have already established the learner's sense of ownership. The gap between a confident answer to item 1 and a vague answer to item 3 is the session's content, and it is legible to the learner only because both were written by the same hand in the same ten minutes.

Items 1, 2, 4 and 5 are not filler. Item 5 — what it means that the organisation which named the role declines to specify its skills — is the only item asking the learner to evaluate the programme itself, and it is included so that the concession in §3.5 arrives as something the learner has already noticed rather than as an admission extracted later.

**4.3 A defect found while rebuilding this session, and the amendment.**

The first version of this session asked five opening items and never returned to them. The learner generated, failed, read an explanation and produced an artefact — but was never instructed to put the explanation beside what they had written.

**That is precisely the condition the mechanism in §3.2 requires.** Side-by-side presentation of a correct and an incorrect solution produced no benefit in the studies behind it; only the explicitly instructed comparison did. The session was discarding the mechanism this paper defends, while this paper cited that mechanism approvingly.

**The amendment:** exercise 3 now requires the learner to retrieve their own item-3 answer, place it beside the criterion, and write one sentence naming the difference *before* rewriting. Exercise 5 adds a second comparison prompt against item 2. Both are comparison instructions rather than recall prompts.

**The amendment is recorded rather than silently applied**, because the defect is the instructive part: a design can cite a mechanism correctly, describe it accurately and fail to implement it, and nothing in the citation catches that. Only reading a source at the depth of its design conditions catches it.

**4.4 Why the peer refusal is scripted narrowly.** The peer declines the generalisation and gives a roadmap reason. That reason is chosen because it is the one recorded in the primary source — larger contributions validated against a roadmap owned by another division — and because it cannot be answered by working harder. A quality-based refusal invites the learner to improve the proposal, which is the wrong lesson. A roadmap refusal invites the learner to ask who decides, which is the boundary.

**What the source does not supply is the interesting half.** Neither the 2019 article nor the 2020 interview says what a field engineer does when the product team declines. The session asks learners to answer a question their primary source leaves open, and says so in exercise 2's key.

**4.5 What the platform workflow is for.** It is not a tooling tutorial. It exists to make three things physically observable in one sitting: that a specification has a delivery step; that the delivery step fails silently; and that a declared constraint and an enforced constraint are different objects. Every later platform session in this programme is a variation on the third.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The undivided self.** The most likely outcome is a proposal list containing only things the learner did not want anyway. *Countermeasure:* exercise 2 asks a reader to identify any item the learner would in practice do regardless, which surfaces the padding without requiring the learner to admit it.

**Boundary as excuse.** A learner discovers that the boundary can be used to decline responsibility for anything difficult. *Countermeasure:* weak. The specification at [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md) checks the boundary against the decision the learner claims to make, which catches the case where the two contradict, and catches nothing else. **This is recorded as an unsolved problem rather than a managed one.**

**The refusal read as a verdict.** A learner takes the staged decline as evidence about their competence, which is the harm §3.3 concedes the design risks. *Countermeasure:* the script is impersonal and the required response is a rewrite, so the learner ends the movement holding an improved artefact rather than a rejection. Whether that is sufficient is untested, and §9's fourth prediction is where it would be detected.

**Adopting the source's org chart as a law of nature.** A learner concludes that forward-deployed engineering must report into a commercial function. *Countermeasure:* the session names the divisional placement as one organisation's arrangement in one period, and records that the taxonomy has since been restructured. The distinction is stated and not enforced anywhere.

**The green check.** A learner observes the instruction text in a payload, sees the agent comply once, and concludes the boundary holds. *Countermeasure:* step 5 and exercise 4 both require a violation to be produced rather than a pass. The countermeasure works only if learners try hard enough to fail, which §9's third prediction doubts.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S003](/wiki/seminars/S003.md), [S004](/wiki/seminars/S004.md) and [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md).

The boundary written here is what [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md) checks the qualification decision against: a learner who declares at S002 that a build-path change is something they may only propose, and then at S005 records a decision to change it, has produced two documents that cannot both be true. [S004](/wiki/seminars/S004.md) uses the same decision-rights vocabulary against an attendee list rather than against oneself, which is the harder direction because nobody in the room has written their answer down. [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md) and [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) reopen the boundary under commercial pressure.

The instructions file is retrieved differently. It is the first instance of an artefact this programme returns to whenever an executor's behaviour has to be specified, and the payload-verification habit is expected to transfer even where the file does not. That expectation is untested.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. One specification element at [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md): that the learner's claimed decision is consistent with the boundary they wrote here. **Neither the boundary's contents nor its generosity is marked** — only whether the two documents can both be true.

That restriction follows from the entrustment frame. An entrustable activity is a unit of work that can be handed over whole; the judgement is whether a supervisor would let this person do this unsupervised ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). **Drawing a boundary is a plausible activity. Drawing the correct boundary is not**, because correctness depends on the organisation the learner will join, which the assessment cannot hold constant. Consistency between two documents the learner wrote is checkable without any claim about which organisation they are right for.

**Nothing else is assessed at this session.** The instructions file, the payload observation and the prompt record are evidence the learner keeps; none is collected and none is marked.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the role split is one company's organisational arrangement, and teaching it as a discipline mistakes an org chart for a body of knowledge.**

At its strongest: the distinction was published by a company to recruit engineers; the internal name for the role turns out to be a residue of an old team-naming scheme with no semantic content; the divisional structure described is years old and has since been restructured; and no evidence is offered anywhere that the split produces better outcomes than not splitting.

**This is substantially correct and is conceded.** The rebuttal is narrow: the split describes a real and recurring accountability problem — somebody has to be answerable to one customer while somebody else is answerable to many — and that problem exists whether or not any particular company solves it this way. **The declined fix was to teach the role generically, without reference to the source at all.** It was declined because a learner who has never seen the arrangement named will not recognise it when placed inside one, and because a generic account would rest on no primary source at all rather than on a weak one.

**Objection 2: a programme cannot specify competencies its own primary source refuses to specify.**

At its strongest: the organisation that invented the role has more data about it than this programme will ever have, and it declines — "it's hard to pinpoint the exact set of skills required for success". Specifying anyway is a claim to knowledge nobody holds.

**Conceded without qualification.** The programme specifies a competency set because a programme must, and it is a design decision of this programme's own rather than a finding. The paper says so here, [S002](/wiki/seminars/S002.md) says so in its limits section, and no page in this stage claims otherwise. **The declined fix was to teach only the activities and refuse the competency language entirely.** It was declined because assessment requires something to assess against, and an activity list without stated qualities collapses into a checklist of tasks performed — which is exactly the activity-versus-impact confusion the practice corpus this programme is built on falls into and is criticised for. Substituting one known failure for another is not a fix.

**Objection 3: teaching a boundary to an individual whose organisation will not honour it treats an environmental variable as a personal skill.**

At its strongest: a boundary is only a boundary if somebody else respects it. An engineer who states that a platform change is theirs to propose and not to decide, in a team where that distinction is not recognised, has written a document that will be ignored and may be read as evasion. Worse, asserting a boundary is itself a voice behaviour, and voice behaviour is conditional on whether speaking up is safe — which is a property of the team rather than of the person ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md); [research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)).

**Partially conceded.** The session does not address the environment; [S004](/wiki/seminars/S004.md) reads it and [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md) sizes the cost of asserting it. That is late. **The declined fix was to move the psychological-safety material into S002**, declined on load grounds — the session already carries the affective work of a staged refusal, and adding an analysis of when a boundary is survivable would blunt the generation phase §3.2 requires be left unscaffolded. **The cost is that S002 through S008 teach a disposition without its precondition**, and this paper records that as an accepted cost rather than a solved problem.

**Objection 4: the platform workflow is a gimmick. An agent is not a colleague, and verifying that a file reached a model tells you nothing about whether a boundary reached a product owner.**

At its strongest: the analogy is doing all the work, and it is a bad analogy. Human recipients of a boundary have interests, memories and incentives; a model has a context window. The observation the workflow produces — that a file did or did not appear in a payload — is a fact about an editor's configuration, dressed up as a lesson about organisations.

**Not conceded, but the rebuttal is narrower than the workflow's framing suggests.** The claim is not that the agent stands in for a colleague. It is that the *delivery step* is normally invisible in human communication and is inspectable here, and that an engineer who has once watched a specification fail to arrive will ask a different question about the design document nobody acted on. The workflow teaches a habit of asking whether a thing arrived, using the only recipient whose inbox can be opened. **The declined fix was to drop the workflow and teach the boundary as prose**, declined because it would return this session to the condition the programme was rebuilt to escape — a page that asserts a durable claim and never makes it observable. A reader who judges the analogy too thin to carry the section is making a defensible call, and §9's sixth prediction is where it would show.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A majority of learners will place the generalisation decision inside their own accountability list before the session and outside it afterwards. *Instrument:* item 3's written answer against the corresponding line of the finished boundary statement, coded by a second reader as *inside* / *outside* / *unstated*.

**Prediction 2.** Proposal lists will contain systematically less consequential items than accountability lists, judged by whether the learner would perform the item anyway. *Instrument:* exercise 2's binary judgement, collected per item rather than per learner, so that a single genuine concession is not masked by four decorative ones.

**Prediction 3.** A majority of learners will fail to produce a boundary violation in exercise 4, and will report the agent as compliant. *Instrument:* the submitted prompt record, coded for whether a violation was elicited and how many attempts were made. **This predicts the failure of the session's own countermeasure** — the green-check failure mode in §5 is countered by an instruction to try to break the boundary, and an instruction to attempt something is not the same as attempting it. If it holds, step 5 should become a supplied adversarial prompt rather than a learner-authored one.

**Prediction 4.** Learners who receive the roadmap refusal will report lower confidence in their own judgement immediately after the movement and no lower confidence a week later, while learners who receive a quality-based refusal will show the reverse. *Instrument:* a single confidence item at the close of the movement and again at [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md), across a cohort split on the peer script. **This is the direct test of the tension §3.3 concedes**, and a sustained drop in the roadmap arm would mean the impersonal framing does not do the work this paper claims for it.

**Prediction 5.** Learners instructed to compare their item-3 answer against the criterion — exercise 3, added by the §4.3 amendment — will produce boundary lines that name a function and a threshold more often than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* the reviewer's coding across two cohorts, one before the amendment and one after; absent two cohorts, a within-cohort split on whether exercise 3 was completed. **This is the session's only direct test of the mechanism §3.2 rests on**, and a null result would mean the §4.3 amendment was ceremony.

**Prediction 6.** In a majority of learner environments, only one of the two boundary files will appear in the request payload, and learners will not notice until instructed to look. *Instrument:* the recorded payload observation, coded for which files appeared and whether the discrepancy was reported before step 4 or only after. **If both files appear reliably and learners notice unprompted, the session's central platform claim has no demonstration at this scale**, and the step should be rewritten around a deliberately misplaced file instead.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the role is defined by an accountability rather than by a technology; that the accountability has an edge which has to be located before it is tested; and that a boundary nobody received is not a boundary.

**What the evidence licenses.** A description of one organisation's stated model, from that organisation, in material written to recruit. Nothing about whether the model works, transfers, or is accurately described by its own recruiting material. The teaching *method* is better grounded than the substantive thesis, and the two are about different things — **do not let the quality of the pedagogical citations transfer to the claim that boundaries improve engagements**, which nothing here supports.

**The tension worth holding.** §3.3 cites a literature arguing against one of the session's own design choices, and the session keeps the choice anyway with a stated reason and a prediction that would detect the harm. That is the shape an honest design decision takes. It is not the same as being right.

**The concession worth remembering.** The company that invented this role says it cannot say what the role requires. Every competency claim in this programme is downstream of a refusal to make one.

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper**, and none may be inferred from it.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** The one-capability-many-customers contrast, the divisional placement of the role on the commercial side, the roadmap gate on contributions back to the platform, the absence of any published success measure for the product-side role, the semantic emptiness of the internal role name, and the organisation's own refusal to specify the competency set are all recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/palantir-fde-practitioner-writing.md](/research/05-fde-craft/palantir-fde-practitioner-writing.md), which rests on a complete reading of the primary text from a locally held copy. That the sources are recruiting artefacts is recorded in the same note and is not this paper's inference. The one-owning-role-per-phase pattern is recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-fde-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-fde-delivery-practice.md). The engineer-obligation definition, the collective-ownership rule and the scope-discipline rule are recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md), which rests on a complete reading of every page of that site. The Productive Failure design conditions in §3.2 — two-phase and order-dependent, generation unscaffolded, consolidation requiring explicit comparison — are recorded at [research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md), which rests on a complete reading of the source text. The entrustable-activity description template, the adjective prohibition and the granularity test are recorded at [research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md) from an open-access full text. The Bandura quotations in §3.3 are from the 1995 volume, read in full; **the 1977 paper in which self-efficacy was introduced remains unread and paywalled, and nothing here is attributed to it.**

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That attempting an answer before instruction improves later retention of the tested material. That an expectation corrected by experience is more durable than one corrected by assertion. That psychological safety conditions voice behaviour, and that the construct survives comparison with adjacent constructs — the meta-analysis note behind that second claim is written from the paper's official abstract, and the publisher version is closed. That capability in these systems is jagged and fails by confident error rather than by refusal; that note rests on an abstract. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted for any of these.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The decision to teach the boundary before any technique; the four-part form of the artefact and the requirement that every accountability name an instrument; the claim that a boundary which only holds when the other side agrees is not a boundary; the choice of a roadmap refusal rather than a quality refusal as the staged decline; the reconciliation offered in §3.3 between a staged failure and the self-efficacy literature; the judgement in §7 that drawing a boundary is a plausible entrustable activity and drawing the correct one is not; the claim that a model asked to attack produces more specific output than one asked to improve; the transfer of a medical-education description template to an engineering role; the entire existence of Stage 1; the load-grounds rejection of the declined fix in objection 3; and all six predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The four artefact kinds, the separation of authoring source from generated output, the prohibition on an agent recording a human's approval, the requirement that a workflow stop when a required human review is absent, the untrusted-content boundary treating fetched pages and tool output as data rather than authority, and the documented statement that regex-graded behavioural checks test output shape rather than correctness are all recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md), read from a clone at commit `cf29fb457b0fe62745bb71592ec9394b834957f4` on `main`, published package version `3.3.101`. **That was not an all-green revision** — one check failed — and the note records it rather than rounding it up. The corpus's own transparency note states that it ships no model and no safety classifier, that host behaviour varies, and that human oversight remains necessary.

**The silences that matter.** The documentation publishes no measure of whether a declared boundary is in fact honoured by any host or model — the untrusted-content boundary and the human-approval prohibition are declarations whose effectiveness is nowhere reported, which is exactly the property step 5 of the workflow depends on. It publishes no result corpus at all: the evaluation lanes exist, the log directory is empty, and the repository is candid that a grader is not a result. It states no expected behaviour for the case where two boundary files disagree, or for which of a root `AGENTS.md` and a repository instructions file takes precedence when both are present — **which is why step 3 asks learners to record what they observe rather than what the documentation implies.** And it offers no comparative evidence that any of its practices outperforms an alternative, while its own prose uses benefit vocabulary that nothing in the tree supports; the note separates the two and this paper inherits that separation.
