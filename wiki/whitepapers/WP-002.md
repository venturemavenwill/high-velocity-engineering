## In plain terms

The role this programme prepares people for has a public definition, and it comes from the company that invented the title. The definition is one line: a product engineer builds one capability for many customers, and a forward-deployed engineer builds many capabilities for one customer.

That line does more work than it looks like it does. It explains why the role is broad rather than deep, why the same person moves between unrelated domains, and why a decision about what to generalise turns up every week rather than every year. It also explains a fact that surprises people: in the organisation that named the role, **the forward-deployed engineer does not sit in the engineering division.** The role is commercial as well as technical, with a reporting line and a negotiated boundary against product.

The same source has one more thing to say that a curriculum ought to find uncomfortable. Asked what skills the role requires, the company declines to answer, describing its practitioners as a population of individuals whose value lies in each being different. This programme specifies a competency set anyway. It has to. But it specifies it as its own design decision, and this paper is where that is admitted.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching the role's boundary before teaching any of its techniques. The argument is that a forward-deployed engineer is defined by an unusual accountability — for an outcome experienced by a customer, achieved largely through systems the engineer does not own — and that an engineer who has not separated what they decide from what they may only propose will either claim authority they do not have or surrender judgement they do. The session's artefact is that separation, made concrete and tested against a refusal. The paper records that its primary sources are recruiting artefacts, that no outcome of any kind is measured in them, and that the organisation which invented the role publicly refuses to specify its competencies.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

[S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) asks a learner to write a position on whether an engagement should proceed. The obvious objection, raised by learners themselves within a day, is that nobody asked them.

The objection is largely correct and the programme concedes it at S001. But conceding it leaves a hole: if the engineer does not decide, what exactly are they for? A learner without an answer will settle on one of two defaults. Either they treat themselves as an implementer whose judgement begins after the requirements arrive, which forfeits the only vantage point from which some disqualifying conditions are visible at all. Or they treat themselves as the responsible adult in the room, which produces an engineer who blocks work they have no standing to block and is removed from the engagement.

**The session's response is to make the boundary an artefact rather than an attitude**, because an attitude adjusts under pressure and a written list can be checked.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Ordinary professional experience**, plus [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md).

Learners arrive with a strong prior that accountability follows authorship: you built it, therefore you decide about it. In an organisation where the thing you built for one customer may or may not belong in a product used by many, that prior is not merely wrong but expensively wrong, and it is worth disturbing before any technique is taught on top of it.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is low. The concepts are few, and the difficulty is affective rather than cognitive: the work is giving something away and finding that the page still says something.

**3.2 Why the opening items precede instruction.** Attempting an answer before being taught improves later retention of the material and makes the distinctions noticeable when they arrive. The direction is well established; **no magnitude is asserted here or anywhere in this wiki.**

**3.3 Why the refusal is staged rather than described.** Item 3's characteristic error — assuming the generalisation decision is yours — is not corrected by being told. It is corrected by asking for something and being declined for a reason that is not about the quality of your work.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The artefact has two lists and the second is the one being assessed. A learner who produces a rich accountability list and an empty proposal list has not done the exercise; they have written a job description.

**Item 3 is the instrument.** It asks who decides that a customer-specific build becomes part of the product, and it is placed third so that items 1 and 2 have already established the learner's sense of ownership. The gap between a confident answer to item 1 and a vague answer to item 3 is the session's content.

The peer refusal in the work movement is scripted narrowly: the peer declines the generalisation and gives a roadmap reason. That reason is chosen because it is the one recorded in the primary source — larger contributions are validated against a roadmap owned by another division — and because it cannot be answered by working harder.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The undivided self.** The most likely outcome is a proposal list containing only things the learner did not want anyway. *Countermeasure:* the review movement asks a reader to identify any item the learner would in practice do regardless, which surfaces the padding without requiring the learner to admit it.

**Boundary as excuse.** A learner discovers that the boundary can be used to decline responsibility for anything difficult. *Countermeasure:* weak. The specification at [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md) checks the boundary against the decision the learner claims to make, which catches the case where the two contradict, and catches nothing else.

**Adopting the source's org chart as a law of nature.** A learner concludes that forward-deployed engineering must report into a commercial function. *Countermeasure:* the session teaches the boundary and names the divisional placement as one organisation's arrangement in one period. The distinction is stated in the paper and not enforced anywhere.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. One specification element at [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md): that the learner's claimed decision is consistent with the boundary they wrote here. **Neither the boundary's contents nor its generosity is marked** — only whether the two documents can both be true.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the role split is one company's organisational arrangement, and teaching it as a discipline mistakes an org chart for a body of knowledge.**

At its strongest: the distinction was published by a company to recruit engineers, the internal name for the role turns out to be a residue of an old team-naming scheme with no semantic content, the divisional structure described has since been restructured, and no evidence is offered anywhere that the split produces better outcomes than not splitting.

**This is substantially correct and is conceded.** The rebuttal is narrow: the split describes a real and recurring accountability problem — somebody has to be answerable to one customer while somebody else is answerable to many — and that problem exists whether or not any particular company solves it this way. The design's fix is to teach the boundary and not the labels. The declined fix is to teach the role generically, without reference to the source at all, which was rejected because it would leave learners unable to recognise the arrangement when they meet it.

**Objection 2: a programme cannot specify competencies its own primary source refuses to specify.**

At its strongest: the organisation that invented the role has more data about it than this programme will ever have, and it declines. Specifying anyway is a claim to knowledge nobody holds.

**Conceded without qualification.** The programme specifies a competency set because a programme must, and it is a design decision of this programme's own rather than a finding. The paper says so here, [S002](/wiki/seminars/S002.md) says so in its limits section, and no page in this stage claims otherwise.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A majority of learners will place the generalisation decision inside their own accountability list before the session and outside it afterwards. *Instrument:* item 3's written answer against the corresponding line of the finished boundary statement, coded by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Proposal lists will contain systematically less consequential items than accountability lists, as judged by whether the learner would perform the item anyway. *Instrument:* the review movement's binary judgement, collected per item rather than per learner.

**Prediction 3.** Learners who are declined for a roadmap reason will revise their boundary more than those declined for a quality reason. *Instrument:* a split of the peer script across the cohort, with revision extent recorded. **If this fails, the session's staging of the refusal is doing no work** and the movement should be replaced by a written case.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the role is defined by an accountability rather than by a technology, and that the accountability has an edge which has to be located before it is tested.

**What the evidence licenses.** A description of one organisation's stated model, from that organisation. Nothing about whether the model works, transfers, or is accurately described by its own recruiting material.

**The concession worth remembering.** The company that invented this role says it cannot say what the role requires. Every competency claim in this programme is downstream of a refusal to make one.
