# WP-077 — Poisoning, Extraction and Exhaustion

**Seminar day:** [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) · **Module:** [M16](/wiki/modules/M16.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md)
**Strands:** SG · IS · ME · **Complexity class:** 6
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Defender for Cloud AI threat protection and its published alert catalogue · Azure API Management token-limit policy · the Foundry AI Red Teaming Agent · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server · Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching three attacks that share nothing except a victim, and defends refusing the unifying account students will want. The design's target is that poisoning, extraction and exhaustion attack the function, the answer channel and the capacity respectively, that their defences sit in three different places at three different times, and that a system holding a gateway, a rate limit and a filter has covered one and a half of the three. The mechanism the day leans on hardest is a fourth observation record that is a correct match for one of the three attack signatures and contains no adversary at all — the module's thesis delivered on its second day, by the room, from evidence rather than from a slide. The practical turn adds a second: the poisoning trace is now produced rather than read, on a disposable copy, so the room watches its own evaluation set fail to move while the behaviour changes. The principal risk is that extraction still cannot be exercised on any system with real users, so the day is two-thirds engineering and one-third analysis, and §8 concedes what remains and that the specification cannot see the difference.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Students arrive holding one model of attack, supplied yesterday: somebody writes text into a channel the system reads. It is a good model, it is the day's own prerequisite, and **it generalises badly**, because it makes every attack an input problem and every defence an inspection. Two of today's three attacks defeat inspection by construction and the third defeats it by arithmetic.

Four difficulties operate, and they are not equally distributed across the three attacks.

**Poisoning is invisible to the evaluation apparatus the students have spent two quarters building.** The cohort's instinct, correct everywhere else, is that a behavioural change shows up in a measurement. [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) supplies the reason it does not: a model with spare capacity fits a small conditional behaviour at negligible aggregate cost, so the poisoned model scores identically on everything the student measures. The difficulty is not that this is hard to follow. It is that it invalidates the student's default evidence, and there is no replacement evidence available at inference time — the defence is upstream, in a lifecycle the student may not own.

**Extraction has no anomalous request.** Every technique the students hold — thresholds, filters, per-request checks — operates on one request. Extraction is a property of a *set*, and a set is not something the substrate's logging schema has an object for. The difficulty is a level-of-analysis error the design must produce rather than describe, which is what phase 3's T2 is for.

**Exhaustion is the one the cohort can handle and will therefore over-weight.** [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) to [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) gave them the whole apparatus. The risk here is the opposite of the other two: students will build a good exhaustion defence, feel competent, and generalise the feeling. Point 5's non-composition claim exists for that reason and step 4's explicit non-coverage clause is where it is enforced.

**And the fourth difficulty is the one the module exists for.** The three attacks produce records that are also produced by ordinary defects. A retry loop looks like extraction. A schema change upstream looks like poisoning. A client bug looks like exhaustion. **The distinguishing quantity is provenance or population, neither has operational value on the day it would have to be implemented, and therefore neither is implemented.** A day that taught the three attacks without this would produce students who classify confidently on evidence that does not support classification — which is worse than the ignorance it replaced, because it is actionable.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

The day carries **three parallel structures rather than one interacting one**, which is a different load profile from any other day in the module and is why the phase 4 points are grouped as they are.

Within each attack, three elements interact: the quantity attacked, the time at which the defence must exist, and the evidence that would show it. Across attacks, they do not interact at all — knowing the poisoning triple tells you nothing about the extraction triple. **The risk is therefore not overload from interactivity but interference from similarity**, which is a different management problem: the three will blur, and the blurred version is *there are three attacks and you should defend against them*.

Management:

- **The three are separated by quantity rather than by name**, so that the retrieval cue is *function / channel / capacity* and not a list of words. The quantity is what the artefact is organised by at step 1.
- **Each attack has exactly one phase 3 record and one phase 4 point**, so the mapping is one-to-one and the room can hold it.
- **Step 5's arithmetic is confined to exhaustion**, because it is the only one of the three where the student holds a real number, and spreading a quantitative demand across all three would produce three fabricated numbers.
- **Step 2's estimation form is supplied and worked**, since the systematic failure — confident figures with no source — is exactly what specification rule 2 exists to catch, and the day should catch it in the room rather than in the artefact.
- **T4 is introduced last**, after the three are stable, because a payload that arrives before the categories exist destroys the categories instead of bounding them.

Expertise reversal applies to the exhaustion material for the whole cohort, not for a subset: they have done this for a quarter. Point 4 is therefore delivered as a re-expression rather than as instruction, and the guidance is withdrawn entirely at step 5, which is arithmetic they can already do.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Classifying from symptom records with attribution withheld*, phase 1.
- *A pretest item with a smuggled premise nobody is expected to challenge*, item 1, whose finding is the room's non-objection rather than its answers.
- *Committing a classification before attribution*, phase 3.
- *A record that matches a category correctly and belongs to none*, T4.
- *Watching your own evaluation set fail to move after you poison your own index*, phase 3's T1 execution.
- *Being asked what evidence would decide, rather than what evidence would confirm*, task 1.
- *Naming what your own control does not cover, in writing, before a partner uses it against you*, step 4 into phase 7.
- *Being required to observe a control acting rather than to configure it*, step 4.
- *Writing "estimated from no data" as a provenance*, step 2.
- *Writing "this rests on a control whose miss rate I cannot state"*, step 3.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Executing an extraction campaign.* Not available on any system with real users, because the campaign is the attack. **Demonstrated rather than operated at phase 3, and not assessed.** §8 objection 1 examines what remains lost.
- *Defensive technique catalogues.* Canary tokens, watermarking, differential-privacy budgets, membership-inference detectors and their equivalents are SG302's, are perishable per the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), and are excluded so that the specification checks a boundary rather than a technique. **Point 9's treatment of a vendor's alert catalogue is bounded by the same exclusion** and is written so that nothing in it is checkable.
- *Adversarial robustness as an optimisation problem.* Training-time robustness belongs to IS302 and to the literature the programme does not verify; the day treats the model as fixed.
- *Attacks on the humans.* Named in point 1 as an omission with no home in this programme, which is an honest statement of a gap rather than a deferral, because a deferral requires a day that can carry it and there is none.
- *A general ranking of the three attacks by severity.* Refused explicitly at point 8, because the ranking is a property of a system and a general ranking is the kind of portable non-fact a technique catalogue produces. **A vendor's severity assignments are shown at point 9 as an instance of somebody else having made that ranking for you**, and they are not taught as correct.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The three-quantity decomposition is **safe as a way of deciding where and when a defence for a learned component you operate must sit, and as a way of deciding which of your own commitments an adversary will aim at.** It is **unsafe in three named conditions.** It is unsafe as a threat model for a deployment, because it omits evasion at inference, supply-chain compromise below the model, and attacks on the people — and a student who produces this artefact and calls it a security review has excluded the class that causes most real incidents, which is the finding [WP-044](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-044.md) §8 already recorded once. It is unsafe where the student does not own the data lifecycle, because point 2's entire defence is upstream and the artefact will state a control the student cannot implement. And it is unsafe where the component is multi-tenant, because extraction's population defence is per-principal and the frame has no expression for a principal who is themselves an aggregate. **In each case the day's artefact will pass its own specification and describe a system that does not exist**, and step 1's explicit-absence clause is the only element that catches any of it.

**The platform simplification carries its own scope statement.** Point 9 teaches a vendor's alert catalogue as an instance of the three quantities. That correspondence is **safe as a way of knowing what your detection stack can observe — prompts, requests, responses, identities, volumes — and therefore which of the three quantities it can reach at all.** It is **unsafe in two directions and both are consequential.** It is unsafe as coverage: a catalogue is an enumeration of what one product observes, not of what can be done to you, and a student who maps their exposure statement onto it has adopted somebody else's omissions. And it is unsafe as evidence of detection: **no detection rate or false-positive rate is published for any alert in it, for the content filters underneath it, or for the posture assessments beside it**, so the absence of an alert licenses nothing. That second condition is not left in this paper — it is point 9's fourth bullet, it is a required field at step 3, and **it is a fail condition in the specification**, which is the strong form's binding-surface widening applied where the design can afford it.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **T4**, and specifically on the moment a group that has correctly classified three records is told that the fourth is a client bug.

The mechanism is not embarrassment; the misclassification is the correct inference from the evidence supplied, and the design says so in the same breath. What produces the engagement is task 2: having discovered that the record could not have decided, each group is required to say, about their own component, whether it would decide tomorrow. **That converts a finding about a supplied case into a finding about the student's own system, in the same phase, with no instruction in between** — and it is the reason the two tasks are ordered as they are.

The second mechanism is step 5's arithmetic. It is the only place in the module where an adversarial claim becomes a number the student cannot argue with, and the number is built from their own cost model and their own commitment. A student who discovers that a stated adversary spend reaches their budget before it reaches their capacity has learned something about a decision they made in week 64.

Autonomy is supported by step 4's choice of which quantity to defend, and the choice is real because the day explicitly refuses to rank the three. Competence is supported by exhaustion, where the cohort is already good, and the day places that point fourth rather than first so that the competence arrives after two attacks that defeat their apparatus. Relatedness is supplied by the accountable staff member at step 7, who receives the statement and is not obliged to accept it.

The affective risk is **the paranoid turn**, and it is more likely here than at [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) because the day supplies three attack names and a room five days into the module will start seeing them. The countermeasures are structural: T4 is not an attack; the error clinic's case is a six-day misattribution that deployed a control which is still running; and the error-clinic prompt is explicitly two-directional, because a room asked for a time they were fooled by an adversary will supply only that direction and the more expensive error in professional practice runs the other way.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Four symptom records, five sealed items. Item 1's premise is the payload; item 2 is administered here so that step 3 has a pre-instruction reading to be compared against; item 3 joins the module to [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) at the point where students still believe their detection design covers the case; item 4 produces a prediction that step 2's own figures will contradict; **item 5 puts item 1's smuggled premise to the agent**, so the day can report whether either respondent refused it.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals, two of them from four and two quarters back. The bridge produces the poisoning mechanism from [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) plus [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) rather than defining it, and the room answers.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four observation records at the resolution the substrate actually stores, **selected against [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md)'s measured write-path blind spot**, which is this day's rule-6 discharge. T4 is the payload. **T1 and T3 are then executed on the disposable copy and T2 is demonstrated rather than operated and is not assessed.** Task 1 asks what would *decide* rather than what would *confirm*; task 2 asks whether the student's own system records it.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Nine points, three of them one per attack. Point 1 carries the enumeration scope statement in strong form; point 5 carries the non-composition scope statement; point 6 answers item 3 and extends [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 7; **point 7 is the payload and is generated at phase 3 rather than asserted here**, which is the arrangement the pending rule proposed at [WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 would require. **That rule is pending and not in force**; the day complies voluntarily, as [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) did. **Point 9 is the platform-specific content**, is marked perishable, names a default and a documented silence, carries its own scope statement, and is placed last because its third bullet depends on point 6 having been believed.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps producing an exposure statement for a named receiver **and one shipped control observed acting**. Step 2 worked; **step 3 unsupported**. Step 5 is arithmetic against the student's own billed model. Step 7 uses an external observer per rule 1 in the narrate-not-approve form, contingent under rule 5 as amended. **The artefact carries the prompt, intervention and frontier records.**

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A six-day misattribution with a control still deployed. No unreasonable judgement at any step, and a two-directional student prompt. **Then the agent case: three correct controls that all count requests.**

**Phase 7 (30 min).** The partner attacks using the student's own declared non-coverage, then reads the student's own recording gaps back against the constructed attack.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 1's framing read back first and the non-objection rate reported, **with item 5 beside it**; T4's misclassification rate and the post-task revision count; **the T1 evaluation-score movement across groups**; item 3 against point 6; item 4 against the cohort's own cost figures **with the proportion whose provenance was "estimated from no data"**; the pooled recording gaps **with the count resting on an unpublished-accuracy control stated separately**; the exhaustion positions as a distribution.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has worked on abuse or fraud will classify all four records quickly and will classify T4 as an attack faster than anyone, because their priors are trained on populations where it usually is. Predicted failure: **confident attribution with no discriminating evidence**, which is the exact behaviour the day is built to correct and which will be presented fluently. Countermeasure: task 1 asks what would *decide*, not what supports the classification, and phase 7's partner reads the recording gaps back. The assessors are not involved on this day, but the specification checks the recording-gap list rather than the classification, so fluency has nothing to purchase.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is at step 2: a student with no basis for a cost estimate will either fabricate a number or freeze. Countermeasures: the form is supplied and worked with the room; **"estimated from no data" is an explicitly permitted and explicitly checked provenance value**; and the specification checks that the field carries provenance and confidence rather than checking the figure. A student whose three costs are all unsourced estimates, honestly labelled, passes — and the cohort proportion is reported at phase 8, which makes the honesty visible as a group property rather than as an individual admission.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** If a group identifies T4 as a non-attack before the attribution is supplied, the payload has failed for that group. This is plausible where a group contains a student who worked on the substrate's client. Fallback: the group is given the reserved fifth record — **a poisoning trace whose write happened eleven months before the period the record covers, so that the record contains the exploitation and not the cause** — and is asked what evidence would establish the connection and whether the substrate retains it that long. It does not, and the retention window is the finding. The reserve is not used generally because it requires a second temporal reasoning step that phase 3 does not have room for.

**Affective failure.** The paranoid turn, described at §3.3, and one specific to this day: **the student who concludes their component is indefensible.** Poisoning's defence is upstream and may not be theirs; extraction's defence requires a population view their logging does not support; exhaustion's defence costs money they do not control. A student can correctly reason to *nothing here is available to me*.

The countermeasure is step 6 and it is deliberate. **Naming what you are not defending, with its expected cost and an owner, is the day's answer to that conclusion** — it is [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 8's form, it is a real professional act, and it converts an unavailable defence into a stated acceptance with a person attached. The instructor states once that a component with one control and five honest acceptances is in better condition than one with six controls and no acceptance list, and that the second is what the room will see in industry.

## 6. Retention architecture

**Retrieves from** [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) at four quarters, [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) at two, [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) at one week. The [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) retrieval is the longest gap in the module and is scaffolded at phase 2 per the pattern's constraint on retrievals older than two quarters. It is also the one whose failure is most consequential, which §2 branches for.

**Retrieved by** [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) at one week (where the recording gaps become an alerting requirement and where point 7's thresholds-as-budgets observation is operationalised), [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) at three weeks (where the attack-or-accident judgement is the assessed activity), [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md) at two quarters (where capacity, quota and budget are negotiated with a counterparty and the exhaustion position is one of the constraints on the table) and [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) at two quarters (the safety case, which the [Seminar Day Index](/wiki/program/08-Seminar-Day-Index.md) records as retrieving S076–S079 as a block).

**[S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) at three weeks is the consequential retrieval** and it is instrumented: the T4 misclassification rate is the instrument, and [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md)'s incident is selected so that the attack-or-accident question is open at the start.

Blocked rather than interleaved practice is used once and only once in this module, at the between-day day-3 exercise: **one supplied observation record, classified against the three quantities, in five minutes, every third day for a week.** The classification is a constituent skill that needs to become automatic before [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), where it must run under assessment pressure alongside six other demands, and the pattern permits blocked practice for a constituent skill that genuinely requires automaticity. It is the only blocked element in M16.

Retained content: three quantities, three defence locations, three times, and one sentence — *what separates an attack from an accident is a recording decision made before either happened.*

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 1, week 3.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment judgement.** One specification item in SG302 with IS302.

Per specification rule 1 the checked elements are enumerated on the day and end with *nothing else is assessed*. Five hard requirements: a control without its stated non-coverage fails; an empty recording-gap list fails; **a recording-gap entry crediting a platform detection without stating that its accuracy is unpublished fails**; a cost figure without provenance and confidence in the same field fails; an intervention record with no entries fails unless the artefact shows the design could have caught an intervention-worthy error.

**The unpublished-accuracy fail condition is the strong form's binding-surface widening applied here rather than deferred.** The [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) records that a scope statement can function as a licence to disregard the omission it names, and widens the rule so that a day carries an artefact element under which a student applying a simplification outside its safe range would fail. **The widening applies from [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md)'s review forward and this day is not obliged by it.** It is applied anyway, because the unsafe condition here is a single sentence, the fail condition is one clause, and a security artefact that credits an undocumented detection is the exact shape of overclaim the module exists to prevent. This is recorded as a voluntary early application rather than as compliance.

**The empty-list rule is a rule-5 reuse and is recorded as one.** [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) established that an empty not-detected list fails, on the ground that a student claiming to detect everything has either not enumerated or not decided. Step 3's list is the same object in a different register — evidence rather than signals — and instrumentation rule 5 requires the remedy to be applied rather than rediscovered. Under rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 the reuse is **contingent** on [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s reported outcome, and a reported failure there retracts it here.

**The cross-course join is a checked element**, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md). Point 1 defers supply-chain compromise to SG302; step 1's artefact requires the student to name the material used.

**Rule-6 discharge, as required by [WP-076](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-076.md) §7.** [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) named this day's phase 3 as the response occasion for its item 1 exposure-estimate error, specifying that the four traces be selected against whichever write-path class the cohort most systematically failed to mark. **The element exists and it is a case-selection function rather than an acknowledgement: the poisoning and extraction traces are routed through the missed path class, so the printed records differ depending on the [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) result.** It is not a mention; it determines which four records the room receives. The obligation is discharged.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments, both responding inside this module.

- **The T4 misclassification rate**, with the post-task revision count and the item 1 framing-objection rate, responds at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) phase 3 item 3, whose incident is selected so that the attack-or-accident question is genuinely open and whose artefact requires the discriminating evidence for whichever answer is given, including *undecided*. The cohort transition is reported at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) phase 5.
- **The pooled step 3 recording gaps** respond at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) phase 5 step 5, where each alerting design must name one recording change that would make the attack-versus-accident distinction possible, or record that none would.

**[WP-079](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-079.md) §7 and [WP-080](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-080.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

Programme instrumentation: the item 1 framing non-objection rate; T4's misclassification and revision rates; item 3 against point 6, per attack; the proportion of step 2 cost figures whose recorded provenance is *estimated from no data*; and the distribution of which commitment the exhaustion arithmetic reaches first.

ABET and CS2023 contribution: PO1 and PO4. Dispositions: **integrity**, at step 2, where the honest provenance is uncomfortable and is checked rather than scored; **humility**, at step 3 and step 6, where the honest answers are *nothing in my system would decide this* and *nobody owns this*.

**What this day does not license anyone to conclude.** It does not license a claim that a student can detect any of these three attacks, and it does not license a claim about the prevalence or severity of any of them in general. It licenses a claim that the student can say which quantity of their own component is under attack in a given scenario, where and when a defence for it would have to sit, what evidence would decide between an attack and an accident, and what they are accepting instead. **Four statements about reasoning, none of them a security property of a running system.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: two thirds of the day is now engineering and the remaining third is the part that matters most.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the practical turn reshapes rather than removes. **Exhaustion can be reasoned about quantitatively against a real cost model and is now also executed at phase 3 and shipped against at step 4. Poisoning is now produced on a disposable copy**, so the room watches an evaluation set stay flat while behaviour changes, which was previously an assertion. **Extraction remains unexecutable.** A student cannot run an extraction campaign, because the campaign is the attack, and no disposable copy fixes that: the interesting property is the joint coverage of a query population, and a population the student generates against their own copy is a population they designed. So for one of the three attacks the student writes a paragraph about a mechanism they have not seen operate, and the specification checks that the paragraph has the required fields.

**That is still the failure mode this programme was built to correct**, in one third of the day rather than two. [WP-070](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §8 and the [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) withdrawal commitment both state the principle: the alternative to consequence is not a smaller claim but a different one, and describing analysis as engineering is exactly the substitution the design refuses elsewhere.

The rebuttal has three parts and the third is new. First, **the day's assessed object is deliberately the analysis and not the defence**: the checked elements are the quantities, the costs with provenance, the recording gaps, the non-coverage and the acceptance list, and none of them claims a control works. Second, **the extraction material is now labelled**: phase 3 states that T2 is demonstrated rather than operated and the specification does not assess it, which is the [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) rule applied to a case inside a day rather than to a whole day. Third, **the cheap fix [WP-077](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-077.md) previously declined has been made.** That fix was to demonstrate poisoning on the disposable copy; it was declined on the ground that it would occupy the phase 5 slot step 3 holds. **Moving it into phase 3 costs eight minutes and takes nothing from phase 5, and the earlier decline was therefore a scheduling answer to a validity objection that a different scheduling answer dissolves.** That is recorded as an error in the earlier reasoning rather than as a vindication of it.

**What is conceded is what remains.** Extraction is the attack in this module with the widest gap between what the student can say and what the student has seen, and there is no fix available that does not construct the evidence. **The design has narrowed the objection by two thirds and cannot close it**, and it declines the obvious remaining move — generating a synthetic extraction population against the copy — because the population's coverage would be the designer's choice, which is the same reason [WP-078](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-078.md) §8 declines synthetic principals.

**Objection 2: the cost estimates are unverifiable, the day requires them anyway, and it assesses their form.**

At its strongest: step 2 requires, for each of three attacks, what the attack would cost the adversary. **No student in the room can source that number.** The provenance for most entries will be *estimated from no data*, which the design permits and even reports as a cohort proportion. So the day trains the *form* of a quantified threat estimate — a figure with a provenance and a confidence, in one field — over a quantity that has no evidence behind it.

That is precisely the failure [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) to [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md) exist to prevent. **A number with a well-formed qualification attached is more dangerous than no number**, because the qualification licenses it to travel: specification rule 2 was written on the finding that a limitation detached from its figure gets separated from it, and the inverse hazard — a limitation firmly attached to a figure that should not exist — is not covered by the rule and is what this step produces. Point 8 then uses these figures to decide what to defend first.

The rebuttal is that the alternative is worse in a specific way. Adversary cost is the quantity that decides defence priority in real practice, it is always estimated, and a graduate who has never written one will write one anyway on their first day and will write it without a provenance. The day's position is that the honest failure mode — an explicitly unsourced estimate, labelled as such, with the cohort's proportion reported publicly — is a better training target than either a fabricated confident number or no number at all.

**The cheap fix was to require an ordinal ranking with its basis, and no figure at all.** It would remove the problem entirely: a ranking cannot travel as a fact, cannot be quoted, and cannot be multiplied by a probability. **It is declined**, and the reason is that an ordinal ranking cannot be set against step 5's arithmetic, which is the one place in the module where an adversarial quantity meets a number the student was billed for — and the comparison between an unsourced adversary cost and a sourced substrate cost, side by side in one artefact, is the day's clearest demonstration that the two halves of a threat calculation are not equally knowable.

**This objection is not fully answered.** The design has chosen to train a form over an unknowable quantity, has argued that the alternative is worse, and has declined the fix that would remove the hazard because the hazard is also the lesson. It cannot show that students will retain the lesson rather than the form, and §9 prediction 3 is where that would first be visible.

**Objection 3: point 7 is the module's thesis and the day gives it one phase 3 case and one phase 4 point.**

At its strongest: the claim that attack and accident produce the same record, and that what separates them is a recording decision, is the reason [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) and [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) are in a security module. It carries the module. **It is supported by exactly one contrasting case, T4, and one group in four may reach it early and lose the effect entirely.**

The rebuttal is that T4 is not the claim's only support — task 1 and task 2 apply it to all four records and to the student's own system, step 3 makes it an assessed artefact element, the error clinic supplies a second instance from the substrate's own history, and the instrument responds at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) on a real incident. That is four occasions inside the day and one outside it, which is more support than any other single claim in the module has.

It is nonetheless true that the *surprise* rests on one case, and §5's reserve record is a fallback for a group rather than for the cohort. If the cohort as a whole classifies T4 correctly, the day has a good result and a weak phase 3, and the design would rather have the good result.

**Objection 4: the day teaches an attack whose name comes from a vendor, and treats the vendor's silence as content.**

At its strongest: point 9 is eight minutes on one product's alert catalogue, and the day's most memorable term — *wallet attack* — is a name that exists because a vendor coined it for a detection they sell. The day then makes a virtue of the catalogue publishing no detection rate, and converts that into a specification requirement. **A sceptical reader is entitled to see the whole construction as a programme finding pedagogical significance in a marketing document.** Worse, it is self-serving: the design has a standing prohibition on asserting vendor detection rates, and point 9 turns compliance with the programme's own rule into a teaching moment, which is not the same as the rule having been useful.

The rebuttal has two parts. First, **the term is not the content.** What is durable is that usage-based billing creates an attack whose objective is your money rather than your availability, which follows from the pricing model and not from any product, and which the students met at [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) as a commitment before it had a name. Point 4 states the mechanism and point 9 supplies the observation that somebody built a detector for it — in that order, and the perishability register puts the name in the perishable column. Second, **the silence is not treated as significant because it is a silence; it is treated as significant because it has an operational consequence a student will otherwise get wrong.** An engineer who cannot say what an alert's absence licenses will write an incident report next week concluding that no attack occurred because nothing fired. That is a real error, [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) is seven days away, and the requirement is one field.

**One part of the objection is conceded and it is the part about the design's own comfort.** The programme's prohibition on asserting vendor detection rates was written as a constraint on what may be claimed. Point 9 promotes it to a positive finding — *this silence is load-bearing content* — and the promotion has no external warrant whatsoever. **It is this design's judgement that a documented absence is more instructive than a published figure would have been, and that judgement is convenient**, because the design cannot obtain the figure. **The cheap fix is to state the alternative and it is stated: if a detection rate were published, the day would teach it, would require students to check it against their own traffic, and would be a better day for it.** That sentence is now in point 9's neighbourhood in the day itself, and its absence would have left the design looking like it preferred the silence.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Fewer than one student in ten will object to phase 1 item 1's premise that each incident had an adversary. *Instrument:* item 1 responses coded for any challenge to the framing, including hedged ones. **This is the prediction the design most expects to be right and least wants to be**, and a high objection rate would mean the cohort arrives with the discrimination the module is built to install, which would make phase 3's payload redundant and would be reported as such.

**Prediction 2.** At item 3, students will report that their [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) detection design would have caught T1 and T2 at a rate substantially above the true rate, and their T3 answers will be roughly accurate. *Instrument:* item 3 answers per record against the components' actual signal coverage, **reported per attack rather than in aggregate**. The per-attack split is the claim; an overall optimism figure is consistent with general overconfidence and would not establish that the invisibility is specific to the function and the answer channel.

**Prediction 3.** More than half of step 2's cost figures will carry *estimated from no data* or an equivalent, and at phase 7 more partners will attack the control than will interrogate the cost estimate. *Instrument:* provenance values coded, and phase 7 outcomes coded for which of the two the partner engaged. **The second half is the test of objection 2**: if students treat their own unsourced figures as settled once the field is filled in, the day has trained the form and not the scepticism, and the ordinal-ranking fix should be reconsidered.

**Prediction 4.** Step 5's exhaustion arithmetic will reach budget before capacity or quota for a clear majority of components, and the students whose commitment shape was chosen at [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) against a demand forecast will be over-represented in that majority. *Instrument:* step 5 results coded by which constraint is reached first, joined to each student's [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) commitment shape. **If the constraint reached first is unrelated to the commitment shape, point 4's central claim — that the attack lands on whichever commitment is weakest, and that this is a property of your commitments rather than your architecture — is wrong**, and the day is teaching a dependency that does not hold.

**Prediction 5.** At phase 3's T1 execution, the evaluation-score movement will be indistinguishable from zero for every group, and at step 3 the proportion of recording-gap entries resting on an unpublished-accuracy control will exceed the proportion resting on a signal the student's own component emits. *Instrument:* the T1 score deltas per group, and step 3 entries coded for whether the named distinguisher is the student's own instrumentation or a platform detection. **The second half is the test of whether point 9 landed.** If students overwhelmingly cite platform detections as their distinguishers, the day has taught them where the products are rather than what their own systems record, which is the opposite of step 3's purpose and would require point 9 to move after step 3 rather than before it.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That three attacks sharing nothing but a victim must be held separately — they attack the function, the answer channel and the capacity — and that what separates an attack from an accident is a recording decision somebody made before either happened.

**What the evidence licenses.** The mechanisms are **class 2**: direction and mechanism only, with no count of queries, no count of poisoned examples and no severity ranking. Point 9's alert catalogue is **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026, carrying severities with no stated basis and **no detection rate anywhere**. **The three-quantity decomposition, the non-composition claim, and the claim that provenance and population separate an attack from an accident are class 3** — this design's own reasoning, untested. You may say which quantity is under attack and where a defence must sit; not that the component is defended.

**What to do with it.**

- Sort your exposure statement by the five classes on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). **A control is not evidence**: a configured filter is a configured control, and no alert having fired is not an observed result about an adversary.
- Write *estimated from no data* where it is true. It is permitted and it is checked; a confident figure is neither.
- Run the exhaustion arithmetic against your own billed model. Whichever commitment it reaches first is a decision you made earlier.
- Never credit a detection whose accuracy nobody has published without saying so.

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes that it trains the *form* of a quantified threat estimate over a quantity nobody in the room can source, that a well-qualified figure travels further and is therefore the more dangerous, and that it declined the ordinal ranking which would remove the hazard because the hazard is also the lesson. **It cannot show that you will keep the lesson rather than the form.** Hold it to that the next time you write down what an attack would cost an adversary.
