> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Withdrawal list** | The things the delivery team is capable of doing and will not do, because doing them removes the occasion for somebody else to learn |
> | **Reclaim rule** | A condition, written in advance, under which work handed to the recipient is taken back, with a stated recipient of that news and a stated record |
> | **Interleaving** | Placing an activity inside an existing delivery cadence rather than running it as a separate event |
> | **Generation phase** | The part of a two-phase teaching design in which the learner attempts a problem they have not been taught. Scaffolding it removes the mechanism |
> | **Error-management framing** | Brief instructions telling a learner to expect errors and treat them as information, delivered without accompanying assistance |
> | **Task-generated feedback** | What the work itself tells the learner when they get it wrong. Clear where something goes red and says why; unclear where the output is merely different |
> | **Vicarious experience** | Efficacy information obtained by watching somebody else perform, weighted by how similar the observer judges them to be |
> | **Attribution** | What a person names as the cause of their own failure — effort, acquirable skill, the situation, or ability. Observable in a debrief |

## In plain terms

Capability has to be built somewhere, and there are only two places: inside the delivery, or after it.

After it is where everybody puts it, and after it is what gets cut. This is not a failure of intent. Work scheduled after the delivery competes with nothing until the moment the date comes under pressure, at which point it competes with the date and loses, and removing it costs nothing that month. **A plan that puts capability transfer at the end has scheduled its own deletion.**

Inside the delivery is more expensive and it is expensive immediately, which is why it does not happen by default. The customer's engineer is slower. The work they lead takes longer and comes back worse the first time. Every hour of it is visible to a sponsor watching a fixed date.

**This paper does not pretend the cost is small or recoverable, and the best-warranted source behind the session says so in its own words.** The training literature that supports the design also predicts that performance *during* the training will be flat or depressed, with the benefit appearing on transfer afterwards. **That is the session's price and it is written into the mechanism**, not into its execution.

So the session's real artefact is not a teaching plan. It is a **withdrawal list** — the things the delivery team could do quickly and will not — and a **reclaim rule**, written in advance, stating the condition under which the work comes back. A reclaim that follows a written rule is a decision. A reclaim that does not is the plan ending quietly, and nobody records the moment it happened.

## Abstract

This paper defends binding capability transfer to real backlog items inside the delivery rather than to an enablement period after it, and defends stating its cost rather than minimising it.

**The first argument is about incentives rather than about learning.** Work scheduled after delivery is unprotected, its removal is invisible and free, and a plan that schedules it there has scheduled its own deletion. That is a claim about what organisations do, and it is this design's own.

**The second argument is about mechanism, and it is where the paper's evidence is strongest and least comfortable.** The design is generation before instruction under real stakes, and two independent literatures converge on both its benefit and its price. The Productive Failure condition that the generation phase must not be scaffolded is directly at odds with taking the keyboard: an experiment manipulating guidance during problem solving found that prompts which reduced the failure rate reduced learning. The error-management synthesis reaches the same ordering from workplace training and adds the cost explicitly, predicting no advantage on performance measured during training and an advantage on transfer afterwards. **Both are quoted rather than paraphrased in §3, because a design that hides its own price will be adopted and then abandoned.**

**The third argument is a caution against overclaiming.** The practice literature's own corrective finds that accumulated practice explains least in domains with unstable conditions, ambiguous feedback and contested performance criteria — which describes engineering delivery inside somebody else's organisation on every axis. The available claim is therefore that pairing creates the conditions under which capability could develop, and not that it delivers capability.

The platform workflow constrains the tooling so that the customer's engineer remains the author of record rather than the audience, and makes two things observable in one hour: the learner's own intervention count, and the difference between an engineer who has watched and an engineer who has worked.

The paper concedes that the interleaving prohibition it extends is one its source contradicts in its own case material; that nothing behind this programme measures whether capability built during delivery transfers better than capability delivered afterwards; that the strongest objection to the session — that it trades delivery quality the customer is paying for against a benefit nobody has demonstrated — is answered only partially; and that the pedagogical warrant comes from laboratory and school populations and from studies that never measured on-the-job performance.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A delivery team under a fixed date will not hand real work to somebody slower unless it has decided in advance to, in specific terms, against specific items.

Everything about the moment argues the other way, and the arguments are good ones. The item is urgent. The customer's engineer is unavailable this week. Doing it yourself takes an hour and supervising takes three. The consequence of the delay is visible to the sponsor and the consequence of not transferring the capability is visible to nobody until after everybody has gone.

**The team is not choosing convenience over transfer. It is choosing a cost it can see over a cost it cannot**, which is a different failure and is not fixed by being told that transfer matters.

There is a second problem, and the tooling has sharpened it. An agent that will produce a complete change from a one-line request converts the customer's engineer from a driver into a reviewer of somebody else's work in a single response — and does so while everybody feels productive. **The pairing session can now produce a large diff, a satisfied room and no practice at all**, which is a failure mode that did not exist when the constraint was how fast a human could type.

The session's job is to move the allocation decision earlier, to specific named items; to force the reclaim into the open by requiring its rule to exist before its occasion does; and to constrain the tooling so it cannot take the keyboard either.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session assumes the capability statement from [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md), a live backlog with remaining work, and the delivery-pressure material from Stage 5 — in particular the session on review under delivery pressure, since a customer's engineer acting as reviewer of record is a review that arrives when the schedule is worst.

It also assumes [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md) and [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md), where agent-assisted work and its failure modes were established. A learner who has not held the jagged-capability material will choose a pairing task whose failures are fluent rather than red, which §3.4 identifies as the condition under which this design has no support.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why interleaving.** The practice corpus behind this programme states a prohibition repeatedly: design and enablement activity is interleaved into the existing engineering cadence and is never run as a separate workshop or a pause in delivery ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). This session extends the prohibition to capability transfer, which the corpus does not do. **The extension is this design's, and it inherits a defect from its source**, because the corpus contradicts the prohibition in its own case material, whose headline achievements include compressed multi-day workshops. Nothing in the corpus reconciles the two. **Access-controlled material, paraphrased and never quoted; it carries no citation of any kind across roughly ninety documents.**

**3.2 The design is generation before instruction, and its price is stated in the mechanism.**

This is the section a reader should not skip, because it contains the only honest cost estimate the session has.

The teaching design is two-phase and order-dependent: the learner attempts work targeting something they have not been taught, fails, and is then taught ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Its author's framing is proactive: "if failure is so powerful for learning, then we should not wait for it to happen. We should intentionally design for it."

**The binding condition is that the generation phase must not be scaffolded**, and the author tested it directly. An experiment manipulating guidance during the problem-solving phase found that prompts and hints which reduced the failure rate *reduced learning*: "Encouraging or promoting failure facilitates learning. **Reducing failure hurts learning.**"

**Taking the keyboard is scaffolding the generation phase.** That sentence is the whole of the session's practical instruction, and it converts a matter of politeness into a design violation.

The same source reports a comparison that bears directly on the pairing arrangement: generating one's own failed solutions placed ahead of studying somebody else's failed solutions, and both ahead of direct instruction, on deep understanding and transfer — with all three equal on procedural knowledge. **Watching an expert fail is better than watching one succeed; doing it oneself is better than either.** That ordering is the argument for the withdrawal list rather than for a better demonstration.

**Its limits:** a trade book; school, university and laboratory evidence; no adult-professional study in the reference list; and the author's own concession that failure is sufficient but not necessary for the awareness the mechanism depends on. Applying it to a customer's engineers during a live delivery is an extrapolation, and it is the same extrapolation this programme concedes wherever it uses the pattern.

**3.3 The independent convergence, and the sentence that prices the fortnight.**

Error-management training — minimal guidance plus explicit encouragement to make errors and learn from them — was synthesised across two dozen studies, and reaches the same ordering conclusion from a workplace-training literature rather than a school one ([research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md)). **Two independent traditions agreeing on ordering is a stronger warrant than either alone**, and it is why this programme puts generation first everywhere.

The synthesis's first hypothesis distinguishes performance measured *during* training from performance on a separate task afterwards, and predicts an effect on the second and not the first. It held: studies using a within-training criterion produced no significant advantage, studies using a post-training transfer criterion did, and the moderator itself was significant. The authors' own statement of the mechanism carries the clause this paper refuses to bury: in error-management training, **"immediate performance within the training phase may not differ or may even be depressed"**.

**That is the delivery cost, written into the mechanism rather than into its execution.** A team pairing during delivery should expect the fortnight in which it happens to be slower, and should say so before the fortnight rather than after.

The synthesis also specifies the intervention precisely, and names the part that is universally dropped. The framing statements it quotes — *"The more errors you make, the more you learn!"* — are delivered by a trainer who repeats them and reminds participants to reflect, **"but provides no further assistance when an error occurs"**. The framing is delivered; the help is not. **A session that supplied the encouragement and then supplied the help has run the pleasant half of the intervention and none of the working half.**

**Two structural limits govern every use of this source and are stated wherever it appears.** Every study in the pool compared error-management training against *another* training method rather than against no training, so all effects are relative — never "better than nothing". And **no study in the pool measured on-the-job performance**; every outcome is a post-training task in a laboratory, so the transfer established is transfer to a novel task rather than to a job. A third limit is methodological: the pooled homogeneity test was not significant, so the moderators are theory-driven partitions of a pool that did not statistically require partitioning. The authors declare this openly and proceed on stated grounds.

**3.4 The moderator that decides whether the design applies to a given task.**

Because the method withholds guidance, the synthesis argues that **"the task itself needs to provide clearly interpretable and informative feedback"** for it to work — and that on tasks whose feedback cannot be readily interpreted, it may be *worse* than instruction supplying external guidance. The reported contrast went in the predicted direction, with clear-feedback studies showing the effect and unclear-feedback studies not reaching significance.

**The authors are explicit that this hypothesis received only limited support**, that the moderator test itself did not reach conventional significance, and that "it cannot be concluded that EMT was effective only when task-generated feedback was clear". They attribute the inconclusiveness partly to feedback clarity helping the comparison methods too, and partly to low interrater reliability in their own coding of it.

**This paper therefore treats it as a design caution rather than a rule, and it is the caution with the sharpest platform consequence.** A pipeline that fails red is a good teaching surface. **An agent that produces a plausible wrong answer in the same confident register as a correct one is the worst available one**, because the learner receives no signal at all — which is the jagged-capability property established at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) arriving as a task-selection constraint.

**3.5 The counterweight: practice is not sufficient, and this module must not overclaim.**

The corrective is held in this repository precisely so that a capability module does not slide into asserting that hours produce competence ([research/04-professional-formation/macnamara-deliberate-practice-limits.md](/research/04-professional-formation/macnamara-deliberate-practice-limits.md)). The meta-analysis tested the strong claim — that individual differences in performance largely reflect individual differences in the amount of deliberate practice — and found it explains far less than the popular account holds, with the explanatory share varying enormously across domains.

**The pattern is the usable content and the ordering is the finding.** Practice explains most where rules are stable, feedback is immediate and unambiguous, and performance criteria are clearly defined; it explains least where conditions are unstable, feedback delayed or ambiguous, and criteria contested. **Engineering delivery inside somebody else's organisation is at the second end on every axis.**

So the claim available to this session is narrow: pairing on real work creates the conditions under which capability could develop. It does not deliver capability. **A learner who leaves this session believing the plan will produce competent operators has adopted the strong claim the corrective refutes.**

**Its limits:** this note rests on the official abstract rather than the full text; the authors published a corrigendum reanalysing the findings and reported no practical effect; and **no figure from it is reproduced anywhere in this wiki.**

**3.6 What a model actually transmits, and why the elegant demonstration is the waste.**

The efficacy literature explains the failure mode the session's first named warning describes ([research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md](/research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md)). Of the four sources of efficacy belief, **only mastery experience is explicitly ranked** — "The most effective way of creating a strong sense of efficacy is through mastery experiences." Vicarious experience is the second named source, is conditional on perceived similarity, and is symmetric: "Seeing people similar to themselves succeed by perseverant effort raises observers' beliefs... By the same token, observing others fail despite high effort lowers observers' judgments of their own efficacy." And: "If people see the models as very different from themselves their beliefs of personal efficacy are not much influenced by the models' behavior."

**An expert working quickly and elegantly is a model who is not similar to the observer**, so the performance transmits little — and what it does transmit is a comparison the observer loses. The passage that should reorganise a teaching plan is about content rather than form: **"Undaunted attitudes exhibited by perseverant models as they cope with obstacles repeatedly thrown in their path can be more enabling to others than the particular skills being modeled."** The useful demonstration is the one where you are visibly stuck.

Two further passages bind the design. **"If people experience only easy successes they come to expect quick results and are easily discouraged by failure. A resilient sense of efficacy requires experience in overcoming obstacles through perseverant effort"** — which forecloses the safe backlog item, and which corrects an earlier reading of this source that would have licensed engineering every task to be survivable. And on what a teacher's actual lever is: **"Successful efficacy builders do more than convey positive appraisals. In addition to raising people's beliefs in their capabilities, they structure situations for them in ways that bring success and avoid placing people in situations prematurely where they are likely to fail often."** **The lever is task sequencing. The encouragement is incidental to it**, which is why the session's artefact is a dated item list rather than a set of coaching intentions.

The source also supplies the debrief instrument. Efficacious people attribute failure to insufficient effort or to acquirable skill or to the situation; inefficacious people attribute it to ability. **That split is a behaviour a supervisor can observe**, unlike a confidence rating, which is why step 6 of the workflow records the attribution verbatim.

**Its limits:** a conference collection edited by the field's founder, with no search strategy, no inclusion criteria and no disconfirming study in the editor's own chapter; empirical settings in schools, families, clinics, migration and addiction treatment; **no study of adult professionals learning a technical craft**; self-report measurement throughout; directionality conceded as unsettled in field settings; and the foundational 1977 paper unread in this repository. **Its magnitudes are recorded in the research layer and prohibited in this wiki.**

**3.7 Why feeling taught is not being taught.**

The measurement warning that runs through this programme lands here on the recipient. A randomised trial of experienced maintainers on their own repositories found them slower with AI assistance while believing they had been sped up, and — the load-bearing detail — **their post-hoc estimate after actually doing the work still went the wrong way** ([research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md](/research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md)). Direct personal experience did not correct the misperception.

**A customer engineer who reports feeling more capable has told you about their experience of the fortnight.** The only instrument for the capability question is watching them work unaided, which is [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), and which is why this session is designed to end without a claim.

**Its limits are severe.** The population is narrow and adversarial to the hypothesis — expert maintainers of codebases they know intimately. The developer sample is very small; time was self-reported. **The research group now labels the original result out of date and has redesigned the study**, because participants increasingly decline to work without assistance and the control arm is compromised; its later raw numbers point the other way and the group says they are not trustworthy. **Take the perception–measurement divergence and no magnitude.**

**3.8 Why the tooling has to be constrained, and what warrants the constraint.**

The platform half exists because the failure mode in §1 is now cheap to produce. An openly published agent configuration corpus, read at a pinned commit, supplies the two rules the session's pairing convention borrows: an agent is prohibited from checking human-review boxes, and a workflow must stop when a required human review is absent — **human approval is a stop condition, not a checkbox the workflow can satisfy for itself** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)). The corpus's transparency note is the honest companion: it ships no model and no safety classifier, host behaviour varies, and human oversight remains necessary.

**Both rules are declarations rather than enforcement**, and the corpus says so. Whether either holds depends on the host attaching the instruction and the model complying, neither of which the file can guarantee. **Its perishability is extreme** — paths, counts, artefact names and package versions all moved during the reading — and host and model dependence is total. **A single vendor's opinion about agent configuration is not the field.**

**3.9 Why the argument is about incentives rather than learning, and one claim explicitly declined.** There is a well-known family of claims about practice on real problems being superior to instruction removed from the point of use. This session does not rest on them, and deliberately: the version most often quoted at engineers is a retention hierarchy by modality with no traceable empirical basis, which this repository declines by name rather than merely not repeating — it appears in the change-management source cited elsewhere in this module, sourced to a commercial trainer's handbook rather than to any study ([research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md)). **The argument used here is narrower and checkable in principle:** work scheduled after a fixed date is unprotected, and unprotected work is removed under pressure. That is a claim about what organisations do.

**3.10 Why the withdrawal list is the plan.** A plan describing what the delivery team will do alongside the recipient describes the current arrangement with more words. The only element that changes behaviour is the list of things the team will stop doing, because those are the occasions the recipient will otherwise never have.

**3.11 Why the reclaim rule is written first.** A reclaim taken under pressure is taken correctly and recorded nowhere, and the plan therefore ends without anybody deciding it should. Writing the condition, the person told and the record required converts an unnoticed reversal into a visible one. **This has no external warrant and is an argument from auditability**, the same move as the evidence requirement at [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md).

**3.12 Why refusal is information.** A capable group that declines to receive the work is exhibiting unwillingness, not incapacity, and the response to unwillingness is not a better plan. The distinction is the one installed at [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) and its warrant is stated there in full: direction and mechanism only, from a commercially interested practitioner model with no independent evaluation.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The session expects to be resisted at the withdrawal list**, and the resistance is legitimate. A learner protecting a fixed date by doing the work themselves is behaving correctly by the standards of every other stage in this programme, and the session is asking them to accept a cost against a benefit nobody can demonstrate. The design's answer is not to argue them out of it but to make the trade explicit and dated: the plan states the cost in delivery terms and names who agreed to bear it. **A transfer nobody has agreed to pay for will not survive its first bad week**, and identifying the payer is the more useful half of the exercise.

**4.2 The cost is stated in the session's opening rather than discovered in its debrief.** This is a deliberate departure from the programme's usual pattern of withholding the answer, and it is justified on different grounds: the cost is not the thing the generation phase is testing. Item 5 is. **Concealing the price would produce learners who adopt the practice, meet the slowdown, and conclude they were doing it wrong** — which is the predicted abandonment path, and §3.3 supplies the warrant for expecting it.

**4.3 Opening item 5 is the instrument.** *If the date came under pressure next week, what is the first thing you would take back?* Everybody has an answer immediately. Almost nobody has written it down, and the gap between the speed of the answer and the absence of the record is the session's content.

**4.4 The technology function refuses.** The supplied situation is arranged so that the capable group declines the plan. The intended learning is that this is a fact about willingness and that redrafting the plan is the wrong response — and the session does not supply the right one, because the module's position is that unwillingness of this kind is not resolvable by the delivery team alone.

**4.5 The task-selection rule is the one most likely to be ignored and the one §3.4 makes load-bearing.** Learners will choose the interesting task, and the interesting task is frequently the one whose failure is fluent rather than red — a retrieval tuning, an evaluator threshold, a prompt revision. Exercise 2 exists to force the sort before the plan is written, and the standard requires the chosen item's failure to be legible as well as consequential.

**4.6 The pairing convention is the session's only non-report evidence.** Everything else in the session is somebody's account of what happened. The captured payload, and the observed refusal in step 4, are the only artefacts that are not testimony — which is also why they are not assessed, since assessing them would reward learners whose recipients happened to use an editor that exposes payloads.

**4.7 The review movement asks another learner to predict which withdrawal-list item will break first.** The prediction is usually accurate and usually names the item whose absence would be most visible to the learner's own sponsor, which locates the pressure in the learner's incentives rather than in the recipient's capability.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Transfer scheduled at the end.** *Countermeasure:* the plan requires dated items in the first half, and a plan with none is failed on the page rather than in discussion.

**The safe item.** The recipient leads work whose failure has no consequence. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires at least one item whose failure would be visible. **Defeatable**, since visibility is judged by the learner.

**The illegible item.** The recipient leads work whose failure is fluent rather than red, and receives no signal at all. *Countermeasure:* exercise 2's sort, run before the plan is written. **Partial**: legibility is also judged by the learner, and the whole point of the jagged-capability material is that the boundary is not where it feels like it is.

**The demonstration that is a performance.** *Countermeasure:* the plan must state who holds the keyboard, per item, and the review movement asks it directly. The efficacy literature in §3.6 supplies the reason rather than the mechanism, which is why the countermeasure is procedural.

**The agent as the third pair member who does all the work.** *Countermeasure:* the pairing convention in step 3, and the deliberate breach in step 4. **This is the countermeasure most likely to fail silently**, because a convention that did not load is indistinguishable from one that did until something is refused — see prediction 5.

**The encouraging half without the working half.** The learner delivers the error framing and then supplies the assistance. *Countermeasure:* the intervention count in step 5, which converts a felt impression into a number. **Partial**: the learner counts their own interventions unless an observer is available, and §3.7's finding is precisely that self-observation is unreliable in this direction.

**The quiet reclaim.** *Countermeasure:* the reclaim rule. This is the session's only genuinely novel mechanism and its effectiveness is untested.

**The reviewer of record who reviews nothing.** The recipient is nominally reviewing work the learner has already approved. *Countermeasure:* partial — the standard asks whether they have ever returned anything, which is an observable and a weak one.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No specification item. The plan and the reclaim rule contribute observation towards [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), where a learner who reclaimed work without following their own written rule, and reported it, has produced useful evidence rather than a mark against themselves.

**The intervention count is not assessed and is deliberately not compared across learners.** A low count on an easy task is worth nothing, and a high count on a hard one may be correct. What carries forward is whether the learner's estimate matched the count — which is a calibration observable rather than a performance one, and which §3.7 predicts will diverge.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: this trades delivery quality for an unmeasured benefit, and on a real engagement that is a bad trade the customer did not agree to.**

At its strongest: the customer is paying for a delivered system on a date. Handing work to a slower engineer produces a later, worse system in exchange for a capability nobody has demonstrated will materialise or persist. If the customer wanted training, they could buy training, which is cheaper than buying it at delivery rates embedded in critical-path work. And the delivery organisation's own quality standards — the reviewer competence that the whole accelerated model rests on — are weakened exactly where they are load-bearing.

**This is conceded and it is the strongest objection to the module.** The rebuttal is partial and should be read as such. The trade is only defensible where the alternative is a system the customer cannot operate, which is a judgement about this engagement rather than a general position, and the session requires the cost to be stated and a payer named precisely so that the judgement is made by somebody entitled to make it rather than by an engineer being generous with a schedule. **The fix that was considered and declined** was to make capability transfer conditional on explicit sponsor agreement before any item is handed over — declined because it converts the practice into a negotiation that will be lost every time a date is tight, which returns the module to the end-of-engagement training week it exists to prevent. That rejection prioritises the module's survival over the customer's stated preference, and the objection is right that this is what it does.

**Objection 2: the prohibition being extended is one the source contradicts, so the session is built on a rule its own corpus does not follow.**

At its strongest: the corpus states that enablement is never a separate event, and its proudest case material is a compressed workshop. A programme that criticises its source for internal contradiction elsewhere cannot then adopt the half of a contradiction it prefers and present the choice as inheritance.

**Conceded without qualification.** The session states the contradiction on its own page rather than in a footnote. The position taken here is that the prohibition is right for the reason given in §3.9 — an incentive argument the corpus never makes — and that the corpus's authority is not what the session is resting on. **If the incentive argument is wrong, nothing else supports the session.**

**Objection 3: the pedagogical warrant is borrowed from laboratories, and neither supporting literature measured anybody doing a job.**

At its strongest, and it is the objection with the best paperwork: the Productive Failure evidence is school, university and laboratory work, with no adult-professional study in its author's reference list, and the author concedes failure is sufficient but not necessary. The error-management synthesis compared training methods against *other training methods*, never against no training, and **no study in its pool measured on-the-job performance at all** — every outcome is a post-training laboratory task. Its own pooled variability did not statistically require the moderator analysis its conclusions rest on, and the feedback-clarity moderator that this session's task-selection rule depends on received only limited support by the authors' own words. Building a live-engagement practice on that is an extrapolation stacked on a partition of a homogeneous pool.

**Conceded, and it is the most serious methodological objection to this session.** The mitigation is convergence: two independent traditions — one educational, one workplace-training — reach the same ordering conclusion from different populations and different methods, and agreement across traditions raises the prior more than either does alone. That is a real argument and it is not a substitute for a study in this setting.

**The declined fix was to weaken the design to something the evidence covers outright** — demonstrate first, then have the recipient repeat under supervision — and it was declined because the one condition both literatures agree on is that reducing the failure rate during the attempt reduces what is learned. **Adopting the covered design would mean adopting the intervention the evidence says does not work, in order to be able to cite evidence.** A reader who weighs the extrapolation more heavily than this paper does is making a defensible call, and the honest summary is that the session's ordering is well warranted and its setting is not.

**Objection 4: constraining the tooling to keep the recipient driving is theatre, because the constraint is a declaration the host may ignore.**

At its strongest: the pairing convention is a text file whose enforcement depends on a host attaching it and a model complying with it, neither of which is guaranteed, and the corpus supplying the two borrowed rules says so in its own transparency note. A session that presents an unenforceable declaration as a control has taught learners to trust a mechanism that fails silently — the precise error the module's own first session is about.

**Substantially correct, and the design's response is to make the failure the observable rather than to claim the control works.** Step 4 requires a deliberate breach and records what happens, and the standard requires the convention to have been *observed refusing something* rather than observed existing. **The declined fix was to drop the convention and rely on a spoken agreement**, which was rejected because a spoken agreement fails in exactly the same way and produces no artefact to inspect. **The residual position is that this is a control whose failure mode is silence, taught alongside an instruction to go and look**, and that is weaker than a control that works.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Nearly every learner will answer opening item 5 immediately, and few will have the answer recorded anywhere in their existing plan. *Instrument:* the written answers against the plan's contents, coded *recorded* / *not recorded*.

**Prediction 2.** First-draft plans will place most dated transfer items in the second half of the remaining schedule. *Instrument:* item dates against the schedule midpoint, reported as a distribution.

**Prediction 3.** The item another learner predicts will break first will match the item the learner later reclaims, where a reclaim occurs. *Instrument:* the review movement's prediction against the reclaim record. **If these do not match, the review movement is entertainment** and should be replaced by a written pre-commitment.

**Prediction 4.** Learners will respond to the technology function's refusal by redrafting the plan rather than by recording unwillingness. *Instrument:* the post-refusal artefact, coded *plan revised* / *unwillingness recorded* / *escalated* / *abandoned*.

**Prediction 5.** Most learners will complete the pairing session without ever observing the convention refuse anything, and will nonetheless record it as in force. *Instrument:* the workflow record, coded for whether a refusal was observed and, where it was not, whether the absence is stated. **This predicts the failure of the session's own principal platform countermeasure**, and it is included because a control whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption dressed as a mechanism.

**Prediction 6.** Learners will underestimate their own intervention count, and the direction of the error will not correct after a second session. *Instrument:* the observer's count against the learner's pre-disclosure estimate, across two sessions. **This is the session's only direct test of §3.7's mechanism**, and a null result would mean the perception–measurement divergence does not transfer to this setting, in which case exercise 5 is decoration.

**Prediction 7.** Learners who chose a task with illegible failure will report a smoother session and a more satisfied recipient than those who chose a legible one, and will produce fewer recorded incapacities at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md). *Instrument:* exercise 2's legibility sort against the session debrief and the later rehearsal record. **If this holds, the pleasantness of a pairing session is evidence against it**, which is a claim worth having and which nothing currently supports.

**None of these has been measured.** The register is machinery and not a result about this session.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That capability built after delivery is unprotected and will be cut; that the withdrawal list is the plan and everything else is intention; that a reclaim without a written rule is the plan ending silently; and that taking the keyboard is a design violation rather than a small kindness.

**What the evidence licenses.** The *ordering* — attempt before instruction — is well warranted by two independent traditions, and its price is stated in the same sources: no advantage during the training, with the benefit on transfer afterwards. **Nothing licenses the claim that this produces capable operators**, and the practice corrective in §3.5 is the reason to expect that hours alone will not, in a domain with unstable conditions and ambiguous feedback. No source behind this programme reports a customer's engineers becoming more capable by any route.

**Do not let the quality of the ordering evidence transfer to the setting.** The studies behind it never measured anybody doing a job.

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper**, including from the two sources read in full whose effect sizes are recorded in the research layer. That is a rule of this repository rather than an accident of access.

**The concession worth remembering.** The strongest objection to this session is that it trades something the customer is paying for against something nobody can demonstrate. That objection is conceded rather than answered, and a learner should be able to state it before proposing this practice to anybody.

Recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md).

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The two rules the pairing convention borrows — that an agent may not check human-review boxes, and that a workflow must stop when a required human review is absent, so that human approval is a stop condition rather than a checkbox — are recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md), read at commit `cf29fb45` on `main` with its published package version, in a machine-assisted full-tree pass. The same source's transparency note states that the project ships no model and no safety classifier, that host behaviour varies, and that human oversight remains necessary; and the note records that **both rules are declarations rather than enforcement**, whose effectiveness depends on the host attaching the instruction and the model complying. **That note marks the material extremely perishable**: paths, counts, artefact names and package version all moved during the reading, and the revision read is not all-green.

**The silences that matter, and there are three.** First, **no vendor documentation known to this programme states how often an instructions file fails to be applied**, under what host and model conditions, or what a non-application looks like from the output — which is precisely the property step 4 of the workflow depends on and prediction 5 expects to fail. The loading rule is documented; its reliability is not. Second, **the corpus supplying the borrowed rules publishes no committed result of any kind** — no controlled study, no calibrated baseline, no outcome dataset — while its own prose uses the vocabulary of demonstrated benefit; it establishes that the constraint can be expressed and nothing about whether expressing it changes behaviour. Third, and broadest: **vendor delivery guidance and partner enablement material describe workshops, learning paths and structured enablement engagements — all of which are separate events — and none known to this programme describes building customer capability inside the delivery itself, names a cost for doing so, or reports whether either approach leaves anything behind.** The industry's published model of enablement is the model this session argues against, and it argues against it without evidence. If vendor material appears that measures either approach, this paper records its version and what it omits.
