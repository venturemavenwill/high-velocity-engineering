> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Withdrawal list** | The things the delivery team is capable of doing and will not do, because doing them removes the occasion for somebody else to learn |
> | **Reclaim rule** | A condition, written in advance, under which work handed to the recipient is taken back, with a stated recipient of that news and a stated record |
> | **Interleaving** | Placing an activity inside an existing delivery cadence rather than running it as a separate event |

## In plain terms

Capability has to be built somewhere, and there are only two places: inside the delivery, or after it.

After it is where everybody puts it, and after it is what gets cut. This is not a failure of intent. Work scheduled after the delivery competes with nothing until the moment the date comes under pressure, at which point it competes with the date and loses, and removing it costs nothing that month. **A plan that puts capability transfer at the end has scheduled its own deletion.**

Inside the delivery is more expensive and it is expensive immediately, which is why it does not happen by default. The customer's engineer is slower. The work they lead takes longer and comes back worse the first time. Every hour of it is visible to a sponsor watching a fixed date.

So the session's real artefact is not a teaching plan. It is a **withdrawal list** — the things the delivery team could do quickly and will not — and a **reclaim rule**, written in advance, stating the condition under which the work comes back. A reclaim that follows a written rule is a decision. A reclaim that does not is the plan ending quietly, and nobody records the moment it happened.

## Abstract

This paper defends binding capability transfer to real backlog items inside the delivery rather than to an enablement period after it. The argument is about incentives rather than about learning: work scheduled after delivery is unprotected, and its removal is invisible and free. The session produces a teaching plan naming the backlog items through which each capability is built, who holds the keyboard, and what the delivery team will not do; plus a reclaim rule written before the pressure arrives. The paper borrows a prohibition from its practice corpus — that enablement activity is interleaved into the engineering cadence rather than run as a separate event — and concedes that the corpus contradicts its own prohibition in its case material, so the extension made here rests on a rule its source does not consistently follow. It further concedes that nothing behind this programme measures whether capability built during delivery transfers better than capability delivered afterwards.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A delivery team under a fixed date will not hand real work to somebody slower unless it has decided in advance to, in specific terms, against specific items.

Everything about the moment argues the other way, and the arguments are good ones. The item is urgent. The customer's engineer is unavailable this week. Doing it yourself takes an hour and supervising takes three. The consequence of the delay is visible to the sponsor and the consequence of not transferring the capability is visible to nobody until after everybody has gone.

**The team is not choosing convenience over transfer. It is choosing a cost it can see over a cost it cannot**, which is a different failure and is not fixed by being told that transfer matters.

The session's job is to move the decision earlier, to specific named items, and to force the reclaim into the open by requiring its rule to exist before its occasion does.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session assumes the capability statement from [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md), a live backlog with remaining work, and the delivery-pressure material from Stage 5 — in particular the session on review under delivery pressure, since a customer's engineer acting as reviewer of record is a review that arrives when the schedule is worst.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why interleaving.** The practice corpus behind this programme states a prohibition repeatedly: design and enablement activity is interleaved into the existing engineering cadence and is never run as a separate workshop or a pause in delivery. This session extends the prohibition to capability transfer, which the corpus does not do. **The extension is this design's, and it inherits a defect from its source**, because the corpus contradicts the prohibition in its own case material, whose headline achievements include compressed multi-day workshops. Nothing in the corpus reconciles the two.

**3.2 Why the argument is about incentives rather than learning.** There is a well-known family of claims about practice on real problems being superior to instruction removed from the point of use. This session does not rest on them, and deliberately: the version of that claim most often quoted at engineers is a retention hierarchy by modality that has no traceable empirical basis, and which this repository declines by name rather than merely not repeating. **The argument used here is narrower and checkable in principle:** work scheduled after a fixed date is unprotected, and unprotected work is removed under pressure. That is a claim about what organisations do, not about how people learn.

**3.3 Why the withdrawal list is the plan.** A plan describing what the delivery team will do alongside the recipient describes the current arrangement with more words. The only element that changes behaviour is the list of things the team will stop doing, because those are the occasions the recipient will otherwise never have.

**3.4 Why the reclaim rule is written first.** A reclaim taken under pressure is taken correctly and recorded nowhere, and the plan therefore ends without anybody deciding it should. Writing the condition, the person told and the record required converts an unnoticed reversal into a visible one. **This has no external warrant and is an argument from auditability**, the same move as the evidence requirement at [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md).

**3.5 Why refusal is information.** A capable group that declines to receive the work is exhibiting unwillingness, not incapacity, and the response to unwillingness is not a better plan. The distinction is the one installed at [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) and its warrant is stated there in full: direction and mechanism only, from a commercially interested practitioner model with no independent evaluation.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**The session expects to be resisted at the withdrawal list**, and the resistance is legitimate. A learner protecting a fixed date by doing the work themselves is behaving correctly by the standards of every other stage in this programme, and the session is asking them to accept a cost against a benefit nobody can demonstrate. The design's answer is not to argue them out of it but to make the trade explicit and dated: the plan states the cost in delivery terms and names who agreed to bear it. **A transfer nobody has agreed to pay for will not survive its first bad week**, and identifying the payer is the more useful half of the exercise.

**Opening item 5 is the instrument.** *If the date came under pressure next week, what is the first thing you would take back?* Everybody has an answer immediately. Almost nobody has written it down, and the gap between the speed of the answer and the absence of the record is the session's content.

**The technology function refuses.** The supplied situation is arranged so that the capable group declines the plan. The intended learning is that this is a fact about willingness and that redrafting the plan is the wrong response — and the session does not supply the right one, because the module's position is that unwillingness of this kind is not resolvable by the delivery team alone.

**The review movement asks another learner to predict which withdrawal-list item will break first.** The prediction is usually accurate and usually names the item whose absence would be most visible to the learner's own sponsor, which locates the pressure in the learner's incentives rather than in the recipient's capability.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Transfer scheduled at the end.** *Countermeasure:* the plan requires dated items in the first half, and a plan with none is failed on the page rather than in discussion.

**The safe item.** The recipient leads work whose failure has no consequence. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires at least one item whose failure would be visible. **Defeatable**, since visibility is judged by the learner.

**The demonstration that is a performance.** *Countermeasure:* the plan must state who holds the keyboard, per item, and the review movement asks it directly.

**The quiet reclaim.** *Countermeasure:* the reclaim rule. This is the session's only genuinely novel mechanism and its effectiveness is untested.

**The reviewer of record who reviews nothing.** The recipient is nominally reviewing work the learner has already approved. *Countermeasure:* partial — the standard asks whether they have ever returned anything, which is an observable and a weak one.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: this trades delivery quality for an unmeasured benefit, and on a real engagement that is a bad trade the customer did not agree to.**

At its strongest: the customer is paying for a delivered system on a date. Handing work to a slower engineer produces a later, worse system in exchange for a capability nobody has demonstrated will materialise or persist. If the customer wanted training, they could buy training, which is cheaper than buying it at delivery rates embedded in critical-path work. And the delivery organisation's own quality standards — the reviewer competence that the whole accelerated model rests on — are weakened exactly where they are load-bearing.

**This is conceded and it is the strongest objection to the module.** The rebuttal is partial and should be read as such. The trade is only defensible where the alternative is a system the customer cannot operate, which is a judgement about this engagement rather than a general position, and the session requires the cost to be stated and a payer named precisely so that the judgement is made by somebody entitled to make it rather than by an engineer being generous with a schedule. **The fix that was considered and declined** was to make capability transfer conditional on explicit sponsor agreement before any item is handed over — declined because it converts the practice into a negotiation that will be lost every time a date is tight, which returns the module to the end-of-engagement training week it exists to prevent. That rejection prioritises the module's survival over the customer's stated preference, and the objection is right that this is what it does.

**Objection 2: the prohibition being extended is one the source contradicts, so the session is built on a rule its own corpus does not follow.**

At its strongest: the corpus states that enablement is never a separate event, and its proudest case material is a compressed workshop. A programme that criticises its source for internal contradiction elsewhere cannot then adopt the half of a contradiction it prefers and present the choice as inheritance.

**Conceded without qualification.** The session states the contradiction on its own page rather than in a footnote. The position taken here is that the prohibition is right for the reason given in §3.2 — an incentive argument the corpus never makes — and that the corpus's authority is not what the session is resting on. **If the incentive argument is wrong, nothing else supports the session.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Nearly every learner will answer opening item 5 immediately, and few will have the answer recorded anywhere in their existing plan. *Instrument:* the written answers against the plan's contents, coded *recorded* / *not recorded*.

**Prediction 2.** First-draft plans will place most dated transfer items in the second half of the remaining schedule. *Instrument:* item dates against the schedule midpoint, reported as a distribution.

**Prediction 3.** The item another learner predicts will break first will match the item the learner later reclaims, where a reclaim occurs. *Instrument:* the review movement's prediction against the reclaim record. **If these do not match, the review movement is entertainment** and should be replaced by a written pre-commitment.

**Prediction 4.** Learners will respond to the technology function's refusal by redrafting the plan rather than by recording unwillingness. *Instrument:* the post-refusal artefact, coded *plan revised* / *unwillingness recorded* / *escalated* / *abandoned*.

**None of these has been measured.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That capability built after delivery is unprotected and will be cut; that the withdrawal list is the plan and everything else is intention; and that a reclaim without a written rule is the plan ending silently.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing. The interleaving prohibition is inherited from a corpus that contradicts it, the incentive argument is this design's own, and no source behind this programme reports a customer's engineers becoming more capable by any route.

**The concession worth remembering.** The strongest objection to this session is that it trades something the customer is paying for against something nobody can demonstrate. That objection is conceded rather than answered, and a learner should be able to state it before proposing this practice to anybody.

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The extension of the interleaving prohibition to capability transfer; the incentive argument in §3.2; the withdrawal list as the plan's substance; the reclaim rule and its three required elements; the requirement that the cost be stated and a payer named; the requirement that at least one transferred item have a visible failure; and all four predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** **This session has no platform anchor and relies on no vendor documentation.** The session is about how work is allocated inside an engagement, which no product describes. **The silence worth recording:** vendor delivery guidance and partner enablement material describe workshops, learning paths and structured enablement engagements — all of which are separate events — and none known to this programme describes building customer capability inside the delivery itself, names a cost for doing so, or reports whether either approach leaves anything behind. The industry's published model of enablement is the model this session argues against, and it argues against it without evidence. If vendor material appears that measures either approach, this session acquires an anchor and this paper records its version and what it omits.
