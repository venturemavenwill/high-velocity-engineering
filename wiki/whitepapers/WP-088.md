## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

You are about to write down the risks that remain, for someone who will read the page once, months from now, and cannot ask you a follow-up question. The technical half takes half an hour.

**The half that took the programme two years is what you leave out.**

Everything you write will be true. That is not in question; nobody in this room is dishonest. The question is what happens to the reader when you make a judgement, in good faith and for a defensible reason, about what is worth their attention — and never tell them a judgement was made.

The day's answer has three parts and one of them is new. **Is the omitted material available if asked for?** Not enough. **Does the selection serve the reader's decision rather than yours?** Not enough either. The third condition overrides both: **do you know which omission would change what this person decides?** If you do, and you omit it without disclosing that an omission exists, that is deception — whatever else is true about the document. And the knowledge condition has a floor: you are required to look, and after the first time you have been told that your omissions will not fall randomly, not having realised is a failure to perform a duty rather than a defence.

Then the hardest case, which has no author at all. **A system is optimised on a criterion nobody wrote down.** There is no omission, because nothing was selected away; there is nothing for a reader to ask for, because nothing was recorded. The day locates the wrong at the moment of not recording, committed by people who were not thinking about disclosure — from which it follows that **the duty to write the criterion down comes before any duty to disclose it.** That is not documentation hygiene. It is the precondition of every obligation the day imposes.

And it is not hypothetical, because the room finds one in their own repository in eight minutes: an evaluator pass threshold that decides whether runs pass, chosen by nobody, on a scale for which no agreement with human judgement is published anywhere.

**What this paper concedes, and it is a real concession.** A question the design deferred for two years is settled in one phase, using a corpus it commissioned, and the framework is delivered as an answer rather than as a position among positions. **The room is given no material with which to disagree** — no case in which applying the three conditions yields a conclusion they would reject. The fix was one more statement and fifteen minutes, and it was declined **for a scheduling reason rather than a principled one**, which the paper says in as many words.

## Abstract

This paper defends the day on which the programme closes its two longest open questions, and defends the answers it gives. The central argument is that the ethics of selective true reporting are not about what was said but about whether the reader could discover what was not: selection becomes deception at the point where the author knows which omission would change the reader's decision and omits it without disclosing that an omission exists. The paper defends adding a third condition to the two [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) supplied, defends tightening availability into recoverability, and defends a professional floor on the knowledge condition. It defends locating the wrong in [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md)'s unrecorded-criterion case at the moment of not recording rather than at deployment, and deriving from that a recording duty that precedes the disclosure duty. **It defends putting that abstract answer in front of the room as a fact about their own substrate** — an evaluator pass threshold nobody chose, on a scale for which no agreement with human judgement is published — and defends adding a fifth enumeration heading for guarantees the author inherited and did not write. **Section 7 reports the discharge of five named deferrals, one of them two years old.** It concedes that the day answers a two-year question in fifty minutes on constructed materials, that a quarter with no concurrent professional course has forced content into a seminar that should have been supported by one, and that the day's platform content risks converting an ethical finding into a compliance item.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The technical content is thin and the day knows it. A residual-risk statement written from yesterday's bounded case is an enumeration and a translation: four sources of residue, a page in the reader's language, a horizon and a confidence on each quantity, a not-met procedure. A competent student does that in half an hour.

The problem is that **the programme has been carrying an unanswered ethical question for two years and there is nowhere left to put it.**

[S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) phase 7, in week thirteen, asked students to write two true reports from one body of evidence and then asked whether what each concealed from the other's reader was legitimate. The instructor declined to answer and named [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) and this day. [WP-013](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-013.md) §8 recorded the decision as deliberate and recorded its cost: the technique consolidates without the disquiet, and the mitigation — returning the cohort's positions here — is a genuine instrument and a two-year-delayed one. [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) phase 6 point 3 answered the epistemic half and sent the ethical half here in one sentence. [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) phase 6 point 2 supplied two conditions and sent the boundary here. [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §6 sent a harder version here: whether a system optimised on a criterion nobody recorded, deployed to people who never saw it, is an ethical object.

**This day is the terminus.** The [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s deferral rule requires a deferring day to name an occasion that can carry the deferral. Four days name this one. There is no fifth occasion and no later module, so a question that leaves this day unanswered has not been deferred; it has been abandoned, and this paper would have to say so.

Three difficulties sit underneath the ethical one, and none is what the room expects.

**The room's instinct will be that the ethics are about honesty**, and every student here is honest — [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §1 established that and it is still true. A framework that tells honest people to be honest changes nothing. The framework has to bite somewhere they are currently comfortable, and it does: at the omission they made for a good reason, in good faith, without telling anyone they had made it.

**The artefact is permanent and single-shot.** [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s failures were spoken and repairable within the same conversation. Here the statement is read once, in six months, by a person who cannot ask a follow-up question. The two symmetric failures are the same two, and the second one — a limitation stated in language the reader cannot act on — becomes final rather than correctable.

**The hardest case has no author.** [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md)'s unrecorded criterion is not an omission at all. Nobody selected it away. It was never written, so there is nothing to disclose and nothing for the reader to ask for, and the framework's whole apparatus of conditions runs on material that exists. The day has to reach an answer that locates the wrong somewhere other than in a disclosure decision, and it does.

**And the hardest case is in the room's own system**, which is the practical turn's contribution to this day and the reason phase 3 opens with eight minutes of configuration reading rather than with the corpus. The substrate's evaluation harness applies a pass threshold of three on a one-to-five scale, nobody in three years of cohort records chose three, and **no agreement figure between that evaluator and a human judge is published anywhere**, so the threshold is a cut on an uncalibrated instrument. That is [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md)'s case exactly, sitting in a repository these students are about to hand to somebody else. A room that has just found it is much harder to lose to *that would not happen here*, which is the objection an ethics phase built entirely on a commissioned corpus otherwise invites.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Low intrinsic, high evaluative, and the evaluative load is concentrated in one step.**

Steps 1 to 4 are translation and are supported. Step 5 is the day and it is genuinely hard: the student holds the full enumeration from step 2, the selected statement from step 3, and three conditions, and must apply the conditions item by item to their own selection while the discarded items are still visible. **That is a moment with a short half-life.** A week later the discarded items are gone from memory and the author recalls only what they kept, which is why the audit is placed inside the task rather than set as reflection.

Element interactivity is between steps 3 and 5 and it is circular by design: applying the conditions moves items back into the statement, which lengthens it, which pushes against the one-page limit, which pressures the selection again. The design wants that loop and sizes the ninety minutes for two or three passes.

Expertise reversal applies to step 3 for anyone with professional writing experience. Nothing is done about it, because step 5 is where their difficulty is and it is not reduced by fluency.

Management:

- **The safety case is a week old and in front of them.**
- **Step 2's headings come from yesterday's case structure**, so the enumeration is a traversal rather than an invention.
- **Step 4's disclosure form is supplied as R4's four sentences.**
- **The three conditions are on the board.**
- **The reader's three questions are fixed and supplied.**

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Answering the ethical question cold, in your own words, before instruction,* item 4, and comparing it against a two-year-old recorded position at phase 8.
- *Listing what you left out and why, before you know the framework,* items 2 and 3.
- *Finding the unrecorded criterion in your own substrate before meeting it in a case,* phase 3's eight-minute check.
- *A sealed prediction about what the agent will quietly drop from your own enumeration,* item 8.
- *Auditing your own selection with the discarded material still in front of you,* step 5.
- *A silent cold read by a non-technical reader,* step 6.
- *A partner who sees both the enumeration and the statement,* phase 7 — the only reader in the day who can.
- *Recording rather than resolving disagreement about whether an omission was decision-changing,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *A hostile or adversarial recipient.* Every party in this programme's assessed occasions acts in good faith. A reader who is trying to extract an overclaim is [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s audience B at higher intensity and is not the day's subject.
- *A legal or regulatory disclosure framework.* Named as SG390's and out of scope here. The day supplies the boundary conditions, not the jurisdiction.
- *Quantified risk.* As at [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md): the substrate cannot supply intervals for most residual risks and requiring them would produce invented numbers. Horizons and confidences are required where a quantity is stated, and a statement with no quantities is admissible.
- *Assessing the size of the step 5 movement.* Excluded and stated in the specification, for the reason in §7.
- *Running the four statements on a live recipient.* Available in appearance only. The four are **demonstrated rather than operated**, the day says so, and the specification assesses none of them — because what adjudicates a residual-risk statement is a decision that was made once, and a simulated recipient measures a different thing while looking like the same instrument.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The three conditions and the deception boundary are **safe where the author can identify the reader and name the decision the statement supports** — a handover to a known receiving organisation, a risk statement to a named decision-owner, a report to a client. They are **unsafe as a standard for publication to an unknown readership**, where the counterfactual-disclosure condition has no referent: there is no single decision to be changed, the readers' decisions are heterogeneous and unknown, and an author applying the third condition will substitute their own guess about what a typical reader would do — which is the author's judgement wearing the reader's authority, and is worse than no framework because it feels principled. Point 4 states the substitute duty for that case: **disclose the selection rule rather than the omission's relevance.** They are also **unsafe where the reader is adversarial**, since recoverability assumes a reader who wants to know and the framework has no account of a reader who will use the disclosure of an omission as leverage. Both boundaries are stated to students at phase 4 point 4.

**A second strong-form scope statement, on the day's platform content.** Point 10's recording remedy — write down the criterion, including when the criterion is a default you did not choose — is **safe as a duty on any parameter that decides an outcome for somebody who did not set it, wherever the parameter is enumerable: a threshold, a split strategy, a retention window, a licence tier.** It is **unsafe as a sufficient account of the ethics**: recording a default discharges the recording duty and does nothing about the disclosure duty, and a handover that lists forty defaults in an appendix has satisfied point 10 and failed point 3's recoverability condition, because a reader who does not know the list exists cannot use it. It is **unsafe as a general licence to defer**, since a criterion that could have been chosen and was not is not made acceptable by being recorded. **The specification's binding on this is that the phase 3 criterion enters the step 2 enumeration and is then subject to the step 5 audit like every other item** — a student who records it and does not ask whether its disclosure would change their reader's decision has applied the simplification outside its range and fails the day's single binary requirement. This is stated at phase 4 point 10.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **R2**.

R1 is a straw the room will knock over: four pages of accurate unreadable prose is a failure this cohort was taught to name a year and a half ago. R4 is the payload and will land. **R2 is the case that implicates the room**, because R2's author did what every competent engineer does — made a judgement about what was worth the reader's attention, on a defensible probability assessment, in good faith — and the recipient made a decision they would not otherwise have made. There is no carelessness in R2 and no bad motive, and the room will recognise their own habitual practice in it. That recognition is the day's motivational engine and it is why R2 rather than R4 is the case phase 4 point 3 works on.

Competence is protected by the technical half being straightforward and by the ethical half being answered rather than left open — the room is given a position they can apply, argue with and be assessed against, not a discussion.

Autonomy is at steps 3 and 5. What goes in the statement is entirely the student's, and the one binary requirement is not a content rule: it is that any item their own audit marks decision-changing goes back in.

Relatedness is supplied by the recipient at step 6, who is a real person connected to the substrate, and by the two-year loop at phase 8, which shows the cohort what it collectively believed in week thirteen.

The affective risk is at phase 6 and phase 8's last item, and it is the sharpest in the module. The clinic prompt — *something you decided not to say this year, and the reason you gave yourself* — is the most self-incriminating prompt in the programme, three days before an entrustment occasion. The countermeasures are the ones the programme has used since [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) and one addition: the firewall is stated **before** the prompt rather than after; the instructor discloses first; and phase 8's final sentence — an omission the student now judges to have been deception — is **written, kept and never collected**, with that stated before it is written. A prompt of that severity with any collection mechanism would produce nothing true.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Eight items. Items 2 and 3 harvest the students' own omissions and reasons before any framework exists, which is what makes phase 8's read-back a mirror rather than a lesson. Item 4 takes the two-year question cold and verbatim. Item 7 is a self-prediction about a reader, resolved at step 6. **Item 8 is the sealed Copilot prediction and is deliberately about omission**, because summarisation is where an agent's selection is invisible and invisible selection is the day's subject.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Eight retrievals spanning two years to [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md), oldest scaffolded. The bridge asks the room to falsify a rule the programme supplied one quarter ago, and produces the recoverability condition before phase 4 states it.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** **Eight minutes reading the substrate's own evaluation configuration**, then four statements from one case to one recipient, all true, with the corpus's construction disclosed and [WP-087](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-087.md) §8's objection to the same construction acknowledged as applying unchanged. **The four statements are demonstrated rather than operated and the day states it**; the configuration check is the one element that is run. Commit-before-outcome. Payload R4; R2 is the implicating case; R3 is [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md)'s with no author, and the room has already found its counterpart in their own system.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Point 3 supplies the three conditions, point 4 the deception boundary, point 5 the unrecorded-criterion answer. Points 1, 4 and 8 carry scope statements, point 8 in normative form with its conditions stated on the day rather than deferred to SG390. **Point 9 is the day's perishable platform content — a guarantee that changed hands on a published date, with saved queries breaking, blocking rules lapsing and a single toggle disabling everything — and point 10 is its named defaults, with a second strong-form scope statement and the documentation silence that no evaluator's agreement with human judgement is published anywhere.**

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps, with a fifth enumeration heading for inherited guarantees. Step 5 is the day. Step 6 is a silent cold read reusing [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md)'s structure, contingent on that day's outcome under rule 5 as amended. **Step 7 ships the smallest build in the module — a merged check that fails when the statement's expiry event occurs — and the artefact carries a prompt record, an intervention record and a frontier statement.**

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A statement that discharged every duty and was ignored. The author is faultless under the day's own framework, which keeps the clinic about what the framework does not cover. **Then the agent case: a one-page statement produced from a twenty-two-item enumeration in which every quantity lost its horizon and its confidence, in exact compliance with the instruction it was given** — the agent performing, invisibly and on request, the selection the day has spent fifty minutes calling deception when a person performs it.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** The only reader who sees the enumeration and the statement together. Disagreement on the third condition is recorded, not resolved.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 7 against the reader; **item 8 opened, with predicted and observed omission classes side by side**; item 3's reasons read back; **the two-year loop closed**; the R2 ranking; the step 5 movement; one kept, uncollected sentence.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with professional communication experience will produce an excellent step 3 statement quickly. Predicted failure: **step 5 will be performed as ratification.** They will apply the three conditions and find that every omission passes, because their selection was skilled and skilled selection feels justified from the inside, which is precisely what the conditions are supposed to interrupt. Countermeasure: phase 7's partner sees the enumeration and the statement together and is the external observer instrumentation rule 1 requires; and the assessors' checked element is the audit's per-item three answers, not its outcome, so a student who wrote three answers per item and moved nothing has satisfied the specification and left a record that phase 7 can contradict.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is at step 3's translation: a student who cannot render a residual risk in a non-technical reader's language will produce R1 in miniature. Countermeasures: [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) is five days old and supplies the reader model; the one-page limit is a hard constraint rather than a guideline; R4 is in front of them as a form; and step 6's cold read supplies the correction within the same phase rather than a week later.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** A group that ranks R4 first before the outcome has not failed productively, and this is likely for some groups, because R4's disclosure move is visible and this cohort has been trained to like disclosure. The difficulty is relocated to the final question — *what does R4 cost, and who pays it* — whose answer is that R4 moves the unreadable artefact behind a door and puts a sign on the door, which is an improvement and not a resolution. **Fallback if a group answers that too:** they are asked to write the sentence R4's author says when the recipient does request the complete list and cannot read it, which is [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s audience C — a want that no honest formulation satisfies — arriving in written form.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **moral discomfort converting to cynicism**, and it is the same risk [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §5 named at a lower intensity. A cohort told that a defensible good-faith omission was a deception may conclude either that the standard is unmeetable or that everyone deceives constantly and the word means nothing. Both conclusions end the day's usefulness.

Countermeasures: the standard is stated with a knowledge condition, so it is meetable — R2's author is not accused of dishonesty, they are shown a duty they did not know they had; the professional floor is stated as a duty to look, which is an action rather than a character judgement; R4 exists as an available move that costs half a page, so the room leaves with a technique and not only a scruple; phase 6's case has an author who did everything right, which prevents the day resolving into *be more careful*; and the instructor states once that the framework's purpose is to make an omission decidable in advance rather than regrettable afterwards.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) (one week, where the statement becomes part of the handover artefact, its not-met procedures acquire owners who will still be present, and its expiry becomes a warranty term) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) (two weeks, where the statement is delivered to a real decision-maker and EA-3 is judged on it).

**[S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) at two weeks is the consequential retrieval and it is instrumented in both directions.** The EA-3 perturbation set draws, for each student, one item from their own step 2 enumeration that did not reach step 3, and asks why that person does not need to know it. That is the day's audit administered by somebody else, on the student's own material, with a real decision-maker present.

Retained content: the three conditions; the deception boundary and its knowledge condition; the recording duty preceding the disclosure duty; the selection disclosure as a four-sentence move; and the redirected-disclosure exception with its three conditions. Five items.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 1, week 2. **The programme ends before any longer interval is available**, which is stated here because it is a real limitation: the day's content is retrieved twice at short gaps and never at the intervals the rest of the programme uses, and no cohort evidence about its retention will exist.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md).

One specification item in SG390 with PE390. Per rule 1, the checked elements are the named recipient with their decision and the record that they previously decided against the student, **the step 2 enumeration under five headings** including the inherited-guarantee entry naming the phase 3 criterion with its value and the fact that nobody chose it, and including the [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) risk raised and lost, the one-page statement with horizon, confidence and not-met procedure with a named person in the same field as each risk, the four-part selection disclosure, the step 5 audit's three answers per item and every item that moved, the reader's three cold-read answers as they recorded them, the expiry by event and date with a responsible party, **the merged expiry check or the dated reminder with its statement that the event is not detectable from inside the substrate**, the phase 7 classification with the author's response and any recorded disagreement, **and the prompt record, intervention record and frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed**, and **nothing about the four phase 3 statements is assessed**, since they are demonstrated rather than operated.

Five hard requirements: **no selection disclosure fails**; **an item the student's own audit marked decision-changing and did not return to the statement fails**, which is the day's single binary ethical requirement; **a quantity without its horizon and confidence in the same field fails**, per specification rule 2; **a statement addressed to a recipient who has never decided against the student on this component fails** and is rewritten for one who has; and **an expiry with neither a merged check nor a dated reminder fails**, where a check that was written and not merged is not a check. An intervention record with no entries fails, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — and the step 7 check is the accepted example, because a statement that ships its own expiry detector has built the detection the record would otherwise have to evidence.

**Neither the length of the omission list nor the size of the step 5 movement is assessed**, and the specification says so in writing. The reason is [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §7's and it is sharper here: a student rewarded for moving many items will move many, a student penalised for a long omission list will shorten it, and in an ethics audit a strategic answer and a dishonest one are the same object. The requirement is that the audit was performed and its per-item results recorded.

### Discharge of the programme's longest deferrals

**This section is the report four prior whitepapers require, and all five deferrals are discharged.**

**Deferral one — the ethical half of selective true reporting, outstanding for two years.** [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) phase 7 opened, in week thirteen, whether concealment through selection is legitimate; [WP-013](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-013.md) §8 recorded the instructor's refusal to answer as a deliberate design decision and named the cost. [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) phase 6 point 3 answered the epistemic half and stated that **"the ethical half — when selection becomes deception — is not answered today and returns at S088."** **The element exists and it is a fail condition, not a discussion.** Phase 4 point 4 states the boundary: selection becomes deception at the point where the author knows which omission would change the reader's decision and omits it without disclosing that an omission exists, with a professional floor on the knowledge condition — you are required to look, and after the first occasion on which you have been told that your omissions will not be randomly distributed, not having realised is a failure to perform a duty rather than a defence. Phase 5 step 5 makes it operational, and the specification's second hard requirement makes it binary. The obligation is discharged.

**Deferral two — the two-year instrumentation loop.** [WP-013](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-013.md) §7 committed that the cohort's phase 7 legitimacy positions would be **"recorded in aggregate and returned to the cohort at S088, which is the longest instrumentation loop in the programme,"** and [WP-013](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-013.md) §9 prediction 4 required re-elicitation and a direction of change. **The element exists:** phase 1 item 4 re-elicits the position cold, before any instruction, in the students' own words; phase 8's third item returns the week-thirteen aggregate and reports the direction of movement, **including if there is none**, which is the reading [WP-013](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-013.md) §9 specified as indicating that neither day moved anything. The obligation is discharged.

**Deferral three — the boundary [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) left open.** [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) phase 6 point 2 supplied two conditions — the omitted material available on request, and the selection serving the audience's decision — and stated that **"the boundary between that and misrepresentation is not settled today and returns at S088."** **The element exists at phase 4 point 3**, which finds [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md)'s pair necessary and insufficient, tightens availability into **recoverability** (available *and known by the reader to exist*), and adds **counterfactual disclosure** as a third condition that overrides the other two. Phase 2's bridge produces the insufficiency from the room by asking for a case satisfying both [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) conditions in which the reader was still deceived, which is answered in a minute and is the day's opening. The obligation is discharged, and **[S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md)'s stated position is amended rather than merely extended** — a whitepaper-level correction of an earlier day's taught claim, recorded here so that the amendment is legible.

**Deferral four — the unrecorded criterion.** [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §6 named this day as the destination for the question whether **"a system optimised on a criterion nobody recorded, deployed to people who never saw it, is an ethical object and not only an engineering one,"** noting that S054 deliberately did not make that argument. **The element exists at phase 3 case R3 and phase 4 point 5.** The answer is yes, and the day locates the wrong precisely: such a system fails the recoverability condition **structurally rather than through anyone's choice**, because there is nothing for the reader to ask for. The ethical failure is therefore committed at the moment of not recording, by people who were not thinking about disclosure at all, from which the day derives that **the recording duty precedes the disclosure duty** — writing down what a system is being optimised for is the precondition of every obligation the day imposes, not documentation hygiene. R3 supplies the case with no author to blame, which is what makes the location of the wrong argue-able rather than assertable. The obligation is discharged.

**Deferral five — the boundary [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) left open.** [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) phase 4 point 3 restated the question where the authority belongs to somebody else — *the set you did not say determines what they are able to decide* — and stated that "the boundary between legitimate selection and misrepresentation is still not settled and returns at S088." **The element exists at phase 4 points 3 and 4**, which settle it. This is the third day to defer the same boundary to this one, after [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) and [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md); **that three separate days across two years all named the same unwritten day is itself a finding about the design**, and it is recorded rather than treated as diligence: a question that three days can identify and none can answer is a question the curriculum had no home for until it built one. The obligation is discharged.

**Two further inbound obligations are discharged.** [WP-025](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-025.md) §6 named this day as where the promise-with-a-not-met-procedure becomes a residual-risk statement: **that is point 7 and step 3's same-field not-met requirement, with a named person.** [WP-012](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-012.md) identified the expiry-condition competence as assessed at [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) and again here: **that is step 7, by event and date with a responsible party, and it is a checked element.**

**Rule-6 discharge.** One obligation lands on this day and **the element exists.** [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) named this day as the response occasion for its step 6 and step 7 outcomes — the proportion of decisions made against the student's recommendation, and the proportion of students whose record captured the outcome in the decision-maker's own words — specifying that the residual-risk statement "must be written for a reader who decided against the engineer, and must carry one risk the student raised and lost," and requiring this paper to report whether the element exists. **It exists at phase 5 steps 1 and 2, both are checked elements, and the recipient requirement is one of the day's five hard specification requirements.** It is placed at the task's opening rather than appended because it changes the artefact's addressee, and a residual-risk statement written to a receptive reader is a different object from one written to a reader who has already overruled its author. The obligation is discharged.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions. The **step 5 audit movement**, with the triggering condition coded per item, responds at [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) phase 4, where the EA-3 perturbation set includes for every student one item from their own step 2 enumeration that did not reach step 3, asked as *why does this person not need to know that*; the cohort figure is reported at S090 phase 8. Programme instrumentation: the item 4 against week-thirteen position comparison, which is the programme's longest single loop; the item 3 omission-reason distribution; the step 5 movement by triggering condition; the item 7 against step 6 agreement rate; the phase 7 unresolved-disagreement total, which is the design's only measurement of how contestable the third condition is in practice; **and the item 8 sealed-prediction hit rate with its predicted and observed omission classes, read by the following year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md)** per instrumentation rule 9.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: a two-year question is answered in fifty minutes, on constructed materials, by a design with an interest in the answer being tidy.**

At its strongest: [WP-013](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-013.md) declined to settle this question in ten minutes in week thirteen on the ground that it was too important. The design then carried it for two years, and settles it in one phase, using a four-case corpus it commissioned, with an outcome it selected, and reaches a three-condition rule with a clean boundary sentence. **The length of the deferral has been used as evidence of the answer's seriousness.** It is not: a question is not better answered for having been postponed, and the two years bought the cohort more context and bought the design no additional warrant whatever. Meanwhile the corpus objection [WP-087](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-087.md) §8 raised applies here unchanged and the day acknowledges it — which means the day's central reversal, R2 over R4, rests on a case set constructed by the party holding the conclusion.

This is conceded in the second half and rebutted in the first.

The rebuttal: the two years are not offered as warrant. They are a **retention mechanism** — [WP-013](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-013.md) §7's aggregate, returned and re-elicited, is an instrument, and the students' week-thirteen positions in their own words are the only material in the programme capable of showing them that their view moved. The day reports the direction of movement including when there is none, which is exactly the shape of an instrument that can fail.

The concession: **the three conditions and the deception boundary are this design's own reasoning, and they are stated to students with the authority of a taught result.** Phase 4 point 4 is delivered as an answer, not as a position among positions, and the day supplies no dissenting formulation for students to argue against — no case in which the framework yields a conclusion the room would reject. **The available fix is one case.** A fifth statement, R5, in which applying the three conditions requires disclosing something that damages a third party who was not consulted, would put the framework under load and would take fifteen minutes of phase 3. It is declined because phase 3 is already at four cases and the day's payload and implicating case both need their time — and that is a scheduling reason, not a principled one. The honest position is that **the programme's longest-deferred ethical question is answered by assertion supported by a manufactured example, and the room is given no material with which to disagree.**

**Objection 2: the day carries content that a concurrent professional-formation course should have carried, and the quarter has none.**

At its strongest: Q9's four courses are CAP390, ME390, PE390 and SG390, all terminal, none running a teaching sequence. This day's phase 4 spends the majority of fifty minutes on an ethical framework — conditions on selection, a deception boundary, a professional floor, a redirected-disclosure exception with three conditions of its own — which is a course's worth of material compressed into a consolidation phase in a technical module. The [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s cross-course deferral rule would ordinarily apply, and it cannot: there is no concurrent course with a sequence to defer to, and deferring to SG390 — which assesses this material — would be deferring to a course that consumes the seminar's output rather than supplying its input.

**This is the second occurrence of a structural cost this design has now recorded twice.** [WP-069](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §8 recorded the first, where Q7's course allocation left [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) with no concurrent PE course and the day carried its governance content at seminar length. The pattern is the same and the mechanism is the same: **professional-formation content is scheduled where the technical need arises, and the PE strand's course sequence is scheduled where the credit structure allows, and the two are not aligned.** Two instances is not the programme's elevation threshold and this is recorded rather than acted on, so that a third is recognised as a third.

The rebuttal on the immediate point is weak and is stated as such. The day does compress, and the compression is visible in what phase 4 does not do: it states the redirected-disclosure exception's three conditions and does not work a case through them, which for the single most abused clause in professional practice is thin. The mitigation is that SG390 assesses that material with the boundary already drawn, so the course meets a cohort who have a position rather than an opinion.

What the design will not do is pretend the placement is optimal. **The correct structure is a concurrent PE course in Q9 supplying the ethics sequence, with the seminar day supplying the artefact and the audit.** That is a curriculum change, it is outside a whitepaper's authority, and it is argued for here as the second of two instances rather than asserted as a fix.

**Objection 3: the platform content converts an ethical finding into a compliance item, on the day least able to afford it.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn.

At its strongest: point 5 is the day's hardest argument. It says that the wrong in [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md)'s case was committed at the moment of not recording, by people who were not thinking about disclosure, and that the recording duty therefore precedes the disclosure duty. It is an argument about moral location and it took the programme two years to reach. **Point 10 then hands the room a threshold value, a split default and an instruction to write them down.** A student who leaves having learned *record your defaults* has taken a checklist item away from a moral argument, and the checklist item is the part that will survive contact with an employer, because it is the part that is cheap, legible and auditable. **The day will have converted its own deepest finding into configuration hygiene**, which is the same failure [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) named in a different register: a limitation stated in a form the reader can act on, where the acting is the wrong action.

Worse, the platform material is seductive in a way the corpus is not. A commissioned four-case set is visibly a teaching device; **an actual threshold in an actual repository is a fact, and facts crowd out arguments in a fifty-minute phase.**

The rebuttal is structural and partial. Point 10 is placed last, after point 5 has done its work and after points 3 and 4 have set the conditions; the phase 3 check runs for eight minutes and produces one finding rather than a survey; and **the specification does not assess the recording of defaults as such** — the phase 3 criterion enters the step 2 enumeration and is then subject to the same three-condition audit as every other item, so a student who records it and does not ask whether disclosing it would change their reader's decision fails the day's single binary requirement. That binding is the mechanism that keeps the platform item inside the ethics rather than beside it.

**The concession is that the design added this content late and did not restructure phase 4 around it.** Point 10 sits at the end of a phase whose argument peaks at point 5, and a fifty-minute consolidation that peaks in the middle and closes on a configuration value has its emphasis in the wrong place. **The cheap fix is a reordering and it is named: move point 10 to sit immediately after point 5, as the instance of point 5 rather than as the day's closing item, and close on point 8's disclosure exception instead.** It costs nothing, it is not made, and the reason it is not made is that reordering a consolidation phase changes what phase 5's fading assumes has been established and the design has not traced every dependency. **That is a real reason and it is not a good one**, and it is recorded here rather than quietly done so that a reader can disagree with the priority.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At item 3, self-protection will be the least-cited reason for an omission and, at phase 7, partners will identify the author's position as the beneficiary of the omission more often than authors did. *Instrument:* item 3 reasons coded into the four categories, set against phase 7's condition-two classifications for the same items. **The gap is the claim**, not either figure alone: the design predicts that authors and readers of the same omission disagree systematically about who it served.

**Prediction 2.** More than a third of groups will rank R2 first before the outcome is supplied, and the justification will appeal to usability. *Instrument:* phase 3 rankings with justifications coded. **This is the prediction the day depends on**: if the room spots R2's omission unprompted, the implicating case does not implicate and phase 4 point 3 is being taught to a room that already holds it.

**Prediction 3.** At phase 8, the cohort's item 4 positions will have moved from their week-thirteen [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) positions in the direction of a stricter standard, and the movement will be larger for students whose S013 position was permissive. *Instrument:* the [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) aggregate against item 4, coded for strictness and paired per student where the records permit. **No movement is the informative result** and is the reading [WP-013](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-013.md) §9 prediction 4 specified: it would indicate that neither [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) nor this day changed anything, and that a two-year loop bought the programme nothing.

**Prediction 4.** At step 5, the condition that most often triggers an item's return to the statement will be counterfactual disclosure rather than recoverability, and the returned items will be disproportionately those the author's item 3 reason coded as *uncertainty*. *Instrument:* step 5 movements coded by triggering condition and matched to item 3 reasons. **If recoverability dominates instead, the day's third condition is doing no work** and the framework reduces to [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md)'s pair with better wording, which would mean this whitepaper's central claim to have advanced the question is false.

**Prediction 5 — the practical turn's own, per §8 objection 3.** The inherited-guarantee heading will be the **longest** of the five in the step 2 enumerations and the **shortest-lived** in the step 3 statements — that is, students will enumerate many inherited guarantees and carry almost none of them through, and the step 5 audit will rarely mark any of them decision-changing. *Instrument:* step 2 entries and step 3 survivals coded by heading, with the step 5 condition-three answers for the inherited-guarantee items reported separately. **If that pattern holds, point 10 has been received as a recording exercise and objection 3 is correct**, and the response is the reordering the objection names. **If inherited guarantees are marked decision-changing at the same rate as the other four headings, the platform content has been integrated into the ethics rather than appended to it**, which is what the design intends and has not demonstrated.

**Prediction 6 — the frontier item.** A large majority of item 8 sealed predictions will name a *risk* as the thing the agent will drop, and a small minority will name a *qualification* — a horizon, a confidence, an interval. *Instrument:* item 8 responses coded by omission class against what each student's agent actually dropped, with the two distributions reported side by side. **The design predicts that the observed distribution is dominated by qualifications and the predicted distribution by risks**, which would mean the cohort's model of the agent is about content and the agent's actual selection is about form — a frontier finding the phase 6 case states and this instrument would measure. Its reader is next year's cohort.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That the ethics of leaving things out are not about honesty, which you already have, but about recoverability: selection becomes deception at the point where you know which omission would change the reader's decision and omit it without disclosing that an omission exists. And that the duty to write a criterion down comes before any duty to disclose it, because nothing can be asked for that was never recorded.

**What the evidence licenses.** The reporting and licensing discipline is **class 1**, grounded in [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/palantir-fde-practitioner-writing.md](/research/05-fde-craft/palantir-fde-practitioner-writing.md). The licence transition and the evaluator threshold are **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026, and no evaluator on this platform has a published agreement with human judgement. **The three conditions, the deception boundary and the precedence of the recording duty are class 3** — this design's own answer, delivered with the authority of a taught result.

**What to do with it.**

- Enumerate before you select, and keep the discarded list in front of you while you audit.
- Disclose that an omission exists even where you cannot disclose its content. Four sentences do it.
- For an unknown readership, disclose the selection rule rather than the omission's relevance; the third condition has no referent there.
- This paper's evidence status turns the day's own form on a pinned control corpus and on this programme. **Write that sentence about your own artefact** — what has been measured, and which claims therefore rest on a control existing rather than on its effect — using the five classes on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md).

**The concession worth remembering.** The programme's longest-deferred question is answered by assertion, supported by a manufactured example, and **you are given no material with which to disagree** — no case in which the three conditions yield a conclusion the room would reject. The fifteen-minute fix that would have put the framework under load was declined for a scheduling reason rather than a principled one, and the paper says so.
