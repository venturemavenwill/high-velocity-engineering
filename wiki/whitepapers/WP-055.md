## In plain terms

This occasion marks what a plan refuses to claim.

That inversion is the whole design and it needs saying plainly, because a candidate who misreads it will submit the wrong artefact. **A plan in which every threat is unmitigated is a passing plan.** If your closure criterion is evidence discoverable in the repository, and the evidence is not there yet, then unmitigated is the correct status — and **a false claim of implementation is strictly worse than a failure to notice a real one.** That instruction governs the whole occasion and it is carried directly from the material this module is built on, where it is the right instinct in an otherwise self-congratulatory design.

Eight elements are checked and none of them is about how secure the system is. **The specification cannot mark that, because nothing available to this programme supplies a standard for it**, and §8 concedes what that costs.

Three elements are unusual.

**Closure criteria must be evidence and must name their own blind spot.** A criterion is a file and a location. Every such criterion is blind to fixes that leave no trace in the repository — a setting applied in a console, a policy applied above your system — and a candidate who cannot name one has not understood their own instrument.

**The plan must name the point at which a person must agree.** In the material behind this module there is no such point anywhere. Human decisions are inputs; the one machine gate checks a machine-written field; the sign-off row is a form rather than a precondition; and the only place a person is unavoidably present is authenticating a write to a live backlog, where they are asked for a credential rather than for a judgement. **If the candidate's process has the same shape, saying so is a passing answer.** Naming the absence is worth more than inventing a gate.

**And the plan must consider content attacking the process that reads it.** The source contains no adversarial-input handling anywhere, in a chain whose entire subject is threat modelling, which is the most useful thing in it to notice.

## Abstract

This paper defends closing the threat-modelling module with an assessment of a plan's refusals rather than of its coverage, and defends a specification that marks neither completeness nor correctness. The argument is that the only property available for honest assessment is whether the plan's claims are checkable; that a status derived from evidence discoverable in the repository is checkable while a status asserted by a person is not; and that a plan whose items are all unmitigated is therefore a well-formed plan rather than a deficient one. The occasion installs a security plan with a secrets inventory, an exclusion register, an intact unmitigated list, closure criteria paired with their blind spots, a named approval point or a defended statement of its absence, and an adversarial-input entry, and closes its specification with the statement that nothing else is assessed. The paper concedes that the specification will pass a plan which is honest about having reduced no risk, that it cannot distinguish a system at the start of its work from a system in serious trouble, and that no source behind the module reports any outcome for any plan produced this way.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

An assurance artefact is produced under a pressure that runs in exactly one direction. **Everything the plan admits makes the engagement look worse, and everything it claims makes it look better**, and the person assembling it is on the delivery side.

The result is predictable and near-universal. Statuses drift towards done on the strength of an intention. The exclusion register shortens. The open questions resolve into prose. The unmitigated list — the single most useful page in the document — is the one thing removed before circulation, because it reads as an admission of failure to a reader who has not been taught to read it as an inventory of work.

**There is a second pressure which is subtler and worse.** Most assurance rubrics can be satisfied by naming a technology, because a check that asks whether a mitigation is specific usually operationalises specificity as whether a concrete technology is named. **Naming one you have not deployed scores full marks.** A candidate who has learned the rubric can raise the assessment without touching the system, and will not experience this as dishonesty.

The occasion's response is to mark the refusals, to refuse a score, and to have the assessor apply the pressure directly so that the candidate meets it once in a room where declining is safe.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The stage's affective peak, and it is not close. Submitting a document whose central table is mostly empty, to somebody who will mark it, requires trusting a specification against every instinct a delivery role installs.

**3.2 Why derived status rather than asserted status.** A person ticking a box produces a claim. A search of the workspace that finds a file and a location produces a claim somebody else can check, and one that flips back if the evidence is removed. **The second is the only form of closure that survives the author leaving**, and it is the best single idea in the material behind this module.

**3.3 Why the closure criterion must name its own blind spot.** Repository-derived closure is blind to any fix that leaves no repository trace, and such fixes are common and often correct. **A criterion whose author has not identified its blind spot will regenerate work items for problems that are already solved**, and the team will learn to ignore the process. The blind spot is therefore part of the criterion rather than a caveat about it.

**3.4 Why the approval point is asked for and its absence accepted.** The design wants the candidate to look, not to comply. **An element that required a gate would be satisfied by naming somebody who reads nothing**, which is worse than no gate because it distributes responsibility to a person who did not exercise any. Accepting *no such point exists, and here is what would have to change* keeps the element diagnostic.

**3.5 Why no score is requested and any supplied score is not read.** A score in this artefact is a document measurement wearing a system measurement's clothing, and [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) established why. **Declining to read one is the only way a specification can refuse to reward it**, since a candidate who supplies one and is marked well will conclude it helped.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 2 — what would you have deleted before sending this to a customer — is asked before instruction and submitted.** It is asked at that point so that the answer cannot be revised once the candidate learns the deletions are the assessed material. §9 prediction 1 is the measurement of whether the timing works.

**The work movement forces statuses before prose.** Prose written first bends the statuses to fit it, and the ordering is the only countermeasure available that does not depend on the candidate's discipline.

**The defence is adversarial in a specific and unusual way. The assessor offers the candidate a way to make the plan look better** — a plausible mitigation for an unmitigated item, a reasonable basis for marking a status done. **Accepting is the failure and declining with a stated evidential threshold is the strongest available answer.** This is the only element in the specification that cannot be checked from the submission, and §8 of [WP-040](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-040.md) has already conceded the class of problem that introduces; the mitigation here is the same, that the element is marked on the reasoning rather than on the outcome.

**The agent is used to attack the specification rather than the artefact.** Asking an agent to score the plan favourably and watching it succeed teaches the name-satisfiability point cheaply, on the candidate's own document, in a way no lecture does.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The tidy plan.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* opening item 2's timing, the intact-list element, and the standard's warning sign — the plan reads better than the fortnight went.

**The optimistic status.** *Countermeasure:* the statuses-before-prose ordering and the standard's warning sign — you cannot name the file.

**The technology name as a control.** *Countermeasure:* the generic-mitigation element, plus the agent-scoring exercise which demonstrates the failure rather than describing it.

**The invented gate.** *Countermeasure:* the explicit blessing of the absence answer, and the standard's warning sign — nobody would notice if the approver did not turn up.

**The buried refusal.** *Countermeasure:* opening item 5, which asks which page a reluctant reader reads, and the standard's requirement that the answer be the same page as the unmitigated list.

**Capitulation under the assessor's offer.** *Countermeasure:* none available before the occasion. **The design deliberately does not warn the candidate**, because a candidate expecting the offer declines it as a matter of form rather than of judgement, and §9 prediction 2 expects most to accept it.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**This is an assessment occasion.** It generates specification evidence and no entrustment judgement — the programme's entrustment decisions begin at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md).

Eight elements, each checked present and adequate or not: every threat names a component and a flow number; the exclusion register is present and grouped into categories rather than incidents; no mitigation is generic, whether or not the cell is filled; observed and inferred values are distinguishable where the reader meets them rather than only in working files; the unmitigated list is complete and unshortened, with every item whose implementation cannot be found marked unmitigated or unclear; every closure criterion is expressed as evidence discoverable in the repository and names one real fix it would fail to detect; the plan names the point at which a person must agree, what they are agreeing to and what is in front of them, **or states that no such point exists and what would have to change**; and an adversarial-input threat with a control class appears, or the claim that no untrusted content enters the system is defended.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Whether the system is secure is not marked. Whether anything has been implemented is not marked. The number of threats is not marked and a longer registry is not a better one. **The length of the unmitigated list is not marked in either direction, and a plan in which nothing is mitigated satisfies the fifth element.** No score is required, none is requested, and a supplied score is not read. Presentation is not marked. Whether the exclusions were the right exclusions is not marked. Whether the smallest change from [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) would have worked is not marked.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: a specification that marks neither completeness nor correctness marks nothing that matters, and will award full credit to a plan that is honest about having reduced no risk.**

At its strongest: an assurance artefact exists to make a system safer. A candidate who enumerates a handful of threats, finds no evidence for any mitigation, marks everything unmitigated and writes a long exclusion register has produced a scrupulous document and has protected nobody. **This specification passes them.** A candidate who did the harder work of actually securing something scores identically, on elements that do not distinguish them. **The occasion optimises for candour and calls it rigour, and the graduates it produces will be excellent at describing exposure and unpractised at reducing it.**

**Substantially conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to this occasion.** Three partial answers, none complete. The unmitigated list is an assessed element stated at full length, so an empty plan is visibly empty rather than quietly passed — the emptiness is published, not hidden. The closure-criterion element requires each criterion to be operable and to name its blind spot, which is work that cannot be done without understanding what a real mitigation would look like. And the assessor's offer tests directly whether the candidate would inflate under pressure, which is the disposition the objection worries about. **The declined fix is to add an element marking whether the plan's coverage or mitigation is adequate.** It is declined for the reason [WP-040](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-040.md) §8 declines its analogue and [WP-053](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §8 declines its own: **nothing available to this programme supplies a basis.** There is no outcome evidence for any plan produced this way, no calibration of any evaluator against human judgement, and no comparison against a human-authored model. The element would be the marker's opinion in a specification's clothing, and this stage spends [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) teaching learners to detect exactly that. **The gap is real, it is stated, and the design accepts it rather than filling it with something unwarranted.**

**Objection 2: requiring an approval point manufactures a control the industry does not staff, and the element will be satisfied by naming somebody who rubber-stamps.**

At its strongest: a gate whose approver reads nothing is worse than no gate, because it converts an absence of review into a record of review and gives everybody downstream a name to point at. Asking a candidate with no organisational authority to produce one guarantees the nominal version. **The element manufactures the laundering of responsibility.**

**Conceded, and the element is written to accept the objection's own conclusion.** It does not require a gate to exist. It requires the candidate to look for one and to report what they found, and **stating that no such point exists — with what would have to change — is explicitly a passing answer**, blessed in the session text rather than left to marker discretion. That is the diagnostic form of the element and it is the form the design wants, because the honest answer for most real processes is the absence. **The declined fix is to require the approval point to exist.** It is declined because a curriculum cannot require an organisational control the candidate has no authority to install, and because a required gate is satisfied by a form — which is precisely the failure the source demonstrates with its reviewer row of blanks. **The residual risk, that a candidate names a nominal approver to look compliant, is real and the standard's warning sign is the only countermeasure.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most candidates will shorten the unmitigated list between their answer to opening item 2 and the submitted plan. *Instrument:* the submitted opening answers against the plan's contents, compared per candidate by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most candidates will accept the assessor's offered improvement. *Instrument:* the defence recorded as *accepted* / *declined*, with the evidential threshold named where declined.

**Prediction 3.** Named closure blind spots will be restatements of the criterion more often than specific undetectable fixes. *Instrument:* each blind spot coded *specific fix* / *restatement* / *absent* by a second reader.

**Prediction 4.** An agent asked to score a submitted plan favourably will do so without regard to whether any mitigation is implemented. *Instrument:* the agent's assessment against the plan's own status column, paired per candidate. **If this fails — if the agent's assessment tracks implementation — the session's claim that such rubrics are satisfiable by naming has no support at this scale and the work movement's final exercise should be removed.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the only honestly assessable property of an assurance plan is whether its claims are checkable; that a status derived from repository evidence is checkable and one asserted by a person is not; and that the page most likely to be deleted before circulation is the page worth marking.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about safety. **No source behind this module reports any outcome for any plan produced this way**, and this occasion marks form and honesty because it has no basis for marking anything else.

**The concession worth remembering.** This specification cannot tell an honest empty plan from a system in trouble, and it cannot tell either from a system at the start of its work. If you leave the module able to make that distinction yourself, you have acquired something the assessment could not test.
