## In plain terms

This occasion marks what a plan refuses to claim.

That inversion is the whole design and it needs saying plainly, because a candidate who misreads it will submit the wrong artefact. **A plan in which every threat is unmitigated is a passing plan.** If your closure criterion is evidence discoverable in the repository, and the evidence is not there yet, then unmitigated is the correct status — and **a false claim of implementation is strictly worse than a failure to notice a real one.** That instruction governs the whole occasion and it is carried directly from the material this module is built on, where it is the right instinct in an otherwise self-congratulatory design.

**The openly published worked threat model in the vendor's own engineering handbook marks every one of its threats not mitigated**, and the note on that handbook draws the teaching consequence: do not penalise a team for it, because an honest unmitigated list is worth more than a fabricated mitigated one. **That is the closest thing to external support this rule has, and it is a genre exemplar rather than a finding.** The rule itself — that the list must survive review — is **a design choice with no external warrant**, and §3.3 says so before §8 is reached.

Twelve elements are checked and none of them is about how secure the system is. **The specification cannot mark that, because nothing available to this programme supplies a standard for it**, and §8 concedes what that costs.

Five elements are unusual.

**Closure criteria must be evidence and must name their own blind spot.** A criterion is a file and a location. Every such criterion is blind to fixes that leave no trace in the repository — a setting applied in a console, a policy applied above your system — and a candidate who cannot name one has not understood their own instrument.

**One control must be enforced at a named point, and its single verification described as single.** The gateway is where a control stops being intended and starts either passing a request or not. **A control verified once is not a verified control**, and the plan has to say which of its controls have been observed more than once.

**The plan must name the point at which a person must agree.** In the material behind this module there is no such point anywhere. Human decisions are inputs; the one machine gate checks a machine-written field; the sign-off row is a form rather than a precondition; and the only place a person is unavoidably present is authenticating a write to a live backlog, where they are asked for a credential rather than for a judgement. **If the candidate's process has the same shape, saying so is a passing answer.** Naming the absence is worth more than inventing a gate.

**And the plan must consider content attacking the process that reads it.** The source contains no adversarial-input handling anywhere, in a chain whose entire subject is threat modelling, which is the most useful thing in it to notice.

**And every unmitigated entry must be marked *sourced* or *inferred*.** A gap the vendor states in its own documentation and a gap the candidate reasoned to are both legitimate and are not the same kind of claim. **The mark is assessed; neither value scores better than the other; an unmarked entry fails the element.** The reason is that an unmitigated list is the part of the plan a reader is most inclined to dismiss, and a cited boundary changes the question from *is that really a gap* to *do you accept it* — which is the conversation the artefact exists to make possible.

## Abstract

This paper defends closing the threat-modelling module with an assessment of a plan's refusals rather than of its coverage, and defends a specification that marks neither completeness nor correctness.

**First**, the case for marking checkability. The only property available for honest assessment is whether the plan's claims can be checked by somebody else. A status derived from evidence discoverable in the repository is checkable and flips back if the evidence is removed; a status asserted by a person is not. **A plan whose items are all unmitigated is therefore a well-formed plan rather than a deficient one**, and the published worked threat model in the vendor's own handbook is exactly that.

**Second**, the case for a named enforcement point. The gateway documentation supplies the clearest available distinction between a control that is enforced and a control that is intended, along with an argument worth teaching independently of the product: **a shared finite resource with no per-consumer accounting will be consumed by whichever consumer is least careful.** Requiring one enforced control beside the unmitigated ones puts both on the same page in the candidate's own handwriting.

**Third**, the case for treating a verification as a sample. Evaluation statistics is unambiguous that a measurement without a stated uncertainty is a number pretending to be a fact, that observations drawn in related groups violate independence and make a naive reading over-confident, and that power analysis tells you in advance whether an instrument can detect what you care about. **Controls configured together and verified together are close to one observation wearing several hats.** The transfer from model evaluation to control verification is this programme's analogy and is labelled as one.

**Fourth**, the case for saying out loud what the rule costs. **An unmitigated list survives only where naming an unfixed risk is safe**, which the psychological-safety literature establishes as a property of an environment rather than of a person. Requiring the disposition of an individual while the environment determines whether it is exercisable is a known limitation this occasion names and does not solve.

The paper concedes four things. That the specification will pass a plan which is honest about having reduced no risk. That it cannot distinguish a system at the start of its work from a system in serious trouble. That **no source behind the module reports any outcome for any plan produced this way.** And that the rule the occasion is named for — that the unmitigated list must survive — is a design choice with no external warrant, marked against anyway.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

An assurance artefact is produced under a pressure that runs in exactly one direction. **Everything the plan admits makes the engagement look worse, and everything it claims makes it look better**, and the person assembling it is on the delivery side.

The result is predictable and near-universal. Statuses drift towards done on the strength of an intention. The exclusion register shortens. The open questions resolve into prose. The unmitigated list — the single most useful page in the document — is the one thing removed before circulation, because it reads as an admission of failure to a reader who has not been taught to read it as an inventory of work.

**There is a second pressure which is subtler and worse.** Most assurance rubrics can be satisfied by naming a technology, because a check that asks whether a mitigation is specific usually operationalises specificity as whether a concrete technology is named. **Naming one you have not deployed scores full marks.** A candidate who has learned the rubric can raise the assessment without touching the system, and will not experience this as dishonesty.

The occasion's response is to mark the refusals, to refuse a score, and to have the assessor apply the pressure directly so that the candidate meets it once in a room where declining is safe.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The stage's affective peak, and it is not close. Submitting a document whose central table is mostly empty, to somebody who will mark it, requires trusting a specification against every instinct a delivery role installs.

**3.2 Why derived status rather than asserted status.** A person ticking a box produces a claim. A search of the workspace that finds a file and a location produces a claim somebody else can check, and one that flips back if the evidence is removed. **The second is the only form of closure that survives the author leaving**, and it is the best single idea in the material behind this module ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md)). Its governing instruction is carried whole into the specification: **when in doubt, classify as unclear, because a false claim of implementation is worse than a false negative.** That source is internal and access-controlled: paraphrased here, quoted nowhere, attributable to nothing, and **supplying no external warrant for any claim.**

**3.3 The rule this occasion is named for has no external warrant, and the paper says so before §8.**

Requiring the unmitigated list to survive review is a design choice. **No source read for this programme measures whether plans with intact risk lists lead to more fixed vulnerabilities, better decisions, or anything else.** The argument is that a plan whose risk list is empty at sign-off has been edited to look finished, and that an artefact edited to look finished has stopped being an instrument. That is an argument, and the occasion marks against it anyway.

The closest thing to support is a genre exemplar rather than a finding. The openly published engineering handbook's worked threat model — architecture diagram, asset table, dataflow narrative, enumerated threats, properties table — **marks every one of its threats "Not Mitigated"**, and the note on it draws the consequence: **do not penalise a team for it, because an honest unmitigated list is worth more than a fabricated mitigated one** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md)). Its second instructive habit is that assumptions are stated separately from threats, arriving at [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md)'s side-channel discipline independently.

**Its limits are severe and are used as material rather than hidden.** The handbook cannot be dated as a whole; its page footers span years with nothing in the navigation to distinguish them. **It contradicts itself in at least a dozen places without noticing.** Its own threat-modelling page is a signpost rather than a treatment. The worked example's defects are clerical. And it **asserts four magnitudes it never warrants, recorded in this repository as specimens and reproduced nowhere.**

**3.4 Keeping the list intact is a voice behaviour, and voice behaviour is conditional on safety.**

What the occasion asks — write down, in a document that goes to a customer, the things you have not fixed — is speaking up when silence is cheaper. Edmondson's opening case is a neonatal nurse who notices a missing prophylactic order, steps forward and stops, because she had recently seen a physician **"publicly berate another nurse for questioning one of his orders"**; her reading is a quick, **"not entirely conscious, risk calculation"** overweighting the immediate interpersonal cost against a delayed and more important consequence ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)). She generalises it as the avoidance of three interpersonal risks: looking ignorant, incompetent or disruptive.

**The consequence for this occasion is uncomfortable and is stated in the session as well as here. If keeping an unmitigated list intact is conditional on safety, then requiring it of an individual treats an environmental variable as a personal virtue.** A graduate who has learned the disposition and joins a team where an empty risk register is what gets approved has been given a capability they cannot exercise. **The occasion does not solve this.** Its only structural response is to mark the *derivation* of the statuses rather than the courage of the author — a derived status is defensible to a hostile reader in a way a brave one is not.

The quantitative synthesis is why this is treated as established rather than as folklore. It aggregates a large body of independent samples and, beyond pooling, **ranks antecedents by relative strength**, tests **incremental validity** over adjacent constructs such as positive leader relations and work engagement, tests **moderation by research design and national culture**, and tests **homology** across individual and group levels ([research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)). The incremental-validity test is the one that matters for a sceptical curriculum: if the construct were a proxy for having a good manager, it would not warrant separate attention.

**Both sources are read shallowly and the depths differ.** The book was read in full and is trade press: **its case studies are drawn almost entirely from secondary sources** — published teaching cases, journalism, official investigation reports, a documentary — rather than from original fieldwork, and its prescriptive third part is a leadership framework. **The 1999 empirical article that defined the construct has not been read for this repository**; the book's account of it is a summary written two decades later by the same author, and the note keeps the two apart at every point. **The meta-analysis note is written from the official abstract; the full text is closed access and was not read.** The *structure* of its contribution is verified and **no effect size from it is asserted here or anywhere in this wiki.**

**3.5 Why the closure criterion must name its own blind spot.** Repository-derived closure is blind to any fix that leaves no repository trace, and such fixes are common and often correct. **A criterion whose author has not identified its blind spot will regenerate work items for problems that are already solved**, and the team will learn to ignore the process. The source design names exactly this failure among its three caveats — alongside the facts that nothing schedules the re-run and that the acceptance criteria were written by the same model as the mitigation. The blind spot is therefore part of the criterion rather than a caveat about it.

**3.6 Why one verification is not verification.**

Every closure status rests on an observation, and the statistics of evaluation are unforgiving about what one observation licenses ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). That paper's diagnosis of prevailing practice — results reported with a **"highest number is best" mentality**, a figure highlighted in bold and never tested against noise — describes assurance reporting as accurately as it describes model evaluation.

Three of its moves transfer. **A measurement without a stated uncertainty is a number pretending to be a fact**, and the conditions under which a control was observed are a sample of the conditions under which it will operate. **Observations drawn in related groups violate independence**, so a naive reading understates the true uncertainty — and controls configured by one script and verified together are close to one observation wearing several hats. And **power analysis reframes the enterprise from reporting to planning**: given what you want to detect, the arithmetic tells you in advance whether the instrument can detect it, which lets you discover before you build it that **a verification procedure incapable in principle of detecting the failure it exists to detect is a ceremony.**

**Its limits:** analytical rather than empirical, and aimed at language-model evaluation rather than at security controls. Its recommendations are derivations; its illustrative magnitudes are computed from stylised distributional assumptions and labelled as such by the author. **The transfer to control verification is this programme's analogy rather than the author's claim, and no figure from it appears anywhere in this wiki.**

**3.7 Why the plan needs one enforced control rather than a plan full of intended ones.**

The gateway documentation supplies the sharpest available distinction between enforcement and intention, and the framing sentence carries the argument: the AI gateway **"extends API Management's existing API gateway"** and **"is not a separate offering"** — which asserts that AI traffic is API traffic with different resource semantics rather than a new category ([research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md)).

Four mechanisms are durable past the product. **A shared finite resource with no per-consumer accounting will be consumed by whichever consumer is least careful** — which is why a limit needs a counter key. **Precalculating at the edge is fail-fast**: reject before paying to discover the rejection. **Managed identity eliminates a long-lived secret rather than storing it better.** And **metric dimensions are what make spend attributable**, which is what makes governance possible at all.

Two cautions the documentation does not supply and the session does. **Semantic caching's similarity threshold is a correctness knob** — set loosely, the system answers a question that was not asked, silently and plausibly. And **logging prompts and completions creates a privacy surface the documentation does not discuss**, while recommending the logging for billing and auditing.

**Its limits:** first-party product documentation with a commercial frame. **It argues for a gateway and does not discuss the latency, cost or operational burden the gateway itself adds. No benchmarks, no latency figures, no cost measurements, no comparison against other products.** Capability availability **"varies by API Management service tier"**. Policy names, tier availability and preview status change frequently. **Its architectural reasoning is sound and asserted rather than demonstrated.**

**3.8 Why the assessment is a conversation with counterfactuals, and why it produces a reason rather than a mark.**

The frame is entrustment. The mechanism for probing whether a learner holds an activity is the **entrustment-based discussion** — a short focused conversation after the activity which **"should probe the student's deep understanding of what was done, but should include 'what-if' questions, to grasp whether the learner would know what to do"** in unexpected situations ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). The what-if requirement is what distinguishes it from a walkthrough: **retrospective explanation evidences the case the learner met; counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases they did not.** The frame is explicit that an entrustment decision implies accepting risk, because nobody can predict what a learner will encounter — which is precisely why the defence asks what a closure criterion would *fail* to detect.

The second half explains the written reason. Studies of assessors reading competency portfolios found they **relied mainly on narrative feedback**, and that **"grades and qualifications were merely used to confirm impressions based on narratives"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). The same work found assessors reaching **similar final judgements by different reasoning**, and being **"inclined to stick to their initial judgments even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence."** The design consequence the authors draw is that high-stakes decisions **should not be made individually but should result from group discussion**, because deliberation surfaces the divergent reasoning that identical outcomes conceal.

**Their limits:** both are medical-education research, where supervision, observation and the consequences of error are differently shaped. The entrustment source is a primer; its supervision scale and evidence standards are built for clinical training and **its transfer to engineering is this programme's extrapolation rather than the author's claim.** The narrative-assessment note **rests on two open-access studies substituted for several paywalled ones that were not consulted**, and the substitution is recorded deliberately. **Both are used for the shape of the instrument and for no magnitude.**

**3.9 Why the approval point is asked for and its absence accepted.** The design wants the candidate to look, not to comply. **An element that required a gate would be satisfied by naming somebody who reads nothing**, which is worse than no gate because it distributes responsibility to a person who did not exercise any. Accepting *no such point exists, and here is what would have to change* keeps the element diagnostic.

**3.10 Why no score is requested and any supplied score is not read.** A score in this artefact is a document measurement wearing a system measurement's clothing, and [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) established why. **Declining to read one is the only way a specification can refuse to reward it**, since a candidate who supplies one and is marked well will conclude it helped.

**3.11 Why the unmitigated list is now marked for provenance, and why the mark rather than the value is assessed.**

The occasion's rule has always been that the list survives review. **The addition is that a reader can tell where each entry came from.**

The argument is about what an unmitigated list does when it reaches somebody who did not write it. A list of practitioner judgements invites one response — *is that a real gap or are you being cautious* — and the author is then defending their temperament. **A list containing entries cited to a vendor's own statement of what its control does not cover invites a different response**, because the gap is no longer in dispute and the only remaining question is acceptance. That is the conversation the plan exists to make possible, and it is available because this class of documentation is unusually candid about its boundaries while saying nothing about its efficacy ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-365-and-agent-identity.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-365-and-agent-identity.md)).

The material supports it concretely and the session enumerates the instances: interaction types that are audited and not preventable; a control whose subject is not informed that it fired; generated content that does not inherit its sources' classification; runtime protection with a stated boundary excluding agents that do not traverse the sanctioned tool layer; discovery implemented as a list of named applications on managed devices; an inventory with no published completeness measure; and the enforcement trade by which enabling a blocking rule stops near-real-time alerts being generated for the covered agent. **That last one is the most valuable single entry available to this occasion, because the gap is produced by using the control correctly and cannot be closed by configuring anything better.** A candidate who records it with its monitoring consequence has written the sentence that stops a team reading a quiet queue as safety. The build and knowledge layers supply two more of the same shape — the total guardrail override and the opt-in permission default, both traced at [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md).

**The mark is assessed and the value is not, deliberately.** Assessing the value would reward sourcing over reasoning, which is wrong in both directions: it would push candidates to omit real gaps that no vendor states, and to over-claim citations for gaps that are only adjacent to what a page says. **Both failures are predicted in §9.** What the element actually requires is that the candidate know which kind of claim they are making, which is the same discipline §3.2 requires of a mitigation status and §3.5 requires of a closure criterion. **This occasion now asks it of the refusals as well as of the claims.**

One caution belongs in the element rather than in a footnote. **A citation to a coverage boundary is a citation to a page on a date**, and this material is among the most perishable in the repository — generally available for a short time only when read, substantially preview or early-access, with a dated migration inside this material's life and roughly thirty supporting pages read as search excerpts rather than fetched in full. **An entry marked sourced against a page that has since changed carries a mark that has quietly become false**, which is [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md)'s defect arriving inside the artefact this occasion assesses. The element therefore requires the date beside the citation, and the specification does not require the candidate to have re-checked it — only to have made re-checking possible.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The eight sections run situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits — with the exercises doubling as the assessment.

**Opening item 2 — what would you have deleted before sending this to a customer — is asked before instruction and submitted.** It is asked at that point so that the answer cannot be revised once the candidate learns the deletions are the assessed material. §9 prediction 1 is the measurement of whether the timing works.

**The workflow forces statuses before prose.** Prose written first bends the statuses to fit it, and the ordering is the only countermeasure available that does not depend on the candidate's discipline.

**The defence is adversarial in a specific and unusual way. The assessor offers the candidate a way to make the plan look better** — a plausible mitigation for an unmitigated item, a reasonable basis for marking a status done. **Accepting is the failure and declining with a stated evidential threshold is the strongest available answer.** This is the only element in the specification that cannot be checked from the submission, and §8 of [WP-040](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-040.md) has already conceded the class of problem that introduces; the mitigation here is the same, that the element is marked on the reasoning rather than on the outcome.

**The agent is used to attack the specification rather than the artefact.** Asking an agent to score the plan favourably and watching it succeed teaches the name-satisfiability point cheaply, on the candidate's own document, in a way no lecture does.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The tidy plan.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* opening item 2's timing, the intact-list element, and the standard's warning sign — the plan reads better than the fortnight went.

**The optimistic status.** *Countermeasure:* the statuses-before-prose ordering and the standard's warning sign — you cannot name the file.

**The technology name as a control.** *Countermeasure:* the generic-mitigation element, plus the agent-scoring exercise which demonstrates the failure rather than describing it.

**The invented gate.** *Countermeasure:* the explicit blessing of the absence answer, and the standard's warning sign — nobody would notice if the approver did not turn up.

**The buried refusal.** *Countermeasure:* opening item 5, which asks which page a reluctant reader reads, and the standard's requirement that the answer be the same page as the unmitigated list.

**Capitulation under the assessor's offer.** *Countermeasure:* none available before the occasion. **The design deliberately does not warn the candidate**, because a candidate expecting the offer declines it as a matter of form rather than of judgement, and §9 prediction 2 expects most to accept it.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**This is an assessment occasion.** It generates specification evidence and no entrustment judgement — the programme's entrustment decisions begin at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md).

Twelve elements, each checked present and adequate or not:

1. Every threat names a component and a flow number.
2. The exclusion register is present and grouped into categories rather than incidents.
3. No mitigation is generic, whether or not the cell is filled.
4. Every control names a surface, a living owner and a verification method, and the three-way accounting reconciles.
5. Observed and inferred values are distinguishable where the reader meets them, rather than only in working files.
6. The authorisation trace names every step, including those where nothing is compared, and the revocation window is stated as measured or unmeasured and never as zero.
7. The unmitigated list is complete and unshortened, with every item whose implementation cannot be found marked unmitigated or unclear.
8. One control is enforced at a named point, with the enforcement written into the plan and its single verification described as single.
9. Every closure criterion is expressed as evidence discoverable in the repository and names one real fix it would fail to detect.
10. The plan names the point at which a person must agree, what they are agreeing to and what is in front of them — **or states that no such point exists and what would have to change.**
11. An adversarial-input threat with a control class appears, or the claim that no untrusted content enters the system is defended.
12. Every entry on the unmitigated list is marked *sourced* or *inferred*, and every sourced entry names what it is sourced to and the date that source was read. **Neither mark scores better than the other. An unmarked entry fails the element, and so does a sourced mark whose citation does not support the gap as written.**

Each element is recorded as a written reason rather than a mark, for the reason §3.8 gives: assessors of complex evidence rely on narrative and use scores only to confirm impressions already formed, so the useful information is *why* the assessor judged as they did. **The reason is disclosable to the candidate and may be argued with**, which is the only available countermeasure to the finding that assessors tend to hold their initial judgements against disconfirming evidence.

Whether the system is secure is not marked. Whether anything has been implemented is not marked. The number of threats is not marked and a longer registry is not a better one. **The length of the unmitigated list is not marked in either direction, and a plan in which nothing is mitigated satisfies the seventh element.** No score is required, none is requested, and a supplied score is not read. Presentation is not marked. Whether the exclusions were the right exclusions is not marked. Whether the smallest change from [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) would have worked is not marked. Whether a revocation window was measured, as opposed to whether its absence was stated honestly, is not marked.

**Nothing else is assessed.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: a specification that marks neither completeness nor correctness marks nothing that matters, and will award full credit to a plan that is honest about having reduced no risk.**

At its strongest: an assurance artefact exists to make a system safer. A candidate who enumerates a handful of threats, finds no evidence for any mitigation, marks everything unmitigated and writes a long exclusion register has produced a scrupulous document and has protected nobody. **This specification passes them.** A candidate who did the harder work of actually securing something scores identically, on elements that do not distinguish them. **The occasion optimises for candour and calls it rigour, and the graduates it produces will be excellent at describing exposure and unpractised at reducing it.**

**Substantially conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to this occasion.** Three partial answers, none complete. The unmitigated list is an assessed element stated at full length, so an empty plan is visibly empty rather than quietly passed — the emptiness is published, not hidden. The closure-criterion element requires each criterion to be operable and to name its blind spot, which is work that cannot be done without understanding what a real mitigation would look like. And the assessor's offer tests directly whether the candidate would inflate under pressure, which is the disposition the objection worries about. **The declined fix is to add an element marking whether the plan's coverage or mitigation is adequate.** It is declined for the reason [WP-040](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-040.md) §8 declines its analogue and [WP-053](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §8 declines its own: **nothing available to this programme supplies a basis.** There is no outcome evidence for any plan produced this way, no calibration of any evaluator against human judgement, and no comparison against a human-authored model. The element would be the marker's opinion in a specification's clothing, and this stage spends [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) teaching learners to detect exactly that. **The gap is real, it is stated, and the design accepts it rather than filling it with something unwarranted.**

**Objection 2: requiring an approval point manufactures a control the industry does not staff, and the element will be satisfied by naming somebody who rubber-stamps.**

At its strongest: a gate whose approver reads nothing is worse than no gate, because it converts an absence of review into a record of review and gives everybody downstream a name to point at. Asking a candidate with no organisational authority to produce one guarantees the nominal version. **The element manufactures the laundering of responsibility.**

**Conceded, and the element is written to accept the objection's own conclusion.** It does not require a gate to exist. It requires the candidate to look for one and to report what they found, and **stating that no such point exists — with what would have to change — is explicitly a passing answer**, blessed in the session text rather than left to marker discretion. That is the diagnostic form of the element and it is the form the design wants, because the honest answer for most real processes is the absence. **The declined fix is to require the approval point to exist.** It is declined because a curriculum cannot require an organisational control the candidate has no authority to install, and because a required gate is satisfied by a form — which is precisely the failure the source demonstrates with its reviewer row of blanks. **The residual risk, that a candidate names a nominal approver to look compliant, is real and the standard's warning sign is the only countermeasure.**

**Objection 3: the occasion requires a disposition whose precondition it does not supply, and marking an individual on it is close to negligent.**

At its strongest: §3.4 concedes that keeping an unmitigated list intact is voice behaviour and that voice behaviour is conditional on psychological safety, which is a property of a team rather than of a person. The occasion then marks candidates on producing it. **A candidate who joins a team where an empty risk register is what gets approved has been trained into a habit that will cost them**, and the programme has installed the disposition without helping them assess when exercising it is survivable. The strongest form of this objection is that it could cause real harm to a junior engineer.

**Partially conceded, and the concession is the same one [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md) §8 makes about rehearsed refusal — which means this programme has now made it twice and solved it neither time.** The narrow defence is that the occasion marks the *derivation* of a status rather than the courage of the author: a status that is unmitigated because no evidence was found is defensible to a hostile reader in a way a status that is unmitigated because the author was brave is not. **A derived status is a smaller act of voice than an opinion**, and that is a real if partial mitigation.

**The declined fix was to add an element assessing whether the candidate had judged the safety of their own environment before publishing the list.** It is declined on two grounds. It would require the assessor to evaluate a claim about an organisation they cannot observe. And it would convert a genuine environmental constraint into another thing the candidate is marked on, which compounds rather than relieves the problem the objection names. **The cost is that this module ends by rewarding a behaviour whose exercisability it does not control, and this paper records that as an accepted cost rather than a solved problem.**

**Objection 4: the platform half is decorative — one enforced control does not change a plan, and the gateway is there to give the occasion a product to point at.**

At its strongest: the specification's eighth element asks for a single enforced control out of however many the plan contains. **One control does not make a system safer, and requiring exactly one invites the candidate to pick the easiest.** Meanwhile the gateway documentation is first-party marketing-adjacent material with no benchmarks and no comparison against anything, and its capability set varies by service tier, so a candidate on the wrong tier cannot complete the element at all.

**Conceded in part, and the element is deliberately scoped smaller than the objection assumes.** It does not claim the enforced control improves the system. **Its purpose is contrastive: to put one enforced control and several intended ones on the same page in the candidate's own handwriting**, so that the difference between a control that either passes a request or does not, and a control that is written down, becomes a thing they have seen rather than a thing they have been told. Picking the easiest control is not a defect; it is the point, because the easiest enforced control is still categorically different from the most elaborate intended one.

**The declined fix was to require a proportion of controls to be enforced.** It is declined because the proportion would be a number this programme has no basis for, which is the fault §3.6 and [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) exist to teach against. **On the tier objection: a candidate who cannot enforce the control because their tier does not support the mechanism records that as the finding**, which is a passing answer under the same logic as the absent approval point — and is arguably the more instructive outcome, because it names a real constraint the plan otherwise would not have surfaced.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most candidates will shorten the unmitigated list between their answer to opening item 2 and the submitted plan. *Instrument:* the submitted opening answers against the plan's contents, compared per candidate by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most candidates will accept the assessor's offered improvement. *Instrument:* the defence recorded as *accepted* / *declined*, with the evidential threshold named where declined.

**Prediction 3.** Named closure blind spots will be restatements of the criterion more often than specific undetectable fixes. *Instrument:* each blind spot coded *specific fix* / *restatement* / *absent* by a second reader.

**Prediction 4.** An agent asked to score a submitted plan favourably will do so without regard to whether any mitigation is implemented. *Instrument:* the agent's assessment against the plan's own status column, paired per candidate. **If this fails — if the agent's assessment tracks implementation — the session's claim that such rubrics are satisfiable by naming has no support at this scale and the workflow's final step should be removed.**

**Prediction 5.** Most candidates will describe their single control verification as though it established a general property, until prompted. *Instrument:* the submitted plan's verification language, coded *conditions stated* / *unconditioned* by a second reader, before and after exercise 3's prompt.

**Prediction 6.** A minority of candidates will notice that several of their controls were configured together and verified together, and are therefore closer to one observation than to several. *Instrument:* the plan's verification section, coded for whether the grouping is acknowledged. **This is the weakest link in the occasion's statistical argument** and if the minority is very small the transfer in §3.6 is too abstract to teach in this position and should move to a measurement session.

**Prediction 7.** Candidates whose plans state that no approval point exists will outnumber those who name one, and a minority of those who name one will name a person who reads nothing. *Instrument:* the submitted plans, coded *absence stated* / *substantive gate* / *nominal gate* by a second reader who is permitted to ask the candidate what the approver reads.

**Prediction 8.** Candidates instructed to compare their item-2 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 4) will retain more of their own uncomfortable material in the submitted plan than candidates who read the same criterion without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* the opening answer against the plan, coded for retention, across two cohorts — or, absent two cohorts, a within-cohort split on completion of exercise 4. **This is the occasion's only direct test of the mechanism its opening rests on.**

**Prediction 9.** The elements a second assessor disagrees on will cluster on 3, 9 and 11 — generic mitigation, closure blind spot, and adversarial input — rather than on the structural elements. *Instrument:* double-marking of a sample, with disagreements recorded by element. **This predicts the failure of the specification's own claim to be checkable without domain expertise**, and if it holds those three elements need either a worked reference or removal, because an element two assessors read differently is a marker's opinion in a specification's clothing — which is the fault [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) teaches candidates to detect.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the only honestly assessable property of an assurance plan is whether its claims are checkable; that a status derived from repository evidence is checkable and one asserted by a person is not; that one enforced control beside several intended ones teaches a distinction no lecture does; and that the page most likely to be deleted before circulation is the page worth marking.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about safety. **No source behind this module reports any outcome for any plan produced this way**, and this occasion marks form and honesty because it has no basis for marking anything else. **The rule the occasion is named for has no external warrant at all** — §3.3 says so — and the closest thing to support is a published worked example that happens to mark all its threats not mitigated. The psychological-safety material establishes that the disposition is environmentally conditioned and is read here at two shallow depths, one of them an abstract. The statistical argument is an analogy from a different field.

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper.** That holds for the meta-analysis, whose effect sizes are not asserted; for the evaluation-statistics paper, whose worked figures are not reproduced; and for the engineering handbook, which asserts four magnitudes it never warrants.

**The concession worth remembering.** This specification cannot tell an honest empty plan from a system in trouble, and it cannot tell either from a system at the start of its work. **If you leave the module able to make that distinction yourself, you have acquired something the assessment could not test** — and if you leave it able to keep an unmitigated list intact in a room that would rather you did not, you have acquired something the assessment could not give you.
