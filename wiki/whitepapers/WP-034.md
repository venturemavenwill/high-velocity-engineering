# WP-034 — Data You Were Promised and Data You Have

## In plain terms

At discovery somebody told you the data exists, the field is populated, the history goes back years and an extract can be arranged. Four claims, none checked, and the one that fails is usually the one nobody bothered to state.

This session's artefact keeps the promise and the reality in separate columns and refuses to reconcile them. That is [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md)'s discipline — observed, reported, inferred — applied to a system rather than to a person, and the reason is identical: **a record that merges them cannot be checked by anybody who was not there.**

Three things make the session harder than it sounds.

**The right informant is almost never the person who invited you.** The sponsor knows the data exists. The maintainer knows what is in it. The person who receives the complaints knows the three ways it lies, and can tell you immediately, without notice, if you ask.

**Access is not permission and permission is not purpose.** Being able to read something establishes none of the other two, and the purpose question — what was this collected for, and does that cover this — belongs to somebody whose name you probably do not have yet.

**And you may well not be allowed to look.** The openly published playbook that this programme draws on states, for engagements of exactly this kind, that production data is not to be accessed directly. That is a constraint on how you satisfy the session, not an exemption from it — and **a learner who can obtain nothing at all has produced the session's most useful finding**, provided they record it as a fact rather than as an apology.

The platform workflow supplies the cheapest honest instrument available for the gap. **An indexer configured not to stop on failures will tell you how many documents it processed, how many it could not, and why** — and the failure list is the finding. A corpus described as one size that indexes as another, with a list of reasons attached, has answered a feasibility question nobody had stated, in an afternoon, before anything was committed to. **The session's other platform observation is more general and matters more: a transformation carries what it was told to carry and drops the rest in silence.**

## Abstract

This paper defends treating data availability as a feasibility question resolved by inspection rather than as a dependency resolved by assurance. The argument is that every clause in a data promise is a separate claim, that the failing clause is typically the unstated one, and that the informant capable of falsifying a promise is systematically different from the informant who made it. The session installs a record separating promised from held from inspected, a routing of provenance, correctness, labelling, permission and purpose to three different roles, and a treatment of missing dependencies as design findings rather than as risks.

Three literatures supply what the practice sources cannot. **The published failure taxonomy for grounded systems** locates the first failure point in the corpus rather than in the model, and states that validation is only feasible during operation — which bounds what any pre-build data record can claim. **The production-systems literature** supplies the vocabulary that turns a data complaint into a locatable defect: drift against skew, the three skew types, and encoding ambiguity as a defect with the same status as a null. **The field-research literature** supplies the mechanism by which an honest description of a dataset is nonetheless unreliable, and the four principles of enquiry that repair it.

The paper concedes that the practice corpus's silence on data establishes only that the corpus is silent; that the session is built largely on that inference; that the rules actually governing a learner's access belong to jurisdictions and customers this programme does not know; and that its own largest claim — that the promised-versus-actual gap is the commonest cause of a wrong feasibility finding — rests on three converging absences and no measurement.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A design rests on data, and the team's knowledge of that data is a sentence somebody said in a room.

The sentence is rarely false. It is incomplete in a specific and repeating way: the speaker describes the data as it is understood by the part of the organisation that commissioned the work, which is usually the part furthest from it. The field exists and is populated with a default. The history goes back years and changed shape in the middle. The extract can be arranged and requires an approval nobody present has ever sought. **Each of those is a true sentence with a fatal omission, and the omission is invisible to the speaker.**

There is a second failure that is not about the customer at all. A team can hold a schema, a data dictionary and a dashboard, and believe it holds a dataset. **Those three artefacts describe data and are produced by people describing it**, which means they inherit exactly the same omissions.

The session's response is to require that something be looked at, to name what was not looked at, and to send the three questions that matter — is it correct, is it labelled, may we use it for this — to three different people.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate. Individually the questions are simple; the load is in maintaining the promised-versus-inspected separation while a helpful maintainer is answering faster than you can write.

**3.2 Why promise and inspection cannot share a column.** They have different warrants and different failure modes, and a record that merges them presents the weaker one in the stronger one's voice. The mechanism is identical to [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md)'s and the argument is not repeated there or here — it is applied.

**3.3 Why the informants are plural.** Provenance is held by whoever built the pipeline. Correctness is held by whoever receives the complaints. Purpose is held by whoever is accountable for the collection. **Those are three roles and are rarely one person**, and asking all three questions of the sponsor produces three confident answers with a single point of failure behind them.

**3.4 Why a blocking absence is not a risk.** A risk is something that may occur and can be mitigated. A dependency that does not exist has already occurred, and the correct response is a different design rather than a mitigation. Converting the first into the second is a category error that makes an impossibility look manageable, and it is the failure this session cares most about.

**3.5 Why completeness can be evidence of a defect.** A field that is never null is either genuinely always populated or populated by a default, and the second is more common in systems where the field is not used for anything. **Variance is the check, not presence.**

The production-systems literature names this as a defect class rather than as a curiosity, and its example is exact: **an elevation reading of zero that may mean sea level or may mean that no measurement arrived** ([research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md)). **Encoding ambiguity has the same status as a missing value**, and it is worse in one respect: a null announces itself and an ambiguous zero does not.

**3.6 Drift and skew are different comparisons, and the vocabulary is the diagnostic.**

The same source supplies the distinction that converts a data complaint into a locatable defect, and this session takes it exactly rather than approximately. **Drift compares successive iterations of one source over time**, splitting into data drift — a change in the statistical properties of the inputs — and concept drift, a change in the relationship between inputs and labels which **invalidates the mapping a system learned rather than merely moving the inputs**. **Skew compares two different sources**, characteristically development against operation, and splits into **schema skew, feature skew and distribution skew.**

**Schema skew is this session's subject under a technical name**, and stating it that way is what lets a learner route the defect rather than describe it. The source also supplies the propagation rule that makes derived fields dangerous: **where one system's output is another system's input, errors propagate over time.**

**The limits are stated wherever the vocabulary is used.** It is **practitioner guidance from a commercial technology publisher, not research**: no study design, no comparison group, no sampling frame for the experiences reported, and no measurement of whether teams adopting these practices produce better outcomes. **Three of its four authors work at the vendor whose toolchain it defaults to**, so it is a legible account of how one large organisation operates production systems and no evidence at all about comparative practice. **Every quantity in it is a property of a particular system after a particular run; none transfers, and none is reproduced in this paper or in the session.**

**3.7 Why the corpus is the first place to look, and what bounds any pre-build record.**

The most-cited experience report on grounded systems distils seven failure points from three systems in the research, education and biomedical domains, arranged across an indexing pipeline and a query pipeline ([research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md)). Its value is diagnostic: it converts *the answer was wrong* into *which stage failed*. **Its first failure point is missing content — a property of the corpus, not of the model** — and it names the reason the failure surface is so large: a grounded system **inherits the limitations of both information retrieval and generation**, so the compound has a larger surface than either part.

Two of its stated takeaways bound what this session can claim, and the design states them rather than working around them. **"Validation of a retrieval-augmented generation system is only feasible during operation"**, because the space of queries, documents and interactions cannot be adequately anticipated before deployment. And **robustness evolves** rather than being designed in at the start. **Read against this module, that means a data reality record is not a substitute for operating the thing and cannot become one.** What it can settle is whether the corpus can support the claim at all, which is smaller and is the only question available before anything exists.

**The limits are recorded.** An **experience report, not a study**: no controlled comparison, no frequency data, no severity data — it says what can fail and not what usually fails or what matters most. Three domain-specific case studies are not a representative sample. It predates widespread agentic retrieval and multi-hop patterns. **And this repository's reading records that the names of the seven failure points are reconstructed rather than quoted**, which is why the session uses the taxonomy's shape and its two takeaways and never enumerates it. Its operational-validation claim should be taught as a strong position to be examined rather than as an axiom, since it has consequences for release governance in regulated settings.

**3.8 Why an honest description is still unreliable.**

The *promised* column is not a record of dishonesty and the design would be misread if learners took it that way. The field-research literature gives the mechanism: **people know their own activities intimately and do not ordinarily articulate their structure**; familiar action becomes tacit, retrospective accounts omit detail, general questions invite general answers, and requests stated as solutions are shaped by the tools people already hold ([research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md)). **A description of a dataset is a retrospective account of a familiar activity, which is the category the literature identifies as least reliable.**

Its four principles transfer to data without modification and are what the maintainer demonstration implements: **context** — stay with real recent cases rather than typical ones; **partnership** — investigate with the person rather than receive a tour, since a curated extract is a tour; **interpretation** — state the emerging reading and let it be corrected while the context is present; **focus** — pursue the project's concern while treating surprises as corrections to it.

**That source is a method book from a commercial publisher whose account of effectiveness rests on practitioner experience rather than comparative evidence**, and nothing in it is a threshold or a legal position.

**3.9 Why an index is the right instrument.**

An index is a copy, and **a copy inherits nothing it was not given.** The documentation for one such system makes the general point sharply while discussing access control: when a skillset chunks documents, metadata **"move from indexer field mappings to index projections"**, and without that projection **"chunk-level references aren't filtered"** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/azure-ai-search-and-acl-patterns.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/azure-ai-search-and-acl-patterns.md)). **The security case is the documented one; the general case is the teachable one** — a transformation carries what it was told to carry and drops the rest without announcing it.

The same page supplies the second observation the session uses: **enforcement runs against indexed metadata and not against the live source**, so source-side changes take effect only after synchronisation, and its preview terms state that **"a timing lag occurs"** without publishing any figure for it. **An unquantified lag is a measurement task rather than a caveat**, and workflow step 8 is that measurement.

**The limits are heavy and the note records them.** Most of the page describes a **preview API** whose surface changes without notice; the terms place responsibility for compliance, geographic boundaries and testing on the implementer; **no latency figures, no failure-rate data and no security comparison of the four approaches against each other are published**; and it is single-vendor, so the principles transfer and the mechanisms do not.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 2 is the instrument.** Learners list every data dependency and then write what they have personally looked at, with *nothing* as an explicit permitted entry. Most discover that the entire position rests on descriptions and that nobody on the team has seen a row. **That discovery is the session's content and it does not survive being told in advance.**

**The record's structure does the enforcement.** Promised, held and inspected are separate columns with separate headings, and the standard requires at least one row filled from direct inspection. **The blank entries are load-bearing** — a record whose inspected column is empty is a complete and honest record of a serious position, and the design is explicit that it passes as a record and fails as a proof.

**The access constraint is treated as a design input rather than an obstacle.** The session names four legitimate ways to establish reality — a maintainer's demonstration, a permitted extract, a synthetic construction checked against described distributions, or a documented refusal — and requires the record to say which was used. **The fourth is a pass**, and saying so plainly is the only way to stop learners manufacturing the first three.

**The review movement swaps three entries between the promised and inspected columns at random and asks a peer to find them.** If they cannot, the two columns are written in the same voice, and a reader six weeks later will not be able to tell them apart either. **That reader is the person who builds on this**, which is the movement's actual argument.

**4.1 What the platform workflow is for.**

Not search-service knowledge, most of which is preview surface that will move. It exists to give the session's central claim an instrument, because until this design had one the session was an interviewing exercise with a table attached. **An indexer run is the cheapest honest comparison available between a described corpus and an actual one, and almost nobody runs it before committing to a design.**

Three elements carry the weight.

**The index is defined against the schema the learner was *described*, deliberately and without correction.** Correcting it first destroys the instrument, because the gap is the measurement. This is the same discipline as writing a sampling frame before looking at an outcome, and learners resist it for the same reason.

**The failure-tolerance setting is the whole instrument.** Configured to stop at the first bad document, a run reports that the data has a problem. Configured not to stop, it reports **what the problem is, how much of the corpus it affects, and whether it is one defect or several.** The difference between those two outputs is the difference between a worry and a finding, and it is one line of configuration — which is exactly the kind of thing a curriculum can teach and a manual will not emphasise.

**The chunking step is included for a reason that is not about chunking.** The documented case concerns permission metadata failing to reach chunks without projection. **The general case — a transformation carries what it was told to carry and silently drops the rest — is the shape of every quiet data defect in this session**, and it is more convincing watched once than asserted three times.

**Two costs the design accepts and records.** The workflow requires a search service and something permitted to index, which not every learner will hold; **a learner with neither should run steps 1 to 4 and record the refusal as the session's finding**, which is a pass. And the page the platform observations rest on is mostly preview API, so **the mechanisms are expected to move and only the two durable claims — a copy inherits nothing it was not given, and enforcement lags its source — are meant to survive the term.**

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The promised extract.** The likeliest failure. The whole position rests on something arriving later and arriving correct. *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — your data column is a date — and the requirement that at least one row be filled by inspection.

**The schema mistaken for the data.** *Countermeasure:* the record's separation of held from inspected, and the standard's requirement that the learner name what they were shown.

**The wrong informant.** The sponsor answered all three questions. *Countermeasure:* the routing to three roles, and the standard's warning sign — your provenance and your invitation come from the same role.

**The smoothed absence.** A blocking dependency acquires a mitigation. *Countermeasure:* the record's consequence column, [S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md)'s routing vocabulary, and an assessed element at [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md). **This is the failure the module is least able to prevent**, because converting a blocker into a risk is what the surrounding organisation will reward.

**Consent assumed from access.** *Countermeasure:* the permission and purpose column, which requires a person's answer rather than a system name. **Partial, and the paper does not overstate it.** A column does not confer authority, and the session is explicit that the governing rules are the customer's and this programme does not know them.

**The run that reports one defect.** The learner configures the indexer to stop at the first failure, learns that the data has a problem, and stops. *Countermeasure:* the workflow's explicit failure-tolerance setting and the exercise 2 check that returns the learner to it when no failures are reported. **This countermeasure is a configuration line rather than a discipline**, which makes it the most reliable one in the session and the least generalisable.

**The corrected schema.** The learner fixes the described schema before indexing, so the gap the session exists to measure never appears. *Countermeasure:* the workflow's instruction to define the index against the description deliberately. **Weak**, because correcting is what a competent engineer does automatically and the instruction runs against a trained reflex.

**The record that reads like a proof.** A learner establishes reality by a permitted extract or a synthetic construction and then writes the record as though the corpus had been observed. *Countermeasure:* the requirement to name which of the four routes was used, in the record, per row. **The taxonomy's own claim that validation is only feasible during operation is what makes this failure serious** — the record was never going to be a proof, and a record that reads like one has overclaimed twice.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the session is built on a silence, and inferring a practice from what a document does not contain is the weakest move available.**

At its strongest: the corpus's discovery instrument has no data questions. That is a fact about a question bank. It does not follow that the organisation ignores data provenance — engineers who deliver machine-learning systems for a living plainly do not — only that the questions live somewhere the reading did not reach, or in the heads of people who do not need a prompt. This programme has built an entire session, and a standing artefact, on the absence of a section from one document.

**Conceded without qualification.** The inference is weak and the design says so in the session's own limits movement. The rebuttal is narrower than the objection: whatever the organisation does in practice, **the instrument it hands to a practitioner going into a room contains no data questions**, and a practitioner working from that instrument will not ask them. The session's claim is about the instrument's coverage, not about the organisation's competence. **The declined fix is to soften the session into an optional extension of [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md)'s data block.** It is declined because a data dependency that fails is the commonest way a proved design becomes unbuildable, and the design would rather over-invest in a real failure than calibrate to the strength of its evidence about a document.

**Objection 2: the session requires an inspection that most learners will be forbidden to perform, and a requirement that cannot be met teaches evasion.**

At its strongest: security will refuse, the data is regulated, the customer's own staff cannot show it on a shared screen, and the playbook this programme cites says plainly that production data is not to be accessed directly. A standard whose central element is unattainable produces one of two outcomes — a fabricated record, or a learner who quietly reclassifies a description as an inspection. Both are worse than not asking.

**Partly conceded, and the design's response is a substitution.** The session names four ways to establish reality and marks a documented refusal as a pass, which removes the incentive to fabricate. The rebuttal to the remainder is that the requirement is not to *see the data* but to *establish what is true by some means and say which means* — and that a documented refusal, naming who refused and on what grounds, is a stronger artefact for the [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) finding than an unexamined promise. **The declined fix is to accept a supplied sample as satisfying the inspection requirement.** It is declined because a supplied sample is chosen by the supplier, which is [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md)'s defeater arriving in a different costume. **Whether the four-route substitution survives a real security review is untested**, and §9 prediction 4 is the closest thing to a test the design can run.

**Objection 3: the session's own sources say that a data record cannot settle what it is being used to settle, and the design proceeds anyway.**

At its strongest: the failure taxonomy this paper cites approvingly states that **validation of a grounded system is only feasible during operation**, because the space of queries, documents and interactions cannot be adequately anticipated before deployment — and that robustness is accumulated by observing real failures rather than designed in. **The whole of this session happens before deployment.** A record assembled from a maintainer's demonstration, a permitted extract or a synthetic construction is by construction unable to represent the failure distribution the system will actually meet. Worse, the indexer workflow produces numbers — documents processed, documents failed — and numbers are believed. **The design has built an instrument that manufactures false confidence about exactly the thing its own source says cannot be established this early.**

**Conceded, and the concession changes what the artefact is for rather than defending its reach.** The record does not claim to establish that the system will work. It claims to establish whether the corpus can support the design's claim **at all**, which is a disqualifying question rather than a confirming one — and disqualification is available early in a way that confirmation is not. **An indexer that cannot read a third of the corpus has settled something; an indexer that reads all of it has settled almost nothing**, and the session's standard is written so that the second outcome is treated as an absence of a finding rather than as a positive one.

**The declined fix is to defer the whole data question to operation, where the taxonomy says validation belongs.** It is declined because a design committed before its data is inspected reaches operation as a commitment rather than as a question, which is the failure [M07](/wiki/modules/M07.md) exists to prevent — and because the disqualifying half of the question is genuinely answerable early. **The cost of declining is exactly what the objection names: some learners will read a clean indexer run as evidence that the data is fine.** §5's fourth countermeasure and §9's prediction 6 address it, and neither is strong.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** In answers to opening item 2, most learners will record *nothing* for a majority of their dependencies. *Instrument:* the written answers, with the inspected entries counted per learner against the number of dependencies listed.

**Prediction 2.** Records will name the sponsor as the source for provenance, correctness and purpose more often than they name three distinct roles. *Instrument:* the informant fields, coded for role distinctness.

**Prediction 3.** In the swap-detection review, peers will fail to identify at least one planted swap in a majority of records. *Instrument:* the detection rate per record, with the planted positions known to the reviewer's marker and not to the reviewer.

**Prediction 4.** Blocking absences will survive intact into the [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) finding more often than into the [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md) pack. *Instrument:* each recorded blocking absence traced through both submissions and coded *intact* / *mitigated* / *absent*. **If it does not degrade, the module's assumption that assembly smooths evidence is wrong, and the duplicated assessed element at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md) is unnecessary.**

**Prediction 5.** A majority of first indexer runs will surface at least one defect that contradicts an entry already written in the learner's promised column. *Instrument:* the run's failure reasons matched against the promised column, per learner, by a second reader. **This is the direct test of the session's central claim** that the described corpus and the actual corpus differ, and a low rate would mean the claim is overstated for the kinds of data these learners work with.

**Prediction 6.** Learners whose indexer run reported no failures will describe their data position more confidently in the [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) finding than learners whose run reported failures, controlling for how much of the corpus each actually indexed. *Instrument:* the finding's data section, coded for confidence by a second reader, against the run's processed-and-failed counts. **This predicts the failure of §5's fourth countermeasure and is the direct test of §8's third objection**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption. **If it holds, the instrument is manufacturing confidence and the session must report the clean run as an absence of evidence rather than as a result.**

**Prediction 7.** A majority of learners will correct the described schema before indexing, despite the workflow instructing otherwise. *Instrument:* the submitted index definition compared against the schema recorded in the promised column. **This predicts the failure of §5's fifth countermeasure**, and if it holds the instruction should be replaced by a supplied index definition the learner does not author.

**Prediction 8.** No learner will report a measured synchronisation lag from workflow step 8 unless exercise 6 requires it, and a minority will report one even then. *Instrument:* the record's freshness row, coded *measured* / *asserted* / *absent*. **The documentation states that a lag occurs and publishes no figure**, so an asserted answer is an adopted promise from nobody — and this prediction measures how readily learners adopt one.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a data promise is several claims, that the person who made it is not the person who can falsify it, and that a missing dependency changes the design rather than adding a risk to it.

**What the evidence licenses.** An absence in one instrument, verified; an observation method whose own source states it establishes no effect; and one organisation's published access rule. **Everything about whether this improves delivery is unwarranted.**

**The concession worth remembering.** This session is built on a silence and says so. Hold the artefact because the failure it addresses is common and cheap to check, not because the evidence for it is strong — it is not.
