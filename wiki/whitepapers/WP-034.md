# WP-034 — Consistency Models as a Contract with the Caller

**Seminar day:** [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md) · **Module:** [M07](/wiki/modules/M07.md) · **Quarter:** [Q4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md)
**Strands:** SY · PE · CF · **Complexity class:** 3
**Platform anchor:** API Management policy scope as a per-operation contract; the gateway's caller-visible anomalies · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (write) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching consistency models as caller-facing contracts whose operative content is a catalogue of observable anomalies, and defends closing the module's accumulated negativity with a direct public question rather than with a reassurance. The first argument is that a consistency model's name conveys nothing actionable to a caller — a caller who knows a system is causally consistent still does not know what they will see — whereas a catalogue of concrete anomalies lets them build correctly on a system they do not understand; and that the near-universal alternative, an unwritten contract, causes the caller to assume the strongest model and fail in ways neither party can attribute. The second argument is that per-operation contracts resolve the apparent conflict between callers with incompatible needs, and that almost no student proposes this because they treat consistency as a property of a system rather than of an interaction. The paper additionally defends deploying the contract rather than only writing it, on the ground that a clause nobody has tested is a claim; and records that this day discharges the deferral [WP-033](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-033.md) §5 made under the standard's fourth register. It concedes that the day's document-only protocol has been used three times without adaptation, and that an agent can now produce a fluent, accurate and wrong contract faster than any student can check one.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

S031 established that correctness is chosen. S032 established that evidence is scarce. S033 established that failure is partial and unattributable. All three are facts about the system, and a student holding all three has no way to help anyone else use it.

A consistency model is where that changes, provided it is understood correctly. Taught as an implementation property — this system is causally consistent — it is a label, and a caller receiving the label knows nothing operational. Taught as a contract, it becomes three things the caller can act on: what they may assume, what anomalies they will observe, and what they must do about them.

The second of those is the operative half and is the day's central claim at point 7. A caller who is told the model's name cannot proceed; a caller who is told "you may read a value, write a new one, and read the old value back within roughly a second" can decide whether that matters and what to do if it does. The name is for the engineer; the catalogue is for the caller.

**The gateway supplies the catalogue's entries and the module has spent three days generating them.** A consumer charged above a stated limit; a 503 produced by a healthy service protecting a dependency; a confident answer to a question nobody asked. Each is an anomaly with a mechanism the students now understand and a consumer somewhere who does not, and each exists because a promise was defaulted rather than written. That is the day's material, and it is not an example set — it is the accumulated output of the module's own build.

The day's second problem is what happens when the contract is not written. The caller does not proceed without assumptions; they assume the strongest model, because that is what a naive reading of any interface suggests. They then build correctly on that assumption, and the resulting failure is rare, unattributable and blameless — neither team did anything wrong. Phase 6's disclosure is exactly this and is the module's fourth consecutive no-villain failure, which the design now names to the room rather than leaving as a pattern to be noticed.

The third problem is that students treat consistency as a property of a system. Phase 3 supplies four callers with genuinely incompatible needs and asks whether one service can serve all four. It can — by promising differently on different operations — and almost no group proposes this. The obstacle is conceptual rather than technical: a contract is an attribute of an interaction, and a student who locates it in the system cannot see the move. **On this platform the move is unusually cheap to demonstrate**, because policy is applied at a scope and one of the available scopes is the operation, so the idea's implementation is a directory structure rather than an architecture.

The fourth problem is the module's. [WP-033](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-033.md) §5 deferred M07's accumulated negativity to this day and, under the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s fourth register, named its verification: phase 8's direct question about what students can now promise. That question is the deferral's discharge and is instrumented as such.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Moderate, and deliberately the module's lowest.** This is the module's fourth demanding day and its resolving one, and the design lowers intrinsic load in favour of synthesis and writing.

The interactivity that remains is at step 2, where per-operation contracts require holding several callers and several operations and matching them — and at step 3, where each contract's anomalies must be derived from its guarantee rather than recalled.

Management:

- **The gateway, its consumers and its S031 specification are all the student's own** and are three days old.
- **Four callers supplied at phase 3,** with their needs stated and their clients written.
- **Costs supplied rather than derived** at phase 3's second stage.
- **The catalogue form and the contract's three-part structure are both supplied.**
- **One page,** bounded.
- **The anomalies are already observed.** Step 3's hardest demand — producing a concrete example — is met from three days of the students' own measurements rather than from imagination, which is the largest single load reduction the platform anchoring supplies anywhere in this module.
- **No new mechanism.** Every policy the day deploys has been met on a previous day; **the only new content is where to put it.**

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Reading real documentation for what it actually promises,* item 2.
- *The four-caller incompatibility,* phase 3, whose resolution almost no group proposes.
- *Deriving observable anomalies from a guarantee,* step 3 — the day's discriminator.
- *Testing one clause of your own contract,* step 6.
- *Naming what the contract does not tell you,* phase 7's third question.
- *Comparing against the S031 gap,* step 9, closing a loop left open for three days.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Implementing any consistency mechanism.* Refused; SY201's material.
- *The precise formulations of the availability-consistency trade.* Named at point 3 and treated as a single result, with the strong-form scope statement.
- *Quorum arithmetic and replication protocols.* Omitted.
- *Withholding per-operation contracts.* Point 5 states it after phase 3 has produced the difficulty.
- *Testing every clause.* One is required at step 6. Requiring all of them would convert a writing day into a testing day and would make the artefact's length the measure.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** Point 3 treats the availability-consistency trade as one named result and omits the more precise formulations distinguishing latency from availability and applying during normal operation rather than only under partition. **Safe** for choosing a contract and explaining it to a caller — which is the day's task. **Unsafe** as a basis for arguing about a specific system's published claims, which requires the precise version and which students will encounter in vendor documentation. The seminar page carries this statement.

**The platform instance, scoped in the strong form.** The day treats policy scope as the mechanism by which a per-operation contract is expressed.

*Safe.* Deciding what to promise on which operation; explaining an anomaly to a consumer in terms of the mechanism that produced it; and recognising that one mechanism can serve one consumer and endanger another — which phase 3's caching result demonstrates directly.

*Unsafe.* **As a claim that any contract you can state can be enforced.** The available scopes differ by policy, by service tier and by gateway type: the token-limit policy applies at five scopes including workspace, the semantic-cache policies at four and not on the workspace gateway at all, and neither set is uniform across tiers. A promise made at a granularity the platform cannot express is a promise being kept by hand, and a contract that does not say so is misleading in exactly the way the day exists to prevent. Step 5's requirement to record promises the scopes could not express is the artefact element under which a student applying the simplification outside its safe range would fail, which is the form the scope rule's latest widening requires.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **something finally being promisable**.

Three days have subtracted. This one produces an artefact a caller can use, about a gateway the student specified on day one, closing a gap they named themselves. The structural satisfaction is real and the design depends on it: phase 8's question — what can you now promise that you could not four days ago — is asked because the answer exists.

Phase 3's closing question supplies the intellectual moment. Four callers with incompatible needs, a service that appears unable to serve them all, and a resolution nobody proposed. Per-operation contracts are obvious in retrospect and invisible beforehand, which is the profile of a genuinely useful idea.

**Phase 3's caching result supplies a second and sharper moment the previous design did not have.** Running the four consumers rather than reasoning about them produces a fact no group predicts: the cache that makes caller D's near-identical request fast, free and consistent is the same cache that would serve caller B a stale answer for an irreversible decision. **One switch is simultaneously the best and the worst answer in the room, and it is currently on for both.** That is the per-operation argument arriving as a measurement instead of as a proposal.

Item 2 supplies the day's opening discomfort and it is unusually pointed because the documentation is real. Students read vendor pages and their own configuration, are asked what they actually promise, and find they promise almost nothing about what a consumer will observe — and then discover at item 4 that they are nonetheless *read* as promising more.

The competence risk is at step 3. Deriving anomalies from a guarantee is genuinely hard and a student who produces a thin catalogue has produced a contract that fails the specification. Countermeasures: the catalogue form is supplied with worked examples for two of the four models; phase 3's cost reveal has already exposed students to concrete anomalies; and **three days of their own observations are available as source material**, which converts the step from derivation to selection and justification.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Real documentation, real reported anomalies. Item 4's two-directional answer — the documentation promises less precisely and is read as promising more — is the day's subject and is discovered rather than stated. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction** and is administered here per instrumentation rule 8; asking what an agent's consumer documentation will *omit* is the same discrimination step 3 demands, which makes the sealed item a pretest for the day's discriminator rather than a separate exercise.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** The bridge converts S033's unhandled failures into caller-visible anomalies, which is the transition the day depends on and is delivered as retrieval.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four consumers, one gateway, **run rather than reasoned about**, then a cost reveal, then the incompatibility question. The design's decision to supply costs rather than have students derive them keeps the phase's difficulty in the matching rather than in the arithmetic. The executed result — that the cache serving caller D is the cache endangering caller B — is the phase's payload and was not available to the previous design, which could only assert that consumers conflict. The closing question remains the phase's intellectual peak.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Eleven points. Point 1 is the reframing. Point 2 restates S031's properties as promises and anomalies, which is the same content in the operative register. Point 3 carries the scope statement and the impossibility framing. Point 5 resolves phase 3. Point 7 is the day's most important claim, **and its second sentence — that the catalogue is bounded by the callers listed at step 1 — is the repair §8 objection 1 specified.** Point 8 imports S025. **Points 9 to 11 are the platform content: policy scope as the mechanism of a per-operation contract together with the scopes that are not available; three defaults that are contract terms rather than conveniences; and the silence that no rate is published for any of the three behaviours the vendor itself warns about.** Point 10 is the day's strongest sentence and is placed deliberately: on a platform, a default is not a convenience, it is a term, and a consumer cannot tell a term you chose from one you inherited.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Nine steps at class 3 on the student's own S031 gateway. Step 3 is the discriminator and its evidence requirement — examples from observations rather than from the model — is what makes the catalogue both bounded and checkable. **Step 6 is new and is the practical turn's contribution to this day**: a contract clause nobody has tested is a claim, and the day now requires exactly one to be tested, which is enough to install the habit and not enough to convert the day into a testing exercise. Step 9 closes the module's opening problem, and the closure is the day's structural payoff — a student who can say what remains open is in a materially different position from one who named a gap and never returned to it.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A caller who assumed a stronger guarantee, built correctly, and failed rarely; then the agent case. The instructor disclosure is the module's fourth no-villain failure, and the first where the pattern is named explicitly to the room: four consecutive failures with nothing to blame is the module's most important professional observation and it has been left implicit for three days deliberately, so that naming it lands on accumulated evidence. **The agent case is the same failure with a new author** — documentation generated faithfully from a configuration, describing the configuration and not the system — and it is chosen because it makes the day's central distinction operational rather than rhetorical. It is also the direct justification for step 6.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Document only, three questions. The third — name something the contract does not tell you that you need — is new and is the module's terminal demand: a contract is incomplete, and its incompleteness should be discoverable by the reader rather than concealed by the writer.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** The documentation gap resolved, the sealed item 5 opened, then the deferral's discharge. Collecting and reading out the answers is deliberate: a claim about the cohort's state is checked in front of the cohort.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with distributed systems background will name models fluently and may write a contract in model terms. Predicted failure: a technically correct contract with a thin anomaly catalogue, because their own understanding runs on the model name and they do not need the catalogue to use the system. Countermeasure: the specification fails a contract naming a model without its observable anomalies, requires each anomaly to trace to an observation, and phase 7's partner reads the document alone and cannot supply the missing derivation.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** Step 3's derivation is the day's hardest demand. Countermeasures: two worked catalogue examples; phase 3's concrete anomalies; the supplied form; and three days of their own recorded observations. Residual risk: their catalogue may be a restatement of the guarantee rather than a set of observations, and the specification's traceability requirement is the check.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** The four caller-model matchings are usually completed. The cost reveal reshuffles some rankings and the incompatibility question is where the phase bites. Fallback if a group proposes per-operation contracts unprompted: ask what happens when a single caller uses two operations with different guarantees, which is a genuine composition problem and is hard.

**The platform fails on the day.** Lower risk than on S032 and S033, because the day's core work is writing. Countermeasure: where the gateway is unavailable, phase 3's consumers are adjudicated from the module's recorded observations, and **step 5's deployment and step 6's verification are demonstrated rather than operated and are not assessed.** The contract, the catalogue and the not-met procedure are unaffected, which is why this day is the module's most robust to platform failure and is deliberately the one carrying the deferral discharge.

**Affective failure.** This day's assigned job is to repair the module's accumulated negativity, and the risk is that it does not. The countermeasures are structural — a usable artefact, a closed loop, a deployed contract, a public question — and their adequacy is the deferral's open question.

The design's honest position, sharpened by [WP-029](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-029.md) §8's finding about exactly this pattern in M06: **phase 8's question is a weak instrument with an informative failure mode.** A cohort that answers it fluently is weak evidence; a cohort that cannot answer it is strong evidence of failure. That asymmetry is stated here rather than discovered later, which is what the standard's fourth register was added to require.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by S035 (breaking a system that appears to work, one day later, where the contract becomes the thing to falsify), S039 (requirements as negotiated constraint), S060 (shipping retrieval into production), S073 (protocols, tools and the boundary of trust — a tool contract is this day's artefact with a language model as the caller), S086 (what actually transfers) and S089 (the handover artefact and its warranty).

S089 at roughly two years is the principal long-range target and the correspondence is close: a handover artefact states what the receiving organisation may assume, what they will observe, and what they must do — which is point 6's three parts at complexity class 6.

S073 is the nearer and more surprising target: a tool boundary is a contract whose caller is a language model that cannot read documentation and will assume the strongest interpretation, which is phase 6's failure with an unusually literal-minded caller. **What transfers is contract-as-interaction and the anomaly catalogue, not the policy scopes**, and the seminar page's register says so.

Retained content: contract-not-implementation, the anomaly catalogue as the operative half, per-operation contracts, the three-part contract form, and the not-met procedure. Five items, no numbers.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, then the module set before S035.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement.

One specification item, jointly in SY201 and PE201. **A contract naming a model without its observable anomalies fails.** This is the day's central clause and it is checkable: either concrete observations appear or they do not.

Per the [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s rule 1, the checked elements are stated on the seminar page: per-operation contracts; concrete anomalies traceable to observations rather than to the model; costs in three terms; the deployed policy scopes and any promise the scopes could not express; the step 6 verification and its result; a not-met procedure; the S031 gap comparison; survival of phase 7's document-only test; and the **prompt record, intervention record and frontier statement**. **Nothing else is assessed**, and the page states explicitly that a clause which failed its own experiment and says so is a better artefact than one never tested — which is the clause that prevents step 6 from becoming an incentive to test only what will pass.

Programme instrumentation: item 4's two-directional documentation gap is the cohort's contract-reading baseline, re-probed at S073; the per-operation proposal rate at phase 3 is the interaction-versus-property measure; **phase 8's answers are the [WP-033](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-033.md) §5 deferral's verification** and are recorded verbatim; the phase 7 third-question quality is the incompleteness-discoverability measure carried to S089; **item 5's specific-omission rate is this day's frontier datum**, read by next year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) per instrumentation rule 9.

The frontier statements are a sample and the day says so, per instrumentation rule 3: one cohort, one gateway, four consumers written by staff to conflict.

Dispositions: **humility**, at step 9, where reporting that the S031 gap remains open is the honest and less satisfying answer.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the anomaly catalogue is unbounded and the day supplies no completeness criterion.**

At its strongest: point 7 makes the catalogue the contract's operative content, and step 3 requires it, and nothing anywhere says when it is finished. A weak model permits indefinitely many observable anomalies, and a student can always add another. The specification requires concrete examples and does not say how many, so the assessed standard is presence rather than sufficiency — which means a contract with two anomalies and one with twenty both pass, and a caller receiving the first will be surprised by something the writer could have listed.

The rebuttal is that completeness is not the right standard and was never available. A contract's catalogue should cover the anomalies its callers can encounter and act on, which is a function of the callers rather than of the model — and step 1's caller enumeration is what bounds it. A student who has listed their callers and given each an anomaly they would actually observe has done the work; one who has enumerated model-theoretic possibilities has not.

**The concession is discharged.** The original concession was that this bounding was nowhere stated — the design held a correct position and had not written it into the day — and it specified the missing sentence. **Point 7 now carries it**, and step 3 has been strengthened beyond the sentence: each concrete example must trace to an observation rather than to the model, which bounds the catalogue by what the gateway actually did as well as by whom it serves. The concession is recorded rather than deleted so that the original diagnosis remains legible.

**Objection 2: the phase 7 document-only protocol has now been used three times without adaptation.**

At its strongest: S028 handed over licensing sentences, S031 handed over a specification, and S034 hands over a contract, each time asking a partner to state what they understand from the document alone. The protocol is good and its repetition means students know precisely what will be asked by the third occasion. They will write documents that answer the three questions conspicuously, which is the competence being taught — but it means the protocol is no longer *testing* anything by S034, it is prompting. The measurement the design records as a comparable series across three days is not comparable, because the third administration is to a cohort that has learned the instrument.

This objection the design does not fully answer, and it is a stronger version of the concern [WP-024](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-024.md) §8 raised about its comprehension test degrading. The additional element here is the third question — name something the contract does not tell you that you need — which is new at S034 and which students have not seen. That question is doing genuine work and the first two are not.

What follows is that the design should stop treating the first two questions as measurement. They remain useful as an intervention and their recorded rates across S028, S031 and S034 should not be compared, because the instrument changed by being used. **The design had been recording them as a comparable series** — [WP-031](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-031.md) §7 explicitly paired the S028 and S031 rates — and that comparison is invalid. This is the correction: those rates measure familiarity with the protocol as much as document quality, and no conclusion should be drawn from their trend. **The retraction has been applied**: WP-031 §7 now records it and substitutes a within-day prediction that one administration can answer.

**Objection 3: an agent can now write a fluent, accurate and wrong contract faster than a student can check one, and the day's response is a single test.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn and it is the strongest in this paper.

At its strongest: contract writing is the task large language models are best at and the task where being wrong is hardest to see. The agent case in phase 6 is real — documentation generated from a configuration, accurate about the configuration, wrong about the system, and reviewed by people who checked it against the configuration. The day's answer is step 6: test one clause. **One clause out of a page is a token.** A student who tests the clause they are most confident about has satisfied the specification and learned nothing, and the specification cannot require otherwise without turning the day into a testing exercise. Worse, the day has just made contract production cheap: a student can now generate a plausible one-page contract in a minute, which means **the artefact the module's terminal writing day assesses is the artefact the agent produces best.** The design's protection is the traceability requirement at step 3, and traceability to an observation is itself something an agent can fabricate convincingly from a metrics dashboard it was shown.

The objection is conceded in part.

What is defensible: the assessed competence is the *catalogue*, not the prose, and the catalogue's entries must trace to observations the student's own gateway produced over three days. An agent that has not been given those observations cannot invent them, and one that has been given them has been given them by a student who collected them. The day's assessment is therefore attached to three days of the student's own measurement rather than to a document produced in the last ninety minutes, which is the strongest structural defence the module has anywhere.

What is conceded: **step 6's single clause is a compromise the design cannot justify on principle, only on budget.** The honest number is not one and is not all; it is whichever clauses a consumer would rely on for an irreversible decision, which is a judgement the day could ask for and does not. **The cheap fix is available and is not made**: step 6 could require the student to test the clause their *phase 7 partner* nominates rather than one they choose, which costs nothing, removes the self-selection entirely, and uses an external observer already present in the room — which is instrumentation rule 1 exactly. It is not made because phase 7 occurs after phase 5 and the reordering would require the verification to happen outside the build phase, and the design has not established that students can return to a deployed gateway after phase 7. **That is a sequencing obstacle rather than a reason**, and it is recorded as an unmade fix with a known route.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A large majority will read item 2's documentation as promising a stronger guarantee than it states, and a majority will also judge it imprecise when asked directly. *Instrument:* item 2 and item 4 responses, paired.

**Prediction 2.** Fewer than a fifth of groups will propose per-operation contracts before point 5. *Instrument:* phase 3 closing-question responses.

**Prediction 3.** Students with distributed systems backgrounds will produce anomaly catalogues rated thinner than those without, controlling for contract correctness. *Instrument:* blind catalogue ratings against contract correctness and the admission background survey.

**Prediction 4.** Phase 8 answers will name a per-operation contract or an anomaly catalogue rather than a model, for a majority. *Instrument:* phase 8 answers coded. **This is the [WP-033](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-033.md) §5 deferral's verification; a cohort that cannot answer, or that answers only with model names, indicates the resolution did not occur.**

**Prediction 5.** No group will predict, before running the four consumers, that the caching configuration serving caller D is the one endangering caller B. *Instrument:* phase 3 pre-run commitments coded for any statement that one mechanism both serves and endangers. **If groups do predict it, the executed payload is redundant and phase 3 can return to its supplied form, which would free ten minutes the §8 objection-3 fix needs.**

**Prediction 6.** A majority of item 5 sealed predictions will describe the agent's documentation accurately and fewer than a third will name a specific missing anomaly. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded for accurate-description and for specific-omission, against the documentation produced. **The specific rate is expected to correlate with step 3 catalogue quality within student; a weak correlation would indicate that predicting an omission and producing a catalogue are different competences, and the day treats them as one.**

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The scope and anomaly behaviour used at phases 3 to 5 is taken from Microsoft Learn's `llm-token-limit`, `llm-semantic-cache-lookup` and `llm-semantic-cache-store` policy references, the API Management policy reference, the backends page and the AI gateway overview, all read 2026-07-26: policy scopes of global, workspace, product, API and operation for the token-limit policy, and of global, product, API and operation for the cache policies; gateway availability differing between the two, with the cache policies **unsupported on the workspace gateway** and the token-limit policy unsupported in the Consumption tier; a `retry-after-header-name` defaulting to `Retry-After`; an optional `vary-by` element documented as the means of controlling cross-user access to cache entries; a tripped circuit breaker **returning 503 Service Unavailable to the client**; and the vendor's own three cautions — that rate limiting is never completely accurate because throttling is distributed, that circuit-breaker tripping rules are approximate for the same reason, and that semantic caching can surface responses that are incorrect, outdated or unsafe for the current request.

**One silence is recorded and it is taught, and it has a contractual consequence.** The vendor warns about all three behaviours above and **publishes no rate, bound or frequency for any of them; no accuracy figure appears anywhere in this stack.** That is the silence point 11 names to students, and the day draws the operative conclusion rather than complaining about it: **you cannot promise a consumer a bound you have not measured, and nobody upstream will measure it for you**, so the catalogue's numbers come from the operator's own traffic or they are invented. Consistent with the folder-wide asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](/research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), the pages state scopes, tiers, status codes and defaults throughout and state no accuracy anywhere. They carry no version number and are dated by their content; every scope list, tier restriction and status code the day uses is marked perishable at points 9 to 11.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** Contrasting cases across callers with a fixed system, late cost reveal, retrieval practice at expanding intervals, cognitive load in synthesis and composition tasks, and error management training are cited for **direction and mechanism only**. No effect size is asserted for any of them, and **none is asserted for AI-assisted productivity anywhere in this paper.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The contract framing and the anomaly catalogue as its operative half; the four-consumer incompatibility, its executed caching result and its per-operation resolution; the eleven points of phase 4; the nine-step phase 5 with the traceability requirement at step 3, the single-clause verification at step 6 and the S031 gap closure at step 9; phase 7's third question; the explicit naming of the module's four no-villain failures at phase 6; the choice of an agent failure in which faithful documentation of a configuration is a false description of a system; phase 8's public deferral verification; and all six §9 predictions are this design's own reasoning, untested. The §8 objection-1 concession is discharged: point 7 now states that the catalogue is bounded by the callers listed at step 1, and step 3 additionally requires each example to trace to an observation. The objection-2 concession establishes that the document-only protocol's first two questions have become a prompt rather than a measurement through repetition, **retracts the comparison [WP-031](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-031.md) §7 drew between the S028 and S031 rates as invalid, and records that the retraction has been applied at WP-031 with a within-day substitute.** **The objection-3 concession states that the day's terminal artefact is the artefact an agent produces best, that the traceability requirement rather than the single-clause test is the real defence, that step 6's single clause is justifiable on budget and not on principle, and that the available fix — having the phase 7 partner nominate the clause to be tested — is blocked by phase ordering rather than by cost and is recorded as unmade with a known route.**
