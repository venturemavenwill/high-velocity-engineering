## In plain terms

The person running a discovery session is usually the most technically capable person present, and that is a problem rather than an advantage.

Everything an engineer knows about being useful — correcting an error, supplying a missing option, filling an awkward gap with a helpful example — transfers into the facilitator's chair as damage. Each of those moves takes authority away from the person whose situation you came to learn about and gives it to you. **You end up in a conversation about your understanding rather than theirs, and it will feel like it is going well.**

So facilitation is taught here as a technical skill with observable behaviours and, more importantly, with prohibitions. A prohibition is what you need when your judgement is degraded, which is exactly when you are running late, the room is quiet and you badly want to rescue it.

The session also teaches something the corpus contains but does not present as teaching material: a facilitator wellness document. It tells people to protect sleep and meals, not to run consecutive intensive engagements, to rotate the demanding roles, and to watch colleagues for signs of difficulty. **Nobody writes those instructions prospectively.** It is an incident record in the grammar of advice, and its remedies appear in none of the documents that actually schedule anyone's time.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching facilitation as a bounded technical skill rather than as a disposition. The argument has three parts: that the expert's habitual helpfulness is the principal failure mechanism in a discovery session; that the useful unit of instruction is a self-imposed prohibition rather than a repertoire, because prohibitions bind under the conditions that produce the failure; and that structural changes to what speaking costs are more reliable than encouragement, because encouragement does not alter the cost. The session installs a facilitation plan with a forbidden list, a named owner for remote participants, and one amendment that makes wellness guidance binding rather than advisory. The paper concedes that no facilitation technique in the corpus has ever been compared with any other, and that the psychological-safety literature it borrows from is carried here at the level of direction and mechanism only.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Facilitation is treated in most engineering organisations as a personality trait: some people are good with customers.

That framing has two costs. It makes the skill untrainable, so nobody trains it. And it makes failure attributable to the individual rather than to a practice, so nothing is learned when a session goes badly.

The specific mechanism the session targets is the transfer of authority. A facilitator who corrects a factual error, however gently and however correctly, has established that the room's business is getting things right by their standard. **After that, participants stop volunteering the messy true account and start producing the tidy account they think is wanted**, and the facilitator's expertise — which is real — has removed the only thing the session was for.

The second mechanism is the supplied option. A question followed by examples is not the same question, because the examples become the response set. This is the same defect that [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) diagnoses in written form, met here in its live form, and meeting it live first is deliberate.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** Any reader who has run a meeting has enough. [Stage 1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md) adds [S004](/wiki/seminars/S004.md)'s room-reading, which the facilitation plan uses but does not require.

The prior it works against is that a good facilitator keeps things moving. Learners arrive believing that silence, confusion and dead air are failures to be repaired. **The session's central move is that some of them are the instrument working.**

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Low intrinsic, high affective. The material is small; the difficulty is doing less than you know how to do, in front of peers, while being marked on restraint. That is why the drill is short and repeated rather than long and single.

**3.2 Why prohibitions rather than techniques.** A technique is selected when you have capacity to select. Under time pressure, in a quiet room, with an audience, capacity is what is missing. A pre-committed prohibition removes the selection. **This is design reasoning; no source behind this session tests it.**

**3.3 Why structure rather than encouragement.** People withhold what they know at work because of a rapid, largely unconscious calculation about interpersonal risk, and the calculation is asymmetric: staying quiet has an immediate and certain benefit, speaking has a delayed and uncertain one. Inviting candour does not alter either side of that. Changing who is present, what is asked, and of whom, does. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.**

**3.4 Why silence is taught with a warning attached.** The field-interviewing source that supplies the drill is explicit that silence is read differently by different people and in different cultures — it can create shared readiness, signal effort, expose confusion, or produce stress — and that it must be read and adjusted to rather than applied as a trick. **The drill can teach a bad habit if that warning is dropped**, and the session states it in the same movement.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**The drill is in threes and the third role is the point.** The observer's record — every supplied option, every interruption, every occasion the facilitator answered their own question — is the only place where the failure is visible, because it is invisible to the facilitator and unremarkable to the participant.

**The forbidden list must contain something the learner is inclined to do.** A prohibition against a move you would never make is a costless signal. This is the same requirement as [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s condition capable of firing, applied to behaviour rather than to a position.

**The wellness reading is placed here rather than in a pastoral session** because it is evidence about the practice rather than about the practitioner. Two properties are taught: that its justification has been shifted onto delivery effectiveness rather than obligation to the person, and that **its remedies appear in none of the artefacts that schedule work** — not the lifecycle, not the timeline, not the checklist, not the readiness gate. Advisory guidance inside a system of checklists is guidance that loses, and the learner is required to move one item from the advisory layer into their own plan.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The costless forbidden list.** The learner forbids themselves moves they were never going to make. *Countermeasure:* the review movement, where a peer names which item was broken; a list that survives is required to be re-tested against a harder participant.

**Encouragement substituted for structure.** The plan says the facilitator will make people comfortable and specifies nothing observable. *Countermeasure:* the plan's required field is what the segment does *before* the question, not what the facilitator says.

**The correction deferred and then made anyway.** The learner holds the correction through the segment and delivers it in the debrief, believing the prohibition satisfied. *Countermeasure:* partial. The session states that a correction may be offered afterwards only where it benefits the participant rather than the facilitator's need to be right, which is a judgement the session cannot verify.

**Wellness read as a welfare topic.** The learner treats the reading as a message about self-care and changes nothing structural. *Countermeasure:* the required amendment must appear in the facilitation plan itself, which is an assessed artefact at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md).

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: withholding expertise from a customer who is factually wrong is a disservice to them and an abdication.**

At its strongest: the customer is paying for judgement. If an engineer sits through a description of a deployment process containing a serious error and says nothing, the customer leaves with the error intact, and the engineer has prioritised the purity of their data collection over the interests of the person in front of them. In a safety-relevant or security-relevant case this is not merely unhelpful, it is wrong.

**Conceded in the safety and security case without qualification.** The prohibition does not extend to a situation where silence leaves somebody exposed, and a facilitator who reads it that way has read it wrong. In the ordinary case the rebuttal holds: the correction can be made after the session, when it costs the data nothing, and the interviewing sources say the same. **The declined fix is a general exception for material errors.** It is declined because *material* is judged by the person holding the expertise, in the moment, under exactly the conditions that produce the failure — which makes the exception the rule.

**Objection 2: the psychological-safety material is being used far outside what this repository has verified.**

At its strongest: the construct comes from a trade book read for this repository, whose underlying empirical paper has not been read here, and whose case material is largely secondary. Using it to justify a claim about how a customer workshop should be structured extends a construct developed for standing teams to a room of strangers meeting once, and no source establishes that it transfers.

**Conceded.** The transfer is unwarranted and is design reasoning. The paper's position is that the mechanism — silence as a risk calculation rather than a disposition — is the part being borrowed, and that mechanisms travel further than findings. **The declined fix is to remove the material and teach the drill alone.** It is declined because the drill without the mechanism teaches a trick, and a facilitator who waits through silence without understanding what the silence might cost the other person will wait through the wrong silences.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** In the first drill round, most facilitators will supply at least one option, example or completion within their first three questions. *Instrument:* the observer's tally, collected per round.

**Prediction 2.** Forbidden lists written before the first drill will be judged costless more often than those revised after it. *Instrument:* a peer's binary judgement on whether the list contains a move the author is inclined to make, collected at both points.

**Prediction 3.** Learners will name the remote-participant owner less often than any other required field of the plan. *Instrument:* field-presence counts across submitted plans.

**Prediction 4.** The wellness amendment will most often be written as an addition to the plan rather than as a removal from it. *Instrument:* the amendments, coded *adds an activity* / *removes or bounds an activity*. **If additions dominate, the session has taught the learner to document the constraint rather than to obey it**, and the movement should be rewritten to require a removal.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That facilitation is a trainable technical skill whose most useful unit is a prohibition, and that what makes speaking cheap is structural rather than attitudinal.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The corpus's facilitation material is codified experience with no comparison behind it. The psychological-safety mechanism is class 2 and is being applied outside the setting it was developed in. The silence technique comes from a manual that reports no measured effect and warns explicitly against mechanical application.

**The concession worth remembering.** The prohibition on correcting a participant does not survive a safety or security case, and a rule presented without its exception is a rule that will be broken at the wrong moment.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That withholding at work follows an asymmetric risk calculation in which silence pays immediately and certainly while voice pays later and uncertainly, and that the conditions for speaking sit at the level of the group rather than the organisation. **These rest on a trade book read in full for this repository whose underlying empirical paper has not been read here; a summary written two decades later by the same author is not the paper.** Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The forbidden list as the unit of instruction; the claim that a pre-committed prohibition binds where a technique does not; the transfer of the psychological-safety mechanism from standing teams to a one-off customer room; the requirement to move one wellness item into the plan; and all four predictions in §9.
