# WP-013 — Hashing and Amortised Reasoning

**Seminar day:** [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) · **Module:** [M03](/wiki/modules/M03.md) · **Quarter:** [Q2](/wiki/quarters/Q2.md)
**Strands:** CF · MS · **Complexity class:** 1
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Foundry embedding deployment under TPM and RPM quota — the 429, the per-minute reset, and the two token counts · **MCP servers:** Microsoft Learn MCP Server · Azure MCP Server (Read only = true)

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching amortised analysis as a claim with a reader rather than as a technique, and defends the day's terminal requirement that a student produce two different true reports about one measurement for two different callers. The first argument is that amortised, worst-case, expected and observed-distribution costs are four distinct claims about the same structure, that students collapse them into one, and that the collapse is not a gap in analysis but a failure to ask who is asking — which makes it an instance of S004's reader problem rather than a new topic. The second argument is that producing two true and materially different reports from one body of evidence is the cheapest available rehearsal of a competence the programme assesses at S065, S083 and S088, and that it also opens, unresolved, the question of when selective truth becomes misrepresentation. The paper additionally defends running the day's four callers against a real Foundry deployment under quota, on the ground that the platform independently instantiates every claim the day makes — a manufactured tail at a counter boundary, an averaged success rate that conceals a throttle, and **two documented, simultaneously true token counts for one request, one deciding throttling and one deciding billing.** It concedes that the day leaves its ethical question open for two years, that its central pedagogical device may license the behaviour it intends to problematise, and that the platform layer supplies a ready-made excuse for reporting the number that flatters.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Students say a hash table has constant-time lookup. The sentence is a claim about an expected value over a distribution of keys, or an amortised cost over a sequence of operations, depending on which property they mean — and they usually do not know which, because they have never had a reason to distinguish them. Stated flatly, the sentence is what S004 calls an unstated assumption: true under conditions the speaker has not identified and is not tracking.

The practical consequence is specific. A structure with excellent amortised cost can produce individual operations orders of magnitude slower than the average, and those operations land on particular requests belonging to particular users. A student reasoning in averages will not predict this, will not monitor for it, and will report system health using a statistic that conceals it. The failure is invisible in aggregate and acute in the tail, which is the shape of most performance incidents in interactive systems.

The day's second problem is the one that makes it worth six hours rather than one. The question *which claim is adequate* has no answer that is not relative to a caller. A batch job is correctly served by an amortised bound. An interactive request behind a queue is not, and the reason involves a mechanism — queueing amplification — that the day plants and does not explain. A real-time control loop is served by neither, and needs a worst-case bound the structure cannot supply.

So the day cannot teach a rule. It has to teach a question, and the question is S004's: who is reading this, and what are they entitled to conclude? That correspondence is why the day retrieves from S004 at a seven-week gap rather than from the structures material alone.

The third problem is professional and is introduced here without being solved. Step 6 asks for two true reports. Phase 7 asks what each conceals from the other's reader, and whether the concealment is legitimate. That is the entry point to a question the programme takes seriously — S050's statement of what a measurement does not license, S083's communication of risk to a decision-maker, S088's residual-risk statement — and it is opened in week thirteen deliberately, so that by the time it is answered it has been carried a long way.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge assumptions

- **S012's three-term decomposition,** two days old, and one experience of separated measurement.
- **S011's pair,** used at phase 4 point 1 without re-teaching.
- **S004's four argument-failure classes,** seven weeks old, retrieved five times, and the day's longest bridge.
- **Two measurement experiences,** both on supplied apparatus, neither with any framework for variability.
- **Default reliance on hash tables,** near-universal, and near-universal inability to say what they discard.
- **One prior encounter with the embeddings endpoint,** at S011 and S012, where every call succeeded. This day is the first time the students see it refuse.
- **No model of queueing.** Caller C is deliberately beyond the students' current reach and is the day's planted forward reference.
- **No model of admission control or capacity.** The 429 is taught as a documented behaviour with a documented boundary and not as a mechanism; the mechanism is Q7's.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**The module's highest.** Amortised reasoning requires holding a sequence as a single object while simultaneously reasoning about individual members of it, and the two views cannot be considered separately — the aggregate argument is precisely an argument about how individual costs distribute. That is irreducible element interactivity and it is the reason the day sits at the module's midpoint with lower-load neighbours on both sides.

A second load source is that the day maintains four distinct claim types in parallel at point 6, and students must keep them distinct while applying them to four callers. Sixteen cells, most of which do not need to be filled, and the discrimination between them is the content.

Management:

- **One structure, one implementation.** No comparison across structures anywhere in the day. All variation is in the caller and the claim.
- **The aggregate doubling argument is worked completely** at point 4 rather than sketched, because a half-worked amortised argument is worse than none.
- **Phase 3 supplies the callers and their requirements.** Groups judge adequacy; they do not construct callers.
- **The distribution is supplied, not derived.** Students see the timing distribution; they do not have to reason about it analytically.
- **Step 3's prediction template is supplied,** which is unusual for this programme and is justified by the fact that students have never predicted a distribution's shape and have no vocabulary for it.
- **The four platform cases mirror the four analytic callers exactly.** No new caller taxonomy is introduced with the platform; the phase runs the same contrast in a second medium, which is a load reduction rather than an addition.
- **The deployment used for case C is a low-quota one held for the purpose,** so that the boundary is reached in seconds rather than being chased.
- **The module dataset persists.**

The design accepts that this day will overrun for some students and has placed the fading at step 1 and step 3, the two points where stalling is most likely.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Two true reports from one measurement,* step 6. The day's payload.
- *Predicting a distribution's shape including the tail,* step 3, which most students have never attempted.
- *Identifying the caller from the report,* phase 7's first half, and stating what it conceals, the second.
- *Caller C's queue amplification,* which is deliberately beyond reach and is left unresolved with the mechanism named.
- *A seven-week bridge to S004* in phase 2.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Deriving the amortised bound.* Worked, not generated. The potential-function formulation is MS-strand work and appears at S022.
- *Implementing hash functions or collision strategies.* Refused; CF102's conventional work.
- *Explaining queueing.* Planted, deferred to S019.
- *Comparing hash tables against alternatives.* Refused. The day is about two claims regarding one structure, and a comparison would return it to the catalogue.
- *Explaining how a rate limiter is built.* Refused. The day uses the throttle as a phenomenon with documented behaviour; token buckets, admission control and queue disciplines are S019's.
- *Resolving the concealment question.* Deliberately left open, and defended in §8.

**The scope statement on the platform instance, in strong form.**

The day teaches that a Foundry deployment under quota behaves like a structure with a manufactured tail, and that a request has two true token counts. Both are simplifications and both carry stated conditions.

The throttle account is **safe** for reasoning about what a caller experiences and what a report to that caller must contain: that the boundary is temporal rather than load-proportional, that an identical request can succeed and fail a second apart, and that a retried 429 has moved the cost onto the caller's clock rather than removed it. It is **unsafe** as an account of *why* the limit is where it is, because that requires capacity and admission-control material the students do not have until Q7; the day names the deferral rather than gesturing at it.

The two-counts account is **safe** as the claim that the number deciding throttling and the number deciding billing are documented as different, and that the response body reports only one of them. It is **unsafe** as an arithmetic — no relationship between the two is published and none is asserted here — and it is **unsafe at any version other than the one in front of you**, since a rate-limit estimation rule is a service implementation detail that carries no stability guarantee and has been revised before. The same condition binds the retry defaults, the per-minute reset and dynamic quota's preview status.

The artefact element enforcing the boundary is step 6. A student whose two reports rest on a claim outside these ranges — a promised worst case, a stated cause for the limit, an inferred relationship between the two counts — fails the specification on the ground that the reports must be true, not merely different.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **a disagreement the room cannot resolve by knowing more**.

Phase 1 item 2 — uniform 10ms against a 4ms average with 900ms spikes — divides the room, and the division is stable under discussion because both answers are correct for different callers. Students arrive expecting one of the two positions to be revealed as naive, and neither is. Item 3, which asks for whom their own answer is wrong, is the move that converts an argument into a question, and most students cannot make it unaided.

That structure is more engaging than a straightforward reveal because it withholds the satisfaction of being right while offering something better — a reframing that makes the disagreement dissolve. Phase 4 point 6 delivers it.

Step 6 carries a different motivation, closer to craft than to puzzle. Producing two true, materially different reports is a writing task with a definite standard, and students find it satisfying in the way a well-turned proof is satisfying. Phase 7's concealment question then makes it uncomfortable, which is the intended sequence: the satisfaction comes first, and the disquiet arrives after the skill has been enjoyed.

Competence is protected by the fact that phase 3's callers A, B and D are tractable and only C is not, and by the explicit statement that C requires a mechanism the students have not been taught.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Item 1 tests whether a claim about a sequence can be violated by an individual, which is the day's distinction in its simplest form. Item 2 produces the disagreement. Item 3 is the perspective shift and is the day's diagnostic. Item 4 is calibration on the room rather than on oneself, which is the S004 construction reused.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** The final item asks which S004 failure class an unqualified constant-time claim commits, and the answer — unstated assumption — makes the day continuous with the argument strand. This is the module's longest bridge and it is answerable, which is the design's alternation working as intended.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four callers, one structure, a distributional adjudicator, then the same four callers executed against a real deployment. The module's first contrast across callers rather than across structures, which is the class-2 move being previewed two days before S015 makes it explicit. Caller C is the planted case: groups will identify that something is wrong and will not have the mechanism, which is the productive-failure state S019 resolves. The four platform cases mirror the four analytic callers one-to-one, which is a deliberate load decision — the phase teaches one contrast twice rather than two contrasts once — and case D is the payload because it is the case where the fix the room proposes turns out to be a redistribution of the cost onto somebody who was not consulted.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Point 3 is the framing correction §8 objection 2 conceded was missing and is now made: the general phenomenon is stated first and amortised resizing is introduced as the instance you can see all the way through today. Point 4 is worked completely. Point 5 is the day's most important sentence and is stated negatively — amortised analysis does not bound any individual operation — because the positive framing is what students already over-extend. Point 6 makes the four claims parallel, assigns each a reader, and now states what the list omits. Point 8 names tail latency and defers it. Point 9 is the platform's own instance of step 6 and is the design's strongest available answer to the objection that the two-report demand is artificial. Point 10 carries the perishable material with its strong-form scope.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Six steps on a real component under real load, ending in an instrumented build rather than an analysis. Steps 1 and 2 are the claim work; steps 3 to 5 are the commit-measure-reconcile structure on its fifth application; step 6 is the payload. Step 4's requirement that latency, reported usage and non-200 status be recorded *separately* is what makes step 6 possible at all: a student with one aggregate number can only paraphrase it, and the specification's assertional-difference clause would then be unsatisfiable rather than demanding.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** Two disclosures, deliberately paired. A resize-spike incident where the average looked healthy throughout, with attention to *what the monitoring reported*, which seeds S068's detectability material and S079's alerting material. Then the agent case: a retry wrapper that treated a permanent 400 as a transient 429, converting an immediate failure into a slow one while leaving the success rate unchanged. Both are systems that concealed a problem from the person who could have acted on it — one by averaging, one by retrying — and the pairing is the phase's argument. The review detail is the transferable part: the reviewers checked whether errors were *handled* and not whether they were *distinguished*.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Identify the caller, then state what the report conceals and whether that is legitimate. The second half is left unresolved, with the instructor explicitly declining to answer and naming S050 and S088 as where it is taken up. Declining is the design decision; the question is too important to be settled in ten minutes in week thirteen.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** The item 3 answer rate before and after instruction is the day's cleanest learning measure. The final commitment — name an average presented as a property — is deliberately aimed at claims students have *heard*, including from staff, which is an invitation the programme intends to honour.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with production experience will know about tail latency and may find points 5 and 8 familiar. Predicted failure: they produce two competent step 6 reports and treat phase 7's concealment question as obvious — of course you tell each caller what is relevant to them — without noticing that this is precisely the reasoning that produces misleading status reports. Countermeasure: their partner is briefed to press the legitimacy question hardest against confident answers, and the instructor's refusal to resolve it applies equally to them.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The day's high interactivity falls hardest here. A student who has not internalised S012's decomposition will struggle to hold four claim types across four callers. Countermeasures: the worked amortised argument; the supplied distribution; the supplied prediction template at step 3; and the fact that only step 6 is fully independent. Residual risk: step 3's distribution prediction may be guessed rather than reasoned, in which case step 5's reconciliation has nothing to correct. The template mitigates this by requiring a *mechanism* for the predicted tail, not just a shape.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Callers A, B and D are separable by most groups within twenty minutes. The difficulty is concentrated in C and in the closing task — stating what claim would be adequate — which is hard for B as well as C. Fallback: ask the group whether any single number could serve caller B adequately, which forces them toward a distributional answer and previews S028.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **cynicism about reporting**, arriving at phase 7. A student who has just produced two true reports from one measurement, and has been told the legitimacy question will not be answered for two years, may reasonably conclude that engineering communication is spin with better vocabulary. This is a serious risk and the design's countermeasures are weak: the instructor states that the question is genuinely open, that the programme has a position it will not state yet, and that the reason for the delay is that the answer depends on material the student does not have. Whether that reads as intellectual honesty or as evasion depends on the instructor, which is not a design property.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by S019 (contention and queueing, which resolves caller C at a two-week gap), S022 (amortisation and expected cost at class 2, where the potential function arrives), S028 (sampling and intervals, where the distribution becomes an object of inference), S050 (saying what the measurement does not license, which answers phase 7's first half), S057 (dense retrieval), S062 (batching and the latency you promised) and S088 (residual risk, stated plainly, which answers phase 7's second half).

The phase 7 question is the longest deliberate open loop in the programme — thirteen weeks to S050 for the epistemic half and roughly two years to S088 for the ethical half. Students are told this.

Retained content: the four claim types, the caller-relative adequacy principle, and tail latency as the honest report. Three items.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 6.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence.

One specification item in CF102 requiring both claims, two named callers, the committed distribution prediction with mechanism, the measurement from the instrumented build, the omitted-mechanism reconciliation, the two reports, and the three artefact elements.

The distinctive clause: **two reports differing only in wording fail; they must differ in what they assert.** This is the difference between audience-appropriate communication and presentation, and it is checkable — a report for a batch caller that omits the tail and a report for an interactive caller that leads with it make different claims, while two paraphrases of the same claim make one.

The second distinctive clause: **an intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the build records a retried 429 rather than absorbing it. The exemption is a property of the shipped instrumentation and is therefore markable, which a general detectability claim would not be.

Programme instrumentation: the phase 1 item 3 before-and-after rate is the day's learning measure and the cohort's perspective-shift baseline; step 3 tail predictions against measured tails extend the calibration series into distributional prediction; the phase 7 legitimacy positions are recorded in aggregate and returned to the cohort at S088, which is the longest instrumentation loop in the programme; the phase 7 third question's positions are recorded alongside them as the paired comparison for §9 prediction 5; and the phase 1 Copilot prediction, scored at phase 8, extends the frontier map. Rule 4 branch for the frontier item: a cohort that does not anticipate the agent's failure to distinguish transient from permanent errors receives that distinction as an explicit S019 pretest item, and the result is read there.

Dispositions: **integrity**, observable at step 6 in whether the report for the tolerant caller omits something the student knows matters.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: teaching students to write two different true reports teaches them to manage stakeholders, and the day's refusal to say when that becomes dishonest makes it worse.**

At its strongest: step 6 is a rehearsal of selective disclosure. The skill is real and is used, and it is also the mechanism by which organisations conceal problems from the people who could act on them — a status report to the person paying that omits the tail, a report to the operator that omits the cost. The day teaches the technique, demonstrates that it produces two defensible artefacts, and then explicitly declines to say where the line is, for two years. A student who takes away the technique and not the disquiet has been trained in exactly the practice the programme claims to oppose, and the design has chosen this ordering deliberately.

The rebuttal is that the alternative is worse and the objection assumes a choice the design does not have. Engineers write audience-directed reports; this is not optional and not corrigible. The realistic options are that students learn to do it reflectively or that they learn to do it unreflectively, and there is no option in which they do not do it. The day makes the practice visible, names it, and immediately attaches a question to it — which is more than the practice usually receives anywhere in an engineering education.

The concession on timing is nonetheless substantial and the design accepts it. Two years is a long time to leave an ethical question open after teaching its associated technique, and the risk that the technique consolidates without the disquiet is real. The mitigation — recording the cohort's phase 7 legitimacy positions and returning them at S088 — is a genuine instrument and it is also a two-year-delayed one. A stronger design would resolve the epistemic half at S050 and the ethical half at S069, thirty weeks earlier, and there is no strong reason the ethical half must wait for S088. That resequencing is not currently made.

**Objection 2: the day's central distinction is not amortisation at all, and calling it that obscures what students actually need.**

At its strongest: the interesting distinction in phase 1 item 2 is between a mean and a tail, which is a distributional question, not an amortisation question. Amortised analysis is a specific technique for bounding sequences with occasional expensive operations; tail latency arises from many mechanisms of which amortised resizing is a minor one — garbage collection, lock contention, scheduling, network variance. By teaching the distribution question through the amortisation lens, the day attaches a general and important idea to a narrow and comparatively unimportant technique, and students may come away believing that tail latency is a data-structure phenomenon.

This objection has considerable force and the design partly concedes it. The distributional question *is* the more important idea, and amortisation *is* a narrow instance. The day's defence is one of tractability: amortised resizing is the only tail-producing mechanism whose cause students can fully understand in week thirteen, because they can see the doubling, count the copies and predict the spike exactly. Garbage collection, contention and scheduling are all better examples and all require M04 material the students do not have.

**The concession is discharged.** This paper previously conceded that the rationale was not stated to students and that point 3's framing put amortisation at the centre rather than treating it as an instance — while points 4, 5 and 7 gestured at the general idea and the load-bearing point did not. **Phase 4 point 3 now states the general phenomenon first, names five of its causes, and introduces amortised resizing as the one instance that can be seen all the way through today.** The repair is annotated at the point of change. The platform layer strengthens it at no cost: the per-minute counter boundary is a second tail-producing mechanism, with a completely different cause, that students can also see all the way through — which converts "the general idea, illustrated once" into "the general idea, illustrated twice with disjoint mechanisms," and that is the form in which a general idea is actually learned rather than attached to its example.

**Objection 3: adding a live quota to this day gives students a professional excuse for reporting the number that flatters, and the day hands it to them at exactly the wrong moment.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection that bites hardest on the practical turn: phase 4 point 9 tells students that the platform itself maintains two true token counts for one request and reports only one. The design offers this as vindication — see, the two-report demand is a real situation. But a student who has spent phase 5 producing two true reports, and is then shown that a major vendor's own service does the same thing, has been given something better than a technique. They have been given a **precedent**. The lesson available on the way out of the room is not "audience-directed reporting is a professional responsibility to be exercised carefully" but "everyone does this, including the people who built the thing you are calling." And phase 7 then declines to say where the line is. The design has strengthened its most dangerous element and postponed its correction.

**This objection is conceded, and the cheap fix is named.** The rebuttal available — that the platform's two counts are an implementation necessity rather than a communication choice, since an admission controller cannot know the true count before processing — is *correct and insufficient*, because the necessity explains the two numbers and does not explain reporting only one. The vendor could publish the admission estimate in the response and does not, and that is a choice about what a caller is told. **The cheap fix is to make the platform the object of the phase 7 question rather than a supporting example:** the pair's second task becomes *what does the response body conceal from you, and would you call that legitimate?* — asked about a service the student did not build and has no stake in defending. It costs one sentence, it is in the design, and it moves the student from the position of the person deciding what to disclose to the position of the person who was not told. That is a materially easier place to notice the problem from, and it is available thirteen weeks before S050 and two years before S088 without pre-empting either.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Phase 1 item 3 will be answered substantively by fewer than a third of students before instruction, and by a large majority when re-asked at phase 8. *Instrument:* item 3 before-and-after, coded for whether a caller is named.

**Prediction 2.** Step 3 tail predictions will underestimate the measured tail for a large majority, and the underestimate will be larger for students who predicted a shape without naming a mechanism. *Instrument:* predicted against measured tail, split by whether the template's mechanism field was completed substantively.

**Prediction 3.** In phase 7, a majority of students will initially judge the concealment legitimate, and the proportion will be lower among students whose own step 6 reports differed substantively rather than in emphasis. *Instrument:* phase 7 positions recorded in aggregate, against blind-rated report differentiation.

**Prediction 4.** At S088, students' stated positions on residual-risk disclosure will differ from their recorded S013 positions in a consistent direction rather than randomly. *Instrument:* the S013 aggregate returned and re-elicited at S088. **This is a two-year loop and the direction of change is the finding; no change would indicate that neither day is moving anything.**

**Prediction 5.** Students will judge the platform's non-reporting of the admission estimate illegitimate at a higher rate than they judge their own step 6 omissions illegitimate, within the same thirty minutes. *Instrument:* phase 7's two legitimacy positions, paired within student. **The gap is the claim, and its size is the day's estimate of how much of the concealment question is about seat rather than about principle.** A zero gap would mean the third question adds nothing and should be removed.

**Prediction 6.** A majority of phase 1 Copilot predictions will say the agent retries the 429 and will not say whether it distinguishes the 400. *Instrument:* sealed predictions coded for whether the 429/400 distinction is mentioned, against the agent's actual behaviour.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.**

*What the documentation says.* Microsoft Learn states that assigning TPM to a deployment sets both the tokens-per-minute and requests-per-minute rate limits; that **rate limits are enforced against the maximum number of tokens *estimated* to be processed at the time the request is received**, which "isn't the same as the token count used for billing, which is computed after all processing is completed"; that the estimate is derived in part from the **character count** of the request and includes the prompt, `max_tokens` and `best_of`; that the running count **resets each minute**; and that requests above the limit receive **429** until the counter resets ([Manage Azure OpenAI in Microsoft Foundry Models quota](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/foundry/openai/how-to/quota#understanding-rate-limits)). Dynamic quota is documented as a **preview** feature that lets a standard deployment opportunistically exceed its configured TPM when capacity exists, with the extra requests "still billed at the regular pricing rates", and it can only ever increase the available quota ([Azure OpenAI Dynamic quota](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/ai-foundry/openai/how-to/dynamic-quota)). The embeddings response body is documented as returning `usage.prompt_tokens` and `usage.total_tokens` ([Azure OpenAI Embeddings, API version v1](https://learn.microsoft.com/rest/api/microsoft-foundry/azureopenai/embeddings)). The Azure MCP Server's retry defaults are three attempts, exponential mode, a two-second base, a ten-second cap and a hundred-second network timeout ([Azure MCP Server tools](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/developer/azure-mcp-server/tools/#tool-parameters)).

*At what version and date.* All pages retrieved 2026-07-26. The embeddings REST reference is at API version `v1`. Dynamic quota is labelled preview in its own title. The quota page exists in current, Foundry-classic and Azure-AI-Foundry variants whose text is near-identical; the Foundry variant is the one cited, and the existence of three live variants of the same page is itself a perishability signal recorded here.

*What the documentation does not report that a reader of this day would need.* Four silences. **It does not publish the estimation function**, so a caller cannot compute in advance whether a given request will be admitted — the day therefore teaches the two-counts distinction and asserts no relationship between them. **It does not report the response header or field that would expose the admission estimate**, and the day's phase 7 third question is built on that absence. **It reports no latency distribution, percentile or tail figure for any deployment type**, which is the quantity this entire day is about; the sampling bound is precise and the distribution it bounds is undocumented, which is the folder-level asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](/research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md) appearing in its sharpest form. **And it states no interaction between the documented retry defaults and the per-minute reset**, so a caller's worst-case wait is not derivable from the documentation. No magnitude is asserted for any of these anywhere in this paper.

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The four-item phase 1 and item 3's perspective-shift construction; the phase 1 Copilot prediction; the four-caller contrast, caller C as a planted queueing case, and the four executed platform cases mirroring them one-to-one; case D as the payload; the ten points of phase 4, the negative framing of point 5, and the general-before-instance repair at point 3; the six-step phase 5 with a separately instrumented build and the two-report requirement at step 6; the supplied distribution-prediction template and its mechanism field; phase 7's identify-then-concealment protocol, the instructor's explicit refusal to resolve it, and the third question about the response body; the specification clause requiring assertional rather than verbal difference; the two-year instrumentation loop to S088; and all six §9 predictions are this design's own reasoning, untested. The §8 objection-1 concession accepts that the ethical question is left open longer than necessary and identifies an unmade resequencing to S069. The objection-2 concession is **now discharged in the day** — point 3 states the general phenomenon before the technique — and the discharge is annotated rather than absorbed. The objection-3 concession accepts that the platform layer supplies a precedent for selective reporting at the moment the student is least equipped to resist it, and names the phase 7 third question as the cheap fix.
