## In plain terms

The person running a discovery session is usually the most technically capable person present, and that is a problem rather than an advantage.

Everything an engineer knows about being useful — correcting an error, supplying a missing option, filling an awkward gap with a helpful example — transfers into the facilitator's chair as damage. Each of those moves takes authority away from the person whose situation you came to learn about and gives it to you. **You end up in a conversation about your understanding rather than theirs, and it will feel like it is going well.**

So facilitation is taught here as a technical skill with observable behaviours and, more importantly, with prohibitions. A prohibition is what you need when your judgement is degraded, which is exactly when you are running late, the room is quiet and you badly want to rescue it.

The session also teaches something the corpus contains but does not present as teaching material: a facilitator wellness document. It tells people to protect sleep and meals, not to run consecutive intensive engagements, to rotate the demanding roles, and to watch colleagues for signs of difficulty. **Nobody writes those instructions prospectively.** It is an incident record in the grammar of advice, and its remedies appear in none of the documents that actually schedule anyone's time.

And then the session does something a paper-only version could not. It has the learner write the same forbidden list as a constraint an agent must obey, and then break it on purpose. In a room, "I didn't supply options" is a memory you have every reason to be generous about. In a transcript it is a line of text or it is not — **and when the constraint holds perfectly and the questions are still bad, the learner has met, in ten minutes, the distinction the measurement stages spend a module on.**

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching facilitation as a bounded technical skill rather than as a disposition, and defends requiring the learner to render their own prohibitions as machine-checkable constraints.

The argument has four parts. **First**, the expert's habitual helpfulness is the principal failure mechanism in a discovery session, because correction and supplied options both transfer authority to the person who came to learn. **Second**, the useful unit of instruction is a self-imposed prohibition rather than a repertoire, because a prohibition binds under exactly the conditions — time pressure, a watching room, a segment that is not landing — in which a technique cannot be selected. **Third**, structural changes to what speaking costs are more reliable than encouragement, because encouragement does not alter either side of the calculation a participant is actually making. **Fourth**, a prohibition that cannot be checked by reading the record is not yet a prohibition, and an agent configuration is the cheapest available instrument for finding out which of a learner's prohibitions are real.

The paper records an amendment made while writing it: the session as first designed asked learners to keep a forbidden list whose observance could only be reported by the learner or by a peer watching for ninety seconds, in a module whose thesis is that self-reported observance of an invisible rule is worthless. The correction adds the platform artefact and is documented at §4.5 rather than applied silently.

The paper concedes that withholding expertise from a factually mistaken customer is a real cost and that the prohibition does not survive a safety or security case; that the psychological-safety construct is being applied well outside the setting this repository has verified; that no facilitation technique in the corpus has ever been compared with any other; and that an enforceable constraint tests output shape and cannot test whether a question was worth asking.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Facilitation is treated in most engineering organisations as a personality trait: some people are good with customers.

That framing has two costs. It makes the skill untrainable, so nobody trains it. And it makes failure attributable to the individual rather than to a practice, so nothing is learned when a session goes badly.

The specific mechanism the session targets is the transfer of authority. A facilitator who corrects a factual error, however gently and however correctly, has established that the room's business is getting things right by their standard. **After that, participants stop volunteering the messy true account and start producing the tidy account they think is wanted**, and the facilitator's expertise — which is real — has removed the only thing the session was for.

The second mechanism is the supplied option. A question followed by examples is not the same question, because the examples become the response set. This is the same defect that [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) diagnoses in written form, met here in its live form, and meeting it live first is deliberate.

There is a third problem underneath both, and it is what the platform work exists to reach. **A facilitator has no record of their own behaviour.** The moves that cause the damage are invisible to the person making them and unremarkable to the person receiving them, so the practice cannot improve by reflection: there is nothing to reflect on. A session that teaches prohibitions and provides no record against which to check them has asked the learner to police a rule using the faculty the rule exists to distrust.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** Any reader who has run a meeting has enough. [Stage 1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md) adds [S004](/wiki/seminars/S004.md)'s room-reading, which the facilitation plan uses but does not require.

The prior it works against is that a good facilitator keeps things moving. Learners arrive believing that silence, confusion and dead air are failures to be repaired. **The session's central move is that some of them are the instrument working.**

The platform work splits the cohort. Learners who already configure agents will write elegant constraint files and will under-rate the enforceability question, because a well-formed file feels binding. Learners who do not will write prose that reads as guidance and will discover that nothing enforces it. **Neither group arrives holding the fact that decides the exercise: that whether a constraint holds is a property of the host and the model rather than of the file.**

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Low intrinsic, high affective. The material is small; the difficulty is doing less than you know how to do, in front of peers, while being marked on restraint. That is why the drill is short and repeated rather than long and single. The platform movement adds a different load: the learner is confronting a record of their own behaviour, which is uncomfortable in a way that reading about the behaviour is not.

**3.2 Why prohibitions rather than techniques.** A technique is selected when you have capacity to select. Under time pressure, in a quiet room, with an audience, capacity is what is missing. A pre-committed prohibition removes the selection. **This is design reasoning; no source behind this session tests it.**

**3.3 Why structure rather than encouragement.** People withhold what they know at work because of a rapid, largely unconscious calculation about interpersonal risk, and the calculation is asymmetric: staying quiet has an immediate and certain benefit, speaking has a delayed and uncertain one ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)). Inviting candour does not alter either side of that. Changing who is present, what is asked, and of whom, does.

The construct's negative definition is the highest-value part of the source for a facilitator, because the failure mode when this material reaches a room is that it collapses into being nice, which inverts it. In the author's own words it is "about candor, about making it possible for productive disagreement and free exchange of ideas", and "nice, in short, is not synonymous with psychologically safe". It is also **not immunity from consequences**, not a personality factor, not another word for trust, and not lowered standards. And it is **not sufficient**: it takes off the brakes rather than powering the car.

One further property decides how a facilitator can use it at all: **silence is invisible.** A moment where somebody could have spoken and did not leaves no trace except in the person who held back. You will therefore never receive feedback about what your session failed to elicit, which forecloses learning by outcome and is the strongest available argument for learning by prohibition.

**Its limits, and they are load-bearing.** The 2019 trade book was read in full for this repository; **the 1999 empirical paper it restates has not been read here**, and a summary written two decades later by the same author is not the paper — the paper's two theoretically load-bearing findings do not appear in the book at all. The book's review of the literature is narrative rather than systematic: no protocol, no inclusion criteria, no quality appraisal, and **no disconfirming study reported anywhere**, in a literature where the reviewer is the most-cited author. Its case studies are secondary and outcome-selected. Its leadership framework is drawn partly from consulting experience and **no trial of it is reported.** And it contradicts itself on comfort, asserting it in the introduction and denying it a chapter later. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.**

The quantitative synthesis is what earns the construct curricular time in a programme sceptical of disposition training ([research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)). It aggregates many independent samples across individuals and groups, **ranks** antecedents rather than listing them, tests **incremental validity** against positive leader relations and work engagement — the sceptic's question asked properly — tests moderation by research design and by national culture, and **tests rather than assumes** homology across levels.

**Its limits.** This repository's note is written from the abstract, which **reports no numerical effect sizes at all**; no correlation, ranking or magnitude may be taken from it and none is. The pooled literature is predominantly cross-sectional and self-report, so **correlation does not establish that raising safety raises anything.** Whether homology was supported is not recorded either way — which matters directly here, because a group-level construct is being applied to a room of strangers meeting once.

**3.4 Why silence is taught with a warning attached.** The field-interviewing source that supplies the drill is explicit that silence is read differently by different people and in different cultures — it can create shared readiness, signal effort, expose confusion, or produce stress — and that it must be read and adjusted to rather than applied mechanically ([research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md](/research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md)). **The drill can teach a bad habit if that warning is dropped**, and the session states it in the same movement.

The same source supplies the correction prohibition and its exception. Do not correct a mistaken product belief or repair a workflow during data gathering, because either shifts authority to the interviewer and may convert research into support; a correction may be offered afterwards **if it benefits the participant rather than the interviewer's need to be right.** It also supplies the diagnostic posture for a reticent participant: several possible causes, only one of which is about the person — a different rhythm, a need for more structure or silence, a reaction to the topic, a better fit with another interviewer, or inhibition by observers or a manager. **The field team must diagnose rather than label somebody difficult.**

**Its limits.** A first-person practitioner synthesis with **no controlled trials, no sampling framework for its cases, no measured causal effects and no validated saturation threshold**; its own empathy claim rests on an analogy in a footnote. This is the 2013 first edition and nothing from the later edition is available here.

**3.5 Why facilitation is a staffing decision as much as a personal skill.** The design-research literature treats interpretation as a team activity with **defined roles that keep the session moving and make participation active**, and treats external representations as working surfaces rather than as documentation produced after thinking — because notes and models hold the conversation where everyone can inspect and challenge it, and **reduce dependence on memory, status and rhetorical force** ([research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md)). It also insists that facilitation and project management are part of the method rather than support around it, and that rooms, materials **and rest** are scheduled items.

That is the deep reason the remote provision must name a person rather than an intention, and the deep reason the wellness amendment must enter the plan rather than sit beside it.

**Its limits.** Practitioner experience rather than comparative experimental evidence; role structures and timings are heuristics; and it states that observation is not neutral — the method manages subjectivity and does not eliminate it.

**3.6 Why the wellness document is evidence rather than pastoral care.** The corpus's facilitator wellness guidance covers sleep, meals, consecutive engagements, role rotation and peer observation ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). Two properties are taught: that its justification has been shifted onto delivery effectiveness rather than obligation to the person, and that **its remedies appear in none of the artefacts that schedule work.** Advisory guidance inside a system of checklists is guidance that loses.

**Its limits.** Access-controlled, white-labelled, uncheckable by any public reader; a stratum contradicting itself in many places; no failed session, no comparison and no outcome anywhere in it.

**3.7 Why the prohibitions become configuration.**

The platform's contribution is a single property: **a constraint written as configuration has a truth value that a constraint written as an intention does not.**

The published agent-configuration corpus is the clearest available worked example of the idea at scale ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)). Its organising claim is that a coding agent's behaviour is an engineering artefact and should therefore be authored, versioned, reviewed and distributed like any other, and it separates **instructions** — constraints applied automatically to matching work — from prompts, agents and skills, which have different application models.

Three of its declarations transfer into a facilitation plan without modification. **Execution completion and acceptance outcome are different results**, so a segment that ran is not a segment that worked. **An agent may not record a human's approval**, and a workflow must stop where a required human review is absent. And the corpus is candid where most tooling is not: its behavioural checks **test output shape and not correctness**, a check may be **advisory at one tier and authoritative at another**, and its untrusted-content boundary is a declaration whose effectiveness depends on the host attaching it and the model complying.

**That last cluster is the session's real platform lesson, and it is a negative one.** A facilitation mode can enforce that no example was appended to a question. It cannot enforce that the question was worth asking. A learner who leaves believing a green run means good facilitation has learned the wrong thing from an unusually honest instrument — and the corpus's own sentence is the one to carry out of the module: **the existence of a control is not evidence that the control works.**

**Its limits.** Read at a pinned commit at which one of its own checks was failing, recorded rather than tidied away. **Perishability extreme** — paths, counts, plugin names and package versions moved while it was being read. **Host and model dependence is total**: every artefact is inert without a host that loads it, and a result on one client and model is not a result about the corpus. It contains benefit vocabulary its own evidence does not support. **A grader exists; no result corpus does.** And nothing in it licenses a claim about learning.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The drill is in threes and the third role is the point.** The observer's record — every supplied option, every interruption and every occasion the facilitator answered their own question — is the only place where the failure is visible, because it is invisible to the facilitator and unremarkable to the participant.

**4.2 The forbidden list must contain something the learner is inclined to do.** A prohibition against a move you would never make is a costless signal. This is the same requirement as [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s condition capable of firing, applied to behaviour rather than to a position.

**4.3 The wellness reading is placed here rather than in a pastoral session** because it is evidence about the practice rather than about the practitioner. Two properties are taught: that its justification has been shifted onto delivery effectiveness rather than obligation to the person, and that **its remedies appear in none of the artefacts that schedule work** — not the lifecycle, not the timeline, not the checklist, not the readiness gate. Advisory guidance inside a system of checklists is guidance that loses, and the learner is required to move one item from the advisory layer into their own plan.

**4.4 What the platform workflow is for.** Not tooling competence. It makes two things observable that prose cannot, and the second is the more valuable.

The first is that **a prohibition has a truth value.** Written as an intention, observance is a memory the learner has every reason to be generous about. Written as a constraint, with a transcript, it is a line of text or it is not.

The second is the deliberate disappointment. When the constraints all hold and the questions are still bad, the learner has met the distinction between **output shape and correctness** — the same distinction the corpus states about its own graders, and the same one that makes a green check dangerous. Step 6 is constructed so that the learner also discovers a third possibility, which is that the constraint did not hold and nothing announced it: **whether a rule binds is a property of the host and the model, not of the file.**

The attack step is placed after both, and asks the agent for the three situations in which obeying the list would produce a worse session than breaking it. That question is the only mechanism in the session that protects against the failure §8's first objection names.

**4.5 A defect found in this session's own design, and the amendment.**

The session as first written produced one artefact: a facilitation plan with a forbidden list, reviewed by two peers watching a ninety-second segment.

**Observance of the prohibitions was therefore established by the learner's own report, or by a peer watching a fraction of one segment** — in a session whose central claim is that the facilitator's own perception is exactly the faculty that cannot see these failures. The design taught that silence is invisible, that a supplied option is unremarkable to the participant, and that the moves which do the damage leave no trace, and then asked the learner to police those moves using the faculty it had just discredited.

Worse, the session's own standard — that guidance not sitting in the artefact which schedules the work does not bind — applied to itself. The forbidden list lived in a plan and was checked by goodwill.

The amendment adds produce-items 6 to 8 and workflow steps 2 to 7. **It does not assess the configuration**, and this is deliberate: assessing a constraint file would produce learners writing enforceable prohibitions rather than useful ones, which is the failure §8's third objection describes.

**The amendment is recorded rather than silently applied.** Assume there are others this paper has not caught.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The costless forbidden list.** The learner forbids themselves moves they were never going to make. *Countermeasure:* the review movement, where a peer names which item was broken; a list that survives is required to be re-tested against a harder participant.

**Encouragement substituted for structure.** The plan says the facilitator will make people comfortable and specifies nothing observable. *Countermeasure:* the plan's required field is what the segment does *before* the question, not what the facilitator says. Exercise 2 supplies the enforceability test that catches it.

**The correction deferred and then made anyway.** The learner holds the correction through the segment and delivers it in the debrief, believing the prohibition satisfied. *Countermeasure:* partial. The session states that a correction may be offered afterwards only where it benefits the participant rather than the facilitator's need to be right, which is a judgement the session cannot verify.

**Wellness read as a welfare topic.** The learner treats the reading as a message about self-care and changes nothing structural. *Countermeasure:* the required amendment must appear in the facilitation plan itself, which is an assessed artefact at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md).

**The mechanical wait.** The learner leaves with "always wait" rather than "never rescue", and waits through a silence that was distress. *Countermeasure:* the cultural-variation warning is stated in the same movement as the drill and repeated in the failure-mode list, and §3.4 is released before the session for this reason alone. **It is a weak countermeasure against a rule that is easier to remember than its exception.**

**Shape mistaken for quality.** The learner's constraints all hold and they conclude the facilitation was good. *Countermeasure:* the corpus's own statement that behavioural checks test output shape rather than correctness, taught in the same movement, and the attack step's second question. Prediction 5 in §9 predicts this countermeasure failing.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S014](/wiki/seminars/S014.md), [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md), [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) and [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md).

The supplied-option failure is met live here and in written form at [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md), one module apart, deliberately: the learner should recognise the specimen answers in the question bank as the thing they were forbidden from doing aloud.

The platform artefact exports two things at very different ranges. The **enforceability test** — could a reader of the record tell that this had been broken — returns at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md) as the self-recorded breach and much later wherever this programme distinguishes a control from a check. The **output-shape-versus-correctness** distinction is the long export: it is installed here as a ten-minute disappointment and is the thing the evaluation stages are built on.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence.** One specification item at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md): that the prohibitions in the forbidden list were honoured during the assigned difficulty, or that the breach was recorded by the learner rather than found by the assessor.

**Never assessed:** whether the room went well; the quality, enforceability or completeness of the constraint file; and whether the constraint held on the learner's host.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: withholding expertise from a customer who is factually wrong is a disservice to them and an abdication.**

At its strongest: the customer is paying for judgement. If an engineer sits through a description of a deployment process containing a serious error and says nothing, the customer leaves with the error intact, and the engineer has prioritised the purity of their data collection over the interests of the person in front of them. In a safety-relevant or security-relevant case this is not merely unhelpful, it is wrong.

**Conceded in the safety and security case without qualification.** The prohibition does not extend to a situation where silence leaves somebody exposed, and a facilitator who reads it that way has read it wrong. In the ordinary case the rebuttal holds: the correction can be made after the session, when it costs the data nothing, and the interviewing sources say the same. **The declined fix is a general exception for material errors.** It is declined because *material* is judged by the person holding the expertise, in the moment, under exactly the conditions that produce the failure — which makes the exception the rule.

**Objection 2: the psychological-safety material is being used far outside what this repository has verified.**

At its strongest: the construct comes from a trade book read for this repository, whose underlying empirical paper has not been read here, whose narrative review reports no disconfirming study, and whose case material is largely secondary and outcome-selected. Its quantitative companion is held here from an abstract that reports no effect sizes at all and does not record whether the construct behaves the same way at the level this session applies it to. Using all of that to justify a claim about how a customer workshop should be structured extends a construct developed for standing teams to a room of strangers meeting once, and no source establishes that it transfers.

**Conceded.** The transfer is unwarranted and is design reasoning. The paper's position is that the mechanism — silence as a risk calculation rather than a disposition — is the part being borrowed, and that mechanisms travel further than findings. **The declined fix is to remove the material and teach the drill alone.** It is declined because the drill without the mechanism teaches a trick, and a facilitator who waits through silence without understanding what the silence might cost the other person will wait through the wrong silences — which is the failure the cultural-variation warning exists to prevent and which prediction 4 measures.

**Objection 3: encoding prohibitions as agent constraints will produce facilitators who write enforceable rules instead of useful ones.**

At its strongest, and it is the objection the §4.5 amendment most deserves: the exercise rewards a property — checkability by reading a transcript — that is orthogonal to good facilitation. The rules that matter most in a room are precisely the unenforceable ones: notice when somebody is uncomfortable, change what you were going to do next, hear the thing that was said quietly. A learner taught to discard prohibitions a reader could not verify has been taught to discard the judgement half of the skill, and has been handed a green check as consolation. Worse, the corpus supplying the pattern says of its own graders that they test output shape rather than correctness — so the design has knowingly built a movement around an instrument that cannot see the thing it is teaching.

**Partially conceded, and this is the amendment's real cost.** Three defences, none complete. The forbidden list is written on paper **before** the file, so the enforceable set is derived from the practice rather than the practice from the tooling. The configuration is **not assessed**, so there is no mark to optimise. And the shape-versus-correctness limitation is taught explicitly in the same movement, from the source that admits it about itself, rather than being left for the learner to discover. **The declined fix was to have learners write the constraint file and never run it** — keeping the enforceability discipline and dropping the transcript — rejected because a file that is never run cannot deliver the disappointment in step 6, and the disappointment is the point. **The cost of that rejection is exactly the narrowing this objection names, and prediction 5 is the only thing that would detect it.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** In the first drill round, most facilitators will supply at least one option, example or completion within their first three questions. *Instrument:* the observer's tally, collected per round.

**Prediction 2.** Forbidden lists written before the first drill will be judged costless more often than those revised after it. *Instrument:* a peer's binary judgement on whether the list contains a move the author is inclined to make, collected at both points.

**Prediction 3.** Learners will name the remote-participant owner less often than any other required field of the plan. *Instrument:* field-presence counts across submitted plans.

**Prediction 4.** The wellness amendment will most often be written as an addition to the plan rather than as a removal from it. *Instrument:* the amendments, coded *adds an activity* / *removes or bounds an activity*. **If additions dominate, the session has taught the learner to document the constraint rather than to obey it**, and the movement should be rewritten to require a removal.

**Prediction 5.** Asked after the platform work what their constraint file established, a majority of learners will describe a property of their questions rather than a property of their questions' form. *Instrument:* a single free-text item, coded *shape* / *quality* / *conflated* by a second reader. **This predicts the failure of §8's third countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 6.** In step 6, the most common observed outcome will be that the constraint did not hold and nothing announced it. *Instrument:* the recorded outcome, coded *held and reported* / *held silently* / *did not hold*, per learner and per host-and-model pair. **If constraints hold reliably across hosts, the session's claim that enforceability is a property of the host rather than the file is wrong and §3.7 should be rewritten.**

**Prediction 7.** Learners instructed to compare their item-4 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 5) will produce plans whose second field names a condition rather than an encouragement more often than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* a second reader's binary judgement, split within cohort on exercise-5 completion. **The same prediction is made at [WP-010](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-010.md), [WP-011](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-011.md) and [WP-012](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-012.md), so a null across all four would indict the comparison pattern rather than any one session.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That facilitation is a trainable technical skill whose most useful unit is a prohibition; that what makes speaking cheap is structural rather than attitudinal; and that a prohibition nobody could check by reading the record is not yet a prohibition.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The corpus's facilitation material is codified experience with no comparison behind it and no public reader can check it. The psychological-safety mechanism is class 2, rests on a book whose underlying paper has not been read here, and is being applied outside the setting it was developed in; **its quantitative companion is held from an abstract that reports no effect sizes at all.** The silence technique comes from a manual that reports no measured effect and warns explicitly against mechanical application. The agent-configuration corpus establishes that behaviour can be authored and checked, was read at a commit where one of its own checks was failing, and **commits no results of any kind.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The prohibition on correcting a participant does not survive a safety or security case, and a rule presented without its exception is a rule that will be broken at the wrong moment. And note §4.5: for its first version this session taught that a facilitator cannot see their own failures and then asked them to police those failures by self-report. **The failure the session names is one the session committed.**
