# WP-037 — The Prototype That Fails Immediately

**Seminar day:** [S037](/wiki/seminars/S037.md) · **Module:** [M08](/wiki/modules/M08.md) · **Quarter:** [Q4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md)

**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Agent Framework evaluations run before the build, on purpose, so the first result is a failure with a cause

## In plain terms

You built the cheap version, you showed it, and it broke. You believe the exercise worked. The sponsor believes it went badly. **Both readings are reasonable and only one of them is available by default.**

The corpus states the claim in a line — a cheap prototype that fails immediately is an instant win — and then says the thing that makes it a professional skill rather than an attitude: **stakeholders are to be actively coached into reading it that way.** That clause concedes the reading is not natural. It also puts the conversion on the engineer rather than on the stakeholder's disposition, which is where it belongs, because the engineer is the only person in the room who knows what the failure cost.

Three things have to be in the sentence you say next. **What broke.** **What that removes from the plan** — a design option, an assumption, a week, a cost — stated in terms the listener holds. **And what it cost to find out**, because the whole claim rests on the price and a failure reported without its price is just a failure.

The session's platform half exists to make the failure arrive with a cause attached rather than as an impression, and it does so by inverting the usual order. **The evaluation is written, committed and run before the artefact exists**, so the first thing anybody learns about the system is a scored failure with a named fault. That inversion is possible because the framework's evaluation model is small — three types, two evaluator tiers, one split decision — and because one of those tiers runs locally without API calls, so the harness costs minutes rather than a day. **An evaluation harness that takes a day to stand up is a release gate, and a release gate cannot produce a failure early enough to be cheap.**

The same platform supplies the session's sharpest single lesson and it is not about agents. **The rule that splits a conversation into a query and a response decides what every number you produce means**, and an agent that recovers from a bad first attempt will pass under one split and fail under another. Neither verdict is wrong. A team that never chose is answering a question it did not ask.

The session's second half is about what happens when the coaching does not work, and the design's position is that this is the normal case rather than the exception. A sponsor who says *this is a setback* has applied a different and defensible frame. **You are not there to change their mind. You are there to leave them holding an accurate account**, and a record in which every stakeholder was successfully converted is a record of a conversation that did not happen.

## Abstract

This paper defends producing a failure deliberately, cheaply and in public, and treats the reframing of that failure as an engineering responsibility rather than as a stakeholder attitude. The argument is that a fast, cheap failure removes options from a plan at a fraction of the cost of discovering the same thing later, that the resulting reading is not the default one, and that the price of the failure is a necessary component of the report rather than an optional one.

The session's platform half supplies the condition under which any of that is legible. An agent is a system that reports success while having selected the wrong tool, passed a wrong argument or ignored what a tool returned — which is the unclear-feedback case in which the error-management literature's own effect is not significant. **The design's response is to require the evaluation harness to exist before the artefact, so the failure arrives with a named cause**, and to make the learner declare a split strategy and justify a threshold, because both silently determine what the resulting number means and neither has any published calibration behind it.

The session installs a failure-first run whose break is chosen from the [S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md) causes, a prohibition on repairing the artefact before showing it, a sealed set of pre-build criteria whose later revision is itself a deliverable, and a recorded account including the cases where the reframing was refused. The paper concedes that the corpus reports no failures at all and so cannot supply a case where the framing was tested, that the learning-design source behind the comparison step supplies no adult professional evidence, that the same source's book and meta-analysis disagree about how far its design generalises to exactly the kind of content this session teaches, and that the boundary condition the platform work is built on **received only limited support in its own authors' analysis.**

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A prototype breaks in front of somebody who is deciding whether to fund the work. Two accounts of that event now exist and they are not compatible.

The engineer's account is that a cheap artefact removed an expensive option from the plan. The sponsor's account is that the thing does not work. **Neither is a misunderstanding.** They are different frames applied to the same event, and the sponsor's is the one that arrives without effort — because in almost every other professional context, a demonstration that fails is a demonstration that failed.

There is a second problem underneath, and it is the one that produces most of the damage. **The engineer, anticipating the sponsor's frame, repairs the prototype before the meeting.** That is a rational response to a social situation and it destroys the exercise: the demonstration now tests the repair, the failure has been converted into private information, and the option that should have been removed from the plan stays in it.

The session's response is to prohibit the repair, require the account, and record the cases where the account did not land.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** [S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md) and [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md) are both required and both are load-bearing.

[S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md) supplies the cause. The break is not invented for the session — it is taken from the pre-mortem's list and produced on purpose, which is what makes this session the pre-mortem's justification rather than a separate exercise. **An imagined failure and a purchased one are different objects and the pairing is the module's argument.**

[S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md) supplies the artefact, and the dependency is affective rather than technical: **a rough thing is much easier to let fail than a finished one.** A learner who arrives with a polished artefact will repair it, whatever the standard says.

The prior the session works against is that failing in front of a client is a professional error. Most learners have been trained, correctly, that it is — and the session is asking them to distinguish the cheap deliberate case from the expensive accidental one, which is a distinction their training does not contain.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The module's affective peak. The intrinsic content is small. The load is in declining to repair something you could repair, and then holding a frame in front of somebody who has already rejected it.

**3.2 Why the price is part of the report.** *A failure is a win* is false in general and true when the failure was cheap. Reporting the failure without the price asserts the general form, which the listener will correctly reject. **The price is not modesty; it is the premise.**

**3.3 Why the case must not be chosen by you.** [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md) established that a result on a chosen input is a result about that input. It applies symmetrically to failures: a break you engineered on a case you selected demonstrates that you can construct a break.

**3.4 What this session borrows from the programme's own teaching design, and how far it may borrow it.** This programme puts the attempt before the explanation on the argument that being stuck is what makes the explanation land, and that the phase converting failure into learning is an **explicit, prompted comparison** between what the learner produced and what turned out to be right. The source is emphatic that showing both versions is insufficient without the prompt to compare, and that helping learners towards the answer during the attempt reduces the failure and the learning together. **The session uses this as its own design warrant and hands the same structure to the learner as professional technique.**

**The borrowing carries three limits, stated at every use.** The source's evidence is school, university and laboratory evidence; its author's claim that the cycle works in corporate and adult education is asserted in his own voice and cited nowhere in his reference list, so **this programme's use of it on adult professionals is an extrapolation.** The author concedes that designed failure is sufficient but not necessary for the awareness it produces. And **his book and his meta-analysis disagree about how far the design generalises** — the book claims domain-general design principles and names business and writing among its applications, while the meta-analysis abstract reports a reversal favouring instruction-first for the learning of domain-general skills. **Only the abstract has been read here. The disagreement is unresolved and it bears directly on a session made of domain-general craft.**

**3.5 Why the norms are stated before the run.** Re-norming is a layer of a failure-based design rather than a preamble to it, and a cohort in which a broken prototype reads as incompetence will not produce broken prototypes.

**3.6 The boundary condition that makes this session's platform work necessary rather than decorative.**

The independent convergence on ordering comes from error-management training, where learners are given minimal guidance and explicit encouragement to make errors, and the measured advantage appears on transfer rather than during the session ([research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md)). Two independent traditions agreeing on ordering is a stronger warrant than either alone, and it is why this programme puts generation first everywhere.

But the method carries a condition that is usually dropped when it is quoted. **Because guidance is withheld, the task itself must supply interpretable feedback**; on tasks whose feedback cannot be readily interpreted the measured effect was not significant. The authors' own verdict on that boundary is explicitly partial — it **"received only limited support"**, its moderator test did not reach conventional significance, and it rested on the variable with the weakest agreement in their own coding.

**An agent is an unclear-feedback task by construction.** It completes a run and reports success while having chosen the wrong tool, passed a malformed argument, or ignored what the tool returned — and the response is fluent in every case. **So the error-management rationale does not apply to an agent exercise unless an instrumented feedback path is built for it**, which is exactly what the evaluation harness is. The design implication is not softer exploration; it is that an exercise on a silent-failure system without a harness lands in the quadrant where failure teaches nothing.

**The limits are severe and are stated at every use.** The evidence is laboratory software training with mostly student samples; **no study in the pool used a no-training control**, so every effect is relative to another training method; **no study measured on-the-job performance at all**, which the authors flag as a gap and label their own reasoning about it speculation; the pool's homogeneity test was not significant, so the moderators partition a body of studies that did not statistically demand partitioning; three of its studies favoured the comparison method; and the whole literature **predates AI-assisted work entirely.**

The counterweight belongs here too. Effortful practice is not self-justifying, and the domain in which accumulated practice explains individual differences least is professional work — the one this programme prepares people for ([research/04-professional-formation/macnamara-deliberate-practice-limits.md](/research/04-professional-formation/macnamara-deliberate-practice-limits.md)). **The useful reading is that ill-structured domains lack the fast unambiguous feedback which converts practice into performance, so the curriculum's job is to manufacture the missing structure**, which is a description of an evaluation harness. **That note is written from an abstract; the structural reading of its domain ordering is the note's own interpretation rather than the paper's claim; and none of its figures appears anywhere in this wiki.**

**3.7 Why the instrument is written before the artefact, and what the platform makes possible.**

The framework's evaluation model is deliberately minimal: `EvalItem`, `Evaluator` and `EvalResults`, with a stated design principle of **"zero friction"** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). The abstraction says evaluation is a function from an interaction to a judgement, aggregated over a set. The two evaluator tiers — a local evaluator running checks without API calls for inner-loop and CI use, and a cloud service for model-based scoring — encode the cost gradient the measurement literature argues for in the abstract, **so the API itself carries the discipline.**

Three of its features do real design work in this session. The **split strategy** is the unit-of-analysis decision, and the documentation is explicit that how you split determines what you are evaluating; the design requires it to be declared and its meaning written out, because it is the clearest available demonstration that a metric without its unit of analysis is not a measurement. The **five-way decomposition of tool failure** supplies a debugging vocabulary that replaces *it didn't work* with four distinguishable faults, two of which — wrong arguments and ignored output — **produce a system that reports success and is wrong**, which is the exact failure class the sponsor will trust. And **repetitions**, offered to detect non-deterministic behaviour, is the documentation conceding that one sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement.

**The threshold is where the design takes its own position.** A threshold converts a score into a verdict, is separable from the measurement, and moving it changes the pass rate without changing the system. **No rationale is published for any default and no agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator**, so the session requires the learner to justify their own. The practitioner literature's formulation is the one the session adopts: **"pass rate is a product decision"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md)), and the corresponding warning that a model used as a judge is a meta-problem requiring its own validation against human labels, reported as precision and recall rather than raw agreement.

**The limits are heavy.** The platform note is first-party documentation, definitional rather than empirical, read through search excerpts; most agent evaluators are **preview**; one consulted page is a **superseded classic variant**; and **no calibration data of any kind is published.** This programme asserts no evaluator's agreement with human judgement, here or anywhere. The practitioner source is **a single case study at one company with no control condition and no measured improvement**, and its tooling advice is dated.

**3.8 Why the pre-build criteria are expected to be wrong, and why that is the deliverable.**

The obvious objection to writing an evaluation first is that you cannot know what to measure before seeing outputs. **The objection is correct, it has a name, and the name is why the exercise is shaped as it is.** Criteria drift is the circularity in which users need criteria to grade outputs while grading outputs is what allows them to define criteria, and some criteria are **dependent on the specific outputs observed** rather than independently definable in advance ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md)).

The design's response is to keep the sealed version and assess the diff. **The pre-build criteria are not a prediction the learner is expected to get right; they are the fixed point against which their own drift becomes visible**, and a criteria file that did not move is evidence that the outputs were not read rather than evidence of a stable rubric. This is the same instrument as the two-column comparison in §3.4, applied to the measuring apparatus rather than to the learner's reasoning, and the parallel is deliberate.

**Two limits.** The evidence is **qualitative, from a small study of a research prototype**, establishing that the phenomenon is real without quantifying it. And it leaves a problem this session inherits and does not solve: **outputs used to revise a rubric cannot then serve cleanly as a held-out set.** A learner making a claim somebody will act on must freeze the rubric and hold out fresh cases, and the session says so rather than pretending the revision is free.

One further discipline governs whether the failure means anything at all. An evaluation is an experiment, and **a run that could not have detected the difference it was looking for has established nothing, with nothing in the output to say so** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). **The session therefore requires the power question to be answered before the run**, because otherwise a failure of the instrument is indistinguishable from a failure of the artefact — and the whole session turns on the learner being able to say which one they bought. **That source is analytical rather than empirical and its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The movements run situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**Opening item 2 is the instrument.** Item 1 asks for the sentence the learner says when the prototype breaks; item 2 asks what the sponsor is thinking while they say it. Most learners can produce a competent item 1 and have never attempted item 2, and **the gap between the two is the session's content.**

**The repair prohibition is stated as a rule and defended as a mechanism.** A prototype fixed in the hour before a meeting tests the hour. The standard's warning sign — nothing went wrong — is [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md)'s warning sign for a polished demonstration, arriving on the other side of the table.

**The instrument is built, committed and run before the artefact, and the ordering is enforced by the commit history rather than by trust.** The learner writes the cases, seals the criteria, declares the split strategy and justifies the threshold, then points the harness at a stub and watches everything fail. **That last step is the movement most likely to be skipped and the one that catches the most defects**, because a harness that passes against a stub is not testing what its author believes it is testing, and finding that out afterwards costs a session rather than a minute.

**The criteria diff is an assessed artefact rather than a tidy-up.** After the run the learner reads the outputs, revises the criteria, and records a reason per changed line citing the output that triggered it. The design expects movement and treats its absence as a finding about the learner rather than about the rubric.

**The disagreement is recorded rather than closed.** The standard requires the receiver's actual reaction, including refusal, and states that a record in which the coaching always worked is not a record. **This is the session's most important design decision** and it exists because the corpus, which supplies the coaching instruction, has never recorded a case in which it failed — it has never recorded a case in which anything failed.

**The comparison is made an artefact rather than a conversation.** The learner writes what they did and what they would now do in two columns, side by side. **Doing it in your head does not count**, and that is the source's own finding rather than this programme's preference: presenting both versions achieves nothing unless the comparison is explicitly required.

**The review movement is adversarial by construction.** A peer plays the sponsor, briefed privately to read the failure as a setback and to stay there. **The learner passes by leaving them with an accurate account, not by converting them** — which is the only version of the exercise that does not reward persuasion over honesty.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The rescued prototype.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* the repair prohibition, the standard's warning sign, and the requirement that the break be dated and attributed to a case the learner did not choose.

**The failure framed as a learning.** The account uses the word and names nothing. *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that the account name what the failure removed — an option, an assumption, a week, a cost — which is a class of noun rather than an abstraction.

**The private failure.** It broke, it was fixed, nobody was told. *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — the only people who know are the people who built it — and the assessed element at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md).

**The coached conclusion.** The learner talks the sponsor into believing the thing works. *Countermeasure:* the review movement's pass condition, which is an accurate account rather than a converted stakeholder. **This is the countermeasure the design is proudest of and least sure about**, because a persuasive learner can satisfy both.

**The expensive failure sold cheap.** *Countermeasure:* the price requirement in the standard, and the cost ledger at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md).

**The unarmed harness.** The evaluation passes against a stub and was therefore never testing the agent. *Countermeasure:* the stub run, required before the artefact exists, whose expected outcome is that everything fails.

**The inherited threshold.** The pass line arrives with the tooling and is never defended. *Countermeasure:* the justification field in the configuration, and the standing fact that **no rationale is published for any default and no agreement with human judgement is published for any evaluator.** *Residual risk:* a learner can write a justification that is a sentence rather than a reason, and nothing in the artefact distinguishes the two.

**The undeclared split.** A score is reported and nobody can say what unit it was computed over. *Countermeasure:* the requirement that the split's meaning be written out in the same commit and repeated wherever a number appears.

**The frozen rubric.** The criteria did not move, and the learner presents this as rigour. *Countermeasure:* the criteria diff as an assessed artefact, with the design's stated expectation that movement is the normal case.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. Four specification items at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md): that the failure-first run is submitted including any stakeholder who did not accept the framing; that the price of the failure is stated; that the instrument existed before the artefact and declares its split strategy and a justified threshold; and that the criteria diff carries a reason per changed line.

**Never assessed:** whether the prototype failed in an interesting way, whether the stakeholder was converted, whether the learner's account was persuasive, or whether the evaluators chosen were the right ones.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: deliberately breaking something in front of a client is professionally reckless, and dressing it as pedagogy will get graduates removed from engagements.**

At its strongest: the trust an engineer has with a customer is the asset the whole engagement runs on, and it is spent by demonstrations that fail. The claim that a fast failure is a win is made by an organisation that has never recorded a failure of any kind — so it has never paid the price it is telling other people to pay. A sponsor who watches a supplier's prototype break does not update towards *this is efficient*; they update towards *this supplier ships broken things*, and no sentence delivered afterwards repairs that.

**Partly conceded, and the concession is about scope.** The design's answer is that the objection is correct about *undeclared* failure and mostly wrong about declared failure: an artefact presented in advance as an attempt to break an assumption, on a case the customer chose, is a different social object from a demonstration that went wrong. **The rebuttal depends entirely on the declaration happening first**, which the session requires and cannot enforce beyond the classroom. **The declined fix is to run the failure privately and report it afterwards.** It is declined because the private version is the one the objection describes — the engineer learns something the customer never sees, and the option that should have been removed from the plan stays in it. **Whether the declared version survives a real commercial relationship is untested, and the corpus that recommends it has no case to offer either way.**

**Objection 2: the session borrows a learning-science design as a professional technique, and the borrowing is exactly the kind of unwarranted transfer this programme criticises everywhere else.**

At its strongest: the evidence for designed failure is about students learning concepts in schools and laboratories. A sponsor in a funding meeting is not a novice acquiring a concept; they are an experienced adult making a resource decision under organisational pressure. The source's own author asserts that the cycle works with adults and corporate audiences and cites nothing for it. **And the source's own two documents disagree about whether the design even applies to domain-general skills — which is what this entire session is made of.** Using it anyway, and footnoting the problem, is having it both ways.

**Conceded, and the concession is the most serious in this module.** The design's position is narrow: it uses the source as a warrant for **its own teaching structure** — the ordering of this session and the requirement that the learner produce a written comparison — and as a **named analogy**, explicitly labelled, for what the learner does with a stakeholder. It does not claim the stakeholder is a learner or that the mechanism has been shown to work on one. **The declined fix is to drop the analogy and teach the stakeholder conversation purely from the corpus's coaching instruction.** It is declined because that instruction is a single unevidenced line, and replacing a labelled extrapolation with an unlabelled assertion is worse. **The domain-generality disagreement is carried forward unresolved into [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md), [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) and [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md), and it is not resolved by this paper.**

**Objection 3: requiring an evaluation harness before the artefact makes the cheap failure expensive, which defeats the session's own premise.**

At its strongest, and it is a good objection: the whole argument for failing early is that the failure was cheap. **Writing cases, sealing criteria, declaring a split strategy, defending a threshold and standing up a harness is real work**, and if it costs more than the artefact then a session about spending as little as possible to find out you are wrong has just spent a great deal to find out you are wrong. Worse, it front-loads the effort onto the phase where the learner knows least — they are writing criteria for a system they have never seen, which the criteria-drift literature says is close to impossible, and which this paper cheerfully concedes will produce wrong criteria. **Spending a morning producing a rubric you have already predicted will be wrong is a strange definition of cheap.**

**Conceded on the cost and rejected on the conclusion, and the rejection rests on a boundary condition rather than on enthusiasm.** The concession first: the harness is not free, and a session that pretended otherwise would be doing what the corpus does. The rejection: without it, an agent exercise is an unclear-feedback task, and **on unclear-feedback tasks the measured advantage of the whole error-management approach was not significant.** The alternative to a harness is not a cheaper failure but an uninterpretable one — a learner who watches a fluent wrong answer and cannot say whether the tool was wrong, the argument was wrong, or the output was ignored has bought an impression rather than a finding. **The declined fix was to run the failure first and instrument it afterwards, only where the cause was unclear.** It was declined because *the cause was unclear* is a judgement made by the person who has just been convinced by a fluent answer, and the whole failure class this session teaches is the one where the system reports success and is wrong. **The residual cost is accepted and is real: on an artefact whose failures are genuinely visible, the harness is overhead, and the design has no test for which case a learner is in.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will produce a competent answer to opening item 1 and a thin or absent answer to item 2. *Instrument:* the two written answers, coded *specific* / *generic* / *absent* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** A substantial share of learners will repair the artefact before the run despite the prohibition, and will report the repair only when asked directly. *Instrument:* the run record against a single post-hoc question — did you change anything after choosing the break — administered separately.

**Prediction 3.** Accounts that state the price of the failure will be accepted by the briefed peer more often than accounts that do not. *Instrument:* the peer's post-review judgement of whether they hold an accurate account, against the presence of a price in the account, coded independently.

**Prediction 4.** Learners will over-report successful reframing: fewer records will contain an unconverted stakeholder than the review movement's design implies. *Instrument:* the count of records containing a refusal, against the reviewers' own record of whether they were converted. **If records and reviewers agree, the design's assumption that learners smooth their own accounts is wrong**, and the standard's requirement can be relaxed.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of learners will inherit the default threshold and write a justification that restates the number rather than defending it. *Instrument:* the `threshold.justification` field, coded *defended* / *restated* / *absent* by a second reader who does not see the value. **This predicts the failure of the session's own countermeasure**, and it is included because a justification field that anybody can satisfy with a sentence is a control in appearance only.

**Prediction 6.** Sealed criteria will move for most learners, and the learners whose criteria did not move will have read fewer outputs. *Instrument:* the criteria diff, against a count of outputs the learner annotated. **If criteria do not move even among learners who read many outputs, the criteria-drift finding does not transfer to this population or this task type**, and §3.8's whole justification for the sealed-criteria design fails.

**Prediction 7.** Learners will report the platform half as the more useful and the account-to-the-sponsor half as the less useful, and the reverse will hold at [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md). *Instrument:* a two-item preference question at the close of S037 and again at the close of S069. **This is a prediction about the gap between what feels like engineering and what turns out to be the job**, and a null result at the second measurement would suggest the affective content of this module does not transfer at all — which is the same failure [WP-035](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-035.md) §9 prediction 4 already anticipates.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a failure is a win only when it was cheap, that the price belongs in the report, that the reframing is your job and will often not work, and that a failure without a named cause is an impression rather than a finding.

**What the evidence licenses.** A practitioner instruction from a corpus with no evidence base and no recorded failures; a learning-science design with real evidence in a population unlike yours and an unresolved disagreement about the content type this session is made of; a boundary condition that its own authors describe as only partially supported; a qualitative finding about criteria drift; and a platform whose documentation describes what its tooling computes and offers **no evidence whatever that what it computes is right.** **Direction and mechanism only. No magnitude is asserted.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The corpus that tells you to coach stakeholders into reading a failure as a win has never reported a stakeholder who refused, because it has never reported a failure. And the vendor that supplies your evaluators publishes **no agreement rate with human judgement for any of them.** Both are asking you to trust a judgement whose accuracy nobody has shown you. **The response in both cases is the same: build the local evidence yourself, and report what it does and does not cover.**

**The silences that matter, and they are the reason the session's threshold work exists.** The documentation publishes **no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no account of where any default threshold came from.** That is a significant gap given that most of these evaluators are model-based judges, and it is why this paper, this session and this wiki **assert no evaluator's agreement with human judgement anywhere** and require the learner to justify a threshold rather than accept one. The documentation also publishes nothing about how often an evaluator's own reasoning is wrong, nothing about the cost of a judged run at any scale, and nothing about which evaluators are worth running for which system — **so the catalogue must not be mistaken for the set of things worth measuring**, and a design that measured what the catalogue makes easy would have chosen its criteria by convenience. Most consequentially for this session: **the documentation describes what the tooling computes and offers no evidence at all that what it computes is right**, which is exactly the position the corpus occupies on the other half of the day, and the session's answer in both cases is the same — build the local evidence and report what it does not cover.
