## In plain terms

This is the join, and it is where a threat model most often becomes useless while looking finished.

The failure is the **junk drawer**: broad hardening attached to everything, so that every threat has a control, every control has a threat, and the coverage summary has no gaps. It is produced by conscientiousness rather than laziness, and it is invisible in every summary the artefact will ever generate.

The session's content is a single distinction that experienced practitioners hold and rarely state. **When choosing which security domains to consider, over-inclusion is cheap and under-inclusion is catastrophic. When mapping controls to threats, the identical instinct is a disaster.** Both are true. **Nothing in the material behind this module says when to switch**, and this paper does not supply the rule either — it requires the learner to write their own down so that somebody can argue with it.

Three tests come from the practice corpus and each names a distinction practitioners routinely collapse. **Layer** — channel against payload. **Direction** — ingress against egress. **Plane** — data plane against management plane. A fourth check is a search rather than a distinction: **bypass**, because a credited control that a documented alternative path avoids is not a control at all.

This session adds a fourth, and takes it from the platform rather than from the practice corpus. **Surface.** The vendor ships two separate posture products for what a junk drawer files as one thing: one governs what data is doing in AI systems, the other governs what AI systems exist at all. They have different administrative surfaces and, in most organisations, different owners. **When your mapping attaches *governance* to a threat, ask which of those two it means. If the answer is both or neither, you have found your drawer** — using a distinction the vendor's own product estate refuses to collapse.

And one uncomfortable observation closes the session. **You will finish it heavily instrumented against over-mapping and not instrumented at all against under-mapping**, which is exactly what the source does. **The under-mapped threat is the one that hurts you**, because it survives into the plan reading like everything else.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching the threat-to-control join as a precision problem governed by mechanical tests, and defends requiring the learner to declare the point at which their own instinct switches from over-inclusion to precision.

**First**, the case against the drawer. A control credited to a threat it does not stop is worse than no control, because it consumes the place a real mitigation would occupy and reads as coverage. Component overlap is not relevance. **The junk drawer is a conscientious failure invisible to every summary the artefact produces**, and its diagnostic signature is a catalogue in which everything maps to *governance* or *monitoring* — two words broad enough to be true of almost any pairing.

**Second**, the case for the fourth test. This programme adds **surface** to the source design's layer, direction and plane, and derives it from an observed product split rather than from any finding. Data security posture management for AI and AI security posture management answer two different questions with two administrative surfaces and two typical owners. That split gives a learner a concrete, checkable way to detect the drawer: a control whose surface cannot be named has not been identified, and **a control whose owner could not fix the threat if they wanted to has been credited across a surface boundary.**

**Third**, the case for the verification method. The published engineering handbook's requirement that a non-functional requirement name how it would be verified is imported wholesale, because it converts a control from a claim into a procedure. That handbook simultaneously demonstrates why: it **contradicts itself in at least a dozen places without noticing**, which is how every control catalogue accumulates and nothing detects it.

The paper concedes four things. That the unmapped preference understates real defence in depth and will cost a reader's confidence in a well-defended system. That the declared switch point is this programme's invention and may simply describe what the learner did anyway. That the session inherits the source's precision–recall asymmetry without repairing it. And that **neither posture product publishes a detection rate, a false-positive rate or a coverage measurement**, so the surface split is usable as an organising distinction and is no evidence at all that either surface is well covered.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A learner with a registry of bound threats and a catalogue of controls will produce a complete mapping, because completeness is what the artefact appears to be asking for and because leaving a gap feels like an admission.

**The mechanism of the failure is that most controls are plausibly relevant to most threats.** Encryption is relevant to almost anything involving data. Access control is relevant to almost anything involving a component. A conscientious mapper attaches them widely, honestly, and ends with a coverage summary that has no gaps and a plan in which nothing specific has been addressed.

The second problem is subtler and it is about credit rather than volume. **A control can be real, deployed, correctly configured, and still not stop the threat it is credited against** — because it protects the channel and the threat is in the payload, or it inspects ingress and the threat leaves by egress, or it guards the data plane and the threat arrives through management. Each of those is a distinction practitioners collapse routinely, and each collapse produces an entry that will never be revisited.

The third is a plan-presentation problem masquerading as a mapping problem. **An unmapped threat still appears in the plan and still reads as handled.**

The session's response is three mechanical tests, an accounting that must reconcile, and a requirement that absence be marked where the reader will meet it.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The module's highest procedural load. Four tests and a bypass search applied to every candidate pair, plus a reconciliation, plus a deliberate reversal of instinct partway through. The evaluative peak is [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md); the mechanical peak is here.

**3.2 Why over-inclusion reverses.** Selecting a domain costs you the work of enumerating it and nothing else — a domain considered and dismissed leaves a record. **Skipping one leaves nothing, and nothing downstream can detect the absence.** Mapping is the opposite: an attachment is not free, because it occupies the place where a real mitigation would have gone and it converts a gap into apparent coverage. **The asymmetry flips because the cost of a false positive flips**, and that is the whole of item 4.

**3.3 Why the tests are mechanical rather than judgemental.** Layer, direction, plane, surface and the bypass search can each be answered by looking at the model, the inventory or an organisational chart rather than by weighing a risk. **A test a reviewer can apply without the author present is worth more than a judgement the author is better placed to make**, which is the same argument this programme has made for evidence since [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md).

**3.4 Why the join runs on compact indexes.** Full text invites the name match, because a shared component name is the most salient thing in two long entries. A projection carrying keys and a short reason removes the salience. **This is mechanism and direction only; no magnitude is claimed and none is available.**

**3.5 Why the selection rule is written once.** In the source material a near-identical domain-selection heuristic appears in several places and drifts between them ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md)). **Two stages selecting by slightly different rules select different domains for the same system**, and the plan is then graded against controls a different stage never fetched. **Nothing in the design detects it**, and the countermeasure available to a learner is to have one rule rather than to have consistency checked. That source is internal, access-controlled, paraphrased here, quoted nowhere, and **supplies no external warrant for any claim.**

**3.6 Why surface is the fourth test, and why it comes from a product split rather than from a principle.**

The vendor ships two posture products for what a junk drawer would file as one thing ([research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md)). **Data security posture management for AI** governs what sensitive information flows into and out of AI tools, who is oversharing what, and which interactions are captured for compliance. **AI security posture management** governs which AI applications exist in the estate, what they are built from, how they are misconfigured, and what an attacker could chain together.

**They answer two different questions and the fact that these require separate products is itself the lesson.** Different administrative surfaces, different licensing, and in most organisations different owners — one with information protection and compliance, the other with cloud security operations.

The pedagogical value is that this converts an abstract instruction (*discriminate*) into a concrete one (*name the surface and the owner*). It also converts a silent failure into a loud one: **a control whose owner could not fix the threat if they wanted to has been credited across a surface boundary**, and the learner discovers this by trying to find the owner rather than by reasoning about it.

The underlying data-side argument is the strongest reasoning on those pages and is durable past the products. Generative AI **"amplifies the problem of oversharing data"** by surfacing content that is obsolete, over-permissioned or lacking governance controls. **Deploying a capable retrieval system converts every dormant permissions error in an organisation into a live one.** The permissions were always wrong; nobody was reading. That is why a permissions fix belongs to one surface and an endpoint hardening to the other, and why crediting one against a threat belonging to the other is a category error rather than a matter of degree.

The same material supplies the governance dilemma in one sentence: removing a sharing link is flagged as **"an action to be used sparingly because it could prevent people from accessing the item legitimately."** **The safest action is the most disruptive, and somebody has to own that trade-off** — which is the argument for requiring a named owner rather than a team alias.

**Its limits are stated flatly and bound everything above.** First-party and definitional. **No detection rate, no false-positive rate and no coverage measurement is published for either surface — none is asserted here or anywhere in this wiki.** The data-posture page is explicitly a classic version stating it has been replaced, and the replacement was not read; the workload-posture material was read through search-returned excerpts rather than fetched in full, so its structure is reconstructed. Agent-level capability moves to a separate licence during the life of this material. Its stated operational bounds — assessment scope, cadence and result latency — are **the resolution of the instrument**, and are paraphrased qualitatively throughout rather than reproduced.

**3.7 Why where a control sits is part of what the control is.**

The gateway documentation is the clearest available demonstration that a control's location determines its reach ([research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md)). Prompt moderation implemented as a gateway policy **guarantees uniform enforcement across every consumer** and **centralises a decision some applications might legitimately want to make differently.** Both halves are true simultaneously, and a mapping recording only *content safety applied* has erased the trade-off.

The same page supplies a control-class distinction that a junk drawer destroys. **Managed identities authenticate "so you don't need API keys for authentication"** — **the elimination of a long-lived secret, not merely its better storage.** A key in a vault is still a key. Crediting a secrets-management control against a threat that requires secret elimination is a layer error with a plausible label, and it is one of the few in this session that a learner can check against a real deployment in minutes.

**Its limits:** first-party with a commercial frame. It argues for a gateway and does not discuss the latency, cost or operational burden the gateway itself adds. **No benchmarks, no latency figures, no cost measurements, no comparison against other products.** Capability availability varies by service tier. Its architectural reasoning is sound and **asserted rather than demonstrated.**

**3.8 Why every control must name a verification method.**

The published engineering handbook's non-functional-requirement form has several fields, and the note on it records which are load-bearing: **a requirement that cannot name how it would be verified has not been written** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md)). Imported into a control inventory, that single field is what separates a control from an intention, and it is checkable by a reader with no security knowledge.

The same handbook demonstrates the failure mode it protects against. **It contradicts itself in at least a dozen places without noticing** — secrets in a key vault on one page and encrypted into the repository on another, mocking frameworks recommended and discouraged, three different branch-naming conventions. **None is an error of fact; all are the residue of many hands over years with no reconciliation pass**, which is exactly how a control catalogue accumulates and exactly what nothing detects.

**Its limits are severe and are used here as content rather than hidden.** It cannot be dated as a whole; its page footers span years with nothing in the navigation to distinguish them. About half the site is not what its titles promise. **Its threshold catalogue carries no source at all — usable as an example bank, never as a benchmark.** And it **reaches for a magnitude in at least four places and warrants none of them; those four are recorded in this repository as specimens and reproduced nowhere.**

**3.9 Why a coverage summary is not coverage.**

The production machine-learning literature states the general form: **"a model with good performance 'on average' may still exhibit failure modes that are not apparent by looking at an aggregate metric"**, from which slicing follows, with the observation that **which slices matter is a domain question rather than a statistical one** ([research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md)). A mapping with excellent aggregate coverage can be uniformly blind to one surface, one layer or one direction, and no summary will show it. The session's requirement to slice the mapped set by surface, layer and direction before believing any total is that argument applied.

**Its limits are heavy:** practitioner guidance from a commercial publisher, no study design, no comparison group, three of four authors at one large vendor, some claims resting on **anonymous practitioner testimony**, and every quantity a property of one system after one run. **Mechanism transfers; no magnitude does, and none appears here.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.0 The movement sequence.** Situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**Opening item 4 is the instrument and it is a trick.** It has two correct and opposite answers, and a learner who supplies one confidently before instruction has demonstrated the failure the session exists to prevent. **The gap between their answer and the second answer is the session's content.**

**Opening item 5 is asked because its honest answer is uncomfortable.** A threat with no control mapped to it reads as handled, and every learner knows this the moment it is put to them and has never acted on it.

**The workflow is ordered around the switch.** Domain selection first, generously; then the switch, stated on the record; then the tests, applied ruthlessly. **Putting the switch in the middle of the workflow rather than in the standard makes it an act rather than a principle**, which is the only form in which it is checkable.

**The tests are given as questions with a yes or no answer**, not as considerations. A consideration is weighed and a question is answered, and the difference determines whether a reviewer can apply it.

**Exercise 4 asks a reviewer to find a wrong credit, and supplies them the tests.** This is deliberate: the design does not rely on the reviewer's security knowledge, only on their willingness to apply four questions the author has stopped seeing.

**4.1 Why the platform step asks for an owner rather than a portal path.** Workflow step 7 has the learner place every existing control on a posture surface and then **find the person who owns that surface.** The portal path will be wrong within a term; the organisational fact will not. **The uncomfortable finding is the intended one:** at least one surface usually has an owner the learner has never spoken to, which is the actual dependency their plan rests on, surfaced for the first time.

**4.2 Why the schema forbids four words rather than encouraging precision.** The control inventory's comment block names *governance*, *monitoring*, *best practice* and *hardening* as forbidden values. A prohibition is checkable and an encouragement is not, and the failure this session exists to prevent is produced by conscientious people who believe they are being precise. **The forbidden-word list converts a judgement into a search**, which is the same move [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) made with hedging language.

**4.3 Why the attack step asks for the bypass explicitly.** The agent prompt names all four tests and then asks for the documented path that avoids the control. Left to itself, an agent handed a mapping will check internal consistency and find it consistent — the mapping is, after all, self-coherent. **The bypass is the only one of the five checks that requires looking outside the artefact**, and it is therefore the only one that has to be asked for by name.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The junk drawer.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — your coverage summary has no gaps — plus the explicit preference for leaving a threat unmapped, plus the three-way accounting that makes unmapped a legitimate outcome rather than an omission.

**The name match.** *Countermeasure:* the compact-index diet, and the standard's requirement that the reason state a mechanism rather than restate the two entries.

**The wrong-layer credit.** *Countermeasure:* the layer and direction tests, and the standard's warning sign — the reason works equally well in both directions.

**The bypassed control.** *Countermeasure:* the bypass search. **This is the weakest of the checks**, because finding a second path requires knowing the architecture better than the mapping does, and a learner working from their own model will find only the paths they drew.

**The silent unmapped threat.** *Countermeasure:* the requirement that unmapped status appear in the plan where the reader meets it, not in an appendix, plus exercise 4's second question.

**The drifting selector.** *Countermeasure:* the one-sentence rule. **This is a discipline rather than a control**, and the paper says so.

**The two-word catalogue.** Every control classified as governance or monitoring. *Countermeasure:* the forbidden-word list in the inventory schema, plus exercise 1, which requires the learner to count their own. **Counting is what makes this visible**, because each individual instance looks defensible and only the total looks like a drawer.

**The wrong-surface credit.** A workload hardening credited against a data-exposure threat, or the reverse. *Countermeasure:* the surface field and the named-owner requirement, and the standard's warning sign — the control's owner could not fix the threat if they wanted to. **This is the countermeasure the design has most confidence in**, because it is settled by an organisational fact rather than by an argument.

**The unverifiable control.** A control nobody can confirm is in place. *Countermeasure:* the mandatory verification method, and exercise 5, which requires the learner to actually run four checks against a real deployment or record that they cannot.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. Three constituents are assessed at [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md): that no mitigation in the plan is generic; that unmapped threats are marked as unmapped where the reader meets them; and that the three-way accounting reconciles.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: preferring to leave threats unmapped understates real defence in depth, and produces a plan that misrepresents a well-protected system as a poorly protected one.**

At its strongest: defence in depth *is* broad controls applied widely, deliberately, on the argument that no single control is trusted. Network segmentation, least privilege and centralised logging genuinely do reduce risk across many unrelated threats, and that is not a junk drawer — it is the strategy. A rule that forbids crediting them against specific threats produces a plan whose coverage summary is full of holes for a system that is actually well defended, **and the customer reading it will draw exactly the wrong conclusion.**

**Substantially conceded.** The rebuttal is a distinction rather than a defence: the prohibition is on *crediting* a broad control against a specific threat as though it addressed that threat's mechanism, not on *deploying* it or on describing it. A broad control still belongs in the plan as posture; it simply does not discharge an individual entry, because a discharge that cannot survive the layer, direction and bypass tests cannot later be verified or falsified. **The declined fix is to permit class-level credit — a broad control mapped once to a named class of threats rather than individually.** It is declined because a class credit cannot be checked by the tests: there is no single layer, direction, plane or surface to test it against, and it therefore reconstitutes the junk drawer with a more respectable name. **The residual cost is real. A plan built this way will read as weaker than the system is, and this design accepts that in exchange for every credited line being checkable.**

**Objection 2: requiring learners to invent their own switch point is an abdication dressed as pedagogy.**

At its strongest: the curriculum has identified a genuine open question — when does over-inclusion stop being cheap — and has handed it back to the least experienced person in the room. A self-declared switch point is unfalsifiable and will simply describe whatever the learner did anyway. **A real curriculum would supply the rule.**

**Conceded in part, and the decline is principled rather than convenient.** Two things make the switch more than a box: it is marked on whether it is specific and defensible, and it is checked against the learner's own mapping, so a declared switch inconsistent with observed practice is detectable by a second reader. **The declined fix is for this programme to supply a switch rule.** It is declined because no source available supports one, and a curriculum-supplied threshold would be a designer's judgement rendered as a rule — **which is precisely the practice this stage condemns in its own source, where not one calibration constant cites a study, a measurement, a pilot or a rationale.** This programme will not commit the fault it teaches learners to detect. **The open question stays open and is handed over as an open question.**

**Objection 3: the surface test is derived from one vendor's product boundary, and teaching a product boundary as an analytic distinction will not survive the product boundary moving.**

At its strongest: data posture and workload posture are separated because of how a company organised its portfolio, its licensing and its acquisition history — not because the world divides that way. **The documentation behind this session is already mid-migration**, with agent-level capability moving to a different licence during the life of this material. A learner taught to sort controls into two boxes because two products exist has been taught a fact about a price list.

**Substantially conceded, and the defence is narrower than the objection.** The durable claim is not *these two products* but **that data governance and workload governance have different owners, different evidence and different remediation costs, and that a control catalogue which cannot say which one it is addressing has stopped discriminating.** The product split is offered as the current instance and appears in the session's perishability register as one. **The declined fix was to teach an abstract two-surface distinction with no product attached.** It is declined because the abstraction is exactly the kind of thing a learner nods at and does not apply; the concrete version forces a learner to go and find an owner, and finding an owner is the step that produces the surprise. **The cost is that the session's clearest teaching device is bound to a vendor arrangement that will change**, and the register says so.

**Objection 4: the session assesses whether the accounting reconciles, and reconciliation is arithmetic that a bad mapping satisfies exactly as well as a good one.**

At its strongest: three-way accounting proves that every threat and every control was accounted for. It proves nothing about whether any pairing is correct. **The assessed properties are therefore all satisfiable by a learner who applies no judgement at all** — a junk drawer with a full accounting balances perfectly. Worse, the source design's own accounting machinery exists to absorb a structural defect: two adjacent stages with opposed uncertainty policies, one retaining when uncertain and one dropping, guaranteeing an unmapped tail that the accounting then reports tidily. **The programme has adopted a reporting mechanism whose original purpose was to make a structural problem look managed.**

**Conceded, and the session states it in its own limits rather than only here.** Reconciliation is assessed because it is checkable without domain expertise, and because an unreconciled mapping has certainly lost something. **It is not evidence of quality and the session says so in the sentence that closes the workflow.** The mapping's correctness is not assessed anywhere in this module, and §7 says so plainly. **The declined fix was to assess a sample of mappings for correctness by expert review.** It is declined because expert availability is the binding constraint on this programme's whole assessment architecture, and an assessed property that cannot be applied consistently across a cohort produces variance that looks like judgement. **The cost is that a learner can pass this session's assessed constituents with a drawer**, and the only defences against that are the reviewer exercise and §9's second prediction, both of which depend on a willing second reader.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first mappings will credit at least one control against threats spanning unrelated mechanisms without a per-attachment defence. *Instrument:* the submitted mapping, with each control's attachments coded *defended individually* / *undefended* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Reviewers applying the four tests will find a wrong-layer, wrong-direction or wrong-surface credit in most mappings. *Instrument:* exercise 4's written outcome, recorded per learner as *found* / *not found*, with the test that caught it.

**Prediction 3.** Declared switch points will be inconsistent with the learner's own mapping behaviour. *Instrument:* the declared switch against the breadth of the submitted mapping, coded *consistent* / *inconsistent* by a second reader.

**Prediction 4.** Mapping reasons produced from full entries will restate the entries more often than mapping reasons produced from compact indexes. *Instrument:* reasons coded *mechanism* / *restatement*, compared across learners by recorded working method. **If this fails, the information-diet claim carried from [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) has no support at this scale and the compact index should be taught as an efficiency rather than as a quality measure.**

**Prediction 5.** In exercise 1, learners will classify a substantial share of their own controls as *governance* or *monitoring* when forced to choose one word, and the share will be higher for controls they did not personally deploy. *Instrument:* the learner's own count, audited by a second reader, split by whether the learner deployed the control.

**Prediction 6.** For most learners, at least one posture surface will have an owner they cannot name at the start of the session and can name at the end. *Instrument:* the control inventory's owner fields, compared against a pre-session written attempt at the same list. **If this fails — if learners can already name both owners — the surface test is teaching an organisational fact the cohort already holds**, and its value is confined to the layer, direction and plane tests it accompanies.

**Prediction 7.** Every submitted three-way accounting will reconcile, and reconciliation will not correlate with any second reader's judgement of mapping quality. *Instrument:* the reconciliation outcome against an independent quality rating of the same mappings. **This predicts the failure of an assessed property of this session**, and it is included because §8's fourth objection concedes that arithmetic is satisfiable by a drawer. If it holds, the reconciliation should be retained as a hygiene check and removed from anything that reads as a quality signal.

**Prediction 8.** Learners instructed to compare their item-1 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 3) will produce negative control claims naming a test more often than learners who read the same criterion without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* the submitted mapping's unmapped-control rationales, coded *names a test* / *hedges* by a second reader, across two cohorts — or, absent two cohorts, a within-cohort split on completion of exercise 3.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a control credited to a threat it does not stop is worse than no control; that component overlap is not relevance; that the same instinct is correct at domain selection and disastrous at mapping; and that a control which cannot name a surface, an owner and a verification method has not been identified.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about mapping quality. **The four tests are practitioner judgement carried from experienced people and validated nowhere**, the fourth is derived from a product arrangement rather than from a finding, and the source measures precision elaborately while declining to measure recall at all. **Neither posture product publishes a detection rate, a false-positive rate or a coverage measurement**, so the surface split organises your thinking and tells you nothing about whether either surface is covered.

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper.** The handbook this session borrows its verification-method requirement from asserts four unwarranted magnitudes; none is reproduced here or anywhere in this repository.

**The concession worth remembering.** You will leave this session well defended against attaching too much and undefended against attaching too little, and the second is the one that will hurt you. Notice that the artefact cannot tell you when it has happened — and that your accounting will balance either way.

**The silences that matter.** **Neither posture product publishes a detection rate, a false-positive rate or any coverage measurement**, so the surface split is an organising distinction and is no evidence whatever that either surface is well covered — which is why §8's third objection is conceded and why the session's perishability register carries the split as an instance rather than a principle. The documentation states operational bounds — assessment scope, cadence and result latency — and **those bounds are the resolution of the instrument rather than incidental limits**; this paper paraphrases them qualitatively and reproduces none. **Neither product's documentation says anything about how the two surfaces should be reconciled when a control belongs to both**, which is the case the session's exercise 2 predicts learners will find hardest and where a plan is most likely to assume somebody else is handling it. The gateway documentation **does not discuss the privacy implications of logging prompts and completions**, which it recommends for billing and auditing — a control whose own risk it does not analyse. And no source read for this paper measures whether any control catalogue's classification scheme affects any outcome at all.
