# WP-053 — The Transformer, Assembled

**Seminar day:** [S053](wiki/seminars/S053.md) · **Module:** [M11](wiki/modules/M11.md) · **Quarter:** [Q6](wiki/quarters/Q6.md)
**Strands:** IS · ME · **Complexity class:** 4
**Platform anchor:** Azure AI Search retrieval pipeline — HNSW against exhaustive KNN, semantic ranker, hybrid arms · Foundry reasoning deployments — `reasoning_effort` · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server · Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends presenting the transformer as a decomposition with seams rather than as a diagram to memorise, on the ground that S007's seam argument transfers exactly and that a student who holds the architecture as a design survives the architecture changing. It defends a pretest constructed so that two items are wrong in opposite directions — over-predicting the cost of removing positional information, under-predicting the redundancy of a middle block — because a pretest whose errors all run one way teaches miscalibration while one whose errors run both ways teaches the absence of a model. Its central design argument concerns phase 3's two-part reveal: aggregate ablation metrics are supplied first and per-class breakdowns second, so that students commit to the conclusion the aggregate supports before seeing what it concealed. **It defends splitting the phase into an architectural arm that is demonstrated rather than operated, because serverless deployments do not expose model weights, and a pipeline arm that is operated at query time against a live index** — and argues that the substitution is not a concession, because a query-time component ablation is a closer analogue of the decision the students will face than a residual-path removal they will never perform. The paper defends making sensitivity-before-ablation a hard requirement, which discharges the obligation S052 placed on this day under instrumentation rule 6. It concedes that the day's most confident folk-model break — that layers are not stages — is asserted rather than demonstrated, **the second instance of a pattern opened four days earlier**; it concedes that the day teaches per-class reporting while being structurally unable to teach class discovery; and it concedes that the operated arm demonstrates the day's method on a system whose components are not the components the day names.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The assembly is two operations alternating: mix across positions, then compute per position. [S052](wiki/seminars/S052.md) built the first one from a hash table. The second is a function applied independently at each position. Everything else in the architecture — residual paths, normalisation, positional injection — exists so that a deep stack of those two remains trainable.

That is a short lecture and it is not what the day is for.

The day is for the three false beliefs students hold about the assembly, all of which are confident, none of which is idle, and each of which produces a specific engineering error:

**That layers are stages.** Students imagine a pipeline — early layers do something like syntax, late layers do something like meaning. The belief produces engineers who truncate models at a layer they think has "finished" a job, and who read probe results as confirmation of a pipeline that is not there.

**That contribution is proportional to size.** Removing a component should damage the output in proportion to its parameter share. It does not. Some components can be removed with almost no aggregate effect and others cannot be removed at all.

**That damage is uniform.** A one-percent aggregate degradation is understood as one percent worse everywhere. It is frequently a very large degradation on a small class, and the class is usually one somebody depends on.

The third belief is the expensive one and it is the day's real target. **It is also not a fact about transformers.** It is [S026](wiki/seminars/S026.md)'s construct argument and [S049](wiki/seminars/S049.md)'s unit-of-analysis rule arriving in a domain where the aggregate is unusually seductive, and it is the direct precursor of [S059](wiki/seminars/S059.md).

**The fourth problem is that the day cannot do the thing it teaches.** An ablation is a removal, and the students cannot remove anything from the models they will ship against: serverless deployments host weights on Microsoft-managed infrastructure and expose an inference API, and managed compute puts weights on virtual machines the students control but does not make surgery on them a ninety-minute task. A day that taught ablation by describing ablations would be the exact failure this design's own [WP-051](wiki/whitepapers/WP-051.md) and [WP-052](wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) spent two days avoiding.

The resolution is to notice that the students *do* own an assembly with removable components. A retrieval-and-inference pipeline has an approximate-nearest-neighbour graph, a lexical arm, a fusion step, a reranker and a per-request compute dial, and each of them can be switched off in one request parameter against the same index and the same corpus. **The three false beliefs are beliefs about assemblies, not about transformers**, and they are all reproducible there — including the third, which the platform supplies in a defaulted-on form so blatant that it is worth the day on its own.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Moderate on mechanism, high on measurement.** The architecture is presented in about twelve minutes because every component is either retrieved or trivially composed. The load is concentrated at phase 5, where a behaviour, an input class, an ablation and a sensitivity statement must be produced together — and the sensitivity statement is a genuinely new technical object.

Management:

- **All four phase 3 ablations are supplied already run,** with both aggregate and per-class results.
- **Step 3's sensitivity statement is worked with the room,** which is the heaviest scaffolding on any step in the module.
- **The model, substrate and input taxonomy are supplied.**
- **The architecture is derived from two known operations plus a seam argument.**

**Step 2 is unsupported.**

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Ranking five components before knowing what any of them does,* item 1.
- *Two pretest items wrong in opposite directions,* items 3 and 4.
- *Committing to a conclusion from the aggregate before the per-class breakdown arrives,* phase 3.
- *Stating a measurement's sensitivity before running it,* step 3.
- *Stating what you would have concluded from the aggregate alone,* step 7.
- *A dual defeat requiring both the claim and its detection to fail,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Training dynamics.* Why residuals help optimisation is IS202's; the day treats them as a seam, which is the engineering-relevant framing.
- *Attention variants and efficient architectures.* [S061](wiki/seminars/S061.md)'s.
- *Probing and circuit-level interpretability.* Named at point 4 as the other evidence source and not developed.
- *Scaling behaviour.* Excluded entirely from the module; the programme does not train at scales where it is observable and declines to teach it as received wisdom.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** Point 5's redundancy content is a statement about *a* trained network, not about the architecture. **Safe** as a warning that redundancy exists and must be measured on the model in front of you. **Unsafe** in every generalising form: which components are redundant, how much redundancy there is, and whether a component redundant in one model is redundant in another are all properties of a particular training run, and none of them transfers. A student who learns "middle feed-forward blocks are redundant" has learned something false about every model except the one on the bench. This is stated to students at phase 4 and is repeated in the perishability register.

**The platform instance, scoped in the same form.** Arm B teaches four query-time ablations as the operable form of the load-bearing argument, and the correspondence must carry its safe and unsafe conditions.

*Safe.* Reasoning that load-bearing is a property of a configured system on a class of inputs; that an aggregate can be flat while a class collapses; that a component whose removal costs nothing in quality may cost a great deal in latency or storage and the reverse; that a measurement's sensitivity must be stated before it is run; and that a metric's name is not its definition. Every one of those is exercised in arm B on components the students configured, and none of them is a claim about transformers.

*Unsafe.* Reasoning that arm B's components **are** the components arm A names. They are not, and the day would be doing damage if a student left believing that turning off a reranker is the same kind of act as removing a normalisation layer. A reranker is a stage with an interface; a residual path is a structural property of a training regime. **The correspondence is a correspondence of the reasoning procedure and not of the object**, and §8 objection 3 is where the design pays for it. It is also unsafe as a claim about how much any of arm B's ablations costs on another corpus, at another scale, or under another algorithm's parameters — ablation P's near-indifference is measured on this index at this size and is the property most likely to reverse as the corpus grows, which [S058](wiki/seminars/S058.md) makes its entire subject.

The boundary is stated to students at points 9 and 10 and enforced at step 2, which requires a component the student can actually remove and therefore forces the distinction to be made rather than assumed.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **the gap between two reveals**.

Phase 3 supplies the aggregate numbers first. Groups reach the obvious conclusions — ablation C cost little, ablation D cost nothing — and those conclusions are reasonable, defensible, and what a competent engineer would report. The per-class breakdown then arrives and one of them is wrong in a way that would have shipped.

The staging is the whole mechanism. Supplying both tables together would let students notice the discrepancy without having endorsed the aggregate reading, and the endorsement is what makes the lesson stick. **Students must have been persuaded by the aggregate to learn what the aggregate hides.**

The second mechanism is items 3 and 4, which are wrong in opposite directions. A student who over-predicts on one and under-predicts on the other cannot conclude that they were simply cautious or simply naive. They discover that they had no model, only a disposition — and that is the finding phase 8 reports with the two error magnitudes shown separately and signed.

Phase 6 supplies the affective content and is deliberately blameless: the ablation study was correctly designed, correctly run, correctly reported, and correctly interpreted at the level it was reported at. **The error was the unit of reporting**, which is not a rigour failure and cannot be prevented by care.

The competence risk is at step 3. A sensitivity statement is unfamiliar and students will produce weak ones. The instructor states before phase 5 that a weak sensitivity statement honestly written is worth more than a strong one asserted, and that phase 7 will attack it either way.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items. Item 2 — what the first layer does that the last does not — is the folk model's most confident claim and is collected in the students' own words so that phase 8 can read them back. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction** and is administered here per instrumentation rule 8; the design expects the agent to produce a competent study with no sensitivity statement and aggregate-only reporting, which is exactly the pair of failures the specification treats as fatal.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals and a seam bridge. The architecture arrives as a decomposition, which is the framing that survives it changing.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Two arms, disclosed. **Arm A is four architectural ablations, supplied pre-run, demonstrated rather than operated and not assessed.** Predictions committed, aggregates, then per-class; the two-part reveal is the design's central choice and is defended in §3.3. **Arm B is four query-time ablations the groups run themselves** against the substrate's index and deployment, over the real query log, in the same commit-aggregate-then-per-class order. Q reproduces C's shape and P reproduces D's, so the two arms are matched and phase 4 can index to either.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Eleven points. Point 4 is the folk-model break and is the subject of §8 objection 1. Point 6 is the retained content. **Point 7 is this day's rule-6 discharge**, stated as a rule because an obligation delivered as advice is not an obligation. Point 9 supplies arm B's mechanism, without which the arm is a set of switches rather than an assembly. **Points 10 and 11 are the required perishable point and the required defaults**; point 11's fourth default is a shipped, documented, defaulted-on instance of point 6 and is the strongest teaching artefact on the day.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps, **ablating a running pipeline rather than reading supplied tables**. Step 3 before step 4 makes the sensitivity a pre-commitment. Step 2's added constraint — a component you can actually remove — is the day's platform honesty made assessable. Step 7 is the discriminator and is the sentence S059 retrieves. The artefact carries the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A study with no methodological error, acted on, wrong — then an agent-built harness whose two defects were both absences: an unrequested breakdown and an unset parameter. The agent case is chosen because **a default does not appear in the code**, which is what makes it invisible to review.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Dual defeat — the claim and its detection — reusing S029's structure, which is validated under the amended rule 5.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Rankings against results; the two error magnitudes signed and separate; item 2 read back; **item 5 opened and coded for the two failure classes**; step 7 counterfactual rate.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has read ablation work will predict items 3 and 4 correctly and will find phase 3 confirmatory. Predicted failure: they treat step 3's sensitivity statement as a formality because they already know what they will find. Countermeasure: phase 7 requires their partner to defeat the sensitivity statement specifically, and a statement written as a formality is trivially defeated — which is the intended lesson delivered by a peer rather than by an assessor.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is step 3. Countermeasures: worked with the room; four supplied ablations with their own sensitivity properties visible; and the instructor's statement that an honest weak statement beats an asserted strong one.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Ablations A and B are unambiguous. The difficulty is in C's per-class result and in explaining why D costs nothing. Fallback if a group handles both: ask what would have to be true of the training data for ablation C's failing class to be the class it is, which is a question about the data rather than the architecture and has no supplied answer.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **learned helplessness about measurement** — if the aggregate lies, the per-class breakdown depends on classes you chose, and redundancy does not transfer, a student may conclude that ablation tells you nothing. §8 objection 2 shows this concern is partly correct, which makes the risk sharper.

Countermeasures: ablations A and B are cases where the aggregate is entirely adequate, so the day does not teach that aggregates are always wrong; point 7 gives a constructive procedure rather than a caution; and the instructor states that the day's standard — state the sensitivity, report per class, say what the aggregate alone would have told you — is three sentences of extra work and is achievable on every study they will ever run.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement.

One specification item in IS202. Per rule 1, the checked elements are the behaviour and its input class, the ablation and expectation, the pre-run sensitivity statement, the result, aggregate and per-class reporting, the load-bearing claim with confidence, the aggregate-only counterfactual, the phase 7 outcome, **the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

**Phase 3 arm A is not assessed.** The architectural ablations are demonstrated rather than operated, because serverless deployments do not expose model weights and no supported route exists by which a student removes a residual path from a deployed model. Per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) the specification does not assess a case the programme cannot run, and the day states this at its opening rather than in a footnote.

Three hard requirements: **an ablation without a pre-run sensitivity statement fails**; **a result reported only in aggregate fails**; and **an intervention record with no entries fails** unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**Rule-6 discharge, as required by [WP-052](wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) §7.** S052 named this day as the response occasion for its step-4-against-step-5 divergence rate, specifying that each ablation design must state its sensitivity before the ablation is run. **The element exists and is assessed: phase 5 step 3, supported by phase 4 point 7, and it is one of the day's hard specification requirements.** It is not a mention; it is a fail condition. **The practical revision does not weaken it and sharpens it**: because arm B's ablations are run by the student over a real query log, the per-class n is a fact about their own sample rather than a number supplied with a table, and a sensitivity statement written against a sample you drew is a different and harder object than one written against a sample you were given. The obligation is discharged.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named occasions: the item 3 and item 4 error magnitudes respond at [S055](wiki/seminars/S055.md) phase 3, whose demonstrations are selected against whichever direction the cohort got worse; and the step 7 aggregate-only counterfactual rate responds at [S059](wiki/seminars/S059.md) phase 1, where the same measurement is retaken on published results. **WP-055 §7 and WP-059 §7 must report whether those elements exist**, per the commitment WP-051 §8 established.

Item 5's hit rate joins the running frontier map per instrumentation rule 9. Per rule 3 the day states its sampling: one cohort, one index, one query log, one deployment family.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day's most confident folk-model break is the one it does not demonstrate.**

At its strongest: point 4 states that layers are not stages. That is the belief item 2 elicits, it is the belief students hold most confidently, and it is the belief with the clearest engineering consequences — truncating a model at a layer thought to have finished a job, or reading probe results as confirming a pipeline.

**None of the day's four ablations tests it.** A, B and C ablate component *types* across the whole stack. D removes one middle feed-forward block, which demonstrates redundancy and says nothing about depth structure. The claim is therefore delivered by assertion at phase 4 and reinforced by reading item 2's answers back at phase 8, which is rhetoric rather than evidence. A student who leaves believing point 4 believes it because they were told, which is exactly the condition §1 of this paper says cannot dislodge a folk model.

The fix is nearly free and the design should have taken it. **Ablation D already removes one block from the middle. Running the same ablation at three depths — early, middle, late — and supplying the damage profile would cost two additional rows in a table that already exists.** If the profile is not stage-shaped, point 4 is demonstrated by the same phase that demonstrates everything else. If it is stage-shaped in some respect, the day has found something more interesting than the claim it intended to make.

**This is the second instance of the pattern [WP-049](wiki/whitepapers/WP-049.md) §8 opened at one instance**: the design stages the claims it expects students to resist and asserts the claims it finds obvious once stated. Point 4 feels obvious to whoever wrote it. It is not obvious to the room, and item 2 exists precisely because it is not. The pattern is now at two instances and is recorded, not yet elevated.

**Objection 2: the day teaches per-class reporting and cannot teach class discovery.**

At its strongest: the day's central lesson is that aggregates hide class-specific catastrophe, and its remedy is to report per class. But the classes come from the substrate's existing taxonomy — supplied at phase 3, and at step 1 chosen by the student as the class their behaviour lives on. **A catastrophe in a class nobody has enumerated is invisible to the day's entire method.** Phase 3 works only because the designers, knowing where ablation C fails, included that class in the breakdown. A real ablation fails on a class that is not in anyone's taxonomy, and the day's procedure would report it as a small aggregate loss with clean per-class numbers.

This objection is conceded, and it is sharper than it first appears, because it means the day's remedy shares a defect with the failure it corrects: both find only what was already thought of.

The partial rebuttal is that step 1 is a class-construction act — the student names the class their behaviour lives on rather than accepting one. But that is the class they care about in advance, not the class that breaks, and the two are different by construction.

**The fix is a real technique and costs one step:** require step 5 to include **one class constructed after the results, from the residuals** — group the worst-affected inputs, characterise what they share, and report that class alongside the pre-specified ones. It is standard practice, it is within reach at class 4, and it converts the day's method from confirming known classes to discovering unknown ones. It is not in the design, and the day's scope statement does not currently warn students that the method has this limit. **Both omissions are recorded.**

**Objection 3: the operated arm demonstrates the day's method on a system whose components are not the components the day names.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn, and it is the one an expert reader will reach first.

At its strongest: the day is titled *The Transformer, Assembled*. Its phase 4 teaches residual paths, normalisation, positional injection and feed-forward blocks. Its assessed phase 5 ablates **an approximate-search graph, a reranker, a keyword arm and a compute dial** — none of which is in the assembly, three of which are not in any model, and all of which are components of a product the students happen to be able to configure.

The substitution is defended in §1 on the ground that the three false beliefs are beliefs about assemblies rather than about transformers. That defence is true and it is not sufficient, because **the day still needs the students to hold a mechanical account of a transformer**, and [Q6](wiki/quarters/Q6.md) says so explicitly: Q7's serving material is meaningless without one. The mechanical account now rests entirely on arm A, which is supplied, unassessed, and forty-five minutes long. **The day has moved its assessment to the arm it can run and left its stated subject in the arm it cannot**, and a student could pass this day's specification with a shaky account of the architecture and a good account of a search pipeline.

The rebuttal is partial and worth stating. The architectural content is not unassessed by the programme, only by this day: [S061](wiki/seminars/S061.md) makes the assembly a cost model and cannot be passed without it, and [S052](wiki/seminars/S052.md)'s derivation is assessed the day before. Phase 1's items 1 to 4 and phase 8's read-back are on the architecture and are the day's actual folk-model instrument. And the alternative — assessing an architectural ablation the programme cannot run — is not available.

**But the objection is conceded, and what is conceded is a real cost of the practical turn.** The day's title now overstates what its specification checks. A reader comparing the two would be right to say the assessment drifted toward what was operable, which is the ordinary way a practical turn hollows out a theoretical claim, and this design is not immune to it merely because it noticed.

**The cheap fix is available and is not taken.** Phase 5 step 2 currently requires a component the student can actually remove. **Requiring in addition that step 6's load-bearing claim state which of arm A's four components the chosen pipeline component is *not* analogous to, and why, would cost one clause** and would force the architectural account into the assessed artefact at the point where the analogy is doing its work. It would also make §3.2's unsafe condition checkable in the artefact, which is what the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s widened scope rule requires of days from S090's review forward and which this day is not yet bound by. It is not in the design. The reason is that phase 5 already carries seven steps and three artefact elements, and the design judged the marginal clause more likely to be answered formulaically than thoughtfully — **which is a judgement about student behaviour with no evidence behind it, offered here so that it can be disagreed with.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Item 3 errors will over-predict damage and item 4 errors will under-predict redundancy, and both will be large. *Instrument:* items 3 and 4 against measured ablation results, reported **signed and separately**. The signed split is the claim.

**Prediction 2.** At item 1, positional information will be ranked less damaging than residual connections by a majority — and the ranking will be correct for the wrong reason, since most students do not know residuals exist. *Instrument:* item 1 rankings paired with the free-text justification.

**Prediction 3.** More than half of students will report at step 7 that the aggregate alone would have led them to a different conclusion. *Instrument:* step 7 counterfactuals coded for conclusion change. **If fewer than a third do, phase 3's per-class reveal did not transfer to their own work**, and the two-part staging is not doing what §3.3 claims.

**Prediction 4.** Phase 7 partners will defeat the load-bearing claim more often than they defeat the sensitivity statement, and the gap will be large. *Instrument:* phase 7 outcomes coded for which of the two demands succeeded. **The gap is the measure of how seriously step 3 was taken.**

**Prediction 5.** At item 5, a large majority will predict that the agent's ablation study omits something, and **fewer than a third will name either aggregate-only reporting or a missing sensitivity statement specifically.** *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded against the two fail conditions, compared with what each agent actually produced. **If the specific rate is high, the cohort already holds the day's two hard requirements and phase 4 point 7 is redundant; if the generic rate is also low, the sealed item is measuring nothing.**

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** The transformer's component structure, the role of residual and normalisation paths in deep stacks, and permutation-equivariance with injected positional information are grounded in [research/02-technical-foundations/](research/02-technical-foundations/collected-materials.md) and specifically in [research/02-technical-foundations/vaswani-attention-is-all-you-need.md](research/02-technical-foundations/vaswani-attention-is-all-you-need.md). **Correction, recorded rather than applied silently: an earlier version of this sentence also placed *component redundancy in trained networks* here, on that citation.** The citation does not support it. That note records an ablation table over *architectural variations* — different models trained under different choices — which is not the same object as removing a component from a network that has already been trained, and is not evidence that anything survives such a removal. The claim is reclassified below with its wording unchanged. The aggregate-hides-class argument follows [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md) and [research/03-measurement-evaluation/thakur-beir.md](research/03-measurement-evaluation/thakur-beir.md) and the [S026](wiki/seminars/S026.md) and [S049](wiki/seminars/S049.md) designs. The seam argument is [S007](wiki/seminars/S007.md)'s and follows [research/01-course-structure/](research/01-course-structure/collected-materials.md). The approximate-nearest-neighbour graph structure underlying arm B's ablation P is grounded in [research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md](research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md).

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** Arm B's four ablations and points 9 to 11 were read from Microsoft Learn on 2026-07-26.

*What the documentation says.* Azure AI Search offers two vector search algorithms: **exhaustive k-nearest-neighbours, which performs a brute-force scan and does not consume vector index size quota**, and **a hierarchical navigable small world graph, which performs approximate nearest-neighbour search and requires all data points to reside in memory, consuming that quota.** A vector query may invoke the exhaustive algorithm **at query time even where the field is indexed for the graph**, and the documentation names building a ground-truth neighbour set for evaluating an approximate algorithm's recall as a use case for the exhaustive one. The semantic ranker is documented as adding **secondary ranking over an initial result set already scored by BM25 or by reciprocal rank fusion**, using multilingual deep-learning models adapted from Microsoft Bing. Hybrid queries run full-text and vector arms in parallel and merge them by reciprocal rank fusion, which **uses the inverse of result rankings rather than the scores**; the documentation states that fused scores are consequently on a different magnitude from similarity scores, that this is by design, and that a low fused score can still indicate a strong match. A vector query's weight defaults to one and is a multiplier against rank within its result set; **the text side of a hybrid query is documented as carrying an implicit neutral weight of one**, adjustable only by changing the recall window. That window defaults to 1,000 documents and has a maximum of 10,000. **The default counting mode is documented as using the full hybrid result set, including documents that match the text query but fall outside the window and are not retrieved for ranking.** A response returns fifty results when neither `k` nor `top` is set. On the inference side, `reasoning_effort` accepts three named levels and a higher setting is documented as producing more reasoning tokens.

*What version.* No page carries one. The stable search API version at reading was 2026-04-01; the recall window and the low-score threshold are documented against preview versions, and two of arm B's four ablations therefore depend on preview parameters.

*What it does not report that a reader would need.* **The documentation states which algorithm is approximate and states no recall figure for it** — not a range, not a typical value, not a bound — so the size of ablation P's effect is unknowable in advance and must be measured, which is the day's own point arriving as a silence. It likewise **states that the semantic ranker improves quality and states no magnitude**, so ablation Q's effect is equally unknowable and the day's prediction about it is the design's own and not the vendor's. And, consistent with the folder-wide asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), the material states defaults, maxima, latencies and design intentions throughout and **states no accuracy anywhere.** No claim about any ablation's effect size is made in this paper on the documentation's authority, and none could be.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** Pretesting with bidirectional error construction, staged partial reveals to force premature commitment, pre-commitment before measurement, contrasting cases with an adjudicator, and error management training are cited for **direction and mechanism only**. No effect size is asserted for any of them, and none is asserted for AI-assisted productivity anywhere in this paper.

**Component redundancy in trained networks sits here rather than in class 1.** That some components can be removed from a trained network with almost no aggregate effect, and that the amount varies unevenly across components, is established in the wider ablation literature and is **not verified in this repository**. It is cited for **direction and mechanism only**, and point 5 is already written that way: it states that redundancy is real, uneven, and a property of *this* trained network, that it does not transfer, and that [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md) point 8 applies. **§3.2's scope statement independently forbids every generalising form of it**, and the day's ablation D is a demonstration of the phenomenon on one supplied model rather than evidence for its extent. **No ablation magnitude, redundancy figure or depth-behaviour claim is asserted for any named model**, and **no retrieval quality figure is asserted for any configuration**; all figures in the day are measured on the day's own materials and are marked perishable.
