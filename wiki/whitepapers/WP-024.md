## In plain terms

Code says what was done. It never says what was rejected, and it never says why.

Six months later, an engineer who was not present looks at something and asks why it is like that. The people who know have moved on. What is left is an artefact that records the winner of an argument nobody can reconstruct.

The published specification this session uses is unusually good and its field list is not the reason. **Two properties do the work.**

**The record enters review as a proposal, before the thing is built.** That is a claim about timing rather than content, and it is the difference between reasoning that could still change something and a minute of a decision already taken. A record written after the code is not a decision record; it is an explanation.

**The context section must be value-neutral — an account of the forces at play, written so that a reader cannot tell which way the decision went.** This is genuinely difficult to produce, it is mechanically checkable by handing the section to somebody, and it catches the commonest defect in written engineering judgement: **reasoning composed after the conclusion, to fit it.**

The session marks against that and against very little else.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching the architecture decision record as a specification rather than as a habit, and marking it on the value-neutrality of its context and the exercise of its status vocabulary. The argument is that a record's engineering value comes from entering review as a proposal and from stating what got worse, and that both properties are checkable while the quality of the decision is not. It requires one record in every set to be superseded by another that names it, on the grounds that a status vocabulary nobody exercises is decoration — a defect visible in the source's own published examples. The paper concedes that value-neutrality cannot be distinguished from careful omission by the test it proposes, and that the specification's own worked examples do not meet it, which the session teaches as material rather than resolving.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Most decisions in delivery are not taken; they accrete.

Somebody tries something under time pressure. It works well enough. Nobody objects, because objecting requires an alternative and nobody has one ready. Within weeks the thing is load-bearing, and by the time anyone calls it a decision it has no author, no rejected alternative and no stated cost. **It cannot be revisited because there is nothing to revisit** — a decision with no recorded reasoning can only be reversed by re-deriving the whole situation, which nobody has time for, so it is not reversed.

There is a second problem the session addresses in the same movement, and it is about writing rather than about process. **Engineers asked to record a decision write advocacy.** The context section explains why the chosen option was obviously right, because by the time the record is written the author believes it and believes it was always obvious. The record then documents a conclusion and its supporting argument, which is exactly the artefact that cannot help a later reader — the later reader needs the forces, not the verdict.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** Practitioners have usually met decision records and have usually met them as a compliance artefact filled in at the end. That prior is the session's principal adversary and it is stronger than ignorance would be.

The session retrieves [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md), [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) and [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md) because the decisions it records are the ones those sessions forced. **A learner arriving without a requirement set will write records about nothing**, which is why this session is fourth in the module rather than first.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Deliberately moderate, and lighter than [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md). It is placed fourth so that the assessment at [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) follows a session the cohort found manageable rather than one that exhausted them. The load that exists is affective: writing a value-neutral context for something you already believe is uncomfortable in a small, particular way.

**3.2 Why timing rather than content is the primary property.** A record that enters review before the work can change the work. A record written afterwards cannot, whatever its quality. **This is the same argument [S012](/wiki/seminars/S012.md) makes about readiness gates and [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) makes about stopping rules — a criterion formed after the outcome is known cannot discriminate between outcomes.** Three appearances, one claim, and the design says so each time.

**3.3 Why value-neutrality is checkable and quality is not.** Whether the decision was correct depends on facts nobody has yet. Whether a reader can guess the decision from the context is a property of the text and can be established in a minute. **The session prefers the checkable property**, and this paper concedes in §8 what that preference costs.

**3.4 Why a superseded record is required.** A status vocabulary exists to be exercised. **Neither of the published specification's own worked examples ever reaches the superseded state**, and a status nobody uses is a field, not a mechanism. Requiring one supersession per set forces the learner to discover that superseding is a piece of writing rather than a deletion — the old record stays, its status changes, and the new one names what it replaced.

**3.5 Why consequences must include something that got worse.** Every real decision costs something, and a consequences section listing only benefits is unusable to a later reader deciding whether the trade still holds. **This is design reasoning; the specification requires a consequences section and does not require a concession in it.**

The same argument gives the session its central claim, which is stronger than the specification's and is this design's: **a record listing only the chosen option is a description rather than a decision.** The code already tells a later reader what was done. What it cannot tell them is the shape of the space the decision was made in — which alternatives were live, which were dismissed, which were never available, and what the chosen option cost. **Without that, a considered decision and an unexamined default are indistinguishable in the running system.**

**3.6 Why value-neutrality has a warrant, and it is not a style argument.**

The requirement that a context reveal nothing is usually defended as good writing. There is a better reason, and it is about the reader rather than the author.

Studies of assessors judging complex written evidence, using think-aloud protocols, reconstruct a three-phase cyclical process — acquiring, organising and integrating evidence — followed by a return to the remaining material to look for confirming or disconfirming evidence. The load-bearing finding is what happened at that return: **"assessors were inclined to stick to their initial judgments even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence"**, and final judgements were more elaborate than preliminary ones but not substantially different ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). A companion study in the same literature reports that most raters began forming an impression of the person the moment they began observing.

**Read as a claim about the reader of a decision record, that changes what the context section is for.** A context that leaks its conclusion in its first sentence does not merely read badly; it fixes the reader's judgement before the forces have been presented, after which the rest of the record elaborates a conclusion rather than being weighed against it. **A value-neutral context is a defence against anchoring, not a courtesy.**

The same literature supplies the reason the artefact is prose rather than a score: assessors used narrative to form judgements and used numerical ratings only to confirm impressions already formed from the prose.

**Its limits, and the transfer is this repository's.** Clinical-education research, conducted with think-aloud protocols on mock portfolios in a single institution and one portfolio type. Think-aloud cannot capture fast or automatic processing and carries a risk that participants confabulate explanations for the researcher. The reported magnitudes concern rater variance in performance ratings and **none is reproduced here.** Nothing in that literature concerns engineering decision records; the analogy is this paper's and is offered as one.

**3.7 Why the platform anchor is a configuration corpus rather than a document tool.**

The session's argument needs one thing a document exercise cannot supply: a large, real, well-engineered artefact in which decisions are recorded *as constraints* and the reasoning is absent. Agent configuration is exactly that.

An openly published corpus of instructions, prompts, agent definitions and skills — authored, versioned, reviewed, packaged and distributed like any other artefact — was read at a pinned commit ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)). **Its organising claim is that a coding agent's behaviour is an engineering artefact**, and three of its properties are decision-record properties in another register: maturity and retirement expressed through declared membership and metadata rather than inferred from a file's age; durable on-disk state under stable identifiers, so a decision made in one phase is addressable in a later one; and the explicit separation of execution completion from acceptance outcome. That last is precisely the distinction a consequences section makes and a benefits list does not.

**But the corpus's teaching value here is the reasoning it does not contain.** Every constraint in an instructions file forecloses something, and the argument that produced the constraint is not in the file. **Configuration records the winner and discards the argument**, which is the same failure as a benefits-only consequences section, committed by a different genre.

**And the citation discipline is the second lesson, which is why this session rather than another carries this anchor.** This repository cites that corpus only with its commit, its published package version, the host it ran on and the model it ran against — because every one of those changes what the artefacts do, and an artefact is inert without a host that loads it. **Pinning does not slow the decay. It makes the decay visible.** A citation carrying a commit will one day be obviously stale; a citation without one will be quietly wrong and nothing in it will say so. That is the same property a dated, superseded record has, and it is why §3.4 argues the status vocabulary matters more than the field list.

**Its limits, and they bound §7 completely.** Perishability is extreme — file counts, paths, plugin names, package version and collection membership all changed during the period the reading describes. Host and model dependence is total. The repository contains benefit vocabulary in its own prose with no support, and **at the revision read no committed study, baseline, dataset or outcome corpus was found**, by its own evaluation machinery or anybody else's. Its own documentation states that its behavioural graders test output shape rather than correctness, and that some checks are advisory while others are authoritative. One build check was failing at that revision, and the reading records it rather than rounding it away. **A grader is not a result.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

Eight movements: situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**Opening item 2 is the instrument.** Naming a decision nobody wrote down is easy; naming the person who took it is frequently impossible, and the impossibility is the session's opening content. Accretion is invisible from inside and becomes visible the moment somebody asks for an author.

**The workflow requires two records for structural reasons rather than for practice.** One documents a decision already taken, which exposes the advocacy habit. One documents a decision not yet taken, which is the use the specification is actually for and the use most teams never make. **Writing both in one session is what makes the difference between them legible**, and neither alone would produce it.

**The workflow's first step is provenance, before any reading.** Commit, package version, host, model. It is placed first because a learner who records it afterwards records what they can remember rather than what was true, and because doing it first makes the discipline physical: **you notice, while writing the line, how many things have to be true for your reading to mean anything, and that every one of them will be false within a year.**

**Reading configuration as a decision is the movement that converts the session from documentation practice into engineering.** Every constraint forecloses something; the foreclosure is stated and the argument is gone; and reconstructing the argument is the exercise. A learner who has done this once will not again mistake a well-written instructions file for a record of reasoning.

**The supersession is a further piece of writing and is required.** It is placed last so that the learner has something to supersede and so that the act is performed on their own reasoning rather than on a supplied example. It also carries a stricter demand than the specification does: the superseding record must name **what changed in the world**, not what changed in the author's mind. A reversal condition that is a change of mind is not a reversal condition, for the same reason a disqualifying condition that could never fire is not a condition.

**The review movement withholds the decision on purpose.** The context section is handed over alone and unlabelled, and the reviewer is asked which way the decision went. **This was the session's only mechanical check until the workflow's step 6 was added**, and §8 records what it cannot distinguish.

**Step 6 asks the agent to argue for the rejected option using only what the context says.** That is a stronger test than the human guess, because it fails on a different property: a human who cannot guess may be reading a balanced account or a carefully incomplete one, whereas a context which cannot support an argument for the other side has demonstrably omitted the force that would have grounded it. **The two tests together narrow §8's first objection and do not close it.**

**The specification is taught as a specification that its own examples do not meet.** The published records do not exercise the full status vocabulary and do not refer to a documented comparison although the parent page instructs that a data-driven decision should. Pages read 2026-07-30. **That is handed to learners as material**, on the same reasoning [M03](/wiki/modules/M03.md) used for the contradiction it found in the internal corpus.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The advocacy context.** The likeliest failure by a wide margin. *Countermeasure:* the review movement, and the standard's instruction that a comparative adjective in the context is a warning sign.

**The fait accompli.** The record documents something already built and enters review with nothing at stake. *Countermeasure:* the requirement that one of the two records concerns a decision not yet taken. **This does not prevent the failure; it makes the learner experience the alternative once.**

**The consequence-free record.** *Countermeasure:* the standard requires a sentence beginning with a concession. Weakly enforced — a concession can be written about something that does not matter.

**The unexercised status.** *Countermeasure:* the required supersession, which is the strongest countermeasure in the session because it cannot be satisfied without producing a third artefact.

**The orphan record.** Well written, attached to nothing, in a folder nobody opens. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires the record to be tied to the work it came from, which is the specification's own collision rule repurposed as a findability rule.

**The manufactured decision.** The learner writes a record for a choice that was never genuinely open, because those are easier to write. *Countermeasure:* none available at this session. **[S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md) supplies the related discipline** — that a comparison should not be run where the choice is already clear — and this session's parallel case is not checked.

**The unpinned reading.** The learner reads the configuration corpus without recording the commit, the package version, the host or the model, and produces a finding about nothing in particular. *Countermeasure:* the workflow's first step and the provenance block in the record template. **This is a strong countermeasure against forgetting and no countermeasure at all against a learner who fills the fields from memory afterwards**, which prediction 6 in §9 predicts most of them will do.

**The reconstructed argument invented rather than inferred.** Asked what a configuration file rejected, a learner writes a plausible rationale rather than one the artefact supports. *Countermeasure:* none. The session cannot distinguish a reconstruction from an invention, and **it should not be read as teaching learners to recover authorial intent** — it teaches them that the intent is missing.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment evidence.** One specification item at [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md): at least one decision record whose context does not reveal which way the decision went, checked by handing the section to the assessor.

**The supersession is not separately assessed at [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md)** and that is a deliberate narrowing. It is required in the session and marked nowhere, on the grounds that the specification at the assessment should be short enough to be applied consistently. **The cost is that the session's strongest countermeasure has no assessment behind it.**

**Three further things are excluded from assessment and are stated to learners in advance.** The correctness of a decision is not assessed, because it depends on facts nobody has yet. The reconstructed rationale for a configuration file is not assessed, because the session cannot distinguish reconstruction from invention. And the provenance line is not assessed, although it is required — marking it would test whether four fields are populated rather than whether the learner understood why they are needed, and a populated field filled from memory is worse than an empty one because it looks like evidence.

**Nothing else is assessed.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the value-neutrality test cannot distinguish neutrality from careful omission.**

At its strongest: a learner who understands the test will write a context that conceals the decision by leaving out the force that determined it. The reviewer cannot guess, the item passes, and the record is now worse than an honest advocacy piece — it has the appearance of balance and is missing the thing a later reader most needs. **The test rewards a skill adjacent to the one it is meant to develop**, and there is no version of the test that separates them, because both produce the same observable.

**Conceded without qualification, and it is the stronger objection in this paper.** The rebuttal is only about the base rate: the observed default is advocacy rather than concealment, so a test that catches advocacy catches most of what is there, and a learner sophisticated enough to conceal has at least understood what a context section is for. **The declined fix is to have the reviewer read the context and then the decision, and judge whether the context accounts for it.** It is declined because that check requires a reviewer with domain knowledge of the decision, which the session cannot guarantee, and a check that only works with an expert reviewer will silently become a check nobody runs.

**Objection 2: decision records are an artefact of organisations that keep them, and teaching the specification teaches an artefact the learner will have no authority to introduce.**

At its strongest: the specification assumes a repository the team controls, a review process the team runs, and a culture where a proposal can be argued with before the work happens. **Forward-deployed engineers frequently have none of these.** They work inside somebody else's process, on somebody else's repository, under a delivery plan that has already committed to the decisions the record would examine. A graduate arriving with a decision-record habit will find nowhere to put one.

**Substantially conceded.** The rebuttal is narrow and is about the writing rather than the process: the value-neutral context and the concession in the consequences are properties of a paragraph and can be produced in a project wiki, a comment, or an email, in an organisation that has never heard of the specification. **The declined fix is to teach the writing without the apparatus.** It is declined because the apparatus is what makes the timing property real — a paragraph can be written after the fact and a proposal entering review cannot — and the timing property is the one this paper argues carries the value. **That is a genuine tension and it is not resolved.**

**Objection 3: reading a configuration corpus as a source of decision records reads reasoning into artefacts that never contained any.**

At its strongest: an instructions file is not a decision record with the reasoning removed. It is a different genre with different obligations, written by people who had no intention of recording a rationale and no obligation to. Asking a learner what the author rejected invites them to invent a deliberation that may never have occurred — the constraint may be a convention, an inheritance, a copy from another repository, or an accident nobody has revisited. **The exercise therefore trains a habit of confident reconstruction, on an artefact that cannot confirm or refute it**, and the session has no check that distinguishes a good inference from a fluent one.

**Conceded, and §5 records that there is no countermeasure.** The rebuttal narrows the claim rather than defending it: the exercise is not asking what the author thought, it is asking what the artefact *forecloses*, which is a property of the file and is checkable by reading it. The rejected alternative in the sense the session cares about is the behaviour the constraint prevents, not the meeting that produced the constraint. **The declined fix was to supply a corpus with published decision records attached, so that reconstruction could be scored against them.** It is declined because no such corpus is available at the scale required, and a constructed one would have the property the session exists to teach against — reasoning written to be found. **The cost is that this session's most engineering-flavoured movement is the one with the weakest check on it.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first-draft context sections will allow a reviewer to guess the decision correctly. *Instrument:* the review movement's guess, recorded per record, before revision.

**Prediction 2.** Learners will find the record for the decision already taken harder to write than the record for the decision not yet taken, and will say so when asked. *Instrument:* a single question put after both are written, coded *already taken harder* / *not yet taken harder* / *no difference*.

**Prediction 3.** Consequences sections will contain concessions about matters the author does not care about more often than about matters they do. *Instrument:* the concessions, coded by a second reader against the record's own stated decision. **If concessions are evenly distributed, the countermeasure is working better than this paper expects and the warning sign should be withdrawn.**

**Prediction 4.** Where a cohort is not required to supersede a record, none will. *Instrument:* status distributions in any cohort where the requirement is relaxed, compared against one where it is not.

**Prediction 5.** Contexts that survive the human guess will more often fail the agent test at workflow step 6 than the reverse. *Instrument:* the two outcomes recorded per record. **This is the direct test of §8's first objection**, and a null would mean the agent step is redundant and should be cut.

**Prediction 6.** Most learners will complete the provenance line after reading rather than before, and a substantial share will be unable to state the model version they used. *Instrument:* the provenance block, checked against the session's own timestamped step order, plus a single closing question about the model. **This predicts the failure of §5's countermeasure for the unpinned reading**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 7.** Superseding records will more often cite a change of mind than a change in the world, before the exercise's criterion is applied. *Instrument:* the superseding records, coded *external event* / *change of mind* / *neither* by a second reader, collected before and after exercise 6.

**Prediction 8.** Learners instructed to compare their item-4 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 4) will produce consequences sections containing an actionable concession more often than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* a second reader's binary judgement, split within cohort on exercise-4 completion. **The same prediction is made at [WP-021](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-021.md), [WP-022](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-022.md) and [WP-023](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-023.md), so a null across all four would indict the comparison pattern rather than this session.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a record entering review as a proposal can change the work and one written afterwards cannot; that a context revealing its own conclusion is advocacy; that a record listing only the chosen option is a description rather than a decision; and that a status vocabulary nobody exercises is decoration.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The field list is a specification from a page read on 2026-07-30, from a publication offering no comparative evidence for any of its practices and claiming none. Nothing establishes that teams keeping these records decide better, or that later engineers read them. The anchoring evidence behind the value-neutrality requirement is clinical-education research on mock portfolios, and **the transfer to engineering records is this repository's rather than the source's.** The configuration corpus establishes architecture and configuration claims and nothing else: **at the revision read, no committed study, baseline, dataset or outcome corpus supported any benefit claim attached to it.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The session's checks cannot tell a balanced account from a carefully incomplete one, and its most engineering-flavoured movement — reading configuration as a decision — has the weakest check of all, because nothing can confirm that a reconstructed rationale was ever anybody's. Both are recorded rather than smoothed over, and §8's first and third objections are the ones to hold on to.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** That the corpus separates artefact kinds into instructions, prompts, agents and skills with distinct application rules; that authoring source, packaging manifest and generated output are separated and the generated tree declared non-canonical; that maturity and retirement are expressed through collection membership and metadata rather than inferred from a file's age; that durable on-disk state under stable identifiers is declared authoritative over session memory, for provenance and restartability; that execution completion and acceptance outcome are distinct results; that untrusted fetched, uploaded and tool-returned content is to be treated as data rather than authority, with embedded instructions reported rather than obeyed; that an agent may not record a human's approval; that a gate may be advisory or authoritative depending on the tier it runs in; and that the project's own documentation states its regex-graded behavioural checks test output shape rather than correctness — all recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md), **read at a single pinned commit, with the published package version, the build state including one failing check, and the machine-derived file counts recorded at that revision.**

**The silences that matter.** **The corpus contains evaluation machinery and no committed results** — no controlled study, no calibrated baseline, no time-to-delivery dataset, no defect-rate comparison, no human-rated outcome dataset and no learner study were found at the revision read, and their absence is the finding rather than an omission in the reading. That absence is load-bearing here, because the corpus's own prose uses causal and outcome vocabulary that nothing in it warrants, and **a reader who does not separate the mechanism claims from the benefit claims will take the second on the authority of the first.** The repository publishes nothing about how often its untrusted-content boundary actually holds, which depends on the host attaching the instruction and the model complying with it, neither of which the file can guarantee — so a declared control is being read here as a declaration and not as an enforcement. It publishes no rationale for any of its constraints: **an instructions file states what is forbidden and nowhere states what was considered and rejected**, which is precisely the property this session uses it to demonstrate and is therefore a silence the design depends on rather than regrets. And absence at one commit is not proof of absence in the project; **this note should be re-run against a later revision before any stronger statement is made in either direction**, which is the same discipline the session teaches about its own records.
