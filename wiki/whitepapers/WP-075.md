# WP-075 — Evaluating a Trajectory, Not an Answer

**Seminar day:** [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md) · **Module:** [M15](/wiki/modules/M15.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md)
**Strands:** ME · IS · SG · **Complexity class:** 5
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Agent Framework — conversation split strategies and cloud evaluation · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends the day that closes the gap [WP-052](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) §6 named and left open: that an agent trajectory is a routing record which reads as an explanation, in a domain where the artefact is more convincing than an attention map, and that S052 did not name the trajectory case. It defends delivering that closure as a phase 2 retrieval rather than as exposition, and defends the register argument — a trajectory persuades because it is prose in the first person, and no property of the computation changed to make that so. It defends three units of judgement as the day's assessed choice, and a contrasting set whose payload is the evaluation any organisation would adopt on evidence and which is blind to both failure shapes that report success. It defends running all four over stored runs rather than describing them, and teaching that the platform's three conversation split strategies are those three units with the cheapest one set as the default. **Section 7 reports the [WP-052](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) closure, the rule-6 obligation [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) placed here, and the instrument [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §9 carried forward.** Section 8 concedes that the day's most general claim rests on a single record it chose, that its headline instrument is transparent to the students it measures, and that the instrument students build with produces the blind unit fastest.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

[WP-052](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) §6 predicted this day's difficulty six months in advance and predicted it correctly:

> **S075 at roughly six months is the most valuable target and the least likely to fire.** An agent trace is a routing record that reads as an explanation, and it is the same error in a domain where the artefact is even more convincing. Nothing in this day names the trajectory case, which is a gap.

The prediction has two halves and both are the problem. **Most valuable**, because the trajectory is the artefact every student will reach for when an agent does something unexpected, for the rest of their careers, and reading it as an account of what happened is the default. **Least likely to fire**, because the transfer required is from a heatmap of numbers to a page of first-person prose, and nothing in the surface of the two artefacts suggests they are the same kind of object.

**The asymmetry is the day's central difficulty and it is a fact about register rather than about computation.** An attention map is a heatmap. A student has to be *talked into* reading a story in it, and [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) spent a day talking them out of it again. A trajectory needs no such persuasion. It arrives in sentences, in the first person, giving reasons, and it reads as an explanation **because it is written in the form of one.** The system that produced it was not describing its computation; it was producing text, and [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) established exactly what optimisation selects for in text produced under judgement — confidence, length, well-formedness, agreeableness.

The design's response is to make the closure a **retrieval** rather than an exposition. Phase 2's bridge asks what a trajectory is evidence of and what would establish that a step caused the outcome, and the room answers from [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) in under two minutes. A claim in that position is stronger than any argument the instructor could make. **And the design's own expectation — tested at §9 — is that the same room will then adopt E3 in phase 3 anyway**, and that the gap between what the cohort can say in phase 2 and what they choose in phase 3 is the day's actual content.

Three further difficulties sit underneath.

**The unit of judgement is a choice students do not know they are making.** Outcome, step and episode answer three different questions, need three different judges, and have three different dependency structures. A team that has not chosen one has chosen the episode by default, because that is what the tooling emits.

**The judge's degrees of freedom multiply and [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md)'s method breaks in a specific place.** [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §6 said the characterisation method does not straightforwardly extend and did not say where. It is the reorder test: you cannot score the same trajectory twice reordered, because **the order is the content.** A student who reports having run it has reported something that did not happen.

**And the record is produced by the loop that failed.** [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) point 8 handed this day the problem by name. For a selection or an output-utilisation failure the trajectory shows a clean run, so the artefact the evaluation reads is structurally unable to exhibit the two failure shapes that report success — which is why point 8 of this day is the module's most important sentence: **the evaluation that agrees best with human raters is blind to both of them.**

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**High, and the module's peak.**

The day asks the student to construct a criterion, choose a unit, build an evaluation, characterise a judge, run an intervention, construct a class from residuals, and write a licensing statement. Each has been done before in this programme — at [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md), [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md), [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) and [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) respectively — and none has been done over an artefact with no fixed unit of analysis.

The interactivity is between steps 1, 2 and 7 and it is unusually tight. The criterion constrains which units can express it; the unit determines which failure shapes are visible; and step 7's licensing statement is a function of both. **A student who chooses the unit before the criterion produces an evaluation that measures what the tooling emits**, which is the professional default the day exists to interrupt, and the ordering of the steps is the countermeasure.

Management:

- **Everything is carried in.** The capability, the drawing, the crossings and the shapes are all four days old; nothing is scoped on the day.
- **The run log is indexed and the evaluation harness is supplied.**
- **[S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md)'s characterisation method is supplied as a form**, so the student's work is locating the break rather than reconstructing the method.
- **The replay procedure is worked with the room**, since it is the day's only genuinely technical operation and it fails silently when done wrong.
- **One intervention, not four.** Step 5 requires a single run.
- **Phase 3's four evaluations, their records and the panel agreements are all supplied.**

**Step 2 is unsupported**, and it is the class-5 boundary: the unit choice determines what the rest of the artefact can possibly say, and it is the decision the design refuses to scaffold.

Expertise reversal applies at points 5 and 7 for a student who has built an evaluation pipeline professionally; the design's answer is that such a student is being asked for the blind-shape statement at step 2 and the method break at step 4, neither of which experience supplies.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Judging a run, then judging it again with the tool results removed,* items 1 and 2.
- *Naming what your own criterion could not detect, before the day supplies the answer,* item 1's second clause.
- *A right answer by a wrong path against a wrong answer by a right path,* item 3.
- *Ranking four evaluations by what each licenses, before the records,* phase 3.
- *An evaluation that wins on every criterion you would use to choose,* E3.
- *Naming the shapes your own chosen unit cannot see,* step 2.
- *Finding where a method you were taught does not extend,* step 4.
- *Reporting a step that read as load-bearing and was not,* step 5.
- *Having a partner name a causal claim in your own report that your evidence does not support,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Statistical machinery for clustered trajectory data.* [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) supplies the direction and point 7 states it; the estimator belongs at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), where the horizon makes it necessary.
- *Building a step criterion.* Point 5 says nobody has written one and stops. Constructing one is [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md)'s activity at a scale the day cannot support, and attempting it would consume phase 5 entirely.
- *Adversarial manipulation of the trajectory.* [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md)'s and [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s. A record an attacker can write is a different problem from a record the system writes about itself.
- *Cost per episode as a unit.* Named in point 4's scope statement as an omission and assigned to [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md)'s material, because introducing it here would make the unit choice an economic decision and the day is about what each unit can see.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The three units are **safe** for choosing what to measure on a capability you operate and can replay. They are **unsafe** as a complete account in three named ways — they omit cost per episode, which is often the unit the operator actually optimises; they omit the unit that applies when several runs interact on shared state, where no single run is the object; and they omit the user-session unit spanning episodes, which is where a system that is individually acceptable and cumulatively intolerable becomes visible. They are additionally **unsafe wherever the system cannot be replayed**, because the outcome unit is then the only one available and the other two are unavailable rather than rejected. **That distinction is the one most likely to be lost**: a team reporting *we evaluate at the episode level* on an unreplayable system is describing a constraint as a choice, and the day's artefact makes the boundary checkable by requiring the shapes-not-seen statement at step 2, which cannot be written honestly without knowing which units were actually available.

**The platform instance carries its own scope statement, at point 9.** The correspondence between the three units and the three shipped split strategies is **safe** for choosing a split deliberately, for reading a reported score and asking what it is about, and for absorbing a new split strategy by asking which unit it produces. It is **unsafe** in three directions. It is **not evidence** that three is the right number of units — point 4's scope statement names three omissions from the same list, and a vendor implementing three does not make three complete; the documentation's agreement is agreement about the *claim that the split determines the meaning*, which is the durable content, and about nothing else. It is **not a mapping any other framework will preserve**, because a split strategy is an implementation of a unit and the implementations differ. And it is **unsafe as a claim about what the custom splitter buys**: an arbitrary callable can produce a unit outside the three, which is useful, and it can also produce a unit nobody has a criterion for, which is [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md)'s problem with a new surface. Step 2's *name the split and say whether it is the default* clause is the artefact element under which a student applying the correspondence outside its range would fail.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **item 2**.

A student judges a trajectory, states a criterion, and commits. The same trajectory arrives with the tool results removed and only the system's own narration left. The judgement either moves or it does not, and the student finds out which in the same twenty-five minutes. **A student whose judgement does not move has been reading the narration**, and nobody has to tell them so. §8 objection 2 concedes what is wrong with this instrument.

The second mechanism is **E3**, and its construction is the day's most deliberate decision. E3 does not lose. It has the highest agreement with the human panel, the highest between-run reliability, and the lowest cost of the three non-trivial options — **it wins on every criterion a competent team would use to choose an evaluation**, which is why every organisation adopts something like it. And its six-month record shows it ranking a narrating system above a non-narrating one on runs whose outcomes and effects are identical. The room does not get to conclude that E3 is a bad evaluation built by careless people. They have to hold that the best-performing option on the evidence available is blind in a specific place.

The third is phase 6. Three careful readers, three reasonable causal claims, one change carried for a year that prevented nothing, and a correction that arrived by accident eleven months later. **Nobody misread the record; the record does not contain the information the reading required.** Every student in the room has read a log as an account of what happened.

Autonomy is complete at steps 1, 2 and 6. Competence is supported by everything being carried in and by the replay being worked with the room. Relatedness is supplied by the human rater panel, whose judgements are the adjudicator at phase 3, and by the partner at phase 7.

**The competence risk is at step 5** and it is the sharpest in the module: the required finding is one that undermines the artefact the student has just built. A student who reports that a step their evaluation scored well on had no effect on the outcome has produced evidence against their own work. The instructor states before phase 5 that this finding is the day's most valuable output, that the cohort's rate is reported at phase 8 and is expected to be substantial, and that an artefact containing no such finding will be read as one where the intervention was chosen to be safe.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items. Item 2 is the closure administered before it is named. **Item 1's second clause applies the elevation [WP-074](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-074.md) §8 argued for**, one day after it was argued, and §7 records the reuse as contingent. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction**, and it is the module's fifth and hardest: the failure worth predicting is not a defect but a correct evaluation of the wrong thing.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals, one scaffolded at three quarters, and the bridge that closes [WP-052](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) §6 as a retrieval item.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four units of judgement over one run set, **all four run**, **selected against the shape this cohort most often mis-attributed at [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md)**, disclosed under instrumentation rule 3. E3 is the payload and wins on every choosing criterion. The running supplies the cost gradient — E1 free and immediate, E3 cheap and fast, E4 slow and expensive — which is most of why organisations choose the blind one.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Point 2 is the closure stated, placed second so it lands while E3's record is still visible. Point 4 carries the strong-form scope statement with three omissions and an availability caveat. Point 6 discharges [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §6's flagged difficulty by locating the break. Point 8 joins the module and is the module's sentence. **Points 9 and 10 are the platform layer, and their placement is argued in the day itself**: point 9 is the only place in the module where the vendor's documentation states the programme's own claim in the programme's own words, and leading with it would teach students to accept a claim because a vendor made it. The claim is derived at point 4 and corroborated at point 9, in that order.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps. Step 2 gains the split clause and the agent-proposed-unit record added in §8. Step 5's required finding is the closure in the student's own hands. **Step 2 is unsupported.** The artefact gains the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement, the last carrying a fifth-instance sentence about whether the module's five frontier statements describe a frontier.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** Careful readers, reasonable claims, a year of a change that prevented nothing, and an accidental correction — then an agent case containing no defect at all, in which the default split answers a different question under the right heading.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** An inversion construction — [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §9's carried instrument — and a causal claim in the student's own report attacked.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 1 against item 2, **with its demand characteristic stated**; item 1's second clause against step 7 as a movement; item 3 sorted by what students asked for; item 4 against E3; **item 5 read across the module's five predictions**; the step 5 intervention rate; the inversion rate beside [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md)'s; the unit choices cross-tabulated against the shapes they cannot see; and **the units the agent proposed against the units the students chose.**

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has built an evaluation pipeline will produce E-something-like-E3 at step 3 fluently, with a defensible rubric and a characterised judge. Predicted failure: **they will answer step 2's third clause from the taxonomy rather than from their own evaluation** — writing "cannot see selection and output utilisation" because point 8 said so, without checking whether their particular rubric happens to include an intent field that makes selection partially visible. Countermeasures: step 7 requires the evidence a reader would need to see the blind shape, which cannot be produced from a memorised claim; phase 7's second obligation attacks a causal claim in their own report; and the specification fails a unit chosen without the shapes named, which forces the check rather than the recall. **§8 objection 1 concedes a related and larger problem with how the general claim itself is warranted.**

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is step 5's replay, which is the only genuinely technical operation and which fails silently. Countermeasures: the procedure is worked with the room; one intervention is required rather than four; the recorded states are pre-captured and indexed; a failed replay is reported as a failed replay and is a correct output; and steps 1, 2, 6 and 7 are unaffected by it, so a student whose replay does not run still produces a complete artefact minus its causal element, with the absence recorded rather than concealed.

**Phase 3 succeeds too easily.** E1 and E2 are ranked quickly: outcome-only is cheap and coarse, step-level is rich and expensive, and both are legible from the descriptions. **The difficulty is E3 against E4**, which are the two that look like the sophisticated options and which differ in the only way the day cares about — E4 intervenes and E3 reads. A group that ranks E4 above E3 on *what it licenses*, before the agreement figures arrive, has done the day's work in phase 3. **Fallback:** ask which of the four they would run weekly on the substrate, at what cost, and **which of the four they would put in front of an incident review** — the second question separates them and no group answers it well on first attempt, because E4 is unaffordable weekly and is the only one that survives the review.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **evaluative nihilism**, and it is the module's fatalism risk arriving at its natural destination. The cohort has learned that two failure shapes are silent, that the record is produced by the loop that failed, that the narration is a produced output, that the best-agreeing judge scores the narration, and that the method they were taught for characterising judges does not fully extend. The available conclusion is that agent evaluation is not possible and that everybody doing it is fooling themselves.

Countermeasures, and this is where the module's answer has to be strongest because it is the last day. **E1 and E4 both work.** Outcome evaluation is cheap, coarse and honest, and it sees output-utilisation failure, which nothing else does; replay is expensive and is the only causal evidence available, and it is available. **Step 5 is constructive and every student runs one**, so the day ends with each of them holding a causal result about their own system that no amount of trajectory reading would have produced. Point 8 is stated as a constraint on what you can conclude rather than as a verdict on the enterprise. Phase 6's case shows a correction arriving — late, by accident, and arriving. And the instructor states once, at point 8, that the day's argument is that **the trajectory is evidence of routing and there are three other things you can measure**, which is a positive claim and is the sentence the design has withheld from points 1 to 7 so that it lands after the difficulty rather than in place of it.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) at two weeks (where the unit of judgement decides what drift is observable at all, and where the compound blind spot reported here selects the cases), [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) at four weeks (where an incident's trajectory is read in front of the people involved and every causal claim must carry a replay or be marked as routing), [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) (the safety case, where an evaluation is evidence in an argument and its blind shapes are what the argument cannot cover), [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) (residual risk, where the shapes-not-seen statement becomes something told to a receiving organisation) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md).

**[S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) at four weeks is the consequential retrieval and it is instrumented.** A blameless forensic review is a room full of people reading a routing record as an explanation, under pressure, about something that harmed somebody. The step 5 intervention result is the instrument, and S080's phase 3 must require every causal claim drawn from the incident's record to be supported by a replay or marked as routing only — with this cohort's own rate reported to the room before the forensic work begins, so that the discipline arrives as their own finding rather than as a procedural rule.

**[S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) at four quarters is the longest.** A safety case assembled from an evaluation blind to two failure shapes is a safety case with a hole in a stated place, and this day's step 7 statement is the only artefact in the programme that names the hole in advance. That retrieval is named and is not instrumented, which is a gap and is recorded as one.

Retained content: the trajectory as a routing record, the register argument, intervention as the only attribution, the three units and their questions, the break in [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md)'s method, and point 8. Six items.

No constituent skill is scheduled for blocked practice. The unit choice is exercised four times in phase 3 and once in phase 5, then interleaved into the between-day sets alongside [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md)'s five differences and [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md)'s four shapes, which are the two enumerations it is most confusable with — and which it must be held against rather than beside, since the day's whole point is the cross-product of unit and shape.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 4.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment judgement**, per [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md).

One specification item in ME302 with IS302. Per rule 1, the checked elements are the criterion in [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md)'s form with a party named who would not recognise it; the unit with the question it answers, the question a reader will believe it answers, the [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) shapes it cannot see, **the split it corresponds to with a statement of whether that split is the default, and the unit the agent proposed or an explicit none**; **the built evaluation run over the run log**; the judge characterisation with the method break stated and its replacement, **with every judged score reported as uncalibrated**; one intervention result including any load-bearing-looking step whose removal changed nothing; one residual-constructed class with its taxonomy status; the licensing statement with its blind shape and the evidence a reader would need; both phase 7 outcomes; **the prompt record**; **the intervention record**; and **the frontier statement with its fifth-instance sentence**. **Nothing else is assessed.**

Three hard requirements: **a causal claim supported only by the trajectory fails**, applied to the student's own report as strictly as to the material; **a unit chosen without naming the shapes it cannot see fails**, and **a unit reported without its split fails**; and **a judge characterisation claiming [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md)'s method applied unchanged fails.** Two more: **a judged score reported without the statement that it is uncalibrated fails**, since no agreement with human judgement is published for any evaluator in this stack; and, imported from the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), **an intervention record with no entries fails** unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error, with step 7's licensing statement as the qualifying demonstration on this day.

**Neither the agent's proposed unit nor a student's agreement with it is assessed.** The record exists so that the cohort distribution can be reported at phase 8, and §8 objection 3 explains what it is for.

Humility and integrity are observed within the activity — the first at step 7 and at whether an intervention that dissolved the student's own account was reported, the second at step 5's required finding.

### Closure of the gap WP-052 §6 named and assigned to this day

**This paragraph is the report [WP-052](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) §6 requires, and the gap is closed.**

WP-052 §6 recorded that **an agent trace is a routing record that reads as an explanation, that it is the same error as the attention-map case in a domain where the artefact is even more convincing, that S075 was the most valuable target of its own retrieval and the least likely to fire, and that nothing in S052 named the trajectory case — which it called a gap.**

**The case is named, four times, in four registers.**

- **As a retrieval, at phase 2's bridge.** The room is asked what a trajectory is evidence of and what would establish that a step caused the outcome, and answers from [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) points 3 and 4. The closure arrives as a consequence of a fact the cohort already holds, which is the position WP-052 §6 was hoping for when it named a target rather than asking S052 to cover the case itself.
- **As a measurement, at phase 1 items 1 and 2.** The same trajectory is judged with its tool results and then without them. **A student whose judgement does not move has been reading the narration**, and the cohort's movement rate is the day's headline figure. §8 objection 2 concedes that this instrument is transparent to the students it measures and states what the design did about it.
- **As instruction, at points 1 and 2.** Point 1 states the routing-record claim and adds what S052 could not have added — **the stated reasoning is not part of the record at all**, being an output the system produced under the selection pressures [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) established. Point 2 states the register argument that WP-052 §6 gestured at: a trajectory is more convincing than a map because it is prose in the first person, and no property of the computation changed to make that true.
- **As a specification condition, at phase 5 step 5 and in the day's single hard requirement.** A causal claim supported only by the trajectory fails, and step 5 requires an intervention on the student's own run with **a step that read as load-bearing and was not** as a required finding if one is found. **This is the part WP-052 could not have specified**, because at class 4 the students had no system of their own to intervene on.

**What the closure does not do.** It does not show that the transfer fires. WP-052 §6 predicted this retrieval would be the least likely of its five to succeed, and the design's own §9 prediction 1 is that the room will produce the routing-record argument at phase 2 **and adopt E3 anyway at phase 3** — which, if it holds, means the closure has been made and the transfer has not, and the gap WP-052 named has moved from *unnamed* to *named and not yet effective.* The design believes that is the honest state and has arranged the day so that the difference is measurable rather than assumed.

**Rule-6 discharge.** One obligation lands here and **the element exists.** [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) named this day's phase 3 as the response occasion for its step 4 observation-position error rate, read as a shift against item 3, specifying that the four trajectory evaluations be selected against the shape the cohort most often mis-attributed. **The element exists: phase 3's run set is drawn from runs exhibiting that shape, the selection rule and the cohort's own shift are disclosed at the start of the phase per instrumentation rule 3, and the task-after requires each evaluation to be assessed for which shapes it can see** — so the unit-of-judgement work is done on the cohort's weakest observational reasoning rather than on a convenient set.

**Two older forward links discharged.** [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §6 recorded that judging a trajectory multiplies the judge's degrees of freedom and that its characterisation method does not straightforwardly extend; **that is point 6 and step 4's second clause, and the day locates the break rather than restating that one exists.** [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §9 carried its phase 7 inversion-construction success rate here; **that is phase 7's first obligation, and phase 8 reports this cohort's rate beside their own [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) rate.**

**A pending elevation applied voluntarily, and recorded as contingent.** [WP-074](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-074.md) §8 argued yesterday for elevating to an instrumentation rule the observation that **an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall**, with the remedy being to administer it before phase 4 as well and report the movement. **The rule is not in force and no number is assigned to it.** This day nonetheless applies it: item 1's second clause probes the blind-spot claim before the taxonomy exists, step 7 is the same question about the student's own evaluation, and phase 8 reports the movement. **Per instrumentation rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8, this is a proposed remedy and the reuse is contingent** on the outcome reported for [WP-074](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-074.md)'s proposal; a reported failure retracts it here along with every other contingent reuse.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments, **both responding outside this module.** **The step 2 unit choice cross-tabulated against the shapes it cannot see** — and specifically the count of students who chose a unit blind to a shape they had already declined to discriminate at [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) — responds at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) phase 3, whose drift cases must include one visible only at a unit this cohort did not choose. **The step 5 intervention result** responds at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) phase 3, where every causal claim drawn from the incident's trajectory must carry a replay or be marked as routing only, with this cohort's rate reported before the forensic work begins. **[WP-079](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-079.md) §7 and [WP-080](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-080.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

Programme instrumentation: the item 1-against-item 2 movement, reported with its demand characteristic; the item 1-to-step 7 blind-spot shift; the step 5 intervention rate; the phase 7 inversion rate against the [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) baseline; and the unit-by-shape cross-tabulation.

ABET and CS2023 contribution: PO2, PO4 and PO6.

**What this day does not license anyone to conclude.** A student's evaluation is evidence that they can choose a unit for a capability they operate and can replay, and can state what it does not see. **It is not evidence that the evaluation is correct**, and it is not evidence about any other system's evaluation. **One intervention on one run establishes one causal fact**, and the day is explicit that a student who reports a load-bearing-looking step that was not has learned something about that step and nothing about their system's other steps. And phase 3's records license nothing about which unit to choose — that is the point of requiring the question a reader will believe it answers alongside the question it answers.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day's most general claim rests on a single record the design chose, and the design preferred the record to the argument because the record is more persuasive.**

At its strongest: point 8 says *the evaluation that agrees best with human raters is blind to the two failure shapes that report success.* That is a strong structural claim, it is the module's concluding sentence, and it is warranted in the day by **E3's six-month record on one substrate, with one judge, one held-back panel and one run set.** The programme has spent [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md), [S042](/wiki/seminars/S042.md), [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md), [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md) and [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) teaching students that a single record does not support a general claim, and this day asks them to leave holding one that does.

The design has a deductive warrant available and does not lead with it. **An episode rubric reads the record; the record is produced by the loop; therefore the record cannot contain what the loop did not observe; therefore an episode rubric cannot see the shapes [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) point 2 established have no observer.** That argument needs no record at all. It is available from yesterday's material, it is three sentences, and it is what actually makes point 8 true.

**The day presents the record as the evidence and the argument as the commentary, when the dependency runs the other way.** A student who accepts point 8 on E3's agreement figures has accepted exactly the inference the programme forbids, and the fact that the conclusion happens to be correct makes it worse rather than better — an argument this design has made about other people's work at [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md).

**The fix is a reordering and it is cheap.** State point 8 as a deduction from [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) point 2 at the top of phase 4, and present E3's record as a confirming instance rather than as the warrant. It costs nothing but sequence.

**It is declined, and the reason is that the design preferred to be persuasive.** Productive-failure sequencing requires the room to meet E3 and be defeated by it before receiving the account, and reordering phase 4 does not repair phase 3 — the record is still where the room's belief is formed. The design could have kept phase 3's defeat and then framed point 8 explicitly as a deduction with the record as illustration, and it did not, **because a six-month record showing a judge preferring narration is more convincing than three sentences of valid reasoning, and the design wanted the sentence to survive the module.**

**The objection is conceded and not answered.** The day teaches a structurally-warranted claim on empirical grounds because the empirical grounds land harder, in a programme whose central discipline is the opposite. The minimum available repair is not taken here either, because the honest repair is the reordering and the design has declined it on stated grounds rather than on good ones.

**Objection 2: the day's headline instrument is transparent to the students it measures.**

At its strongest: phase 8's most quoted figure is how many students' judgement of a run changed between item 1 and item 2 — the same trajectory, with the tool results removed. **Item 2 is visibly item 1 with something taken out.** A student who notices that, which is most of them, will infer that they are meant to change their answer, and demand characteristics will produce a movement rate that measures compliance with a perceived expectation rather than a change in reading. The figure is then reported to the cohort as evidence about how they read trajectories, and it is used in this paper as the closure's principal measurement.

The rebuttal is partial. The two items are separated by two other items and the whole set is sealed, so no student sees their own item 1 answer while writing item 2. Item 1's criterion sentence is committed in writing, which makes a costless reversal slightly less costless. And the movement is reported per student rather than pooled, so a student who changed everything is visible.

**None of that removes the demand characteristic and the design concedes it.** The clean instrument is a between-groups split: half the cohort sees the full trajectory at item 1 and the stripped version at item 2, half the reverse, and the comparison is between groups rather than within students. It costs one printing decision.

**It is declined for a reason that is pedagogical rather than methodological, and that is the concession.** A between-groups design destroys the thing the day actually wants: **the student seeing their own judgement move.** That experience is the day's engagement mechanism and it is not replaceable by a cohort statistic. The design chose a transparent instrument with pedagogical value over an opaque one with evidential value, and it also halved the n on the day's headline measure in a cohort of one class, which is a real secondary cost.

**The available half of the repair is taken.** Phase 8 now reports the figure **together with its demand characteristic, stated to the room in those words**, and names it as an upper bound on how much the removal actually moved anyone — which is instrumentation rule 3's discipline applied to the day's measurement of itself rather than to its case selection. **And the closure's real evidence is stated to be elsewhere**: the step 5 intervention rate is a measurement of what students found in their own systems, is not transparent to them in advance, and is the figure [WP-075](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-075.md) §9 prediction 3 is written against. That instrument is smaller and it is not compromised.

**Objection 3: the day teaches which unit is blind and then hands students an instrument whose path of least resistance produces it.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn, and it is the module's recursion arriving at the one place where it costs something.

At its strongest: phase 5 step 3 is a build, done in Copilot agent mode. **The evaluation an agent produces fastest from a plain request is an episode-level rubric with a model judge**, because that is what the tooling defaults to, what the documentation's first example shows, and what the phase 6 agent case demonstrates in one prompt. E3 is the payload of phase 3 precisely because it is what a competent team adopts; **it is also, now, what a competent prompt returns.** So the day spends fifty minutes establishing that the best-agreeing evaluation is blind to the two silent shapes, and then puts in front of every student an instrument that will build exactly that evaluation unless they intervene.

And the specification permits it. **A unit chosen without naming the shapes it cannot see fails; a unit chosen *with* them named passes**, however blind it is. That is the correct rule — the day's claim is about what you can say, not about which unit to pick — but it means **the assessed outcome is a declared blindness rather than a mitigated one**, which is the same shape [WP-073](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-073.md) §8 objection 3 found one layer down and is now the second instance in the module of the design accepting a declaration where the day's own argument asks for more.

**The expensive fix is named and declined.** Require two evaluations at different units and a report of their disagreement. It would settle the matter and it is a second build in a ninety-minute phase whose interactivity §3.1 already describes as the module's peak; the day cannot carry it and no rearrangement of the phase makes room.

**The cheap fix is named and made.** Step 2 now records **the unit the agent proposed before the student chose**, or that it proposed none, and phase 8 reports the distribution against the units the students actually chose. It costs one field and no build time. It does not stop a student adopting the blind unit — nothing in the day should, since the blind unit is often the right one — **but it converts the recursion from an observation into a measurement**: if the instrument proposes the episode unit and the cohort mostly departs from it, the day's instruction survived contact with the path of least resistance, and if it does not, the design has taught a lesson and supplied a tool that undoes it. §9 prediction 5 states which the design expects and the record decides.

**What remains is conceded.** The field records a proposal, and a student who prompted their way to a unit in three turns will report the last proposal rather than the first pull. The measurement is therefore a lower bound on the instrument's influence, which is the same direction of error as §8 objection 2's, and this paper does not have a fix for it that costs less than the disagreement build it has already declined.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** The room will produce the routing-record argument at phase 2's bridge in under three minutes, **and a majority of groups will still rank E3 first at phase 3** before the records arrive. *Instrument:* phase 2 bridge timing and answer quality recorded; phase 3 group rankings recorded before records are supplied. **The conjunction is the claim.** If both hold, the closure [WP-052](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) §6 asked for has been made and the transfer has not, which is the state §7 reports as honest. **If the room ranks E4 first, the transfer fires and WP-052 §6's pessimism about this retrieval was wrong** — which would be the most encouraging result in the module.

**Prediction 2.** At item 2, fewer than half of students will change their judgement of the run when the tool results are removed. *Instrument:* item 1 and item 2 judgements paired per student, reported with the demand characteristic per §8 objection 2. **The direction is the claim and the magnitude is not asserted**, because the instrument is compromised in the direction of over-reporting movement; a result above half is uninterpretable and a result below half is informative, which is an asymmetric instrument and is stated as one.

**Prediction 3.** More than a quarter of students who run an intervention at step 5 will find a step their evaluation scored well on whose removal did not change the outcome. *Instrument:* step 5 results coded for outcome movement, per student, with the intervention type recorded. **This is the closure's real evidence and the prediction the design most wants on record**, because it is a measurement of the students' own systems rather than of their reading. **If the rate is near zero, either the substrate's capabilities are unusually tight or students are choosing safe interventions**, and the two are distinguished by the intervention-type distribution.

**Prediction 4.** The phase 7 inversion-construction success rate will be **lower** than the same cohort's [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) rate. *Instrument:* phase 7 first-obligation outcomes recorded and joined per student to the [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) baseline. **The direction is the claim and it is [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §6's own prediction tested**: constructing a pair of runs an evaluation ranks wrongly requires holding the unit, the judge and the trajectory's ordering simultaneously, which is strictly harder than constructing an output pair. **If the rate is equal or higher, WP-048 §6 was wrong that its method does not extend**, and point 6's break at the reorder test is narrower than the day claims.

**Prediction 5.** At step 2, **the modal agent-proposed unit will be the episode**, and more than half of students will choose a different one. *Instrument:* step 2's agent-proposed-unit record and the student's chosen unit, tabulated jointly. **The conjunction is the claim and it is §8 objection 3's test.** If the agent proposes the episode and students mostly depart from it, the day's instruction has survived the instrument's default and the recursion is being managed rather than merely named. **If students mostly accept the proposal, the day has taught the lesson and supplied the tool that undoes it**, and the disagreement build §8 declined has to be found room for by cutting something else from phase 5.

**Prediction 6.** At item 5, fewer than one student in five will predict that the agent's evaluation will be correct and will answer a different question; the modal prediction will be a defect in the code or the configuration. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded defect / wrong-question / other, against what each student's agent produced. **The low wrong-question rate is the claim**, and it is the module's closing frontier finding: five days of predicting agent failure will have taught the cohort to expect errors and not to expect correct answers to unasked questions, which is the harder and more consequential prediction and the one the design has not managed to teach anywhere.

**Instrumentation note.** No compliance-register entry is required by this paper. All three §8 findings are design judgements between defensible options — a sequencing choice, an instrument choice and a phase-budget choice — and are **recorded** rather than fixed under the register's criterion, with the two cheap repairs taken and the two expensive ones named and declined. The one shared-page change this paper depends on is [WP-074](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-074.md) §9's, which supplies the wording for the elevation this day applies voluntarily and contingently.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That an agent trajectory is a routing record which reads as an explanation, and that it persuades for a reason unrelated to the computation: it arrives in sentences, in the first person, giving reasons. The unit of judgement — outcome, step or episode — is a choice, and a team that has not made it has taken whatever the tooling emits.

**What the evidence licenses.** Judge bias, the non-averaging of systematic judge error, and what optimisation selects for in produced text are **class 1**, verified here. The split strategies are **class 4**: three are documented, one as the default, and the documentation states in those words that how you split determines what you are evaluating — while **stating no consequence of getting it wrong**, raising no error, and publishing no evaluator agreement with human judgement. The three units, the register argument and the method break at the reorder test are **class 3**, untested.

**What to do with it.**

- **Only an intervention attributes.** A step that reads as load-bearing and changes nothing when removed is the finding worth reporting.
- **Name the shapes your unit cannot see**, and say which split you used and whether it is the default.
- **A record addressable by identifier buys provenance and restartability, and buys nothing about correctness** — see the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). Your trajectory is exactly that record, and the best-instrumented example available is silent in the same place.
- **Report every judged score as uncalibrated.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes and does not answer that its most general claim — that the best-agreeing evaluation is blind to both failure shapes that report success — is warranted by a single record the design chose, when a three-sentence deduction was available that needed no record at all. **Hold the design to that.** The reordering fix costs nothing but sequence, and the stated reason for declining it is that the record is more persuasive than valid reasoning.

Three strategies are documented: **last turn**, described as the default, splitting at the last user message and suited to response quality at a specific point; **full**, treating the first user message as the query and the entire remainder as the response, suited to task completion and overall trajectory; and **per-turn**, scoring each user-to-assistant exchange independently with cumulative context, for fine-grained analysis.
