# WP-075 — A Handover with a Named Receiving Owner

**Seminar day:** [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md) · **Module:** [M15](/wiki/modules/M15.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md)

> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Support taper** | A declining schedule of delivery-team availability after handover, with a stated end date and a stated state beyond it |
> | **Unaccepted line** | A manifest item with no receiving owner, recorded as such, with the consequence of that stated |

## In plain terms

Four sessions produced four artefacts. This one puts a name against every line and lets somebody argue with the names.

The assessment is deliberately narrow, and the narrowness is the design's main commitment. It checks form: that there is an owner or an explicit absence, that acceptance conditions are evaluable without the learner, that thresholds carry reasons, that a runbook was executed by somebody else, that the trigger is a register state, that steps leave artefacts, that the taper ends. It does not check whether the handover would have worked, because nothing available to this programme could establish that and an assessor who tried would be marking their own intuitions about a fictional organisation.

The one place the specification takes a position rather than checking a form is the unaccepted line. **A handover that records an unowned critical item and states the consequence passes; a handover with an owner for every line because nobody was asked does not.** That is a judgement about which failure is more recoverable, and it is argued in §3 rather than assumed.

## Abstract

This paper defends a closed-specification assessment at the end of a handover module, and defends its narrowness. The argument is that the only checkable properties of a handover at the time it is made are formal ones — presence of owners, evaluability of acceptance conditions, provenance of transferred values, existence of an execution record, mechanisation of the withdrawal trigger, and termination of the support taper — and that every substantive property is only observable after the last moment this programme can observe. The occasion is an artefact plus a live defence against a challenger playing the capable, unwilling receiving party. The paper concedes that a learner can satisfy every checked element and still have designed a transfer that fails, that the specification therefore measures conformance rather than competence, and that no source behind this programme reports sustainment of any delivered system.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A handover is judged by an outcome that arrives after everybody who could learn from it has gone.

That is not a solvable problem and the session does not pretend to solve it. What it does is separate the properties that can be checked now from the properties that cannot, assess only the first, and say plainly that the second exists. **The alternative, which is common and worse, is an assessment that appears to judge the outcome by judging the confidence of the person presenting it.**

The second problem is social. Every pressure in a handover conversation pushes towards agreement: the delivery team wants to leave cleanly, the sponsor wants it finished, and the receiving party would rather not have an argument in front of their own management. The result is a document everybody has agreed to and nobody has read. The challenger exists to remove the agreement pressure for one hour.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The occasion assumes all four artefacts from [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md)–[S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md), and the instruction to assemble rather than rebuild is explicit on the page.

A learner who rebuilds is not penalised by the specification and is disadvantaged by the clock, which is the intended pressure: an engagement's last week is not the moment to reconsider the object of transfer, and a learner who discovers that here has learned it cheaply.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why the specification is closed and published in advance.** A specification that surprises the learner assesses their ability to guess an assessor's preferences. A published closed list assesses whether the artefact has the stated properties. The programme's assessment position is that the second is the only defensible thing to check when the substantive question is unobservable.

**3.2 Why an unaccepted line passes and a fully owned manifest may not.** Two failures are possible. In the first, an item has no owner and this is recorded, so the receiving organisation begins with a known gap. In the second, every item has an owner, some of whom were never asked, so the receiving organisation begins with an unknown gap and a document asserting there is none. **The first is recoverable by somebody reading the document; the second is discovered by an incident.** That is the argument, and it is an argument about the recoverability of error, not a finding.

**3.3 Why the taper must end.** An availability that declines without terminating is not a schedule; it is a standing assumption that the delivery team will answer, held privately by both sides, budgeted by neither. Requiring a date and a stated state on the far side converts an assumption into a commitment somebody can decline.

**3.4 Why the defence is short and live.** An artefact supplies evidence and a conversation supplies narrative. The occasion's provisional reading comes from the artefact, and the defence is used to confirm, revise or overturn it — never the reverse, since forming an impression in conversation and then reading the artefact for support is where assessor variance lives.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**The challenger plays the technology function**, and the brief is specific: capable, unwilling, and correct that the system was done to them rather than with them. That last clause is the important one. A challenger who is merely obstructive teaches the learner to push through; a challenger with a legitimate grievance teaches them that the grievance is information about willingness, which is where [M16](/wiki/modules/M16.md) begins.

**The challenger's easy offer is the instrument.** They will accept everything, today, if the learner routes the difficult lines to the willing under-authorised team leader. Taking the offer produces a fully owned manifest, which is the failure §3.2 describes, and which will feel like success in the room. The offer is not a trick — it is exactly what happens in practice, made visible.

**The specification's ordering is deliberate.** Transfer items come before teardown items, because a learner who has produced an excellent withdrawal specification and a thin manifest has reproduced the source corpus's own asymmetry, and reading the specification in this order makes that visible to the assessor before they reach the part the learner is proud of.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The immaculate teardown.** Excellent hygiene, thin transfer. *Countermeasure:* specification ordering, and item 4's requirement that the six categories each appear or their absence be reasoned.

**The signature farm.** Owners assigned to people who were not asked. *Countermeasure:* item 3's requirement that acceptance conditions be evaluable by the owner with the learner absent — which is a proxy and defeatable, since a learner can write a good condition for an owner they never spoke to.

**The taper without a floor.** *Countermeasure:* item 9, which is the cheapest check in the specification and the one most often failed on first submission.

**Conceding under challenge.** *Countermeasure:* item 10, which asks the learner to distinguish a line held with confidence from a line held on hope. A learner conceding indiscriminately cannot answer it.

**Defending the assignment instead of recording the problem.** *Countermeasure:* none inside the specification. It is visible in the defence and enters the entrustment evidence at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) rather than the mark here.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**A closed specification check with ten items, published in advance.** No entrustment level is recorded here; the occasion contributes observation towards [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md).

The ten items are on the session page and are not restated. Three properties of the set are worth stating here.

**It checks the presence of an absence.** Items 1 and 2 pass a manifest that records an unowned line and fail one that hides it, which is unusual and is the design's own commitment.

**It checks that somebody else could act.** Items 3, 5, 6 and 8 each require the artefact to be usable by a person who is not the learner — the owner, the obliged party, the executor, the later reader. That is the module's whole content expressed as a test.

**It does not check the system.** The quality of the underlying design, the recipient's satisfaction, and whether the transfer would have succeeded are excluded by name, because an assessor drawn towards any of them is assessing something the occasion cannot see.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the specification measures conformance to a documentation format and calls it engineering judgement.**

At its strongest: a competent learner will read the ten items and write to them, producing an artefact with every checked property and no thought behind any of it. Owners can be named without conversations, acceptance conditions can be phrased evaluably without being evaluable, provenance lines can be invented, and a taper date can be chosen arbitrarily. The specification then certifies the presence of ten strings.

**This is conceded.** Every checkable property is gameable by somebody who understands it, and the specification is published in advance precisely so that everybody understands it. The rebuttal is limited to two points. The execution record at item 6 is not gameable at low cost, because it requires another person to have actually run something. And the live defence at item 10 is not answerable from a written artefact, because it asks the learner to sort their own claims by confidence. **The fix that was considered and declined** was an unpublished specification or a secret weighting, which was rejected because it converts the occasion into a guessing exercise and destroys the artefact's standing as evidence. The objection stands against the other eight items.

**Objection 2: refusing to assess whether the handover would have worked is an abdication, and the programme is hiding behind unobservability.**

At its strongest: experienced practitioners can read a handover and form a well-calibrated view of whether it will hold. Refusing to ask them to means the assessment ignores the only judgement in the room that has any real-world content, and the learner receives a mark that certifies tidiness.

The rebuttal is that the judgement exists and has been placed somewhere else rather than discarded. It is exactly what [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) records, as an entrustment level with a mandatory narrative, formed by more than one assessor across more than one occasion — because a single assessor's holistic view of a fictional handover, recorded as a mark, is the least reliable instrument available and the most confidently used. **The programme accepts that this splits an assessment somebody would rather have whole.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first submissions will fail item 9. *Instrument:* the taper field, coded *date stated* / *no date* / *no taper*, on first submission.

**Prediction 2.** Learners who accept the challenger's easy offer will produce fully owned manifests, and will describe the occasion afterwards as having gone well. *Instrument:* the owner column against the post-occasion self-report, paired per learner.

**Prediction 3.** Withdrawal specifications will be longer than manifests for a substantial share of the cohort. *Instrument:* a length comparison of the two artefacts, reported as a distribution and never as a threshold.

**Prediction 4.** Item 10 will discriminate more strongly between learners than any of the nine written items. *Instrument:* the pass rate per item across the cohort. **If item 10 does not discriminate, the live defence is redundant at this occasion** and the assessment should become a written submission, which would be cheaper and should then be done.

**None of these has been measured.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the checkable properties of a handover are formal; that recording an unowned line is a better artefact than assigning it to somebody willing; and that a taper without an end is an unbudgeted commitment.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The specification's ten items are this design's choices, defended by argument and unmeasured.

**The concession worth remembering.** You can pass this occasion and have designed a transfer that fails. The specification says so on its face, and a learner who leaves believing otherwise has been misled by the fact that something was marked.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That the source corpus specifies no named receiving owner, no exit criteria, no acceptance and no support taper, and reports no sustainment evidence for any delivered system — no record of whether any system survived, was maintained by a customer, or was still running later. Recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md).

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That assessors given both narrative and score reason from the narrative and use the score to confirm an impression already formed, and that idiosyncratic rater variance is a substantial component of variation in performance ratings — which is why §3.4 requires the provisional reading to come from the artefact. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The ten specification items and their ordering; the position that a recorded unowned line passes while a fully owned manifest may not; the requirement that a taper terminate; the challenger's brief and the easy offer; the exclusion of system quality, recipient satisfaction and probable success from assessment; and all four predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** **This session has no platform anchor and relies on no vendor documentation.** The occasion assesses artefacts about ownership, acceptance and availability, none of which is a product surface. **The silence worth recording:** no vendor documentation known to this programme defines what it means for a customer to have accepted a delivered system, what a support taper should contain, or what evidence of handover a delivery organisation should retain. Commercial handover templates exist widely in the professional-services world and are not vendor documentation; where a team is required to use one, this session's specification should be read as a set of properties that template must be checked against rather than as a competitor to it. If vendor documentation appears defining any of the three, this occasion acquires an anchor and this paper records its version and what it still leaves out.
