## In plain terms

Four sessions produced four artefacts. This one puts a name against every line, has that named person run the stack in front of the learner, and lets somebody argue with the names.

**The intervention is the name.** An artefact handed to a team is handed to nobody. Teams do not start harnesses, do not read runbooks at three in the morning and do not pause releases; people do, and only people who know they are the person. Everything else in the module exists so that there is something specific for a named person to accept.

The second half of the intervention is that the person demonstrates. **Transfer is demonstrated, not declared** — and there is a well-warranted psychological reason rather than a rhetorical one. Mastery experience is the most effective source of the belief that one can do a thing, and that belief determines whether the thing is attempted at all. A recipient who does not believe they can operate the harness will not weigh whether to; they will simply never open it, and will not experience that as a decision. **The demonstration is therefore not a test administered to the recipient. It is the intervention.**

The assessment is deliberately narrow, and the narrowness is the design's main commitment. It checks form: an owner or an explicit absence, acceptance conditions evaluable without the learner, thresholds carrying reasons, a runbook executed by somebody else, a register-bound trigger, steps that leave artefacts, a taper that ends, and a supervision level recorded per demonstrated act. It does not check whether the handover would have worked, because nothing available to this programme could establish that and an assessor who tried would be marking their own intuitions about a fictional organisation.

The one place the specification takes a position rather than checking a form is the unaccepted line. **A handover that records an unowned critical item and states the consequence passes; a handover with an owner for every line because nobody was asked does not.** That is a judgement about which failure is more recoverable, and it is argued in §3 rather than assumed.

## Abstract

This paper defends a closed-specification assessment at the end of a handover module, defends its narrowness, and defends the demonstration at its centre.

The argument runs in four parts. **First**, entrustment is not an analogy for handover; a handover is a claim that somebody can now do something unsupervised, which is the definition of an entrustable professional activity, and the framework's provisions — an ordinal supervision scale with sub-levels, an entrustment-based discussion containing mandatory what-if questions, the distinction between ad-hoc and summative entrustment, and expiry through disuse — apply without modification. **Second**, whether the recipient believes they can operate the system determines whether they will attempt it, and efficacy beliefs pre-empt cost–benefit reasoning, so an absence of objection in a handover meeting is not evidence of agreement; mastery is the most effective source of that belief, which makes the demonstration the intervention rather than the check. **Third**, the demonstration only produces information if the recipient can say *I cannot run this*, which is a voice behaviour under a structural asymmetry that makes silence cheaper. **Fourth**, only formal properties of a handover are checkable at the time it is made, and every substantive property becomes observable after the last moment this programme can observe.

The occasion is an artefact, a demonstration record with a supervision level per act, and a live defence against a challenger playing the capable, unwilling receiving party.

The paper concedes that a learner can satisfy every checked element and still have designed a transfer that fails; that eight of the eleven items are gameable by anybody who reads the published specification; that one morning's observation is an ad-hoc entrustment being run in the shape of a summative one; that the efficacy literature has never been tested on adult professionals learning a technical craft; and that no source behind this programme reports sustainment of any delivered system.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A handover is judged by an outcome that arrives after everybody who could learn from it has gone.

That is not a solvable problem and the session does not pretend to solve it. What it does is separate the properties that can be checked now from the properties that cannot, assess only the first, and say plainly that the second exists. **The alternative, which is common and worse, is an assessment that appears to judge the outcome by judging the confidence of the person presenting it.**

The second problem is social, and it is the one the demonstration exists for. Every pressure in a handover conversation pushes towards agreement: the delivery team wants to leave cleanly, the sponsor wants it finished, and the receiving party would rather not have an argument in front of their own management. **The result is a document everybody has agreed to and nobody has read** — and, worse, a recipient who has said nothing and is believed to have assented.

The third problem is that the receiving side is usually named as a function. A function cannot start a harness. The specification's first item exists to convert an institutional noun into a person, and the demonstration exists to find out whether that person can do the thing the noun was assumed to be able to do.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The occasion assumes all four artefacts from [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md)–[S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md), and the instruction to assemble rather than rebuild is explicit on the page.

A learner who rebuilds is not penalised by the specification and is disadvantaged by the clock, which is the intended pressure: **an engagement's last week is not the moment to reconsider the object of transfer, and a learner who discovers that here has learned it cheaply.**

The occasion also assumes the learner has already run one silent observation, at [S072](/wiki/seminars/S072.md). The demonstration here is the same instrument at higher stakes with a real recipient, and a learner meeting the rule for the first time will spend the morning fighting the urge to speak rather than recording what happens.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why the specification is closed and published in advance.**

A specification that surprises the learner assesses their ability to guess an assessor's preferences. A published closed list assesses whether the artefact has the stated properties. The programme's assessment position is that the second is the only defensible thing to check when the substantive question is unobservable, and §8's first objection takes the cost of that seriously.

**3.2 Entrustment is the subject, and the framework applies without modification.**

An entrustable professional activity is a unit of professional practice — "activities with a beginning and an end", which "are only entrusted to trained personnel" ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). Four of its provisions do real work here.

**Entrustment is expressed on an ordinal supervision scale, not as a score**, and the sub-levels are where a handover actually lives: the distance between all findings double-checked, key findings double-checked, and a distant supervisor with prompt review is exactly the range a transfer moves through. A single overall verdict on a handover cannot see it.

**The entrustment-based discussion must contain what-if questions.** Retrospective explanation shows the recipient understood the case they met; counterfactual questioning is the only purchase available on the cases they did not — and the framework states why it matters: "an entrustment decision implies the acceptance of risk, as it can never be fully predicted what the student will encounter."

**Ad-hoc entrustment is not summative entrustment.** The summative standard requires "an agreement between multiple observers", multiple observations and multiple evidence types. **One morning with one observer is ad-hoc**, and the specification requires the record to say so. Almost every handover in industry is an ad-hoc entrustment reported as a certification, and naming that is worth more than any additional document.

**Entrustment expires through disuse.** A handover assuming permanence claims more than the model it borrows from, and the taper's end date is not the expiry.

The framework also supplies three professionalism characteristics stated behaviourally — integrity, reliability and humility, the last defined as "the ability to observe own limitations and willingness ask for help when needed" — and item 11 is where humility is assessed, on the learner rather than on the recipient.

**Its limits:** a clinical model from its originator, reporting adoption rather than effectiveness; medicine has patients, legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint; **the full evidence standard is expensive and this programme runs a reduced version and says so.**

**3.3 Whether the recipient believes they can operate it determines whether they will.**

This is the module's best-warranted psychological claim and it is under-used elsewhere in this programme.

Perceived self-efficacy is "beliefs in one's capabilities to organize and execute the courses of action required to manage prospective situations", and those beliefs determine the goals people set, the effort they expend, how long they persevere and their resilience to failure ([research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md](/research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md)).

Three consequences follow for a handover.

**Mastery is the most effective source and is the only one explicitly ranked.** Not explanation, not encouragement — doing the thing. That is the warrant for the demonstration, and it converts it from a verification step into the substantive intervention of the session.

**Efficacy beliefs pre-empt cost–benefit analysis.** "Such wholesale exclusions of large classes of options are made rapidly on perceived efficacy grounds." A recipient who does not believe they can operate the harness will not weigh whether to; the option will not reach a decision. **An absence of objection in a handover meeting is therefore not evidence of agreement**, and this is the single most useful sentence the psychological literature supplies to this stage.

**Efficacy building is task design, not encouragement.** "Successful efficacy builders do more than convey positive appraisals… they structure situations for them in ways that bring success and avoid placing people in situations prematurely where they are likely to fail often." **Sequencing the demonstration is the intervention.** Persuasion is also asymmetric — far easier to destroy efficacy with words than to build it — which is why a careless remark during a demonstration is more consequential than a reassuring one.

One correction must travel with this and the session states it: **only easy successes build a brittle sense of efficacy.** "If people experience only easy successes they come to expect quick results and are easily discouraged by failure," and "A resilient sense of efficacy requires experience in overcoming obstacles through perseverant effort." The constraint is on frequency and timing, not on difficulty, and a demonstration nobody could fail is not kindness.

**Its limits are serious and travel with every use.** The volume read is a conference proceedings edited by the field's founder, with no search strategy, no inclusion criteria and no study in the framing chapter whose result went the wrong way; several load-bearing results are cited as unpublished manuscripts. **Its empirical settings are schools, families, clinics and migration — there is no study of adult professionals learning a technical craft**, so the application here is an inference. Measurement is almost always self-report immediately before a performance that is often self-rated. Directionality is unsettled in field settings and the volume concedes it twice. **No magnitude from it is asserted anywhere in this wiki, and reading it in full does not change that.**

**3.4 The demonstration produces information only if the recipient can fail out loud.**

Psychological safety is "the belief that the work environment is safe for interpersonal risk taking", where the risk is appearing ignorant, incompetent or disruptive; it is explicitly **not** immunity from consequences ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)). Most people report at least one occasion when they felt unable to raise a concern with a superior despite believing the issue important.

**The asymmetry that produces the silence is structural rather than personal: voice pays the organisation, later and uncertainly; silence pays the self, now and certainly.** In a handover the recipient's incentives have exactly that shape. Saying *I cannot do this* costs standing in front of their own colleagues immediately, to protect an outcome months away that may never be traced back to the room.

Two items from that source are operational. **Situational humility** — "Humility is the simple recognition that you don't have all the answers", with "Confidence and humility are not opposites". And the four phrases available regardless of rank: *I don't know. I need help. I made a mistake. I'm sorry.* The accompanying observation applies to the learner rather than the recipient: **"Sometimes, you have to take an interpersonal risk to lower interpersonal risk."** Workflow step 3 is that sentence turned into a procedure.

The change-management literature converges independently and supplies the operational form: a supervisor must know "How to create a safe environment that allows employees to practice and to make mistakes without retribution" ([research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md)). **Its limits are the module's standing ones: a trademarked practitioner model published by the firm that sells its training, with no independent evaluation anywhere in it.**

**The safety source's limits.** A narrative synthesis by the construct's originator, citing densely and selectively; several results reported from studies not read here; **the foundational 1999 paper is paywalled and has not been read by this repository**, so the narrower team-level definition and the mediation finding rest on an abstract. No magnitude reproduced.

**3.5 Why the artefact forms the judgement and the conversation confirms it.**

The assessment literature on how assessors actually judge is unambiguous and uncomfortable. Assessors reading real portfolios **used the narratives to form judgements and the numbers only to confirm them**; a substantial share of rating variance is attributable to the rater rather than the performance, **and it does not improve with experience**; assessors anchor early and then seek confirmation, so additional evidence elaborates the initial judgement rather than overturning it; and agreement on an outcome does not imply agreement on the reasoning ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)).

Three design consequences. The provisional reading comes from the artefact and the defence confirms, revises or overturns it — never the reverse, since forming an impression in conversation and then reading the artefact for support is precisely where assessor variance lives. **The remedy the literature proposes is group deliberation with substantiated judgements rather than better rating scales**, which is why item 11 asks for reasons and why the assessor records a written reason rather than a mark. And single-assessor high-stakes decisions are indefensible given the measured idiosyncrasy, which is why this occasion contributes evidence to [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) rather than producing an entrustment level itself.

**Its limits:** single-institution medical-education studies with mock portfolios and assessors who did not know the students; think-aloud methods cannot capture fast or automatic processing and participants may construct explanations for the researcher, which the authors state. **They establish how assessors reason, not that narrative judgement is more valid than scored judgement.** No magnitude reproduced.

**3.6 What the demonstration is performed on.**

The operate stack is four acts, each mapping to an artefact the learner already holds: start the harness and read one result's `reason`; work one runbook to its stated end state; read the cost position; locate one withdrawal-step evidence artefact.

The first is anchored deliberately. Evaluation output is pass or fail with a `reason` alongside the score, and **the `reason` is what makes an automated judgement auditable rather than merely numeric** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). A recipient who can only read a score has inherited a number they will eventually stop believing; a recipient who reads a reason can disagree with it, which is the precondition for acting on it.

**Its limits and its silence:** read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches; most agent evaluators are preview; one consulted page is superseded. **No agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data and no threshold rationale — assert none.** The recipient reading a `reason` is reading a model's stated rationale, not a validated one.

**3.7 Why an unaccepted line passes and a fully owned manifest may not.**

Two failures are possible. In the first, an item has no owner and this is recorded, so the receiving organisation begins with a known gap. In the second, every item has an owner, some of whom were never asked, so the receiving organisation begins with an unknown gap and a document asserting there is none. **The first is recoverable by somebody reading the document; the second is discovered by an incident.** That is the argument, and it is an argument about the recoverability of error, not a finding.

**3.8 Why the taper must end.** An availability that declines without terminating is not a schedule; it is a standing assumption that the delivery team will answer, held privately by both sides and budgeted by neither. Requiring a date and a stated state on the far side converts an assumption into a commitment somebody can decline. The delivery standard's own operating principle points the same way — leave the customer's own engineers more capable rather than more dependent — though it reports no outcome for that or for anything else ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-fde-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-fde-delivery-practice.md)).

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The challenger plays the technology function**, and the brief is specific: capable, unwilling, and correct that the system was done to them rather than with them. That last clause is the important one. A challenger who is merely obstructive teaches the learner to push through; **a challenger with a legitimate grievance teaches them that the grievance is information about willingness**, which is where [M16](/wiki/modules/M16.md) begins.

**4.2 The challenger's easy offer is the instrument.** They will accept everything, today, if the learner routes the difficult lines to the willing under-authorised team leader. Taking the offer produces a fully owned manifest — the failure §3.7 describes — and it will feel like success in the room. **The offer is not a trick. It is exactly what happens in practice, made visible.**

**4.3 The specification's ordering is deliberate.** Transfer items come before teardown items, because a learner who has produced an excellent withdrawal specification and a thin manifest has reproduced the source corpus's own asymmetry, and reading the specification in this order makes that visible to the assessor before they reach the part the learner is proud of.

**4.4 What the demonstration makes observable.** A supervision level recorded per act, using sub-levels. **A single overall judgement of a handover is unfalsifiable and always positive; four levels against four acts is a shape** — and the shape usually shows one act at the lowest level that everybody in the room had been counting as transferred. That act is the finding, and no document review produces it.

**4.5 The silence rule is inherited rather than re-taught.** Anything the learner says during the demonstration is not in the artefact, so speaking does not help the recipient; it destroys the reading and leaves the defect in place for the person who will be alone with it. The same rule, for the same reason, as [S072](/wiki/seminars/S072.md).

**4.6 Why the risk is lowered before it is asked for.** Workflow step 3 requires the learner to state one thing about the system they could not make work, before the recipient is asked to run anything. This is §3.4 turned into a procedure, and it is placed before the demonstration rather than after because a recipient who has already failed silently will not retroactively report it.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The handover to a function.** Every line owned by a team and no line owned by a person. *Countermeasure:* item 1 and the demonstration, which a team cannot perform.

**The immaculate teardown.** Excellent hygiene, thin transfer. *Countermeasure:* specification ordering, and item 4's requirement that the six categories each appear or their absence be reasoned.

**The signature farm.** Owners assigned to people who were not asked. *Countermeasure:* item 3's requirement that acceptance conditions be evaluable by the owner with the learner absent — **which is a proxy and defeatable**, since a learner can write a good condition for an owner they never spoke to. The demonstration catches it only for the acts demonstrated.

**The demonstration nobody could fail.** The acts are sequenced so the recipient succeeds at everything and nothing is learned. *Countermeasure:* item 10's requirement that a level be recorded per act, and the scoring rule that a record with no stops and uniform high levels is a failed observation. **This countermeasure detects the symptom and cannot detect a learner who chose four easy acts.**

**The rescue.** The learner speaks. *Countermeasure:* the inherited silence rule, and a record field for interventions.

**The taper without a floor.** *Countermeasure:* item 9, the cheapest check in the specification and the one most often failed on first submission.

**Conceding under challenge.** *Countermeasure:* item 11, which asks the learner to distinguish a line held with confidence from a line held on hope. A learner conceding indiscriminately cannot answer it.

**Defending the assignment instead of recording the problem.** *Countermeasure:* none inside the specification. It is visible in the defence and enters the entrustment evidence at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) rather than the mark here.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**A closed specification check with eleven items, published in advance.** No entrustment level is recorded for the learner here; the occasion contributes observation towards [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md).

The eleven items are stated in full on the session page and are not restated here. What is assessed is:

- **The presence of an absence.** Items 1 and 2 pass a manifest that records an unowned line and fail one that hides it. This is unusual and is the design's own commitment, argued at §3.7.
- **That somebody else could act.** Items 3, 5, 6, 8 and 10 each require the artefact or the observation to involve a person who is not the learner — the owner, the obliged party, the executor, the later reader, the demonstrator. **That is the module's whole content expressed as a test.**
- **That the entrustment claim is honestly classed.** Item 10 requires a supervision level per act with sub-levels, and the record to be labelled ad-hoc unless the summative standard was genuinely met.
- **That the commitment terminates.** Items 7 and 9: a register-bound trigger with a named permitted actor and reuse prohibited, and a taper with an end date and a stated state beyond it.
- **One disposition.** Item 11, assessed live, on whether the learner can sort their own claims into confidence and hope with differing reasons. The assessor records a written reason rather than a mark, following §3.5.

**What is excluded, by name.** The quality of the underlying system is not assessed. The recipient's competence is not assessed. The recipient's satisfaction is not assessed. Whether the transfer would have succeeded is not assessed, and could not be, because it becomes observable after the last moment this programme can observe.

**Nothing else is assessed.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the specification measures conformance to a documentation format and calls it engineering judgement.**

At its strongest: a competent learner will read the eleven items and write to them, producing an artefact with every checked property and no thought behind any of it. Owners can be named without conversations, acceptance conditions can be phrased evaluably without being evaluable, provenance lines can be invented, and a taper date can be chosen arbitrarily. The specification then certifies the presence of eleven strings.

**This is conceded.** Every checkable property is gameable by somebody who understands it, and the specification is published in advance precisely so that everybody understands it. The rebuttal is limited to three points. The execution record at item 6 is not gameable at low cost, because it requires another person to have actually run something. The demonstration at item 10 is not gameable at all, because it requires a named individual to perform acts in front of the assessor's record. And item 11 is not answerable from a written artefact, because it asks the learner to sort their own claims by confidence. **The declined fix was an unpublished specification or a secret weighting**, rejected because it converts the occasion into a guessing exercise and destroys the artefact's standing as evidence. The objection stands against the other eight items.

**Objection 2: refusing to assess whether the handover would have worked is an abdication, and the programme is hiding behind unobservability.**

At its strongest: experienced practitioners can read a handover and form a well-calibrated view of whether it will hold. Refusing to ask them to means the assessment ignores the only judgement in the room with real-world content, and the learner receives a mark that certifies tidiness.

The rebuttal is that the judgement exists and has been placed elsewhere rather than discarded. It is what [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) records, as an entrustment level with a mandatory narrative, formed by more than one assessor across more than one occasion — because a single assessor's holistic view of a fictional handover, recorded as a mark, is the least reliable instrument available and the most confidently used, and the assessor-variability literature in §3.5 is unambiguous that idiosyncrasy does not improve with experience. **The declined fix was a holistic assessor rating alongside the specification**, rejected because §3.5 predicts it would anchor the specification check rather than supplement it. **The programme accepts that this splits an assessment somebody would rather have whole.**

**Objection 3: one demonstration by one recipient on one morning is an anecdote, and dressing it in an ordinal supervision scale makes it look like a measurement.**

At its strongest: the framework's supervision levels were built for repeated observation across a training programme, with multiple supervisors, over months. Recording a sub-level from a single observation borrows the vocabulary of a calibrated instrument for a judgement that has none, and a level written as `3b` will be read as data by everybody downstream. The specification's own honesty requirement — label it ad-hoc — is a word inside a document that presents a number-like scale.

**Conceded, and the tension is real.** The mitigation is that the framework itself makes the ad-hoc/summative distinction central rather than incidental, and that recording a level per act is used here to produce a *shape* rather than a score — the finding is the act at the lowest level, not the level itself. **The declined fix was to record free-text observations with no scale**, rejected because free text does not reveal the asymmetry across acts, and the asymmetry is the whole output of the exercise. A reader who thinks a borrowed scale is worse than an unstructured note is making a defensible call, and §9's fourth prediction is the closest available test.

**Objection 4: the efficacy argument is doing heavy lifting and the literature behind it has never met this population.**

At its strongest: §3.3 is the paper's most confident psychological claim, and its source is a conference proceedings edited by the construct's originator, drawn from schools, families, clinics and migration, measured by self-report, with unsettled directionality in field settings and no disconfirming study in its framing chapter. Building a session's central intervention on it is exactly the extrapolation this programme criticises elsewhere.

**Partially conceded, and the concession is about scope rather than content.** What the design takes is a mechanism — mastery outranks persuasion, and options excluded on efficacy grounds are excluded before being weighed — both of which are observable in a room and neither of which requires a magnitude. **The declined fix was to drop the efficacy framing and defend the demonstration purely as verification**, rejected because verification and intervention are different designs: a verification can be scheduled last and cut, whereas an intervention is sequenced deliberately and cannot. The extrapolation is real, is recorded in the session's limits, and a reader who weighs it more heavily than this paper does is making a defensible call.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first submissions will fail item 9. *Instrument:* the taper field, coded *date stated* / *no date* / *no taper*, on first submission.

**Prediction 2.** At least one demonstrated act per learner will come out at a supervision level the learner had assumed was transferred. *Instrument:* a pre-demonstration written prediction of the level per act, compared with the recorded level. **If predictions and recordings agree, the demonstration is confirming what the learner already knew** and the session should be shortened to the defence.

**Prediction 3.** Learners who accept the challenger's easy offer will produce fully owned manifests, and will describe the occasion afterwards as having gone well. *Instrument:* the owner column against the post-occasion self-report, paired per learner.

**Prediction 4.** A majority of demonstration records will contain no recorded stop, **despite the scoring rule stating that a record with no stops is a failed observation.** *Instrument:* the stop field, coded present or absent, against the assessor's scoring. **This predicts the failure of the session's own countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 5.** Withdrawal specifications will be longer than manifests for a substantial share of the cohort. *Instrument:* a length comparison of the two artefacts, reported as a distribution and never as a threshold.

**Prediction 6.** Item 11 will discriminate more strongly between learners than any of the written items. *Instrument:* the pass rate per item across the cohort. **If item 11 does not discriminate, the live defence is redundant at this occasion** and the assessment should become a written submission, which would be cheaper and should then be done.

**None of these has been measured.** No cohort has run this occasion.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the intervention is a name; that transfer is demonstrated rather than declared; that the checkable properties of a handover are formal; that recording an unowned line is a better artefact than assigning it to somebody willing; and that a taper without an end is an unbudgeted commitment.

**What the evidence licenses.** The entrustment framework's provisions are verified against its open-access full text and are reported by their author as adoption rather than effectiveness. The efficacy mechanism is direction and mechanism from a source that has never met this population. The assessor-variability findings are single-institution medical-education studies. **The eleven specification items are this design's choices, defended by argument and unmeasured.**

**The sentence worth carrying out of the module.** An absence of objection is not evidence of agreement — because options excluded on efficacy grounds are excluded before they are weighed, and because silence pays the self now while voice pays the organisation later. Both literatures reach that conclusion from opposite directions, and it is the strongest thing this stage can say.

**The concession worth remembering.** You can pass this occasion and have designed a transfer that fails. The specification says so on its face, and a learner who leaves believing otherwise has been misled by the fact that something was marked.

**The silences that matter.** **No agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data and no threshold rationale** — so a recipient reading a `reason` is reading a model's stated rationale, not a validated one, and this paper asserts nothing about how well any evaluator's verdict corresponds to a competent human's. More broadly, **no vendor documentation known to this programme defines what it means for a customer to have accepted a delivered system, what a support taper should contain, what evidence of handover a delivery organisation should retain, or what a recipient must be able to demonstrate before a transfer may be claimed.** Commercial handover templates exist widely in professional services and are not vendor documentation; where a team is required to use one, this occasion's specification should be read as a set of properties that template must be checked against rather than as a competitor to it. If vendor documentation appears defining any of the four, this paper records its version and what it still leaves out.
