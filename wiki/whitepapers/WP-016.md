## In plain terms

The corpus behind this stage ships a large discovery question bank. It is experienced work and parts of it are very good: it forces a breadth-first pass before any deep enquiry, it asks about prior attempts, it asks what would be good enough, and several of its questions are properly behavioural — a recently shipped feature and where it stalled, how often tests get switched off under deadline, whether security has ever actually blocked a release.

**And it cannot produce an answer that would stop the engagement**, for four reasons that can be diagnosed one at a time.

It asks the customer, in every section, where the interviewer's own solution category would help — asked by the person who has come to deliver that solution. It ships example answers with nearly every question, and appended examples narrow the response before it is given. It contains no observation at all. And it never consolidates, shipping instead a table that converts one person's single remark straight into a prescribed intervention.

Then there are three things it never asks. **Nothing about data** — provenance, access, correctness, labelling, consent. **Nothing about what result would show it had not worked.** **Nothing about who loses if it succeeds**, in a practice whose commercial material carries an explicit headcount claim.

This session repairs the first two failures and adds the three missing questions. The other two failures belong to [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) and [S018](/wiki/seminars/S018.md).

**Then it does the thing that makes it this module's opening rather than merely its first session.** It hands the learner a tool to check their repaired questions for leadingness — and the tool is a model, and the documented failure modes of models used as graders include preferring longer answers and preferring outputs from their own family. **A learner builds an instrument that will approve of their work for reasons unrelated to their work.** That is the question bank's defect, arriving from the opposite direction, in an artefact the learner constructed themselves an hour earlier.

The movement's output is therefore a disagreement rate rather than a score. It is the only number in the session that means anything, and the learner has to produce it by hand-labelling, because **no agreement with human judgement is published for any evaluator on the platform.**

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching question design through the repair of a defective instrument rather than through principles. The argument is that the defects in a practitioner question bank are individually diagnosable against published interviewing method, that the diagnosis is more transferable than the principles because it is performed on an artefact of the kind the learner will actually be handed, and that the instrument's absences are more consequential than its errors. The session runs three passes — strip the specimens, classify and rewrite the evaluative questions, add the data, disconfirmation and who-loses questions — and treats the rewrite as the assessable work.

It then defends a second and reflexive movement: the learner builds a model-based check for leading questions and measures how much it disagrees with their own labels. The argument for that placement is that a judged evaluator's documented biases include preferring longer outputs and preferring its own family's outputs, so the learner constructs, from a different direction, exactly the defect they spent the session removing — and that **the mechanism being taught is not an analogy but a single claim: an instrument's authority rests on a measured agreement with something outside it.** The movement's required output is a disagreement rate rather than a pass rate, because the platform publishes no agreement with human judgement for any of its evaluators.

The paper concedes that all three interviewing sources behind the diagnosis are practitioner syntheses that report no measured effect; that the strongest available claim is structural and negative; and that the evaluation movement may cost an hour and improve no question set, in which case its defence rests entirely on what it teaches about instruments and should be argued on those terms or dropped.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A question bank is the most dangerous artefact in discovery, because it is the one that makes a novice feel prepared.

The specific danger is not that the questions are bad. It is that a bank is used in the room by somebody who did not write it, under time pressure, in front of a customer, and every property the bank has becomes a property of the conversation. If the bank leads, the conversation leads. If the bank asks the customer to endorse the solution, the customer endorses the solution — politely, because they are a professional and you are a guest.

**And the failure is invisible from inside**, because a leading question produces a fluent, confident, apparently useful answer. There is no moment at which the interviewer notices that nothing has been learned.

The session's response is to make the learner perform the diagnosis themselves, on an artefact, before they are ever in the room.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** A reader with ordinary professional experience has been in enough customer conversations. [Stage 1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md) adds [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s *who loses if this succeeds*, which returns here as one of the three additions and lands harder for a reader who has met it before.

[M03](/wiki/modules/M03.md) is a strong prerequisite in practice though not formally required. A learner who has facilitated a room understands why specimen answers exist — the question lands badly, the room goes quiet, and an example rescues it. **The specimens are a solution to a real problem, and a learner who has felt that problem writes a better repair.**

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate. The classification pass is mechanical and low load; the rewrite pass is where the difficulty is, because a rewrite must preserve what the original wanted to learn while removing how it asked. That is a genuine constraint-satisfaction problem and it does not decompose.

**3.2 Why the interviewer owns question quality.** Respondents are socially inclined to encourage, speculate and offer ideas. That is a predictable condition of the situation rather than a fault of the person, and it means the practitioner must ask questions for which politeness cannot manufacture a useful-looking answer. **This is the central move of the customer-conversation literature and it is a craft hypothesis, not a measured effect.**

**3.3 Why breadth precedes depth.** Once an interviewer assumes a problem category matters, respondents will usually help rank details inside it even when the category is irrelevant to their priorities. **The corpus rediscovered this independently and enforces it structurally rather than by advice**, which is the strongest single thing in its bank and is credited as such.

**3.4 Why the specimens are stripped first.** The figures embedded in example answers function as norms whether or not anybody intended it: an engineer who has read them hears a real customer's number as high or low against a scale nobody validated. **Stripping is placed first so that the classification pass is not performed while holding the anchors.** No figure from that material is reproduced anywhere in this repository, and the mechanism is teachable without them.

**3.5 Why this session's own instrument is the session's subject, and not an illustration of it.**

The module opens on questions that invite flattering answers and teaches them using a tool that is documented to be agreeable. That is deliberate, and treating it as incidental would waste the strongest teaching device available.

The judge-validation literature names four systematic biases in model-based scoring and supplies a mitigation for each: position, verbosity, self-enhancement, and limited reasoning ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/zheng-mt-bench-chatbot-arena.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/zheng-mt-bench-chatbot-arena.md)). Two of them apply directly to a rubric that scores questions. **Verbosity bias means a longer question scores better independent of quality. Self-enhancement bias means a judge from the model family that helped write the questions will prefer them.** A learner therefore builds an instrument that approves of their work for reasons unrelated to their work, which is exactly the defect the question bank has, arriving from the opposite direction.

The same source supplies the standard that makes judged evaluation defensible at all: for a subjective task there is no ground truth, so **judge quality is reported against the rate at which humans agree with each other**, never against an imagined perfect accuracy. **Its limits:** the agreement result is for strong judges on one task family, does not transfer automatically to weaker judges or specialist domains, and its bias taxonomy has aged better than its figures — none of which is quoted in this repository.

**3.6 Why the criterion is written before any score is seen.**

Criteria drift is a genuine circularity rather than a workflow inefficiency: users need criteria to grade outputs, and grading outputs is how users define criteria ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md)). Some criteria appear dependent on the specific outputs observed rather than definable in advance, which the authors say raises serious questions for approaches assuming the independence of evaluation from observation of model outputs.

Applied to this session it has a sharp consequence. **A learner who writes the leadingness criterion after seeing their own rewritten questions will write a criterion those questions pass.** The workflow therefore fixes the ordering — criterion and hand-labels first, scores second — and the design accepts the cost the paper does not resolve: once criteria are revised after seeing outputs, the same outputs cannot cleanly serve as a held-out set. **Its evidence is qualitative** and its user study is small by construction; the paper establishes that drift occurs and matters, not how often or how much.

**3.7 Why the deterministic check is written before the judged one.**

The evaluation ladder is the practitioner discipline the platform's API happens to instantiate. Decompose the system into features and scenarios, turn each scenario into an assertion, run cheap deterministic assertions constantly and expensive judged ones on a cadence ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md)). The framework's two evaluator tiers — local checks without API calls for inner-loop and continuous-integration use, cloud model-based judging for periodic use, mixable in one run — express that ladder directly, which means **the architecture can carry the lesson rather than the instructor having to assert it** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)).

The same source supplies the statistical warning that makes step 6 of the workflow worth the time: **raw agreement is misleading under class imbalance, so precision and recall must be reported separately.** A rewritten question set is imbalanced by construction, because the learner has already removed the obvious cases. **Its limits:** a practitioner essay resting on a single sustained case, with no control condition, no measured before-and-after and no released dataset; its tooling names are dated.

**3.8 The silence that bounds the whole platform movement.**

**No agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator in this catalogue. No calibration data. No account of where any default threshold came from.** Most of the evaluators are model-based judges, and the independent literature is unambiguous that judges carry measurable biases the vendor documentation does not discuss.

That absence is not a reason to avoid the tooling; it is the reason the session's platform movement ends in a hand-labelling step rather than in a score. **The documentation is an accurate description of what the tooling computes and no evidence at all that what it computes is right**, and the disagreement rate the learner measures themselves is the only warrant their number will ever have. This repository asserts no such agreement anywhere.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Three passes, in a fixed order, and the order carries the argument.** Strip removes the anchors. Classify makes the defect visible as a category rather than as a judgement about individual questions. Rewrite is the work.

**The three additions are ordered by increasing discomfort.** The data question is uncomfortable because it must be asked of somebody other than the sponsor. The disconfirmation question is uncomfortable because a customer who cannot answer it has told you something you would rather not know. The who-loses question is uncomfortable because you are being paid by somebody who may be the answer.

**The review movement is the instrument.** A partner playing a maximally polite customer answers the whole set; every question they can answer flatteringly is a question that has not been repaired. This is cheap, it is external, and it produces a count rather than an opinion.

**The platform movement exists to make the review movement's lesson survive the room.** The polite-customer review is a good instrument and it is unavailable in the field: nobody in a real engagement will answer your whole set twice while trying to flatter you. The evaluator is the durable version of the same check — it runs on every edit, it needs no partner, and it fails without anybody's cooperation.

**And it fails in a way the polite-customer review does not**, which is why it is placed second rather than first. A peer knows a flattering answer when they give one. An evaluator does not, and its reason string will be fluent either way. **A learner who has watched a judge approve a question they know is leading has learned something about judged evaluation that no amount of reading conveys** — and has learned it in the one session where the analogy to a customer's politeness is exact rather than laboured.

The ordering inside the workflow is doing work too. The deterministic check comes first because it is cheap and because writing it forces the feature-and-scenario decomposition. The dataset and the learner's own labels come before any score, because criteria drift is otherwise unopposed. The judged evaluator comes third, and the comparison against the learner's own labels comes last, because **the only defensible output of the movement is a disagreement rate rather than a pass rate.**

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The cosmetic rewrite.** The likeliest failure. The product name is removed and the shape survives, so the question still signals the hoped-for answer. *Countermeasure:* the polite-customer review, which is insensitive to naming and sensitive to shape.

**Specimens retained for the novice.** The learner keeps the examples with a plan for when to use them. *Countermeasure:* the strip pass requires writing down what each specimen would have made them expect, which converts the specimen from a support into an anchor in the learner's own account.

**The safe dangerous question.** A disconfirmation question no customer would ever answer in the disconfirming direction. *Countermeasure:* the standard's test — would you be comfortable asking it in front of your own sponsor — which is deliberately about the learner's discomfort rather than the question's wording.

**The compliance data block.** Data questions written as yes-or-no items for the wrong informant. *Countermeasure:* partial. The standard names the informant; nothing in this session verifies that the learner asked them. It is verified at [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) and assessed at [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md).

**The approving evaluator accepted.** A learner builds the judged evaluator, it passes their whole set, and they conclude the set is repaired. *Countermeasure:* the workflow's step 6 requires a comparison against the learner's own labels and forbids reporting raw agreement. **This is a weak countermeasure against a strong temptation** — a number with a reason attached reads as authoritative, and the learner has just spent an hour building the thing that produced it. §8's third objection concedes the risk and prediction 5 in §9 predicts the countermeasure failing.

**The rubric written to fit the rewrite.** A learner writes the leadingness criterion after looking at their own rewritten questions, and the criterion drifts until the questions pass. *Countermeasure:* the fixed ordering in the workflow — criterion and hand-labels before any score. **The ordering is unenforceable outside a classroom**, and the design says so rather than pretending a numbered list is a control.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: specimen answers are a legitimate training aid and removing them harms novices most.**

At its strongest: an inexperienced interviewer in a difficult room with a silent participant needs something. The specimens exist because somebody watched novices fail and gave them a handhold. A design that strips them, on a theoretical objection about anchoring, will produce interviewers who ask a clean question, get silence, and leave with nothing — which is worse than a slightly narrowed answer.

**Substantially conceded.** The specimens are a real solution to a real problem and the session says so. The rebuttal separates two uses: as preparation, read before the session and not carried in, they are defensible; as material available in the room, they are the defect. **The declined fix is to permit specimens in the room for learners below a stated experience level.** It is declined because the level cannot be checked, and because a handhold available under pressure will be used under pressure — which is the same argument [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) makes about prohibitions and is the honest reason rather than a principled one.

**Objection 2: the whole diagnosis rests on sources that establish nothing.**

At its strongest: three practitioner books, all of which state that they report no controlled comparison, no measured effect and no validated threshold, are being used to condemn a practitioner question bank that has exactly the same evidential status. The bank's authors could reply that their instrument reflects field experience, and the reply would be as good as the objection to it. **This paper is holding one unevidenced craft tradition against another and calling the second one wrong.**

**This is the strongest objection available and it is only partly answered.** The design's position is that the criticism is structural rather than empirical: a question whose every answer leaves the interviewer in the same position cannot change a decision, and that is a property of the question's form which requires no outcome data to establish. **The declined fix is to present both traditions as equally warranted and let the learner choose.** It is declined because the structural argument is not symmetric — the interviewing sources describe how their questions could fail, and the bank contains no question capable of failing. **A learner who is unconvinced by this rebuttal has understood the objection correctly.**

**Objection 3: the platform movement is a different session, wearing this one's title.**

At its strongest: question design and evaluation engineering are separate skills with separate literatures, and an hour spent on split strategies, thresholds and precision-and-recall is an hour not spent rewriting questions — which is the assessable work. The reflexivity is a rhetorical device rather than a pedagogical one: the fact that a judge is agreeable and a customer is agreeable is an analogy, not a mechanism, and analogies do not transfer skill. Worse, the movement introduces a tool whose correctness the paper itself says is unestablished, in a session whose whole point is not to trust an instrument that agrees with you. **The design is teaching learners to build the exact artefact it spends four sections warning them about.**

**Partly conceded, and the last sentence is conceded without qualification** — it is the design's intent rather than its oversight, and a reader who finds that too clever is entitled to. Three defences, none complete. The movement's output is a **disagreement rate, not a score**, which is the one artefact a learner cannot produce by trusting the tool. The polite-customer review is unavailable in the field and the evaluator is its durable form, so the movement supplies something the rest of the session cannot. And the analogy is not doing the work the objection says it is: the mechanism taught is that **any instrument's authority rests on a measured agreement with something outside it**, and the customer and the judge are two instances rather than a metaphor and its referent.

**The declined fix is to move the evaluation work to a later session in the measurement strand and leave this one purely on question design.** It is declined because the reflexive placement is the only thing that makes the lesson land on a learner who has just been told that instruments which agree with you are useless — and because the measurement stages would then teach judged evaluation to learners who had never built one they had reason to distrust. **The cost of that rejection is exactly what the objection names: some learners will leave with a worse question set and a working evaluator.** Prediction 6 in §9 is the only thing that would detect it.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** On opening item 1, most learners will write a question a polite respondent could answer flatteringly, and will not notice. *Instrument:* the written question and the written polite answer, coded together by a second reader before instruction.

**Prediction 2.** After one polite-customer review, the count of flatterable questions will fall; after a second, it will fall less. *Instrument:* the counts across rounds, per learner. **If the second round shows no change, one review is sufficient and the session's time should move to the additions.**

**Prediction 3.** The who-loses question will be the least often asked of anybody other than the sponsor. *Instrument:* the [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) packets, coded for the informant of each addition.

**Prediction 4.** Learners will strip specimens more readily than they will rewrite evaluative questions. *Instrument:* completeness of each pass across submitted sets. **If so, the classification pass is doing less work than the design assumes and should be shortened.**

**Prediction 5.** A majority of learners will report raw agreement between their own labels and the evaluator's, despite the workflow requiring precision and recall separately — and those who report it will over-rate their question set. *Instrument:* the submitted evaluation output, coded for which statistics were reported, cross-tabulated against the polite-customer review's flatterable count for the same set. **This predicts the failure of §5's approving-evaluator countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 6.** Learners who completed the platform movement will produce question sets no better than those who completed only the polite-customer review, as judged by flatterable count. *Instrument:* the flatterable count from a common final review, split within cohort on whether the evaluator was built. **This is the direct test of §8's third objection, and it is the prediction this paper would least like to lose.** A null result would mean the movement costs an hour and buys nothing for question quality — in which case its defence has to rest entirely on what it teaches about instruments, and should be argued on those terms or dropped.

**Prediction 7.** Most learners will find their evaluator performs worse on their own rewritten questions than on the originals. *Instrument:* per-item agreement, split by whether the item came from the strip pass or the rewrite pass. **If the reverse holds, the leadingness rubric is detecting vocabulary rather than form**, and the session's central claim that the defect survives paraphrase would be unsupported by its own instrument.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a discovery instrument's defects are diagnosable from its form, that its absences matter more than its errors, and that the repair is a skill rather than an opinion. And that an instrument's authority — a question bank's, a judge's, your own — rests on a measured agreement with something outside it.

**What the evidence licenses.** A vocabulary and a set of checks. **No effect.** All three interviewing sources decline to supply one, and this paper does not supply one on their behalf. The platform documentation is accurate about what its tooling computes and **is no evidence at all that what it computes is right**, because no agreement with human judgement is published for any of it.

**The concession worth remembering.** This is one craft tradition being held against another. The asymmetry that justifies it is thin — the interviewing sources say how they could be wrong and the bank does not — and a learner who finds that thin is reading well. The second concession is newer: **the evaluation movement may improve nothing about anybody's questions**, and prediction 6 is the only thing that would tell us.

**The silences that matter, and they bound the whole platform movement.** **No agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator in this catalogue. No calibration data. No account of where any default threshold came from.** That is the most serious gap in the source and it is load-bearing here, because most of the evaluators are model-based judges and the independent literature is unambiguous that judges carry biases the documentation does not discuss. This repository asserts no such agreement anywhere, and the workflow accordingly ends by requiring the learner to measure a disagreement themselves rather than to cite one. The documentation also **publishes no guidance on how large a labelled sample must be before a disagreement rate is trustworthy**, which is precisely the question the statistical literature answers and the tooling does not raise — so a learner's measured disagreement is itself an unqualified number, and this paper does not pretend otherwise. Further: **the rubric-evaluator surface used to carry a custom leadingness criterion is preview** and may change or disappear; **one consulted page is an explicitly superseded classic variant**; API surfaces differ across programming languages so examples are not interchangeable; and cloud evaluation requires a project with a model deployment and incurs cost, which is why the workflow's decisive steps run locally. Finally, **the catalogue is a set of things convenient to measure, and the documentation nowhere suggests that this is the same set as the things worth measuring** — a question whose defect nobody has built an evaluator for will find no support in it at all, and that absence is not evidence that the defect is small.
