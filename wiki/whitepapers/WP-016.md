## In plain terms

The corpus behind this stage ships a large discovery question bank. It is experienced work and parts of it are very good: it forces a breadth-first pass before any deep enquiry, it asks about prior attempts, it asks what would be good enough, and several of its questions are properly behavioural — a recently shipped feature and where it stalled, how often tests get switched off under deadline, whether security has ever actually blocked a release.

**And it cannot produce an answer that would stop the engagement**, for four reasons that can be diagnosed one at a time.

It asks the customer, in every section, where the interviewer's own solution category would help — asked by the person who has come to deliver that solution. It ships example answers with nearly every question, and appended examples narrow the response before it is given. It contains no observation at all. And it never consolidates, shipping instead a table that converts one person's single remark straight into a prescribed intervention.

Then there are three things it never asks. **Nothing about data** — provenance, access, correctness, labelling, consent. **Nothing about what result would show it had not worked.** **Nothing about who loses if it succeeds**, in a practice whose commercial material carries an explicit headcount claim.

This session repairs the first two failures and adds the three missing questions. The other two failures belong to [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) and [S018](/wiki/seminars/S018.md).

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching question design through the repair of a defective instrument rather than through principles. The argument is that the defects in a practitioner question bank are individually diagnosable against published interviewing method, that the diagnosis is more transferable than the principles because it is performed on an artefact of the kind the learner will actually be handed, and that the instrument's absences are more consequential than its errors. The session runs three passes — strip the specimens, classify and rewrite the evaluative questions, add the data, disconfirmation and who-loses questions — and treats the rewrite as the assessable work. The paper concedes that all three interviewing sources behind the diagnosis are practitioner syntheses that report no measured effect, and that the strongest available claim is structural and negative.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A question bank is the most dangerous artefact in discovery, because it is the one that makes a novice feel prepared.

The specific danger is not that the questions are bad. It is that a bank is used in the room by somebody who did not write it, under time pressure, in front of a customer, and every property the bank has becomes a property of the conversation. If the bank leads, the conversation leads. If the bank asks the customer to endorse the solution, the customer endorses the solution — politely, because they are a professional and you are a guest.

**And the failure is invisible from inside**, because a leading question produces a fluent, confident, apparently useful answer. There is no moment at which the interviewer notices that nothing has been learned.

The session's response is to make the learner perform the diagnosis themselves, on an artefact, before they are ever in the room.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** A reader with ordinary professional experience has been in enough customer conversations. [Stage 1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md) adds [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s *who loses if this succeeds*, which returns here as one of the three additions and lands harder for a reader who has met it before.

[M03](/wiki/modules/M03.md) is a strong prerequisite in practice though not formally required. A learner who has facilitated a room understands why specimen answers exist — the question lands badly, the room goes quiet, and an example rescues it. **The specimens are a solution to a real problem, and a learner who has felt that problem writes a better repair.**

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate. The classification pass is mechanical and low load; the rewrite pass is where the difficulty is, because a rewrite must preserve what the original wanted to learn while removing how it asked. That is a genuine constraint-satisfaction problem and it does not decompose.

**3.2 Why the interviewer owns question quality.** Respondents are socially inclined to encourage, speculate and offer ideas. That is a predictable condition of the situation rather than a fault of the person, and it means the practitioner must ask questions for which politeness cannot manufacture a useful-looking answer. **This is the central move of the customer-conversation literature and it is a craft hypothesis, not a measured effect.**

**3.3 Why breadth precedes depth.** Once an interviewer assumes a problem category matters, respondents will usually help rank details inside it even when the category is irrelevant to their priorities. **The corpus rediscovered this independently and enforces it structurally rather than by advice**, which is the strongest single thing in its bank and is credited as such.

**3.4 Why the specimens are stripped first.** The figures embedded in example answers function as norms whether or not anybody intended it: an engineer who has read them hears a real customer's number as high or low against a scale nobody validated. **Stripping is placed first so that the classification pass is not performed while holding the anchors.** No figure from that material is reproduced anywhere in this repository, and the mechanism is teachable without them.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Three passes, in a fixed order, and the order carries the argument.** Strip removes the anchors. Classify makes the defect visible as a category rather than as a judgement about individual questions. Rewrite is the work.

**The three additions are ordered by increasing discomfort.** The data question is uncomfortable because it must be asked of somebody other than the sponsor. The disconfirmation question is uncomfortable because a customer who cannot answer it has told you something you would rather not know. The who-loses question is uncomfortable because you are being paid by somebody who may be the answer.

**The review movement is the instrument.** A partner playing a maximally polite customer answers the whole set; every question they can answer flatteringly is a question that has not been repaired. This is cheap, it is external, and it produces a count rather than an opinion.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The cosmetic rewrite.** The likeliest failure. The product name is removed and the shape survives, so the question still signals the hoped-for answer. *Countermeasure:* the polite-customer review, which is insensitive to naming and sensitive to shape.

**Specimens retained for the novice.** The learner keeps the examples with a plan for when to use them. *Countermeasure:* the strip pass requires writing down what each specimen would have made them expect, which converts the specimen from a support into an anchor in the learner's own account.

**The safe dangerous question.** A disconfirmation question no customer would ever answer in the disconfirming direction. *Countermeasure:* the standard's test — would you be comfortable asking it in front of your own sponsor — which is deliberately about the learner's discomfort rather than the question's wording.

**The compliance data block.** Data questions written as yes-or-no items for the wrong informant. *Countermeasure:* partial. The standard names the informant; nothing in this session verifies that the learner asked them. It is verified at [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) and assessed at [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md).

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: specimen answers are a legitimate training aid and removing them harms novices most.**

At its strongest: an inexperienced interviewer in a difficult room with a silent participant needs something. The specimens exist because somebody watched novices fail and gave them a handhold. A design that strips them, on a theoretical objection about anchoring, will produce interviewers who ask a clean question, get silence, and leave with nothing — which is worse than a slightly narrowed answer.

**Substantially conceded.** The specimens are a real solution to a real problem and the session says so. The rebuttal separates two uses: as preparation, read before the session and not carried in, they are defensible; as material available in the room, they are the defect. **The declined fix is to permit specimens in the room for learners below a stated experience level.** It is declined because the level cannot be checked, and because a handhold available under pressure will be used under pressure — which is the same argument [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) makes about prohibitions and is the honest reason rather than a principled one.

**Objection 2: the whole diagnosis rests on sources that establish nothing.**

At its strongest: three practitioner books, all of which state that they report no controlled comparison, no measured effect and no validated threshold, are being used to condemn a practitioner question bank that has exactly the same evidential status. The bank's authors could reply that their instrument reflects field experience, and the reply would be as good as the objection to it. **This paper is holding one unevidenced craft tradition against another and calling the second one wrong.**

**This is the strongest objection available and it is only partly answered.** The design's position is that the criticism is structural rather than empirical: a question whose every answer leaves the interviewer in the same position cannot change a decision, and that is a property of the question's form which requires no outcome data to establish. **The declined fix is to present both traditions as equally warranted and let the learner choose.** It is declined because the structural argument is not symmetric — the interviewing sources describe how their questions could fail, and the bank contains no question capable of failing. **A learner who is unconvinced by this rebuttal has understood the objection correctly.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** On opening item 1, most learners will write a question a polite respondent could answer flatteringly, and will not notice. *Instrument:* the written question and the written polite answer, coded together by a second reader before instruction.

**Prediction 2.** After one polite-customer review, the count of flatterable questions will fall; after a second, it will fall less. *Instrument:* the counts across rounds, per learner. **If the second round shows no change, one review is sufficient and the session's time should move to the additions.**

**Prediction 3.** The who-loses question will be the least often asked of anybody other than the sponsor. *Instrument:* the [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) packets, coded for the informant of each addition.

**Prediction 4.** Learners will strip specimens more readily than they will rewrite evaluative questions. *Instrument:* completeness of each pass across submitted sets. **If so, the classification pass is doing less work than the design assumes and should be shortened.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a discovery instrument's defects are diagnosable from its form, that its absences matter more than its errors, and that the repair is a skill rather than an opinion.

**What the evidence licenses.** A vocabulary and a set of checks. **No effect.** All three interviewing sources decline to supply one, and this paper does not supply one on their behalf.

**The concession worth remembering.** This is one craft tradition being held against another. The asymmetry that justifies it is thin — the interviewing sources say how they could be wrong and the bank does not — and a learner who finds that thin is reading well.
