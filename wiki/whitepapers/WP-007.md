# WP-007 — Who Is Not in the Room

**Seminar day:** [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md) · **Module:** [M02](/wiki/modules/M02.md) · **Quarter:** [Q1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 2
**Platform anchor:** Azure AI Search security trimming, and Microsoft Agent 365's tenant-wide tool consent and published agent-risk role matrix — the absent people whose rights and approvals decide what the system may return and who may investigate it

> ## Release rule
>
> **§§4, 5 and 9 are post-session reading.** §4 explains why the objection is read aloud by somebody other than its author and records an amendment to the artefact, §5 names the failure the session is designed to produce, and §9 gives the predicted cohort behaviour. §§1, 2, 3 and 8 spoil nothing.
>
> **The reason is structural rather than dramatic.** The five opening items are a generation phase. A learner who has read §5 will write a map that avoids the failure the session needs them to make, and will not have discovered that they were going to make it.

> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Affected non-buyer** | Somebody whose work changes if the engagement succeeds and who holds no purchasing authority |
> | **Late blocker** | A party able to stop delivery after it is built — security, legal, a data owner, an external assessor |
> | **Commitment** | Evidence of seriousness in which the other party gives up something they value: time, reputation or money |
> | **Security trimming** | Removing unauthorised documents from a result set rather than blocking the query |
> | **Trust boundary** | The point at which an access decision stops being your application's responsibility and becomes the service's, or the reverse |
> | **Revocation window** | The interval between a permission being withdrawn at its source and that withdrawal taking effect at retrieval |
> | **Index projection** | The mechanism that carries document-level fields, including permissions, onto chunk rows |
> | **Voice behaviour** | Speaking up with a concern when silence is the lower-risk option |

## In plain terms

The people you meet were chosen by the person who invited you, and that person's map of who matters is the map you will inherit unless you build another one.

The absences that matter fall into three kinds and the session treats them differently. There are people who can stop the work later — a security function, a data owner, an external assessor — and consulting them late is expensive but recoverable. There are people who will be worse off if the work succeeds, who usually hold no budget, are usually not introduced, and whose objection is the one an engagement is least equipped to hear.

**And there are people who will never attend anything, whose interest is already encoded as an access control list.** They are stakeholders in the strictest sense: what the system may say is decided by their rights. A retrieval system either honours that encoding or silently replaces it with a convention of your own, and nothing in the system's output distinguishes the two cases.

**The single most useful question in this session is asked in almost no discovery instrument in professional use: who loses if this works?** Its absence from the corpus behind this programme is conspicuous, because the same body of material makes a commercial claim about headcount elsewhere — so the question is not unthinkable there, merely unasked in the room.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching stakeholder absence as a qualification concern rather than a delivery one, and defends extending it to a third class of absent party whose stake is an access right rather than an opinion.

The argument runs in four parts. **First**, the composition of an engagement's evidence is set by whoever made the introductions, and the systematic omissions are precisely the parties whose evidence would change a qualification decision. **Second**, two distinct classes of absence — the late blocker and the affected non-buyer — have different consequences and different remedies, and the countermeasure common to both is an ask that could be refused, because an ask with only one possible answer is an announcement. **Third**, a third class exists that no conversation reaches: people whose rights are held in an identity system and whose exclusion from a retrieval result is either enforced by the search service against a directory token or improvised by application code, with nothing in any response distinguishing the two. **Fourth**, the two ways this class is most often lost are silent — permission metadata not projected onto chunks, and a revocation that has not yet synchronised — and both produce a system that appears to work, which is what makes them a qualification concern rather than a testing concern.

The paper records an amendment made while writing it: the absence map as first designed had no enforcement row, so a party whose entire stake was an access right had no place on the page and would have been dropped as not a stakeholder. The correction is documented at §4.5 rather than applied silently.

The paper concedes that the session rests on an absence in its primary source rather than on a finding; that the strongest-objection device has no reliable defence against a learner writing a rebuttal in quotation marks; that the session diagnoses an ethical problem it then handles as an evidential one; and that its two change-management sources establish mechanism while citing, between them, almost nothing.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Discovery evidence is sampled, and the sampling frame is the sponsor's address book. That would be a minor problem if the omissions were random. They are not: the systematically absent parties are those whose interests diverge from the sponsor's, which is precisely the population whose evidence would change a qualification decision.

The consequence at qualification is specific. An engagement can look well-supported — enthusiastic sponsor, willing team, clear brief — while the person who will refuse to use the thing, the function that will block its release, and the party whose data it depends on have all never been asked. **Each of those is a disqualifying condition that is invisible from inside the room, and none of them becomes cheaper to discover later.**

There is a second problem, and it is the one this revision of the session adds. Some absent parties cannot be asked at all, because their stake is not an opinion. It is an entitlement, recorded in a directory or a permission list, which decides what a system is allowed to tell somebody. A brief that proposes grounding on an existing document estate has made a commitment to those entitlements without naming them, and the commitment has a runtime shape: a mechanism, a trust boundary, and a window during which a revocation has not yet taken effect.

**The reason this belongs in qualification rather than in build is that the answer sometimes changes the engagement.** If the authoritative permission system cannot be reused, if the integration requires a privilege the security function will not grant, or if the revocation window is unacceptable to the party whose rights are at stake, the work as scoped does not proceed. That is a Stage 1 finding arriving in Stage 1.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Either**, plus the room reading from [S004](/wiki/seminars/S004.md) and the decision from [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md). The register from [S006](/wiki/seminars/S006.md) is not required and is useful, because the estate whose permissions this session enforces is the estate the previous session assessed.

Learners with research or service-design backgrounds arrive holding the distinction between users, buyers and affected parties. Learners from delivery backgrounds usually hold a version restricted to people who can approve things. Both populations produce a map; only one of them produces an affected non-buyer without prompting, and the difference is visible on the page.

The platform half inverts that advantage. Learners who have built retrieval systems will recognise the mechanisms and will tend to treat the exercise as configuration; learners who have not will treat the search service as a black box that either does or does not have security. **Neither group reliably arrives holding the idea that the boundary between service-enforced and application-enforced access is invisible in the response**, and that idea is the session's platform content.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is moderate. The mapping work is low-load and affectively difficult — the session asks a learner to construct the strongest available argument against work they have been preparing to do. The enforcement work is the reverse: emotionally neutral and conceptually dense. The design puts them in that order deliberately, so that the mechanism arrives as an answer to a stakeholder question rather than as a security topic with a stakeholder framing bolted on.

**3.2 Why segmentation precedes aggregation.** Evidence merged across parties with incompatible goals, workflows and buying processes is not one signal but several, and mixed feedback in that condition indicates unlike populations rather than a divided market. The direction is well established in the interviewing literature; **no magnitude is asserted here or anywhere in this wiki.**

**3.3 Why the ask must be refusable.** A request that cannot be declined produces no information. What makes an ask evidential is that the other party gives up something — time, reputation, access — and can decline to. **Direction and mechanism only; commitment is evidence of seriousness and not proof that anything will work.**

There is a convergent mechanism from the change literature, and it explains why the announcement fails rather than merely that it does. Hiatt's position is that sharing information does not by itself produce awareness, because awareness is a property of reception rather than of transmission, and that reception is governed in part by whether the stated reasons are contestable — an internally-motivated change invites legitimate dispute, and a reasonable person may debate its grounds ([research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md)). **An unrefusable ask presents a contestable reason and offers no channel for the contest**, which is the shape of the failure rather than a description of bad manners.

**That source's warrant must travel with it.** The model is a registered commercial asset of the firm that publishes the book and sells the training and certification; every quantitative claim in it traces to that firm's own practitioner surveys, with no methodology section, sampling frame or response rate reproduced; and no independent evaluation of the model appears anywhere in it. Its own author states that its lifecycle begins after a change has been identified, so it is silent on whether the change is right — which is this stage's entire question.

**3.4 Silence is a voice behaviour, and its payoffs are asymmetric.**

A person who attended every meeting and said nothing is also not in the room, and the session's fifth exercise exists because learners map absences by attendance.

Edmondson's account is that withholding is a quick risk calculation made largely below conscious attention, and that its structure is asymmetric: voice benefits the organisation later and with low certainty, while silence benefits the individual immediately and with high certainty ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)). Her observation that silence "rarely announces itself" — the moment passes and nobody is the wiser except the person who held back — is the reason a stakeholder map built from attendance and contribution is not a map of who had something to say.

**The source read here is a trade book and its limits are extensive**, and this paper does not lean on it for anything load-bearing. Its literature chapter is a narrative review with no search protocol, no inclusion criteria and no reported disconfirming finding; its case studies are secondary and were selected after the outcomes were known; and no trial of its leadership framework is reported anywhere in it. The construct's quantitative synthesis is a separate source and is used at [WP-010](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-010.md), where more weight rests on it.

**3.5 Why access rights are a stakeholder question at qualification.**

The design claim is that an entitlement is a stake, and that a system which reads a document estate has taken a position on every entitlement in it.

The mechanism is documented precisely. Azure AI Search's overview of document-level access control states that it is essential for grounding data in agentic systems and retrieval applications requiring authorisation at document level, and offers four approaches with an explicit selection rule ([research/06-microsoft-platform/azure-ai-search-and-acl-patterns.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/azure-ai-search-and-acl-patterns.md)). The rule is the transferable content: **reuse the authoritative permission system rather than reimplementing it.**

Two structural facts from that source do the session's work. First, the two-stage check — the service verifies that the client application may read the index, and then, given the end user's token carried on a separate request header, verifies per-document user and group permissions and excludes what does not match. Application authorisation and user authorisation are separate concerns evaluated separately, and a design that collapses them has removed a check without removing the appearance of one. Second, the documentation's own word for the string-comparison alternative: contrasting it with permission metadata recognised as directory authentication, the page calls security trimming by string comparison a *workaround*. **The trust boundary in that pattern is your application, and the search service will not tell you so.**

**3.6 Why the two silent failures are taught rather than the mechanism.**

Both of the session's platform failures share a property: the system continues to appear correct.

When a skillset chunks documents, permission metadata moves from indexer field mappings to index projections, and without that projection chunk-level references are not filtered. The parent document remains correctly protected. A permissions audit on the source passes. A retrieval test at document granularity passes. **The failure is visible only at the granularity the system actually returns**, and chunking is standard practice.

And enforcement is against indexed metadata rather than live source permissions, so a revocation takes effect only after synchronisation — with the preview terms stating that a timing lag occurs before permission changes are recognised. There is a further asymmetry: changes on items with unique permissions refresh on each successful indexer run, while changes inherited from a parent scope require an explicit resynchronisation. **The inherited case is the more common one and the one that does not self-heal.**

The retrieval-engineering literature this programme uses does not centre access control at all. The seven-failure-point experience report is drawn from three deployed systems in unrelated domains and its taxonomy is about content, retrieval and generation ([research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md)). **That omission is evidence about the field's attention rather than about the failure's rarity**, and it is stated here as such: that report offers no frequency or severity data for anything, and its seven failure names are recorded in this repository as reconstructed rather than quoted.

**3.7 Why the map has a dropped column.**

The chained threat-modelling pipeline recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md) — internal material, paraphrased and never quoted — requires every threat to be bound to at least one named component and at least one numbered dataflow, discarding anything that cannot be, with a logged reason.

The discipline is good and its consequence is structural: it removes everything concerning the operator, the supply chain, human process and out-of-band administration, because those are not components. Each discard carries a plausible justification that nobody reads, and the evaluator downstream declines to deduct for what is missing.

**The general form is the one the session teaches: a filter that keeps an analysis concrete removes exactly the participants who are not represented in the architecture.** A stakeholder map drawn from a system diagram has the same filter. The dropped column is the countermeasure and it is a weak one — it makes the discards visible without making anybody read them.

That artefact reports no observed result of any kind and no outcome evidence whatever. It is a specimen showing the pattern is buildable by competent people, and nothing about frequency.

**3.8 The counterweight, which is about this paper's own sources.**

Two of the sources in §3.3 and §3.6 are practitioner literature with weak evidential standing, and one of them is worse than that. Kotter's account of blockers is used in the session and his own preface states that the book contains no footnotes or endnotes, that he drew no examples or major ideas from any published source except his own writing, and that he did not attempt to cite evidence ([research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md](/research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md)). Every case in it is anonymised and uncheckable; the 2012 reissue corrects nothing.

**This is stated here rather than in a footnote because the session's substance is a claim about who matters, and a claim about who matters is exactly the kind that practitioner literature asserts confidently and never tests.** The session uses these sources for the shape of a failure and for nothing else.

**3.9 Two absences that are roles rather than people, and both are legible from a published table.**

§3.5 argues that an entitlement is a stake. There is a stronger version of the same move, and it needs no inference at all because the vendor has printed it.

The first is the **consent-holder**. Granting agents access to a tool is documented as a tenant-wide consent, grantable by exactly two roles — AI Administrator and Global Administrator ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-365-and-agent-identity.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-365-and-agent-identity.md)); and the tool exposing Microsoft 365 work data to an agent additionally requires a global administrator to consent to a named application permission, with a licence requirement attached to every calling user ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-iq-intelligence-layer.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-iq-intelligence-layer.md)). **This is a late blocker with a property the session's existing taxonomy did not anticipate: the object of their decision is the tenant, not the design, so no conversation about the design can bring them forward.** The only available move is to name them in writing before design proceeds, which is why the second workflow pass asks for that and nothing more.

The second is sharper and is the more valuable teaching material. The same documentation publishes a matrix of which administrative roles may investigate an agent risk in which product, and the matrix states that **the role that governs agents — approving them, assigning owners, granting tenant-wide tool consent — cannot open the threat-detection or data-governance evidence behind the risks it is being asked to act on**, that security-oriented roles can open the first and not the second, and that **no role in the table can investigate the data-governance risks at all**, those requiring separate insider-risk roles granted inside the governance product by somebody the table never names.

**The design's claim is that this is an absence problem rather than a security one, and the claim is testable rather than rhetorical.** A triage process built from that matrix requires an actor who is not in it. That is precisely the failure §3.7 describes for threat models — an analysis whose filter removes the participants it depends on — except that here the omission is in the vendor's own operating model rather than in the learner's artefact, and it can be proved on paper in one movement. The session's `absent-roles.yaml` and its seventh exercise exist to make the proof the deliverable, because a learner who has written "escalate to security" has moved the gap rather than found it.

**The source's limits are severe and are stated in the session.** It was read on 2026-07-31, shortly after general availability, with large parts of the described surface in preview or behind a separate early-access programme; twelve pages were fetched and read in full and roughly thirty more read as search-returned excerpts. **Its authority is definitional: it states exactly what each role may do and publishes no detection rate, no false-positive rate and no measure of whether the triage it describes is ever completed.** None is asserted here.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The movement sequence.** Situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits. The conversational classes come before the enforcement class, because a learner who meets access control first will file the whole session under security and produce a map with no people on it.

**4.2 Three classes, three remedies.** A late blocker is brought forward: the ask is a meeting with a defined purpose, and the failure mode is scheduling it after the design. An affected non-buyer is harder — they may have no legitimate currency to give up, no authority to commit and no reason to trust the person asking — and the session's only honest instruction is that their objection be recorded in their terms and not converted into a risk item. **The third class is not asked anything at all**; it is represented, and the artefact's enforcement row is the representation.

**4.3 The read-aloud device carries the conversational half.** An author reading their own objection supplies the intonation that makes it answerable. A stranger reading it flat exposes whether it is an argument or a straw figure, and does so without anybody having to accuse the author of anything.

The supplied brief carries three purpose-built absences: an external assessor who has never spoken to the technology function, field engineers whose notes are the input and who have not been asked why the notes are as they are, and a back-office team whose size has been discussed in meetings they did not attend. All three are invented for this session, and they are chosen so that one is a late blocker, one is a source of evidence and one is an affected non-buyer.

**4.4 What the platform workflow is for.** It is not a security tutorial. It exists to make three things physically observable in a way prose cannot: that an absent person's rights are a runtime property with a latency; that the boundary between service-enforced and application-enforced access is invisible in every response; and that the commonest way to lose somebody's rights is not to violate them but to chunk the document they were attached to.

Steps 1, 2, 6 and 7 are executable against the published documentation alone, because a session whose lesson requires a provisioned search service is a session that teaches provisioning. **Step 6 in its documentation-only form — writing down that no revocation latency is published and that the window is therefore unknown — is the entry this session most wants a learner to have made**, and it is available to everybody.

**4.5 A defect found in this session's own artefact, and the amendment.**

The absence map as first designed had three parts: the affected-but-absent, what each would say, and one ask.

Under that design there was **nowhere for the third class to appear.** A party whose entire stake is an access right says nothing, cannot be asked, and would have failed the artefact's own test of what belongs on it — so a well-executed map, produced by a diligent learner, would have contained every person the sponsor could introduce and none of the people the system was about to answer on behalf of. The map would have reproduced, at a smaller scale, exactly the sampling failure §1 describes.

The amendment adds the enforcement row and the dropped list, and adds workflow steps 3 to 7. **The amendment is recorded rather than silently applied**, because the defect is the session's own subject appearing inside the session's own instrument: a filter that seemed to be about relevance was in fact about who could be introduced. Assume there are others this paper has not caught.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Charitable ventriloquism.** The most likely outcome: every absent objection is written in a form the learner is already equipped to defeat. *Countermeasure:* the read-aloud device, which is unreliable and is the best available.

**The map that becomes a risk register.** A learner converts affected parties into risks with mitigations, which reads as thorough and removes every one of them from the qualification decision. *Countermeasure:* the artefact has no mitigation column and the ask is required to be refusable, which slows the conversion without preventing it.

**Instrumental consultation.** A learner treats the ask as a technique for obtaining agreement rather than evidence. *Countermeasure:* none available here. The distinction between asking somebody and manoeuvring them is not enforceable by a specification, and the session states it rather than checking it. **This is the largest unhandled failure in the session.**

**The convention recorded as a boundary.** A learner writes an enforcement row naming a mechanism that is in fact their own application filtering results, and records it as though the service were enforcing it. *Countermeasure:* the row requires a trust boundary to be named explicitly, and exercise 2 requires the consequence of that party being wrong to be written down. Prediction 5 in §9 predicts this countermeasure failing.

**Verification at the wrong granularity.** A learner confirms that protection works by retrieving documents, in a system that returns chunks. *Countermeasure:* workflow step 5 makes the failure happen rather than describing it, which is the strongest countermeasure in the session and the one most likely to be cut for time.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md) and [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md). The affected non-buyers identified here are among the parties whose continuing cost is priced at [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md), and the exclusion of an affected party is one of the conditions that fires against the second supplied brief at [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md).

Backwards, this session completes an argument begun at [S006](/wiki/seminars/S006.md). That session establishes that a permissions estate is a constraint discoverable by an instrument; this one establishes how the constraint is enforced at retrieval and that the enforcement lags. **The pair is one argument, and the split is deliberate**: the constraint must be experienced as a discovery before it is met as a mechanism, because a learner who meets the mechanism first will believe the problem is solved by configuration.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence and no specification element of its own. The absence map is an input to [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md), where a refusal that rests on an excluded party must show that the exclusion was established rather than assumed.

**The enforcement row is the part most likely to be assessed later and is deliberately not assessed here.** A learner who records a revocation window of "unknown, no published figure" has answered correctly, and a learner who invents a figure has produced the failure [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md) is built against. Marking the row would create an incentive to fill it.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: this is an ethical problem and the session handles it as an evidential one.**

At its strongest: the reason to ask the people who will be worse off is not that their answers improve your qualification decision. It is that doing things to people without asking them is wrong, and a session which justifies consultation by its evidential yield has taught learners to consult when it is useful and to stop when it is not. That is a worse position than not raising the question.

**Conceded, and the concession is not repaired.** The design's reason for the evidential framing is that this stage's instrument is a decision and the paper can only defend what the session assesses. **The declined fix was to place the obligation here as a principle without an artefact**, rejected because an unassessed principle in a programme full of assessed artefacts is decoration. Neither the fix nor its rejection is satisfactory, and the honest statement is that Stage 1 handles the evidential half and leaves the other half to Stage 6.

The platform half sharpens the objection rather than answering it. Representing somebody's access rights correctly is a technical achievement that can be delivered by a team which never spoke to them, and this session teaches both halves without claiming that the first substitutes for the second.

**Objection 2: the session rests on an absence in its source, which is a weak basis for a practice.**

At its strongest: that a question bank does not ask who loses is not evidence that asking helps. Inferring a practice from a gap in a document is the same move this programme criticises elsewhere, and the criticism applies to itself.

**Conceded without qualification.** The whole of Stage 1 is built this way and every session in it says so. The narrower defence is that the absence here is unusually diagnosable: the same corpus makes a headcount claim in its commercial material, so the question is answerable within its own frame and is simply not asked in the room. That is a description of an instrument, not a finding about outcomes.

**Objection 3: an access control list is not a stakeholder, and calling it one is a rhetorical move that will produce sloppy thinking.**

At its strongest: a permission entry is a technical artefact. Treating it as a person's stake conflates two things that a competent engineer keeps separate, and it invites the reverse error — believing that because permissions are correctly enforced, the people behind them have been considered. A team that has projected its ACLs onto its chunks has done a good piece of engineering and has still not asked anybody anything.

**Partially conceded, and the reverse error named in the objection is real and unhandled.** The rebuttal is narrower than the objection: the claim is not that an ACL is a person, but that at qualification the question "may this system read that content" has an answer that already exists, is held by somebody, and can disqualify the work — and that no other session in the stage would ask it. **The declined fix was to move the enforcement material into the build stages**, rejected because a permission model discovered after acceptance is a change request rather than a qualification input. The cost is that the session risks teaching learners to feel they have consulted people they have configured, and this paper records that as an accepted cost with no countermeasure.

**Objection 4: the platform material is preview and will be wrong before the cohort finishes.**

At its strongest: most of the source page describes a preview API, feature availability differs between API versions and SDK packages, and the four approaches are compared on fit rather than on security strength — so a learner may leave holding a mechanism that no longer exists and a comparison the documentation never made.

**Conceded, and it is why the perishability table is unusually long.** The durable claims are stated without product names: an index is a copy; application and user authorisation are separate checks; chunking discards permissions unless they are projected; enforcement lags the source. **The narrower point is that a preview surface teaches something a stable one cannot** — that a control's guarantees have a version number, and that reading the terms is part of the design.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most first maps will contain nobody who bears a cost and holds no purchasing authority. *Instrument:* the map's entries, coded *buyer* / *user* / *affected non-buyer* / *blocker* / *entitlement-only* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Objections judged straw by a reader who did not write them will be revised more often than those judged genuine, and the effect will be greater when read aloud than when read silently. *Instrument:* the read-aloud judgement against the presence of a revision, with a cohort split by reading mode.

**Prediction 3.** Learners who write a refusable ask will more often record a changed belief than learners whose ask admits only agreement. *Instrument:* the ask, coded *refusable* / *announcement*, against whether the artefact records a belief change after the staged decline. **If this fails, the refusability requirement is a stylistic preference** and should be dropped rather than defended.

**Prediction 4.** Answers to the second half of opening item 4 — who is currently forbidden from reading the data — will be absent or generic in most cases, while answers to its first half will be specific. *Instrument:* the written answers, coded per half as *specific* / *generic* / *absent* by a second reader. **This is the prediction that the generation phase is aimed at the right item.**

**Prediction 5.** A majority of enforcement rows will name a mechanism without naming a trust boundary, or will name the service as the boundary in designs where the application supplies the filter string. *Instrument:* the enforcement rows, coded for whether the stated boundary matches the described mechanism. **This predicts the failure of the countermeasure introduced by the §4.5 amendment**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 6.** Learners who complete workflow step 5 — chunking without projection — will name granularity as a verification concern in later sessions more often than those who read about it. *Instrument:* mentions of retrieval granularity in the [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) artefact, split by step-5 completion.

**Prediction 7.** Learners instructed to compare their item-4 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 3) will produce enforcement rows judged complete more often than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* a reviewer's four-part completeness judgement, split within cohort on exercise-3 completion. **A null result would mean the comparison prompt is ceremony**, and the same prediction is made at [WP-006](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-006.md) so a null in both would indict the pattern rather than the session.

**Prediction 8.** Where the customer's estate contains anything built outside the delivery team, a majority of maps will omit that population entirely on first attempt, and of the maps that include it, most will classify it as a user rather than as an affected non-buyer. *Instrument:* the map's entries, coded for presence of a builder-population row and for its class, by a second reader, against a binary record of whether such an estate exists in the brief. **If most learners include the population unaided, §3's addition is redundant and should be cut to a single line in the produce list rather than kept as a subsection.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That an engagement's evidence is sampled by the sponsor; that the systematic omissions are the ones that would change a decision; that an ask which cannot be refused yields nothing; and that some stakeholders are represented by an entitlement rather than by an opinion, and are lost by a chunking step rather than by a decision.

**What the evidence licenses.** The segmentation and commitment mechanisms are direction-and-mechanism only, and commitment is evidence of seriousness rather than of success. The change-management sources establish the shape of a failure and, in one case, cite nothing at all. The platform mechanisms are first-party documentation describing mostly preview functionality, compared on fit rather than on security strength, with **no synchronisation-latency figure published anywhere** — so the revocation window is genuinely unknown and no figure appears here. That consultation improves engagements has no evidence behind it at all.

**The concession worth remembering.** This session gives an evidential reason for something that also has a moral one, and it does that because the moral reason is not assessable here. Notice when the two come apart — and notice the sharper version the platform half introduces: **you can enforce somebody's rights perfectly and never have asked them anything.**

**The silences in the role material, which are of a different kind and are worse.** The role matrix is unusually candid — it prints the gap rather than concealing it — and then publishes nothing whatever about consequence. **No detection rate and no false-positive rate is published for any of the products the matrix routes an investigator into**, consistent with this repository's standing prohibition, which the source confirms rather than weakens. **No measure exists of how often the triage sequence is completed**, of how long the handover between the two required roles takes, or of whether a risk that nobody in the table can investigate is investigated by anyone. **No measure of registry coverage exists**, so the population of agents that could appear on the at-risk list in the first place is itself unbounded. And the source is silent on the obvious governance question its own table raises: whether the separation of approval authority from investigative authority is deliberate design or an artefact of assembling a control plane from products that already had role models. **The session teaches the gap and declines to explain it**, because the documentation does not.
