## In plain terms

This is where the module's four artefacts meet a room that will not cooperate.

The assessment is unusual in what it refuses to look at. It does not care whether the customer committed. It does not care whether the room enjoyed it, whether the candidate recovered from the difficulty, or whether the finding was correct. **Every one of those is either the customer's behaviour or luck, and assessing a candidate on either is assessing the wrong person.**

What it checks is whether the artefacts held: that the charter's failure condition could fire and was observed against, that the gate's inward condition was actually checked, that every waiver names somebody, that the prohibitions survived pressure or the breach was self-reported, that a commitment was distinguished from a warm sentence by what somebody gave up, and that an absence was recorded rather than smoothed.

**The most important of those is the last.** A closing record saying nobody committed to anything is a stronger submission than one describing a session that built strong foundations, and candidates find that genuinely difficult to believe until they have written both.

## Abstract

This paper defends an assessment that examines a practitioner's artefacts and refuses to examine the engagement's outcome. The argument is that outcome-bearing assessment in a delivery context evaluates the customer's disposition and the assessor's luck, and that the properties worth certifying — a fireable condition, a named waiver, an honoured prohibition, a commitment identified by its cost, a recorded absence — are all checkable and all owned by the candidate. The assessment introduces an unannounced difficulty drawn from [S014](/wiki/seminars/S014.md) and requires the closing record before any debrief. The paper concedes that the scheme cannot detect a candidate who produced excellent artefacts and learned nothing about the customer, and that its central claim — that a record written before the debrief differs from one written after — is design reasoning nobody has measured.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

An assessment of facilitated work will, unless prevented, measure charm.

The mechanism is simple. The observable output of a session is the room's response, the room responds to the person, and an assessor watching a session forms a judgement about the person before forming one about the work. That judgement is not worthless — it tracks something real — but it is not the thing the module taught, and it correlates with prior confidence rather than with the module.

The second problem is that the useful evidence is produced after the room empties, when the candidate is tired, relieved, and about to be told by colleagues what the session was like. **The closing record is the artefact most vulnerable to that conversation** and is therefore required before it.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** All four preceding sessions are load-bearing and the assessment reads their outputs directly.

The prior it works against is that an assessment must have a right answer visible in the outcome. Candidates arrive expecting to be marked on whether it went well. **The published specification is the countermeasure**, and it is published in advance for exactly that reason.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** High, and mostly extraneous by design: time pressure, an audience, an unannounced difficulty and four artefacts to keep in view. That is the condition the module claims prohibitions are for, and an assessment run under comfortable conditions would not test the claim.

**3.2 Why the difficulty is unannounced.** A rehearsed difficulty tests preparation. An unannounced one tests whether the prohibition survives surprise, which is the property [S014](/wiki/seminars/S014.md) actually asserts.

**3.3 Why the closing record precedes the debrief.** A group that has discussed a session has agreed a story about it, and the record written afterwards is a record of the story. **This is design reasoning; nobody has measured it here.**

**3.4 Why an absence scores.** The corpus reports no failed workshop and no declined engagement, and its case studies all succeed. A programme that only rewards positive findings reproduces that shape in its graduates. **Rewarding a recorded absence is the cheapest available counterweight and it is an argument rather than a finding.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The assessment runs the module's own instrument on the module's own artefacts.

**The four artefacts are submitted as amended rather than as originally written**, with amendments visible. This is the first appearance of the discipline that later becomes the decision record: a settled artefact is changed by recording the change, not by editing away the evidence that it changed.

**The unmet gate condition on the morning is arranged, not incidental.** Every candidate meets a decision about whether to proceed, and the assessed property is the record rather than the decision. **Both answers pass.** A candidate who proceeds with a named waiver and a candidate who stops have both satisfied the specification; a candidate who proceeds with an unattributed waiver has not.

**The three defence questions are fixed and published.** *What did they give up? What would show this finding is wrong? Which of your own prohibitions did you break?* Publishing them removes the assessment's ability to surprise and leaves its ability to discriminate, which is the correct trade for a specification-based scheme.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Interest banked as commitment.** The likeliest failure. A warm sentence about future intent enters the closing record as a next step. *Countermeasure:* the first defence question, and the [S019](/wiki/seminars/S019.md) treatment which follows this assessment rather than preceding it — deliberately, so that the failure is met before it is named.

**The rescued difficulty.** The candidate breaks a prohibition, it works, and they are pleased. *Countermeasure:* self-reporting is accepted in place of an unbroken record, which makes honesty cheaper than concealment.

**The smoothed absence.** Nothing was committed and the record describes foundations. *Countermeasure:* the specification's explicit clause, and the assessor's instruction to read the closing record's most concrete noun.

**Artefact excellence without learning.** A candidate produces four immaculate artefacts and learns nothing about the customer. *Countermeasure:* the required paragraph on what was learned about the customer's situation — which the candidate may honestly leave empty, and which is therefore **not a countermeasure at all but a disclosure.** The scheme cannot detect this case and the paper concedes it in §8.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**Seven specification items, published in [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md) and repeated here.** Each checked present and adequate, or not.

1. The charter's failure condition is capable of firing, and the candidate can say what was observed against it.
2. The readiness gate contains at least one condition about the candidate's own organisation, and that condition was checked rather than assumed.
3. Every waiver carries a named person. A waiver without a name is recorded as an unmet condition.
4. The prohibitions were honoured during the assigned difficulty, or the breach is self-recorded.
5. The closing record distinguishes a commitment from an expression of interest by what was given up.
6. An absence of commitment is recorded as an outcome.
7. The finding is stated so that it could be wrong, and what would show it is named.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Not enjoyment, not customer commitment, not recovery, not timing, not presentation quality.

**Entrustment evidence generated: none.** This is a specification check. The programme's entrustment decisions are [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md), and nothing here anticipates them.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: an assessment that ignores whether the customer committed is an assessment of paperwork.**

At its strongest: the module's own claim is that a session producing no commitment has failed. An assessment which then declines to look at commitment has separated the standard from the mark. A candidate can satisfy all seven items while running a session that achieved nothing, and the scheme will certify them — so the scheme certifies documentation, not delivery.

**This is the strongest objection to the design and it is partly conceded.** The gap is real: item 5 checks that the candidate can tell a commitment from a warm sentence, not that they obtained one. The rebuttal is that the alternative assigns the mark to the customer's disposition, the sponsor's calendar and the assigned difficulty's severity, none of which the candidate controls — and a scheme that varies with those is not measuring the candidate. **The declined fix is to weight the mark by whether a commitment was obtained.** It is declined because it would make the assessment's outcome depend on the same customer-disposition variable that [S012](/wiki/seminars/S012.md) spends a session teaching candidates to distrust. **The concession stands: the scheme cannot distinguish a well-documented failure from a well-documented success, and the paper does not claim otherwise.**

**Objection 2: publishing the defence questions destroys the assessment.**

At its strongest: a candidate who knows the three questions prepares three answers. What is then observed is preparation, not practice, and the assessment measures whether the candidate read this paper.

**Not conceded.** The three questions cannot be answered from preparation alone — *what did they give up* has no answer unless somebody gave something up, and *which prohibition did you break* has no safe answer. Preparing for them is doing the work. **The design's position is that a specification a candidate could not have read is a trap rather than a standard**, and this programme's assessment architecture rejects traps. That position is itself contestable and is stated here so it can be argued with.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Item 5 will be the most frequently failed specification item. *Instrument:* per-item pass rates across the cohort, reported without aggregation.

**Prediction 2.** Candidates who stop on the unmet gate condition will be rarer than those who proceed with a named waiver, and both will be rarer than those who proceed with an unattributed one. *Instrument:* the submitted gates, coded three ways.

**Prediction 3.** Closing records written before the debrief will contain more specific nouns than a matched set written after it. *Instrument:* a deliberate split in which half the cohort writes a second record after the debrief, compared blind by a second reader. **The design does not currently create that split, so the prediction is unmeasurable as the assessment stands.** Recorded rather than fixed.

**Prediction 4.** Self-recorded prohibition breaches will exceed assessor-found ones. *Instrument:* the two counts. **If assessor-found breaches dominate, the honesty incentive in item 4 is not working and the item should be rewritten.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the certifiable properties of facilitated discovery are properties of the practitioner's artefacts, and that outcome-bearing assessment measures the customer.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about whether the practice works. Seven checkable properties, all owned by the candidate, none of them evidence about an engagement's result.

**The concession worth remembering.** This assessment cannot tell a well-documented failure from a well-documented success. That is not a flaw that was overlooked; it is the price of refusing to mark the customer, and it is paid knowingly.
