## In plain terms

This is where the module's four artefacts meet a room that will not cooperate.

The assessment is unusual in what it refuses to look at. It does not care whether the customer committed. It does not care whether the room enjoyed it, whether the candidate recovered from the difficulty, or whether the finding was correct. **Every one of those is either the customer's behaviour or luck, and assessing a candidate on either is assessing the wrong person.**

What it checks is whether the artefacts held: that the charter's failure condition could fire and was observed against, that the gate's inward condition was actually checked, that every waiver names somebody, that the prohibitions survived pressure or the breach was self-reported, that a commitment was distinguished from a warm sentence by what somebody gave up, and that an absence was recorded rather than smoothed.

**The most important of those is the last.** A closing record saying nobody committed to anything is a stronger submission than one describing a session that built strong foundations, and candidates find that genuinely difficult to believe until they have written both.

The assessment then closes the module with the one move the stage has been building towards. The candidate writes the session's finding as a **declarative agent specification** — who it serves, what it may use, what it may do, where it stops, and what would show it wrong — and then uses the thing built from it to argue that the finding is an artefact of who happened to be in the room. **A finding in prose can absorb any amount of contradiction, because prose has no interface.** A specification has one, and the gaps stop being rhetorical.

## Abstract

This paper defends an assessment that examines a practitioner's artefacts and refuses to examine the engagement's outcome, and defends closing it by requiring the finding to be written as an executable specification.

The argument runs in four parts. **First**, outcome-bearing assessment in a delivery context evaluates the customer's disposition and the assessor's luck, so the properties worth certifying are the ones the candidate owns. **Second**, those properties are all checkable by a reader who was not present: a fireable condition, a named waiver, an honoured prohibition, a commitment identified by its cost, a recorded absence. **Third**, the record must be written before the debrief, because a group that has discussed an event has agreed a story about it and the assessment literature finds that judgement anchors early and that further evidence elaborates rather than revises. **Fourth**, a finding stated in prose has no interface and can absorb any contradiction, whereas a declarative specification forces it to name its sources, its boundaries and its falsifier — which turns an unfalsifiable sentence into a visible gap.

The paper records an amendment made while writing it: the assessment as first specified required the finding to be "stated so that it could be wrong" and provided no artefact in which the falsifier had to live, so a candidate could satisfy the element with a sentence nobody could check. The correction adds the specification and is documented at §4.6 rather than applied silently.

The paper concedes that the scheme cannot detect a candidate who produced excellent artefacts and learned nothing about the customer; that its central claim about pre-debrief records is design reasoning nobody has measured; that the assessment literature it borrows from is single-institution medical education using methods its own authors say may elicit reconstructions rather than reasoning; and that **this assessment generates no entrustment evidence and should not be read as certifying anything about a person.**

## 1. The problem this session must solve

An assessment of facilitated work will, unless prevented, measure charm.

The mechanism is simple. The observable output of a session is the room's response, the room responds to the person, and an assessor watching a session forms a judgement about the person before forming one about the work. That judgement is not worthless — it tracks something real — but it is not the thing the module taught, and it correlates with prior confidence rather than with the module.

The second problem is that the useful evidence is produced after the room empties, when the candidate is tired, relieved, and about to be told by colleagues what the session was like. **The closing record is the artefact most vulnerable to that conversation** and is therefore required before it.

The third is the one the module has been circling for four sessions. A finding written in prose has no interface: it can be qualified, softened, extended and reinterpreted indefinitely, and nobody can tell from reading it what the session did and did not establish. **An assessment that certifies a candidate on the strength of an unfalsifiable sentence has certified a sentence.**

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** All four preceding sessions are load-bearing and the assessment reads their outputs directly.

The prior it works against is that an assessment must have a right answer visible in the outcome. Candidates arrive expecting to be marked on whether it went well. **The published specification is the countermeasure**, and it is published in advance for exactly that reason.

The specification exercise splits the cohort in a way worth naming. Candidates who build agents routinely will produce a well-formed configuration quickly and will treat the falsifier fields as documentation. Candidates who do not will struggle with the syntax and will, in this design's experience of similar exercises, write better falsifiers because they are not distracted by the artefact. **The exercise is not assessed on its structure for exactly that reason.**

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** High, and mostly extraneous by design: time pressure, an audience, an unannounced difficulty and five artefacts to keep in view. That is the condition the module claims prohibitions are for, and an assessment run under comfortable conditions would not test the claim.

**3.2 Why the difficulty is unannounced.** A rehearsed difficulty tests preparation. An unannounced one tests whether the prohibition survives surprise, which is the property [S014](/wiki/seminars/S014.md) actually asserts.

**3.3 Why the closing record precedes the debrief, and what the evidence for that is.**

A group that has discussed a session has agreed a story about it, and the record written afterwards is a record of the story.

The assessment literature supplies the mechanism, and it is stronger than the intuition. Assessors reading competency portfolios reached judgements through a three-phase cyclical process of acquiring, organising and integrating evidence, then re-entered acquisition to seek confirming or disconfirming material — and **"assessors were inclined to stick to their initial judgments even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence"**, producing final judgements more elaborate than their preliminary ones but **"not substantially different"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). Further evidence improved the articulation of a conclusion already reached.

If that is what a trained assessor does with the record in front of them, a tired facilitator reconstructing a session after colleagues have narrated it is not recording anything.

The same literature explains two further features of this assessment. It found that **"assessors mainly relied on narrative feedback to inform their judgment… Grades and qualifications were merely used to confirm impressions based on narratives"** — the prose was the evidence and the numbers checked a conclusion formed from it, which is why this assessment's output is a substantiated pass or fail rather than a score. And it found that assessors reaching the same outcome differed in their reasoning, driven by their beliefs about which evidence is trustworthy, their private theories of competence — which **"tended to deviate from the formal assessment criteria"** — and the inferences they drew about the person. The recommended remedy is multiple assessors, substantiated judgements and group discussion for high-stakes decisions, which builds shared mental models over time.

**Its limits, and they are not small.** Two studies from medical education at a single institution with a specific portfolio type and competency framework; transfer to engineering assessment is an argument rather than a given. The portfolio study used **mock portfolios and assessors who did not know the students**, which the authors note cuts both ways. **Think-aloud methods cannot capture fast or automatic processing, and participants may construct plausible explanations for a researcher rather than report actual reasoning** — the authors state this themselves. Several of the group's most-cited works are paywalled and were not consulted here. And the findings describe how assessors reason; **they do not establish that narrative-based judgement is more valid than score-based judgement.**

**3.4 Why a commitment is identified by its cost.** The distinction is between **commitment**, in which a stakeholder gives up something valued, and **advancement**, in which the relationship moves to a defined next stage; the currencies are meaningful time, reputational risk and money ([research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md)). The source's two cautions matter for element 5: a trial is not automatically a commitment, and **a person without authority may be unable to commit despite a genuine need** — which is why the element checks that the candidate can tell the difference rather than that they obtained one.

**Its limits.** A founder-oriented practitioner handbook; **no controlled comparison, no sampling framework, no measured effect**; conversation counts are anecdotal; the local copy establishes no publisher, year or ISBN.

**3.5 Why this is a specification check and not an entrustment decision, and why the defence questions are what they are.**

The entrustment framework separates the **ad-hoc** judgement a supervisor makes continuously from the **summative** decision that "has the nature of certification" and is taken about future responsibilities — and states that a summative decision **"should include an agreement between multiple observers that this entrustment is justified, to avoid subjective bias, and multiple observations"** ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). This assessment meets neither condition and therefore generates no entrustment evidence, which §7 states plainly.

Two of its mechanisms are used directly. The **entrustment-based discussion** is a short post-activity conversation that probes deep understanding and **must include what-if questions**, because retrospective explanation shows only that the learner understood the case they met and **"an entrustment decision implies the acceptance of risk, as it can never be fully predicted what the student will encounter"**. That is the warrant for publishing *what would show this finding is wrong* as a fixed defence question. And its three professionalism characteristics are stated behaviourally — **integrity** as truthfulness in reports to a supervisor, **reliability** as stable and conscientious working, **humility** as the ability to observe one's own limitations and ask for help — which is why a self-recorded breach scores where an assessor-found one does not.

**Its limits.** A primer by the concept's originator: guidance rather than evaluation, reporting adoption rather than effectiveness. A clinical model with legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint, whose stakes a course must construct artificially — **and the construction is the hardest part of the transfer.** Its full evidence standard is expensive and this assessment is an explicitly reduced version of it.

**3.6 Why an absence scores.** The corpus reports **no failed workshop, no declined engagement and no delivered system reported to have survived**; every case study succeeds and its single failure narrative is a synthetic composite, fictional by its own admission ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). A programme that only rewards positive findings reproduces that shape in its graduates. **Rewarding a recorded absence is the cheapest available counterweight and it is an argument rather than a finding.**

**3.7 Why the finding becomes a declarative specification.**

The platform contribution is not tooling and the session says so. It is that a **prompt agent** — declarative configuration, no compute of its own — has a small number of mandatory parts, and each one corresponds to something a discovery finding is normally allowed to leave vague.

The documented distinction is a hosting one: prompt agents use "declarative config in portal or code", are "managed by Foundry with no agent compute", and suit "agents that don't include custom code or require custom hosting", where hosted agents run "your code (containerized)" on managed container compute ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). **The declarative form is the right instrument here precisely because it cannot do very much** — the candidate is not building the system, they are writing down what the session established in a form where the gaps are structural rather than rhetorical.

Two further documented facts bear on the artefact. The rapid setup path "lacks network isolation" while the controlled one gives "fine-grained control over data, security, and networking", so a specification assuming production topology must say so. And the family states that the options "aren't mutually exclusive" and compose through MCP, which makes a specification's boundary a claim about components rather than about products.

**Its limits.** First-party vendor selection guidance, commercially framed, **read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches**, with several comparison tables truncated, comparing Microsoft options only with each other. **No adoption, cost, failure-rate or outcome data of any kind.** Product names are in active flux. **Nothing in it claims that writing a finding declaratively improves the finding**; that is this module's argument and it is untested.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The assessment runs the module's own instrument on the module's own artefacts.

**4.1 The four artefacts are submitted as amended rather than as originally written**, with amendments visible. This is the first appearance of the discipline that later becomes the decision record: a settled artefact is changed by recording the change, not by editing away the evidence that it changed.

**4.2 The unmet gate condition on the morning is arranged, not incidental.** Every candidate meets a decision about whether to proceed, and the assessed property is the record rather than the decision. **Both answers pass.** A candidate who proceeds with a named waiver and a candidate who stops have both satisfied the specification; a candidate who proceeds with an unattributed waiver has not.

**4.3 The three defence questions are fixed and published.** *What did they give up? What would show this finding is wrong? Which of your own prohibitions did you break?* Publishing them removes the assessment's ability to surprise and leaves its ability to discriminate, which is the correct trade for a specification-based scheme. The second is an entrustment-based discussion's what-if question in its narrowest form; the third tests truthfulness in a report to a supervisor, which the entrustment literature states behaviourally.

**4.4 The decision is deliberated rather than taken alone.** The assessment literature's finding that identical outcomes rest on divergent reasoning, and that assessors' private theories of competence deviate from the formal criteria, makes a single-assessor pass indefensible even for a closed checklist. **Assessors substantiate in writing and discuss before deciding**, and where they initially diverged is recorded, because that record is what builds a shared standard across offerings.

**4.5 What the specification exercise is for.** Not tooling competence, and not architecture. It makes one thing observable that prose cannot: **that a finding has an interface.**

Four of its fields do the work. The knowledge sources force the candidate to separate what somebody gave them from what they assumed. The stop conditions force them to name the questions the session cannot answer. The `finding_is_wrong_if` list forces the falsifier into the artefact where it can be inspected rather than beside it where it can be gestured at. And `not_established` forces the omission to be recorded — **an empty `not_established` list is the clearest possible signal that a candidate has not been honest**, and it is visible at a glance.

The attack step is deliberately aimed at the finding's provenance rather than its content: *build the strongest case that this is an artefact of who was in the room.* That is the objection a customer will never make and a colleague will never make, and it is the one that matters, because the room was assembled by somebody with an interest.

**4.6 A defect found in this assessment's own specification, and the amendment.**

Element 7 as first written required that the finding be "stated so that it could be wrong" and that the candidate "name what would show it".

**A candidate could satisfy that element with a sentence.** Nothing required the falsifier to be observable, nothing required an observer to exist, and nothing required the candidate to record what the session had failed to establish. In a module whose whole argument is that an unfalsifiable success condition is not a condition — taught at [S011](/wiki/seminars/S011.md) about charters and at [S012](/wiki/seminars/S012.md) about gates — the terminal assessment accepted an unfalsifiable finding provided it was phrased in the subjunctive.

The defect is the module's own thesis applied to the module. **A falsifier with no named observer is the same object as a readiness criterion made of dispositions**, and this design did not notice for the length of a module built to teach exactly that.

The amendment adds produce-item 6, workflow steps 2 to 6, and the requirement in element 7 that the falsifier sit inside the specification and that what was not established be recorded. **It does not assess the specification's structure**, because assessing a configuration artefact would produce candidates optimising a file rather than examining a finding.

**The amendment is recorded rather than silently applied.** Assume there are others this paper has not caught.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Interest banked as commitment.** The likeliest failure. A warm sentence about future intent enters the closing record as a next step. *Countermeasure:* the first defence question, and the [S019](/wiki/seminars/S019.md) treatment which follows this assessment rather than preceding it — deliberately, so that the failure is met before it is named.

**The rescued difficulty.** The candidate breaks a prohibition, it works, and they are pleased. *Countermeasure:* self-reporting is accepted in place of an unbroken record, which makes honesty cheaper than concealment.

**The smoothed absence.** Nothing was committed and the record describes foundations. *Countermeasure:* the specification's explicit clause, and the assessor's instruction to read the closing record's most concrete noun.

**Artefact excellence without learning.** A candidate produces four immaculate artefacts and learns nothing about the customer. *Countermeasure:* the required paragraph on what was learned about the customer's situation — which the candidate may honestly leave empty, and which is therefore **not a countermeasure at all but a disclosure.** The scheme cannot detect this case and the paper concedes it in §8.

**The unobservable falsifier.** The specification names three conditions that would show the finding wrong and no person who could ever see any of them. *Countermeasure:* exercise 4 requires each falsifier to name an observer, an instrument and an occasion, and element 7 checks that the falsifier sits inside the artefact. **Neither reaches the case where all three are named and none is reachable**, and prediction 5 predicts this countermeasure failing.

**The empty omission list.** The candidate records nothing under `not_established`. *Countermeasure:* workflow step 4 requires the list to be read back against the charter, and the failure-mode list names it. It is visible at a glance, which is the strongest property this scheme has anywhere.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S019](/wiki/seminars/S019.md), [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) and [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md).

The commitment distinction is met here as a failure and taught properly at [S019](/wiki/seminars/S019.md), one session later. The closing record's absence clause returns at [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) as a packet requirement and at [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) as an engagement-level one.

The specification exports the module's longest-range habit. **A claim written with its falsifier and its omissions inside the same artefact** is the shape every whitepaper in this programme closes with, and a candidate who has produced one for their own finding has performed the move this repository is built on before being told that is what it was.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**Seven specification items, published in [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md) and repeated here.** Each checked present and adequate, or not.

1. The charter's failure condition is capable of firing, and the candidate can say what was observed against it.
2. The readiness gate contains at least one condition about the candidate's own organisation, and that condition was checked rather than assumed.
3. Every waiver carries a named person. A waiver without a name is recorded as an unmet condition.
4. The prohibitions were honoured during the assigned difficulty, or the breach is self-recorded.
5. The closing record distinguishes a commitment from an expression of interest by what was given up.
6. An absence of commitment is recorded as an outcome.
7. The finding is stated so that it could be wrong, the falsifier sits inside the specification rather than beside it, and what the session did not establish is recorded rather than omitted.

Five properties are deliberately excluded and are stated to candidates in advance. **Enjoyment is not assessed**, because it is a fact about the room. **Customer commitment is not assessed**, because it is a fact about the customer. **Recovery from the assigned difficulty is not assessed**, because outcome marking endorses whichever intervention relieved the candidate. **Timing is not assessed.** And **the specification's structure and correctness are not assessed**, because marking a configuration artefact would produce candidates optimising a file rather than examining a finding.

**Nothing else is assessed.**

**Entrustment evidence generated: none.** This is a specification check, run once. A summative entrustment decision requires agreement between multiple observers across multiple observations, and neither condition is met here. The programme's entrustment decisions are [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md), and nothing here anticipates them.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: an assessment that ignores whether the customer committed is an assessment of paperwork.**

At its strongest: the module's own claim is that a session producing no commitment has failed. An assessment which then declines to look at commitment has separated the standard from the mark. A candidate can satisfy all seven items while running a session that achieved nothing, and the scheme will certify them — so the scheme certifies documentation, not delivery.

**This is the strongest objection to the design and it is partly conceded.** The gap is real: item 5 checks that the candidate can tell a commitment from a warm sentence, not that they obtained one. The rebuttal is that the alternative assigns the mark to the customer's disposition, the sponsor's calendar and the assigned difficulty's severity, none of which the candidate controls — and a scheme that varies with those is not measuring the candidate. **The declined fix is to weight the mark by whether a commitment was obtained.** It is declined because it would make the assessment's outcome depend on the same customer-disposition variable that [S012](/wiki/seminars/S012.md) spends a session teaching candidates to distrust. **The concession stands: the scheme cannot distinguish a well-documented failure from a well-documented success, and the paper does not claim otherwise.**

**Objection 2: publishing the defence questions destroys the assessment.**

At its strongest: a candidate who knows the three questions prepares three answers. What is then observed is preparation, not practice, and the assessment measures whether the candidate read this paper.

**Not conceded.** The three questions cannot be answered from preparation alone — *what did they give up* has no answer unless somebody gave something up, and *which prohibition did you break* has no safe answer. Preparing for them is doing the work. **The design's position is that a specification a candidate could not have read is a trap rather than a standard**, and this programme's assessment architecture rejects traps. That position is itself contestable and is stated here so it can be argued with. **The declined fix is to rotate the questions between offerings**, declined because rotation would advantage candidates who happened to be assessed in a year whose questions suited them, which is the same luck the scheme exists to remove.

**Objection 3: requiring the finding as a declarative specification tests configuration literacy and calls it judgement.**

At its strongest, and it is the objection the §4.6 amendment most deserves: a candidate who has built agents will produce a clean artefact quickly and a candidate who has not will spend their remaining attention on syntax, in an assessment whose stated subject is discovery. The specification's structure is not marked, but it is submitted, read and discussed, and an assessor reading a tidy file forms an impression — which is precisely the anchoring the design cites the assessment literature to warn about. Worse, the vendor documentation supporting the artefact **publishes nothing at all about whether a declarative specification improves anything**, so the module's closing move rests on an analogy between a configuration file and an argument.

**Partially conceded, and this is the amendment's real cost.** Three defences, none complete. Element 7 checks the **presence of a falsifier inside the artefact and of a recorded omission**, not the artefact's quality, and §7 states the exclusion explicitly so an assessor can be held to it. The exercise sits **after** the session and the closing record, so a candidate who cannot complete it still submits everything the other six elements need. And the required output of the attack step is a **written record of what changed**, which is prose and is where the judgement lives. **The declined fix was to have candidates write the finding with its falsifier on paper and never build anything** — keeping the discipline and dropping the platform — rejected because a falsifier written in prose has no place it must go, which is exactly the defect §4.6 records. **The cost of that rejection is the literacy advantage this objection names, and prediction 6 is the only thing that would detect it.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Item 5 will be the most frequently failed specification item. *Instrument:* per-item pass rates across the cohort, reported without aggregation.

**Prediction 2.** Candidates who stop on the unmet gate condition will be rarer than those who proceed with a named waiver, and both will be rarer than those who proceed with an unattributed one. *Instrument:* the submitted gates, coded three ways.

**Prediction 3.** Closing records written before the debrief will contain more specific nouns than a matched set written after it. *Instrument:* a deliberate split in which half the cohort writes a second record after the debrief, compared blind by a second reader. **The design does not currently create that split, so the prediction is unmeasurable as the assessment stands.** Recorded rather than fixed.

**Prediction 4.** Self-recorded prohibition breaches will exceed assessor-found ones. *Instrument:* the two counts. **If assessor-found breaches dominate, the honesty incentive in item 4 is not working and the item should be rewritten.**

**Prediction 5.** A majority of specifications will contain at least one falsifier that names no reachable observer, and candidates will identify this themselves in exercise 4 more often than assessors identify it in the defence. *Instrument:* the falsifier entries, coded *observer named and reachable* / *observer named, unreachable* / *no observer*, by a second reader, against the exercise-4 self-assessment. **This predicts the failure of element 7's countermeasure.**

**Prediction 6.** Candidates with prior agent-configuration experience will not differ from those without on element 7's outcome, although they will differ on the time taken. *Instrument:* element 7 pass rates split on a single self-reported prior-experience item, with completion time recorded. **This is the direct test of §8's third objection and it is the prediction this paper would least like to lose** — a difference in outcome means the assessment is measuring configuration literacy.

**Prediction 7.** The `not_established` list will be empty in a substantial minority of first submissions, and empty lists will co-occur with closing records that report a commitment. *Instrument:* the two fields, cross-tabulated. **If they co-occur, candidates are recording completeness where they obtained agreement**, which is the module's central failure arriving in a new costume.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the certifiable properties of facilitated discovery are properties of the practitioner's artefacts; that outcome-bearing assessment measures the customer; and that a finding written with its falsifier and its omissions inside the same artefact is harder to fool yourself with than one written in prose.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about whether the practice works. Seven checkable properties, all owned by the candidate, none of them evidence about an engagement's result. The assessor-cognition findings behind the pre-debrief rule are single-institution medical education using mock portfolios and a method its own authors say may elicit reconstructions rather than reasoning. The entrustment framework reports adoption rather than effectiveness. The commitment distinction is a practitioner heuristic with no measured effect. **The platform documentation nowhere claims that a declarative specification improves a finding, and neither may anybody citing it.**

**The concession worth remembering.** This assessment cannot tell a well-documented failure from a well-documented success. That is not a flaw that was overlooked; it is the price of refusing to mark the customer, and it is paid knowingly. And note §4.6: for the length of a module built to teach that an unfalsifiable condition is not a condition, this assessment accepted an unfalsifiable finding provided it was phrased in the subjunctive. **The failure the module names is one the module committed, in its own terminal assessment.**
