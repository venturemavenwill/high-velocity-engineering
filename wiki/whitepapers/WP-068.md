# WP-068 — Designing for Detectability

**Seminar day:** [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) · **Module:** [M14](wiki/modules/M14.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](wiki/quarters/Q7.md)
**Strands:** SY · ME · SG · **Complexity class:** 5
**Platform anchor:** Foundry observability — tracing, the Agent Monitoring Dashboard and continuous evaluation, the last two preview · Azure Monitor alerts · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server (preview) · Azure MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends treating detectability as a design property fixed before release rather than as an operational activity performed after it, and defends deriving that claim from a quantity the cohort already holds: blast radius is who, how many, how long and what they experience, and only the duration remains under anyone's control once a failure begins. It defends a four-part detection target as the day's assessed form, and defends a phase 3 payload in which the most sensitive of four monitoring designs is the one that reported healthy for nine days, because it was computed by the pipeline that had failed. **It defends the day's practical turn: three of the four designs are now operated, two failure conditions are induced on the students' own telemetry, and the third outcome is demonstrated on a vendor's own troubleshooting table — where an empty chart is documented as meaning a quiet system, a stopped pipeline or an ingestion delay, and where evaluation runs are documented as skipped at an hourly cap with no signal that they were.** That payload discharges two forward links placed on this day by earlier scope statements, from S027 and S047, which turn out to name the same failure. The paper records two corrections made under existing rules — a provenance clause required by specification rule 2, and the transfer of S038's expert-politeness countermeasure required by instrumentation rule 5. It concedes that the day requires a cost figure for failures whose evidence is absent by construction, that the programme applies a weaker liveness standard to the signals this day produces than the day requires of its students, **and that inducing failures to teach blast radius is a blast radius, which it fixes by confining the induction to student-owned resources and records what that costs.**

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The students already know that systems fail in ways nobody predicted. [S033](wiki/seminars/S033.md) taught partial failure, [S035](wiki/seminars/S035.md) had them break a running system on purpose, and [S067](wiki/seminars/S067.md) ended with eleven weeks of regression that no dashboard showed and a user who wrote in. None of that produces the disposition this day needs.

The problem is that **monitoring is learned as a response to incidents, and therefore always describes the past.** Every engineer these students will work alongside has a monitoring system, and every one of those systems is an accretion: a signal per incident, added afterwards by whoever handled it. The result is well-shaped for the failures that have happened and shapeless for the ones that have not, and it is described in design documents as though it were a design.

The day must convert a reflex — *we should monitor that* — into an object with parts. The four-part target is that object: condition, time, observer, false-alarm rate. It is not a sophisticated idea and its difficulty is entirely in the last two parts. Students supply conditions readily and times with prompting. **They do not supply an observer, because in their experience the observer is "the team", which is nobody; and they do not supply a false-alarm rate, because they have never been shown that a false-alarm rate has a cost.**

Three specific difficulties sit underneath.

**The independence problem.** [S027](wiki/seminars/S027.md) point 2 listed four error sources, named three omissions, and assigned *error introduced by the measurement itself* to this day. [S047](wiki/seminars/S047.md) point 1 listed three drift mechanisms, named its omission, and assigned *drift caused by a change in the measurement pipeline* to this day. Two whitepapers, two quarters apart, in two different subject areas, deferred what turns out to be the same failure: **the instrument and the observation moved together, so their difference carried no information.** A monitor computed from the pipeline it monitors is the operational form, it is the most natural thing to build because the data is already there, and it is undetectable by inspection.

**The credibility problem.** A false alarm's cost is not the operator's time; it is the response to the next alarm. The withdrawal is informal and unrecorded — an individual mutes an alert on a Thursday afternoon — and the design documents continue to list a control that no longer exists. Students will treat false-alarm rate as a tuning parameter unless the day shows them that it is a claim on a colleague's attention that will be revoked without notice.

**The liveness problem.** [S033](wiki/seminars/S033.md) point 1 established the third outcome: success, failure, unknown. Monitoring has the same three states and every human reads *nothing* as *fine*. [S066](wiki/seminars/S066.md) point 8 said a gate that has never blocked anything is evidence about the gate; the same sentence applies to a signal that has never fired, and item 3 measures how many students can say, before the day, how they would know a signal had stopped.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Moderate and evenly distributed**, which is unusual for this module. Nothing on the day is technically demanding and the enumeration at step 1 is the only open-ended element.

The interactivity is between steps 1, 3 and 8. The enumeration determines what can be covered; step 3's not-detected list is the enumeration minus the coverage; and step 8's blast-radius computation depends on the committed latency, which depends on the signal built at step 4 and on the liveness bound demonstrated at step 5. A student who enumerates thinly produces a short not-detected list and a flattering blast radius, and the phase 7 partner's first task exists to attack exactly that path.

Management:

- **The component's full monitoring configuration and two-year incident log are supplied.**
- **All four phase 3 designs come with the incident record of their period.**
- **Step 2 is worked with the room**, because the four-part target is the day's assessed form and a student who mis-forms it mis-forms everything downstream.
- **The rota member is scheduled**, so recruiting an observer costs the student nothing.
- **The component and change were chosen at [S066](wiki/seminars/S066.md)** and the residual class at [S067](wiki/seminars/S067.md).

**Step 3 is unsupported.** Expertise reversal applies for a student who has carried a pager: points 1, 3, 5 and 6 will be familiar, and the day's content for them is points 4 and 8 — independence and liveness — which are the two that experienced operators most often lack because both are invisible until they matter.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Attributing every incident in a two-year log to the signal that caught it,* item 1, where the honest answer is often "a person".
- *Estimating detection latency before reading the recorded time,* item 2.
- *Saying how you would know a signal had stopped, before the day names the problem,* item 3.
- *Ranking four designs by time-to-know before the incident record arrives,* phase 3.
- *Producing an explicit list of what you have decided not to detect,* step 3.
- *Arguing independence rather than asserting it,* step 4.
- *A partner who must both defeat the signal and time its death,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Observability tooling.* Tracing, metrics backends, log aggregation and query languages are SY301's. The day is indifferent to the stack and says so.
- *Statistical change detection.* Control charts, sequential tests and drift statistics belong to [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md), where the horizon is long enough for them to be the right tool.
- *Alert routing, escalation policy and on-call design.* [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s and the substrate's.
- *Adversarial evasion of detection.* [S076](wiki/seminars/S076.md)'s and [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s. Point 4's independence argument is the seed and the day does not develop it.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The day's repertoire — signals, probes, thresholds, liveness properties — is **safe for failures that produce some change in a quantity the system can emit**, which covers availability, latency, error rates, throughput, resource exhaustion and most quality regressions. It is **unsafe for failures visible only to a user**, of which a wrong-but-plausible answer is the canonical case: it produces no anomaly in any signal, it is indistinguishable from correct operation at every layer the system can observe, and no amount of instrumentation reaches it. That class requires a channel from the affected party, and the substrate has one — it is how [S067](wiki/seminars/S067.md)'s phase 6 incident ended.

**The boundary is bound in the artefact rather than left in this paper.** Point 7 requires the student to name which of their own component's failures fall in the user-visible-only class, and the specification checks it. This is the structure the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s open question contemplates — the register asks whether a day's artefact must make a scope boundary checkable, and records that a day can satisfy the scope rule completely while assessing work that ignores it. This day does bind it. **Doing so once does not answer the open question**, and the cost here was one clause, which is evidence about this instance rather than about the general case.

**The platform instance, scoped in the strong form.** The day uses Foundry's observability stack as the concrete form of a detection design, and point 11 carries the conditions to students.

*Safe.* Reasoning about any monitoring arrangement the student meets: what does its silence mean, what does it share with the thing it watches, who receives it, and what would tell them it had stopped. Each has a documented answer here; each is the right question anywhere.

*Unsafe.* Two conditions. **Unsafe as a description of what these features will do next term** — the hourly default, the latency and success-rate guidance, the ingestion delay and the preview labels are product decisions. And **unsafe as an argument that this vendor is worse than others**, which is the reading a room primed by two induced failures will reach for. The troubleshooting table that supplies the day's sharpest instance is a *virtue*: the behaviour is documented, the cause is named, the remedy is given. **The platforms that should worry a student are the ones whose empty chart has the same three causes and no table.** The day states this at point 11, and step 3's not-detected list is where a student applying the simplification outside its range would fail — an entry that names a vendor rather than a condition is not a detection decision.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **M4**.

Four designs are ranked by time-to-know. M4 is the sophisticated one: a derived signal, sensitive to real degradation, built by someone who understood the component. Groups rank it first, correctly, and the incident record confirms them on every incident but one. On that one it reported healthy for nine days while the metric it was derived from was wrong, **because the fault was in the shared pipeline and the signal moved with the metric.**

The construction matters. M4 is not a straw design and it is not defeated on the ranking dimension the groups used. It wins on speed and loses on a dimension the ranking did not have, which is what makes the task-after — *state the failure each cannot detect, and how long until you notice the monitor has stopped* — arrive as the obvious next question rather than as an instruction.

The second mechanism is item 1. Students attribute each incident in a two-year log to the signal that caught it, with the log in front of them, and discover that for a substantial share the detector was a person: a user, an engineer noticing something, a colleague in another team. That finding costs nothing to produce and it undermines the premise every monitoring conversation begins from.

Phase 6 supplies the affective content and it is chosen because everyone in the room has done it. An alert fires weekly, is spurious every time, and is muted on a Thursday afternoon by one person with no record. **Nobody decided to stop monitoring that condition.** Each individual dismissal was correct; the aggregate was a policy change nobody made, by someone who would have been alarmed to be told they were making one.

Autonomy is supported by step 3, which is the only place in the programme where a student is asked to decide what not to observe and is told that the decision is theirs to make and to own. Competence is supported by the four-part target, which is short enough to hold and specific enough to check. Relatedness is supported by step 6's rota member, who is a colleague rather than an assessor.

The competence risk is at step 6. **A student whose narration reveals that the rota member would not act on their signal may read that as a rejection of their design.** The instructor states before phase 5 that a signal a colleague would not act on at three in the morning is the day's most valuable finding, that discovering it now costs nothing, and that discovering it during an incident costs the incident.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items against a real log. Item 1 is the opening finding and requires no framework; item 3 is the payload administered before the payload, so phase 8 measures a shift rather than a recall. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction** and is deliberately the same question as item 3 asked about an agent's design rather than a human's.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals and a bridge that turns detection latency into the one controllable term of a quantity the cohort already computes.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four designs with real incident records, **selected against the [S067](wiki/seminars/S067.md) difference the cohort could not anticipate** — which is the rule-6 element, not a framing choice — and **three of them operated.** M4 is the payload and is defeated on a dimension the ranking did not contain. **Two conditions are then induced on the group's own agent and telemetry: emission stopped, and the evaluation cap exceeded.** M2 is demonstrated rather than operated and nothing depending on running it is assessed.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Eleven points. Point 2 is the assessed form. Point 4 is the payload and discharges the S027 and S047 deferrals. Point 7 carries the strong-form scope statement and is bound in the artefact. **Points 9 and 10 are the platform points the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) requires** — four inherited defaults, and independence given an address — and point 11 delivers the perishability boundary.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Eight steps producing an input to [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md), **now building the signal rather than specifying it.** **Step 4's second clause is the [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md) rule-6 element** and requires a detector for the class the student could not anticipate. **Step 5 is new and is the day's answer to instrumentation rule 7**: point 8 is the claim the room's folk model resists, phase 3 stages it, and step 5 requires the student to satisfy it in their own artefact by stopping their signal and showing that something said so. **Step 6 applies [S038](wiki/seminars/S038.md)'s expert-politeness countermeasure as instrumentation rule 5 requires**, and step 3's provenance clause is a correction under specification rule 2; both are recorded in §7. The artefact carries the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A policy change made by attrition. **Then the agent case: a monitoring setup that was correct, green, capped at a default and entirely without liveness.**

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Dual defeat, with the second demand attacking the monitor rather than the system and now tested against the student's own step 5 bound, and its answer recorded in minutes as the detection-timeliness measure [WP-044](wiki/whitepapers/WP-044.md) §7 carried here.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** The person-detected fraction; latency estimates signed; item 3 against step 4; **item 5's frontier hit rate; the step 5 demonstrated detection times against the step 8 committed latencies**; the cohort's pooled accepted blindness stated plainly.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has been on call will produce a competent enumeration, a plausible latency and a signal that works. Predicted failure: they skip the independence argument at step 4, because in their experience deriving a signal from existing metrics is simply how it is done and nothing has ever gone wrong with it. Countermeasure: independence is the day's single hard requirement and a fail condition, and phase 7's partner is required to time the signal's death — which, for a derived signal with no separate liveness path, is unbounded and obvious.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is step 1's enumeration, which is open-ended and which they have no experience to populate. Countermeasures: the two-year incident log is a supplied enumeration of what has actually gone wrong with this component; phase 3's four designs each name the failures they cannot see; and the [S067](wiki/seminars/S067.md) residual class supplies one condition the student already knows is real.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** A group that has read about correlated failure will identify M4's dependence immediately. The difficulty is then in M2 and M3, where the trade is between coverage and latency and neither dominates. Fallback if a group handles all four: ask them to design the cheapest addition to M4 that would have caught the nine-day incident, which forces them to invent an independent path and is the step 4 content arriving early — and the group is then asked what that addition costs when it produces a false alarm.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **futility**. A day whose findings are that people detect a large share of incidents, that the most sophisticated monitor was blind for nine days, that alerts get muted without anyone deciding to, and that a whole class of failure is undetectable in principle, invites the conclusion that monitoring does not work. That conclusion produces engineers who ship without detection designs and rely on complaints, which is the exact behaviour the module exists to prevent.

Countermeasures: M1's aggregate threshold catches total failure in seconds and this is stated, so the day does not teach that signals fail; point 5 gives the constructive argument, that halving detection time halves the harm and is available on every design; step 3 reframes the undetectable class as a decision made rather than a defeat suffered; **step 5's liveness demonstration is a thing that works, built by the student, in the phase** — which is the day's only unambiguous success and is placed there partly for that reason; the rota member at step 6 is a person whose work is made better by the student's design, which is the day's only direct evidence that this is worth doing; and the instructor states once that the difference between an engineer and an operator is that the engineer decided in advance which failures they would learn about from a stranger.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) (where accepted blindness acquires owners), [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) (where the detection commitment enters a ship decision), [S074](wiki/seminars/S074.md) (tool failure, where two of the four shapes report success and are wrong), [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md) (drift and alerting, at a longer horizon and with statistical machinery), [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md) (forensics, where detection latency is the first question asked) and [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md) (the safety case, where a detection commitment is a claim the case rests on).

**S070 at two weeks is the immediate target** and the link is instrumented: the phase 7 signal-death latency responds at S070's decision artefact, which must state what happens if the signal itself fails.

**S079 at roughly two quarters is the deep target.** This day designs detection for a failure that starts; S079 designs it for a condition that arrives gradually and has no start, and the four-part target does not straightforwardly apply to a quantity with no threshold. The design's claim is that a student who has built and had defeated one detection design receives S079's statistical material as a refinement rather than as an introduction, which is the claim [WP-018](wiki/whitepapers/WP-018.md) §6 made about this day and which this day now makes about S079.

**S074's retrieval is the one most likely to fail.** Point 3's third detection failure — the signal exists, someone is watching, and it is indistinguishable from normal — is exactly the condition an agent's confidently-wrong output produces, and this day frames it as a discrimination problem in a signal rather than as a property of an answer. S074 must supply the bridge; this day cannot.

Retained content: detectability as a pre-release design property; the four-part target; the three detection failures; independence of the detection path; latency as the controllable term; the credibility cost of false alarms; accepted blindness as an output; and the third outcome in monitoring. Eight items, above the design's usual ceiling, accepted because four of the eight are one-sentence restatements of content the cohort holds in another domain.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 5.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md).

One specification item in SY301 with ME301. Per rule 1, the checked elements are the failure enumeration, a four-part detection target per condition, the not-detected list with each cost carrying its provenance and confidence in the same field, one built signal with its independence argument, **its liveness property with a demonstration that stopping it produced a report inside the committed time**, a second signal for the [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md) class, its false-alarm rate and the recorded three-a.m. narration, coverage of one class outside the taxonomy, the committed latency with its blast radius, the named user-visible-only failures, **the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement**, and the phase 7 outcome. **Nothing else is assessed.**

Four hard requirements: **a signal whose independence is not argued fails**, **a detection target missing any of its four parts fails**, **an empty not-detected list fails**, and **a signal whose stopped state is indistinguishable from its healthy state fails.** The fourth is new with the practical turn: an argument that liveness is present is not a demonstration that it works, and after phase 3 the room has no excuse for the confusion. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the design demonstrates it could have detected an intervention-worthy error, of which the step 5 liveness path is the qualifying artefact. **Nothing that depends on operating M2 is assessed.**

**Two corrections made under existing rules, annotated at the point of change.**

- **Specification rule 2.** An earlier form of step 3 required an expected cost for each undetected failure and did not require the figure's confidence or provenance. That is the detached-qualification failure [WP-033](wiki/whitepapers/WP-033.md) §8 identified as the third instance of one failure mode, and the rule was written to stop it. **Step 3 now requires provenance and confidence in the same field as the cost.** §8 objection 1 argues the correction is necessary and not sufficient.
- **Instrumentation rule 5.** Step 6's original form asked the rota member to agree to the signal, which is the structure [WP-038](wiki/whitepapers/WP-038.md) §5 identified as destroyed by expert politeness and which [WP-046](wiki/whitepapers/WP-046.md) §8 recorded as the fourth instance of a remedy not carried across. **Step 6 now asks the rota member to narrate a three-a.m. response and to say where they would stop, and the student records the narration rather than requesting approval** — S038's countermeasure, transferred rather than rediscovered. Under rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 this reuse is **contingent** on S038's reported outcome.

**Rule-6 discharge, as required by [WP-067](wiki/whitepapers/WP-067.md) §7 and [WP-060](wiki/whitepapers/WP-060.md) §7.** Three obligations land here and **all three elements exist.** The S067 step 3-against-step 6 attribution matrix responds at phase 3: the four monitoring designs are selected against whichever of the five differences the cohort could not anticipate, so the case set is a function of the previous day's measured blind spot rather than a fixed asset. The S067 step 5 novel-class rate responds at phase 5 step 7, which requires each detection design to cover one class the substrate's taxonomy does not contain and which is a checked element rather than a prompt. **The S060 phase 5 prediction-match rate — how often a student's predicted lost documents matched the queries that actually failed — responds at phase 5 step 4's second clause, which requires each student to build a detector for the failure class they did not anticipate at S060 and to say what made it invisible in advance.** That clause is a checked element and is **deliberately tolerant of a weak detector**: S060's instrument measured a failure of prediction, and the response the rule requires is that the unanticipated class acquire a detector, not that the student produce a good one on a class they have just been shown they cannot foresee. All three obligations are discharged.

**Older forward links discharged.** [S027](wiki/seminars/S027.md) point 2 and [S047](wiki/seminars/S047.md) point 1 each deferred an omitted mechanism here; **both are point 4, which is the phase 3 payload.** [WP-029](wiki/whitepapers/WP-029.md) §6 claimed this day formalises S029's steps 3 and 4; **that is point 2's four-part target.** [WP-044](wiki/whitepapers/WP-044.md) §7's detection-timeliness measure and [WP-019](wiki/whitepapers/WP-019.md) §7's signal-comparison baseline are **consumed at phase 7's second task and reported at phase 8.** None of these was written under rule 6 and all four are reported because an unchecked forward link is the failure rule 6 exists to prevent.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** The pooled step 3 not-detected list responds at [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) phase 5 step 6, where every entry is assigned an owner or recorded as having none; the phase 7 signal-death latency distribution responds at [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) phase 3 item 4. **WP-069 §7 and WP-070 §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

**A third instrument, under rule 9.** Item 5's sealed prediction accuracy and the step 5 liveness demonstrations are this day's frontier data. Their named reader is the following year's cohort, through the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), which carries the re-verification of the four defaults named at point 9. **Per instrumentation rule 3 the sampling is disclosed to students**: one cohort, one telemetry stack, one component chosen by staff because its incident log is long enough to attribute from.

Dispositions: **reliability**, at step 8, where the committed latency is a promise the student will be held to at S070 and at S080.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day requires a number for a quantity whose evidence is absent by construction.**

At its strongest: step 3 requires, for each failure the student has decided not to detect, the expected cost of not detecting it. **That cost is the harm from a failure that will not be observed, to users who will not report it, over a duration nobody will measure.** [S027](wiki/seminars/S027.md)'s condition D is the exact description: the diagnostic evidence is missing because the thing is missing. The day teaches, in point 3 and again in point 7, that a whole class of failure produces no evidence anywhere, and then asks students to quantify the consequences of it.

The provenance correction recorded in §7 makes this visible rather than solving it. A student who writes *low, estimated from the three comparable incidents in the log, low confidence* has satisfied the rule and has still produced a figure derived from the failures that were detected, which are by construction the wrong sample.

**This is conceded and the fix is cheap.** The honest output for each entry is not a cost but two facts: **who would experience it, and by what route anyone would ever learn of it.** Those are checkable, they are what [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) actually needs in order to assign an owner, and a student who cannot name the route has produced the finding that matters. The design keeps the cost figure **because [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s decision artefact needs something to weigh**, and that is a bad reason: a decision weighed on an unmeasurable number is the failure [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md) exists to prevent, and the module cannot both teach that and require it.

The design should replace the cost with the affected party and the discovery route, and require a cost only where a comparable detected incident exists to derive one from. It does not, and the reason is that the change would propagate into S069 step 6 and S070 phase 3 and was noticed after both were written. That is an ordering accident presented as a design decision, and it is recorded as such.

**Objection 2: the programme applies a weaker standard to the signals this day produces than the day requires of its students.**

At its strongest: point 8 requires every signal to carry a liveness property of its own, and phase 7 makes a student time their own monitor's death. The signals built at step 4 then enter operational use on the substrate — the [Substrate and Infrastructure](wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page names this day explicitly in risk 7 and states that student-built instruments have two characteristic failures, a construct-instrument gap and **silent decay after a correct start**, both invisible to inspection.

The safeguard that page adopts is a review date: every adopted student instrument is withdrawn automatically unless revalidated. **A review date is a timer, not a liveness property.** It bounds the exposure and it does not detect the decay, which is precisely the distinction point 8 makes and which the day fails a student for missing. So the programme teaches a standard at seminar and applies a weaker one to the artefacts the seminar produces, in the module whose subject is that the thing you are not watching is the thing that fails.

The rebuttal is partial. The review date was introduced in response to [WP-029](wiki/whitepapers/WP-029.md) §8, it is a real improvement on an indefinite exposure, and the second safeguard on that page — limitation propagation enforced by tooling rather than by convention — is stronger than anything this day requires of students. So the programme is not uniformly weaker; it is weaker on exactly the property this day is about.

**The fix is cheap and is not this paper's to make.** Every signal adopted from this day should be required to carry the liveness property the day requires of the design, enforced by the substrate's tooling in the same way limitation propagation already is, so that a student-built signal that stops reporting raises an alarm rather than quietly reading as healthy. That requires one line on the [Substrate and Infrastructure](wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page's adoption conditions. **This objection is not fully answered here**, because the day can require whatever it likes of student designs and cannot make the substrate enforce it, and a requirement with no enforcement is the shape instrumentation rule 4 exists to catch.

**The practical turn makes this worse and the paper says so.** Step 5 now requires the student to *demonstrate* liveness — to stop their own signal and show that something reported it, inside the time they committed to — and the specification fails a design that cannot. **So the standard the day applies to a ninety-minute student artefact is now strictly higher than the standard the programme applies to the same artefact once it is adopted into operational use.** The gap was a difference of rigour; it is now a difference the day itself has made measurable, since every adopted signal arrives with a demonstrated detection time for its own death and the adoption conditions do not ask for it. **That strengthens the objection rather than answering it**, and it makes the one-line repair cheaper to argue for, which is the only thing this paper can contribute to it.

**Objection 3: a day that teaches blast radius induces failures to make its point.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn and it is conceded.

At its strongest: phase 3's two induced conditions are the day's best moments. Stopping span emission and driving traffic past an evaluation cap are what convert point 8 from a claim into an experience, and no description reaches the same place. **They are also, described neutrally, deliberately breaking observability on a system with real users**, taught by a day whose central quantity is who is affected, how many, for how long and what they experience. If the substrate's shared telemetry path is the one that goes dark, the rota member who arrives at step 6 is on call for a system whose monitoring a seminar has just disabled, and the [Substrate and Infrastructure](wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page's disclosure requirement — established when [WP-035](wiki/whitepapers/WP-035.md) §8 found that proportionality is not consent — applies with full force.

**The fix is made and it is not free.** Both conditions are induced on the group's own agent, its own telemetry resource and its own evaluation rule, never on the substrate's shared observability path, and the day states this where the conditions are described. The demonstration survives intact: the charts are real, the skip is real, the confusion between three causes of an empty chart is real, and it is the student's own screen.

**What is lost is recorded.** A student-owned telemetry path is a path nobody else depends on, so the induced failure has no blast radius — which means **the day demonstrates the detection problem and not the consequence problem.** The nine-day incident in M4's record is a shared-path failure, and shared-path failures are the ones that produce the durations this module exists to shorten. The demonstration is therefore of the mechanism at zero cost, and the cost is exactly what makes the mechanism matter. **No cheap fix exists**: the version that would carry the cost is the version the disclosure rule forbids, and the design is not willing to argue that a teaching demonstration justifies degrading a real service's monitoring. The gap is left open and named rather than closed badly.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At item 1, more than a third of the log's incidents will be attributed by the cohort to a signal, and the log will show the actual detector was a person; the cohort will over-attribute to signals rather than under-attribute. *Instrument:* item 1 attributions against the log's recorded detector, coded for direction of error. **The direction is the claim.**

**Prediction 2.** At item 2, detection-latency estimates will be systematically low, and the underestimate will be largest for the partial-degradation incidents rather than the outages. *Instrument:* item 2 estimates against recorded times, split by incident class.

**Prediction 3.** Fewer than one in five students will give a usable answer to item 3 before the day, and more than three-quarters will produce a liveness property at step 4 after it. *Instrument:* item 3 against step 4, paired per student. **If the before-figure is high, point 8 is not this day's content**; if the after-figure is low, the four-part target is not carrying liveness and needs a fifth part.

**Prediction 4.** At step 6, more than a quarter of rota narrations will reveal that the recipient would not act on the signal as designed — and the proportion will be higher for signals whose false-alarm rate the student described as acceptable. *Instrument:* narrations coded for whether the recipient would act, cross-tabulated against the student's stated false-alarm rate. **The cross-tabulation tests whether the S038 countermeasure transferred**: if narrations are uniformly approving, the expert-politeness remedy did not survive the change of domain and step 6 is measuring courtesy again.

**Prediction 5.** At step 5, more than a third of students will discover on their first attempt that stopping their signal produces no report at all, and the median gap between the demonstrated detection time and the step 8 committed latency will be large and in one direction — detection of the monitor's death slower than detection of the failure it watches for. *Instrument:* step 5 first-attempt outcomes and demonstrated times, paired against step 8 commitments. **The direction is the claim.** If the two are close, students are building liveness into the same path as the signal, which passes the step and fails point 4, and the specification needs a fifth part to the detection target rather than a separate step.

**Prediction 6.** At item 5, a large majority will predict that the agent's monitoring contains an error, and fewer than one in five will predict specifically that it will omit a liveness property. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded for generic error, wrong-threshold, and missing-liveness predictions, against what each agent produced. **This is the day's frontier measure and its second half is the one that matters**: a student who predicts a missing liveness property before the day has the day's content already, and if that rate is high the design's ordering is wrong.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** Error introduced by the measurement process, drift arising in a measurement pipeline rather than in the criterion, the unobservability of partial failure, the third outcome and its consequences for a caller, blast radius as a function of duration, and instrument decay after a correct start are grounded in [research/03-measurement-evaluation/](research/03-measurement-evaluation/collected-materials.md) and [research/01-course-structure/](research/01-course-structure/collected-materials.md). The operational and release-workflow framing follows [research/01-course-structure/cmu-17-645-ml-in-production-ai-engineering.md](research/01-course-structure/cmu-17-645-ml-in-production-ai-engineering.md) and [research/06-microsoft-platform/](research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), the latter as instance rather than as ground. The expert-politeness countermeasure is [S038](wiki/seminars/S038.md)'s and follows [research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md).

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** Every platform statement in this day comes from Microsoft Learn, read 2026-07-26.

*What it says.* Foundry's observability is documented as three capabilities — evaluation, monitoring and tracing — with monitoring integrated into Azure Monitor Application Insights and tracing built on OpenTelemetry. The Agent Monitoring Dashboard's settings panel is documented as controlling continuous evaluation, scheduled evaluations, red team scans and alerts. **Continuous evaluation is documented as running evaluators on sampled agent responses at a rate the operator sets.** The dashboard's guidance describes latency above ten seconds as possibly indicating model throttling, complex tool calls or network issues, and a run success rate below ninety-five per cent as warranting investigation into failed runs. **The dashboard's troubleshooting table lists, for the symptom of empty charts, three causes: no recent traffic, a time range that excludes the data, or an ingestion delay. For the symptom of skipped evaluation runs it lists one cause — the hourly run limit was reached — with the resolution of raising `max_hourly_runs` or waiting for the next hour, and states that the default limit is one hundred runs per hour.** Trace evaluation is documented as requiring the project's managed identity to hold Log Analytics Reader on both the Application Insights resource and its linked Log Analytics workspace, with a further role for protected tables, and states that **where the input and output message attributes are empty or missing, quality evaluators return no score, while safety evaluators can still produce scores with partial data that might not be meaningful.** Application Insights is documented as taking thirty to ninety seconds to ingest spans.

*What version it says it at.* Dated by content rather than by version number. **The Agent Monitoring Dashboard, continuous evaluation, scheduled evaluations, red team scans and alerts all carry preview notices**; tracing is documented as generally available for prompt and hosted agents and in preview for workflow and external agents. The day's numbers are marked perishable at point 11.

*What it does not report.* Four silences. **Nothing states that a skipped evaluation run emits a signal** — the behaviour appears only as a troubleshooting symptom, which is to say that the documented route to discovering it is noticing that results are absent, and that is the day's sharpest single fact. **Nothing states a detection rate, a false-positive rate or an alert precision for any of the alerting capabilities**, consistent with the folder-wide asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md); no such figure is asserted here. **No evaluator's agreement with human judgement is published**, so the quality signals a continuous evaluation rule produces are uncalibrated, and the day describes them that way. And **the ten-second and ninety-five per cent figures are given as guidance without any statement of what they were derived from**, which is why point 9 treats them as inherited defaults rather than as thresholds a student may adopt.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** Pretesting against a real record with the answer available, contrasting cases adjudicated by incident history, narration in place of evaluative approval, dual-defeat peer articulation, expanding-interval retrieval and error management training are cited for **direction and mechanism only**. No effect size is asserted for any of them. **No magnitude is asserted for any detection latency, false-alarm rate, person-detected fraction or incident duration**; every figure in the day is measured on the substrate's own log and is marked perishable.

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The four-part detection target; the three-detection-failure decomposition; the four monitoring designs and M4's construction; **the two induced conditions and their confinement to student-owned resources**; **step 5's liveness demonstration as a staging of point 8 under instrumentation rule 7**; the fail conditions on an unargued independence claim, an empty not-detected list and an indistinguishable stopped state; the user-visible-only class bound in the artefact; phase 7's signal-death timing; and all six §9 predictions are this design's own reasoning, untested. The §8 objection-1 concession is unqualified, states that the day requires a figure the day's own material says cannot be evidenced, names the replacement — affected party and discovery route — and records that the design retained the figure for an ordering reason presented as a design one. The §8 objection-2 concession is not fully answered, and **the practical turn widens it: the day now demands a demonstrated liveness property from a ninety-minute student artefact while the programme applies a review-date timer to the same artefact in operational use.** The §8 objection-3 concession is unqualified, takes the confinement fix, and **records that no cheap fix exists for what the fix costs — the day now demonstrates the detection problem without the consequence problem, and the version that would carry the consequence is the version the disclosure rule forbids.**
