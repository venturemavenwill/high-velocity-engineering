## In plain terms

This occasion marks one thing. **What work did the assessment cause?**

Not compliance. Not thoroughness. **Whether somebody is doing something on Monday that they were not doing on Friday**, and whether it traces to something the candidate wrote.

The module's durable claim closes here and it is the sentence to carry out of the stage. **An assessment that produces a signature has changed nothing. An assessment that produces a work item has.** A signature records agreement; a work item records a change in what somebody does. They look similar in a report and are not the same kind of thing.

**The occasion adds one mechanism to that claim and it is the module's last idea.** An open concern with no failing test attached can be closed by assertion — somebody says it is handled, the item is marked done, and nothing anywhere disagrees. **Bind the concern to an evaluation that currently fails, and closing it by assertion becomes visible**, because the test is still red.

The strongest argument for that mechanism comes from watching a well-built system do the opposite. **The multi-agent pipeline this stage has studied ends every stage in a self-verification checklist**, and in one place renders that checklist into the deliverable with the model's own ticks — **a configured control wearing the visual language of an audit trail.** The verdict in the note is the one this occasion is built on: **if a control must be able to fail without the model's cooperation, that pipeline has roughly half a dozen controls and several hundred instructions.**

Four further elements do most of the remaining work and three come straight from practitioner judgement in the source material.

**The team reads and discusses the findings before anything is tracked.** The source is emphatic about this and **its own chain violates it**, terminating in an automated write to a live backlog with nobody required to have read anything. A candidate is marked on not doing what that chain does.

**The tail is withheld deliberately.** Teams given every finding at once fix none of them, so the candidate chooses what the team gets now and records what waits, with a date. **The line between withholding and concealing is entirely the record**, and the occasion says so.

**At least one finding is reclassified as a legitimate engineering trade-off**, or the absence is defended. A deliberate decision written up as non-compliance teaches a team to ignore the scorer, and **false positives destroy reviewer trust, which costs more than the findings were worth.**

**And the permanent record is a different document from the action plan.** Merged, they produce an artefact that is out of date within a month and authoritative for a year — which is the same failure [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md) diagnosed in the assessment itself.

The defence has one move the candidate is not warned about. **The assessor offers to sign the assessment off.** Accepting a signature on an assessment that generated no work is the failure this occasion exists to catch.

## Abstract

This paper defends closing a responsible-AI module with an assessment marked on the work it generated rather than on its completeness or its approval; defends a specification in which an unapproved assessment that produced work passes ahead of an approved one that did not; and defends requiring at least one open concern to be bound to an evaluation that is failing at submission.

The argument runs in five steps. **First**, a governance artefact's only observable effect is a change in what somebody does — compliance is a claim about a document and safety is a claim nobody here can substantiate. **Second**, an open concern with no external check attached can be closed by assertion, and the sharpest available demonstration is a well-engineered pipeline whose self-verification checklists are configured controls rendered into deliverables with the model's own ticks. **Third**, the mechanism that fixes this already exists in the platform: evaluations can be run over stored traces rather than re-runs, mixing deterministic and judged tiers, with the split strategy and the threshold both stated as choices because each silently determines what the result means. **Fourth**, binding a concern to a judged evaluator relocates the trust problem rather than solving it, so the occasion requires either a deterministic check or a validated judge, and reports precision and recall separately rather than raw agreement. **Fifth**, findings delivered in bulk change nothing, a deliberate trade-off scored as a violation destroys the reviewer trust the whole activity depends on, and a permanent technical record and an expiring action plan are different genres.

The occasion installs work items with named owners, dates, system-specific reasons and testable acceptance criteria; at least one open concern bound to a currently failing evaluation with its evaluator, split strategy and justified threshold declared; a dated discussion preceding any tracked item; a recorded withheld tail; a reclassified trade-off; the living-document instrumentation; and an unassessed list.

The paper concedes that marking generated work creates an incentive to manufacture findings which the specification counters only indirectly; that requiring prior discussion delays action on findings a candidate believes are urgent; that a bound evaluation is gameable through the two policy choices the candidate makes; and that the evaluation documentation publishes **no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator**, so a judged verdict carries no established relationship to a human's.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A governance assessment's default terminal state is a signature, and a signature is the one outcome that requires nothing of anybody.

**Everything about the artefact's usual form points that way.** It has a submission, an approver and a filing location. It has no owner list, no dates, and nowhere for a task to live. **The document is shaped like a permission**, and permission is what it produces.

There is a second problem which is the opposite failure and is nearly as common. **An assessment that produces a large volume of findings, delivered at once, produces no change either** — the team is handed a list, cannot start, and starts nothing. Volume feels like diligence and functions like noise.

A third problem sits underneath both. **Some of what an assessment finds is not a defect but a decision** — a trade-off somebody made deliberately, for reasons, accepting a cost. Written up as a violation, it tells the team that the assessor does not understand the system, **and a team that has concluded that will ignore the next finding too, including the one that mattered.**

The occasion's response is to mark the work, to require the discussion before the tracking, to require the tail to be withheld and recorded, and to require at least one reclassification.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The module's evaluative and interpersonal peak together. The candidate has to decompose a governance concern into something testable, deliver findings to a team, absorb being corrected, reclassify their own work, and then decline an offered approval.

**3.2 Why generated work is the only observable effect.** Compliance is a claim about a document. Safety is a claim nobody here can substantiate. **A change in what somebody does is observable, datable and attributable**, and it is the only property of a governance artefact this programme can honestly mark.

**3.3 Why a self-checked control is not a control, and why that argues for a bound evaluation.**

The pipeline this stage has studied ends every stage in a numbered self-verification checklist — identifier uniqueness, referential integrity, arithmetic reconciliation. **These are prompt-engineering devices that measurably improve output quality and they are not controls: the model checks its own output and reports that it did** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md)). In one agent the checklist is rendered into the deliverable **with the model's own ticks**, which makes a configured control wear the visual language of an audit trail. **The note's verdict is the one this occasion is built on: if a control must be able to fail without the model's cooperation, that suite has roughly half a dozen controls and several hundred instructions.**

The same source supplies the right answer, and it is not more instructions. **A threat's status is re-derived from the workspace rather than ticked by a person**: the status stage searches source, pipeline definitions, container files, infrastructure dialects, configuration and documentation for evidence, and **a claim of "implemented" must cite a file and a location.** **Evidence in the repository is the closure criterion**, which is the right shape and which most organisations do not have.

Three cautions come with it and the occasion inherits all three. **Nothing schedules the re-run.** A fix that is real but invisible in the repository — a portal setting, a tenant policy — never closes and regenerates indefinitely. **And the acceptance criteria a human might close a ticket against were written by the same model that wrote the mitigation.**

**Limits.** Internal and access-controlled; paraphrased and never quoted; **it cannot supply an external warrant for any claim.** No outcome evidence exists for any of it — **nothing establishes that plans produced this way lead to more fixed vulnerabilities than plans produced any other way.** Its implied observed failures are nowhere recorded. **The source contains operational attack detail that is not reproduced here and should not be.**

**3.4 Why the platform's evaluation surface is the right instrument, and what it makes the candidate declare.**

The framework's programming model is deliberately minimal: an item wrapping a conversation with a query and response derived by a split strategy, an evaluator that scores items, and aggregated results ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). Its two-tier design — a **local evaluator** running deterministic checks with no API calls, and a cloud tier for model-judged evaluation, **mixable in one run** — expresses the evaluation literature's cost-and-speed ladder directly in an interface, which means the architecture carries the discipline.

Four properties make it right for binding a governance concern.

**Stored responses can be evaluated "from logs or previous runs… without re-running the agent."** Synthetic queries test what you thought to ask; a trace contains what occurred, and a governance concern is almost always about what occurred.

**The split strategy silently determines what a score means**, and the documentation states it: **"How you split determines *what you're evaluating*."** Last-turn, full-conversation and per-turn options answer different questions, and **an agent that recovers well from a bad first attempt scores well under one and badly under another.** Neither is wrong; a team that never chose is reporting an answer to a question it did not ask.

**The threshold is policy, separable from the measurement.** Output is pass or fail with a `reason`, plus a numeric score for scale-based evaluators. **Moving the threshold changes the pass rate without changing the system**, so it must be justified rather than inherited — and the `reason` field is what makes a verdict auditable rather than merely numeric.

**And repetitions exist "to detect non-deterministic behavior"**, the documentation's own acknowledgement that one sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement.

**The silence is the point rather than a footnote.** The documentation is definitional and accurate about what the tooling computes, and **no agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold.** Most of these evaluators are model-judged. **No agreement figure is asserted anywhere in this paper or in the session.** Pages were read through search excerpts rather than full fetches; most agent evaluators are preview; one consulted page is an explicitly superseded variant; cloud evaluation incurs cost.

**3.5 Why binding to a judged evaluator relocates the problem, and what the occasion requires instead.**

**LLM-generated evaluators inherit all the problems of the LLMs they evaluate, requiring further human validation** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md)). The mechanism that repairs it is alignment against human grades: label a subset by hand, then prefer the implementation whose verdicts best reproduce those labels. **That is what converts an unvalidated generated evaluator into one with an evidentiary basis**, and it is the step teams skip.

The practitioner literature adds the operational detail. Track judge agreement in a table holding the model's response, its critique and verdict beside a human's, a batch at a time — and **"using raw agreement is generally not recommended and can be misleading when classes are imbalanced. Instead, you should typically measure precision and recall separately"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md)). The same source supplies the decomposition that makes a concern testable at all — **features and scenarios**, where each situation becomes an assertion — and the correction most engineers need: **unlike traditional unit tests, an all-green suite is not the target — the pass rate is a product decision.**

**So the occasion accepts a deterministic check without validation and requires a judged one to carry it**, which is a proportionate rule rather than a strict one, and the specification does not mark whether the validation was done — only that the choices were declared. §8's third objection is where that limit is argued.

**Limits.** The alignment work is **qualitative**, establishes the phenomenon rather than its prevalence, and rests on a small study by construction. The practitioner essay rests on **one company's case study with no control condition, no measured improvement and no released dataset**, with dated tooling references — **take the pattern, not the product.** And a statistical caution belongs on any bound evaluation: **an eval is an experiment**, a pass rate without an interval is not a result, and a check too small to detect the claimed difference produces the same output as a system with no difference ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). That source is analytical; its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions; **statistical validity says nothing about construct validity**; and its power analysis needs prior variance data a first-time team lacks, which is why the occasion accepts *unknown*.

**3.6 Why the discussion must precede the tracking.** A tracked item is a claim on somebody's time made without their participation. **Creating them before the team has read the findings converts an assessment into an imposition**, and the trust cost is paid on every subsequent finding. The source states this as a rule and its own chain breaks it, **which is the clearest available demonstration that stating a rule and building one are different acts.**

**3.7 Why the tail is withheld.** Attention is the scarce resource, not information. **A team handed everything starts nothing**, and the withholding is a professional act — but only while the withheld set is recorded and dated. **Undated, it is concealment**, and the difference is a property of the record rather than of the intention.

**3.8 Why one reclassification is required.** An assessment that found no legitimate trade-offs either examined a system with none or did not listen. **Requiring the reclassification forces the listening**, and permitting a defended absence stops the element becoming a ritual.

**3.9 Why the two documents are separated.** A permanent technical record and a temporary action plan have different lifetimes and different readers. **Merged, the artefact inherits the shorter lifetime and the longer authority**, which is the worst available combination.

**3.10 Why the defence is an entrustment-based discussion with mandatory counterfactuals.**

The form is borrowed rather than invented. An **entrustment-based discussion** is a short focused conversation after the activity that probes deep understanding of what was done **and must include what-if questions**, because an entrustment decision implies accepting risk about situations that were not encountered ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). **Retrospective explanation shows only that the candidate understood the case they met; counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases they did not.**

The same framework supplies the summative standard — **multiple observers in agreement, multiple observations**, across direct observation, longitudinal observation, discussion and product evaluation — and the honest label for anything less: **ad-hoc.** This occasion is one observer on one occasion and §7 records that rather than claiming otherwise.

The judgement is recorded as reasoned narrative rather than as a mark, because **assessors form judgements from narrative and use scores only to confirm them**, they **anchor early and resist disconfirmation**, and **convergence on an outcome conceals divergence in reasoning** — two assessors can pass the same candidate for incompatible reasons, invisible in the result ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). The remedy those studies recommend is group deliberation with **thoroughly substantiated** judgements.

**Limits.** The entrustment framework is a **clinical model** whose stakes any other setting must construct artificially, and it is guidance from the concept's originator and an advocate, **reporting adoption rather than effectiveness.** The assessor studies are of clinical raters in a single institutional setting, using think-aloud methods that cannot capture fast or automatic processing and that risk confabulated explanations. **Both transfers are this design's inference, not the authors'.**

**3.11 Why the opening items are still a generation phase, even at an assessment occasion.** The five items are answered and submitted before the work begins, and the design wants item 2's emptiness to be the candidate's own discovery. **The generation phase must not be scaffolded**, and consolidation must **explicitly prompt comparison** rather than merely present a correct answer beside the candidate's ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)) — which is what exercise 3 does. **School, university and laboratory evidence; no adult-professional study in the reference list; and an Unproductive Failure quadrant in the author's own model.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The movements run situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**4.1 Opening item 2 — who is doing something differently, and can they name what — is the centre of gravity** and is asked before instruction. A candidate who names nobody has diagnosed their own assessment.

**4.2 Opening item 3 is the one candidates skip**, and the session says so in its own text rather than leaving it as a marker's observation, because the cost of skipping it is borne by the team rather than by the candidate.

**4.3 The bound evaluation must be red on the day it is written, and this is the newest element in the specification.** A check that passes at submission was written to pass. **The element's purpose is to make closure-by-assertion visible**, and a green check cannot do that. A candidate whose check passes has two honest routes: the concern was already handled, in which case close it with the evidence and bind a different one; or the scenario is not the concern, in which case the decomposition is wrong and that is the finding.

**4.4 The decomposition into a feature and a scenario is where the session's time should go.** A governance concern phrased as a commitment about future behaviour — *we will monitor*, *outputs will be reviewed* — cannot be reduced to a situation, and **the failure to reduce it is the finding rather than an obstacle.** The design deliberately does not supply a worked decomposition for the candidate's own concern, because the difficulty is the content.

**4.5 The split strategy and the threshold are declared rather than chosen silently.** Both are policy decisions that change what the result means without changing the system, and a candidate who inherits a default has made a decision they did not notice and cannot defend. **The specification marks the declaration and the justification, not the values**, because this programme has no basis for holding a view about what threshold is right.

**4.6 The workflow puts the discussion first and the tracking last**, and states the ordering as the thing being marked. It also names, directly, that the chain the module learns from breaks this rule — **which is the clearest instance in the stage of a source being right in its prose and wrong in its build.**

**4.7 The agent attacks the binding rather than the assessment.** The prompts ask it to describe how the bound evaluation could be satisfied without addressing the concern, and to find acceptance criteria only their author could check. **Searching a policy surface for the cheapest satisfying path is a task the tooling suits**, and the design reserves the judgement about which paths matter for the candidate — while requiring them to record that the instrument shares a model family with the evaluators they just wrote and is therefore not independent of them.

**4.8 The defence is adversarial in a way that mirrors [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md) and inverts it.** There, the assessor offered to make the plan look better. **Here, the assessor offers to approve it.** Both offers are attractive, both are the failure, and a candidate who has met the first form recognises the second — which is the module pairing its two occasions deliberately rather than repeating an instrument.

**4.9 The passing answer is to decline and to say what would have to exist for the signature to mean anything.** That is stated in the session rather than left to marker discretion, for the reason [WP-040](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-040.md) §4 gives: a candidate who suspects that declining is a trap will accept.

**4.10 The what-if questions are mandatory rather than at the assessor's discretion**, for §3.10's reason. Retrospective walkthrough is not entrustment evidence, and a defence composed entirely of *what I did* has produced no evidence about the cases the candidate did not meet.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The sign-off.** The likeliest failure and the one the whole occasion is built against. *Countermeasure:* the work-item elements, the specification's explicit exclusion of approval, and the assessor's offer.

**The self-verified concern.** *Countermeasure:* the bound-evaluation element, and the standard's warning sign — your evidence for a closed concern is a sentence in your own document.

**The green binding.** The candidate binds a concern to a check that already passes. *Countermeasure:* the requirement that the current result be red at submission, and the warning sign — you were pleased that it passed. **§9's prediction 5 measures this.**

**The undeclared split.** *Countermeasure:* the declaration element, and the warning sign — you cannot say whether a recovery from a bad first attempt counts as a pass.

**The dump.** *Countermeasure:* the withheld-tail element, and the standard's warning sign — you are pleased with the volume, and nothing has moved.

**The pre-emptive backlog.** *Countermeasure:* the dated discussion record, and the warning sign — somebody found out they had a new task by being assigned one.

**The violation that was a decision.** *Countermeasure:* the reclassification element and the warning sign — the team's response was to explain, and you did not change the finding.

**The merged document.** *Countermeasure:* the separation element, and the warning sign — you have a status column in your permanent record.

**The complete assessment.** *Countermeasure:* the unassessed list, which imports [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)'s wrongly excluded case and [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md)'s dodge check so that the element cannot be satisfied with generalities.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**This is an assessment occasion.** It generates specification evidence and no entrustment judgement — the programme's entrustment decisions begin at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md). **It is one assessor on one occasion, which the entrustment literature would label ad-hoc rather than summative**, and this paper records that rather than claiming otherwise.

**Nine elements are assessed**, each checked present and adequate or not.

1. Every work item has a **named owner and a date**, and lives where the team's work lives rather than in the assessment.
2. Every work item carries **testable acceptance criteria and a reason specific to this system** rather than one that would fit another.
3. **At least one open concern is bound to an evaluation that is currently failing**, with the evaluator named, the **split strategy declared**, and the **threshold stated together with a justification for choosing it.**
4. The **permanent technical record and the temporary action plan are separate documents.**
5. The **team read and discussed the findings before any tracked item was created**, with the record dated.
6. A **deliberately withheld tail** exists, is recorded, and carries a delivery date.
7. **At least one finding is classified as a legitimate engineering trade-off** rather than a violation, or the absence is defended.
8. The **living-document instrumentation from [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md) is present** with a state-change trigger naming an actor.
9. The **unassessed list** names what was not covered, including the case the candidate's own scope test wrongly excludes and their dodge-check answer.

The defence is conducted as an **entrustment-based discussion** and **must include what-if questions**, and the assessor's judgement is recorded as **reasoned narrative rather than as a mark**, for §3.10's reasons.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Whether the system is compliant is not marked. Whether the work items were completed is not marked. Whether the bound evaluation ever passes is not marked, and **an evaluation still failing at the defence is not a defect.** Whether the threshold chosen is the right threshold is not marked — only that it was chosen and can be justified. Whether a judged evaluator was validated against human labels is not marked. **The number of findings is not marked and more is not better.** Whether the assessment was approved is not marked, and **an unapproved assessment that generated work passes ahead of an approved one that did not.** Presentation is not marked. Whether the withheld tail was the right tail is not marked. Whether the trade-off classification was correct is not marked.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: marking an assessment on the work it generated creates an incentive to manufacture findings, and the specification contains nothing that penalises a false positive.**

At its strongest: a candidate who wants to pass needs work items, and the cheapest route to a work item is a finding. Nothing in the eight elements marks whether a finding was real, proportionate or worth anybody's time. **The occasion therefore rewards precisely the behaviour the session's own text condemns** — it quotes the judgement that false positives destroy reviewer trust and costs more than the findings were worth, and then builds a specification that pays for volume.

**Conceded, and the internal tension is real rather than apparent.** Three counterweights, all indirect. **The number of findings is explicitly unmarked and the specification says more is not better**, so volume buys nothing. The reclassification element requires the candidate to have listened to the team and to have moved at least one finding out of the violation column, which a manufacturer of findings will find hard to do convincingly. And the work-item elements demand a system-specific reason and a testable acceptance criterion, both of which are expensive to fabricate and easy for a second reader to spot. **The declined fix is to add an element marking whether the findings were accurate or proportionate.** It is declined for the reason this stage has declined the same class of element three times — at [WP-053](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §8 for a switch threshold, at [WP-055](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-055.md) §8 for coverage adequacy, and here for finding accuracy: **nothing available to this programme supplies a basis, so the element would be the marker's opinion in a specification's clothing.** The incentive is real, the counterweights are indirect, and the design accepts the gap rather than filling it with something unwarranted.

**Objection 2: requiring the team to have read and discussed the findings before anything is tracked delays action on real exposure, and makes the assessment hostage to a meeting.**

At its strongest: delivery teams are distributed and asynchronous, and gathering them is expensive and slow. Meanwhile a genuine exposure sits unrecorded because a procedural courtesy has not been satisfied. **The rule optimises for the team's feelings over the system's risk**, and in a serious case it is indefensible.

**Conceded in part, and the rebuttal narrows the requirement rather than defending the strong form.** The element requires that the team **read and respond**, with a dated record — not that a meeting occurred. Asynchronous review satisfies it, and the record is a set of responses rather than minutes. The deeper answer is that the objection's alternative does not achieve what it claims: **a tracked item nobody has read is not action either**, it is a queue entry, and the trust cost makes the next finding less likely to be acted on than this one. **The declined fix is to exempt high-severity findings from the discussion requirement.** It is declined because severity is assigned by the same assessment whose accuracy this specification does not mark — **an exemption keyed to a self-assigned severity is a self-granted exemption**, which is structurally the pattern [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) traces end to end. **The residual delay is real and the design accepts it, on the argument that the discussion is cheap and the lost trust is not.**

**Objection 3: the bound evaluation is gameable through exactly the two fields the specification asks the candidate to declare, so the element rewards declaration rather than rigour.**

At its strongest: the two policy choices — the split strategy and the threshold — are made by the candidate, are the levers that determine whether the check passes, and are not marked for correctness. **A candidate can therefore bind a concern, choose a split under which the system looks bad, submit a red check, and satisfy the element without the check bearing any relationship to the concern.** Worse, the element's requirement that the check be red **actively rewards choosing the strategy that fails**, which is the mirror image of the ordinary problem and no better. The specification marks that a choice was declared, not that it was the right one, and **a declared bad choice is still a bad choice.**

**Substantially conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to the newest element.** Three partial answers. The exercise requiring the candidate to design a dishonest route to a passing check makes the gaming surface explicit rather than latent, and a candidate who has written down how to game their own binding has a harder time doing it inadvertently. The justification field is a second reader's foothold: a threshold justified in one clause that would fit any threshold is visible in a way a bare number is not. And the element sits beside the discussion record, so a binding the team has never seen is discoverable from the dates. **The declined fix is to mark whether the split and threshold were appropriate.** It is declined for the reason this stage has declined the same class of element several times: **nothing available to this programme supplies a basis for judging a threshold, so the element would be the marker's opinion wearing a specification's clothing** — and worse here than elsewhere, because the vendor documentation itself **publishes no rationale for any default threshold and no agreement rate for any evaluator**, so not even the supplier can say what a right threshold is. **The gap is accepted and §9's prediction 6 measures whether it is being exploited.**

**Objection 4: an occasion that requires a red test at submission teaches candidates to leave problems open, which is the opposite of what a governance assessment is for.**

At its strongest: the element rewards a candidate for arriving with an unresolved concern and a failing check. A candidate who worked hard, fixed the concern and can show a green check with evidence has done the better job and satisfies the element only by finding something else to leave open. **The specification is therefore paying for open problems**, which is an odd thing for an assurance stage to do, and it risks teaching that governance is a matter of maintaining a visible backlog of concerns rather than closing them.

**Partly conceded, and the design's answer is narrower than the objection assumes.** The occasion states an explicit route for the candidate whose check passes: **close the concern with the evidence and bind a different one**, which takes no longer and is a better artefact. The element is not asking for an unresolved problem; it is asking for **one demonstration that the candidate can build a closure criterion something outside their own assertion could fail.** In any assessment of a real system there will be at least one open concern, and a candidate who claims none has produced the complete assessment the standard already names as a failure mode. **The declined fix is to accept a passing bound evaluation with evidence in place of a failing one.** It is declined because a passing check is indistinguishable, at submission, from a check written to pass — **and the element's entire purpose is to be the one thing in the assessment that could have disagreed with its author.** **The cost of declining is that a candidate with a genuinely well-closed system does slightly more work than one without**, which the design accepts and records here rather than hiding.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most candidates will name nobody in answer to opening item 2 before instruction. *Instrument:* the submitted opening answers, coded *named person with named change* / *named person only* / *nobody* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most assessments will contain no finding reclassified as a legitimate engineering trade-off. *Instrument:* a presence check for a reclassification or a defended absence, recorded per candidate.

**Prediction 3.** Most candidates will accept the assessor's offered sign-off. *Instrument:* the defence recorded as *accepted* / *declined*, with the condition named where declined.

**Prediction 4.** Withheld tails will be recorded without a delivery date. *Instrument:* a presence check for a date on the withheld set. **If this holds, the withholding requirement is producing concealment rather than sequencing and the element should be rewritten to lead with the date.**

**Prediction 5.** A substantial minority of bound evaluations will be green at submission, and the candidates concerned will report having chosen the concern after writing the check rather than before. *Instrument:* the submitted result, plus a single ordering question asked at the defence. **This predicts the failure of §5's newest countermeasure.**

**Prediction 6.** Threshold justifications will be generic — a sentence that would justify any threshold equally well — more often than they are specific to the concern. *Instrument:* the justifications coded *specific to this concern* / *generic* / *absent* by a second reader. **This is the instrument attached to §8's third objection**, and a confirmation means the declaration element is producing compliance rather than reasoning.

**Prediction 7.** Almost no candidate will validate a judged evaluator against hand labels, and most will bind their concern to a judged evaluator rather than to a deterministic one. *Instrument:* the binding coded *deterministic* / *judged and validated* / *judged and unvalidated*. **If judged-and-unvalidated dominates, the occasion is teaching candidates to relocate the trust problem while believing they have solved it**, and the specification should require the deterministic tier by default.

**Prediction 8.** Candidates will be unable to answer at least one of the three what-if questions with a counterfactual, answering instead by restating what they did. *Instrument:* the defence transcript, with each what-if answer coded *counterfactual* / *restatement* / *unanswered*. **This is the direct test of whether the borrowed discussion form is producing the evidence §3.10 claims for it**, and a null result means the occasion is a walkthrough with a better name.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the only observable effect of a governance artefact is a change in what somebody does; that an open concern with no failing test can be closed by assertion; that findings delivered in bulk change nothing; and that a deliberate trade-off recorded as a violation costs more than the finding was worth.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about whether the work was right. **No source available reports any outcome for any assessment of this kind**, the regime publishes no evidence that an assessment ever changed a system, and the surrounding corpus records no failure, no cost side, no quality evidence and no post-deployment record for any case. **The evaluation documentation publishes no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data and no threshold rationale**, so a judged verdict carries no established relationship to a human's. The specimen pipeline has **no outcome evidence of any kind.** The discussion form is borrowed from a clinical framework **reporting adoption rather than effectiveness.**

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper.** That is a rule of this repository rather than an accident of what was read.

**The concession worth remembering.** This occasion pays you for generating work and cannot tell whether the work was worth doing. **Notice that, notice that the withheld tail is one undated record away from being concealment, and notice that your bound evaluation is gameable through the two fields you chose yourself.** All three are techniques with failure modes, and you are the only instrument that will ever check them.

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The nine assessed elements and the closed specification. **The bound-evaluation element in its entirety** — the requirement that at least one open concern carry an evaluation, that the evaluation be failing at submission, and the claim that a red check makes closure-by-assertion visible in a way a paragraph cannot. The rule that an unapproved assessment which generated work passes ahead of an approved one that did not. The requirement that the withheld tail carry a delivery date and the claim that the date is what separates withholding from concealment. The requirement that at least one finding be reclassified or the absence defended. The import of [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)'s wrongly excluded case and [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md)'s dodge check into the unassessed list. The assessor's offered sign-off and the rule that declining with a stated condition is the passing answer. The decision not to mark threshold appropriateness or judge validation. And all eight predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The evaluation framework states three design principles — provider-agnostic, zero friction, progressive disclosure — and a three-type model of an item wrapping a conversation with a query and response derived by a split strategy, an evaluator that scores items, and aggregated results with pass and fail counts and per-item detail. It distinguishes a **local evaluator** running checks without API calls, suited to inner-loop development and continuous-integration smoke tests, from a cloud tier for model-judged evaluation, and states that the two **can be mixed in a single run**. It states that pre-existing responses can be evaluated **"from logs or previous runs… without re-running the agent"**; that **repetitions** exist **"to detect non-deterministic behavior"**; and that multi-turn conversations must be split into query and response halves, with the flat statement that **"How you split determines *what you're evaluating*"** and three strategies offered plus a custom splitter. Output is pass or fail with a `reason`, with a numeric `score` alongside for scale-based evaluators, and the documented worked example shows a score below its threshold producing a fail. Recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md), **read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches, with the page structure reconstructed.**

**The silences that matter, and the first is load-bearing for this whole occasion.** **No agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold.** Most of these evaluators are model-judged, and **no agreement figure is asserted anywhere in this paper or in the session.** The consequence is stated rather than smoothed: a candidate binding a concern to a judged evaluator has attached it to an instrument whose relationship to a human's verdict is unknown to them and, so far as this repository can establish, unpublished by the people who built it — **which is why §7 accepts a deterministic check without validation and does not mark whether a judged one was validated.** **The documentation publishes no guidance on choosing a split strategy for a governance purpose**, only on what each strategy measures, so the mapping from a concern to a split is the candidate's and is unwarranted. **Most agent evaluators are marked preview and may change or disappear**, and one consulted page is an explicitly superseded variant. **And the documentation says nothing about binding an evaluation to anything** — the idea that an evaluation can serve as a closure criterion for a governance concern appears nowhere in it and is this design's invention.

**Nothing in this session or paper is legal or regulatory guidance**, and the approval, retention and reporting duties that will actually govern a learner's assessment belong to their organisation, their customer, their regulator and their jurisdiction. This programme describes no work-tracking system, whose behaviour is not load-bearing here.
