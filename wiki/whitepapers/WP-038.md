## In plain terms

The sponsor liked the prototype. That is not evidence, and the reason has nothing to do with their honesty: **they will not use it.**

The person who will use it has not seen it. In several organisations you will meet, they have been told not to worry about it. And the person who loses if it succeeds has not been mentioned at all — the corpus's own discovery instrument contains no question asking who that is, in a body of commercial material that carries an explicit claim about headcount.

Three disciplines carry the session.

**Keep the populations apart.** A user cannot tell you whether it will be funded. A budget holder cannot tell you whether the workflow is right. Mixed answers from unlike people are not a contradictory market — they are several markets, and averaging them destroys the only thing they were good for.

**Mark compliments as compliments.** They are data about the conversation and not about the idea, and they may be recorded but must never appear in a conclusion. A record whose strongest evidence is an adjective has measured politeness.

**And make sure somebody in the room could have said no.** If nobody could, the test could not fail, which is [M07](/wiki/modules/M07.md)'s sentence arriving in a room full of people rather than in front of a machine.

The session's hardest half is not a technique. **[S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) refused to substitute a model's simulation of a user for a user, and recorded that the refusal was an argument rather than a finding.** Here the learner has to build the best case against that refusal, properly, and then say the one circumstance in which they would accept it and exactly what they would write down when they did. **The last clause is the assessed part**, because a substitution recorded in a test record becomes an unrecorded one by the time the record is summarised.

## Abstract

This paper defends testing a prototype with populations that cannot fund it, and treats the separation of those populations as the artefact rather than as a courtesy. The argument is that different stakeholder positions can answer categorically different questions, that aggregating them produces a customer voice belonging to nobody, and that a test whose participants are all capable of approving is a test that cannot fail. The session installs a role-classified test record, an explicit marking of compliments and generic self-description, an observation requirement inherited from [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md), and a written argument against this programme's own refusal of the simulated informant. The paper concedes that the refusal is as unwarranted as the proposal it refuses, that the handbook supplying its vocabulary reports no measured effect and no threshold, and that the corpus's defects are verified while the remedy for them is this programme's own argument.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A prototype exists and the easiest person to show it to is the person who asked for it.

That person is available, interested, already briefed, and socially disposed to be encouraging about work they commissioned. **Every property that makes them convenient makes them a bad instrument**, and the convenience is what determines who gets shown things in practice.

The deeper problem is that the corpus's own discovery instrument builds this in rather than failing into it. It asks the customer to endorse the interviewer's own solution in every block. It ships specimen answers that narrow the response before it is given. It contains no observation of any kind. It never consolidates across informants, and it ships an artefact that converts one person's single remark into a prescribed intervention. **A prototype test run on that population, by that person, with that instrument, is structurally incapable of producing a disconfirming answer** — and nothing about it looks wrong from the inside.

The session's response is to make the populations explicit, to make the compliment visible, and to require that at least one participant be capable of rejection.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** Three retrievals are load-bearing and one is affective.

[S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) supplies the leading-question repair, and the temptation returns at full strength here: with an artefact in front of you it is nearly irresistible to ask whether it would help. [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) supplies the observation requirement and the simulated-informant refusal. [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md) supplies the artefact, and the dependency is mechanical — **a rough artefact can be altered by the participant, which converts the session from an assessment of the learner's work into a joint alteration of a rough thing.** Nobody redraws a finished artefact, which is why a polished one collects compliments.

[S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md) supplies the question the corpus never asks: who loses if this succeeds.

The prior the session works against is that a test is a demonstration with feedback attached. Learners arrive intending to show the thing and hear what people think, which is a description of a demonstration.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate to high. The individual moves are simple; the load is in maintaining the role classification while several people with different positions respond to the same artefact, and in resisting the pull to reconcile them.

**3.2 Why aggregation destroys the evidence.** Mixed feedback across unlike populations may indicate not a contradictory market but one conversation with each of several different markets. **Aggregating them produces a customer voice that belongs to nobody and cannot be acted on**, and the aggregation is invisible afterwards because the resulting statement is coherent.

**3.3 Why compliments are recorded and never counted.** A compliment is socially generated and evidentially empty. Deleting it loses information about the conversation; counting it manufactures support. The handling is to record and mark.

**3.4 Why observation survives into this session.** The artefact is new but the mechanism is not: what a person says about a workflow is shorter and tidier than the workflow, and the gap is a property of expertise rather than of honesty. **Watching them use the artefact on their own case is the only way to see where they stop.**

**3.5 Why the segment must be reachable.** A population you cannot reach is a population you will substitute for, and the substitution — a colleague, a proxy, a simulation — is made silently because it is made under schedule pressure.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 3 is the instrument.** A participant says *this would be really useful* and the learner is asked what they have learned. The correct answer — that the participant was polite — is available to almost nobody before instruction, and it is much more effective as a discovered gap than as a definition.

**The record's role column is the artefact.** The review movement removes it and asks a peer to reconstruct which population each response came from. **If they cannot, the learner has written one voice**, and the separation exists only in their memory of the sessions.

**The second review question is [M07](/wiki/modules/M07.md)'s sentence.** Which response in here could have stopped the work? A record with no such response documents a test that could not fail.

**The simulated-informant argument is required and its shape is prescribed.** The learner must build the best case *against* this programme's position, and the case has real material: the practical alternative is frequently no informant rather than a real one; the refusal has no more warrant than the proposal; and a preference stated as a standard is exactly what this programme criticises the corpus for. **The learner then names the one circumstance in which they would accept the substitution and the exact words they would record.** The words are the assessed part, and the reason is stated in the session: a recorded substitution becomes an unrecorded one by the time the record is summarised, so the only durable defence is a form of words that survives summarisation.

**The design is deliberately asymmetric about this and says so.** [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) taught the refusal; [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md) attacks it. **A prohibition that has never met its strongest objection is a preference in a rule's clothing**, and the programme would rather the learner held the position than inherited it.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The demonstration to the sponsor.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* the record's role column, which makes a single-population test visible on its face, and the standard's requirement that at least one participant could have rejected the idea.

**The merged segment.** *Countermeasure:* the label-reconstruction review, and the standard's prohibition on merging the buyer's response into a finding with the user's.

**The compliment as validation.** *Countermeasure:* the marking requirement, and the standard's warning sign — your strongest quotation is an adjective.

**The coached participant.** The learner explained until agreement was reached. *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — you spoke more than they did — and the observation requirement, which is hard to satisfy while talking.

**The simulated participant.** *Countermeasure:* the required argument and the recorded circumstance. **This is a disclosure control and not a prohibition**, and the paper does not claim it prevents anything; it makes the substitution legible to a reader who was not there.

**The unreachable population, silently replaced.** *Countermeasure:* the record's *who was not tested and why* field, which is an assessed element at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md).

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. Three specification items at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md): that the test record is submitted with populations unmerged; that at least one participant could have rejected the idea, or the absence is stated as the finding; and that anyone not tested is named with the reason.

**Never assessed:** the number of participants, whether they liked it, or which position the learner took on the simulated informant.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: requiring the learner to argue against the programme's own refusal, while still recording the refusal as the programme's position, is a rigged exercise that teaches compliance dressed as debate.**

At its strongest: the learner knows what the curriculum believes. They will produce a competent argument for the other side and then decline to be persuaded by it, because the assessed artefact is the argument rather than the conclusion and nobody has ever been marked down for agreeing with their institution. The exercise generates the appearance of intellectual openness at no cost to the position, which is the most corrosive thing a curriculum can model.

**Conceded, and the design's response is partial.** The session marks the recorded circumstance and the form of words, not the position, and **a learner who concludes that the substitution is acceptable in a broad class of cases passes provided their recording discipline is specific.** That is a real degree of freedom and it is stated in the session rather than hidden. The rebuttal's weakness is that the programme's position remains on the page at [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md), and no exercise removes that asymmetry. **The declined fix is to withdraw the refusal and present the question as open.** It is declined because [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md)'s argument — that a model has access to the articulated layer of a practice and the whole case for observation is that practitioners cannot articulate their tacit steps — is a real argument that the design believes, and withdrawing a believed position to appear neutral is its own dishonesty. **The asymmetry is recorded rather than resolved.**

**Objection 2: the population separation is unaffordable, and a method that requires access to users, buyers, budget holders and blockers will simply be reduced to whoever answered the email.**

At its strongest: in most engagements an engineer gets the sponsor and, on a good day, one operational person the sponsor selected. Security will not participate, the people who lose from the change will certainly not be produced by the person driving it, and the budget holder has a diary measured in months. A standard requiring four populations produces either a fabricated record or a learner who reclassifies one participant into several roles.

**Partly conceded.** The design's answer is that the record's *who was not tested and why* field is the artefact for exactly this case, and that it is an assessed element rather than an afterthought — **a test record naming three untested populations and one tested one is a complete and honest record of a weak position**, and it produces a correspondingly modest ask at [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md). The rebuttal's limit is that this converts a methodological requirement into a documentation requirement, which is weaker than the session's own rhetoric implies. **The declined fix is to require a minimum number of populations.** It is declined because a minimum invites the reclassification the objection predicts, and because the programme asserts no thresholds anywhere. **Whether an honest record of a weak test actually produces a more modest ask is untested**, and §9 prediction 4 is the closest available check.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Before instruction, most learners will treat *this would be really useful* as evidence for the idea. *Instrument:* the item 3 answers, coded *evidence* / *politeness* / *ambiguous* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most first records will contain fewer than two distinct populations. *Instrument:* the role column, with distinctness checked by a second reader against the participants' described relationship to the work.

**Prediction 3.** In the label-reconstruction review, peers will most often misassign user responses to buyers. *Instrument:* the reconstruction errors, coded by direction.

**Prediction 4.** Learners whose records name more untested populations will make smaller asks at [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md). *Instrument:* the untested-population count against the currency and size of the [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md) ask, coded independently. **If this fails, §8 objection 2's rebuttal is unsupported and the documentation requirement is doing no work.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That stakeholder position determines which questions a person can answer, that aggregation destroys the answer, and that a test in which nobody could say no is not a test.

**What the evidence licenses.** A diagnostic vocabulary from a handbook that reports no controlled comparison and no threshold and says so; an inquiry method whose own source states it establishes no effect; and a verified inventory of one instrument's defects. **The remedy is this programme's argument. Direction and mechanism only.**

**The concession worth remembering.** This session asks you to attack a position the programme holds and marks you on the attack rather than on the conclusion — and it concedes that the asymmetry does not go away. Notice how you responded to that, because you will meet the same structure in every organisation you work for.
