## In plain terms

The sponsor liked the prototype. That is not evidence, and the reason has nothing to do with their honesty: **they will not use it.**

The person who will use it has not seen it. In several organisations you will meet, they have been told not to worry about it. And the person who loses if it succeeds has not been mentioned at all — the corpus's own discovery instrument contains no question asking who that is, in a body of commercial material that carries an explicit claim about headcount.

Three disciplines carry the session.

**Keep the populations apart.** A user cannot tell you whether it will be funded. A budget holder cannot tell you whether the workflow is right. Mixed answers from unlike people are not a contradictory market — they are several markets, and averaging them destroys the only thing they were good for.

**Mark compliments as compliments.** They are data about the conversation and not about the idea, and they may be recorded but must never appear in a conclusion. A record whose strongest evidence is an adjective has measured politeness.

**And make sure somebody in the room could have said no.** If nobody could, the test could not fail, which is [M07](/wiki/modules/M07.md)'s sentence arriving in a room full of people rather than in front of a machine.

The platform half of the session makes the last of those three concrete in a way no rule can. **Whether an artefact is invoked by an individual who chooses to, or lives in a shared surface where a group can see it, is a build decision — and it silently determines who is able to disagree with you.** Microsoft's own guidance splits agents on exactly this axis: one kind is designed for individuals and relies on user-initiated interactions, the other supports group collaboration and can act without direct user input, with a Teams channel named as the group case. A user-initiated agent samples people who already wanted it. **The person most likely to tell you the idea is wrong is the person who will never type its name**, and you did not choose that bias — you inherited it from a decision probably made on cost.

So the session publishes the artefact into one channel whose membership can be enumerated, says nothing for a day, and logs who did not use it. **The non-invokers are the most valuable population in the record and they appear in no telemetry as anything but absence.**

The session's hardest half is not a technique. **[S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) refused to substitute a model's simulation of a user for a user, and recorded that the refusal was an argument rather than a finding.** Here the learner has to build the best case against that refusal, properly, and then say the one circumstance in which they would accept it and exactly what they would write down when they did. **The last clause is the assessed part**, because a substitution recorded in a test record becomes an unrecorded one by the time the record is summarised.

## Abstract

This paper defends testing a prototype with populations that cannot fund it, and treats the separation of those populations as the artefact rather than as a courtesy. The argument is that different stakeholder positions can answer categorically different questions, that aggregating them produces a customer voice belonging to nobody, and that a test whose participants are all capable of approving is a test that cannot fail.

It then argues something the source literature does not: that **the initiation model of an agent is a recruitment instrument.** An individually-scoped, user-initiated artefact restricts the population capable of forming a view to those who chose to invoke it, which is a biased sample in a predictable direction; a shared surface inverts the exposure and introduces the conformity effects the interviewing literature warns about. Neither is correct, and the design's requirement is that the model be chosen deliberately and its exclusions written down before publication.

The session installs a role-classified test record, a declared initiation model with a named excluded person, an exposure-and-engagement log, an explicit marking of compliments and generic self-description, an observation requirement inherited from [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md), and a written argument against this programme's own refusal of the simulated informant. The paper concedes that the refusal is as unwarranted as the proposal it refuses, that the handbook supplying its vocabulary reports no measured effect and no threshold, that the corpus's defects are verified while the remedy for them is this programme's own argument, and that **the vendor documentation says nothing whatever about who a build path silences** — that inference belongs to this design.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A prototype exists and the easiest person to show it to is the person who asked for it.

That person is available, interested, already briefed, and socially disposed to be encouraging about work they commissioned. **Every property that makes them convenient makes them a bad instrument**, and the convenience is what determines who gets shown things in practice.

The deeper problem is that the corpus's own discovery instrument builds this in rather than failing into it. It asks the customer to endorse the interviewer's own solution in every block. It ships specimen answers that narrow the response before it is given. It contains no observation of any kind. It never consolidates across informants, and it ships an artefact that converts one person's single remark into a prescribed intervention. **A prototype test run on that population, by that person, with that instrument, is structurally incapable of producing a disconfirming answer** — and nothing about it looks wrong from the inside.

The session's response is to make the populations explicit, to make the compliment visible, and to require that at least one participant be capable of rejection.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** Three retrievals are load-bearing and one is affective.

[S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) supplies the leading-question repair, and the temptation returns at full strength here: with an artefact in front of you it is nearly irresistible to ask whether it would help. [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) supplies the observation requirement and the simulated-informant refusal. [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md) supplies the artefact, and the dependency is mechanical — **a rough artefact can be altered by the participant, which converts the session from an assessment of the learner's work into a joint alteration of a rough thing.** Nobody redraws a finished artefact, which is why a polished one collects compliments.

[S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md) supplies the question the corpus never asks: who loses if this succeeds.

The prior the session works against is that a test is a demonstration with feedback attached. Learners arrive intending to show the thing and hear what people think, which is a description of a demonstration.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate to high. The individual moves are simple; the load is in maintaining the role classification while several people with different positions respond to the same artefact, and in resisting the pull to reconcile them.

**3.2 Why aggregation destroys the evidence.** Mixed feedback across unlike populations may indicate not a contradictory market but one conversation with each of several different markets. **Aggregating them produces a customer voice that belongs to nobody and cannot be acted on**, and the aggregation is invisible afterwards because the resulting statement is coherent.

**3.3 Why compliments are recorded and never counted.** A compliment is socially generated and evidentially empty. Deleting it loses information about the conversation; counting it manufactures support. The handling is to record and mark.

**3.4 Why observation survives into this session.** The artefact is new but the mechanism is not: what a person says about a workflow is shorter and tidier than the workflow, and the gap is a property of expertise rather than of honesty. **Watching them use the artefact on their own case is the only way to see where they stop.**

**3.5 Why the segment must be reachable.** A population you cannot reach is a population you will substitute for, and the substitution — a colleague, a proxy, a simulation — is made silently because it is made under schedule pressure.

**3.6 Why the buyer is worse than a stranger, and it is not politeness.**

The general claim is that people supply answers whose effect they can see would please the asker — not maliciously, and the repair is to ask about past behaviour rather than future intention and about specifics rather than generalities ([research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md)). That is the reason a stranger flatters you.

**The buyer has a second and independent reason, and it does not respond to being told to be honest.** They have already committed: they argued for this internally, their name is near a budget line, and a finding that the idea is wrong is a finding about a decision they defended. The same source names the mirror of this from the interviewer's side — exposing your own emotional stake causes people to protect you even when invited to be candid — and the sponsor's stake points the same way. **A design that treats the buyer as a merely polite respondent has mistaken an interest for a manner**, and will try to fix it by asking better questions, which addresses the first mechanism and not the second.

The same source supplies the coverage rule the session enforces: users, buyers, budget owners, administrators, partners and anybody able to block adoption, with the flat statement that **seniority is not a substitute for representativeness. Limits: a practitioner handbook with no controlled comparison, no sampling method, no measured effect and no validated threshold; its conversation counts are anecdotal; and its local copy has no publisher, year or edition.**

**3.7 The initiation model is a sampling frame, and this is the paper's own argument rather than the documentation's.**

Microsoft's comparison guidance separates two agent kinds on an axis that is usually read as architectural. **Declarative agents** are documented as designed for individuals, limited to the host's orchestrator and models, without proactive capability, and relying on user-initiated interactions. **Custom engine agents** support individual use and group collaboration and can trigger actions without direct user input, with group productivity — multiple users collaborating with or benefiting from the same agent in a channel or meeting — given as a published trigger for choosing them ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)).

**Read as evidence rather than as architecture, that split decides who can disagree with you.** An artefact that must be invoked by name can only be evaluated by somebody who knows it exists and chooses to try it, and the disposition that produces early invocation is the same disposition that produces favourable responses. The bias is not introduced by the researcher and cannot be removed by better questions. **It arrives with a build-path decision, is invisible in every response you collect, and is usually chosen on cost.**

Publishing into a shared channel inverts the exposure — the roster becomes the frame, including people who did not opt in and including the person who loses — and buys a different problem, which §3.8 handles. **Neither arrangement is correct.** The design's requirement is that the model be declared before publication with a named person whose view would have been lost under the alternative, which converts an inherited bias into a stated one.

**The limits are heavy and the inference is not the documentation's.** First-party selection guidance with definitional authority; **no adoption, cost, failure-rate or outcome data**; one vendor's options compared against each other; read through search excerpts with several comparison tables truncated; **highly perishable**, and initiation boundaries are exactly the sort of thing that moves. **The documentation describes what each kind can do and says nothing about whose testimony each kind makes unobtainable.**

**3.8 What a shared channel costs, and why two sessions are run separately.**

The interviewing literature is specific that **multiple participants at once introduce conformity and power effects**, so some topics need separate sessions or separate portions of one ([research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md](/research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md)). A channel containing a manager and their reports is that condition in its strongest form, running for a whole working day, in writing, where every response is visible to everybody.

The same source makes two further points the session depends on. **Recruiting is data**: difficulty finding a population says something about your own conception of the market. And **a participant who appears wrongly recruited usually reveals ambiguity in your screener or in your category** rather than an error by the recruiter, so the response is to complete the session, learn what is available and revise. **Limits: 2013 first edition only; a practitioner synthesis with no controlled comparison of techniques and no measured causal effect.**

**3.9 Agreement conceals divergence, and the finding comes from a literature that measured it.**

The segmentation rule is usually taught as an assertion. There is a better warrant available, from a literature that studied judges rather than customers. When assessors evaluated the same evidence, they **reached similar final judgements while differing in their information-processing approaches and the reasoning behind them**; and they **relied on narrative to form the judgement, using scores only to confirm impressions already formed from prose** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)).

**Both findings transfer to a test record directly.** Two participants who both say the idea would help may mean incompatible things, and a record of ticks cannot hold that difference at all — which is an argument for narrative capture that does not rest on preference. **The transfer is this programme's argument**, and the limits are stated: two studies in medical education at one institution, one portfolio type, using think-aloud methods that by the authors' own account cannot capture fast or automatic processing.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The movements run situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**Opening item 3 is the instrument.** A participant says *this would be really useful* and the learner is asked what they have learned. The correct answer — that the participant was polite — is available to almost nobody before instruction, and it is much more effective as a discovered gap than as a definition.

**The record's role column is the artefact.** The review movement removes it and asks a peer to reconstruct which population each response came from. **If they cannot, the learner has written one voice**, and the separation exists only in their memory of the sessions.

**The initiation model is declared before publication, with a named excluded person.** The learner writes one sentence — how the artefact is invoked, therefore who is sampled, therefore whose view is unobtainable — and it is written first, because written afterwards it becomes an explanation of the sample rather than a decision about it. **The exposure log then records the non-invokers as a population rather than as missing data**, which is the movement that produces the session's most reliable surprise: the roster and the invocation list are not the same people, and the difference is legible in a day.

**Silence is required for a working day.** No announcement beyond the minimum consent notice, no walkthrough, no encouragement. **An invocation the learner prompted is not an invocation**, and the temptation to help is the same temptation the productive-failure design warns about in a different register.

**The second review question is [M07](/wiki/modules/M07.md)'s sentence.** Which response in here could have stopped the work? A record with no such response documents a test that could not fail.

**The simulated-informant argument is required and its shape is prescribed.** The learner must build the best case *against* this programme's position, and the case has real material: the practical alternative is frequently no informant rather than a real one; the refusal has no more warrant than the proposal; and a preference stated as a standard is exactly what this programme criticises the corpus for. **The learner then names the one circumstance in which they would accept the substitution and the exact words they would record.** The words are the assessed part, and the reason is stated in the session: a recorded substitution becomes an unrecorded one by the time the record is summarised, so the only durable defence is a form of words that survives summarisation.

**The design is deliberately asymmetric about this and says so.** [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) taught the refusal; [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md) attacks it. **A prohibition that has never met its strongest objection is a preference in a rule's clothing**, and the programme would rather the learner held the position than inherited it.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The demonstration to the sponsor.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* the record's role column, which makes a single-population test visible on its face, and the standard's requirement that at least one participant could have rejected the idea.

**The merged segment.** *Countermeasure:* the label-reconstruction review, and the standard's prohibition on merging the buyer's response into a finding with the user's.

**The compliment as validation.** *Countermeasure:* the marking requirement, and the standard's warning sign — your strongest quotation is an adjective.

**The coached participant.** The learner explained until agreement was reached. *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — you spoke more than they did — and the observation requirement, which is hard to satisfy while talking.

**The simulated participant.** *Countermeasure:* the required argument and the recorded circumstance. **This is a disclosure control and not a prohibition**, and the paper does not claim it prevents anything; it makes the substitution legible to a reader who was not there.

**The unreachable population, silently replaced.** *Countermeasure:* the record's *who was not tested and why* field, which is an assessed element at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md).

**The self-selecting sample.** Everybody who formed a view chose to invoke the artefact, so the record measures enthusiasm. *Countermeasure:* the declared initiation model and the exposure log, which make the absence of the non-invokers a row rather than a silence.

**The channel as a room with a manager in it.** Junior responses are given where the line manager can read them, and the conformity effect is invisible in the transcript. *Countermeasure:* the requirement to record the membership structure alongside the responses, and to run separately any session where the effect would bind. **The paper expects this countermeasure to be the weakest in the list**, because the learner has to notice a power relationship they are not part of.

**The prompted invocation.** The learner mentioned it in a stand-up, so use rose. *Countermeasure:* the silent working day, and the standard's requirement that the log record what non-invokers did instead — which is the field a prompted cohort cannot fill.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. Five specification items at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md): that the test record is submitted with populations unmerged; that the initiation model is declared with a named person it excluded; that exposure and engagement are recorded separately; that at least one participant could have rejected the idea, or the absence is stated as the finding; and that anyone not tested is named with the reason.

**Never assessed:** the number of participants, whether they liked it, which initiation model was chosen, or which position the learner took on the simulated informant.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: requiring the learner to argue against the programme's own refusal, while still recording the refusal as the programme's position, is a rigged exercise that teaches compliance dressed as debate.**

At its strongest: the learner knows what the curriculum believes. They will produce a competent argument for the other side and then decline to be persuaded by it, because the assessed artefact is the argument rather than the conclusion and nobody has ever been marked down for agreeing with their institution. The exercise generates the appearance of intellectual openness at no cost to the position, which is the most corrosive thing a curriculum can model.

**Conceded, and the design's response is partial.** The session marks the recorded circumstance and the form of words, not the position, and **a learner who concludes that the substitution is acceptable in a broad class of cases passes provided their recording discipline is specific.** That is a real degree of freedom and it is stated in the session rather than hidden. The rebuttal's weakness is that the programme's position remains on the page at [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md), and no exercise removes that asymmetry. **The declined fix is to withdraw the refusal and present the question as open.** It is declined because [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md)'s argument — that a model has access to the articulated layer of a practice and the whole case for observation is that practitioners cannot articulate their tacit steps — is a real argument that the design believes, and withdrawing a believed position to appear neutral is its own dishonesty. **The asymmetry is recorded rather than resolved.**

**Objection 2: the population separation is unaffordable, and a method that requires access to users, buyers, budget holders and blockers will simply be reduced to whoever answered the email.**

At its strongest: in most engagements an engineer gets the sponsor and, on a good day, one operational person the sponsor selected. Security will not participate, the people who lose from the change will certainly not be produced by the person driving it, and the budget holder has a diary measured in months. A standard requiring four populations produces either a fabricated record or a learner who reclassifies one participant into several roles.

**Partly conceded.** The design's answer is that the record's *who was not tested and why* field is the artefact for exactly this case, and that it is an assessed element rather than an afterthought — **a test record naming three untested populations and one tested one is a complete and honest record of a weak position**, and it produces a correspondingly modest ask at [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md). The rebuttal's limit is that this converts a methodological requirement into a documentation requirement, which is weaker than the session's own rhetoric implies. **The declined fix is to require a minimum number of populations.** It is declined because a minimum invites the reclassification the objection predicts, and because the programme asserts no thresholds anywhere. **Whether an honest record of a weak test actually produces a more modest ask is untested**, and §9 prediction 4 is the closest available check.

**Objection 3: publishing a prototype into a shared channel to see who ignores it is covert research on colleagues, and the session has dressed a consent problem as a sampling insight.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the paper finds hardest: the people in that channel did not agree to be studied. The design instructs the learner to say nothing for a working day and then to record, by name and by role, who declined to use a tool their employer put in front of them. **That is observation of non-participation without consent, in a written surface their manager can read**, and the fact that the resulting data is genuinely valuable is precisely what makes it worth being careful about. The interviewing literature that this session leans on is explicit that keeping a conversation casual must not become covert research, and that in employment settings formal consent may be both mandatory and ethically preferable. **The session quotes that source approvingly and then designs an exercise that sails close to the thing it warns against.**

**Conceded, without qualification, as a real hazard rather than a misreading.** The design's mitigations are partial and are stated as partial: the minimum consent notice at publication, the requirement that the log record roles rather than individuals wherever the finding survives the abstraction, and the standing rule that a record must state who was not tested and why. **The declined fix was to announce the study fully and ask people to try the artefact.** It was declined because a full announcement converts the exposure log into a compliance measure — people try things they have been asked to try — and the entire finding this movement exists to produce would disappear. **That is a self-serving reason and the paper knows it.** The honest position is that the design has chosen evidential value over the cleaner consent posture, has said so, and expects any real deployment of this exercise to be governed by whatever the employing organisation's research and employment rules actually require — **which this programme does not know and must not pretend to.** §9 prediction 7 is the only check the design has on whether the mitigation is doing anything.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Before instruction, most learners will treat *this would be really useful* as evidence for the idea. *Instrument:* the item 3 answers, coded *evidence* / *politeness* / *ambiguous* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most first records will contain fewer than two distinct populations. *Instrument:* the role column, with distinctness checked by a second reader against the participants' described relationship to the work.

**Prediction 3.** In the label-reconstruction review, peers will most often misassign user responses to buyers. *Instrument:* the reconstruction errors, coded by direction.

**Prediction 4.** Learners whose records name more untested populations will make smaller asks at [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md). *Instrument:* the untested-population count against the currency and size of the [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md) ask, coded independently. **If this fails, §8 objection 2's rebuttal is unsupported and the documentation requirement is doing no work.**

**Prediction 5.** Exposure and engagement will differ substantially in most cohorts, and the non-invokers will contain at least one participant whose stated reason is social rather than practical — not wanting to be seen trying it, or not regarding the surface as theirs. *Instrument:* the exposure log's `what_non_invokers_did_instead` field, coded *practical* / *social* / *unaware* / *absent* by a second reader. **If the field is empty in most records, the silent working day was not observed**, and the movement produced a roster rather than a finding.

**Prediction 6.** Most learners will declare an initiation model and be unable to name a specific person their choice excluded. *Instrument:* the declaration, coded *names a person* / *names a category* / *names nobody*. **This predicts the failure of the session's own countermeasure**: a declaration satisfiable by a category is a compliance artefact, and the design's claim that the initiation model is a recruitment instrument only bites when the excluded person has a name.

**Prediction 7.** In records where the channel contained a manager and their reports, junior responses will agree with senior ones more often than in records where the populations were seen separately. *Instrument:* the recorded membership structure against the response pairs, coded for agreement, comparing shared-surface sessions with separately-run ones. **This is the only available check on the conformity hazard §3.8 asserts and §8 objection 3 concedes**, and a null result would mean the design is imposing a cost — running separate sessions — for a problem it has not demonstrated.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That stakeholder position determines which questions a person can answer, that aggregation destroys the answer, that a test in which nobody could say no is not a test, and that **the way an artefact is invoked decides who is able to disagree with it.**

**What the evidence licenses.** A diagnostic vocabulary from a handbook that reports no controlled comparison and no threshold and says so; an inquiry method whose own source states it establishes no effect; a finding about divergent reasoning behind identical verdicts, borrowed across a large gap from medical education; a verified inventory of one instrument's defects; and a set of product distinctions from documentation that offers **no outcome data of any kind.** **The remedy is this programme's argument, and so is the whole initiation-model inference. Direction and mechanism only.**

**The concession worth remembering.** This session asks you to attack a position the programme holds and marks you on the attack rather than on the conclusion — and it concedes that the asymmetry does not go away. It also asks you to observe colleagues who did not agree to be observed, and concedes that too. **Notice how you responded to both, because you will meet the same structures in every organisation you work for**, and in most of them nobody will have written the concession down.
