# WP-020 — Your First Capacity Argument

**Seminar day:** [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) · **Module:** [M04](/wiki/modules/M04.md) · **Quarter:** [Q2](/wiki/quarters/Q2.md)
**Strands:** SY · PE · MS · ME · **Complexity class:** 2
**Platform anchor:** Foundry quota and capacity; the quota increase request as the real decision · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends assessing a quantitative claim about an unobserved future rather than a demonstrated competence about an observed past, and defends weighting the assessment on the identification of the assumption most likely to be wrong rather than on the accuracy of the number. The first argument is that engineering judgement is exercised under exactly this condition — a decision must be made before the evidence exists — and that every assessment of a settled question measures something else. The second argument is that a correct number obtained from an unexamined model is indistinguishable, at the moment of decision, from a lucky one, whereas an identified breaking assumption is the only part of a capacity argument that remains useful after the argument turns out to be wrong. The paper further defends placing a real decision-owner in the assessment, defends deferring the outcome comparison by a full quarter, and defends running the preparation with the agent present because the professional condition includes it. It concedes that the assessment's validity depends on a load change whose properties the design does not control, that weighting assumptions over accuracy creates an incentive the design has not closed, and that the weighted element is also the element an agent supplies most fluently.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Every assessment the programme has made so far concerns a settled question. A proof is correct or not; a layout is faster or not; a cliff is where it is. The student's claim can be checked against something that exists, which means the assessment measures whether the student found the right answer to a question that had one.

Professional judgement is not exercised under those conditions. The decisions that matter are made before the evidence exists: whether a system will take a load it has not seen, whether a model will hold up on data not yet collected, whether a release is safe. In each case the correct answer is unavailable at the moment of decision, and the competence being exercised is the construction and defence of a claim under that unavailability.

The programme's terminal entrustment activities are all of this kind. EA-1 is a ship decision made before the consequences are observable. EA-3 is a risk communication about something that has not happened. If the programme never assesses forward claims until year three, students meet the genre for the first time under maximal stakes.

The day's second problem is what to weight. The obvious candidate is accuracy — was the number right — and it is the wrong one for a reason that is not merely pedagogical. A capacity argument's number is a function of a model and its assumptions; if the assumptions are unexamined, a correct number is a coincidence, and the argument provides no information about what to do when reality diverges. The part that survives divergence is the identified breaking assumption: it tells the decision-owner what to watch, what would change the answer, and in which direction. That is why step 4 is weighted and the number is not.

The third problem is authenticity of consequence. An argument defended to an assessor is an examination. An argument defended to a person who owns the decision, who will act on what they hear, and who asks what they would actually ask, is a different act — and the difference is visible in how students prepare. The decision-owner's presence is the day's most important structural feature and the least reproducible.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge assumptions

- **Four cost models,** one per module day, none yet combined.
- **Nineteen recorded predictions** across the quarter, all about observable present states.
- **The S019 curve, knee and operating point,** one day old, with a rehearsed defence to a paying stakeholder at phase 7.
- **The S015 caller experience,** four weeks old, which established that a real person changes the work.
- **The S010 firewall,** demonstrated once and about to be demonstrated a second time under assessment conditions.
- **No experience of a claim about the future.** This is the first, and the design's expectation is that students will substantially under-specify their claims at phase 3 step 1.
- **Four days of platform operation and four shipped builds,** each with a prompt record, an intervention record and a frontier statement. **No experience of an assessed artefact produced with the agent**, and no experience of defending one without it.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**High in synthesis, low in novelty.** Nothing on this day is new. The difficulty is that four cost models must be applied to one situation, and determining which binds requires reasoning across all four simultaneously — the memory cliff, the scheduling policy, the queueing knee and the layout costs interact, and the binding constraint may shift as load rises.

Management:

- **No new content whatever.** The day introduces nothing.
- **Preparation is individual with factual support available.** Staff answer questions of fact and refuse questions of reasoning, which keeps the load intrinsic rather than social.
- **The required output is enumerated in six parts,** which removes the structural uncertainty and leaves the analytical difficulty.
- **The defence questions are published,** including their order.
- **Consolidation follows assessment,** so students are not holding instructional content during the assessed activity.
- **The same substrate component across five days,** so comprehension load is zero.

The load the design deliberately does not manage is the fourth defence question — if I have budget for one change, what should I spend it on — which is unprepared and requires converting an analysis into a recommendation live. That is the assessment's discriminating item.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *A claim about an unobserved future,* which admits no verification at assessment time.
- *Identifying the assumption that would most change the answer,* step 4, weighted above accuracy.
- *The unprepared fourth defence question.*
- *A defence to a real decision-owner* rather than to an examiner.
- *Extended unblocked cumulative retrieval* at gaps of one to nineteen days, with three synthesis items.
- *A sealed pretest on the assessed question,* opened after the assessment.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *New material.* None.
- *Group work in preparation.* Individual, because the assessment is individual and shared preparation would make the evidence ambiguous.
- *Unpublished defence questions.* Refused; the fourth question supplies the demand without ambush.
- *Accuracy weighting.* Explicitly refused and defended in §8.
- *An immediate outcome check.* The load change has not happened; the comparison waits a quarter and this is deliberate.
- *Withdrawing the agent from preparation.* Refused. The three unassisted occasions the programme runs are S010, S045 and S090; this is not one of them, and making it one would trade the day's forward-claim authenticity for a dependency measurement the programme already takes elsewhere.

**The platform instance, scoped in the strong form.** The day teaches the quota-versus-capacity distinction on a Foundry deployment, and the simplification must carry its safe and unsafe conditions.

*Safe.* Reasoning about why an allocation you hold may not be deployable; about why a request for more quota is not the same as a request for more capacity; about why releasing a resource is not reversible on demand; and about why a commercial commitment is a third thing again. Each of these is documented and each generalises to any rationed shared infrastructure.

*Unsafe.* Treating the distinction as **a property of this vendor's product line**. It is a property of any resource that is rationed by policy and constrained by physics, and a student who learns it as three product terms will not recognise it in a build queue, a licence pool or a hiring plan. It is equally unsafe to treat **capacity as static enough to be a planning input**: the documentation states that provisioned capacity in a region changes through the day, so a capacity check is a point-in-time observation and not a forecast, and an argument that treats one as the other has misused its strongest evidence.

The boundary is stated to students at phase 5 point 5. **The artefact element at which a student applying it outside its range would fail is step 3**, which requires the binding constraint to be identified as a quota limit, a capacity limit or a shared-pool limit — three constraints with three remedies and three lead times, and an argument that names the wrong kind fails on the third defence question regardless of its arithmetic.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **a question nobody in the room knows the answer to**.

This is rarer in education than it should be and its effect is immediate. The assessors do not know whether the system will take the load; the decision-owner does not know; the staff answering factual questions do not know. Students detect this quickly and the character of the preparation changes — there is no answer to infer, so the only available strategy is to reason.

The decision-owner's presence supplies the second and stronger motivation. Students are told, truthfully, that this person will act on what they hear, and phase 5 point 6 tells them the outcome will be checked. The combination — a real decision, a real consequence, a deferred verdict — produces preparation quality that the design has not observed from any assessed artefact.

Phase 5 point 2's spread display is the day's instructional payload and carries its own engagement: the cohort's claims, unattributed, differ by a wide margin, and point 3 traces the spread to specific assumptions rather than to competence. That is a more useful thing to learn about disagreement than any amount of instruction about uncertainty.

The competence risk is managed by the weighting itself. A student who produces a poorly-estimated number and a well-identified assumption passes, and this is stated before preparation begins.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**The judgement is stated before phase 1**, in a block read to the room: two assessors form the level from the artefact independently before the defence begins, per rater discipline rule 7; the weighted element is the assumption and not the number; nothing carries forward from S016 to S019 into this judgement and nothing carries from this judgement into Q3's first entrustment occasion; the failure conditions are named; and the error clinic is firewalled. Stating all five before the first item is opened is the design's position that **an assessment whose rules are discoverable only afterwards is a different assessment**, and it is also what makes the weighting defensible: a student who learns after preparing that the number did not matter has been assessed on a criterion they could not act on.

**Phase 1 (25 min).** The assessed question asked before preparation, sealed. Item 3 elicits the assumption-identification move before it is coached, which makes phase 8's comparison a measure of what preparation added. Item 4 predicts one's own movement. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction and is the only one in the module that enters a long-horizon series**; it is administered here and may not migrate, per instrumentation rule 8.

**Phase 2 (30 min).** Extended cumulative retrieval with three synthesis items. The memory-cliff-and-queueing-knee interaction is the module's hardest question and is answered well by very few, which is expected and is the design's honest measure of how far the integration got. **The quota-and-capacity item is added because the defence turns on it**: a student who cannot state the distinction will produce an argument that stops at the first limit it finds a number for, which is the phase 6 failure.

**Phase 3 (75 min).** Individual preparation with factual-only support and **the agent present**. Step 1's insistence on a number with a unit and a condition is the day's first discipline: students write "the system will cope" and are required to convert it. **Step 3's three-way constraint classification is the platform's contribution and is where most preparations fail** — an argument that identifies a configured allocation as the binding constraint has identified something that can be changed by asking, and has therefore not found a constraint. Step 4 is the weighted element. Step 5 — what you would measure in the first hour, with its lead time and observable window — connects the argument to S018's and S019's detectability work and is the part decision-owners value most.

**Phase 4 (60 min).** Twelve-minute defences to two assessors and the decision-owner, both assessors having already formed a level from the artefact. The published questions establish the shape; the fourth is unprepared and requires a recommendation rather than an analysis, and its options are real and priced. **The third question is targeted at an output the student attributed to the agent**, which costs no time and converts ownership from an assessor's impression into a checkable element. Level movement during the defence is recorded, because a level that never moves would mean the defence is not doing the work the design claims for it.

**Phase 5 (40 min).** Consolidation after assessment. Point 1's four parts are the retained structure. Point 2's spread display is the central device — the cohort sees that competent people using the same data reached materially different conclusions. Point 4 has the decision-owner state what they needed, which is usually not what students prepared. Point 5 consolidates the quota-capacity-reservation distinction after the defence has made students want it. Point 7 announces both deferred checks.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** The decision-owner opens with their own wrong capacity decision, including what they had said beforehand about what would make them wrong. Having the disclosure come from the person whose role students have just occupied is the design's strongest available demonstration that the weighted element is the one professionals also treat as central. **The agent case is an argument that stopped at the configured allocation** — sound model, correct arithmetic, consistent units, wrong constraint — and is chosen because it is the day's own weighted element failing in a form where everything checkable is correct. Assessors absent; firewall applies; second demonstration under assessment conditions.

**Phase 7 (25 min).** Assumptions compared rather than conclusions. Where two students identified different breaking assumptions, determining whether both are load-bearing is genuine analytical work and frequently reveals that one dominates.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Sealed answers opened, then the frontier prediction read against the frontier statement the same student wrote five hours later about the same tool on the same task. The S010 instrument re-administered identically — the psychological-safety series' second point. The recorded expectation about the load change is what the Q3 report is compared against.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with capacity-planning experience will produce a well-structured argument quickly. Predicted failure: they produce a confident claim with a narrow confidence statement, because professional practice rewards decisiveness, and their step 4 assumption is chosen for defensibility rather than for load-bearingness. Countermeasure: the third defence question — what would have to be true for you to be wrong — is asked by an assessor who has read their step 4, and a defensible-but-minor assumption is visible immediately. Phase 7's comparison also exposes it when a peer identifies something larger.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** A student who struggled through M04 faces a synthesis of four models they hold shakily. Countermeasures: the enumerated output structure; factual staff support; published questions; and the weighting, which means a poorly-estimated number is survivable. Residual risk: they may not identify a binding constraint at all, which is a specification failure. Tokens are available and resubmission is a written argument without a second defence, which the design accepts as a weaker but adequate remedy.

**Phase 2's synthesis items are too hard.** Expected. The final item is answered well by very few and the design does not treat this as a failure — it is the honest measure of a module that ran four consecutive high-load days. The instructor supplies one worked interaction rather than leaving it open.

**Affective failure.** Two.

The first is **preparation panic**: seventy-five minutes, an unfamiliar genre, and an assessment. Countermeasure: the enumerated structure means no student spends time deciding what to produce, and staff circulate to answer factual questions, which keeps students moving.

The second and more consequential is **the defence experienced as an interrogation**. Twelve minutes, five questions, two assessors and a decision-owner is an intense format in the second quarter. Countermeasures: the questions are published; the decision-owner is briefed to ask the fourth question as a genuine request rather than as a test; and the firewalled clinic follows immediately, opened by the decision-owner's own failure, which is the strongest available signal that the defence was not an examination of the student's worth. Whether twelve minutes with three people is too much for week twenty is the design's principal open question about this day.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by S026 (what are you actually measuring, where the capacity argument's model becomes a measurement question), S029 (designing an instrument for a question), S030 (the first measurement you are asked to defend — the direct structural successor), S039 (requirements as negotiated constraint), S064 (capacity is not quota, which is this day at class 5 with money and contracts attached) and S065 (the cost argument you present to someone who pays).

S064 and S065 at roughly five quarters are the long-range targets and the correspondence is close enough that the design treats S020 as their rehearsal. S030 at ten weeks is the nearer structural repeat, with measurement rather than capacity as the subject.

The four-part structure at point 1 is the retained content and is the most reused single structure in the programme after the seminar pattern itself.

**The two long-horizon series and the days that must discharge them.** This day records a capacity series and a frontier series, neither resolved on it, and the deferral rule requires each forward day to be named and checked for whether it can actually carry the claim.

- **Q3's report** resolves both. It can carry them: the load change will have happened, its outcome is measurable from the substrate's own records, and the frontier statements are already written down. This is the only unconditional link of the four.
- **[S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md)** retrieves both as a worked instance. It can carry the capacity series; whether it can carry the frontier series depends on its own artefact carrying an element that consumes the result, and **that element does not yet exist and must be added**. It is recorded here as a commitment against S040 rather than as a discharged deferral.
- **[S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)** retrieves both at the mid-programme point, where the cohort's frontier map is large enough to compare against. Same condition, same commitment.
- **[S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md)** is the terminal reading and is the only day that can carry the frontier series properly, **because it is one of the programme's three unassisted occasions.** A frontier prediction made in week twenty can be read there against work the same student produced without the instrument. No other day in the programme offers that comparison, which is why the terminal reading is placed there and not at a more convenient point.

The reader of both series is named per instrumentation rule 9: the cohort at the Q3 report, and the following year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md).

Own schedule: the inter-quarter spaced sets, then the Q3 outcome report.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**Assessment-bearing, no entrustment judgement.**

Two outputs: the module artefact, and the capacity argument with its defence. Both are specification decisions.

**The weighted element is step 4, and the stated rule is that a correct number with no identified breaking assumption does not pass while a wrong number with a well-identified one does.** This inverts the ordinary expectation and is stated before preparation.

**The artefact carries four further checked elements:** the prompt record, the intervention record, the frontier statement and the origin attribution. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that its own checks could have detected an intervention-worthy error. The origin attribution is a factual record and is not itself a judgement: an argument assembled largely from agent output and understood completely is a good argument, and one assembled by hand and understood poorly is not. Its function is to give the third defence question a target, which is the §8 objection-3 fix.

**Levels are formed from the artefact by two assessors independently before the defence**, per rater discipline rule 7, and defence movement is recorded. **No carry-forward** in either direction. The failure conditions are named on the day's page and are read to the room before phase 1.

Dispositions: **integrity**, in whether step 6's confidence statement matches the evidence — specifically, whether a student whose model rests on a shaky assumption says so.

Programme instrumentation: **the phase 1 sealed answers against post-preparation claims** measure what preparation added, separating movement in the answer from movement in the assumption; the phase 5 spread is the cohort disagreement baseline, re-measured at S030 and S050; the S010 instrument's second administration is the psychological-safety series' second point; **the load-change outcome comparison in Q3 is the programme's first long-horizon prediction adjudication**; and **the frontier series is the second, reported alongside it and never combined with it.**

The frontier series is a sample and this paper says so, per instrumentation rule 3: one cohort, one substrate, one model family, one task genre, over five consecutive days chosen by staff to contain known agent failure modes. It describes this module's tasks. It does not describe the agent, and any use of it as a general capability map would be the error the day exists to prevent.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: weighting assumptions over accuracy creates an incentive to be vague.**

At its strongest: a student who states an imprecise claim with wide bounds and identifies a large obvious assumption is difficult to fault, and has done less work than a student who commits to a narrow claim and identifies a subtle assumption. The rule as stated — wrong number with good assumption passes, right number with no assumption fails — does not distinguish these. Worse, it rewards the professional posture that engineering education should most resist: the hedged claim that cannot be wrong because it never said anything.

The rebuttal is that step 1's specification is designed against exactly this. A claim must be a number, with a unit and a condition; "the system will cope" is rejected and "latency will be somewhere between fifty and five thousand milliseconds" is a number and is challengeable at the second and third defence questions, both of which press on the model rather than on the claim. A student who hedges has not thereby produced a defensible argument, because the defence asks which constraint binds and how they know.

The concession is that this protection is entirely in the defence rather than in the specification. The written specification would pass a hedged claim with a good assumption; only the oral questioning catches it, which means the protection depends on assessor skill and is not written down anywhere as a criterion. The fix is straightforward — add a specification clause requiring the claim's interval to be narrow enough that at least one of the four cost models binds within it — and it is not made. Given that the programme's own assessment material warns that assessors reason from narrative and that unstated criteria drift, leaving this protection unwritten is a known-risk omission rather than an oversight.

**Objection 2: the assessment's validity depends on a load change the design does not control.**

At its strongest: the entire day is built around a real forthcoming load change, and the design specifies nothing about it. Its magnitude, its predictability, which constraint it stresses and whether it happens at all are properties of the substrate's operational life, not of the curriculum. A change that stresses only the queueing constraint makes three of the module's four cost models irrelevant on the assessed day. A change that is trivially within capacity makes the argument vacuous. A change that is postponed removes the Q3 outcome comparison entirely, which is the day's most distinctive instrument. And across cohorts the changes will differ, so the assessment is not comparable year to year.

This objection the design does not fully answer, and it is the same class of problem as WP-015's caller dependency — the day's most valuable property is also its least controllable. Three partial mitigations exist. Staff select the change from among those available and can defer the day within a two-week window. The phase 2 synthesis items and the four-part structure at point 1 are assessed regardless of which constraint binds. And the specification's weighted element is the assumption, which is present whatever the change stresses.

None addresses comparability across cohorts, and the design does not claim it. The programme's assessment position is that judgements are specification decisions against stated criteria rather than comparative rankings, which makes cross-cohort comparability less load-bearing than it would be under a graded scheme — but "less load-bearing" is not "irrelevant," and an external reviewer asking whether two cohorts were assessed equivalently would not receive a satisfactory answer.

The unmade improvement is to specify a *minimum* profile the selected change must have — that it must stress at least two of the four models, and that its magnitude must be within a stated factor of current capacity — so that the variation is bounded rather than open. That is specifiable, it would cost nothing, and its absence means the day's difficulty is set by operational accident.

**Objection 3: the weighted element is the one the agent supplies most fluently, and the assessment cannot see that it did.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn and it bites on the assessment's centre.

At its strongest: the day weights the identification of the breaking assumption above everything else, on the argument that it is the part of a capacity argument that survives the argument being wrong. That argument is sound. What has changed is that **a competent agent will produce a plausible breaking assumption on request, every time, in seconds.** Ask it what assumption would most change this answer and it will name one, with a direction and a rough magnitude, in the form the specification asks for. The student who accepts it has not corrected anything, so **nothing appears in the intervention record** — acceptance leaves no trace where correction does. The prompt record would show the request, but reading it against the artefact is not a written criterion and depends on an assessor thinking to do it. The design has therefore weighted, above all other elements, the element most easily outsourced and least easily detected as outsourced.

And the deeper form: the preparation is a joint product of a student and an agent, and the defence is not. A student who can defend a model they did not construct passes; one who constructed a good model and defends it badly fails. The programme's claim is that a graduate can architect a system by prompting **and** say what it does badly. This day assesses the second half well and the first half not at all, while presenting itself as an assessment of the argument.

The rebuttal is partial and honest about being so. The third and fourth defence questions press on the model rather than on the claim, and a student who accepted an assumption without owning it will be visible under a competent assessor within a minute. But that protection lives entirely in the oral defence and is not written as a criterion — **which is the second time in this paper that the same defect has been recorded**, objection 1 having found it for hedged claims. Two instances of "the protection exists only in the defence" is a finding about the day rather than about two elements: this assessment's written specification is systematically weaker than its oral practice, and the programme's own assessment material warns that unstated criteria drift.

**The cheap fix is one clause plus a targeting rule, and it is made.** The artefact now carries an **origin attribution** across all six required outputs — which of them originated with the student and which with the agent — and the third defence question is asked about one the student attributed to the agent. The attribution costs a line each, it is not a confession because the day states plainly that an argument assembled from agent output and understood completely is a good argument, and it converts the assessor's unwritten habit into a written element. It also does the thing the intervention record cannot: **it makes acceptance visible, where the intervention record only makes correction visible.**

What is conceded and not fixed is that attribution is self-reported and unverifiable. A student who attributes everything to themselves cannot be contradicted, and the design's only response is that the third question will be asked regardless and that the resulting answer is the evidence. That is weaker than the fix it accompanies, and it is the residue the design accepts rather than the problem it solves.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A large majority of phase 1 item 1 answers will be qualitative rather than numeric, and a large majority of phase 3 step 1 claims will require at least one revision before meeting the number-unit-condition specification. *Instrument:* phase 1 responses coded for numeric content, and step 1 revision counts.

**Prediction 2.** The assumption identified at phase 1 item 3 will differ from the one identified at phase 3 step 4 for a majority of students, and the phase 3 assumption will be rated more load-bearing. *Instrument:* blind-rated load-bearingness of both, paired.

**Prediction 3.** Performance on the fourth defence question — the unprepared budget recommendation — will correlate with step 5 quality more strongly than with step 2 quality. *Instrument:* defence ratings against blind-rated step 2 and step 5. **The claim is that students who thought about what to measure can recommend, and students who only built a model cannot.**

**Prediction 4.** In Q3, the cohort's claims will be biased optimistic relative to the observed outcome. *Instrument:* recorded claims against the measured post-change behaviour. **This is the programme's first long-horizon adjudication; a pessimistic or unbiased result would be a finding against the design's assumption that capacity intuition errs toward optimism.**

**Prediction 5.** A majority of students will attribute their step 4 breaking assumption at least partly to the agent, and their performance on the targeted third defence question will be lower for attributed outputs than for their own. *Instrument:* the origin attribution against blind-rated third-question responses, paired within student. **This is the §8 objection-3 estimate. If performance is equal across the two, either students own what they accepted or the question is not discriminating, and the design cannot distinguish these without a second targeting.** If a large majority attribute nothing to the agent while their prompt records show the assumption was requested, the attribution element has failed and the day is back to the unwritten protection objection 3 was raised about.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That the competence worth assessing is a claim about a future nobody can check yet, and that the part of a capacity argument which survives being wrong is not the number but the assumption you named as the one most likely to break it.

**What the evidence licenses.** The capacity-argument structure and the assessment posture — specification decisions rather than rankings, the firewall, the warning that assessors reason from narrative and that unstated criteria drift — sit in **class 1**, grounded in notes recorded here. The quota-and-capacity material sits in **class 4**, read on a stated date, with three silences: no schedule for how regional capacity moves through the day, no turnaround for a quota-increase request, and no published probability that one succeeds. **The inversion rule itself — a wrong number with a good assumption passes, a right number with none fails — is class 3**, this design's own reasoning, untested. You may say quota and capacity are different things. You may not say how likely you are to obtain either.

**What to do with it.**

- **Make the claim a number, with a unit and a condition.** "The system will cope" is not a claim and cannot be wrong.
- **Classify the constraint before you price the remedy.** A quota limit, a capacity limit and a shared-pool limit carry three remedies and three lead times, and naming the wrong kind fails the defence whatever the arithmetic says.
- **Say what you would measure in the first hour, with its lead time.** That is the part a decision-owner actually wants from you.
- **Provenance is not correctness.** The [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md) separates where an output came from and whether it is right; this day states plainly that an argument assembled from agent output and understood completely is a good argument, so the attribution exists to be questioned rather than confessed.

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes that the day weights, above everything else, the one element a competent agent supplies fluently on request — and that **acceptance leaves no trace where correction does**, so nothing appears in the intervention record when you simply take what you were given. **Hold the design to that.** The origin attribution is the fix, it is self-reported, it is unverifiable, and the paper records that as residue it accepts rather than as a problem it has solved.
