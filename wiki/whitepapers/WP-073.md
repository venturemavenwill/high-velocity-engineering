# WP-073 — The Evaluation Harness They Inherit

**Seminar day:** [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) · **Module:** [M15](/wiki/modules/M15.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 5
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Agent Framework evaluations, and conversation-level evaluation drawn from production traces inside a bounded lookback under Microsoft Foundry Control Plane roles — a harness the recipient cannot run on their own credentials has not been transferred, whatever the handover document says

> ## Release rule
>
> **§§4, 5 and 9 are post-session reading.** §4 explains why the session requires the learner to fail at reconstructing their own selection rule, and why the supplied situation is built so that the honest obligation is uncomfortable. §5 names the failure the session cannot detect. §9 states the predicted cohort behaviour on the opening items. §§1, 2, 3, 6, 7 and 8 spoil nothing.
>

> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Harness** | The apparatus that decides whether the system still performs acceptably: dataset, criteria, thresholds, run procedure, interpretation rule |
> | **Provenance line** | The recorded reason a threshold has the value it has, sufficient for somebody else to decide whether the reason has expired |
> | **Obligation** | The act a result compels, held by a named role with stated authority. Distinct from the act it merely permits |
> | **Split strategy** | The rule turning a multi-turn conversation into a query and a response for scoring. Determines what a score means |
> | **Deterministic / judged tier** | A check that runs without a model call, versus one scored by a model. They differ in what the runner must hold |
> | **Advisory / authoritative gate** | The same check with different consequences depending on where it runs. Indistinguishable in a green summary |
> | **Criteria drift** | The circularity by which people need criteria to grade outputs and need to grade outputs to discover their criteria |
> | **Model decay** | Degradation of a deployed model's usefulness as the world moves away from its training data |

## In plain terms

The evaluation harness is the only instrument the customer will have that can tell them the system has stopped working properly. It is also the item on the manifest least likely to survive, and it fails in a way that is almost invisible: it keeps running, keeps producing results, and nobody acts on any of them.

Four things decay independently, and only one of them is loud.

**The recipient cannot start it.** The checks that carry most of the harness's apparent value are model-judged, which means they need a subscription, a quota and a credential. Yours. The handover document said the harness was transferred and it was — as a readable artefact, in the same sense a locked building is transferred when you hand over the address.

**The dataset stops resembling what the system now sees**, so the harness passes reliably while measuring something that no longer happens.

**The thresholds lose their reasons**, so nobody can revise them, because nobody can tell whether the reason has expired. An inherited threshold with no provenance can be obeyed or ignored. It cannot be changed.

**The obligation is never assigned**, so a failing result reaches a person with no authority and stops there.

The last is the one this session is built around, and the source corpus supplies an unusually pure specimen — not of a bad evaluation harness, but of a well-built instrument that produces nothing. Its value framework instructs its reader to establish a baseline and never shows one being established; catalogues metric definitions and reports none measured; ships model workbooks with no populated values. **It is a number-generating apparatus containing no numbers**, and the reason is not that the apparatus is defective. **It is that no party is obliged by its output.**

The cost model travels in the same class and for the same reason, and the session treats them together.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching evaluation transfer as the transfer of an operable instrument with an obligation attached, rather than as the transfer of an artefact.

The argument runs in four parts. **First**, a harness fails after handover in four independent ways — the recipient cannot start it, the dataset drifts, threshold provenance is lost, and no obligation is assigned — of which only the first is observable in a single session and only by observation. **Second**, the platform's evaluation surface makes the first failure architectural rather than accidental: it offers a deterministic tier that runs anywhere and a model-judged tier that requires a subscription and a quota, and a harness composed mainly of the second is unrunnable by a recipient who holds neither. **Third**, the same documentation makes a set of decisions inheritable only if they are written down — the conversation split strategy, which the documentation states outright determines what is being evaluated; the repetitions setting, which exists because one sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement; and the threshold, which is policy separable from measurement. **Fourth**, the criteria a harness embodies are partly a record of the outputs its authors happened to observe, which the human-computer-interaction literature names as criteria drift and describes as a genuine circularity, so a record presenting its criteria as fixed transfers a snapshot as a standard.

The session produces a seven-part harness handover record and a run record produced by the recipient on the recipient's own machine.

The paper concedes that a departing supplier cannot create an obligation inside somebody else's organisation; that requiring reconstruction of an unrecorded selection rule may teach confident retrospective invention; that the tier-and-authority marking is invented; and that no source behind this programme reports whether any delivered evaluation apparatus was ever operated by a customer.

**No evaluator's agreement with human judgement is published by any vendor known to this programme, and none is asserted anywhere in this paper.**

## 1. The problem this session must solve

At the end of an engagement the harness works, and its working conceals five things.

**It runs on your access.** The credential, the subscription, the quota, the network path and the machine are yours, and none of them presented itself as part of the harness because none of them was ever absent.

The dataset was assembled by somebody who understood what they were selecting for and did not write it down, because at the time the selection was a decision rather than a fact. The thresholds were set by tuning, which is a legitimate way to set a threshold and a poor way to justify one. The interpretation is performed by a person who can tell a degraded system from a moved world because they watched both happen. And the obligation is discharged informally by the delivery team, who notice a bad result and simply act, so nobody has ever had to ask who is obliged.

All five are properties of the team rather than of the harness, and all five leave with the team. **The instrument transfers perfectly and its operation does not transfer at all.**

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session assumes measurement work from Stage 7 — a baseline, a stated attribution position, and the named party responsible for establishing that an observed change was caused by the system — and a manifest from [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md) on which the harness and the cost model appear as lines with owners and acceptance conditions.

It also assumes the learner has met, at [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md), the failure mode of an apparatus that generates figures while asserting nothing, and has audited the same pinned corpus for machinery against results. **This session is where that failure mode acquires a countermeasure**, and the countermeasure is not better machinery.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Operability is prior to everything else, and it is the only failure a session can observe.**

An instrument transfers when somebody else can run it. This is the [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md) criterion extended by one clause: an object transfers if a stranger can find it without asking anyone **and can operate it without becoming you**.

The distinction is stated most precisely in the change-management literature, where **ability** is "turning knowledge into action", achieved "when a person or group has the demonstrated capability to implement the change at the required performance levels", and where the named barriers to it include available time and available support resources ([research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md)). Both barriers bite at a harness handover: nobody's time was allocated to receiving it, and the support arrangement ends the day the engagement does.

**Its limits travel with every use.** A trademarked practitioner model published by the firm that sells its training; ordering argued from the meanings of the words; every quantitative claim tracing to the publisher's own practitioner surveys; **no independent evaluation of the model anywhere in the book.** The distinction is taken. Nothing else is.

**3.2 The tier split is where operability is decided, and the documentation makes it visible.**

The framework's two-tier design is its main structural idea. `LocalEvaluator` "runs checks locally without API calls — ideal for inner-loop development, CI smoke tests, and fast iteration"; the cloud tier connects "for cloud-based LLM-as-judge evaluation", and the two can be mixed in a single run ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)).

The documentation presents this as a cost-and-speed gradient, which it is. **This session reads it as an inheritance gradient**, which the documentation does not say: a deterministic check survives the loss of a subscription and a judged check does not. The catalogue contains exactly one agent-behaviour evaluator that takes an expected action sequence and requires no model parameter, and it is therefore the only check in that group whose result does not depend on a judge — a small fact with a large consequence for a recipient with no quota.

**3.3 Three decisions become uninheritable unless written down.**

**The split strategy.** The documentation is unambiguous: "Multi-turn conversations must be split into query and response halves for evaluation. How you split determines *what you're evaluating*." Three strategies are supplied and a custom one is permitted. **An agent that recovers from a bad first attempt scores well under one and badly under another, and neither number is wrong.** A team that never chose deliberately is reporting an answer to a question it did not ask, and a recipient inheriting the number inherits the unasked question.

**Repetitions.** They exist "to detect non-deterministic behavior" — which is the platform conceding that one sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement. A harness running each case once hands the recipient an instrument that will read ordinary variation as regression on their first bad morning.

**The threshold.** Output is pass or fail with a `reason`, with a numeric score for scaled evaluators, and **the threshold is a policy choice separable from the measurement**: moving it changes the pass rate without changing the system. The `reason` field is what makes the judgement auditable rather than merely numeric, and is the part a handover record should tell the recipient to read.

One further mechanism decides whether the harness stays attached to reality: **stored responses can be evaluated "without re-running the agent"**. A harness fed from production traces tests what happened; one fed from a synthetic set tests what its authors thought to ask.

**The documentation's limits and its silence.** Read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, with structure reconstructed; most agent evaluators are preview; one consulted page is an explicitly superseded variant. **It publishes no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data and no rationale for any default threshold**, and most of these evaluators are model-judged. That bounds what may be claimed and is not evidence that the evaluators are poor.

**3.4 The judge inherits the problems of what it judges, and the criteria will not hold still.**

Using a model to grade a model **relocates the trust problem rather than solving it**: generated evaluators "simply inherit all the problems of the LLMs they evaluate, requiring further human validation" ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md)). The repair is alignment against a human-graded subset, and it requires a person on the recipient's side.

The harder finding is **criteria drift** — "users need criteria to grade outputs, but grading outputs helps users define criteria" — a genuine circularity rather than a workflow inefficiency, with the sharper form that some criteria appear dependent on the particular outputs observed, which the authors say "raises serious questions for approaches that assume the independence of evaluation from observation of model outputs."

**The consequence for a handover is uncomfortable and is the reason the record must date its criteria: your criteria are partly a record of what you happened to see.** The recipient will see different outputs and their criteria will drift away from yours, correctly.

**Its limits:** the drift evidence is qualitative, establishing that the phenomenon is real rather than how prevalent; the user study is small by construction; the tool is a research prototype; it addresses criteria alignment and says nothing about statistical validity.

**3.5 The set will stop testing the system, and the vocabulary for saying so exists.**

**Data drift** moves the input distribution; **concept drift** invalidates the mapping between inputs and labels; **model decay** is the resulting loss of usefulness ([research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md)). **The rate at which ground truth changes determines the labelling strategy** — slow change permits a curated set, fast change forces direct feedback or weak supervision — and a scheduled retraining trigger "assumes a steady rate of change in the world which frequently does not hold", so a cadence in a handover record must carry the assumption it rests on. Separately, aggregate metrics conceal subgroup failure and the choice of slices is a domain question, which means **the recipient is better placed than you to choose slices and worse placed to know that they should.**

**Its limits:** a practitioner survey from a commercial publisher, not research; no study design, no comparison group; the toolchain is the authors' employer's; every number is a property of a particular system and none is reproduced here.

**3.6 A gate's authority is a property of where it runs, and a green summary hides it.**

An openly published agent configuration corpus documents that **a check can be advisory at pull-request tier while the nightly run treats the same check as authoritative**, and that its regex-graded behavioural checks test **output shape and not correctness** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)). It also holds substantial evaluation machinery and no committed result corpus, with the results storage an empty placeholder.

**A recipient reading a green summary cannot tell whether the check that passed could have stopped anything.** If the record does not mark it, they will treat every check as decoration or every check as gospel, and both are wrong.

**Its limits are extreme:** perishability total on paths, counts and versions; host and model dependence absolute; benefit vocabulary its own machinery does not support; the revision pinned is not all-green.

**3.7 Why the obligation rather than the instrument, and the pedagogical frame.**

An instrument produces information. Information changes behaviour only where somebody is required to respond to it and has the authority to do so. The two conditions are separable and both are commonly absent: the person who reads the result cannot act, and the person who can act does not read it. **This is an argument about organisations, not a finding**, and it is the session's load-bearing claim.

The change-management literature supplies the closest thing to a supporting case, and it is a failure. A bank deployed a quality programme well and disbanded the implementation team; within a year the programme was dead, and the diagnosis was that the team assumed managing change is essentially completing activities in a project plan. The same source names accountability systems as the strongest form of reinforcement and requires reinforcement to be free of countervailing negative consequences. **Read that last condition against a harness whose bad result creates work for its reader and no consequence for anyone else: the incentive is to stop reading.** No amount of documentation fixes that. An obligation attached to a role with authority does, or nothing does.

The opening items are an unscaffolded generation phase, and exercise 3 is a comparison instruction rather than an invitation, because side-by-side presentation without an instruction to compare has been shown to achieve nothing ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). **School, university and laboratory evidence; no adult-professional study in its reference list; no magnitude asserted.**

**3.8 The input is a second object with a second owner, which the seven-part record did not previously separate.**

The record's first part asks for the dataset and its selection rule. That framing quietly assumes the set is a thing the delivery team made and can hand over. **Where the set is drawn from production traffic, it is not — it is a query against a store owned by somebody else, and three separate properties of that arrangement each break the transfer in a different way.**

**The sampler.** A cloud AI platform offers conversation-level evaluation drawing from the connected telemetry store by agent name and version, under one of two sampling strategies: uniform draw, or a service-managed heuristic biased towards conversations judged interesting ([research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-agent-build-and-runtime.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-agent-build-and-runtime.md)). **The heuristic's internals are published nowhere**, so a set drawn under it cannot be described as a sample of production traffic — only as a sample of what an undisclosed rule selected. This is the [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) apparatus problem with the opaque step relocated from the judge to the sampler, and it is a stronger instance, because the judge at least has a published catalogue.

**The window.** The lookback is bounded, so a set defined as recent traffic is a moving window. That is the right instrument for detecting change in the world and the wrong one for establishing a baseline, and the two are routinely reported as one number. §4 therefore requires the set to be **materialised** — drawn once, committed — and the moving query kept beside it as a separate instrument answering a separate question. **That requirement is this design's invention** and is not a practice the documentation proposes.

**The retention.** The platform states no retention period of its own for traces; there is a portal display window, which is a display window, and beneath it retention follows the configuration of a monitoring resource somebody must own. **The evaluation set therefore has an undefended upstream dependency on a cost decision made by people who have never heard of the harness**, and its failure mode is silent: a shorter history, a number, no error. This is the clearest available case for [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md)'s claim that a dated obligation belongs in a register rather than in a person.

**What the recipient must hold turns out to be two lists rather than one.** Running the checks needs a credential for the judged tier. *Reproducing the set* needs a reader role on the telemetry workspace, a further privileged role where the underlying tables are protected, and access to somebody holding the platform role that may re-enable tracing if the connection is lost ([research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-control-plane-and-governance.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-control-plane-and-governance.md)). Separately, **permissions held by the project identity do not carry across when an agent is published**, so a harness that passed before publication and fails after it has found an unreassigned permission and will report it as a quality regression.

**One consequence of that grant is a decision rather than a step, and §4 requires it to be recorded as one.** The documentation states that trace data may contain personal data and customer content and is readable by anyone holding the telemetry reader role. **Granting the recipient the ability to reproduce the set grants them the ability to read what customers typed**, and a handover that performs that grant without naming it has moved a privacy boundary silently.

**The limits are the same shape as everywhere else in this paper and §8 concedes them.** Definitional authority only. **No measure of tracing completeness is published by any source read** — a trace is presented as a record and never as a sample — so the set inherits an unbounded completeness, and this paper asserts nothing about its size. The heuristic sampler is undescribed rather than described badly. And the platform publishes numeric guidance in quantity with no study, curve or source behind any of it, which is why §3.8 names windows, ceilings and caps as mechanisms and reproduces no value from any of them.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The seven parts are supplied because the session's difficulty is not in listing them.** It is in parts three, six and seven: producing a provenance line for a threshold set during tuning, naming a person who is obliged rather than a person who is interested, and watching the recipient fail to start something you have run daily for months.

**4.2 The workflow is ordered so that operability is discovered before the record is written.** Steps 1 and 2 split the harness by what a runner must hold, before any provenance work. A learner who writes the record first will write it about the harness they operate, and step 6 will then expose that the document describes an instrument the recipient cannot start — which is a better outcome than not knowing, and a worse one than having designed for it.

**4.3 The supplied situation is built so that the honest answer to part six is uncomfortable.** The person who would notice a degradation holds no budget, no change authority and no subscription access. The people who could act are a technology function that did not want the system and a managed-service supplier that has attended nothing. **There is no arrangement of these facts that produces a tidy obligation, and a learner who produces one has invented a mitigation to make the document look finished.** The session states in advance that recording the gap is the passing answer, which converts a trap into a standard, and the `authority_held: false` field exists so that the gap has somewhere to be written.

**4.4 The reconstruction is required to fail.** The selection rule for the evaluation set was never recorded. Asking the learner to reconstruct it puts them in the position their recipient will occupy permanently, and the correct output is a stated uncertainty rather than a confident description. **A stated uncertainty about how a set was built is more useful to a recipient than a confident description that is wrong**, because the first can be tested and the second will be trusted. §8 concedes what this risks.

**4.5 What the platform workflow makes observable.** Step 6: the recipient runs it, on their machine, with their credentials, and the learner stays silent. **There is no argument about whether a harness has transferred once somebody else has failed to start it**, and no amount of documentation quality substitutes for the observation. The gap between *I have given you this* and *you can run this* is invisible from both sides until somebody tries.

**4.6 The review movement supplies the awkward case deliberately.** A result that is worse but not badly worse, close to the assessor's sampling window, is where an interpretation rule either resolves or reveals that it only ever handled clear cases.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The unrunnable inheritance.** Every check is judged and the recipient has no subscription. *Countermeasure:* workflow steps 1 and 2 force the tier audit before the record is written, and step 6 tests it. **This is the one failure in this session with a countermeasure that actually detects it.**

**The invented provenance.** A learner produces a plausible reason for a threshold they set by tuning. *Countermeasure:* none reliable. The session asks for stated uncertainty and **cannot distinguish an honest reconstruction from a confident invention**, which §8 names as the design's own risk.

**The tidy obligation.** The gap is closed by assigning the obligation to the willing under-authorised party. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires the authority to be stated alongside the role, and the supplied situation makes the mismatch explicit on the page.

**The unstated split.** The strategy was a default, so the number answers an unasked question. *Countermeasure:* the record has a field for it, which is a weak countermeasure — a learner can fill a field with a default's name and learn nothing. Exercise 4 is the real one, because it requires the learner to state what would change.

**The frozen set left frozen.** *Countermeasure:* the standard requires the selection rule to be stated well enough for somebody else to extend the set without changing what it measures.

**The interpretation rule that only handles clear cases.** *Countermeasure:* the review movement's awkward case.

**The uniform green.** Advisory and authoritative checks look identical in a summary. *Countermeasure:* the record's authority field. **The countermeasure marks the record and not the dashboard**, and the recipient will read the dashboard.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. Three specification items at [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md): every transferred threshold carries a provenance line; the harness record names the obligation a failing result creates together with the authority the obliged party holds; and a run record exists showing the recipient started the harness themselves.

**None of these assesses the quality of the evaluation.** Whether the harness measures the right thing was settled in Stage 7 and is not reopened here; what is checked is whether it can be operated and acted on by somebody else.

**Nothing else is assessed.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: requiring a reconstruction of an unrecorded selection rule teaches confident retrospective invention, which is worse than the gap it fills.**

At its strongest: the learner cannot know why the set was assembled as it was, the session rewards producing something, and a plausible reconstruction written by a departing engineer will be read by the recipient as documentation rather than as a guess. The programme would then have manufactured exactly the artefact it warns about elsewhere — an unattributed statement that functions as a norm because nobody can trace it.

**This is conceded.** The mitigation is thin: the session asks for the uncertainty to be stated and marks the statement rather than the reconstruction. **The declined fix was to forbid reconstruction entirely** and require the record to say only that the rule is unknown. It was rejected because a recipient told nothing about how the set was built cannot extend it and will replace it, which is a worse outcome — but that is a judgement about relative harms with nothing behind it, and the objection stands.

**Objection 2: an obligation cannot be created by a departing supplier, so requiring one is theatre.**

At its strongest: obligations are created by the receiving organisation's own management, budget and reporting lines. A handover document saying a named role is obliged to act on a failing result has no standing once the delivery team has gone, and writing it down changes nothing except the delivery team's feelings about having left.

**This is substantially correct and the rebuttal is narrow.** The session does not claim the document creates the obligation. It claims that naming the obligation surfaces its absence while there is still an engagement in which to raise it — the artefact's function is diagnostic rather than binding. Whether raising it changes anything depends entirely on the sponsor, and no source behind this programme reports a case either way. **The declined fix was to drop the obligation field and assess only operability**, which is the part the session can actually observe; it was declined because a runnable harness nobody is obliged by is the source corpus's exact failure, reproduced by a programme that had read the corpus.

**Objection 3: pushing checks from the judged tier to the deterministic tier trades the harness's usefulness for its portability, and the session presents that trade as a free improvement.**

At its strongest: the judged evaluators exist because the properties that matter — whether an answer was relevant, grounded, adherent to instructions — are not computable by a deterministic check. A team that migrates its harness to what runs without a subscription will end up with an action-sequence comparison and a set of regex assertions, which test output shape rather than correctness. **The recipient will then be able to run an instrument that cannot detect the failures the instrument existed to detect**, and will believe they are covered.

**Conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to the session.** The workflow's step 2 states that most checks will not move, but the session's framing rewards the ones that do. The mitigation is the authority field: a deterministic check may be marked advisory, and a judged check marked authoritative with its access requirement stated as an unaccepted manifest line, which is the honest configuration. **The declined fix was to drop step 2 entirely** and treat the access requirement purely as an unaccepted line for [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md) to carry; it was declined because some checks genuinely do move, and a team that never looks will move none. The trade is real and the session should say so more loudly than it does.

**Objection 4: the tier-and-authority marking is invented, and the paper builds an assessment item on it.**

At its strongest: *deterministic versus judged* is read off a product's current API surface and will not survive it; *advisory versus authoritative* is generalised from one repository's internal convention. Neither is a finding about evaluation, and requiring learners to mark every check by both is asking them to apply a taxonomy whose usefulness is asserted.

**Partially conceded.** The first distinction is genuinely product-bound and the session's perishability register says so. The second is a verified observation about one corpus, generalised by this design. The defence is that both are *recording* distinctions the recipient will otherwise have to infer, and inference from a green summary is exactly what [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) established that people do badly. **The declined fix was to require a single free-text note per check instead of two marks**, declined because free text is not comparable across checks and a recipient scanning twenty checks needs to see the shape at a glance. A reader who thinks two invented axes are worse than one unstructured note is making a defensible call.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most recipients will fail to start the harness on the first attempt at workflow step 6. *Instrument:* the run record, coded *started unaided* / *started after intervention* / *did not start*, with the blocking cause named. **If most recipients start it unaided, the session's central operability claim is not a live problem for this cohort** and the material should shift weight to the obligation.

**Prediction 2.** Most learners will produce at least one threshold whose provenance line reduces, on reading, to *set during tuning* in different words. *Instrument:* provenance lines coded *reconstructible reason* / *restated value* / *stated unknown* by a second reader.

**Prediction 3.** Learners will name the willing under-authorised party as the obliged role more often than they record the obligation as unassigned, **despite the session stating in advance that recording the gap is the passing answer.** *Instrument:* the obligation field, coded against the supplied situation's authority map. **This predicts the failure of the session's own countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 4.** Most learners will be unable to state their harness's split strategy before workflow step 3. *Instrument:* one written question asked before the step and again after, tallied per learner.

**Prediction 5.** Interpretation rules will resolve the clear degradation case and fail on the marginal one presented in review. *Instrument:* the reviewer's proposed result and the learner's answer, coded *resolved* / *not resolved* / *deferred to judgement*.

**Prediction 6.** Learners who state uncertainty about the dataset selection rule will produce more extensible rules than those who reconstruct confidently. *Instrument:* the selection-rule text, rated for extensibility by a second reader blind to whether uncertainty was stated. **If this fails, §4.4 is wrong and the session should stop asking for the reconstruction.**

**None of these has been measured.** No cohort has run this session, and the prediction register of this repository is machinery rather than evidence.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That an evaluation harness fails after handover through unrunnability, drift, provenance loss and the absence of an obligation; that only the first is observable in a session and only by watching somebody try; and that transferring an operable instrument without an obligation is transferring a decoration.

**What the evidence licenses.** That a well-built apparatus can produce nothing anybody acts on is verified as a property of one documented framework. That the platform's evaluator tiers differ in what a runner must hold is first-party documentation at a moment in time, definitional and preview-heavy. That criteria are partly a record of the outputs observed is qualitative and real. **That assigning obligations fixes anything is this design's argument and is untested.**

**No evaluator's agreement with human judgement is published, and you must assert none.** If you find one published, this session's opening is due for revision — record the revision with its date.

**The concession worth remembering.** A departing supplier cannot create an obligation inside somebody else's organisation. The most this session produces is a record of where one is missing, delivered while somebody senior is still in the room. And §8's third objection is the one to carry: making a harness portable can make it useless, and the session's framing quietly rewards the trade.

**The silences that matter.** **No agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold** — while most of these evaluators are model-judged. A team can therefore stand up a complete harness, produce a dashboard of scores, and have no published basis for believing those scores correspond to what a competent human would say. The documentation also publishes **no threshold-setting protocol, no provenance requirement for the thresholds a team chooses, and no account of who is obliged to act when a result comes back bad** — so a team adopting the tooling inherits an instrument with no published basis for its cut points and no published obligation attached to its output, which is the exact configuration this session treats as a defect. It publishes nothing about handover, succession or what a recipient must hold to run a harness; **this session's reading of the tier split as an inheritance gradient is an inference from a design decision and not a claim the documentation makes.** If a vendor publishes calibration, threshold rationale or an obligation model, this session's opening is due for revision and this paper records the new version and what it still omits.
