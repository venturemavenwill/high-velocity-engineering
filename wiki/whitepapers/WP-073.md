> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Harness** | The apparatus that decides whether the system still performs acceptably: dataset, criteria, thresholds, run procedure, interpretation rule |
> | **Provenance line** | The recorded reason a threshold has the value it has, sufficient for somebody else to decide whether the reason has expired |
> | **Obligation** | The act a result compels, held by a named role with stated authority. Distinct from the act it merely permits |

## In plain terms

The evaluation harness is the only instrument the customer will have that can tell them the system has stopped working properly. It is also the item on the manifest least likely to survive, and it fails in a way that is almost invisible: it keeps running, keeps producing results, and nobody acts on any of them.

Three things decay independently. The dataset stops resembling what the system now sees, so the harness passes reliably while measuring something that no longer happens. The thresholds lose their reasons, so nobody can revise them, because nobody can tell whether the reason has expired. And the obligation is never assigned, so a failing result reaches a person with no authority and stops there.

The third is the one this session is built around, and the source corpus supplies an unusually pure specimen of it — not of a bad evaluation harness, but of a well-built instrument that produces nothing. Its value-conversation framework instructs its reader to establish a baseline and never shows one being established; catalogues metric definitions and reports none of them measured; ships model workbooks with no populated values. **It is a number-generating apparatus containing no numbers**, and the reason is not that the apparatus is defective. It is that no party is obliged by its output.

The cost model travels in the same class and for the same reason, and the session treats them together.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching evaluation transfer as the transfer of an obligation rather than of an instrument. The argument is that a harness fails after handover in three independent ways — dataset drift, threshold provenance loss, and the absence of an assigned obligation — and that only the third is invisible from inside a working system. The session produces a six-part harness handover record covering dataset selection rule, criteria, threshold provenance, run procedure, interpretation rule and obligation, and applies the same treatment to the cost model. The paper concedes that its central claim is unmeasured, that no source behind this programme reports whether a transferred evaluation apparatus was ever operated by a customer, and that the requirement to reconstruct an undocumented selection rule may teach a form of confident retrospective invention.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

At the end of an engagement the harness works, and its working conceals four things.

The dataset was assembled by somebody who understood what they were selecting for and did not write it down, because at the time the selection was a decision rather than a fact. The thresholds were set by tuning, which is a legitimate way to set a threshold and a poor way to justify one. The interpretation is performed by a person who can tell a degraded system from a moved world because they watched both happen. And the obligation is discharged informally by the delivery team, who notice a bad result and simply act, so nobody has ever had to ask who is obliged.

All four are properties of the team rather than of the harness, and all four leave with the team. **The instrument transfers perfectly and its operation does not transfer at all.**

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session assumes measurement work from Stage 7 — a baseline, a stated attribution position, and the named party responsible for establishing that an observed change was caused by the system — and a manifest from [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md) on which the harness and the cost model appear as lines.

It also assumes the learner has met, earlier in the programme, the failure mode of an apparatus that generates figures while asserting nothing. This session is where that failure mode acquires a countermeasure.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why the obligation rather than the instrument.** An instrument produces information. Information changes behaviour only where somebody is required to respond to it and has the authority to do so. The two conditions are separable and both are commonly absent: the person who reads the result cannot act, and the person who can act does not read it. **This is an argument about organisations, not a finding**, and it is the session's load-bearing claim.

**3.2 Why provenance rather than documentation.** A documented threshold tells a recipient what the value is. A threshold with provenance tells them what would have to change for the value to be wrong. Only the second permits revision, and a threshold nobody may revise is a threshold that will eventually be ignored rather than changed.

**3.3 Why the reconstruction is required to fail.** The selection rule for the evaluation set in the supplied situation was never recorded. Asking the learner to reconstruct it puts them in the position their recipient will be in permanently, and the correct output is a stated uncertainty rather than a confident description. **A stated uncertainty about how a set was built is more useful to a recipient than a confident description that is wrong**, because the first can be tested and the second will be trusted.

**3.4 Why the cost model is in the same class.** It is an instrument, built by the delivery team, whose output the customer needs after disengagement, and which nobody on the receiving side currently operates. The source corpus reaches this conclusion once — as a reflection inside a narrative about business value — and never turns it into a step in any procedure. The session takes the instinct and gives it the procedure.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**The six parts are supplied because the session's difficulty is not in listing them.** It is in item three and item six: producing a provenance line for a threshold set during tuning, and naming a person who is obliged rather than a person who is interested.

**The supplied situation is built so that the honest answer to item six is uncomfortable.** The person who would notice a degradation is the back-office team leader, who holds no budget, no change authority and no subscription access. The people who could act are a technology function that did not want the system and a managed-service supplier that has attended nothing. There is no arrangement of these facts that produces a tidy obligation, and **a learner who produces one has invented a mitigation to make the document look finished.** The session states in advance that recording the gap is the passing answer, which converts the trap into a standard.

**The review movement supplies the awkward case deliberately.** A result that is worse but not badly worse, close to the assessor's sampling window, is where an interpretation rule either resolves or reveals that it only ever handled clear cases.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The invented provenance.** A learner produces a plausible reason for a threshold they set by tuning. *Countermeasure:* none reliable. The session asks for stated uncertainty and cannot distinguish an honest reconstruction from a confident invention, and this is named in §8 as the design's own risk.

**The tidy obligation.** The gap is closed by assigning the obligation to the willing under-authorised party. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires the authority to be stated alongside the role, and the supplied situation makes the mismatch explicit on the page.

**The frozen set left frozen.** The learner transfers the dataset without a rule for extending it. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires the selection rule to be stated well enough for somebody else to extend the set without changing what it measures.

**The interpretation rule that only handles clear cases.** *Countermeasure:* the review movement's awkward case.

**The harness that requires the author.** A run step contains a judgement only the author can make. *Countermeasure:* partial. It is detected at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) when the recipient runs the harness under observation, which is two sessions later and after the handover has been drafted.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. Two specification items at [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md): every transferred threshold carries a provenance line, and the harness record names the obligation a failing result creates together with the authority the obliged party holds.

**Neither item assesses the quality of the evaluation.** Whether the harness measures the right thing was settled in Stage 7 and is not reopened here; what is checked is whether it can be operated and acted on by somebody else.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: requiring a reconstruction of an unrecorded selection rule teaches confident retrospective invention, which is worse than the gap it fills.**

At its strongest: the learner cannot know why the set was assembled as it was, the session rewards producing something, and a plausible reconstruction written by a departing engineer will be read by the recipient as documentation rather than as a guess. The programme would then have manufactured exactly the artefact it warns about elsewhere — an unattributed statement that functions as a norm because nobody can trace it.

**This is conceded.** The mitigation is thin: the session asks for the uncertainty to be stated and marks the statement rather than the reconstruction. **The fix that was considered and declined** was to forbid reconstruction entirely and require the record to say only that the rule is unknown. It was rejected because a recipient who is told nothing about how the set was built cannot extend it and will replace it, which is a worse outcome — but that is a judgement about relative harms with nothing behind it, and the objection stands.

**Objection 2: an obligation cannot be created by a departing supplier, so requiring one is theatre.**

At its strongest: obligations are created by the receiving organisation's own management, budget and reporting lines. A handover document that says a named role is obliged to act on a failing result has no standing whatever once the delivery team has gone, and writing it down changes nothing except the delivery team's feelings about having left.

**This is substantially correct and the rebuttal is narrow.** The session does not claim the document creates the obligation. It claims that naming the obligation surfaces its absence while there is still an engagement in which to raise it — that the artefact's function is diagnostic rather than binding. Whether raising it changes anything depends entirely on the sponsor, and no source behind this programme reports a case either way.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will produce at least one threshold whose provenance line reduces, on reading, to *set during tuning* in different words. *Instrument:* provenance lines coded *reconstructible reason* / *restated value* / *stated unknown* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Learners will name the willing under-authorised party as the obliged role more often than they record the obligation as unassigned. *Instrument:* the obligation field, coded against the supplied situation's authority map.

**Prediction 3.** Interpretation rules will resolve the clear degradation case and fail on the marginal one presented in review. *Instrument:* the reviewer's proposed result and the learner's answer, coded *resolved* / *not resolved* / *deferred to judgement*.

**Prediction 4.** Learners who state uncertainty about the dataset selection rule will produce more extensible rules than those who reconstruct confidently. *Instrument:* the selection-rule text, rated for extensibility by a second reader blind to whether uncertainty was stated. **If this fails, §3.3 is wrong and the session should stop asking for the reconstruction.**

**None of these has been measured.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That an evaluation harness fails after handover through drift, provenance loss and the absence of an obligation; that only the third is invisible from inside a working system; and that transferring the instrument without the obligation is transferring a decoration.

**What the evidence licenses.** That a well-built apparatus can produce nothing anybody acts on is verified as a property of one documented framework. That assigning obligations fixes it is this design's argument and is untested.

**The concession worth remembering.** A departing supplier cannot create an obligation inside somebody else's organisation. The most this session produces is a record of where one is missing, delivered while somebody senior is still in the room.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That the source corpus's value-conversation framework contains no magnitudes at all — no claimed multiples, no savings figures, an unpopulated metric catalogue and an empty case-study section — while instructing its reader to establish a baseline it never exhibits being established, and shipping model workbooks whose outputs cannot be traced back to any proposition in it; that its cost side is engineered and its return side elicited; and that no party in it is assigned responsibility for establishing that an observed change was caused by the system. Also verified: that the corpus states once, as a reflection inside a business-value narrative and never as a procedural step, that the customer should be left holding the cost-modelling capability. All recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md).

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That an evaluation dataset held fixed while the input distribution moves ceases to test the live system; and that information changes behaviour only where a party is both required and able to respond to it. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The six-part handover record; the requirement of a provenance line per threshold; the claim that a value with no recorded reason can be inherited but not revised; the treatment of the cost model as the same class of object as the harness; the requirement that the interpretation rule separate a degraded system from a moved world; the position that recording an unassigned obligation is the passing answer; and all four predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** **This session has no platform anchor and relies on no vendor documentation.** Evaluation tooling is among the fastest-moving parts of the platform layer, and anchoring a session about obligation to a product surface would date the durable claim along with the perishable one. **The silence worth recording is specific and consequential:** no vendor documentation known to this programme publishes a threshold-setting protocol for an automated evaluator, a provenance requirement for the thresholds a team chooses, or any account of who is obliged to act when a result comes back bad. A team adopting such tooling therefore inherits an instrument with no published basis for its thresholds and no published obligation attached to its output, which is the exact configuration this session treats as a defect. If a vendor publishes either, this session acquires an anchor and this paper records the version and what it still omits.
