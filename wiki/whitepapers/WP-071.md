## In plain terms

Handover is usually treated as an event. It is a list.

The list is the thing that goes wrong, because the obvious items — the code, the credentials, the documentation — transfer themselves, and the items that decide whether the system survives do not appear on anybody's list. Infrastructure definitions, the deployment pipeline, the runbooks, the evaluation harness, the cost model and the monitoring are not the product. They are how the product keeps working, and each belongs to nobody's job description on the receiving side.

There is a second class of item that is worse, because it is not an item at all. It is the reason the threshold is where it is. It is the convention everyone follows and nobody wrote down. It is which alerts are noise. **These do not go missing at handover; they were never anywhere to go missing from**, and the handover meeting is the mechanism by which everybody comes to believe otherwise, because explaining something to an attentive person feels exactly like giving it to them.

The paper's operational claim is one sentence: **what transfers is what is versioned.** Everything else is a performance of transfer, and it is a convincing one.

The practice corpus behind this programme is a good specimen of the failure, and an unusually clear one, because the same document set is meticulous about the adjacent problem. It specifies the removal of the delivery organisation's own assets in detail — bound to a register, triggered by a state change, with reuse after project close treated as a policy violation. It says nothing about giving the customer the running system. **The team knows precisely how to leave. It has never been asked what to leave behind.**

## Abstract

This paper defends opening the transfer stage with an enumeration exercise rather than with a process. The argument runs in four parts.

**First**, handover fails at the level of the object rather than the ceremony: the items that determine whether a system survives its delivery team are neither the deliverable nor the infrastructure, and are therefore invisible to both sides. **Second**, a distinct and worse class of holding has no artefact at all — reasons, conventions, judgements and relationships — and the standard handover ritual disguises its absence, because a knowledge intervention is being scored as an ability outcome, a distinction the change-management literature states directly and this programme borrows with its limits attached. **Third**, the operational criterion the session installs — an object transfers if a stranger can find it without asking anyone — has a worked platform demonstration in an openly published agent configuration corpus that makes on-disk state authoritative over session memory, separates authoring source from generated output, and declares the generated tree non-canonical. **Fourth**, the manifest line borrows its shape from the entrustment literature: bounded work with a beginning and an end, delegable whole, with an expiry.

The session produces a transfer manifest, pinned to a revision, with a named receiving owner and an evaluable acceptance condition per line, and requires at least one line to be recorded as unaccepted.

The paper concedes that the six named categories are supplied rather than discovered and will function as a norm; that the manifest's warrant is entirely negative, inferred from what a practice corpus omits rather than from anything measured; that the versioning criterion answers availability and says nothing about comprehension; and that no source behind this programme reports whether any delivered system survived, was maintained, or was abandoned.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A delivery team at the end of an engagement holds a large number of objects, and it can see almost none of them.

This is not carelessness. The objects it cannot see are the ones it uses daily and never had to acquire: the knowledge of which environment is which, the spreadsheet the cost projections come out of, the rule by which the evaluation set was assembled, the reason a threshold has the value it has. Each of those was created without a decision, and none is on any list because no list was ever made.

Meanwhile the receiving side is looking at the product. They will ask for access, documentation and a contact. All three are cheap to give and none is the transfer.

There is a structural reason the delivery team cannot see its own holdings, and it is worth stating precisely because it makes the failure blameless and therefore predictable. **During the engagement, the delivery engineer is the translator.** Evans's account of what a ubiquitous language is for is an account of translation cost: bilingual team members "become bottlenecks of information flow, and their translations are inexact," and "Translation blunts communication and makes knowledge crunching anemic" ([research/05-fde-craft/evans-domain-driven-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/evans-domain-driven-design.md)). While the bottleneck is present, its inexactness is continuously repaired by the person occupying it. At handover the bottleneck is not widened. It is removed, and everything that was passing through it stops.

**The session's job is to make the object of transfer explicit before anybody negotiates about it**, because a negotiation over an unenumerated object is one the delivery team wins by default and loses months later.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session assumes the learner arrives holding a running system with an operational readiness argument, an incident history that is not empty, a measurement with a stated attribution position, a cost model, an unmitigated-risk list and an impact assessment. Those come from Stages 6 and 7 and are not re-derived; [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md) states which session produced each and is entitled to send a learner back.

A learner without them can still write a manifest. It will be a short one, and its shortness will be read as simplicity rather than as an absence, which is the reason the stage sits where it does in the arc.

The session also assumes the learner has met, at [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md), the distinction between evaluation machinery and evaluation results, and has audited the same pinned corpus this session uses. **The corpus is deliberately reused rather than replaced.** The second visit asks a different question of the same object, which is cheaper for the learner than a new object and which demonstrates, incidentally, that a well-pinned artefact supports more than one investigation — the property the session is arguing for.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is moderate. The concepts are few — object, location, owner, acceptance, expiry — and mutually independent, but the enumeration task itself is long and the learner is doing it against their own engagement rather than a supplied case. The surplus is spent on the affective work of discovering that several things they believed they had delivered exist nowhere. This is a design judgement and carries no external warrant.

**3.2 Enumeration precedes judgement, and the order is not arbitrary.**

The session is an availability problem before it is a judgement problem. A learner cannot decide who should own the evaluation harness while the evaluation harness is not on their list, and it is not on their list because it has never been an object of attention — it has been a thing they use.

This is the same structure as the audit discipline at [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md), where an absence is evidence only if the search for it was competent, and it inherits the same requirement: **the search must be described, not just its outcome.** A manifest that omits an object because nobody looked and a manifest that omits it because it does not exist are indistinguishable from the page.

**3.3 The opening items are a Productive Failure generation phase, and that is a warranted design rather than a stylistic one.**

Kapur's Productive Failure is a two-phase, order-dependent design: learners generate solutions to a task targeting a concept they have not been taught, fail, and are then taught ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). The mechanism the session leans on is the **failed generation effect** — attempting an answer before instruction improves later learning *even when the attempt is wrong*.

Two of Kapur's stated design conditions bind this session and are conditions rather than preferences. **Do not scaffold the generation phase**: prompts and hints that reduce the failure rate reduce learning, which is why the release rule above gates §§4, 5 and 9 and why the session's five items are stated without any indication of what a good answer looks like. **The consolidation phase must explicitly prompt comparison**: Loibl and Leuders showed that presenting correct and incorrect solutions side by side achieves nothing on its own, and only the condition instructed to compare them learned more. Exercise 3 is that instruction, and it is written as an instruction rather than as an invitation for that reason.

**The limits are recorded and they are serious.** Every study behind Productive Failure is a school, university or laboratory study; Kapur states that the cycle works in adult and corporate settings and cites nothing for that sentence. **No magnitude from this literature is asserted here or anywhere in this wiki**, and the extrapolation to working professionals is conceded in §8.

**3.4 Knowledge is not ability, and a handover meeting produces the first.**

This is the session's best-motivated psychological claim and the reason it is taught before any process.

Hiatt's ADKAR model separates **knowledge** — "the information, training and education necessary to know how to change" — from **ability**, which "is turning knowledge into action" and "is achieved when a person or group has the demonstrated capability to implement the change at the required performance levels" ([research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md)). He is blunt that the first does not produce the second: "The presence of knowledge is often insufficient by itself," and, unusually for a book of its genre, "Some employees, depending on the change, may never develop the required abilities."

He also names the barriers to ability directly — psychological blocks, physical constraints, intellectual capability, available time, available support resources — and two of those five are the ones a handover most often ignores. **Nobody's time was allocated to receiving.** Nobody's support was arranged for the week after.

**The limits on this source are substantial and travel with it.** ADKAR is a practitioner model published by the consultancy that owns its trademark and sells the training and certification in it. Its ordering claim is argued from the meanings of its terms rather than from any study, and its completeness claim likewise. Every quantitative statement in the book traces to the publisher's own practitioner surveys, which report what practitioners *say* caused outcomes rather than measured outcomes. **No independent evaluation of ADKAR appears anywhere in the book, and none is cited.** It also carries a retention-by-modality claim sourced to a commercial training handbook, which this repository names as a member of the learning-pyramid family and reproduces nowhere. The distinction between knowledge and ability is defensible on its own terms, is directly observable, and is the only thing taken.

**3.5 Why the criterion is versioning rather than documentation.**

A weaker version of this session would say *write it down*. That version fails, and the reason is worth having.

An openly published agent configuration corpus, read at a pinned commit ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)), makes files under a tracking directory **authoritative over session memory**, under stable identifiers, so that a decision made in one phase is findable by identifier in another. The stated purpose is provenance and restartability. The consequence this session takes is different and is not the corpus's claim: **state on disk is transferable and state in a session is not.**

The corpus supplies a second distinction the session needs and that a documentation framing would miss. It separates authoring source, packaging manifest and generated output, and declares the generated tree non-canonical. **A generated artefact is not a transferable object**, because handing it over transfers an output whose source of truth remains behind. A team that exports its dashboards and hands over the images has performed a transfer whose subject is a picture.

**Its limits are extreme.** Perishability is total on paths, counts, plugin names and package versions, all of which moved while the note was being written. Host and model dependence is absolute; a result observed on one client and one model is not a result about the corpus. Its own prose carries benefit vocabulary — words in the family of *accelerate*, *prevents*, *proven* — that nothing in the tree supports, and the revision this programme pins is not all-green. **A single vendor's opinion about agent configuration is not the field**, and the artefact-kind taxonomy in particular is a product decision taught here as one instance of a durable idea.

**3.6 The manifest line borrows its shape from the entrustment literature.**

Ten Cate's granularity test for an entrustable professional activity is behavioural: activities are "the units of professional practice that constitute what clinicians do as daily work", "activities with a beginning and an end", and they "are only entrusted to trained personnel" ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). Could a supervisor delegate this whole thing and walk away, and would the recipient know when they had finished?

That test does real work on a manifest. *Own the harness* has no end. *Understand the architecture* is not an activity. It also supplies the field the session's manifest carries and that ordinary handover documents never do: **entrustment expires.** If the recipient "has not practiced the EPA for a long time, the entrustment decision may expire." A handover assuming permanence claims more than the model it borrows from.

**Its limits:** a clinical model from its originator, reporting adoption rather than effectiveness. Medicine has patients, legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint. The full summative standard — multiple trained observers, multiple observations, deliberated judgement — is expensive, and any version this programme runs is reduced and says so.

**3.7 Why the agent is asked what is missing rather than whether the document is good.**

An instrument that agrees with you has not been used — the claim installed at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md). An omission-finding prompt is a task with an answer; a review prompt is an invitation to approve. The division of labour is the one that session established: the tooling is given retrieval and enumeration, and the judgement about who should own what is reserved for the learner.

**3.8 The one-directional artefact chain, and change as a record.**

A customer-embedded engineering organisation within a large technology vendor specifies that once a technical specification is settled, a change to it is recorded as a decision record rather than as an edit ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-fde-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-fde-delivery-practice.md)). **An edited specification loses the fact that it changed, and with it the reason** — and the reason is precisely the class of holding §1 identifies as invisible.

That standard reports no outcome, no evaluation, no comparison and no measurement of anything, describes no engagement run under it, and is itself silent on transfer. It is a competent organisation writing down how it intends to work. **Nothing in it is evidence that any of it works**, and it is cited here for a discipline it states rather than for a result it does not have.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The movement sequence.** Situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**4.2 The opening items are the instrument, and item 2 is the one the design cares about.** *Which item on your list is held somewhere that is not the repository?* Most learners answer with a misplaced file — usually the cost model — and stop, because a misplaced file is the only kind of wrongness the question appears to be asking about. The intended answer is a class of holding with no file anywhere, and the gap between the two is the session's content.

Item 5 — *if somebody accepted your handover, what would they have done?* — is the second instrument and fails differently. Most learners describe an act of their own: a meeting held, a document sent, permissions granted. It is the same shape as the gap at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) between the confident answers and item 2, and the repetition is deliberate: **the stage that closes the engagement arc opens with the same instrument as the stage that opened it**, and a learner who notices that has been given something the session does not have to say.

**4.3 The six named categories are supplied rather than discovered.** Infrastructure definitions, pipeline, runbooks, evaluation harness, cost model and monitoring are given, because leaving them to discovery consumes the session on a search whose answer is already recorded as an absence. **The warrant is that they are exactly the items the source corpus never mentions transferring** — not that they are the right six for every engagement, which is not established and is the subject of §8's first objection.

**4.4 What the platform workflow is for.** It is not a tooling exercise. It exists to make one comparison physically observable: the list of things in the corpus that a stranger could reconstruct, beside the list of things a stranger could only learn by asking. The first takes an afternoon and is long. The second takes minutes and is short and embarrassing. **The two lists sit on one screen, and no amount of arguing produces that.**

The workflow deliberately runs on a corpus the learner did not build, because a learner auditing their own artefact will find the second list empty — not through dishonesty but because the reasons are still in their head and their presence there is invisible from the inside. Auditing somebody else's corpus makes the missing reasons observable, and the transfer to their own manifest is then an inference the learner draws rather than a claim the session makes.

**4.5 The pin is a requirement rather than good practice.** Every count in a manifest is a count at a revision. A manifest written against a moving target describes nothing, cannot be checked by the recipient, and — the failure that matters — cannot be checked by the author six weeks later when the recipient disputes it.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Access mistaken for transfer.** The likeliest outcome. A learner grants permissions and marks the line complete. *Countermeasure:* the acceptance condition must be evaluable by the recipient with the learner absent, and a permission grant satisfies none. **The countermeasure operates on the artefact, not on the belief**, and a learner who holds the belief can produce a conforming manifest. §9's fifth prediction is about exactly this.

**The generic list.** A learner produces the list a handover *should* contain rather than the list this engagement holds, and the six invisible items are present but empty. *Countermeasure:* each line must name a location, and a generic line has none.

**The empty second list.** A learner runs step 4 and finds nothing, concluding the corpus is fully documented. *Countermeasure:* the step names three specific shapes to look for — an undeclared convention, a threshold with no stated derivation, a check whose authority had to be inferred — because an unshaped search for absences returns nothing.

**Documentation substitution.** Every unfindable reason acquires a promise of a document rather than an owner. *Countermeasure:* exercise 6 forbids answering a challenge with a document, and moves the line to unaccepted. This is the cheapest countermeasure in the session and the one most likely to be quietly ignored when nobody is watching.

**The willing under-authorised owner.** One name against every difficult line. *Countermeasure:* the supplied situation puts willingness and authority in different people, and the review asks what the named owner would have to *do*.

**Manifest as inventory.** A complete, unordered list, which transfers the problem to the recipient. *Countermeasure:* none inside this session. The ordering pressure arrives at [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md), where a challenger asks what each line costs them.

Retrieved by [S072](/wiki/seminars/S072.md), [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) and [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md), each of which takes one manifest line and works it; consolidated at [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md); and reopened at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), where the unaccepted line is what the recipient is asked to acknowledge they are not accepting.

The retrieval is structural rather than incidental. S072 takes the runbook line and asks what its reader must already hold. S073 takes the harness line and asks what authority travels with it. S074 takes the location field and asks what discovers an object nobody listed. **Each of the three is a failure of this session's manifest to have been sufficient**, which is the argument for the module's sequence rather than an admission about it.

It is retrieved again in Stage 9 at S086, where reusable starting points are assembled, and at S088, where the engagement's own limits are stated.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. The manifest is one input to the specification check at [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md), where it is assessed on the presence of a receiving owner and an evaluable acceptance condition per line, on the presence of at least one unaccepted line with a stated consequence, and on the presence of a pin.

**The manifest is never assessed on completeness**, because completeness cannot be judged from outside the engagement, and an assessor who marks for it will reward length. That boundary follows the entrustment literature's own logic: the assessable thing is the bounded activity and the reasoning behind the judgement, not agreement with an assessor's list.

**Nothing else is assessed.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the six categories are asserted, and asserting a checklist is exactly the failure this programme criticises elsewhere.**

At its strongest: the programme's own analysis of its source material complains that specimen answers function as implicit norms, because an engineer who has internalised them hears a real answer as high or low against a scale nobody validated ([S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) makes this its central hazard). Supplying six categories does the same thing. A learner who internalises them will arrive at an engagement whose critical transfer object is none of the six and will not see it.

**This is correct and is conceded without qualification.** The mitigation is weak and is stated as weak: the six are labelled in the session as the items this corpus omits rather than as the items that matter, and the manifest is assessed on locations, owners and acceptance conditions rather than on matching the list. **The declined fix was to withhold the categories and let learners discover them**, and it was declined because the discovery consumes the session and its answer is already recorded in the research note. That trades a known cost for an unknown one. The objection stands.

**Objection 2: enumerating a transfer object is administration, not engineering, and a session spent on it is a session not spent on the system.**

At its strongest: manifests are the kind of artefact organisations produce to demonstrate diligence, they are generated most enthusiastically by people with the least to do, and the ones that exist in practice are rarely read.

The rebuttal is narrow. The claim is not that a manifest is valuable as a document; it is that the act of enumeration surfaces holdings the team cannot otherwise see, and that the surfacing must happen before the negotiation about ownership rather than after. **Whether the resulting document is ever read again is not this session's claim and is probably false.** The declined fix was to replace the manifest with a rehearsal — put the recipient in front of the system on day one and see what breaks — which was declined because a rehearsal tests the objects you thought to include and is silent on the ones you did not. [S072](/wiki/seminars/S072.md) runs the rehearsal, after the enumeration, for that reason.

**Objection 3: "what transfers is what is versioned" is false, and the session knows it.**

At its strongest: a great deal of durable organisational capability is carried in people, relationships and habits, none of which is versioned and much of which transfers perfectly well by ordinary means — apprenticeship, sitting next to somebody, working a rota together. A criterion that classifies all of it as untransferable is not a sharp instrument; it is a wrong one, and it will push learners to write down things better learned by doing, producing exactly the documentation substitution §5 warns about.

**Partially conceded, and the concession changes the claim's scope rather than its content.** The criterion is about what transfers *across a departure with a fixed date and no successor overlap*, which is the situation Stage 8 is built on. Where there is an overlap period, apprenticeship is available and is better than a document; where there is not — and in this stage there is not — an unversioned holding has no route. The session should state that boundary more prominently than it does. **The declined fix was to weaken the criterion to a heuristic**, and it was declined because a heuristic produces no observable second list at step 4, and the second list is the session's entire instrument.

**Objection 4: the psychological warrant is a trademarked consultancy model with no independent evaluation, and the paper leans on it hard.**

At its strongest: ADKAR's ordering and completeness claims rest on the meanings of the words; its empirical base is its own publisher's practitioner surveys; the book contains marketing copy in its front matter; and this paper's §3.4 uses it to carry the session's central psychological claim. Citing a vendor model for the mechanism while criticising vendor documentation elsewhere is inconsistent.

**Conceded in part, and the inconsistency is real but narrower than stated.** What is taken is a *distinction* — knowledge versus demonstrated capability at the required performance level — not a finding, and the distinction is independently observable in the room: a recipient either performs the act unaided or does not. Nothing in the session rests on ADKAR's ordering, its completeness claim, its barrier-point threshold or any figure from it. **The declined fix was to drop the citation and assert the distinction as design reasoning**, and it was declined because the distinction is stated more precisely in that source than this design would state it, and because attributing a borrowed idea to oneself is the worse fault. A reader who weighs the source's provenance more heavily than this paper does is making a defensible call.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A substantial share of learners will answer opening item 2 with a misplaced file rather than with an unrecorded holding. *Instrument:* the five written answers, with item 2 coded *misplaced artefact* / *unrecorded holding* / *both* / *absent* by a second reader. **If most learners produce unrecorded holdings unprompted, the session's central finding is not a finding for this cohort** and the material should be shortened accordingly.

**Prediction 2.** A substantial share of learners will answer opening item 5 with an act performed by themselves rather than by the recipient. *Instrument:* item 5 coded *recipient act* / *delivery act* / *absent* by a second reader.

**Prediction 3.** First-draft manifests will omit the evaluation harness and the cost model more often than they omit the pipeline and the monitoring. *Instrument:* presence or absence of each of the six categories in the pre-agent draft, tallied per learner.

**Prediction 4.** The step-4 list of unrecorded holdings will be shorter for learners auditing their own artefact than for learners auditing the pinned corpus. *Instrument:* a within-cohort split, with half the cohort running step 4 against their own engagement artefact and half against the corpus, comparing item counts. **This tests §4.4's claim that the reasons are invisible from the inside**, which is the reason the workflow uses somebody else's corpus, and a null result would mean the choice was arbitrary.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of learners will produce at least one acceptance condition that is an act of their own, despite the stated requirement that it be evaluable in their absence. *Instrument:* the submitted manifest, with each acceptance condition coded *recipient act* / *delivery act*. **This predicts the failure of the session's own countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 6.** Learners who prompted an agent to name omissions will add more lines than those who prompted it to review the manifest. *Instrument:* the prompt record against the line-count difference between pre-agent and post-agent drafts. **If this fails, the session's use of the agent is decoration** and the movement should be rewritten as an unaided second pass.

**None of these has been measured.** No cohort has run this session, and the prediction register of this repository is machinery rather than evidence — which is [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s subject and applies to this page exactly as to any other.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That handover fails at the level of the object; that the objects deciding survival are structurally invisible to both sides; that a second class of holding has no artefact at all and is disguised by the ritual; and that naming them, with owners, acceptance conditions and a pin, is the first act of a transfer.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. That a particular practice corpus specifies teardown in detail and transfer not at all is verified against a complete reading. That a particular configuration corpus makes on-disk state authoritative over session memory and declares its generated tree non-canonical is verified at a pinned commit and is perishable. **That transfer matters, that a manifest helps, and that these six categories are the right ones are all this design's arguments.**

**The knowledge-versus-ability distinction is borrowed and its source is weak.** Take the distinction, which you can observe in a room. Take nothing else from it.

**The concession worth remembering.** Every strong claim in this session is inferred from a silence in a document. That is a weak form of reasoning, this programme uses it twice, and both times it says so.
