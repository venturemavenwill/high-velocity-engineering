## In plain terms

Handover is usually treated as an event. It is a list.

The list is the thing that goes wrong, because the obvious items — the code, the credentials, the documentation — transfer themselves, and the items that decide whether the system survives do not appear on anybody's list at all. Infrastructure definitions, the deployment pipeline, the runbooks, the evaluation harness, the cost model and the monitoring are not the product. They are how the product keeps working, and each of them belongs to nobody's job description on the receiving side.

The practice corpus behind this programme is a good specimen of the failure, and an unusually clear one because the same document set is meticulous about the adjacent problem. It specifies the removal of the delivery organisation's own assets in detail — bound to a register, triggered by a state change, with reuse after project close treated as a policy violation. It says nothing whatever about giving the customer the running system. **The team knows precisely how to leave. It has never been asked what to leave behind.**

This session's answer is small and mechanical: write the list, put a name against every line, and find the line that has no name. The line with no name is the session's actual finding.

## Abstract

This paper defends opening the transfer stage with an enumeration exercise rather than with a process. The argument is that handover fails at the level of the object rather than at the level of the ceremony: the items that determine whether a system survives its delivery team are precisely the items that are neither the deliverable nor the infrastructure, and they are therefore invisible to both sides. The session produces a transfer manifest with a named receiving owner and an acceptance condition per line, and requires at least one line to be recorded as unaccepted. The paper concedes that the manifest's warrant is entirely negative — it is inferred from what a practice corpus omits rather than from anything measured — and that no source behind this programme reports whether any delivered system survived, was maintained, or was abandoned.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A delivery team at the end of an engagement holds a large number of objects, and it can see almost none of them.

This is not carelessness. The objects it cannot see are the ones it uses daily and never had to acquire: the knowledge of which environment is which, the spreadsheet the cost projections come out of, the rule by which the evaluation set was assembled, the reason a threshold has the value it has. Each of those was created without a decision, and none of them is on any list because no list was ever made.

Meanwhile the receiving side is looking at the product. They will ask for access, documentation and a contact. All three are cheap to give and none of them is the transfer.

**The session's job is to make the object of transfer explicit before anybody negotiates about it**, because a negotiation over an unenumerated object is a negotiation the delivery team wins by default and loses months later.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session assumes the learner arrives holding a running system with an operational readiness argument, an incident history, a measurement with a stated attribution position, a cost model, an unmitigated-risk list and an impact assessment. Those come from Stages 6 and 7 and are not re-derived.

A learner without them can still write a manifest. It will be a short one, and its shortness will be read as simplicity rather than as an absence, which is the reason the stage sits where it does in the arc.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why enumeration first.** The session is an availability problem before it is a judgement problem. A learner cannot decide who should own the evaluation harness while the evaluation harness is not on their list, and it is not on their list because it has never been an object of attention — it has been a thing they use. Making tacit holdings explicit is the precondition for every subsequent act in this module, and it is cheap.

**3.2 Why the opening items are asked before instruction.** Attempting an answer before being taught improves later retention of the tested material and makes the distinction noticeable when it arrives. The direction is well established. **No magnitude is asserted here or anywhere in this wiki.**

**3.3 Why an unaccepted line is required.** A manifest with an owner against every line is either a genuine finding or an artefact of the learner assigning names to people who were not asked, and the two are indistinguishable from the page. Requiring one unaccepted line makes the honest version cheaper to produce than the dishonest one. This is a design device with no external warrant; it is defended in §8.

**3.4 Why the agent is asked what is missing rather than whether the document is good.** An instrument that agrees with you has not been used — the claim installed at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) — and an omission-finding prompt is a task with an answer, while a review prompt is an invitation to approve.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**The opening items are the instrument, and item 5 is the one the design cares about.** *If somebody accepted your handover, what would they have done?* Most learners describe an act of their own — a meeting held, a document sent, permissions granted — and the gap between the fluent answers to items 1 and 2 and the empty answer to item 5 is the session's content. It is the same shape as the gap at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) between the confident answers and item 2, and it is deliberate: the stage that closes the programme's engagement arc opens with the same instrument as the stage that opened it.

**The six named categories are supplied rather than discovered.** Infrastructure definitions, pipeline, runbooks, evaluation harness, cost model and monitoring are given to the learner because leaving them to be discovered wastes the session on a search whose answer is recorded in the source corpus as an absence. **The warrant for those six is that they are exactly the items the corpus never mentions transferring** — not that they are the right six for every engagement, which is not established.

**The failure modes are given as warning signs rather than definitions**, because a definition is recognised after the fact and a warning sign is noticed during.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Access mistaken for transfer.** The likeliest outcome. A learner grants permissions and marks the line complete. *Countermeasure:* the acceptance condition must be evaluable by the recipient with the learner absent, and a permission grant satisfies none. **The countermeasure operates on the artefact, not on the belief**, and a learner who holds the belief can produce a conforming manifest.

**The generic list.** A learner produces the list a handover *should* contain rather than the list this engagement actually holds, and the six invisible items are present but empty. *Countermeasure:* each line must name a location, and a generic line has none.

**The willing under-authorised owner.** One name against every difficult line. *Countermeasure:* the supplied situation puts willingness and authority in different people, and the review movement asks what the named owner would have to *do*.

**Manifest as inventory.** The learner produces a complete and unordered list, which transfers the problem to the recipient. *Countermeasure:* none inside this session. The ordering pressure arrives at [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md), where a challenger asks what each line costs them.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. The manifest is one input to the specification check at [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md), where it is assessed on the presence of a receiving owner and an evaluable acceptance condition per line, and on the presence of at least one unaccepted line with a stated consequence.

**The manifest is never assessed on completeness**, because completeness cannot be judged from outside the engagement, and an assessor who marks for it will reward length.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the six categories are asserted, and asserting a checklist is exactly the failure this programme criticises elsewhere.**

At its strongest: the programme's own analysis of its source corpus complains that a question bank's specimen answers function as implicit norms, because an engineer who has internalised them hears a real answer as high or low against a scale nobody validated. Supplying six categories does the same thing. A learner who internalises them will arrive at an engagement whose critical transfer object is none of the six and will not see it.

**This is correct and is conceded.** The mitigation is weak and is stated as weak: the six are labelled in the session as the items this corpus omits rather than as the items that matter, and the manifest is assessed on locations and owners rather than on matching the list. **The fix that was considered and declined** was to withhold the categories and let learners discover them, which was rejected because the discovery consumes the session and its answer is already recorded in the research note — but the rejection trades a known cost for an unknown one, and the objection stands.

**Objection 2: enumerating a transfer object is administration, not engineering, and a session spent on it is a session not spent on the system.**

At its strongest: manifests are the kind of artefact that organisations produce to demonstrate diligence, they are generated most enthusiastically by people with the least to do, and the ones that exist in practice are rarely read.

The rebuttal is narrow. The claim is not that a manifest is valuable as a document; it is that the act of enumeration surfaces holdings the team cannot otherwise see, and that the surfacing has to happen before the negotiation about ownership rather than after. Whether the resulting document is ever read again is not this session's claim and is probably false.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** A substantial share of learners will answer opening item 5 with an act performed by themselves rather than by the recipient. *Instrument:* the five written answers, with item 5 coded *recipient act* / *delivery act* / *absent* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** First-draft manifests will omit the evaluation harness and the cost model more often than they omit the pipeline and the monitoring. *Instrument:* presence or absence of each of the six categories in the pre-agent draft, tallied per learner.

**Prediction 3.** Learners who prompted an agent to name omissions will add more lines than those who prompted it to review the manifest. *Instrument:* the prompt record against the line-count difference between pre-agent and post-agent drafts. **If this fails, the session's use of the agent is decoration** and the movement should be rewritten as an unaided second pass.

**Prediction 4.** Where a learner names the same person against several difficult lines, that person will be the willing under-authorised one rather than the authorised unwilling one. *Instrument:* the owner column, coded against the supplied situation's authority map.

**None of these has been measured.** No cohort has run this session.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That handover fails at the level of the object; that the objects which decide survival are structurally invisible to both sides; and that naming them, with owners and acceptance conditions, is the first act of a transfer.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. That a particular practice corpus specifies teardown in detail and transfer not at all is verified. That transfer matters, that a manifest helps, and that these six categories are the right ones are all this design's arguments.

**The concession worth remembering.** Every strong claim in this session is inferred from a silence in a document. That is a weak form of reasoning, this programme uses it twice, and both times it says so.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That the practice corpus specifies withdrawal as a register-triggered teardown of the delivery organisation's own assets, and contains no description of transferring the running system, its infrastructure definitions, its pipelines, its runbooks, its evaluation harness, its cost model or its monitoring to the customer; that it names no receiving owner, no exit criteria, no acceptance and no support taper; and that it imposes no evidentiary requirement that a teardown was performed. Also verified: that the corpus reports no sustainment evidence of any kind and no post-deployment record for any case. All recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md), which rests on a complete reading.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That attempting an answer before instruction improves later retention of the tested material, and that making tacit holdings explicit is a precondition for reasoning about them. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The transfer manifest itself; the requirement for a named receiving owner as role and person; the acceptance condition evaluable in the learner's absence; the requirement that at least one line be recorded as unaccepted; the claim that a permission grant is not a transfer; the claim that an object's location is part of its description; the selection of six categories as the ones that go missing; and all four predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** **This session has no platform anchor and relies on no vendor documentation.** The omission is deliberate rather than an oversight: the question of what must transfer for a customer to keep operating a system is not answered by any product's documentation, and a session anchored to one would inherit that product's model of ownership as though it were an argument. **The silence worth recording is therefore an industry-wide one** — no vendor documentation known to this programme publishes a transfer object, an acceptance criterion for a delivered system, or a definition of what a customer must hold in order to be said to own it. If such documentation appears, this session acquires an anchor and the paper is revised to record its version and what it omits.
