## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

Nobody in the room will disagree with the day's claim, and that is the problem. Say out loud that deleting a record is not the same kind of event as writing a bad paragraph, and everybody grants it in under a minute. A day built to establish that would be a day spent proving something to people who already believe it.

The real difficulty is that **the apparatus does not announce which of its parts has stopped working.** Eight quarters have built an evaluation machine whose object is an *answer*: a construct and the gap to its measure, an error decomposition, an interval, a criterion traceable to named people, a judge with a bias, an offline number and an online one, a gate with two error rates, a detection design, a limits statement with an expiry condition. Every one of those keeps running when the object becomes a *sequence of actions*. A criterion still looks like a criterion. A gate still passes. Nothing fails loudly. The machine quietly starts measuring something else and reports normally throughout.

So the day is not built around the claim. It is built around one document. Sixteen weeks ago every student wrote a limits statement, published it to real readers, and those readers acted on it. **One word in it is changed — the system now *does* the thing rather than *describing* it — and the student is asked which sentences are no longer adequate.** There is nothing to interpret. The document was correct and remains correct; it was simply addressed to a reader who could route around a limitation, and the thing it now describes will not consult a reader at all.

Three things sit underneath. **Students will put the question in the wrong place** — in the model, or the prompt, or the framework — and build a boundary around a component. The correction sounds like a quibble when argued and is obvious from a record: four systems over one request, and the one that takes no action at all has the largest realised consequence, because whatever consumes its output cannot decline. An answer something else acts on is an action.

**A boundary that is written and not enforced passes every review**, which is why the day's whole assessable content is one question asked of every prohibited line: what, other than this sentence, prevents it? And when there is no answer, the instruction is to leave the line blank rather than delete it, because the blank is the finding and deleting it destroys the finding.

**And a confirmation step is a control only if somebody's decline rate is not zero.** If nobody has ever declined, you do not have a control; you have an audit trail with a person attached. That is phase 6 in one sentence — nobody approved the action that mattered and nobody failed to — and the day turns it on the room's own habits, because an intervention record is a confirmation step with the student as the approver.

**What this paper concedes and does not answer.** The day claims a boundary differs from a routing aid in that it causes an action not to occur. That is a claim about what happens when the system *tries*. **The day's shadow path never tries.** Nothing is attempted, nothing is refused, no enforcement point is invoked, and a boundary made entirely of mechanisms that do not work would produce an identical artefact and an identical phase 8. The fix — enforce one pooled boundary for real and report the refusals — is named and declined, because a pooled boundary belongs to nobody and the day's force comes from the artefact being yours. The paper judges that preference does not outweigh having no evidence of refusal at all, and takes it anyway.

## Abstract

This paper defends a day whose input is the student's own sixteen-week-old published limits statement and whose output is a boundary around a system the student deploys on the same day, and defends making the argument [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md) declined: a limits statement for a system that answers is a routing aid, and for a system that acts it is a boundary. It defends locating agency in the loop rather than in the component, on a contrasting case in which the system that takes no action produces the largest realised consequence of four — now operated rather than described, so that the absence of any approval prompt anywhere in the safest-looking configuration is watched rather than asserted. It defends the operative test the day supplies — for each prohibited line, what other than the sentence prevents it — and the instruction to leave an unenforced line blank rather than delete it. **Section 7 reports the discharge of both obligations S055 placed on this day under instrumentation rule 6.** Section 8 records an instrumentation rule 1 violation found during this paper's own drafting and fixed in the day, concedes that a shadow path cannot test a boundary because it never tries, concedes that the reader sample discharging WP-055's principal concession is the kind of sample this programme teaches students to distrust, and concedes that the day applies its own decline-rate test to the students' agent practice while omitting the denominator that would make the test computable.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The problem is not that students will disagree with the day's claim. It is that agreeing with it changes nothing.

Say aloud that an action has a consequence a wrong answer does not, and a room grants it in under a minute. Nobody argues that a deleted record and a bad paragraph are the same object. The claim is not counterintuitive, it is not contested, and a day built to establish it would be a day spent proving something to people who already believe it.

**The difficulty is that the apparatus does not announce which of its parts stopped working.** Eight quarters of this programme have produced an evaluation apparatus whose object is an answer — a construct and the gap to its measure, an error decomposition, an interval with a dependency structure, a criterion traceable to named people, a judge with a systematic component, an offline number and an online one, a gate with two error rates, a detection design with four parts, a limits statement with a kind and an expiry condition. Every one of those continues to run when the object becomes a trajectory. A criterion still looks like a criterion. A judge still produces scores. A gate still passes. Nothing fails loudly. The apparatus quietly becomes about something else and reports normally throughout.

So the day cannot be organised around the claim. It has to be organised around **one specific artefact whose inadequacy the student can see in their own handwriting**, and the programme has exactly one candidate: the limits statement each student published to real users at [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md), sixteen weeks ago, which readers acted on. Phase 1 item 2 changes one word in it — the system *does* the thing rather than *describes* it — and asks which sentences are no longer adequate. There is nothing to interpret. The document was addressed to a reader who could route around a limitation; the system it now describes will not consult a reader at all.

Three difficulties sit underneath and none of them is technical.

**Agency is attributed to the component.** Students will locate the question in the model, the prompt or the framework, and will build a boundary around the wrong object. The correction is not available by argument, because the argument sounds like a quibble. It is available from a record, which is what phase 3 is: four systems over one request, of which the one that takes no action has the largest realised consequence, because what consumes its output cannot decline. **An answer that something else acts on is an action**, and the room reads that off a three-month record rather than being told it.

**A boundary is written and not enforced.** This is the commonest professional error in the area and it produces a document that satisfies every review. The day's whole assessable content is the operative question — *what, other than this sentence, prevents it* — and the specification's hard requirement is that a line with no answer is left blank rather than deleted, because the blank is the finding and the deletion destroys it.

**A control is confused with a rate.** A confirmation step is a control if somebody can decline it and is an audit trail otherwise, and the distinguishing property is not whether a human is in the loop but whether their decline rate is distinguishable from zero. Phase 6's case is the whole difficulty in one sentence: **nobody approved the action that mattered and nobody failed to.**

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Low technical, high enumerative, and front-loaded onto a single conversion.**

Nothing on the day is difficult to compute. The classification at step 3 is a three-way choice. The boundary at step 4 is a list. The decline point at step 5 is a person, a rate and a judgement. The shadow run at step 7 is supplied as a harness.

The interactivity is between steps 1, 4 and 5, and it is genuine. The revised statement determines what the boundary must cover; the boundary determines which actions require a decline point; the decline point's rate determines whether the boundary is doing the work or the person is. A student who revises the statement without carrying the revision into the boundary produces two documents that disagree, which is the professional failure mode the day exists to prevent, and the artefact makes it visible by requiring both in one submission.

Management:

- **One capability, chosen at step 2 and held for the whole module.** Nothing is re-chosen at S072 to S075.
- **The reader responses are supplied, coded and summarised**, so no phase time is spent reading correspondence.
- **The boundary form is worked with the room** at step 4, since converting prose into an enforceable list is a new operation.
- **The shadow path is a harness, not a build.**
- **Phase 3's four systems, their records and their realised-consequence figures are all supplied.**

**Step 6 is unsupported**, and it is the only step the day leaves alone.

Expertise reversal would begin to apply at point 3's five arguments for a student who has run a production agent, and the design's answer is that such a student is not being asked to learn the list — they are being asked to apply it to a boundary they wrote and to have it defeated at phase 7.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Re-labelling your own sealed exclusions sixteen weeks later, and naming the intervention you lack,* item 1.
- *One word changed in your own published document,* item 2.
- *Ranking four systems by realised consequence before the records arrive,* phase 3.
- *Marking the last decline point and naming the person, including when there is none,* phase 3's task-after and step 5.
- *Writing an enforcement for every prohibited line and leaving the blanks,* step 4.
- *Predicting what your own boundary permits, before the shadow run,* step 6.
- *Having a partner construct an action your boundary permits,* phase 7.
- *Being told by the person who receives them that they would have stopped reading in week three,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Building the agent.* IS302's. The day's capability is proposed and shadow-run, not implemented, because the boundary is the object and a student debugging a tool call is not writing one.
- *The adversary.* [M16](/wiki/modules/M16.md)'s entirely. A student who writes a boundary against an attacker at this day has skipped the ordinary case, which is where the module's evidence is.
- *Multi-agent delegation.* [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) point 6, two days later. Naming it here would introduce a second permission holder before the first boundary exists.
- *The formal treatment of reversibility.* Compensating transactions and their limits are SY301's, one quarter behind. Point 7 is a triage, and its scope statement says so.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** Point 7's three reversibility classes are **safe** as a triage for actions on a system you operate, whose reversal path you or a colleague hold, and where undoing is a technical operation. They are **unsafe** wherever the reversal is social rather than technical — a message sent, a record disclosed, a decision communicated to someone who has since acted on it. Those are placed in the second class by every student who applies the list unaided, and they belong in the third. The artefact binds the boundary rather than leaving it in this paper: step 3 requires the student to name one action of their own capability whose reversal is social, which is the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s open question about binding surface answered in the affirmative for this instance without being answered in general. The list is also unsafe as an account of an action that is technically reversible and was never observed, since you cannot undo what you have not noticed — which is why detection latency belongs in the same arithmetic and why [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) is a prerequisite rather than a companion.

Point 5's four enforceable things — schema, permission, precondition, rate — carry the same discipline and are developed at [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) point 2, where the enumeration's own scope statement is given.

**The platform instance, scoped in the strong form.** Point 9 teaches two build paths as an instance of the difference between an action set enumerable from outside and one that is not, and the simplification has to carry its conditions because students will otherwise apply it to procurement decisions this day does not supervise.

*Safe.* Reasoning about whether a boundary you have written can be checked against a configuration; about why a capability whose behaviour is defined by its own code cannot be audited from its declaration; and about what a person signing for a permission is actually able to enumerate. In each of those the distinction is sufficient and correct.

*Unsafe.* Reasoning about **which path is more secure**, which the distinction does not address in either direction — a narrowly configured prompt agent and a carefully written hosted agent are both defensible, and a broadly configured prompt agent is worse than either. It is also unsafe as a claim about **any other platform's split between declared and coded capability**, because the boundary between the two is a product decision and moves; and it is unsafe as an account of what the platform *can* know, since a platform that cannot enumerate a hosted agent's action set from its manifest may still constrain it at the identity, the network or the downstream system. The artefact binds the boundary rather than leaving it here: step 2 requires one sentence saying which path yields an enumerable action set and whether the student chose it, and a student who answers *the hosted path is safer* has applied the distinction outside its range in the field where it is visible.

**Point 10's defaults are perishable and ground nothing.** They are taught because an engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system, and the specification assesses none of them.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **item 2**.

A student reads a document they wrote, published, and defended in front of a partner. One word is different. The sentences that were adequate are not, and no framework is required to see which ones. The artefact is theirs, the readers were real, and the inadequacy is not a criticism of the writing — the statement was correct and remains correct; it is simply addressed to the wrong party. That is a very different experience from being shown a defective example, and it is available on exactly one day in the programme.

The second mechanism is **A4** at phase 3. The room ranks it safest, unanimously in the design's expectation, and the record contradicts them with a number rather than an argument. What makes it land is the decline-point task that follows: A1's person refused eleven times, A2's confirmations were declined twice, and A4 has no such point and no such person. **The safest-looking system is the one with no veto anywhere in it.**

The third is phase 6. The confirmation case contains no error of care at any step — the control was designed, reviewed, audited and present — and every student in the room has clicked through something. The disclosure is therefore cheap to make, which is what the [research/04-professional-formation/](/research/04-professional-formation/collected-materials.md) conditions require of a clinic case, and the cost of having ignored it is visible in the same story.

Autonomy is supported at steps 2 and 5: the capability is the student's and *no decline point exists* is a permitted and non-deficient answer. Competence is supported by the fact that the day's hardest object — the boundary — is worked with the room. Relatedness is supplied by the readers, whose responses are in the room, and by the rota member at phase 7.

**The competence risk is at step 4 and it is specific.** A student whose boundary has ten lines and nine blanks may read that as having failed the exercise. The instructor states before phase 5 that the blanks are the finding, that the cohort distribution is expected to be mostly blanks, and that [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) step 5 is where they are closed — with a named forward occasion rather than a reassurance.

The affective risk is the module's and is stated at §5.

**A standing observation, cited as pending.** Three whitepapers have independently proposed elevating to an instrumentation rule the pattern that *this design stages what it expects students to resist and asserts what it finds obvious once stated.* This day is a fourth instance: A4 is staged across a full phase because the room is expected to resist it, and point 1 — that an action has a consequence a wrong answer does not — is asserted in two sentences because it is obvious once said. The observation is cited as **pending** and nothing is renumbered; a fourth instance does not change a status already reached at three.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items. Item 1 is the rule-6 response occasion S055 named and is a re-measurement rather than a recall check. Item 2 is the day's engagement mechanism. Item 4's answer is almost always zero. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction** required by the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), administered here and not later per instrumentation rule 8, and scored in two parts — whether the agent over-grants, and *where* it writes the prohibition.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals, one scaffolded at four quarters, and a bridge that converts S031's executions into trajectories and hands the room [S072](/wiki/seminars/S072.md) a day early.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four permission models over one request, ranked before the records arrive and then **operated**, with A4 as payload and a decline-point task that generalises the finding into a procedure. The operation buys one thing the record cannot: A2 stops and waits in front of the room, and A4 raises no prompt at all, because there is nothing in it that could.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Point 2 is the phase 3 finding named. Point 4 is the argument [WP-055](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-055.md) §6 assigned here. Point 5 is what makes point 4 assessable. Point 7 carries the strong-form scope statement in the material rather than only in this paper. Point 8 is placed last of the argument because it is the point most likely to be dismissed, and its final clause turns the test on the room's own agent practice. **Points 9 and 10 are the platform layer** — one perishable platform-specific point and one naming defaults chosen for the student — and they illustrate points 5 and 7 without grounding either.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps, **producing a deployed capability rather than a proposal**. **Step 1 is the second rule-6 element S055 named**, and the reader-driven / self-driven marking is what makes it a measurement rather than an edit. Step 2 now carries the build and its one-sentence defence. Step 4's blank-line rule is the day's discriminator. **Step 6 is unsupported.** The artefact gains the prompt record, the intervention record with its acceptance count, and the frontier statement.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A control that existed, functioned and was audited, and a decision nobody made — then an agent case in which a prohibition was written correctly into the one place that constrains nothing.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** A peer constructs a permitted action and plays the decline point; **a substrate rota member then answers the same question for real, narrating rather than approving.** The rota member's presence is a correction made in §8 of this paper and is described there.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 1's cohort re-labelling result; item 2's sentence counts; item 4 against step 4; **item 5 opened and scored in two parts**; the reader-response summary with its sampling disclosed; step 6 against step 7; and the two decline-week distributions side by side.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has operated an agent professionally will produce a fluent boundary and will name enforcement for every line. Predicted failure: **fluency at step 4**, where the honest entry for most lines is a blank and a practised engineer will write a plausible mechanism instead, because naming a control is what they have always done. Countermeasure: the enforcement must be *at a named boundary* and is carried to [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) step 5, where it has to be located on the student's own control-flow drawing; a mechanism that cannot be pointed at fails there. Phase 7's partner is instructed to attack the lines that have enforcement rather than the blanks, which targets the confident entries first. This countermeasure is adequate for the boundary and, as §8 objection 1 concedes, is not adequate for the claim the boundary makes about refusal.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is step 1's volume: a sixteen-week-old document, a coded set of reader responses, and a revision with provenance marking. Countermeasures: the responses arrive coded and summarised rather than raw; the revision is a marking exercise on an existing document rather than a rewrite; step 4's form is worked with the room; and the capability at step 2 may be as small as the student likes, because the day assesses the boundary and not the ambition.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** A1 and A3 are ranked quickly and correctly — a human in the path is slower and safer, autonomy is faster and riskier — and a group that stops there has learned nothing. The difficulty is A4 against A2: the two systems that look least alike on a permissions diagram and produce the closest thing to the same failure. A group that separates them has done the day's work in phase 3. **Fallback:** ask which of the four they would deploy on the substrate this afternoon *and where they would put the veto*, which forces the decline-point analysis rather than the ranking and which no group produces well on first attempt.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **a felt loss of standing in the student's own prior work.** A statement they wrote, published and were assessed on is returned to them and shown to be addressed to the wrong party, in a module that will spend four more days finding things it did not cover. That is a different discomfort from being wrong; it is the discovery that careful work has a shelf life, and it is the exact affective shape [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md) phase 6's own clinic case had — a note that was right when written and outlived its truth.

Countermeasures: the instructor states, before phase 1, that the S055 statements were correct and remain correct, and that a routing aid becoming inadequate when the system changes is the expiry condition working rather than the document failing. Item 1 asks what intervention the student *lacks*, which locates the limit outside their diligence. The blank lines at step 4 are pre-framed as the finding with a named forward occasion. And phase 8 reports the reader responses, which for most students will include somebody who acted on their statement — the day's only evidence that the work was consequential, deliberately placed after the discomfort rather than before it.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S072](/wiki/seminars/S072.md) at one week (where the boundary meets a path set and some of the paths cross it), [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) at two weeks (where every prohibition acquires an enforcement point or an accepted re-declaration), [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) at three weeks (where the actions the boundary permitted are classified by failure shape), [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) at five weeks (where an adversary authors the input the boundary was drawn against), [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) (the safety case, where the boundary is evidence in an argument) and [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) (the handover artefact, where a boundary becomes a warranty).

**[S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) at two weeks is the primary target** and it is instrumented rather than assumed: the step 4 unenforced-boundary count is the instrument and S073 step 5 is the element that consumes it.

**[S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) at five weeks is the most consequential retrieval and the one most likely to fail.** This day's boundary is drawn against the system's own behaviour under ordinary input. S076's adversary supplies the input, which means the boundary is being asked to hold against a distribution it was never measured on — the fifth argument of a limit claim, turned on the student's own artefact. **This day cannot supply that bridge and does not attempt to**; it is named here so that S076 knows it is owed.

The [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md) retrieval at sixteen weeks is the longest load-bearing retrieval the programme performs, and it is the only one whose retrieved object is a document the student published to strangers.

Retained content: the action-against-answer difference, agency as a property of the loop, the routing-aid-against-boundary distinction, the four things that can enforce a line, and the decline-rate test. Five items.

No constituent skill is scheduled for blocked practice. The boundary-writing operation is exercised once here and then interleaved into the between-day sets alongside [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md)'s accept lists and [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s owner statements, which are the two artefacts it is most confusable with.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 4.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment judgement**, and the absence is argued on [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md) rather than left implicit: the activity that would be certified is running a system that acts, and [M16](/wiki/modules/M16.md) is where its adversary is named. The reason is stated to students before phase 1, together with the fact that specification items continue at every day of the module.

One specification item in IS302 with SG302. Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are the revised S055 statement with each revision marked reader-driven or self-driven; the proposed action; **the deployed capability with its build path named and defended in one sentence**; its reversibility class with one socially-irreversible action named; the boundary with an enforcement or a blank for every line; the decline point with its person, rate and read-rate judgement or an explicit statement that none exists; the pre-committed step 6 prediction; the shadow-run proposals with the permitted ones marked; all three phase 7 outcomes including the recorded rota narration; **the prompt record**; **the intervention record with its acceptance count**; and **the frontier statement**. **Nothing else is assessed.**

Three hard requirements: **a boundary line with no enforcement named and no blank left fails**; **a boundary that does not name what consumes the system's output fails**; and **a decline point whose rate is not stated fails.** A fourth, imported from the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md): **an intervention record with no entries fails** unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error, and on this day an enforced boundary line is the qualifying demonstration.

**The build path is not assessed for correctness**, and point 9's content grounds nothing. Either path is defensible; what is checked is that the student can say which yields an enumerable action set and whether they chose it.

Reliability is observed within the activity, evidenced by whether the enforcement points named at step 4 exist, and recorded in narrative. It is never scored as an activity.

### Discharge of the two obligations WP-055 §7 placed on this day

**This paragraph is the report [WP-055](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-055.md) §7 requires, and both obligations are discharged.**

**Obligation one — the exclusion distribution.** [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md) named **this day's phase 1** as the response occasion for its distribution of limits reported unlabelled and excluded, specifying a pretest that returns each student's own statement and requires them to state which exclusions they can now label and which they still cannot. **The element exists as phase 1 item 1.** It returns the student's own sealed exclusion list rather than a supplied one, and it requires more than S055 specified: for every exclusion still unlabelled, the student must name the intervention they lack and say whether it is available to them on the substrate. The cohort result — how many re-label, how many do not, and whether the blocking reason is an unavailable intervention or an ambiguous one — is reported at phase 8 and is the day's most quoted figure. §9 prediction 1 tests whether the instrument carries the information the discharge assumes.

**Obligation two — the reader-comprehension result.** [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md) named **this day's phase 5** as the response occasion for what substrate readers reported they stopped doing after reading the published statements, specifying that the statement be revised against reader response **before the system is given the ability to act.** **The element exists as phase 5 step 1**, it is a checked element, and each revision must be marked reader-driven or self-driven so that the readers' contribution is measurable rather than assumed. The ordering clause is satisfied structurally rather than by instruction: [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md) records that the substrate takes no action until week 71 ends, and the sequencing is disclosed to students as a design decision rather than presented as an administrative fact. §8 objection 1 concedes what that ordering guarantee is worth on a day whose only adjudicator is a shadow path, and §8 objection 2 concedes what the respondent sample omits.

**The argument S055 declined to make.** [WP-055](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-055.md) §6 recorded that *a limits statement written for a system that answers is a routing aid; the same statement for a system that acts is a boundary, and the two are not the same document*, that S055 did not make the argument, and that **S071 must**. **It is made, at phase 4 point 4, and it is made assessable at point 5** — the operative question is what other than the sentence prevents each line, and the specification's hard requirement attaches to the answer rather than to the distinction. The design's position is that stating the distinction without point 5 would have discharged the obligation in form and produced a cohort able to name two document types and unable to write the second.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions, both inside this module. **The step 4 unenforced-boundary count** responds at [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) phase 5 step 5, where every blank must acquire an enforcement point at a named boundary or be re-declared as accepted with probability, consequence and confidence in the same field. **The step 6-against-step 7 prediction accuracy** responds at [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) phase 5 step 3, where every unanticipated shadow-run proposal is classified by failure shape, so the four-shape decomposition is exercised on the cohort's own blind spot rather than on supplied cases. **[WP-073](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-073.md) §7 and [WP-074](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-074.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

Programme instrumentation: the item 1 re-labelling rate; the item 4 against step 4 boundary-count gap; the step 4 blank distribution; the two phase 7 decline-week distributions and the gap between them; the count of shadow-run proposals each boundary permits; **the item 5 generic-against-specific prediction rates, which are this day's contribution to the running frontier map and are read by the following year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), per instrumentation rule 9**; and **the intervention records' acceptance counts**, which are the instrument §8 objection 3 introduces and §9 prediction 6 tests.

**The frontier statements are a sample and the day says so**, per instrumentation rule 3: one cohort, one substrate, one capability class, on a task chosen by staff so that a known failure is available. A frontier map assembled from them describes this day's task and not the agent, and no student is asked to conclude otherwise.

ABET and CS2023 contribution: PO2 and PO6.

**What this day does not license anyone to conclude.** A student's boundary is evidence that they can convert a limits statement into a list of prohibited actions and can say, for each, what would prevent it. **It is not evidence that any action was prevented**, because nothing on this day was refused — the shadow path records proposals and executes nothing. It is not evidence about behaviour under an adversary, which is [M16](/wiki/modules/M16.md)'s, and it is not evidence about the boundary's coverage, since the only test of coverage the day contains is one peer for thirty minutes.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: a shadow path cannot test a boundary, because it never tries.**

At its strongest: the day's central claim is that a boundary differs from a routing aid in that a boundary causes an action not to occur. That is a claim about what happens when the system attempts a prohibited action. **Step 7 runs a shadow path, on which nothing is attempted and nothing is refused.** It produces a list of actions the system proposed, and the student marks which of them their boundary permits — which is a comparison between a document and a list, performed by the author of both. The boundary is never asked to hold. Its enforcement points are never invoked. A boundary consisting entirely of enforcement mechanisms that do not work would produce an identical artefact and an identical phase 8.

The same objection weakens the day's rule-6 discharge. [WP-055](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-055.md) required the statement to be revised **before the system is given the ability to act**, and the design reports that clause satisfied because the substrate cannot act until the week ends. That is true and it is nearly vacuous: the revision precedes an action capability that no student exercises inside the day, so the discharge establishes an ordering rather than an effect. Nothing in the day shows that the revision changed what any system did.

**The cheap fix, named precisely, and declined.** Implement one boundary — pooled from the cohort at the end of phase 5, or the substrate's own — as an actual deny rule on the gateway, run the shadow path against it, and report the refusals. It costs one gateway configuration and no phase time, since the shadow run already happens. The design declined it because a pooled boundary is nobody's, and the day's whole force comes from the artefact being the student's own; thirty individual boundaries cannot be simultaneously enforced on one gateway, and serialising them would cost a phase the day does not have.

**That reason is a preference and it does not outweigh what it costs.** The design chose individual attribution over having any evidence of refusal at all, and the honest statement is that a pooled refusal count, attributable to nobody, would have told the cohort something the day currently cannot tell them: that an enforcement point either works or does not. **The objection is conceded and not answered.** [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) step 5 locates enforcement at a named boundary and [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) attacks it, so the programme obtains the evidence eventually; this day, which is the day that makes the claim, does not.

**Objection 2: the day estimates with a peer a judgement an available external observer was positioned to make — and the remedy was already in the design's repertoire.**

At its strongest: phase 7 asks whether a person could read confirmations arriving at the student's stated rate, and it asked a **peer** to answer — a student who has spent six hours on the material, who has just written a decline point of their own, and who receives no confirmations from anything. **The substrate's rota members receive exactly these confirmations from existing automations.** They are the people whose judgement the question is about, they are already scheduled into the module, and [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) step 5 had already established both the practice of bringing one into the room and the correct form of the question — narrate what you would do, rather than say whether this is acceptable, because a person asked whether an alert is acceptable will say yes.

This is an instrumentation rule 1 violation of the shape rule 1 was written for at [WP-024](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-024.md) §8 and repeated at [WP-037](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-037.md) §8. It is also an **instrumentation rule 5 failure**, which is worse: the remedy existed, in this programme, three days of teaching earlier, in a structurally identical situation, and was not carried across. Rule 5 exists precisely because that keeps happening.

**The day is fixed rather than recorded**, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s criterion — this is a violation of a written rule and not a design judgement between two defensible options. A rota member is present for the last twenty minutes of phase 7, answers the decline-week question for each student's stated rate, and narrates rather than approves. Both distributions are reported at phase 8 and the gap between them is the point. The correction is annotated in the day at the point of change. **A compliance-register entry is required and this paper cannot make it**, since the register lives in a shared page; the entry is stated in full in §9's instrumentation note and must be added by whoever maintains the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md).

**What remains wrong is conceded.** The rota member is being asked about a rate the student's system does not yet produce, on a capability that does not yet run. They are predicting their own future behaviour, which is self-report relocated to a better-qualified person rather than an observation. **The only instrument that would settle it is the student's own confirmation stream after the capability runs, and that data will exist by [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md).** The design has not built an element there to consume it, because S079's instrument slots are already committed to [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s and [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md)'s results, and adding a third would displace one of them. That is a scheduling reason and it is a real one; it is also the fourth time in this programme that a measurement has been made with the second-best available observer because the best one was inconvenient, and recording it is the minimum.

**Objection 3: the day applies its own decline-rate test to the students' agent practice and then omits the denominator the test requires.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn, and it bites because the day invites it.

At its strongest: point 8 establishes that a confirmation step is a control only if the person's decline rate is distinguishable from zero, and its final clause says in terms that this applies to the room — an intervention record is a confirmation step with the student as approver. The day then requires an intervention record. **An intervention record is a list of declines.** It contains no count of the outputs that were accepted, so the rate point 8 makes the operative property cannot be computed from it, by the day that made it operative. A student who corrected the agent three times in a hundred acceptances and a student who corrected it three times in five produce identical artefacts, and the day's own argument is that those are different engineering events. **The [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s fail condition — no entries fails — tests whether the numerator is greater than zero**, which is the weakest possible reading of a test the same programme has spent a day arguing must be a rate.

The objection generalises further than this day and the paper states so rather than confining it. Every seminar day in the programme from week one carries an intervention record; none carries a denominator; and the programme's running claim about frontier calibration is assembled from numerators.

**The fix is one field and it is made.** Step 5's intervention record now requires the number of agent outputs accepted without change alongside the corrections. It costs a line in the artefact and no phase time.

**Two things are conceded about the fix.** First, the denominator is self-reported, and a student who is not reading the output is also not counting it — which means the instrument is weakest exactly where it matters most, and no better instrument is available inside a six-hour day. Second, **the fix is proposed and is not exported.** Per instrumentation rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8, a structure the design has invented and not run is used at the day proposing it and enters the repertoire only once that day's outcome is reported, so [S072](/wiki/seminars/S072.md) to [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md) do not carry it and the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) is not amended by this paper. **If it works, it belongs on that page and not on this one**, and §9 states the wording so that whoever maintains the page can act on it without re-deriving the argument.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Fewer than half of the exclusions each student sealed at [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md) will be re-labellable at item 1, and the modal stated reason for the remainder will be an intervention that is unavailable rather than an intervention that was run and gave a mixed result. *Instrument:* item 1 responses coded label-now / cannot-label, with cannot-label reasons coded by whether the named intervention is available on the substrate. **If the modal reason is a mixed result instead, S055's exclusion route was being used to avoid a judgement rather than to record an inconclusive one**, and S055's humility measure means something different from what that day claimed.

**Prediction 2.** More than half of all step 4 boundary lines will carry a blank enforcement field on first submission, and the distribution will be bimodal — a small number of students with almost none, and most with almost all. *Instrument:* step 4 lines coded for enforcement presence, per student, before and after the [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) step 5 revision. **If blanks are rare, either the boundaries are trivial or students are naming enforcement that does not exist**; the two are distinguished at S073 step 5, where the enforcement must be located on the student's own control-flow drawing.

**Prediction 3.** The rota member's decline-week estimates will be systematically earlier than the peers' for the same stated rates. *Instrument:* phase 7's two distributions compared by median week per student. **If the peers are earlier or the two agree, the rule 1 correction bought nothing on this day**, and §8 objection 2's fix is correct in principle and empty in practice — which would be a finding about rule 1's reach rather than about this day.

**Prediction 4.** A majority of step 6 predictions will name no action that appears in the step 7 shadow run, and the modal missed action will be one whose reversal is social rather than technical. *Instrument:* step 6 and step 7 pairs coded for match; misses coded by reversibility class. **This is the prediction the module most needs to be wrong about**, because [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) step 3 is built to consume the unanticipated set and a small set makes that element thin. If the misses concentrate on socially-irreversible actions, point 7's scope statement is the day's most load-bearing sentence and is currently carried by one artefact clause.

**Prediction 5.** At item 5, a large majority will predict that the agent grants more than asked, and a small minority will predict *where* — that the prohibition is expressed in the agent's instructions rather than in a schema, a permission, a precondition or a rate. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded generic / specific, against what each student's agent actually produced at step 2, with the location of the produced prohibition coded into the same six categories used at [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) phase 8. **The gap between the two rates is this day's frontier-calibration measure**, and the design's expectation is that it is wide. **If the specific rate is high, the cohort already holds point 5 and the day's central instruction is redundant**, which would be the most surprising result in the module and would mean two years of agent practice taught something the design assumed it did not.

**Prediction 6.** The intervention records' acceptance counts will be **absent or implausibly round** for a substantial minority of artefacts on first submission. *Instrument:* step 5 acceptance counts coded present / absent / round-number, joined to the correction count. **A missing denominator is the finding**, not a paperwork failure: it is direct evidence for §8 objection 3's claim that a student who is not reading the output is also not counting it, and it is the datum that decides whether the field should be proposed to the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) or withdrawn.

**Instrumentation note, for the compliance register.** The entry this paper's §8 objection 2 requires, stated so that it can be transcribed without re-derivation: *[WP-071](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-071.md) §8 — [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md) phase 7 measured with a peer a judgement an available external observer was positioned to make, and did not carry [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) step 5's remedy across. Rule: instrumentation rules 1 and 5. Status: **Fixed**. Reason: violation of two written rules, with the remedy already in the design's repertoire; no design judgement was in contest.*

**A second note, for the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), argued and not enacted.** §8 objection 3's fix, stated so it can be transcribed if this day's outcome supports it: **"The intervention record states the number of agent outputs accepted without change alongside the corrections. A record of corrections without a count of acceptances is a numerator without a denominator, and the decline-rate test [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md) point 8 establishes cannot be computed from it."** Provenance: proposed at [WP-071](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-071.md) §8, used at [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md) only, contingent under instrumentation rule 5 as amended, and tested by §9 prediction 6. **This paper cannot edit a shared page and does not.**

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That a limits statement written for a system which answers is a routing aid, that the same statement for a system which acts is a boundary, and that the two are not the same document. Agency is a property of the loop, not of the component: an answer something else acts on is an action.

**What the evidence licenses.** The instability of model-computed decisions between runs on the same input, and the planning limitations that make a boundary structural rather than a prompting problem, are **class 1**. The build paths, identities, endpoints and session lifetimes are **class 4**: documented, undated, with adjacent capabilities marked preview and **no rate published for anything** — not for how often an approval is declined, not for how often any boundary refuses. The four permission models, the four enforceable things, the blank-line rule and the decline-rate test are **class 3**, untested.

**What to do with it.**

- **For every prohibited line, ask what other than the sentence prevents it** — and leave the blank rather than deleting the line.
- **A written boundary is a configured control and not evidence** — see the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). It licenses that the constraint was stated; nothing about whether it was ever applied.
- **A confirmation step is a control only if someone's decline rate is distinguishable from zero.** That includes yours.
- **Count what you accepted, not only what you corrected.** A list of corrections is a numerator.

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes and does not answer that a shadow path cannot test a boundary, because it never tries: nothing is refused, and a boundary made entirely of mechanisms that do not work would produce an identical artefact. **Hold the design to that.** The pooled-enforcement fix is named, and the reason for declining it — a preference for evidence attributable to an individual — is one the paper judges does not outweigh having no refusal evidence.
