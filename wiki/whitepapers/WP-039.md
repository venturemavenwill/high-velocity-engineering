## In plain terms

The prototype has done its work. Somebody now has to give something up, or the stage produced an interesting fortnight and nothing else.

The failure this session exists to prevent is not being refused. **It is leaving with neither a commitment nor a refusal** — the warm meeting, the enthusiasm, the request to be kept posted. That outcome looks like progress, consumes the same calendar as progress, and requires courage from nobody.

So the ask is written backwards. **You write the refusal first**, and you write it as something you could receive and still work with the person the next morning. An ask whose author cannot describe a survivable no has not designed one; they have designed an announcement with a question mark, and the commitment it extracts will be withdrawn silently during delivery.

Then you pick the currency, and the currency is the evidence. Time, reputation and money are what a person can actually give, and the strength of the evidence rises with how real the cost is to them. **A free trial account is not a commitment. Onboarding a team, supplying real data, changing somebody's workflow, or agreeing to be named, is.**

The session's warning comes from the corpus and is easy to misread as an advantage. **Working software is hard to argue with**, and political resistance dissolves in front of a running prototype in a way it does not in front of a document. That is true, and it means a prototype can dissolve resistance it has not earned the right to dissolve — buying you a commitment against evidence you do not have, which you will then be delivering against.

The last thing the session does is price the ask correctly, and this is where the platform enters. **The natural sentence in the room is *so we just move it to production*, and it is wrong in a way you can prove with a documentation page.** Microsoft describes its own prototyping topology as being for rapid prototyping and individual development, for speed and ease of access, and states that it **lacks network isolation**; the production topology is described as providing fine-grained control over data, security and networking. **Every property that made the prototype fast is named as a property of the prototyping shape.** Moving changes what the system can reach, who can reach it, and what has to be provisioned and owned. **That is a re-architecture, and it is what the ask is actually funding.** An ask sized to a switch and an ask sized to a re-architecture are different requests, frequently addressed to different people.

## Abstract

This paper defends converting proof into a costly, refusable ask, and treats the refusal path as the design's first-written component. The argument is that enthusiasm is generated by the social situation rather than by the evidence, that only a cost the giver notices carries information, and that an ask which cannot be declined produces a commitment that will be withdrawn silently rather than negotiated.

It then argues that the ask is almost always mispriced, and uses the vendor's own documented split between a prototyping topology and a production one to show why. **Promotion is not a switch. It is a set of named changes with owners**, including the removal of the property — ease of access without network isolation — that made the proof fast in the first place. A proposal that funds a verb rather than a list has under-priced its own request, and the shortfall is discovered during delivery.

The session installs a one-page proposal naming the ask, its currency, what it buys the giver, the evidence it rests on including what the run could not have detected, the itemised transition it funds, the refusal path, a trip wire and the next state. The paper concedes that the framework's source reports no measured effect and no threshold, that the negotiation source is conceptual and case-based rather than an evaluation, that the corpus's two supporting observations are unevidenced practitioner judgement from a body of work that records no refusal because it records no difficulty, and that **the instruction to write the refusal first has no external warrant at all.**

## 1. The problem this session must solve

At the end of a proof, an engineer has evidence and a relationship, and the relationship is generating a signal that looks exactly like the evidence.

**The signal is enthusiasm and it is produced by the situation.** Somebody has been shown a thing built for them, by a person who is present, at the end of an effort they can see. Warmth is the socially normal response, it costs nothing, and it will be reported upward as validation because nobody in the chain has a reason to discount it.

There is a second and worse outcome, which is the one that consumes a quarter. The stakeholder is genuinely interested, genuinely busy, and genuinely non-committal. Meetings recur, the relationship deepens, and no resource moves. **The engagement is now indistinguishable from a live one and is producing nothing**, and the only person who could end it is the one who would have to admit it.

The session's response is to require a costly ask with a written, survivable refusal, and to treat a clear rejection as a result.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** [S019](/wiki/seminars/S019.md) established that a commitment ask has succeeded when a commitment was made *or explicitly refused*, and produced a commitment ledger. This session makes the second half operational and adds the currency question. [S037](/wiki/seminars/S037.md) supplies the failure account, which belongs in the proposal's evidence section rather than being quietly omitted from it. [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md) supplies the populations, and the dependency is direct: **an ask built on a merged population asks the wrong person for the wrong currency.**

[S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md) supplies the cost of doing nothing, and the session requires it stated honestly rather than deployed as pressure — which is the distinction the whole design turns on.

The prior the session works against is that asking is the easy part. Learners treat the ask as a formality following the work, and treat a refusal as a failure of the work rather than as an output of the ask.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate, with the difficulty concentrated in one place: writing an ask you would genuinely accept a no to, when you have spent a fortnight producing the thing you are asking about.

**3.2 Why cost is the evidence.** A response that costs the responder nothing carries no information about their belief, because it would have been given regardless. **The information content of a commitment rises with what it cost the giver**, and the three currencies are the practical forms that cost takes.

**3.3 Why advancement needs a named next state.** A relationship that has *improved* has not moved. A next state expressed as an event on a date can be checked, missed and noticed; a next state expressed as a disposition cannot.

**3.4 Why the refusal is written first.** Writing the ask first produces an ask optimised to be accepted, and the refusal path is then reverse-engineered to be one nobody would take. Writing the refusal first constrains the ask to one that can be declined. **This is a sequencing claim with no external warrant, and §8 takes it seriously.**

**3.5 Why a running prototype is a hazard as well as an instrument.** Resistance that dissolves in front of working software has not been answered — it has been outflanked. The engineer is the only person in the room who knows which of their claims the prototype actually tested, and so is the only person who can price the ask correctly.

**3.6 The refusal path has a name in the negotiation literature, and naming it correctly changes what the session asks for.**

The session's *refusal you would accept* is, in the negotiation framework, not one object but three, and conflating them is the commonest error. A **bottom line** is a rigid acceptance boundary; the literature criticises it because it hides information and blocks consideration of packages that meet interests unexpectedly. A **BATNA** is the best action actually available if no agreement is reached, and the point most often lost is that it is **developed rather than named** — you generate what you could do without the commitment, improve the best of it, and select. **An unknown BATNA is dangerous in both directions**: you may accept less than the alternative, or reject more. A **trip wire** is a threshold set in advance, less favourable than the hoped-for result and better than the alternative, whose function is to force a pause before drift ([research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md)).

Two further instruments enter the session's artefact directly. A **framework agreement** is an agreement-shaped draft with the unresolved terms left visibly open, which is a better proposal than one pretending everything is settled. And the design rule the literature states plainly — **make the decision easy to take and easy to justify to people who are not in the room** — is why the review movement asks a second question about whether the sponsor could explain the yes without the learner present. The same source names the pressure tactics a proposal should not contain: calculated delay, escalating demands after apparent progress, the hardhearted absent partner, and take-it-or-leave-it presented as though no process choice remained. **A deadline used as leverage is on that list, which is why it appears in this session as a warning sign in the learner's own document rather than as a technique.**

**The limits matter and one of them constrains the session's rhetoric.** The framework is conceptual and case-based, developed through analytic distinctions, worked examples and diplomatic and commercial cases; **it is not a controlled evaluation of negotiation methods**, and historical cases can illustrate that a move works without isolating a cause. **The note records that its translation into delivery work is course-design inference and not a claim the authors make.** And the caution the session repeats verbatim: **not every constraint is negotiable.** A regulator, an accountable risk owner or a system owner may hold legitimate decision authority, and clarifying interests is not the same as holding shared decision rights.

**3.7 Why promotion is a re-architecture, and where the documentation actually says so.**

The session's platform claim is that the ask is systematically mispriced because the transition it funds is described with a verb. The vendor's own setup guidance is the cheapest available disproof of the verb.

The **basic setup** is documented as being **"for rapid prototyping and individual development when speed and ease of access are priorities"** and as one that **"lacks network isolation"**. The **standard setup** provides **"fine-grained control over data, security, and networking"**, with a public-networking variant for non-confidential workloads needing enterprise data controls and a private-networking variant for confidential workloads with strict compliance requirements ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)).

**The properties that made the proof fast are named as properties of the prototyping shape.** They are not oversights. Removing them changes the set of things the system can reach, the set of people who can reach it, and creates a body of provisioning that has to be owned. The documentation's own framing is the one the session adopts: **prototyping topology and production topology are different things, and the transition between them is a deliberate act** rather than a configuration detail discovered at the end.

Two further seams in the same family are decisions taken early that bind late. The **hosting fork** separates declarative agents **"managed by Foundry with no agent compute"** from hosted agents running **"your code (containerized)"** on managed container compute — the line is whether somebody is operating compute, and a proof built on one side and a commitment sized for the other are different requests. And the **distribution constraint**: only agents built on named build paths **"can be published to the Microsoft Commercial Store with the Agents Toolkit"**, so a route to market is foreclosed by a decision taken before anybody thought about routes to market. The same rapid-versus-controlled trade recurs at the integration layer, where the fast path **"automatically provisions Azure Bot Service and Entra ID"** — **provisioning things on the learner's behalf that somebody will later have to own**, which the proposal must name.

**The limits are heavy.** First-party selection guidance with definitional authority; **no adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no evidence that its recommended mappings produce better outcomes**; one vendor's options compared against each other; read through search excerpts with several comparison tables truncated; and **highly perishable**, since setup names and boundaries are exactly what moves between releases. **The documentation states that the topologies differ; it says nothing about how a transition should be funded, priced or asked for. That is this design's inference.**

**3.8 Why the proposal must say what the evidence could not have detected.**

An evaluation is an experiment and inherits every limit of one. The limit that matters in a proposal is **power**: a run that could not have detected the difference it was looking for has established nothing, and nothing in the result says so ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)).

The consequence for an ask is direct and is why the session adds a field rather than a paragraph. **In a proposal, *we tested and found no problem* and *we could not have found one* are the same sentence to every reader who was not there** — and the first will be quoted back at the engineer in three months as though it were evidence. Requiring the distinction in writing is the only available defence, and it costs one line. **That source is analytical rather than empirical, its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions, and it addresses comparing scores rather than choosing what to score.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The movements run situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**Opening item 2 is the instrument.** The learner is asked to write the refusal they would accept, before anything is taught. Most cannot, and the ones who can typically describe a refusal that would end the relationship — which is the same failure in a different direction.

**The currency is chosen before the ask is drafted**, structurally, because a learner who drafts first will choose the currency their draft happens to imply.

**The proposal must state what the prototype did not establish.** This is the corpus's *working software is hard to argue with* observation, converted into a countermeasure: the engineer discloses the gap the prototype is papering over, in the document that asks for the resource. **Nothing forces this in practice and the design says so** — it is a professional norm the programme is trying to install at the point where it is cheapest to hold.

**The process-ownership check is a separate move.** If the ask requires somebody to stop doing the thing that makes them useful, the currency is not time but standing, and it must be priced and named as such. This comes from the corpus's observation that resistance originates in ownership of process rather than in technical incapacity, and it is the only place in the module where an ask is repriced upward.

**The transition is itemised against the documented seam, and the page is dated.** The learner opens the setup guidance, works out what changes for their own artefact — what network isolation now excludes, who owns the resources the fast path provisioned, whether anybody is now operating compute — and writes it as a list with owners. **The movement takes about twenty minutes and reliably produces a longer list than the room expects**, which is the design's cheapest demonstration that *productionise* is a verb standing in for work nobody has counted. The date is required because the seam moves.

**The BATNA is developed rather than named, and the trip wire is set before the ask.** The learner writes what they would actually do without the commitment, improves the best option, and sets a threshold — observable by somebody else — at which they will stop even if nobody refuses. **The trip wire is the only instrument in the module aimed at the failure that has no villain**, where sunk cost and executive attention convert a drifting engagement into the only visible path.

**The adversarial rehearsal is briefed on interests rather than on the proposal.** An agent playing the stakeholder is given their position and instructed to refuse. **The refusals the learner had no answer to are the parts of the proposal carried by enthusiasm**, and they will not survive a real room either.

**The review movement asks one question: could I say no to this and still work with you tomorrow?** A no to that question means the ask is coercive regardless of intent.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The friendly nothing banked as progress.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* the required next state expressed as an event on a date, and the standard's warning sign — your next state is *stay in touch*.

**The unrefusable ask.** *Countermeasure:* the refusal-first sequence and the review movement's single question.

**Enthusiasm recorded as commitment.** *Countermeasure:* the currency requirement, which forces the learner to name what was actually given up, and [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md)'s compliment-marking discipline arriving one session later.

**The oversold prototype.** The ask is proportionate to a system rather than to the evidence. *Countermeasure:* the required statement of what the prototype did not establish, and the carry-forward of [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s blocking absences.

**The deadline as leverage.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — your most persuasive sentence is about time running out — and [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md)'s requirement that the cost of doing nothing be sized honestly rather than deployed.

**The wrong authority.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — they said yes immediately and cheerfully — which is usually a sign of somebody agreeing to something they do not control.

**Promotion mistaken for a switch.** The ask funds a verb and the work is a re-architecture. *Countermeasure:* the itemised transition against the documented seam, with owners, and the standard's warning sign — the proposal contains the word *productionise* and no list.

**The orphaned provision.** The fast integration path provisioned identity and bot resources on the learner's behalf and nobody was named to own them. *Countermeasure:* the transition list's ownership column. *Residual risk:* the learner has to know the provisioning happened, and the fast path is designed so that they do not have to.

**The stale seam.** The transition was costed against documentation that has since moved. *Countermeasure:* the dated page and the perishability register. **This countermeasure is weak by construction** — it detects the staleness only if somebody re-reads.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. Four specification items at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md): that the commitment or refusal is described in its currency with what it cost the giver; that the pack states what the giver could have refused; that the transition the ask funds is itemised with owners rather than named; and that the evidence section distinguishes what was tested from what the run could not have detected.

**Never assessed:** whether the commitment was obtained, its size, whether the stakeholder was pleased, or whether the transition estimate was accurate.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: writing the refusal first is a curriculum affectation, and in commercial practice an engineer who designs for their own rejection will be replaced by one who does not.**

At its strongest: sales is a real discipline with its own accumulated craft, and none of it recommends leading with the exit. The organisations that fund this work reward people who close, and a graduate who has been trained to make declining comfortable will be out-competed inside their own firm by colleagues who make it uncomfortable. Teaching this as professional practice, rather than as an ethical preference, misrepresents the environment learners are entering.

**Partly conceded, and the concession is about the environment rather than about the technique.** The design's rebuttal is narrow: the commitments this session is trying to obtain are engineering resources — specialist time, real data, a review owner, a workflow change — and those are given by people who can withdraw them silently at any point. **A commitment extracted from somebody who could not refuse is withdrawn during delivery rather than at the ask**, when it costs the engagement rather than the meeting. **The declined fix is to teach the ask conventionally and add a note about coercion.** It is declined because the sequencing is the only mechanism the design has: a note about coercion is advice inside a system that rewards closing, and [M03](/wiki/modules/M03.md) established what happens to advisory guidance inside a system of incentives. **Whether the sequencing survives commercial pressure is untested and no source behind this session addresses it.**

**Objection 2: the currency framework is a startup handbook's heuristic applied to enterprise engineering, and the transfer has not been checked.**

At its strongest: the source is written for founders talking to prospective customers about products that do not exist. An engineer embedded with an enterprise customer under a signed agreement is in a different structural position — the relationship is already funded, the currencies are governed by procurement rather than by individual enthusiasm, and *reputational exposure* means something entirely different when the person is an employee rather than a buyer. Importing the vocabulary wholesale produces a plausible-sounding framework whose terms have quietly changed meaning.

**Conceded.** The source is explicit about its own scope and this repository records that it establishes no effect and no threshold. The design's response is to use the framework as a diagnostic vocabulary — three questions about what was given up, by whom, and at what cost — rather than as a model of enterprise buying. **The declined fix is to replace the three currencies with an enterprise-specific list: named specialist time, representative data access, a security-review owner, a pilot cohort, workflow change, procurement engagement, budget sponsorship.** It is declined for one reason only: that list is a set of *instances*, and the session's claim is about the underlying property — what would the giver notice losing — which the instances would displace. **The instances are supplied in the session as examples and the design accepts that a learner may reasonably prefer them to the abstraction.**

**Objection 3: itemising a topology transition is not the engineer's job, and a proposal that does it is a proposal written by somebody who has confused their role with the platform team's.**

At its strongest: in most organisations the move from a prototype environment to a governed one is owned by a platform, security or cloud-operations function with its own standards, its own change process and its own view of what network isolation means locally. **An engineer who arrives with a costed transition list has produced an estimate for work they will not do, using a vendor's generic description of a topology their employer has almost certainly customised.** The list will be wrong in detail, it will be treated as a commitment because it is written down, and its main effect will be to annoy the people who actually own the decision. The honest version of the ask is *this needs to move to a governed environment, and that team should scope it.*

**Conceded on the estimate and rejected on the itemisation, and the distinction is the whole of the rebuttal.** The design does not ask the learner to cost the transition; it asks them to **enumerate what changes and name who owns each item**, which is a different artefact and a much weaker claim. The reason it belongs in the ask rather than in a later conversation is that an ask sized to *turn it on* obtains a commitment sized to *turn it on*, and the shortfall surfaces during delivery, when it is expensive and when the engineer has no standing left to reopen it. **The declined fix was to remove the transition section and add a line saying that production readiness is out of scope.** It was declined because that line is exactly what a sponsor reads as *no further cost*, and the paper would rather teach an enumeration a platform team will correct than a silence they will discover. **The residual cost is real and is accepted: some learners will present a generic list as though it were a local one, and §9 prediction 6 is the only check available.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Before instruction, most learners will be unable to write an acceptable refusal at opening item 2, or will write one that ends the relationship. *Instrument:* the written answers, coded *survivable* / *relationship-ending* / *absent* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Proposals will more often name time as the currency than reputation or money, regardless of what the evidence supports. *Instrument:* the currency field, tabulated, against the ask's described content.

**Prediction 3.** In the adversarial rehearsal, the refusals learners cannot answer will cluster on the claims the prototype did not test. *Instrument:* the recorded unanswered refusals, mapped against the proposal's own statement of what the prototype did not establish.

**Prediction 4.** Learners who wrote the refusal before the ask will produce asks judged non-coercive by the review movement more often than those who wrote in the conventional order. *Instrument:* the review's binary judgement against writing order, collected by requiring the two components to be submitted separately and time-stamped. **This is the only test the design has of §3.4, which is otherwise unwarranted.**

**Prediction 5.** Most transition lists will contain at least one item requiring somebody outside the learner's team, and most learners will not have spoken to that person before submitting. *Instrument:* the ownership column of the transition list, against a single post-hoc question about whether the named owner was contacted. **If most learners did contact them, the design's assumption that the platform boundary is invisible from inside a proof is wrong**, and the movement is teaching something learners already do.

**Prediction 6.** A substantial share of transition lists will reproduce the vendor's generic description without a single organisation-specific item. *Instrument:* the list, coded *generic only* / *at least one local item* by a reader familiar with the setting. **This predicts the failure of §8's third rebuttal**, which rests entirely on the enumeration being locally grounded rather than copied.

**Prediction 7.** Trip wires will be set at thresholds that are not observable by anybody other than the learner. *Instrument:* the trip-wire field, coded *externally observable* / *self-assessed* / *absent* by a second reader. **This predicts the failure of the session's own countermeasure for drift**, and it is included because a threshold only the drifting party can evaluate is not a control at all — which is the same defect [M03](/wiki/modules/M03.md) identified in advisory guidance sitting inside a system of incentives.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That enthusiasm is produced by the situation, that only a noticed cost carries information, that the worst outcome available is neither a yes nor a no, and that **promotion is a re-architecture rather than a switch.**

**What the evidence licenses.** A diagnostic vocabulary from a handbook that reports no measured effect and says so; a negotiation framework that is conceptual and case-based rather than an evaluation, and whose delivery translations are explicitly course-design inference; two practitioner observations from a corpus with no evidence base; and a documented distinction between two deployment topologies from a vendor that publishes **no outcome data of any kind.** **Direction and mechanism only. No magnitude is asserted.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The corpus that tells you working software dissolves resistance has never recorded an engagement in which resistance won. Treat the observation as a hazard warning rather than as a technique. And note what the platform section does **not** license: the documentation says the two topologies differ, and says nothing at all about how a transition between them should be funded or asked for. **That inference is this programme's, and a platform team will correct your list. Let them.**
