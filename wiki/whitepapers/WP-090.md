# WP-090 — Entrustment: The Final Defence

**Seminar day:** [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md) · **Module:** [M18](wiki/modules/M18.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](wiki/quarters/Q9.md)
**Strands:** all seven · **Complexity class:** 6 · **Assessment-bearing** · **Closing whitepaper**
**Platform anchor:** the whole stack, handed over — the student's own declarations, deployment stack, project and agent identities, registry entries, evaluation harness, trace store, instructions and prompt files · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (write) · Foundry MCP Server (preview) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server, all three withdrawn for phase 1b

## Abstract

This paper defends the programme's final entrustment occasion and, being the last, defends the programme. It defends carrying all three entrusted activities on one day, defends judging them on the student's own three-year substrate work, and defends forming each level from an artefact before its defence. It defends a handover activity whose measurement is the receiving party's silence-protected answers and their attempt to operate the system, rather than the student's presentation, and defends naming six failure conditions in advance and two non-failures alongside them. It defends lengthening the day to three hundred and fifty minutes to carry a **bounded unassisted build** — the programme's third and last occasion of building without an agent — rather than shortening four assessed phases to make room for something explicitly not assessed. It defends closing four long-horizon prediction checks and a ninety-day frontier map, and reporting to the cohort what the design learned, including where it learned nothing. **Section 7 reports eight rule-6 discharges, a commitment made in week five, the unassisted-build commitment [WP-045](wiki/whitepapers/WP-045.md) §8 made, and the state of two pending rule elevations at programme close.** Section 8 states what this programme most likely gets wrong: not its content, its assessment scale or its substrate, but its instrumentation — it has produced several hundred falsifiable predictions and has never named who will test them. It also answers, as honestly as it can, whether the practical turn worked and what would show that it did not.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The problem is not instructional. Nothing is taught, and the day's only content is the framing sentence phase 2's bridge produces.

The problem is **evidentiary**, and it has three parts.

**The design has to certify a decision it has told everyone it cannot make reliably.** Rater discipline rule 6 requires the programme to publish, to students and reviewers, that rater variance in this class of judgement is large and that human agreement in comparable judgement tasks has a documented ceiling. It does publish that. It then awards three ordinal levels on one afternoon, from two assessors, with no prior level carried forward. Every one of those choices is defensible individually and the combination is uncomfortable, and this paper does not have the option of pretending otherwise.

**The activities are not commensurable and are judged in one room.** EA-2 is a transfer whose evidence is what somebody else can do afterwards. EA-3 is a communication whose evidence is what somebody else decided. EA-1 is a judgement whose evidence is what the student was willing to be wrong about. They recruit different capabilities, they fail differently, and a design that ran them consecutively risks an assessor forming one impression of a student in the first hour and confirming it twice — which is exactly what rater discipline rule 4 names as the site of variance. **The countermeasure is structural and is the day's principal design decision:** each level is formed from its own artefact, before its own defence, and the three artefacts were written in three different weeks.

**The occasion has to close the programme's own instrumentation without becoming a ceremony.** Four long-horizon prediction checks are open. Ninety sealed Copilot predictions terminate here. Ninety days of paragraphs, predictions and sealed items terminate here. A day that resolved all of that in a warm closing session would be the most comfortable failure available, and the cohort would leave believing they had been summed up rather than judged. Phase 6 is placed after the levels are sealed for that reason, and phase 8's closing paragraph states what the programme claims to have certified in the narrowest terms it can defend.

**And the occasion has to test the one thing the programme has claimed for three years and never observed.** Every produced artefact in this degree was built with an agent. The [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) states the risk plainly — a student who has only ever built with an agent may be unable to build without one, and this programme cannot currently detect that — and names three occasions as the whole of its structural response. Two have run. The third is here, and [WP-045](wiki/whitepapers/WP-045.md) §8 objection 3 recorded that the second one tested judgement and left construction untested. **A bounded unassisted build is therefore the last thing the programme owes itself, it does not fit inside any assessed phase, and the design lengthened the day rather than taking minutes from the judgement.** §3.1 states what that costs and §8 objection 4 states what it cannot rule out.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge assumptions

- **The student's own [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)–[S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) artefacts** on one component, produced across four consecutive weeks: a measured transfer gap, a bounded safety case, a residual-risk statement and a handover artefact with a warranty.
- **Three years of substrate ownership**, including the component being handed over.
- **Nine prior entrustment occasions** at [S030](wiki/seminars/S030.md), [S040](wiki/seminars/S040.md), [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md), [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md), [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md), [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md), [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md), [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md) and [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md). **None carried forward, and the students know the convention.**
- **[S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s eight-item decision artefact**, reduced to six here, and its perturbation set, which is EA-1's own.
- **[S083](wiki/seminars/S083.md)'s risk communication and [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md)'s scope negotiation**, both within the quarter — and **[S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md)'s element 2 absent-party concession record and [S084](wiki/seminars/S084.md)'s six-week adoption predictions**, both of which come due here.
- **A real receiving party, a real non-technical decision-maker with a real dependency, a real pending change, and a real accountable owner.** Four external parties in one day, which is the largest staffing demand in the programme and is discussed in §3.1.
- **A three-year psychological-safety record** with eleven prior administrations, and nine prior demonstrations of the error-clinic firewall.
- **Two prior unassisted records**, at [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) and [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md), held for the student and returned to them at phase 8 as the first two points of a three-point series.
- **Eighty-nine sealed Copilot predictions and their outcomes**, one per prior seminar day, which phase 6 part three closes as a cohort series.
- **A repository the student can provision, a stack they deployed, identities they configured and a harness they wrote** — the objects the handover consists of, all produced across the module's four preceding days.

**If the receiving party cannot be present**, the day cannot run EA-2 and the design says so rather than substituting a proxy. A handover performed to a peer is not the activity; it is [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)'s reconstruction test with higher stakes and no receiver. The fallback is to defer EA-2 to PE390's assessment window with the receiving party available, and to record the seminar occasion as carrying EA-1 and EA-3 only. **This is the single largest operational dependency in the programme and it is named here rather than assumed.**

**If a student's component has already been handed over** — possible where a student left the rota early — they hand over the substrate component they most recently worked on, with the assessor narrative recording that the artefacts were written for a component the student did not own alone.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Nothing is built and the load is entirely dispositional and temporal.** This follows [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §3.1 and [WP-070](wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §3.1 and this design accepts it a third time: a judgement about conduct under pressure is invalid if the student is simultaneously constructing the evidence.

**Two exceptions exist and neither is inside a judgement.** Phase 5 part one is a twenty-five-minute decision artefact, which is a selection and assembly task over material already in front of the student rather than a construction. **Phase 1b is a genuine construction and is the reason the exception has to be stated carefully**: it is a build, it is thirty minutes, and it is deliberately placed outside every assessed phase, seen by no assessor, and excluded from every level. The rule the design is honouring is that a student must not be constructing the evidence they are being judged on; phase 1b constructs nothing that is judged.

**The day's real load is that three high-stakes performances occur in one afternoon with no recovery interval.** A student who performs EA-2 badly carries that into EA-3 forty minutes later. The design mitigates in three ways and none of them is sufficient: the levels are formed from artefacts written weeks earlier, so a bad performance damages a defence rather than a level; the three activities have different shapes, so a student weak at transfer is not necessarily weak at communication; and phase 4's decision-maker has read nothing in advance and has no knowledge of what happened at phase 3. **The residual carry-over risk is real and is not managed away**; §8 objection 1 examines it as part of a larger problem. **Phase 1b adds a thirty-minute construction task in front of all of it**, which is a fourth carry-over path the design has created deliberately and cannot measure; §8 objection 4 states why the alternative placements are worse and what the choice cannot rule out.

**Staffing is the binding constraint on the whole design and it peaks here.** Per group of four students, the day requires: two assessors for the full day; a receiving party for seventy minutes; a non-technical decision-maker for thirty-five; and the accountable staff member for forty-five. **Phase 1b adds no external party and adds thirty minutes of room time**, which is the cheapest possible way to buy the provision and is still not free. The [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s open question — how many independent assessor-occasion pairs staffing can support — is not merely live here; **this day is the case that will answer it, and the answer may be that the day is not runnable as designed.**

Management:

- **Nothing is built except a six-item selection at phase 5.**
- **All three artefacts exist and are in the assessors' hands before the day begins.**
- **Phase 3's receipt is scripted with five fixed questions**, so the receiving party needs no training beyond the protocol.
- **Phase 4's perturbation set is published and is four items.**
- **Consolidation does not occur.** There is no phase 4 in the ordinary sense, because there is nothing to consolidate: this day teaches nothing.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Building something real, alone, with the documentation and no agent, on the last day,* phase 1b.
- *Silence during the receipt,* phase 3 part two, which is a failure condition rather than an instruction.
- *Watching somebody else operate your system while you may not speak,* phase 3 part two's sixth element.
- *An eight-minute window with a decision-maker,* phase 4, short deliberately.
- *Being asked to defend one of your own omissions out loud, to the person it concerns,* phase 4.
- *Being asked to account for an instruction in your own prompt record, three weeks after you wrote it,* phases 3, 4 and 5.
- *A decision whose consequences arrive after your departure,* phase 5.
- *Being required to say what your decision does to a warranty you signed ninety minutes earlier,* item 5.
- *Ranking your own three weaknesses before performing any of them,* item 4.
- *Four prediction checks and a ninety-day frontier map closed at once, including where they are unflattering.*

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *A third assessor.* Rule 1 wants more observers and the day has two. §8 objection 1.
- *Separating the three activities across days.* Available, declined, and §8 objection 1 argues it was the wrong call.
- *A hostile party anywhere.* Consistent with every assessed occasion in the programme.
- *Carrying forward any prior level.* Refused on principle at every occasion and refused here, where the cost of refusing is highest.
- *Assessing phase 1b.* Excluded absolutely, and the exclusion is the provision. A single occasion observed once is what rater discipline rule 1 forbids as a basis for entrustment, and the unassisted provision does not get an exemption from a rule the architecture applies everywhere else.
- *Withholding documentation at phase 1b.* Available and rejected. The provision tests construction, not recall, and an engineer who may not look things up is being observed doing something nobody does.
- *A summative reflection.* Excluded. Phase 6 part four returns the [S005](wiki/seminars/S005.md) paragraphs and collects nothing, and phase 8's close states what the programme certifies in the narrowest terms it can defend.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The three levels awarded today are **safe as a statement of what two named assessors, on one occasion, would have let this student do unsupervised on this substrate, with their reasoning recorded.** They are **unsafe as a statement of what the student can do**, because one occasion with two assessors is below the evidentiary standard the programme itself publishes; **unsafe as a prediction of conduct in a different organisation**, because every party in the room acted in good faith and the substrate's domain constraint caps the harm; and **unsafe as a ranking**, because the scale is ordinal, the occasions are not equated, and nothing in the design supports comparing two students' levels as a measure of relative capability. **The exit determination is made across occasions by ME390, PE390, CAP390 and SG390, and today is one input to each.** A reader who takes a level from this day as a certification has taken the strongest available reading of the weakest available evidence, and the day's own entrustment statement says so.

**A second strong-form scope statement, on phase 1b.** The unassisted result is **safe as one point in one student's own three-point series, read by that student, as information about their own trajectory.** It is **unsafe as a measure of capability**, because thirty minutes on one staff-chosen constraint is a single observation on a single task and the programme forbids entrustment on exactly that basis; **unsafe as a cohort comparison**, which is why no distribution is shown; and **unsafe as evidence about the dependency risk in either direction** — a cohort that all finish tells the programme that this task was easy, and a cohort that all fail tells it that this task was hard, and neither tells it whether three years of agent-mediated formation produced engineers who can build. **Three points across three years can show a trajectory and cannot characterise one, and whether three occasions is enough is unknown and recorded as unknown** in the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md), in the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) and here. **The specification's exclusion of the result from every level is where a reader applying it outside that range would fail**, and §9 prediction 5 is written so that the limitation travels with the figure.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **the silence at phase 3 part two.**

A student presents an artefact for six minutes and then stops. The people who will operate their component after they leave say, out loud, in front of two assessors, what they can and cannot do with it — including which ownership entry they cannot use and what the artefact does not tell them — **and then they run one operation from it while the student watches.** The student may not help. **Everything the module has argued for four weeks is settled in those six minutes, by somebody else, and the student's only remaining act is to have written well enough three weeks ago.**

That construction supplies the day's motivation and its principal validity claim at once, and it is [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) point 2 arriving as an assessment mechanism rather than as a lesson.

**Phase 1b supplies a second and smaller motivational structure and it is the only one in the programme that is about the student's relationship to their own instrument.** A cohort that has prompted an agent on ninety days is asked, once, on the last one, to build without it, and is told before they start that the result is theirs and nobody's else. Some will find it easy and be surprised; some will find it hard and be more surprised. **Neither result is scored and both are informative to the person who gets them**, which is the only defensible thing to do with a measurement this coarse.

Autonomy is preserved where it can be: the decision at phase 5 is the student's, both directions are passes, and two non-failures are named in advance so that a student who cannot make a claim knows before phase 1 that saying so is an answer.

Competence is supported by the artefacts being weeks old and by nothing being built.

Relatedness is supplied by four external parties, all of whom are affected by what the student says.

**The affective risk is the largest in the programme and it has two forms.**

The first is **collapse after a bad first activity.** A student whose receiving party answers none of the five receipt questions has watched three years of work fail in public, and has EA-3 in forty minutes. Countermeasures: the levels are formed from artefacts, so the performance damages a defence rather than a level, and this is stated in the entrustment statement; the decision-maker at phase 4 knows nothing of phase 3; the assessors are instructed to record the receipt outcome and to give no reaction; and the module lead is present and is briefed that a student in visible distress is offered the token-funded deferral in the moment rather than after the day.

The second is **the last-day reading of a mediocre level.** A student awarded 2b on the final occasion of a three-year degree has no eleventh occasion. Countermeasures: 3a is named in the entrustment statement as a defensible exit reading rather than a shortfall; the exit determination is explicitly made across occasions by four courses, so today is one input; and the narrative is returned in writing on the day, per [S030](wiki/seminars/S030.md)'s entitlement, so nobody leaves with a level and no account of it. **None of this makes the reading painless and the design does not claim it does.**

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**The minute budget is 350 and is stated on the day.** That is **thirty minutes above** the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md)'s standard total, and a departure from the assessment-day shape [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md) and [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) use, which runs to 305 across one activity and one defence. **Three activities and an unassisted build do not fit that shape and the departures are named here.**

**Departure 1 — the activities carry their own defences rather than sharing one defence phase.** [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md) and [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) place a single sixty-minute defence after a single sixty-minute activity. Three activities sharing one defence would require the assessor to hold three provisional levels through one conversation, which is precisely the impression-formation risk rule 4 names. The defences are therefore embedded: phase 3 part three, phase 4's perturbation set, phase 5 part two. **This is the departure that makes the day possible and it costs the design a dedicated defence phase, which is a real loss** — a defence with its own hour can pursue a line, and an eleven-minute embedded one cannot.

**Departure 2 — phases 1, 2, 6 and 7 are compressed.** Phase 1 falls from 25 to 20 because there is no framework to pretest and every item concerns the student's own artefacts. Phase 2 falls from 30 to 25 while carrying ten items rather than seven, on the ground that eight of the ten have been retrieved repeatedly and are recalled rather than reconstructed. Phase 6 falls from 35 to 30 because it closes four checks and a series that require reporting rather than working. Phase 7 falls from 40 to 35, which is the compression the design is least comfortable with and which §8 does not defend.

**Departure 3 — phase 8 rises from 20 to 35.** It carries six sealed items, three assessor narratives, five instrument closures, an unassisted three-point series returned privately, the twelfth administration of the [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) series with its three-year trajectory, and the programme's closing statement. Twenty minutes would produce a list read at speed.

**Departure 4 — the day is thirty minutes longer, rather than four assessed phases being five minutes shorter.** Phase 1b has to exist somewhere: the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) names three unassisted occasions and this is the third, and [WP-045](wiki/whitepapers/WP-045.md) §8 committed this day specifically to a **build** rather than to another judgement exercise. It cannot sit inside an assessed phase, because a construction task inside a judgement occasion is exactly what §3.1 forbids. **The alternative was to take five minutes each from EA-2, EA-1, the closing checks and the error clinic in order to make room for something explicitly not assessed, which trades judgement quality for a detector the design already describes as coarse.** The design would rather lengthen the last day of a three-year degree than shorten the last judgement of one. The cost is that this is the longest day in the programme, on the day the cohort is least able to absorb one, and §8 objection 4 states what that cannot rule out.

**Phase 1 (20 min).** Six sealed items. Item 2 asks for an attribution as well as a prediction. Item 4 is the programme's only three-way self-ranking. **Item 6 is the ninetieth and last sealed Copilot prediction and the only one that is about the student rather than the agent.**

**Phase 1b (30 min).** No agent, no MCP servers, documentation available. A failing check whose assertion message states a constraint, to be made to pass without weakening it, with the reason left in the code. Not assessed, seen by no assessor, returned to the student at phase 8 as the third point of a three-point series.

**Phase 2 (25 min).** Ten items across nine quarters, the oldest at three years and scaffolded. The bridge produces the day's only framing statement.

**Phase 3 (70 min).** EA-2. Six minutes transfer, six minutes receipt in silence including a receiver-run operation, three minutes response, ten minutes held for the receiving party's record. Level formed from the [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) artefact first, per rule 7. **The [S084](wiki/seminars/S084.md) six-week adoption measurement, the four build records and the [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) reconciliation table are supplied in writing with the artefact and are not presented.**

**Phase 4 (35 min).** EA-3. Eight minutes each against a real decision-maker who has read nothing. Five published perturbations, one of them the student's own omitted item and one an instruction from their own prompt record. The decision-maker's separate record is taken out of the student's hearing.

**Phase 5 (70 min).** EA-1. Twenty-five minutes individual, forty-five minutes perturbation in groups of four. Six published questions, one of them the [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md) absent-party perturbation answered from the student's own record and one drawn from the [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) reconciliation. Item 5 is the integrating demand and the only item requiring two activities to be held at once.

**Phase 6 (30 min).** Levels sealed first. Four checks closed, the ninety-day frontier map closed as a two-rate series, what the design learned stated in four minutes, the [S005](wiki/seminars/S005.md) paragraphs returned and not collected.

**Phase 7 (35 min).** Assessors absent. The accountable staff member's own failed handover, their part stated first, then the programme's own agent case with no student named. The most exposing student prompt in the programme.

**Phase 8 (35 min).** Sealed items including the unassisted prediction, three narratives with divergences and defence movement, five instrument closures, the private three-point unassisted series, the twelfth [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) administration with the full series, and the close.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has handed over professionally will present fluently at phase 3 part one. Predicted failure: **they will break the silence at part two.** Professional handover practice is conversational — the receiver asks, the sender answers — and the reflex to help is trained, well-motivated and immediate. **The receiver-run operation makes it worse**, because watching somebody type a command you know is about to fail is harder to sit through than watching them misdescribe your component. Countermeasures: it is a published failure condition rather than an instruction; the receiving party is briefed to continue speaking and operating if interrupted; and the assessors record the interruption's content, because a student who interrupts to correct a factual error is doing something different from one who interrupts to rescue an impression, and the narrative must distinguish them.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is at phase 4's eight minutes: a student who cannot compress. Countermeasures: [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md)'s statement is one page by specification and is in the decision-maker's hands; [S083](wiki/seminars/S083.md) is within the quarter; and the perturbation set is published, so the student knows in advance that five questions will consume most of the window and can plan for two minutes of opening rather than eight.

**A failure mode belonging to phase 1b: the unassisted build sets the day's tone.** A student who cannot finish it in thirty minutes begins EA-2 having just failed at something, on the last day, having been told in advance that the failure does not count. Being told does not make it not feel like one. Countermeasures: the task is announced in shape a fortnight ahead so nobody is ambushed; **the specification states that not finishing is not a failure and states it before phase 1**; no distribution is shown at any point, so nobody learns that they were slower than the room; the module lead is briefed to say once, before the phase, that the design expects a wide spread and is not measuring the room against a standard; and **phase 2's retrieval set sits between phase 1b and EA-2 as a deliberate twenty-five-minute buffer of an activity every student is good at.** The design does not claim this is sufficient and §8 objection 4 states so.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Where the receiving party answers all five receipt questions well, the activity has succeeded and the assessors have less to discriminate on. The difficulty is relocated to phase 3 part three's assessor question, which is drawn from the receipt and is not pre-set, and to phase 5 item 5, where a successful handover creates a stronger constraint on the ship decision — a student whose warranty is comprehensive has more to invalidate. **Fallback if a student handles both:** they are asked what they would have had to write differently if the receiving party had been an external organisation with no code access, which is the case [WP-086](wiki/whitepapers/WP-086.md) §3.2 names as outside the module's scope and for which the day's repertoire is explicitly inadequate.

**Affective failure.** Two, both described in §3.3 with their countermeasures: collapse after a bad first activity, and the last-day reading of a mediocre level. **Neither is solved and the design says so.** The first is mitigated structurally and remains real; the second is mitigated by framing and by the exit determination sitting elsewhere, and remains real. A programme that puts its highest-stakes judgement on the final day accepts both, and the alternative — moving the occasion earlier and leaving the last week for something else — was considered and rejected because the handover cannot occur before the students are leaving.

## 6. Retention architecture

**None. The programme ends.**

This section exists in every whitepaper and is honest here only if it says that. Nothing retrieves this day. No spacing interval runs. The written reflection due the following morning is the last artefact, and it is read once and changes no level.

Three things persist and are not retention:

- **The receiving party's records from phases 3 and 4** enter the substrate's operating documentation and become the input to the next cohort's [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) phase 3 corpus, together with [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md)'s withdrawn guarantees **and the repositories, stacks and identities this cohort has just handed over, which the next cohort will own and which the cohort after that will study.** **That is the only mechanism in the programme by which one cohort's output becomes another's material**, and it is the closest thing the design has to institutional memory.
- **The three levels and their narratives** go to ME390, PE390, CAP390 and SG390.
- **The [S005](wiki/seminars/S005.md) paragraphs** go home with the students.
- **The ninety-day frontier map** goes to the next cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), per instrumentation rule 9, and **the aggregate of the three unassisted occasions** goes to the annual design review, which publishes what it did about it.

The design notes, without repairing it, that **the last five days of the programme have almost no retention architecture between them.** [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md) is retrieved twice at short gaps; [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) once, at seven days; this day never. The module's genuinely new content — the three conditions on selection, slot-and-occupant, the two-way obligation — is the content with the least spaced practice in the entire programme, in a design whose central empirical commitment is that retention is a scheduling variable. **That is a structural consequence of placing the material last, and the material has to be last.**

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**The programme's tenth and final entrustment occasion, carrying EA-1, EA-2 and EA-3.** Exit target **3b** for all three; 3c available and rare; 4a and 4b not awarded.

Each level is formed independently by two assessors **from the artefact, before the corresponding defence**, per rater discipline rule 7. Divergence beyond one sub-level is documented and resolved by re-observation where the day permits and by recorded disagreement where it does not, per rule 3. **Divergences are never averaged.** **Narrative substantiation is mandatory for each of the three levels separately** and must cite specific moments; a narrative naming no specific observed action is rejected by moderation.

**Rule 7's [WP-060](wiki/whitepapers/WP-060.md) amendment binds EA-1 here and is satisfied in the artefact.** EA-1's phase 5 artefact records a **prospective** decision, so the amendment requires at least one rejected alternative with its reason — the operating point not taken, the promise not made, the condition not imposed. **That is item 6's second half and it is a checked element.** The amendment's justification applies exactly: for a prospective decision the artefact contains only what the student intends and the reasoning lives in the conversation, and a rejected alternative is the only element that restores the artefact-supplies-evidence premise rule 7 rests on. **EA-2 and EA-3 are governed by rule 7 in its original form**, because a handover artefact and a residual-risk statement are completed actions rather than intentions, and the amendment is not extended to them.

**Six failure conditions are published before phase 1**, and two explicit non-failures are published beside them: saying that a claim cannot be made, with what would let it be made; and telling a decision-maker that nothing you have can tell them whether the system is safe for them. Per rule 1, the checked elements are enumerated in the day's Assessment section and **nothing else is assessed** — not whether the change shipped, not whether the receiving party was satisfied, **not whether the receiver's nominated operation succeeded**, **not the four build records, which were assessed once at their own days and are carried here as material for a question**, **not anything about phase 1b**, and not the quality of the component.

**Phase 6's results are not assessed**, and the specification says so in writing, on the argument [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §7 established and this design has accepted without amendment at every subsequent occasion. **Phase 1b's result is not assessed and is not seen by any assessor**, and a student who does not finish it has not failed anything; both are stated in the specification and both are stated before phase 1.

### Rule-6 discharge

**Eight obligations land on this day and all eight elements exist.**

- **[S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)** named phase 3 for its count of items classified as transferable by no medium. **That is the fifth receipt question**, asked of the real receiving party, with the cohort total reported at phase 8.
- **[S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md)** named phase 3 for its phase 7 first-answer hit rate, requiring the same question of a real receiving reader. **That is the fourth receipt question**, and the peer and real-reader rates are compared at phase 8. This is the comparison [WP-086](wiki/whitepapers/WP-086.md) §8 objection 2 identified as otherwise unavailable to the design.
- **[S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md)** named phase 4 for its step 5 audit movement, requiring one omitted item to be read back and defended. **That is the third perturbation item**, and it is the only question in the programme requiring a student to defend an omission aloud to the person it concerns.

The two-week leg was [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)'s and [WP-086](wiki/whitepapers/WP-086.md) §7 reports it. The obligation is discharged.

  **The fix [WP-084](wiki/whitepapers/WP-084.md) §8 asks of this paper is taken**, as it is at [WP-086](wiki/whitepapers/WP-086.md) §7. That objection conceded that [S084](wiki/seminars/S084.md) teaches adoption as a curve and measures it at two points chosen against a known shape, observed that the substrate's telemetry already produces a weekly figure, proposed returning the curve with the predicted points marked at a cost of "a query and no phase time," and asked the elements' owning days to take it. **The defence element is the weekly series with both predicted points marked, not the two figures.** Taking it costs this day nothing — the material is written and unspoken — and it changes what an assessor can ask: two points cannot distinguish a rate that rose and held from one that rose and is collapsing, and only the second makes a warranty on that component's use questionable.

The obligation is discharged.

### The unassisted-build commitment, discharged

**[WP-045](wiki/whitepapers/WP-045.md) §8 objection 3 recorded that [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md)'s unassisted hour tested the student's judgement and left their construction untested, and named this day as the occasion that would take the other half. The element exists at phase 1b and this section is the report that objection requires.**

What it is: **thirty minutes, no agent, all three MCP servers disconnected, documentation available and unrestricted, on a real failing check from the cohort's own [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) work whose assertion message states a constraint, to be made to pass without weakening the constraint and with the reason left in the code.** What is recorded is what the student produced, what they looked up and where they stopped.

What it produces: **a comparison, not a level.** The artefact is set beside the same student's [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) and [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md) unassisted records and returned to them privately at phase 8 as a three-point series. In aggregate and unattributed it goes to the programme's annual design review, which publishes what it did about it, per instrumentation rule 9's naming of a reader. **No assessor of EA-1, EA-2 or EA-3 sees the artefact, is told the outcome, or is told who finished**, and no entrustment level derives from it. That is not a courtesy: a single occasion observed once is what rater discipline rule 1 forbids as a basis for entrustment, and the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) declines an exemption from a rule it applies everywhere else.

Why it is a build. [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) and [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md) both produced reasoning under an unassisted condition. **Reasoning is not the capability the dependency risk is about.** The [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s statement of the risk is that a student who has only ever *built* with an agent may be unable to *build* without one, and a provision whose three occasions all test judgement has instrumented the wrong construct — which is [S026](wiki/seminars/S026.md)'s finding applied to the programme's own safeguard. **[WP-045](wiki/whitepapers/WP-045.md) saw that and this day acts on it.** The obligation is discharged.

What the discharge does not do is establish that the provision works. **Three points across three years can show a trajectory and cannot characterise one, whether three occasions is enough is unknown and is recorded as unknown in three places, and the task is one staff-chosen constraint on one substrate.** §3.2's second scope statement bounds what the result may be read as, §8 objection 4 states what its placement cannot rule out, and §9 prediction 5 is the instrument.

### The four long-horizon prediction checks and the frontier map, closed

Phase 6 closes [S020](wiki/seminars/S020.md)'s capacity arguments, [S040](wiki/seminars/S040.md)'s stopping criteria, [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s category three and [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s detection commitments — the fifth long-horizon check in the programme and the only one closing four at once. **Part two requires the module lead to state which checks produced a result the design did not predict, what is being changed, and where a check produced nothing usable.** That requirement is instrumentation rule 4 applied to the programme rather than to a day: a design that has measured its cohort for three years and reports none of it back has treated the students as instrumentation rather than as its subject.

**Part three closes the frontier map**, which is the programme's only instrument administered on every single day: ninety sealed Copilot predictions, checked at ninety phase 8s. It is reported as a cohort series in **two rates** — how often students predicted the agent would be wrong, and how often they named how — because the first measures a disposition and the second measures a model. Per instrumentation rule 3 the day states the sample: ninety tasks chosen by staff, on one substrate, with a known failure frequently available by construction, so **the map describes this programme's tasks rather than the agent.** Per rule 9 its reader is next year's cohort, through the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), and the series is what they receive.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day is a direct violation of the programme's own first rater-discipline rule, on the occasion where the rule matters most.**

At its strongest: rule 1 states that no entrustment level derives from a single observation by a single assessor, on the ground that rater variance is too large for a single observation to be defensible. This day derives **three** levels from **one** occasion with **two** assessors, and it does so as the final judgement of a three-year degree. The letter of the rule is met — two is not one — and the spirit is not: the rule's purpose is to sample across occasions and observers, and one afternoon samples across neither. **Worse, the three levels are not independent observations of three things; they are three observations of one student by two people in one room over four hours**, and the design's own rule 4 says that impression formation is where the variance lives. A student who is nervous at ten past nine is nervous at three o'clock.

The rebuttal is real and partial. The three levels are formed from **three artefacts written in three different weeks**, before any of the day's performances, and the defences are used only to confirm, revise or overturn. That is rule 7, it is the strongest available countermeasure to the carry-over problem, and it means a student who performs badly all afternoon can still hold three artefact-formed levels. And the day is one occasion among several: EA-1 is assessed at ME390 across at least four occasions with at least three assessors, EA-2 at PE390 and CAP390, EA-3 at PE301, CAP390 and SG390. **The exit determination is not made here and the entrustment statement says so before phase 1.**

**The concession is that the design had an alternative and did not take it.** Splitting the activities — EA-2 and EA-3 here, EA-1 at [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md), where a scope negotiation under constraint is already adjacent to a proceed decision — would have produced two occasions with two assessors each, which is closer to rule 1 and costs no additional staffing. It was not taken because the design wanted the three activities judged against **one** handover of **one** component on the day the student leaves, and because [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md)'s occasion has its own subject. **That is a coherence argument beating a reliability argument, made by a programme whose central claim is that it takes reliability seriously**, and it should be recorded in those terms rather than defended.

**Objection 2: what this programme most likely gets wrong.**

This is the closing whitepaper and it owes the sharpest version of this it can produce. Three candidates, considered in order.

**Candidate one: the substrate cannot carry genuine consequence.** The [Programme Charter](wiki/program/00-Program-Charter.md) names this as its first open question and the design would collapse without it. It is the most catastrophic candidate and it is **not** the most likely, because the design has already committed to withdrawing the operations content rather than faking it, and because [WP-070](wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §8 objection 2 has already conceded the harder version — that the consequence is bounded and the students are not told the bounds. The failure mode is known, named and partly priced.

**Candidate two: the exit target of 3b is not reachable.** Plausible. Three years is short, [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) expects 2b–3a with 3b rare, and a design that expects 3b at exit is claiming a full sub-level of movement in two quarters. If it is not reached, the programme's response is straightforward and cheap: report the distribution, restate the target, and say so. **A missed target is a finding, not a structural defect.**

**Candidate three, and the one this paper commits to: the programme's self-instrumentation is unexecutable, and the design has never noticed because the defect is invisible from inside a whitepaper.**

Ninety whitepapers each carry at least four falsifiable predictions with named instruments. That is several hundred predictions, plus the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s six-row programme table, plus every rule-6 instrument, plus five long-horizon prediction checks, plus twelve administrations of one psychological-safety instrument. **No page in this programme names who analyses any of it, when, with what capacity, or what happens when they cannot.** The Charter commits that "where a cohort's data contradicts a whitepaper's §9 prediction, the seminar day is redesigned and the whitepaper amended." That commitment has a subject that has never been identified.

The prediction is therefore this: **the great majority of the programme's §9 predictions will never be tested, the design will conclude from a handful of measured ones that its instrumentation is working, and the untested remainder will function as decoration** — as rhetoric that made the design feel accountable while costing it nothing.

**The evidence that this is the right candidate is the design's own record.** Instrumentation rule 4 was added after [WP-043](wiki/whitepapers/WP-043.md) §8 named the habit: *this design measures more readily than it responds.* Rule 6 widened it after [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §8 found the deferred case. Both rules bind the design **at design time**: they require an instrument to name what changes and when. **Neither says anything about whether the change can be afforded at run time**, and that is precisely the version of the problem that requires resources rather than discipline. **The design fixed the half of its own diagnosed defect that could be fixed by writing more carefully, and left the half that needs money and people entirely untouched — across two rule amendments, three years of design, and ninety papers whose central discipline is stating what you cannot deliver.**

This is conceded without qualification, and the fix is not cheap, which is why it has been avoided.

**What this paper argues for, as a programme-level closure statement.** The [Seminar Day Design Pattern](wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) should carry a further instrumentation rule, and the [Programme Charter](wiki/program/00-Program-Charter.md) should carry a matching commitment, in the following form: **an instrument must name the party who will read its result, and the programme must publish, before each offering, the subset of instruments it will actually analyse that year — a design that instruments more than it can read has produced a literature, not a measurement.** The published subset makes the constraint visible, forces a priority ordering that currently does not exist, and converts every unlisted instrument from an implied promise into an acknowledged deferral. **It also guarantees an uncomfortable number**, which is the point: if the subset is a small fraction of the corpus, the programme learns something about itself that ninety whitepapers of careful argument have concealed.

**Objection 3: the token economy does not apply evenly to the three activities, and the design discovered this late.**

At its strongest: the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s token economy allows a deferred attempt at one token, re-run within the assessment window. That works for EA-1, which needs a different change, and for EA-3, which needs a different decision-maker. **It does not work for EA-2**, because the handover has occurred: the receiving party holds the component and cannot receive it again. A student who defers EA-2 is assessed on the artefact and the reflection, with the absence recorded — which is a different and weaker basis than every other student's, on the activity the module was built around.

This is conceded and is not fixable within the day. The mitigations are that the artefact is the primary evidence for EA-2 anyway under rule 7, so a deferred student is missing the confirmation rather than the basis; and that PE390 and CAP390 assess EA-2 across further occasions where a handover to a different party is possible. **The residue is that the programme's most important single activity is the one it cannot offer a student a second attempt at, on the day it is judged, and that asymmetry was not designed — it was discovered while writing the day's token clause.** It is recorded rather than smoothed because a design that only notices its structural asymmetries when drafting the last page of the last module should say so.

**Objection 4: the unassisted build is placed where it can contaminate everything that follows it, and the design chose that placement knowing it.**

At its strongest: phase 1b is thirty minutes of solitary construction, without the instrument the cohort has used for three years, immediately before the three highest-stakes performances of the degree. **A student who fails at it is thirty minutes from EA-2.** The design's own §5 lists that as a failure mode and offers a twenty-five-minute retrieval set as a buffer, which is a buffer against nothing: a student does not stop thinking about the check they could not fix because they are answering questions about [S004](wiki/seminars/S004.md). **And the contamination runs in both directions and is unmeasurable in either.** A student who finishes easily arrives at EA-2 confident, and the design will read that confidence as evidence about their handover. The programme has spent three years teaching students to notice exactly this kind of confound in somebody else's measurement design, and has then built one into its own final judgement, in the one place where the judgement has no eleventh occasion to correct it.

The rebuttal is that every alternative is worse and the design should say which. **Placing it after the three activities** puts a construction task in front of a student who has just been judged, which is fatigue plus affect and produces a measurement of neither construction nor composure. **Placing it on a different day** breaks the comparability with [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) and [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md), both of which were administered before their day's assessed work, and a three-point series whose third point was taken under different conditions is not a series. **Omitting it** leaves [WP-045](wiki/whitepapers/WP-045.md) §8's commitment undischarged and the dependency risk instrumented only on judgement, which is the wrong construct.

**The concession is that "least bad" is the whole of the argument, and that the design cannot detect the confound it has created.** No instrument in the day separates a strong EA-2 from a student who was buoyed by phase 1b from a strong EA-2 from a student who would have performed identically without it. **The cheap fix exists and is named: alternate the phase 1b placement across assessment groups — half before phase 2, half after phase 6 — and compare the three level distributions between the two halves.** It costs nothing, it is a within-cohort comparison the day's own scheduling already supports because students run in groups of four, and it would settle the question in one offering. **It is not made, and the reason is that the second half's phase 1b would then be administered after fatigue and after judgement, which makes their unassisted result non-comparable with their own S010 and S045 records — so the design would learn about the confound at the cost of the measurement the phase exists for.** That trade may be worth making and the design has not made it. It is recorded here so that the next offering can choose differently with the reasoning in front of it.

**Objection 5: did the practical turn work, and what would show that it did not?**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn, and being the closing whitepaper it is owed a direct answer rather than a defence. **The honest answer is that nobody knows, that the programme has been rebuilt around a claim it cannot yet test, and that the design can at least state what would falsify it.**

What the turn changed. Every seminar day now names a platform anchor and MCP servers, seals a Copilot prediction at phase 1, runs its contrasting cases against a real service where the subject permits, names a perishable platform point and a default nobody chose, produces a build carrying a prompt record, an intervention record and a frontier statement, and surfaces a case where the agent was confidently wrong and review did not catch it. In this module that has produced four merged artefacts per student, a receiving party who operates the system in front of assessors, a warranty reconciled against the mechanism that enforces it, and an ethical finding located in a threshold value sitting in the students' own repository.

**Three things could be true and only one of them is the claim.**

*The turn worked.* Students who have provisioned, deployed, denied, traced and handed over hold the four-layer model as something they have operated rather than something they can describe, and the durable claims — transfer is measured on the receiver, evidence inherits its error structure, a warranty is conditional, ownership has a slot and an occupant — are more available to them for having had a concrete instance with a failure mode attached.

*The turn substituted product familiarity for understanding.* This is the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](wiki/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md)'s own largest recorded open risk and it is the one this paper takes most seriously. A graduate who can name the five-principal cap and cannot say why an enforcement mechanism's scope is narrower than the prose describing it has learned a fact with a shelf life instead of a claim without one. **[WP-089](wiki/whitepapers/WP-089.md) §8 objection 3 records the exact moment the design felt this pull and did not resist it.**

*The turn made no difference to what students know and a large difference to what the programme can observe.* Also plausible, also not nothing, and much weaker than what the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) claims.

**What would show it did not work.** Four observations, in increasing order of how badly the design would need to respond.

1. **Perishable recall crowding out durable claims in the closing retrieval.** Phase 2's ten items are all durable by construction. If cohort performance on them is *worse* than the equivalent items were before the turn, while students volunteer platform specifics unprompted, the turn has traded curriculum for currency. *Instrument:* phase 2 item-level performance, compared against the pre-turn item bank, with unprompted platform mentions coded.
2. **Frontier predictions that do not improve across three years.** The map's specific-naming rate is the programme's measure of whether students are building a model of their instrument or merely learning to distrust it. **A flat specific rate across ninety days would mean the sealed prediction has been an attitude survey**, and the whole phase 1 addition should be withdrawn rather than tightened.
3. **Intervention records that converge on emptiness.** The [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) already names a cohort trend toward empty records as a finding about the complexity class rather than about diligence. **If it happens in this module — at class 6, on artefacts students will be judged on — the finding is about the turn**: students are accepting agent output on the work that matters most, and the build record has become a form.
4. **A widening assisted-to-unassisted gap across the three occasions.** This is the direct one and it is §9 prediction 5's.

**The design's own position, stated plainly.** The practical turn is defensible on one argument that does not depend on any of the above: **a programme cannot honestly assess a claim about what students can do on a platform if its students have never operated one.** Before the turn, this design assessed handover, safety cases, residual risk and warranties as documents, and its receiving parties read documents. The turn's minimum achievement is that the module's central claim — *a handover succeeded if the receiving team can operate, diagnose and change the system* — is now settled by the receiving team rather than argued by the author. **That is a validity improvement and it is independent of whether students learned more.** Everything beyond it is a hypothesis, and the four observations above are how it fails.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1 — about the programme as a whole, and the one a cohort's outcomes would falsify most directly.** **Fewer than half the cohort will reach 3b on all three activities, and EA-3 will be the lowest of the three for a majority of students.** *Instrument:* the three level distributions from this day, set against the exit determinations made by ME390, PE390, CAP390 and SG390. **If EA-3 is not the weakest, the programme's central diagnosis is wrong** — the [Programme Charter](wiki/program/00-Program-Charter.md) exists because graduates "can measure, and cannot say what the measurement licenses," and EA-3 is the direct test of that claim. A cohort strong at EA-3 and weak at EA-1 would mean the programme identified the wrong binding constraint and rebuilt a curriculum around it.

**Prediction 1b — the same prediction from the other side, and the sharper falsifier.** **A cohort that reaches 3b on all three activities at a high rate falsifies the programme's exit target rather than vindicating its teaching.** The target was set at 3b because three years is short and [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) expects 2b–3a with 3b rare; a cohort clearing it comfortably means either the assessors converged on the target because it was published, which rater discipline rule 4 predicts and rule 2 is designed to detect, or the target was set below what the design achieves. *Instrument:* the three distributions against assessor divergence rates and against the independent-before-deliberation records. **The design commits in advance to reading a high pass rate as a question about the occasion before reading it as a result about the students.**

**Prediction 2 — the instrumentation prediction from §8 objection 2.** **At the end of the first cohort, fewer than one in ten of the §9 predictions written across the ninety whitepapers will have been measured against cohort data, and the ones that were will be disproportionately those measurable inside a single seminar day.** *Instrument:* an audit of the whitepaper corpus against the collected data, conducted before the second offering, reporting the count measured, the count measurable-but-unmeasured, and the count that turned out to be unmeasurable as written. **If most are measured, §8 objection 2 is wrong and the programme is better instrumented than its own closing paper believes.** The composition is as informative as the count: a skew toward within-day instruments would confirm that the design's long-horizon apparatus is the part that does not survive contact with capacity.

**Prediction 3.** **Fewer than half of students will correctly identify, at item 4, the activity on which they are judged worst.** *Instrument:* item 4 against the three awarded levels, with ties resolved by the assessors' narratives. **This is the programme's terminal calibration measure**, and it is the same construction phase 8 has run every week for three years, asked one last time about the students themselves rather than about their material.

**Prediction 4.** **The phase 7 participation rate will be lower than any prior error clinic in the programme, and the [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) instrument's twelfth administration will not register the drop.** *Instrument:* the participation rate recorded by the module lead against the twelve-point series. **This is the prediction the design most needs to be wrong about.** If the behaviour and the instrument diverge, the programme's psychological-safety claim has been resting for three years on a self-report that a room will complete favourably while declining to speak — and the pre-committed response, stated in the day's rule-6 block, is to treat the behaviour as the finding and replace the instrument.

**Prediction 5 — about the dependency risk specifically, and the one this programme has the least right to be confident about.** **Across the three unassisted occasions, the cohort's median completion of the bounded task will not fall, and the *spread* will widen** — that is, the provision will show no cohort-level decline in unassisted construction and a growing minority for whom the gap between assisted and unassisted work is large. *Instrument:* the three-point series in aggregate, reported as median and spread, with the phase 1b completion, look-up and stopping records coded. **Three outcomes and the design's response to each is pre-committed here, because instrumentation rule 4 requires it and because a prediction about the programme's own safeguard must not be allowed to resolve after the fact.**

- **Median falls across the three points.** The dependency risk is real at cohort level, three occasions detected it, and the fading schedule in the [Module Map](wiki/program/07-Module-Map.md) is the thing that changes — not the students, and not the tool policy.
- **Median holds and spread widens.** The predicted result. It means the risk is real for a subset and invisible in any aggregate, which is the worst case for a detector with three points and no per-student resolution, and the response is to increase the number of occasions rather than their difficulty.
- **Median holds and spread does not widen.** The most comfortable result and the one to distrust hardest, because **it is exactly what a task too easy to discriminate would produce.** The response is to check the task before believing the finding, and the design commits to that ordering in advance so that a null result cannot be read as reassurance.

**No effect size is asserted for any of the three, and the programme has no comparison group, no pre-turn cohort and no instrument capable of separating a change in capability from a change in the task.** §3.2's second scope statement bounds the reading, and this prediction is stated as a direction and a shape rather than as a magnitude for that reason.

**Prediction 6 — about the practical turn, per §8 objection 5.** **The cohort's specific-naming rate on the sealed Copilot prediction will rise across the ninety-day series, and the rise will be concentrated in the second and third years rather than distributed evenly.** *Instrument:* the ninety-day frontier map's two rates, plotted by day, with the generic rate reported separately. **If the specific rate is flat, the turn's phase 1 addition has been measuring an attitude toward the tool rather than a model of it, and the correct response is to withdraw the sealed item rather than to tighten it.** If the specific rate rises and phase 2's durable-item performance falls, §8 objection 5's second candidate is the true one and the turn has traded understanding for familiarity — in which case the platform anchors stay, because the validity argument survives, and the phase 4 platform points shrink to one per day.

The §8 objection-1 concession is that an available two-occasion structure was declined so that three activities could be judged against one handover, which is a coherence argument beating a reliability argument in a design that claims to prioritise reliability. The §8 objection-2 concession is the programme's closing admission: the design's instrumentation is very likely unexecutable, the defect is a run-time capacity problem that two instrumentation-rule amendments addressed only at design time, the design fixed the half it could fix by writing more carefully, and this paper argues for a programme-level rule requiring each instrument to name its reader and the programme to publish, before each offering, the subset it will actually analyse. The §8 objection-5 answer is that nobody knows whether the practical turn worked, that its minimum achievement is a validity improvement independent of whether students learned more, and that four named observations would show it did not — of which the design regards perishable recall displacing durable claims as the most likely and a flat frontier specific-naming rate as the most consequential.**
