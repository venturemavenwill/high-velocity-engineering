## In plain terms

This session teaches a rule that is both the best discipline in the module and the source of its largest blind spot, and it teaches both halves at once because they are the same rule.

**A threat survives only if it can be bound to a named component and a numbered flow.** Everything else is discarded with a logged reason.

The rule is genuinely good. It is what stops a threat model becoming a list of things true of software in general. It makes every entry actionable, because an entry that names a component tells an engineer where to go, and it makes the whole registry joinable by the next stage.

**And it removes, systematically and quietly, everything about the operator, the supply chain, human process and out-of-band administration.** Each of those is discarded with an entirely plausible justification, individually written, collectively catastrophic, and read by nobody.

The session's response is not to weaken the rule. It is to add an artefact the source material does not have: **the discards grouped into categories, so that the shape of what the method cannot see is written down once rather than scattered across a log.** That register is the assessed artefact, and it is assessed because **a method that never states its own scope exclusions invites the reader to assume it has none.**

One more thing is installed here and it is easy to skip. **Your candidate threats came from somewhere, and that somewhere has blind spots which are now yours.** Nothing in the pipeline behind this module records the currency of its knowledge source in any output, and a stale source produces a confident, well-formatted, out-of-date model with no signal that this happened.

## Abstract

This paper defends the binding rule as the constitutive discipline of a system-specific threat model, and defends the addition of a grouped exclusion register that the source material lacks. The argument is that binding is what makes a threat about this system rather than about software, that the cost of binding is the systematic and invisible removal of non-dataflow harm, and that the only available response is to make the removal legible rather than to weaken the rule. The session installs a bound registry with a compact index and a reverse index, a discard log read adversarially, an exclusion register grouped by category, and a dated statement of where the candidate threats came from. The paper concedes that the register depends on somebody choosing to read it, that recall remains unmeasured here exactly as it is in the source, and that the session's prohibition on operational detail produces a thinner artefact than a practitioner would build — a constraint imposed from outside the design, which the paper declines to present as a pedagogical virtue.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A learner asked to enumerate threats will produce entries that are true, serious, and about nothing in particular.

*Data could be intercepted in transit.* *An attacker could gain unauthorised access.* Each is correct. **None tells an engineer where to go, none can be joined to a control, and none can ever be closed**, because there is no place to look for evidence that it was addressed. The registry fills, the coverage looks good, and the artefact has become a hazard: it occupies the space where a real finding would have gone and reads, to anyone scanning, as work.

The binding rule solves that completely and creates a second problem in the same motion. **Real harms that involve no dataflow simply vanish** — the administrator acting outside the system, the dependency compromised upstream, the process step where a human is the control. Each is discarded individually, with a reason that is true, and nobody ever sees the pile.

The session's response is to enforce the rule strictly and to make the pile an artefact.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session cannot start without [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md)'s numbered flows and characteristics checklist, and it uses the checklist rather than a mnemonic as the enumeration key.

It retrieves [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md), which is the same shape of question asked of people rather than of threats — who is not in the room — and [S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md), whose pre-mortem is the only prior instrument the learner has for imagining a harm before it occurs. It assumes [S042](/wiki/seminars/S042.md)'s distinction between scope that grows and a backlog item that does not, because a registry entry is going to become a work item.

**The prior it works against is that coverage is the goal.** A learner arrives believing a good threat model is a long one, and the session's first assessed artefact is a list of things they deleted.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Moderate intrinsic load, high evaluative load in the fourth movement. Applying the rule is mechanical; reading your own discard log as an adversary is not, because every reason in it was written by somebody who believed it.

**3.2 Why binding is constitutive rather than optional.** A threat model is a claim about a particular system. A claim that would survive being pasted into a different system's model unchanged is not a claim about this one. **Binding is the cheapest available test of that property** and it is checkable by any reader, which is why the session states it as an artefact property rather than as a quality.

**3.3 Why the discard log is the informative artefact.** A reviewer handed a list of surviving threats will validate them, because plausible entries read as correct and there is nothing to notice. **The information is in what is absent, and absence is only reviewable if it was written down.** This is the same argument [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md) made for the unproven list, applied to threats.

**3.4 Why grouping is the addition that matters.** A log of individual discards, each with an individually sound reason, is unreadable as evidence about the method. **Grouped into categories, the same log becomes a statement of scope exclusion** — one page saying what this method cannot see. The source has the log and not the grouping, and the consequence is visible: nobody there notices what the rule removed.

**3.5 Why the candidate source must be dated.** The knowledge base behind an enumeration is a single point of failure whose currency is invisible. **A stale source does not fail loudly; it produces a complete-looking artefact.** Recording what the source was and when it was current is the only cheap countermeasure, and it is one the source material does not take anywhere in any output.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 3 asks for a harm involving no dataflow at all, and it is asked before the binding rule is stated.** The learner writes something real, and then adopts a rule that deletes it. **That sequence is the session**, and it is why the item cannot be moved after the instruction.

**Opening item 5 has one honest answer.** A threat model with no open questions and no discards is evidence that the rule was not applied, and a cohort that arrives believing it is evidence of thoroughness is the cohort this session is written for. **Be more suspicious, not less, of a threat model with nothing missing.**

**The enumeration key is the characteristics checklist rather than a mnemonic**, because a mnemonic asks the same questions of every system and the checklist asks the ones this system earned. That is [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md)'s artefact doing the work it was built for.

**The review movement withholds the registry deliberately.** The reviewer is given only the discard log. This is the design's one executable check in the session — a person, not the author, looking at the thing the author has the strongest incentive to stop reading.

**The confidentiality restriction is stated inside the session rather than in a preamble.** A learner who meets an unexplained thinness will assume the material is thin; a learner told that operational detail exists and is deliberately withheld will read the artefact correctly. §8 concedes what that costs.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The general threat.** *Countermeasure:* the copy-paste test in the standard — an entry that would survive being pasted into another system's model is not a finding about this one.

**The forced binding.** A real process or people threat attached to an adjacent component so that it survives. *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — the flow number is arbitrary and you know it — plus the exclusion register, which gives the threat somewhere legitimate to go. **The register is what makes the honest discard cheaper than the dishonest binding**, and that is its main design purpose.

**The plausible discard.** *Countermeasure:* the adversarial re-read, and the review movement's inversion. **This is the design's strongest countermeasure and it is still weak**, because it depends on a reviewer who cares.

**The empty exclusion register.** *Countermeasure:* opening item 3, which guarantees every learner has at least one entry before instruction begins.

**The catalogue ceiling.** *Countermeasure:* the dated-source requirement. **This is a disclosure, not a fix.** Nothing here lets a learner see past their source.

**The operational drift.** *Countermeasure:* the stated restriction and the standard's warning sign — somebody could follow it.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the exclusion register is a second document nobody will read, and adding it does not fix the problem it names.**

At its strongest: the source material already has a discard log, and the paper's own diagnosis is that nobody reads it. Grouping the same content into categories does not create a reader. **It creates a tidier artefact with the same fate**, and it lets the author feel the omission has been handled when nothing has been handled at all — which is worse than the untidy log, because the untidy log at least looks unfinished.

**Substantially conceded.** The register does not fix the omission; it makes the omission legible, which is a smaller claim and the only one available. Two things distinguish it from the log: it is assessed, so at least one person reads it; and it fits on a page, so a reader who wants to know what the method cannot see gets an answer rather than a search task. **The declined fix is to make the register a gate — nothing proceeds until every category has been dispositioned by a named person.** It is declined because a gate whose satisfaction condition is that somebody wrote something in a box is precisely the sign-off placeholder the source already has, and which does nothing there. **This module will not add a control it has just finished explaining is not one.** The residual dependence on a willing reader is real and unresolved.

**Objection 2: a threat model with no operational specificity cannot be acted on, and this session teaches a sanitised version of the job.**

At its strongest: knowing whether a control stops an attack requires knowing how the attack works. The distinctions this module insists on at [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md) — channel against payload, ingress against egress, data plane against management plane — are operational distinctions, and a learner who has never seen the operational form will collapse them exactly as the module says practitioners routinely do. **You are asking for judgement that requires knowledge you have withheld.**

**Conceded, and the paper declines to dress the constraint as a virtue.** The restriction is external: the source material is access-controlled, this repository is public, and reproducing operational detail is not a decision this design is entitled to make. **The declined fix is to teach the operational half in a closed setting alongside the public material.** It is declined because a programme whose artefacts are public cannot depend on a private supplement without becoming untranslatable to anyone reading it. **The gap is real, it is stated in the session rather than hidden, and a learner should assume their threat models will be thinner than a practising security engineer's until they acquire that knowledge elsewhere.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most exclusion registers will list incidents rather than categories at first attempt. *Instrument:* the submitted register, coded *categorical* / *mixed* / *incident list* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Learners will discard fewer candidates than a second reader applying the same binding rule to the same candidate list. *Instrument:* independent application of the rule by a second reader, compared against the learner's discard log.

**Prediction 3.** Reviewers given only the discard log will identify a wrongly discarded threat for most learners. *Instrument:* the review movement's written outcome, recorded per learner as *found* / *not found*.

**Prediction 4.** Learners who record their candidate source and its currency will revise their registry after the adversarial second agent; those who do not will not. *Instrument:* the registry's revision record against the presence of a dated source statement, paired per learner. **If this fails, the dated-source requirement is disclosure with no behavioural effect and should be stated as such rather than taught as a countermeasure.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That binding is what makes a threat model about your system, that the same rule blinds it in a predictable direction, and that the honest response is to write the blindness down rather than to weaken the rule.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about recall. **The source declines to measure it and this session cannot measure it either.** The exclusion register makes visible only the omissions you watched happen; the threats nobody thought of remain invisible, and no artefact in this programme changes that.

**The concession worth remembering.** The reasons in your discard log will all be good ones. That is the failure mode, not the defence.
