## In plain terms

The open playbook's reviewer guidance is the best single page on that site, and it contains an instruction nobody obeys under pressure: **read every line changed.** No sampling. It sits beside a two-pass structure and beside the norm that makes review survivable between colleagues — what matters is a bug caught, not who made it, not who found it, not who fixed it.

The same document then declines, on two separate pages, to say when a change is too large to read. And a third page in the same section makes the observation that makes the refusal expensive: **large changes get biased review**, with the senior engineer's approved unread and the junior engineer's not reviewed at all.

That is the ordinary problem, and it is older than any current tooling. **The session exists for the new one.**

The delivery model behind this stage rests entirely on expert human validation. It says the tooling amplifies the operator rather than levelling the field, so an experienced engineer gets usable assets and an inexperienced one generates noise; it therefore makes reviewer competence load-bearing and requires a team member to be somebody you would have trusted on a purely human team. **And across approximately ninety internal documents it never asks what happens when the volume arriving at the reviewer changes.**

Review capacity is the binding constraint on the whole model. It is not discussed once. **This programme has no answer either**, and the session's honest move is to require the learner to state their own capacity position rather than to teach them a resolution nobody has.

## Abstract

This paper defends making a written capacity statement the assessed artefact of a session about code review, rather than the review record itself. The argument is that the review norms the stage teaches were written for a world in which production was the expensive step; that a delivery model resting on expert human validation has review capacity as its binding constraint; and that the corpus supplying the model never examines that constraint, which leaves the learner to hold a position their sources do not supply. The session installs a two-pass review under a stated time budget, a record naming what was not read, and a capacity statement naming a supplier. It uses the playbook's own unwarranted claim about the cost of review as a specimen. The paper concedes that the capacity statement is unverifiable, that the binding-constraint claim is this design's inference rather than any source's finding, and that requiring a learner to write down what they let through invites a socially safe answer rather than a true one.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Review is the only place in a delivery process where one person's work is examined by another before it becomes everyone's problem. It is also the first thing that degrades when a date stops moving, and it degrades silently, because a review that read nothing looks exactly like a review that read everything.

**The playbook's rules address that degradation directly and are unusually good.** Two passes with different objects. Every line read. A depersonalising norm that makes finding a defect a collective event rather than an accusation. Reviewers drawn from both organisations on a joint engagement. And two paired rules that keep the boundary from being litigated in the thread — an out-of-scope issue may not block, an in-scope change may not be deferred.

**They do not address the volume question at all**, and neither does the site's own measures page, which proposes verifying branch policies as evidence — evidence that review was *required*, not that it was *performed well* — and offers three measures it concedes are gathered by hand.

The new problem is arithmetic. If the rate at which reviewable change is produced changes and the rate at which it can be read does not, the every-line rule stops binding, and it stops binding without anybody deciding that it should. **What replaces it is undocumented, unexamined and individually reasonable each time.**

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** Every learner with professional experience has reviewed under pressure and has approved something they did not fully read. What they will not have is a written record that says so, or a capacity statement.

It assumes [S042](/wiki/seminars/S042.md)'s scope vocabulary, because the two most useful non-personal ways to decline a change — *that is a new item* and *that is not what the specification asked for* — come from [S042](/wiki/seminars/S042.md) and [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) respectively. It assumes [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md)'s question about who is not in the room, applied here to reviewer selection.

The prior the session works against is that review quality is a matter of diligence. **It is taught here as a matter of supply**, which reframes an individual failing as a system property and is the only framing under which the honest answer is professionally sayable.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Low intrinsic load and the module's highest affective load. The session asks a learner to record in writing that they approved something they had not fully read, in a professional culture where that admission is career-relevant.

**3.2 Why the capacity statement rather than the review record.** A review record describes one event and can be excellent while the system it sits in is failing. **A capacity statement describes the system**, and it is the only artefact in the stage that can be wrong in a way that matters after the engagement.

**3.3 Why the binding-constraint framing is used.** If a process depends on a scarce validating step, improvements elsewhere raise the arrival rate at that step without raising its throughput. This is an ordinary property of constrained systems and it is cited here as mechanism only. **The specific claim that review is the binding constraint in this delivery model is an inference from the sources' own dependence on expert validation, and it is this design's, not theirs.**

**3.4 Why the unwarranted claim is shown rather than described.** The playbook's evidence-and-measures page closes by asserting that the cost of doing code reviews is actually negative, with no measurement, no baseline, no comparison and no source, and a sibling page asserts a productivity benefit for pairing on the same evidence. **Both are inside an otherwise disciplined document and are written in the same flat register as the well-sourced pages.** A constructed example cannot teach that; a real one can, and this repository's reading records the pattern rather than treating it as an isolated slip.

**3.5 Why item 5 exists.** The internal corpus contains exactly one refusal in approximately ninety documents — declining to replace human reviewers with a machine in a legal-review process, on the grounds that those reviewers catch issues with legal consequences. **A refusal that costs its author something is the most trustworthy content available in a body of work with no citations**, and asking the learner to write their own version installs a habit the corpus itself exercises once.

**3.6 Why the failure is a voice problem before it is a process problem.**

Every mechanism in §1 assumes somebody says something, and under a date that is not moving the sentence is what fails first. The construct that names this defines it as **"the belief that the work environment is safe for interpersonal risk taking"**, where the risks avoided are appearing "ignorant, incompetent, or disruptive" ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)).

Two of its ideas are load-bearing for the session's design rather than decorative. The **asymmetry of voice and silence** — voice pays the organisation, later, uncertainly; silence pays the self, now, certainly — explains why delivery pressure sharpens the incentive rather than merely adding to the workload: the delay voice imposes is immediate and visible, and the defect it prevents is neither. And the **implicit theories of voice** — do not criticise something the boss may have helped create, do not speak without solid data, do not say anything negative in a group — sit exactly on top of the playbook's published observation that large changes get biased review. **The two describe the same event from opposite sides, which is why the design's countermeasure is structural rather than moral: change the record format so that not opening a file must be written down.**

The construct's bounds are taken as seriously as its content. It is **not immunity from consequences**; it is orthogonal to performance standards rather than traded against them; sanction is inside the model, since "psychological safety is reinforced rather than harmed by fair, thoughtful responses to potentially dangerous, harmful, or sloppy behavior"; and it is not sufficient, being "an enabler" rather than the fuel.

**The limits are substantial and this paper states them rather than borrowing the construct's popularity.** The trade-press book is not an empirical study; its literature chapter is a **narrative review with no search protocol, no inclusion criteria, no quality appraisal and no negative finding reported across a hundred-plus articles**, written by the field's most-cited author; its organisational cases are secondary throughout and were selected after the outcomes were known; and **no trial of its leadership framework is reported anywhere in it.**

**3.7 Why the aggregate synthesis is cited instead of the popular statement, and what it may be cited for.**

For the state of the evidence the design uses the meta-analysis, which pools many independent samples, **ranks antecedents against each other rather than listing them**, and tests whether the construct predicts task performance and citizenship behaviours **over and above positive leader relations and work engagement** — the sceptic's question, asked properly ([research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)). It also tests **homology** across individual and group levels rather than assuming it, which matters because a review pair is a dyad and a delivery crew is a group.

**This repository read that work from its official abstract because the publisher version is closed, and the abstract reports no numerical effect sizes.** No correlation for any antecedent or outcome may be quoted from it here, and none is. The pooled evidence is also overwhelmingly cross-sectional and self-report, so causal direction is not established, and construct heterogeneity across many samples means the pooled estimate averages over measures that are not identical.

**3.8 Why felt throughput is not admissible evidence for a capacity statement.**

The capacity statement asks a learner to describe a rate. The design treats a felt rate as an instrument reading and asks whether the instrument is calibrated, because a session built on *how much do you think you are producing* would rest on the one judgement most likely to be wrong.

The available evidence, in one narrow setting, is that measured and perceived speed can diverge and that **direct experience did not correct the perception**: after doing the work, participants still believed the tooling had made them faster ([research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md](/research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md)). **If a team cannot feel its own throughput accurately, it cannot feel its own review debt accurately either**, which is the whole argument for requiring the capacity statement to name hours and suppliers rather than impressions.

**Limits, which are severe:** very small developer sample; expert maintainers in codebases they know intimately, close to a worst case for assistance and not representative of software work generally; self-reported time; one fixed early tooling generation; **an explicit non-claims list published by the authors that must be cited alongside the headline**; and **the research group now labels the result out of date and has redesigned the study**, because participants will no longer reliably work without the tooling. A later run pointed the other way and its own authors judge the signal unreliable. **No magnitude from any of it appears in this paper or the session.**

**3.9 Why review findings need a normalised severity, and why the automated reviewer is input rather than output.**

The published agent-configuration corpus this programme reads is the clearest available worked example of review built as an artefact rather than as a habit. It separates **factual orientation from judgement**, scales **depth to risk**, fixes the **lenses** a reviewer applies, and normalises **severity** so that two reviewers produce comparable findings, persisting them under stable identifiers ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)).

Two of its declarations do more work in this design than any of its mechanisms. **"Execution completion and acceptance outcome are different results"** — which is precisely what an automated reviewer's output is, a completed execution and not an acceptance. And **an agent may not record a human's approval**: the corpus prohibits an agent from checking human-review boxes and requires a workflow to stop when a required human review is absent. Both are declarations rather than enforcement, and the corpus says so.

The corpus is candid about its own gates in ways that transfer directly. Its behavioural checks are **regex-graded and test output shape rather than correctness**, by its own documentation. A check can be **advisory** at one tier and **authoritative** at another, so the same finding has different consequences depending on where it ran. And its summary judgement is the sentence the session ends on: **the existence of a control is not evidence that the control works.**

**Limits:** read at one pinned commit on a named date, **at a revision that was not all-green** — one reported check was failing. Its prose uses causal and outcome vocabulary — "ship faster", "prevents", "proven" — and **none of those readings is established by anything in it**: no controlled study, no calibrated baseline, no defect-rate comparison, no human-rated outcome dataset. **Its evaluation machinery produces artefacts at run time and stores none of them.** Its paths, artefact kinds and package versions are vendor detail that moved during the reading.

**3.10 Why the error framing matters for review comments specifically.**

The error-management literature's method pairs minimal guidance with explicit framing of errors as informative, and gives **no further assistance when an error occurs** — which is close to a description of a review comment. Its most operationally consequential moderator is that the task must supply interpretable feedback of its own, because where feedback cannot readily be interpreted the method may be worse than instruction ([research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md)).

The design's use is narrow and it explains a rule that would otherwise look like etiquette. **A comment naming a defect is interpretable feedback; "I would have written this differently" is not**, and the second is not merely unhelpful but actively worse than instruction under the moderator the literature names. That is why the style review sits in the session's failure list rather than in a note about tone.

**Limits:** every study in the pool compared one training method against another rather than against no training, so all effects are relative; **no study measured on-the-job performance**; almost all taught software skills in a laboratory; and **the feedback-clarity moderator received, in the authors' own words, only limited support.** No effect size from it appears here.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 1 is the instrument and it is asked in an unusual form.** *Say the honest answer* is written into the item, because the item's value is destroyed by the socially safe response and the design would rather ask for honesty explicitly than pretend the pressure is not there. **§8 concedes this is a weak defence.**

**The time budget is stated before the review.** A budget derived afterwards is a description of what happened.

**The capacity statement must name a supplier**, and *nobody in particular* is an accepted answer. That acceptance is deliberate: the commonest true answer is that review capacity is nobody's responsibility, and a design that refused that answer would produce fictions.

**A reviewer from outside the learner's organisation is required or its absence explained**, following the playbook's rule for joint engagements, and connecting to [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md).

**The specification from [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) is required as a review instrument**, so that at least one finding is a conformance failure rather than a preference. Without it, review sessions produce style findings, which are the safest thing to raise and the least useful.

**The work movement puts the playbook's unwarranted claims in front of the learner mid-session**, immediately after they have written their own honest record. The juxtaposition is intentional: the learner has just produced a bounded, disclosed account of their own review, and is then shown a published standard asserting a benefit with nothing behind it.

**4.1 What the platform workflow is for, and why the anchor is a document as much as a tool.**

This session's anchor is deliberately double, and the pairing is the design. On one side, an automated reviewer attached to the learner's own pull requests. On the other, the published standard this programme itself draws on, **with its four unsupported figures named and none of them reproduced.**

The automated reviewer supplies the arithmetic. It is the cheapest available way to make the volume question concrete: findings arrive faster than they can be verified, and the record's `accepted_unverified` field turns that into a number the learner wrote themselves. **The design's operating rule — a machine's findings are input to a review and accepting them is a decision you made — is warranted by the configuration corpus's own distinction between a completed execution and an acceptance**, and by its own prohibition on an agent recording a human's approval.

The document supplies the calibration, and it is the more important half. **A page that reasons carefully and a page that repeats a number are written in the same flat register, and nothing in the navigation, the layout or the prose distinguishes them.** The session's first exercise strips four pages of their footers and asks the learner to rank them by reliance; the ranking is impossible from the prose and easy from the bibliography, which is the entire lesson. Every engineer this programme sends into industry will meet a document like this, usually internal, usually written by people they respect.

**The choice to run the calibration on this programme's own source is deliberate and is not a rhetorical flourish.** A constructed specimen teaches suspicion of constructed specimens. A real one, from the document the learner has been assigned all term, teaches that authority of tone is not evidence — including here.

**No magnitude from any of the four figures may be written down at any point in the session**, and the standard's exercise says so in its instructions rather than trusting the prohibition to be remembered.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The softened record.** The likeliest failure. A learner writes a review record they would be comfortable circulating. *Countermeasure:* the requirement to state the time budget first, and the standard's demand that the record say what was not read *in those words*.

**The style review.** *Countermeasure:* the required conformance finding against [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md)'s specification, and the standard's warning sign — nothing you raised would have caused an incident.

**The capacity denial.** Review treated as free. *Countermeasure:* the requirement to name a supplier, and the standard's warning sign — your statement contains no hours belonging to anyone but you.

**The rubber stamp and the seniority effect.** *Countermeasure:* the playbook's own observation about biased review is given in the situation, before the learner reviews anything, so that the effect is nameable while it is happening.

**The delegated judgement.** A machine reviewer's findings accepted as the review. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires the learner to say which findings they verified, and [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) develops the point properly.

**The comfortable refusal.** Item 5 is answered with something nobody was going to ask for. *Countermeasure:* none available. **§8 concedes it.**

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. Both artefacts are submitted at [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md), where three properties are assessed: that the record still says what was not read, that the capacity statement is unrevised, and that something was refused.

**The capacity statement's accuracy is not assessed.** It cannot be — the counterfactual is unavailable — and the design marks its existence, its specificity and its unrevised survival into the pack instead.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: asking learners to record that they approved unread code is asking them to create a document that could be used against them, and teaching them a habit that a competent professional will abandon on their first day in industry.**

At its strongest: in most organisations, a written record stating *I approved this without reading it* is discoverable, is career-relevant, and in a regulated environment may be worse than that. The session is safe because it is a classroom. The habit it installs is not safe, and a graduate who carries it will either be harmed by it or will quietly stop, in which case the session taught nothing that survives contact.

**Substantially conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to this session.** Two partial answers. The artefact that carries forward is the **capacity statement**, which is a system-level claim and is professionally safe — *our rate of production exceeds what our review can absorb* is a finding about an organisation, not a confession by a person, and the session's work movement says so explicitly. The review record's honest boundary is required here so that the capacity statement has something to rest on, and [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md) assesses the capacity statement's survival rather than the record's circulation. **The declined fix is to drop the per-review boundary and keep only the capacity statement.** It is declined because a capacity statement written without a specific record beneath it is a guess, and the guess is always flattering. **The residual risk is real and the design accepts it while naming it in the session.**

**Objection 2: the binding-constraint claim is invented, and the paper says so in one line while building a whole session on it.**

At its strongest: no source establishes that review is the binding constraint. The sources establish only that the model depends on expert validation. The inference requires an assumption — that production rates changed materially — which this repository refuses to quantify anywhere, and a claim that cannot be quantified without violating the programme's own prohibitions is a claim the programme should not build a session on.

**Conceded in full, without qualification.** The session's response is not to defend the inference but to make the learner produce their own evidence for or against it. The capacity statement asks what the learner's own rate demands, whether the demand exceeded supply, and what happened; **a learner whose demand never exceeded supply produces a finding that counts against the design's inference, and the standard says so.** The declined fix is to remove the framing and teach review hygiene alone. It is declined because the hygiene without the framing is available in any textbook and the framing is the only thing in this session that belongs to forward-deployed work — where the delivery organisation's own model makes validation load-bearing and never budgets for it.

**Objection 3: teaching learners to distrust the programme's own principal source undermines every other session that rests on it.**

At its strongest: the playbook supplies the trade study specification, the decision record fields, the testing norms, the scope rule and the review practice — material used across [M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md) and [M09](/wiki/modules/M09.md) and cited as authoritative throughout. **This session hands learners a method for discrediting it and runs the method on its most quotable page.** A cohort that leaves able to say *the playbook asserts things it cannot support* has been given a general-purpose solvent, and the predictable use of a solvent is not careful calibration but wholesale dismissal of any source that is inconvenient. The design also cannot have it both ways: if the four figures show that the document does not distinguish between a practice it reasoned about and a number it repeated, then the practices it states without evidence — which is all of them — inherit the same doubt, and this programme has built a stage on them.

**Partly conceded, and the second half is conceded entirely.** The practices this programme takes from the playbook are taken as **checkable conventions rather than as validated findings**, and every paper in this module says so in its own evidence-status section: the container rule is adopted because conformance is visible in an artefact's history, not because anybody showed it improves delivery; the every-line rule is taught because its failure mode is legible, not because sampling was measured against it. **That is the same standard the session applies to the four figures, applied consistently.** The solvent risk is real and the rebuttal is that the alternative is worse: a learner who has never been shown a respected document failing its own standard will meet one in their first job with no method at all. **The declined fix is to run the calibration exercise on a constructed specimen or on a source the programme does not otherwise use.** It is declined because a constructed specimen teaches suspicion of constructed specimens, and a stranger's document permits the comfortable conclusion that other people's standards are unreliable. **The cost is that some learners will over-generalise, and prediction 7 is written to detect it.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Answers to opening item 1 will cluster at implausibly high self-reported reading, and will fall when the same learners produce a record with a stated time budget. *Instrument:* the item-1 answer against the record's own unread boundary, paired per learner.

**Prediction 2.** Most capacity statements will name no supplier of review other than the learner and their immediate team. *Instrument:* the supplier field, coded *named individual* / *named team* / *nobody in particular*, by a second reader.

**Prediction 3.** Learners will report demand exceeding supply less often than their production records imply. *Instrument:* the capacity statement against the change volume recorded in the increment pack at [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md).

**Prediction 4.** Learners shown the playbook's unwarranted claims will identify what is missing from them without prompting, having just written a bounded account of their own. *Instrument:* the written response to the specimen, coded for whether it names a missing baseline, comparison or instrument. **If this fails, the juxtaposition in §4 is decorative** and the specimen should be moved to a lecture where it can be taught directly.

**Prediction 5.** In the four-page ranking exercise, learners' rankings will correlate with the pages' bibliographies and not with their prose, and a majority will report having expected the opposite. *Instrument:* the ranking against an independent coding of each page's citation quality, plus a single free-text question on what the learner expected to be decisive.

**Prediction 6.** Records will report more findings accepted unverified than verified, and the ratio will be worse for learners who ran the automated reviewer before their own second pass than for those who ran it after. *Instrument:* the record's verified and unverified counts, split on the declared order of the passes. **If the order has no effect, the session's instruction to review first is ceremony** and should be replaced with a rule about verification quota instead.

**Prediction 7.** A minority of learners will generalise the calibration exercise into a rejection of the playbook as a whole, rather than into a page-level reading habit. *Instrument:* a single free-text question at [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md) asking what the learner now does when handed an internal standard, coded *page-level habit* / *wholesale rejection* / *no change* by a second reader. **This predicts the failure §8's third objection names**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a review which did not read everything must say so; that review is a supply with an owner; and that a delivery model depending on expert validation has an unexamined constraint at its centre.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about whether reading every line produces better software. The playbook states the rule and supplies no comparative evidence, and asserts on its own measures page that review costs less than nothing with no measurement behind it. **That assertion is a specimen here and is adopted nowhere.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The binding-constraint claim at the centre of this session is invented. It is a good argument and it is not a finding, and the session is designed so that your own capacity statement can count against it.
