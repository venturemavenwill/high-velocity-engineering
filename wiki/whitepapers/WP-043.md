# WP-043 — Tests as the Specification

**Seminar day:** [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) · **Module:** [M09](/wiki/modules/M09.md) · **Quarter:** [Q5](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 3
**Platform anchor:** An executable specification for conversation behaviour — the Microsoft 365 Agents Playground's headless conversation driver, deterministic and model-free in CI, beside the evaluator catalogue it neither reaches nor mentions

> ## Release rule
>
> **§§4, 5 and 9 are post-session reading.** §4 explains why the wrong implementation is built on purpose and why no executor is fixed in the header, §5 names the failure the session is designed to produce, and §9 states the predicted cohort behaviour. §§1, 2, 3 and 8 spoil nothing — §3 in particular contains the contradiction the session is built on and is better read before than after.

## In plain terms

The open playbook says two blunt things about tests. Code is **incomplete** if it is not accompanied by tests. Tests are committed in the **same change** as the code they cover, and *I will add tests next* is recorded on that page as not acceptable.

Both are ordinary hygiene. Neither is why the session exists.

**The session exists because of a single piece of practitioner testimony held in speaker notes in the internal corpus, contradicting that corpus's own headline.** On a mature production codebase, the assistant could not be asked to add a feature end to end — the generated code reused outdated patterns and ignored architectural constraints — and the workaround was to **make failing tests the specification and spend most of the human effort there.** The same collection's customer-facing material describes end-to-end feature delivery as the model's signature achievement.

**This paper teaches the contradiction and does not resolve it**, because the testimony is the better-evidenced half and neither half is evidence. The testimony names a codebase property, a failure mechanism and a workaround that costs something. The headline names none of those. **A learner who leaves able to say *this worked here and not there, and here is the property that distinguishes them* has the competence.**

The consequence the testimony implies and the headline conceals is the session's payload. **If the implementation gets cheap, the human effort moves into the specification. That is a redistribution, not a saving**, and nothing in the collection prices where it moved to.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching specification-as-executable-check on the basis of a single contradicted practitioner account rather than on the basis of the testing norms it superficially resembles. The argument is that the interesting claim is not that tests should exist but that a specification which only functions when a human reads it cannot support delegation; that the account of a mature codebase defeating end-to-end generation is the best-evidenced statement in its collection and is still one engineer's experience; and that the redistribution of human effort from implementation to specification is unpriced everywhere it is described. The session installs a failing specification, an untestable residue with named holders, a deliberately wrong passing implementation, and a delegation test conducted against a real executor. The paper concedes that the session's central source is a single unmeasured account, that the residue's boundary is a judgement rather than a criterion, and that the delegation test measures the executor at least as much as the specification.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Most engineers can say what they are building. Very few can say what *done* means for one behaviour in a form that something other than themselves could check.

The gap is invisible while the person who holds the intent is also the person writing the code. **It becomes the whole problem at the moment of delegation**, and delegation is what the rest of this stage is about.

There is a second problem which is newer and worse. When implementation was expensive, the specification could be thin and the expense of implementing was itself a forcing function: an engineer who had to write the code by hand encountered every ambiguity in the specification and resolved it, usually correctly, usually silently. **When implementation becomes cheap, the ambiguities are resolved anyway — by something that has no access to the intent and no obligation to report which resolutions it chose.**

The practitioner testimony names the observable consequence precisely: outdated patterns reused, architectural constraints ignored. Both are ambiguity resolutions made against the wrong prior, and both are invisible in an output that passes.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** Test-first working is widely practised and many learners will arrive with it. What they will not arrive with is the untestable residue, the deliberate wrong implementation, or the position that a specification must not depend on who reads it.

It assumes [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md)'s falsifiable requirement — this session is the same discipline in executable form — and [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md)'s verifiable non-functional requirements, which are where the residue mostly comes from. It assumes [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md)'s slice, because the specification is written for one behaviour inside it.

The prior the session works against is that tests are verification. **Here they are the specification**, and the difference is directional: verification asks whether the code is right, specification asks what right would be, and only the second can be handed to somebody who has not seen the code.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The module's highest intrinsic load. Four distinct artefacts — the failing checks, the residue, the wrong implementation, the delegation record — and the third is the one learners drop when time runs short.

**3.2 Why the contradiction is taught rather than resolved.** The two accounts in the corpus are not equally warranted and neither is warranted enough to adopt. **The testimony is more specific and more costly to its author**, which is the only quality signal available in a body of work with no citations, no measurements and no failures. Teaching the contradiction rather than the testimony preserves the learner's ability to notice which of the two their own experience resembles, which is the transferable act.

**3.3 Why a check that has never failed has specified nothing.** A check written after the implementation is shaped by it and inherits its assumptions. The failure is not that such checks are useless — they catch regressions — but that they are evidence about the code rather than a statement about the requirement.

**3.4 Why the wrong implementation is built.** The gap between *passes* and *correct* is not visible from a passing suite. It becomes visible when somebody tries to satisfy the suite badly. **This is the productive-failure structure the programme uses elsewhere as a teaching design, applied here as a professional technique** rather than as a pedagogy.

**3.5 Why the residue must have a holder rather than a policy.** A constraint that no check can express is going to be enforced by a person or not at all. Naming the person converts an aspiration into an assignment, and an unassigned residue entry is the mechanism by which architectural constraints get ignored — which is exactly what the testimony reports happening.

**3.6 Why no executor is fixed.** If the session named an agent in its header, a learner could satisfy it by writing something a particular model happened to follow that term. **The claim being taught is that the specification must not care who reads it**, and fixing the reader would make the claim untestable.

**3.7 Why the suite is decomposed before it is written.**

A suite organised by file or by module reports that something broke. A suite organised by **feature and scenario** reports which situation broke, which is the only organisation that supports delegation, because the executor needs to know what situation they failed rather than which assertion fired ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md)).

The same source supplies the position this design takes on completeness, and it is a deliberate break with software-testing convention: **"unlike traditional unit tests, you don't necessarily need a 100% pass rate. Your pass rate is a product decision."** Importing the everything-green habit into this setting is a category error, and the design's consequence is that a specification is judged on whether its failures were chosen rather than on whether it is green.

**Limits:** a practitioner essay resting on one company's sustained case study, with no control condition, no measured improvement and no released dataset; its tooling recommendations are dated to early 2024 and will drift; and one figure it uses is explicitly its author's rhetorical estimate rather than a measurement, and is not reproduced here.

**3.8 Why the specification is expected to move, and why that is not a planning failure.**

The strongest objection to specification-first work is that a specification cannot be written before the outputs exist. **The research naming that problem agrees, and states the circularity plainly: "users need criteria to grade outputs, but grading outputs helps users define criteria"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md)). Its sharper half is that some criteria appear to be **dependent on the specific outputs observed**, which "raises serious questions for approaches that assume the independence of evaluation from observation of model outputs."

Two design consequences follow and both are in the artefact. **A specification whose criteria never moved was not examined**, so the standard requires at least one criterion recorded as having become statable only after an output was seen, with its date. And the revision must be versioned rather than silent, because the cost the source raises and does not resolve is real: **outputs that produced a criterion cannot then serve as a clean held-out set for it.** The design's answer is to freeze a version and hold out fresh cases before any claim that matters, which is a discipline and not a solution.

The same work supplies the question the session inherits wholesale — **who validates the validators?** — and the observation that using a generated instrument to check generated output relocates the trust problem rather than solving it.

**Limits:** qualitative evidence from a small interface study; it establishes that criteria drift is real and consequential, not how prevalent or how large; the tool it describes is a 2024 research prototype.

**3.9 Why the split strategy is the platform's most transferable idea.**

An executable specification over a multi-turn interaction has to cut the conversation before it can score it, and the documentation states the consequence without hedging: **"Multi-turn conversations must be split into query and response halves for evaluation. How you split determines *what you're evaluating*"** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)).

**An agent that recovers from a bad first attempt passes under one published strategy and fails under another, and neither score is wrong.** The design uses this as the session's single most compact demonstration that **the unit of analysis is a choice which silently determines what a number means** — a lesson that transfers to every measurement a learner will ever report and outlives every product name in it.

Two further published properties are load-bearing. The **local-versus-cloud evaluator tiering** puts the cost-and-speed ladder in the type system, so the architecture carries the discipline rather than a policy document. And the **threshold is separable from the measurement**: moving it changes the pass rate without changing the system, which is why the standard requires a justification and treats an inherited default as somebody else's release decision executing on the learner's engagement.

**3.10 Why the tool-failure decomposition is taught as vocabulary rather than as tooling.**

The catalogue separates choosing the wrong tool, calling the right tool with wrong arguments, the call failing technically, and ignoring a successful result. **Two of those produce a system that reports success and is wrong**, which is the failure class hardest to find in production and the one a single *did the tool work* check conflates away.

The design's interest is the vocabulary. A learner who can say *this is an input-accuracy failure, not a selection failure* has a debugging language for a class of bug they otherwise describe as *it didn't work* — and the language survives the evaluator names.

**3.11 Why an executable specification inherits every limit of a measurement.**

A specification that gates something is an experiment. **Power analysis before the run is what turns "we tested and found nothing" into a statement about the system rather than about the test** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). Two further disciplines transfer directly: **paired comparison** on the same cases removes the variation two versions share and costs nothing, and **suppressing variance by changing a setting whose purpose is something else** shifts what is being measured rather than measuring it better. The platform's own **repetitions** mechanism exists for the same reason and says so — one sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement.

The practitioner literature adds the constraint that governs the whole artefact: **an evaluation pipeline should be more stable than the thing it evaluates**, because a score that moves for reasons internal to the instrument cannot be used to monitor the application ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md)). That is in tension with §3.8's requirement that criteria move, and the design resolves it by versioning rather than by choosing: **criteria may move, and every movement is dated and justified, so that a change in the score can be attributed to the system or to the instrument.**

**Limits:** the measurement source is analytical rather than empirical, its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions, and it addresses comparing scores rather than choosing what to score — a tight interval around a badly chosen criterion is still meaningless. **No number from it appears here.** The practitioner book is a first edition dated December 2024 whose strongest evaluation claim its author marks as a belief.

**3.12 Why the wrong implementation is built rather than described, and where that warrant stops.**

The design's warrant for building something you intend to throw away is the error-management literature: minimal guidance, explicit framing of errors as informative, and **no further assistance offered when an error occurs** ([research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md)). The meta-analysis supports the approach for transfer to novel tasks and finds no advantage on performance measured during training itself, which is why the wrong implementation is not marked.

**The moderator matters more than the headline for this design.** Because the method withholds guidance, the task must supply interpretable feedback of its own, and where feedback cannot readily be interpreted the method may be worse than instruction. **The authors' own verdict is that this hypothesis "received only limited support"** — the moderator test did not reach significance and its coding had low interrater reliability. The design therefore carries it as a caution and not as a finding, and builds the exercise around checks that go green rather than around scores that go up, because **a failing check is interpretable feedback and a judged score with no reason string is not.**

**Further limits:** every study in the pool compared one training method against another rather than against no training, so all effects are relative; **no study measured on-the-job performance**; almost all taught software skills in a laboratory; and the pool was not statistically heterogeneous, so the moderators were theory-driven partitions rather than partitions the data demanded. **No effect size from it appears anywhere in this paper or the session.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 4 is the instrument.** *What does a test suite that passes on wrong code look like?* Learners who can answer it concretely before instruction are rare, and the distribution of answers predicts the quality of the wrong-implementation exercise almost exactly.

**Item 5 is the paper's payload asked as a question.** *If the implementation becomes cheap, where does the human effort go?* The testimony's answer is *into the specification*, and the session's standard requires the learner to notice if their own specification took less effort than their implementation, which is the observable that says the redistribution did not happen.

**The delegation test permits a colleague from another team as a better instrument than an agent.** This is deliberate and it is the design's hedge against §8's second objection: a human executor from outside the codebase tests the specification's legibility without confounding it with a model's particular failure modes.

**The failure record is the assessed output of the delegation, not the working code.** What the executor got wrong is the specification's quality measurement, and it is the only measurement of specification quality available to a learner in a fortnight.

**The testimony is read against the learner's own result in the same movement.** If outdated patterns and ignored constraints appear, the residue was incomplete. If they do not, the learner must name the property of their codebase or specification that prevented it. **Both branches produce a finding**, which is why the movement can be run on a first offering with no cohort history.

**4.1 What the platform workflow is for, and why the specification is written as a dataset.**

The workflow exists to make one claim physically true in an afternoon: **that a requirement and a wish differ by whether something other than a person can tell they have been met.**

Writing the specification as a dataset rather than as prose is the design's operational move, and the platform supports it directly — test data is JSONL, expected tool calls can be stated separately from expected outputs, and **pre-existing responses can be evaluated "from logs or previous runs… without re-running the agent"**, which is what makes production traces evaluable rather than only synthetic cases ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). The configuration file carries the split strategy, the thresholds with their justifications, and the untestable residue with its holders, so that **the specification's own policy decisions are versioned alongside its cases** rather than living in somebody's memory.

The anchor was chosen for a second reason, and it is the uncomfortable one. **This documentation publishes no agreement rates with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data and no account of where its default thresholds came from**, while shipping a catalogue convenient enough to be adopted wholesale. That combination is the exact condition a forward-deployed engineer meets on every platform they will ever be handed: a well-built instrument with no published validation. **The session's validate-the-validator exercise exists because of that silence, and the silence is the teaching object.**

**The workflow is assessed on the reasoning it produces and never on the harness.** No claim is made anywhere in this paper or the session about any evaluator's agreement with human judgement, because none is published and inventing one is the failure this programme exists to prevent.

**4.2 Why the anchor now includes a layer with no model in it, and why the toolchain's silence is the better half of the lesson.**

Everything in §4.1 is judged evaluation: expensive, slow, stochastic, and dependent on an instrument nobody has validated. **A session about specification cannot rest entirely on that, because a specification whose result varies run to run is a measurement wearing a specification's clothes.** The design therefore adds the cheaper layer beneath it, and the cheaper layer turns out to be the one that behaves like a specification at all.

The vendor's toolchain ships a **headless conversation driver**: a test client that drives multi-turn conversations against the agent's endpoint, an operation isolating each case in a fresh conversation, a response object carrying text, attachments, suggested actions and a type, and an HTTP facade returning per-turn statuses of completed, timed out, errored or skipped ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agents-toolkit-and-sdk-family.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agents-toolkit-and-sdk-family.md)). **It is deterministic, fast, free per run and model-free**, which makes it the right instrument for a session about tests as specification and the right place to install the distinction the measurement strand needs three sessions later.

**The design's second and larger reason for the anchor is a silence, and it is a different silence from §4.1's.** In §4.1 the documentation ships a catalogue and declines to publish its validation. Here the toolchain **has no evaluation surface at all and never says so**: its validation command checks a manifest against schema and rules, its driver drives conversations, **nothing in it scores agent quality, nothing connects to the evaluator catalogue, and no page acknowledges the gap or tells a developer where to go next.** The seam between *valid* and *good* is unbridged and unmarked.

**That combination is the session's thesis rendered as a product decision.** A team that gets its conversation tests green has satisfied every quality signal its toolchain offers and has measured nothing — which is precisely the claim the session makes about specifications, arriving from the vendor rather than from the design. **The workflow's step 8 is therefore assessed on the comment block rather than on the tests**, and the block is required to carry the sandbox's own published limitations verbatim, because that table is the only honest account available of what a green run did not touch.

**The contrast within one product is worth teaching alongside the silence.** The sandbox **publishes what it cannot emulate** — unprocessed manifest features, one supported card type with four of its features excluded by name, no mobile or meeting surfaces, and a comparison table naming what is unavailable. A tool that states its own bound can be reasoned about; a tool that does not state its seam cannot. **The same family does both, on adjacent pages.**

**The limits are unusually severe for a taught surface and the design accepts them because it assesses none of it.** The driver interface is documented **inside an editor extension rather than on any published page**, with no public URL, no currency guarantee and citation by extension version only. **Six pages were fetched and read in full and roughly thirty more reached only through search excerpts**, so no list is exhaustive. Principal names in this area have all changed at least once and one package twice, and two package names for the sandbox appear on live pages simultaneously. **And the decisive absence: no fidelity claim is published anywhere** — a capability difference table exists, and nothing states how often an agent that passes locally behaves differently in a real tenant, which the quieter default channel makes sharper rather than softer.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The retrofitted test.** The likeliest failure and the one every source already warns about. *Countermeasure:* the requirement to watch the checks fail, and the standard's warning sign — you have never seen it red.

**The empty residue.** A learner declares that every constraint is checkable, because declaring it is cheaper than naming a holder. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires a non-empty residue with owners, and [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) collects the residue as the raw material for an instruction set, which makes an empty residue expensive three sessions later.

**The skipped wrong implementation.** The exercise looks like waste because the specification already passes. *Countermeasure:* the work movement names it as the session's most productive period and the one learners skip. **This is a warning rather than a mechanism and §8 concedes it is weak.**

**The shape check.** A check asserts the form of an output and is read as asserting its correctness. *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — your check would pass on a plausible-looking wrong answer — and [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md), where the same distinction is met again in a tool that declares it about itself.

**The effort that vanished.** Implementation got cheap, specification did not get richer, and nobody noticed. *Countermeasure:* the standard's comparison of specification effort against implementation effort, which is self-reported and therefore weak.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the session's central source is one anecdote, and a programme that spends a session on a single unmeasured account while refusing the corpus's other unmeasured accounts is applying its evidential standard selectively.**

At its strongest: this repository's standing discipline is that unwarranted claims are recorded as specimens and adopted nowhere. The testimony has no measurement, no baseline, no independent observation and no named instrument. It is one engineer's report, held in speaker notes. **The design has selected it because it agrees with the design's own scepticism**, which is precisely the failure the programme accuses its sources of — arranging the evidence so that a preferred conclusion is confirmed.

**Conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to this session.** The rebuttal narrows rather than defeats it. The testimony is not adopted as a finding: §3.2 states it is taught as a contradiction rather than as a conclusion, the session's own limits section calls it a hypothesis worth teaching, and the standard marks the learner's *finding* whichever way their delegation test comes out. **What the design does claim asymmetrically is a quality signal — specificity and cost to the author — and that signal is a heuristic, not a warrant.** The declined fix is to teach the testing norms alone and drop the testimony. It is declined because the norms without the testimony are hygiene a learner can get from any source, and the whole reason this session sits in a forward-deployed programme is the delegation problem the testimony describes. **The selectivity is real, it is disclosed here, and the design accepts it.**

**Objection 2: the delegation test measures the executor, not the specification.**

At its strongest: a learner whose specification is excellent and whose executor is poor records a long list of failures; a learner whose specification is thin and whose executor is strong records none. The instrument confounds two variables, and the session's own standard treats the failure record as *the specification's actual quality measurement*, which it is not.

**Substantially conceded.** Three partial mitigations. The session permits a human executor from another team, which changes the confound rather than removing it but makes it visible. The assessed output is the learner's *account* of what went wrong rather than the count of failures, so a learner with a strong executor who cannot explain why nothing went wrong has not passed. And [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) requires the pin — host, model, version — for exactly this reason, so that a result is attributed to a configuration rather than to the idea. **The declined fix is to standardise the executor across the cohort.** It is declined because a standard executor becomes the thing specifications are written for within one cohort, which reinstalls the failure §3.6 exists to prevent.

**Objection 3: the session requires the specification to be written before the implementation and simultaneously teaches that criteria cannot be written before outputs exist, which is incoherent.**

At its strongest: §3.8 adopts a finding that some evaluation criteria are dependent on the outputs observed and cannot be pre-registered, and the standard then requires a criterion recorded as having become statable only after an output was seen. **If that is true, then the artefact the session demands — a failing specification written before the implementation exists — is written under conditions its own theoretical basis says are insufficient.** Worse, the design imports a second constraint that pulls the other way: that an evaluation pipeline should be **more stable than the thing it evaluates**, or the score cannot be used to monitor anything. A learner is therefore told to write criteria early, to expect them to move, and to keep them still. Any behaviour satisfies at least one of those instructions, which means none of them is doing work.

**Partly conceded, and the tension is real rather than apparent.** The rebuttal is that the three instructions apply to different objects and the design should have said so earlier than §3.11. The *cases* are written first and are stable; the *criteria* move and are versioned; the *pipeline configuration* — split, repetitions, thresholds — is the part required to be stable, because it is the part whose movement makes a score uninterpretable. **The design's answer to drift is dating rather than prevention**, so that a change in a result can be attributed to the system or to the instrument. **The declined fix is to drop the before-the-implementation requirement and let the specification be written from observed outputs.** It is declined because a specification written from outputs is a description of the implementation with the failure mode §3.3 names — it inherits the implementation's assumptions and can no longer fail on it — and because the delegation test would then be measuring whether the executor could reproduce code the learner already had. **The cost is that a learner who takes the drift finding seriously will find the first artefact unsatisfying, and prediction 6 is written to detect whether they respond by versioning or by pretending the criteria never moved.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will produce an empty or one-line untestable residue on first attempt. *Instrument:* the residue's entry count and the presence of named holders, before and after the work movement.

**Prediction 2.** Wrong implementations that pass will be produced by a minority of learners, and the ones produced will reveal defects the learner then fixes. *Instrument:* the wrong implementation's presence, and the diff between the specification before and after it.

**Prediction 3.** Delegation failures will cluster on constraints the learner had classified as residue rather than on behaviours specified as checks. *Instrument:* the failure record coded against the specification's own two parts, by a second reader.

**Prediction 4.** Learners will report spending less effort on the specification than on the implementation, contradicting the testimony's account of where the effort goes. *Instrument:* self-reported effort split, collected at the session and again at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md). **The design expects this prediction to hold at this session and to weaken by [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)**, and if it does not weaken, the redistribution claim in §1 has no support at this scale.

**Prediction 5.** Most learners will leave the split strategy at its default on first attempt, and a majority of those will be unable to say what their score would do under a different split. *Instrument:* the configuration file's split field and a single free-text question at the review movement, coded *can state the consequence* / *cannot* by a second reader.

**Prediction 6.** A majority of learners will record no criterion as having become statable only after an output was seen, and their specifications will nonetheless differ between the failing version and the submitted one. *Instrument:* the dated criteria list against a diff of the specification across the session. **A silent revision is the failure §8's third objection predicts**, and this is the only instrument that separates it from genuine stability.

**Prediction 7.** Learners who hand-label traces and compute a disagreement rate against a built-in evaluator will justify their thresholds differently from those who do not, and will be more likely to move a threshold away from its default. *Instrument:* the threshold justification field, split on whether a validation exercise was completed, coded *appeals to default* / *appeals to observed cases* by a second reader. **If this fails, the validate-the-validator exercise is ceremony**, and the session should require it only of learners who intend to gate on the result.

**Prediction 8.** Shown the documented incident — a large passing suite alongside a feature producing no output — and asked what went wrong, a majority of learners will look for a broken or missing *test* before they name a missing *assertion about observable behaviour*. *Instrument:* a single free-text response collected before the diagnosis is given, coded *broken test* / *missing assertion* / *other* by a second reader. **If most learners name the missing assertion unaided, the incident is redundant with §3's shape-check material and should be cut rather than kept.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a specification which only functions when a human reads it cannot support delegation; that a check which has never failed has specified nothing; and that constraints no check can express need a named holder rather than a policy.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about defect rates, delivery speed or software quality. The playbook's testing norms carry no comparative evidence and one of its pages reaches for a cost multiple through a citation that cannot be followed. **That figure is not repeated here.** The testimony is one account.

**The concession worth remembering.** This session selected a single unmeasured anecdote because it agreed with the design's scepticism, and says so in §8. If you leave able to notice that move being made by somebody else — including by this programme — you have the thing it was trying to teach.

**The silences that matter.** **No agreement rates with human judgement are published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no guidance on where the default thresholds came from** — which is the most serious gap in the source and the reason the session's validate-the-validator exercise exists. **No such agreement is asserted anywhere in this paper, in the session, or anywhere in this wiki.** The documentation does not discuss the biases the independent literature documents for model-based judging, although most of its agent evaluators are of that kind. It gives no account of how a judged score behaves across model versions, so a specification gated on one cannot be told from the documentation whether a change in result is a change in the system. It does not say what a threshold's default was calibrated against, so a team inheriting one has inherited a release policy with no stated basis. And it publishes **no measure of how varied a generative instrument's output is across repeated prompts**, which bears directly on the workflow's final step, because that step depends on the instrument producing an implementation the learner did not anticipate and nothing published establishes how often it does.

**The toolchain's silence is of a different kind and is the sharper of the two.** **It has no evaluation surface at all and does not say so.** Its validation command checks a manifest against schema and rules; its driver drives conversations and asserts on the result. **Nothing in it scores agent quality, nothing connects to the evaluator catalogue recorded above, and no page consulted acknowledges the gap or tells a developer where to go next** — the seam between *valid* and *good* is unbridged and unmarked. **No evidence is published that the sandbox predicts deployed behaviour either**: a capability difference table exists and is valuable, a fidelity claim does not exist anywhere, and nothing states how often an agent that passes locally behaves differently in a real tenant. **And the ergonomics vocabulary throughout that material — streamlines, dramatically simplifies, enhanced productivity — is asserted without measurement of any kind and is adopted nowhere in this paper or in the session.**
