## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

How much structure a model is allowed to express is normally taught as a search. Too little and it underfits, too much and it overfits, there is a sweet spot, go and find it. That is not wrong, and it leaves the only interesting question unasked.

**A capacity choice is a claim about the world.** Constrain the model heavily and you have asserted that the underlying pattern is simple. Let it flex and you have asserted that it is not — and, on top of that, that you hold enough data to tell signal from noise at that flexibility. Both are propositions somebody could disagree with. Both are usually settled by a default or by a search rather than by an argument. And both fail quietly: a model whose capacity assumption is wrong still produces plausible outputs and a curve that looks unremarkable.

The instrument for this is the one people least often reach for. Measure how performance moves as you add data. **Flat means the model is the binding constraint, and going out to collect more data — which is what a team does next, at cost — will buy nothing.** That is the most money-saving inference available in applied modelling and almost nobody draws it, because it is a conclusion about your own choice rather than about the data.

The platform makes the point sharper than the argument does. Leave the parameters that set effective capacity unset and the service picks values for you from your own training data, by a procedure that is not published. **An unset parameter is not an avoided decision; it is a delegated one**, and the honest sentence is *a value was chosen from my data by a heuristic I cannot inspect* rather than *the service chose sensibly*.

**What this paper concedes, and it is a real concession.** The day's most persuasive fifteen minutes are its least warranted. It argues that a capacity choice is a structural hypothesis, and then produces a vendor whose method-selection guidance is phrased in exactly that grammar. The room reads that as the frame being confirmed by industry. It is not. **It is product guidance, written to help somebody pick a feature, and the resemblance is one of grammar rather than of provenance** — the design has read its own frame back out of a product description. The day's second-strongest moment is a graph shown and not run, which does the persuasive work of an executed case without its evidential standing.

## Abstract

This paper defends framing a capacity choice as a structural hypothesis about the data, in the sense established at S021 two quarters earlier, and defends planting the previous day's protocol violation inside this day's contrasting cases without announcement. The first argument is that capacity is conventionally taught as an optimisation over a curve with a minimum, that students therefore search for the minimum without asking what a given capacity asserts about the world, and that the assertion is checkable. The second argument is that a transfer claim made at S042 is only tested by an unannounced recurrence, and that the design has an obligation to run such tests rather than to assert transfer in a retention section. The paper additionally defends teaching the learning curve as the primary diagnostic, and defends adding one artefact element the day did not previously have: **a named list of every parameter the student left on a default, with what each assumes.** That element exists because this platform will resolve every fine-tuning hyperparameter for you from your own training data if you leave it on `auto`, which means the day's entire subject is decided by a heuristic nobody in the room can inspect unless the student decides otherwise. It concedes that the day's central account is taught more confidently than the current state of knowledge supports, that it makes a transfer test whose failure it has no plan for, and that its two most persuasive platform moments are a vocabulary correspondence and a demonstration rather than evidence.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Capacity is the amount of structure a model can express, and the conventional treatment is an optimisation: too little capacity underfits, too much overfits, there is a minimum in between, find it. Students absorb this readily and it produces a search.

The search is not wrong and it leaves the important question unasked. **A capacity choice asserts something about the data.** A heavily regularised model claims the underlying pattern is simple; a flexible one claims it is not, and further claims that the available data is sufficient to distinguish signal from noise at that flexibility. Both are propositions about the world. Both are usually chosen by default or by search rather than by argument. And both fail silently — a model whose capacity assumption is wrong produces plausible outputs and a curve that looks unremarkable.

This is precisely the structure S021 taught: a paradigm is a hypothesis about the problem's structure, applying it asserts the hypothesis, and the assertion can be checked. [WP-021](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-021.md) §6 named S043 as the long-range target for that correspondence at a two-quarter gap, and phase 2's bridge asks students to make the identification themselves rather than being told.

The day's second problem is the learning curve, which is the diagnostic students most need and least use. A flat curve says the model is the binding constraint; a steep curve still rising says the data is; a converged curve says neither will move without a change of kind. Setting A in phase 3 is flat, and its lesson — *more data will not help you* — is the single most cost-saving inference available in applied modelling and is almost never drawn.

The third problem is a transfer test the design owes itself. S042 taught that tuning against the reporting set destroys the estimate. Setting D in phase 3 does exactly that, one day later, in a capacity context, **and is not announced as such**. Whether groups identify it is a measurement of whether S042 transferred, and phase 8 reports the result to the room.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Moderate, and lower than S042's.** The day sits in the module's load trough after its peak.

The interactivity is at point 6 and step 6 — capacity and data size are one decision — which requires holding a capacity, a data size and a curve shape together, and reasoning about how the right answer moves when one changes.

Management:

- **The component, the four settings and the five sizes are all fixed at phase 3,** and the component trains in minutes, so groups measure and read rather than design.
- **The curve procedure supplied** at step 3, since measuring one is mechanical and unfamiliar.
- **Regularisation mechanics supplied by IS201.** Three forms worked at point 3 for their *assertions*, not their implementations.
- **The protocol already exists,** so no selection design is needed today.
- **One task and dataset across the module,** fourth consecutive day.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Item 3's flip-point threshold demand,* which requires a quantity students did not ask for at item 1.
- *Setting A's flat curve,* whose lesson is a negative inference students rarely draw.
- *Setting D, unannounced,* as a transfer test.
- *Stating what a capacity assumes,* step 2 — the day's discriminator with step 5.
- *Constructing the data on which the assumption fails,* step 5.
- *The partner's counter-dataset and the detection check,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Regularisation implementation.* IS201's.
- *The formal bias-variance decomposition.* Named at point 5 as a vocabulary and not derived; MS201's.
- *Capacity control by architecture search.* Would reintroduce S042's search problem as the day's subject.
- *The regimes where the classical curve does not hold.* Named at point 5's scope statement and not taught, which is discussed in §8.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** Point 5 treats the bias-variance decomposition as diagnostic vocabulary. It omits the regimes in which the classical curve does not describe observed behaviour — a live research area, not settled, and not summarisable in a seminar. **Safe** as a way of naming which constraint binds on a curve the student has measured. **Unsafe** as a prediction of behaviour at capacities far outside the measured range, which is exactly where students will want to extrapolate and where the classical account is known to be unreliable. The seminar page carries this and it is the most consequential scope statement in the module.

**The platform instance, scoped in the strong form.** The day teaches three fine-tuning methods as three structural hypotheses, teaches `auto` hyperparameter resolution as a delegated capacity decision, and shows one reinforcement fine-tuning job it does not run.

*Safe.* Reasoning about what a method choice claims about your problem's answer structure; about why an unset parameter is a delegated decision rather than an avoided one; about why the cheapest exploration tier being preemptible changes how you interpret a job that vanished; and about why a criterion can be too easy to satisfy. In each case the platform supplies an instance of a claim the day argues on other grounds.

*Unsafe* in three directions. **First, as evidence for the S021 frame.** The vendor's selection criteria are product guidance written to help people choose a feature; their resemblance to this programme's hypothesis language is a resemblance of grammar and not of provenance, and §8's objection 3 concedes that the day's most persuasive moment is its least warranted. **Second, as a specification of what the service actually does.** The heuristics behind `auto` are not published, so the honest statement is *a value was chosen from my data by a procedure I cannot inspect* and not *the service chose sensibly* — and the day requires the former wording. **Third, as a claim about reinforcement fine-tuning.** That case is **demonstrated rather than operated**: the room sees one graph and one documented reading, and no student runs a job, so nothing about it is assessed and nothing in the artefact depends on it.

The boundary is delivered to students at points 8, 9 and 10 rather than left to this paper, per the widening recorded on the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md). **The artefact element under which a student applying it outside its safe range would fail is step 8**, which requires each default to be named with what it assumes, and which fails a student who reports the service's choice as their own.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **a flat curve that means something students do not expect**.

Setting A's curve does not improve with data. The instinctive reading is that the model is bad; the correct reading is that the model is the binding constraint and that collecting more data — which is what a team would otherwise do next, at cost — will not help. That inference is worth money, students recognise it as such, and it arrives from a graph rather than from an assertion.

Setting D supplies the day's second and quieter mechanism, and its effect is on the cohort rather than on individuals. Groups who spot it recognise their own competence; groups who do not are shown at phase 8 that a discipline they committed to yesterday did not fire when it was not labelled. Both are informative and the design reports the split honestly.

Item 1's construction is the opening: two models, which generalises better, and the answer is unavailable without a quantity supplied at item 2 that almost nobody requests. Students discover they answered a question they could not have answered.

The competence risk is at step 5. Constructing data on which one's own assumption fails is genuinely hard, and the fallback — stating why the substrate cannot produce such data — is an acceptable and often correct answer.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five items with a hidden dependency. Item 2's disclosure is the moment the question becomes answerable, and the design records at phase 8 how many students asked for it unprompted at item 1 — which is the cohort's did-you-notice-the-missing-variable measure. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction**, administered here per instrumentation rule 8, and its prompt — *fine-tune a model on this dataset* — names a method and no parameters, which is what a competent engineer would write and is exactly the prompt under which the capacity decision is made twice and stated by neither party.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** The bridge closes a two-quarter correspondence. Asking which S021 hypothesis a model family asserts, and how to check it, delivers the day's frame as retrieval and discharges the deferred connection [WP-021](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-021.md) §6 named.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four capacity settings, five sizes each, then a data-scaling adjudicator, **executed on the group's own component rather than supplied as curves**. The component is chosen to be small enough to train in minutes precisely so that the curves are measurements and not illustrations, which is a different relationship to the evidence and is the whole reason for the change. Setting A is the payload for its negative inference; setting D is the transfer test. The closing task is deliberately about A rather than D, so that the day's stated lesson is the curve reading and the transfer test remains unannounced until phase 8. **A fifth case is demonstrated and not operated** — a reinforcement fine-tuning job's reward metrics, and the documented reading of their divergence as reward hacking requiring more grader engineering — which is asked as one question, *whose capacity was wrong*, and then closed. It carries no assessment weight and is marked as demonstrated on the seminar page, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Point 2 is the diagnostic. Point 3 reframes regularisation as a prior rather than a fix. Point 4 makes the S021 correspondence operational. Point 5 carries the scope statement. Point 7 names setting D after the fact. **Point 8 is the day's platform content** and states the three fine-tuning methods as three claims about the problem's answer structure, in the vendor's own selection language; §8 concedes what that correspondence is and is not worth. **Point 9 names three defaults**, of which `auto` hyperparameter resolution is the sharpest available anywhere in this module: the number of epochs, the batch size and the learning-rate multiplier set effective capacity more directly than anything else on the day, all three accept `auto`, and the service replaces `auto` with a value derived by heuristic from the student's own training data.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Eight steps inside the S042 protocol. Step 5 is the discriminator. Step 6 — the capacity you would choose at ten times the data — is the operational form of point 6 and is the step most students find genuinely new. **Step 8 is new**: name every parameter left on a default and state what each assumes. It is the objection-1 repair in artefact form — the objection said the account's confinement to a safe region was enforced by the specification and never communicated, and an element requiring the student to enumerate what they did not choose makes the boundary something they write rather than something the marking scheme knows.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A capacity correct when chosen and wrong later, then an agent failure. The instructor case is the S007 seam disclosure's structure in a new domain, which the design notes rather than flags to students, and its attention to *what signal would have shown it* connects to S018 and S068. **The agent case is a correct fine-tuning job that chose `supervised` for a task with many acceptable answers.** It is chosen because there is no defect at any step and because of how the hypothesis was expressed: as the value of a required field. A reviewer reads `"type": "supervised"` as configuration, not as a claim that the problem has finite correct answers, and **the artefact that would have caught it is step 2's one sentence** — which the agent was not asked for and does not volunteer.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Counter-dataset plus detection check. Combining S021's construction with S018's signal question is the module's characteristic pairing and by now requires no framing.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** The item 1 missing-variable rate, the flip-point predictions, **the setting D identification rate reported to the room as evidence about S042's transfer**, **the cohort's step 8 default counts shown unattributed**, and **item 5's sealed prediction opened**. The design's commitment to reporting its own instrument results in public, established at S001 and S006, applies here.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student with applied modelling experience will choose a reasonable capacity by intuition and produce a good model. Predicted failure: step 2's assumption statement will restate the capacity rather than what it asserts about the data — "a regularised linear model assumes linear structure" is a restatement, not an assertion about this substrate's patterns. Countermeasure: phase 7's partner must construct a *dataset* on which the assumption fails, which a restatement cannot support; a vacuous step 2 produces a phase 7 the partner cannot run.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** Step 5's construction is the hardest demand. Countermeasures: step 1 is worked; the curve procedure is supplied; and the substrate-reachability fallback is explicitly acceptable. Residual risk: their step 6 ten-times-data reasoning may be a guess, and the specification requires reasoning rather than a specific answer.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Settings A, B and C are read correctly by most groups once the curve shapes are explained — and they are not explained until phase 4, so the phase's difficulty is real. Setting A's negative inference is the harder half. Fallback if a group reads all four including D: ask what setting C's curve would look like if the regularisation strength itself had been tuned on validation across many values, which is S042's failure at a further remove and is genuinely subtle.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **the scope statement read as an invalidation**. Told that the classical account does not describe behaviour in some regimes and that this is unsettled, a student may conclude the day's content is obsolete and disengage. This is a specific hazard of teaching a contested account honestly.

Countermeasure: point 5's framing is that the decomposition is a *vocabulary for reading a curve you measured*, which is unaffected by what happens at capacities you did not measure — the diagnostic use is safe and the extrapolative use is not, and the distinction is operational rather than hedging. The instructor is briefed to make this distinction concrete rather than epistemic.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement — the module's is at S045.

One specification item in IS201. Per rule 1, the checked elements are: a structural hypothesis, a capacity choice with its assumption, a four-point learning curve, a binding-constraint statement with evidence, a failing dataset or reachability argument, a ten-times-data statement with reasoning, a licensing sentence with the uncalibrated clause where applicable, **a named list of every parameter left on a default with what each assumes**, the phase 7 outcome, **and the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement**. **Nothing else is assessed, including the model's performance.**

**A capacity choice with no stated assumption fails.**

**An unnamed default fails; a default named honestly does not.** The accepted honest form is *resolved by the service from my training data; I cannot inspect the heuristic*, and it passes. The requirement is to know what was delegated, not to have delegated nothing — because a rule demanding that every parameter be set by hand would produce worse models and teach a superstition rather than a discipline.

**The demonstrated reinforcement fine-tuning case is not assessed**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s provision for capabilities the programme shows rather than operates.

Programme instrumentation: the item 1 missing-variable request rate is the cohort's what-do-I-need-to-know baseline; **the setting D identification rate is the S042 transfer measurement** and is the module's most direct test of whether a taught discipline generalises to an unlabelled instance; the step 2 assumption quality, blind-rated, is compared against S021's hypothesis-statement quality for the same students; **the step 8 default counts are new and measure how much of the day's own subject the cohort delegated without noticing**, which is the quantity point 9 exists to make visible; **item 5's sealed prediction accuracy** feeds the running frontier map per instrumentation rule 9.

Dispositions: **integrity**, at step 4, where reporting that the model rather than the data is the binding constraint concedes that one's own choice is the limitation; and at step 8, where a short default list is more flattering and less likely to be true.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day teaches a contested account as settled and marks the contest in a scope statement students will not weight.**

At its strongest: the bias-variance framing is presented across points 1, 2, 3 and 6 as the day's operating apparatus, worked on four settings and required in the artefact. Point 5's scope statement — that the classical curve does not describe some observed regimes — is one paragraph in a fifty-minute consolidation. Students weight instruction by time and by whether it is assessed, and by both measures the classical account is the day's content and its limits are a footnote. A student will leave believing the curve is the truth, which the design knows it is not.

The rebuttal is that the day's *use* of the account is confined to the safe region and the specification enforces the confinement. Every assessed element concerns a curve the student measured, at capacities they explored, on data they have. Nothing in the artefact requires or rewards extrapolation, and step 6's ten-times-data question asks for reasoning rather than for a prediction the account cannot support.

The concession is that this confinement is a property of the specification and not a thing the day tells students. **A student cannot infer from the artefact's shape that the account's authority stops where their measurements stop.** The available repair was to add the boundary to the assessed elements, and **it is now made in a second register**: step 8 requires the student to name every parameter left on a default and state what each assumes, which forces an explicit statement about the range of choices they did not explore. That is not the same as stating what their curve does not license about capacities outside the measured range, and the design records the difference honestly — step 8 covers the parameters, and the extrapolation boundary remains taught at point 5 and unassessed. The remaining half of the repair would cost one line in step 6 and is not made, on the same resourcing ground recorded elsewhere in this module.

**Objection 2: the day runs a transfer test it has no plan for failing.**

At its strongest: setting D is planted to measure whether S042 transferred, and phase 8 reports the identification rate to the room. Suppose the rate is very low — most groups fail to notice that a capacity tuned on the test set is meaningless, one day after committing a protocol forbidding exactly that. The design has stated it will report this honestly and has specified nothing about what follows. No remediation is scheduled, S044 and S045 proceed unchanged, and the module's entrustment judgement two days later assumes a discipline the cohort has just demonstrated it does not carry into unlabelled situations.

This objection the design does not fully answer. Running the test is right; reporting it publicly is right; having no branch is not.

What exists is thin. S045's phase 1 asks what students are willing to claim about their model, which would surface a cohort whose protocol discipline is nominal — but it surfaces it *at* the entrustment occasion rather than before it.

**A low setting D rate should trigger something at S044**, which is the intervening day and has capacity: its phase 2 bridge could be replaced with a direct re-probe, or its threat-model work could be scoped to include the evaluation pipeline as an attack surface, which is legitimate content and would re-run the discipline in a third costume. Neither is specified.

This is the second time in this module that the design has built an instrument and not specified what its result changes — after [WP-041](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-041.md) §8's leak-class deferral with no verification. **The pattern is that this design measures more readily than it responds**, which is a better failing than the reverse and is still a failing, and it is now visible enough to name.

**Objection 3: the day's two strongest platform moments are a vocabulary correspondence and a demonstration, and both persuade more than they warrant.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn.

At its strongest, in two parts. **Point 8 treats a resemblance as a confirmation.** The day argues that a capacity choice is a structural hypothesis, and then produces a vendor whose method-selection guidance is phrased in exactly that grammar — supervised where there are finite ways to solve a problem, reinforcement where there are many, preference optimisation where what you hold is comparisons. The room reads that as the frame being independently confirmed by industry. It is not. **It is product guidance, written to help somebody pick a feature, and its resemblance to this programme's language is a resemblance of grammar and not of provenance.** The vendor did not arrive at S021's position; the vendor described three products in the natural terms for describing three products, and this design has read its own frame back out of the description. That is the failure mode [WP-016](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-016.md) §8 recorded in a different register and it is now the module's second instance.

**And the reinforcement case is shown, not run.** It carries the day's most striking single idea — that a criterion has a capacity, and that a model exploiting a weak grader is the grader's failure and not the model's — and it arrives as one graph, one documented sentence and one rhetorical question. **A demonstration does the persuasive work of an executed case without its evidential standing**, and this one is doing more of that work than any other content on the day because it is the only content that generalises upward into M10.

Both halves are conceded.

**Two cheap fixes are made.** Point 8 now states that the vendor's criteria are product guidance and that the correspondence is one of grammar rather than provenance, so the room is told what it is being shown. And the demonstrated case is explicitly marked as demonstrated rather than operated on the seminar page, is asked as a single question rather than worked, and **is not assessed** — which is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s provision applied honestly rather than as a formality.

**What remains unfixed is a forward commitment the design must make and has not.** The demonstrated case introduces M10's central subject — that a criterion is constructed, is fallible, and has a capacity of its own — five days before [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) opens it, and nothing at S046 requires the student to have seen it. Under instrumentation rule 6, an instrument whose response is deferred must name the occasion and that occasion must carry an artefact element consuming the result. **S046 must carry an element requiring the student to state a criterion of their own that would be cheap to satisfy for the wrong reason, and to name the reward-hacking case as its precedent.** That is a commitment this paper makes on S046's behalf and it is recorded here so that its absence is checkable rather than forgettable.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That a capacity choice is not an optimisation to be searched but a claim about the world: a heavily regularised model asserts the underlying pattern is simple, a flexible one asserts it is not and that you hold enough data to separate signal from noise at that flexibility. Both are checkable, both are usually made by default, and both fail without a visible symptom. The learning curve is the diagnostic, and a flat one says that collecting more data will not help.

**What the evidence licenses.** Capacity, regularisation and learning curves are **class 1**, grounded in [research/02-technical-foundations/](/research/02-technical-foundations/collected-materials.md), with the three technique families in [research/02-technical-foundations/ouyang-instructgpt.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/ouyang-instructgpt.md) and [research/02-technical-foundations/rafailov-dpo.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/rafailov-dpo.md). The service is **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026: hyperparameters left on `auto` are replaced by values derived from your own training data by heuristics **that are not published**. **The capacity-as-hypothesis frame itself is class 3.** That some regimes do not follow the classical curve is recorded as unsettled, so the vocabulary is safe for reading a curve you measured and unsafe for extrapolating past it.

**What to do with it.**

- Write one sentence saying what your capacity asserts about the data, then construct the data on which that assertion fails.
- Read the curve before you collect more data. Flat means the model binds, and the collection is wasted.
- **Provenance is not correctness**, per the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). A value derived from your own data by a heuristic you cannot inspect is still a delegated decision: name it, and say what you cannot say about it.
- List every parameter you left unset. A short list is more flattering and less likely to be true.

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes that its most persuasive platform moment is its least warranted. The vendor's method-selection language resembles this programme's hypothesis language, and the room reads that as independent confirmation. It is not. It is product guidance written to help somebody choose a feature, the resemblance is one of grammar rather than of provenance, and the design has read its own frame back out of a product description.

The hyperparameters at point 9 are from the same page and the fine-tuning API reference: `batch_size`, `learning_rate_multiplier` and `n_epochs` are common; `beta` is specific to preference optimisation; `eval_interval`, `eval_samples`, `compute_multiplier` and `reasoning_effort` are specific to reinforcement fine-tuning, with `reasoning_effort` defaulting to `medium`. **The reinforcement page states that the training service automatically replaces hyperparameters set to `auto` with defaults based on heuristics on the provided training data**, and that the system automatically creates an evaluation for each reinforcement job at the interval `eval_interval` defines.
