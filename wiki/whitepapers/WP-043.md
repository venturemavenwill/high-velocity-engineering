# WP-043 — Tests as the Specification

## In plain terms

The open playbook says two blunt things about tests. Code is **incomplete** if it is not accompanied by tests. Tests are committed in the **same change** as the code they cover, and *I will add tests next* is recorded on that page as not acceptable.

Both are ordinary hygiene. Neither is why the session exists.

**The session exists because of a single piece of practitioner testimony held in speaker notes in the internal corpus, contradicting that corpus's own headline.** On a mature production codebase, the assistant could not be asked to add a feature end to end — the generated code reused outdated patterns and ignored architectural constraints — and the workaround was to **make failing tests the specification and spend most of the human effort there.** The same collection's customer-facing material describes end-to-end feature delivery as the model's signature achievement.

**This paper teaches the contradiction and does not resolve it**, because the testimony is the better-evidenced half and neither half is evidence. The testimony names a codebase property, a failure mechanism and a workaround that costs something. The headline names none of those. **A learner who leaves able to say *this worked here and not there, and here is the property that distinguishes them* has the competence.**

The consequence the testimony implies and the headline conceals is the session's payload. **If the implementation gets cheap, the human effort moves into the specification. That is a redistribution, not a saving**, and nothing in the collection prices where it moved to.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching specification-as-executable-check on the basis of a single contradicted practitioner account rather than on the basis of the testing norms it superficially resembles. The argument is that the interesting claim is not that tests should exist but that a specification which only functions when a human reads it cannot support delegation; that the account of a mature codebase defeating end-to-end generation is the best-evidenced statement in its collection and is still one engineer's experience; and that the redistribution of human effort from implementation to specification is unpriced everywhere it is described. The session installs a failing specification, an untestable residue with named holders, a deliberately wrong passing implementation, and a delegation test conducted against a real executor. The paper concedes that the session's central source is a single unmeasured account, that the residue's boundary is a judgement rather than a criterion, and that the delegation test measures the executor at least as much as the specification.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Most engineers can say what they are building. Very few can say what *done* means for one behaviour in a form that something other than themselves could check.

The gap is invisible while the person who holds the intent is also the person writing the code. **It becomes the whole problem at the moment of delegation**, and delegation is what the rest of this stage is about.

There is a second problem which is newer and worse. When implementation was expensive, the specification could be thin and the expense of implementing was itself a forcing function: an engineer who had to write the code by hand encountered every ambiguity in the specification and resolved it, usually correctly, usually silently. **When implementation becomes cheap, the ambiguities are resolved anyway — by something that has no access to the intent and no obligation to report which resolutions it chose.**

The practitioner testimony names the observable consequence precisely: outdated patterns reused, architectural constraints ignored. Both are ambiguity resolutions made against the wrong prior, and both are invisible in an output that passes.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** Test-first working is widely practised and many learners will arrive with it. What they will not arrive with is the untestable residue, the deliberate wrong implementation, or the position that a specification must not depend on who reads it.

It assumes [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md)'s falsifiable requirement — this session is the same discipline in executable form — and [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md)'s verifiable non-functional requirements, which are where the residue mostly comes from. It assumes [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md)'s slice, because the specification is written for one behaviour inside it.

The prior the session works against is that tests are verification. **Here they are the specification**, and the difference is directional: verification asks whether the code is right, specification asks what right would be, and only the second can be handed to somebody who has not seen the code.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The module's highest intrinsic load. Four distinct artefacts — the failing checks, the residue, the wrong implementation, the delegation record — and the third is the one learners drop when time runs short.

**3.2 Why the contradiction is taught rather than resolved.** The two accounts in the corpus are not equally warranted and neither is warranted enough to adopt. **The testimony is more specific and more costly to its author**, which is the only quality signal available in a body of work with no citations, no measurements and no failures. Teaching the contradiction rather than the testimony preserves the learner's ability to notice which of the two their own experience resembles, which is the transferable act.

**3.3 Why a check that has never failed has specified nothing.** A check written after the implementation is shaped by it and inherits its assumptions. The failure is not that such checks are useless — they catch regressions — but that they are evidence about the code rather than a statement about the requirement.

**3.4 Why the wrong implementation is built.** The gap between *passes* and *correct* is not visible from a passing suite. It becomes visible when somebody tries to satisfy the suite badly. **This is the productive-failure structure the programme uses elsewhere as a teaching design, applied here as a professional technique** rather than as a pedagogy.

**3.5 Why the residue must have a holder rather than a policy.** A constraint that no check can express is going to be enforced by a person or not at all. Naming the person converts an aspiration into an assignment, and an unassigned residue entry is the mechanism by which architectural constraints get ignored — which is exactly what the testimony reports happening.

**3.6 Why no executor is fixed.** If the session named an agent in its header, a learner could satisfy it by writing something a particular model happened to follow that term. **The claim being taught is that the specification must not care who reads it**, and fixing the reader would make the claim untestable.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 4 is the instrument.** *What does a test suite that passes on wrong code look like?* Learners who can answer it concretely before instruction are rare, and the distribution of answers predicts the quality of the wrong-implementation exercise almost exactly.

**Item 5 is the paper's payload asked as a question.** *If the implementation becomes cheap, where does the human effort go?* The testimony's answer is *into the specification*, and the session's standard requires the learner to notice if their own specification took less effort than their implementation, which is the observable that says the redistribution did not happen.

**The delegation test permits a colleague from another team as a better instrument than an agent.** This is deliberate and it is the design's hedge against §8's second objection: a human executor from outside the codebase tests the specification's legibility without confounding it with a model's particular failure modes.

**The failure record is the assessed output of the delegation, not the working code.** What the executor got wrong is the specification's quality measurement, and it is the only measurement of specification quality available to a learner in a fortnight.

**The testimony is read against the learner's own result in the same movement.** If outdated patterns and ignored constraints appear, the residue was incomplete. If they do not, the learner must name the property of their codebase or specification that prevented it. **Both branches produce a finding**, which is why the movement can be run on a first offering with no cohort history.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The retrofitted test.** The likeliest failure and the one every source already warns about. *Countermeasure:* the requirement to watch the checks fail, and the standard's warning sign — you have never seen it red.

**The empty residue.** A learner declares that every constraint is checkable, because declaring it is cheaper than naming a holder. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires a non-empty residue with owners, and [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) collects the residue as the raw material for an instruction set, which makes an empty residue expensive three sessions later.

**The skipped wrong implementation.** The exercise looks like waste because the specification already passes. *Countermeasure:* the work movement names it as the session's most productive period and the one learners skip. **This is a warning rather than a mechanism and §8 concedes it is weak.**

**The shape check.** A check asserts the form of an output and is read as asserting its correctness. *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — your check would pass on a plausible-looking wrong answer — and [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md), where the same distinction is met again in a tool that declares it about itself.

**The effort that vanished.** Implementation got cheap, specification did not get richer, and nobody noticed. *Countermeasure:* the standard's comparison of specification effort against implementation effort, which is self-reported and therefore weak.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the session's central source is one anecdote, and a programme that spends a session on a single unmeasured account while refusing the corpus's other unmeasured accounts is applying its evidential standard selectively.**

At its strongest: this repository's standing discipline is that unwarranted claims are recorded as specimens and adopted nowhere. The testimony has no measurement, no baseline, no independent observation and no named instrument. It is one engineer's report, held in speaker notes. **The design has selected it because it agrees with the design's own scepticism**, which is precisely the failure the programme accuses its sources of — arranging the evidence so that a preferred conclusion is confirmed.

**Conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to this session.** The rebuttal narrows rather than defeats it. The testimony is not adopted as a finding: §3.2 states it is taught as a contradiction rather than as a conclusion, the session's own limits section calls it a hypothesis worth teaching, and the standard marks the learner's *finding* whichever way their delegation test comes out. **What the design does claim asymmetrically is a quality signal — specificity and cost to the author — and that signal is a heuristic, not a warrant.** The declined fix is to teach the testing norms alone and drop the testimony. It is declined because the norms without the testimony are hygiene a learner can get from any source, and the whole reason this session sits in a forward-deployed programme is the delegation problem the testimony describes. **The selectivity is real, it is disclosed here, and the design accepts it.**

**Objection 2: the delegation test measures the executor, not the specification.**

At its strongest: a learner whose specification is excellent and whose executor is poor records a long list of failures; a learner whose specification is thin and whose executor is strong records none. The instrument confounds two variables, and the session's own standard treats the failure record as *the specification's actual quality measurement*, which it is not.

**Substantially conceded.** Three partial mitigations. The session permits a human executor from another team, which changes the confound rather than removing it but makes it visible. The assessed output is the learner's *account* of what went wrong rather than the count of failures, so a learner with a strong executor who cannot explain why nothing went wrong has not passed. And [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) requires the pin — host, model, version — for exactly this reason, so that a result is attributed to a configuration rather than to the idea. **The declined fix is to standardise the executor across the cohort.** It is declined because a standard executor becomes the thing specifications are written for within one cohort, which reinstalls the failure §3.6 exists to prevent.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will produce an empty or one-line untestable residue on first attempt. *Instrument:* the residue's entry count and the presence of named holders, before and after the work movement.

**Prediction 2.** Wrong implementations that pass will be produced by a minority of learners, and the ones produced will reveal defects the learner then fixes. *Instrument:* the wrong implementation's presence, and the diff between the specification before and after it.

**Prediction 3.** Delegation failures will cluster on constraints the learner had classified as residue rather than on behaviours specified as checks. *Instrument:* the failure record coded against the specification's own two parts, by a second reader.

**Prediction 4.** Learners will report spending less effort on the specification than on the implementation, contradicting the testimony's account of where the effort goes. *Instrument:* self-reported effort split, collected at the session and again at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md). **The design expects this prediction to hold at this session and to weaken by [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)**, and if it does not weaken, the redistribution claim in §1 has no support at this scale.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a specification which only functions when a human reads it cannot support delegation; that a check which has never failed has specified nothing; and that constraints no check can express need a named holder rather than a policy.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about defect rates, delivery speed or software quality. The playbook's testing norms carry no comparative evidence and one of its pages reaches for a cost multiple through a citation that cannot be followed. **That figure is not repeated here.** The testimony is one account.

**The concession worth remembering.** This session selected a single unmeasured anecdote because it agreed with the design's scepticism, and says so in §8. If you leave able to notice that move being made by somebody else — including by this programme — you have the thing it was trying to teach.
