# WP-004 — Reading a Room You Were Invited Into

> |---|---|

## In plain terms

The customer chooses who comes. That choice is the most honest thing they will do all week, and it is made before you arrive.

A room of managers with no hands-on engineers cannot commit to anything that requires hands. A room of engineers with no manager cannot commit to anything that requires a budget. An invitation asking for a product overview is telling you the customer thinks you are a vendor. An agenda that was finalised before anybody spoke to you is telling you the session's conclusion has already been drafted.

None of that is hidden. It is written on the invitation, and the reason it goes unread is that reading it is uncomfortable and acting on it means asking to change arrangements somebody else has already made.

**The session's actual difficulty is not perception. It is that a signal noticed after the fact is free, and a signal committed to in advance is not.**

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching room-reading as an evidential exercise with a dated artefact rather than as a facilitation skill. The argument is that the observable properties of an invitation — attendance against decision rights, what was asked for, what was not requested — are available before any engagement begins and are systematically unused; and that the countermeasure is not sharper perception but a written pre-commitment, because the failure is one of willingness rather than of noticing. The paper concedes that its source material is an uninstrumented catalogue of practitioner warning signs, that no measurement of any kind supports it, and that the pre-commitment device is this design's own invention with no external warrant.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

An engineer arriving at a customer session is a guest. The arrangements were made by somebody else, the agenda reflects their understanding, and every social pressure in the room runs towards accepting the frame on offer.

The consequence is a specific and expensive failure: the engagement's shape is set by an invitation that nobody treated as a decision. **A session assembled to produce awareness will produce awareness**, and a team can spend a quarter delivering against an outcome that was foreclosed before anybody was in the room.

The corrective is available at no cost — the list, the agenda and the absence of a request for a working environment are all visible days in advance — and it is nonetheless routinely skipped, because acting on it requires asking a customer to change something they have already arranged.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Either.** Learners from consulting and delivery backgrounds usually arrive with a working version of this skill, held tacitly and applied inconsistently. Learners from product and platform backgrounds usually arrive with none, having never been the person in the room whose job is to notice.

The session equalises through the artefact rather than the discussion. Tacit skill does not produce a dated pre-commitment, so both populations are doing the same unfamiliar thing.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Intrinsic load is low. Nearly all of the difficulty is social, and the design spends its surplus on making a small uncomfortable act — writing down in advance what would make you change the plan — rehearsable at no cost.

**3.2 Why the probe is indirect.** A direct question about capability asks somebody to assess themselves in front of colleagues, in a setting where the answer carries status. Asking instead for a small real task changes what is being reported into what is being observed. The direction of the difference between self-report and observation is well established; **no magnitude is asserted here or anywhere in this wiki.**

**3.3 Why the commitment precedes the observation.** A judgement made after an outcome is known is a different instrument from one made before, and only the second can be wrong in a way that teaches anything. This is the same argument that places the opening items before instruction at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md), applied to observation rather than to knowledge.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The three parts of the artefact are ordered so that the hardest one comes last and cannot be rationalised into the earlier ones.

The attendance-against-decision-rights table is mechanical and almost everyone completes it. **The pre-commitment is the session.** It requires a learner to name, in advance and in writing, a thing they might see and what they will do about it — which converts an observation into an obligation, and is the only part of the exercise that costs anything.

The review movement hands the pre-commitment to somebody who has not read the brief. That reader is not assessing the judgement; they are assessing whether the trigger is externally recognisable. **A trigger that only its author can identify is a trigger that will be declined on the day it fires**, and identifying that here is the session's cheapest yield.

The failure modes are given as warning signs rather than definitions, because a definition is recognised afterwards and a warning sign is noticed during. The format is taken from the source corpus, which uses it well and whose catalogue of observable antecedents is the best-constructed thing in it.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The unfalsifiable trigger.** The most likely outcome is a pre-commitment naming a signal like "if the room seems disengaged", which no external reader could adjudicate. *Countermeasure:* the review movement's single binary question, which is designed to catch exactly this and catches little else.

**Reading the room as a personality exercise.** A learner produces a psychological account of the attendees rather than a structural one. *Countermeasure:* the decision-rights column, which does not accept a description of temperament as an entry.

**The unusable finding.** A learner correctly identifies that the room cannot commit to anything, and has no move available. *Countermeasure:* partial. [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md) supplies the ask that brings an absent party in. Between this session and that one, the learner has a diagnosis and no remedy, which is uncomfortable by design and may simply be a defect.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. One specification element at [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md): that the decision records what the room could and could not commit to, and that this is consistent with the attendance reading. **The pre-commitment itself is not assessed for accuracy** — a signal that never fired is not a wrong signal — only for whether it was written before the observation and is externally recognisable.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: this teaches suspicion of the customer, and a customer who senses it will stop being candid.**

At its strongest: the engagement runs on a relationship, the room is composed of people acting in good faith under constraints the engineer cannot see, and an engineer who arrives having pre-decided what the attendee list "reveals" will read hostility into ordinary scheduling. The reading is also unfalsifiable in the wrong direction: any composition can be interpreted as a signal by somebody looking for one.

**Conceded in part.** The design's answer is that the artefact records what the room can commit to, which is a capability claim rather than an attribution of motive, and that no part of the specification rewards a learner for finding something wrong. The declined fix was to make the session's output a question list for the customer rather than a private reading, which was rejected because it removes the pre-commitment and with it the only part of the session that costs anything. **That rejection may be wrong, and the objection is the strongest one against this session.**

**Objection 2: the source is a catalogue with no evidence behind it, presented as if experience were warrant.**

At its strongest: the warning signs are plausible, memorable, unmeasured, drawn from a body of work that records no failure and declines no engagement, and their plausibility is exactly what makes them dangerous to teach as though they predicted anything.

**Conceded without qualification.** The session presents them as observables to be checked rather than as predictors, and the limits section of [S004](/wiki/seminars/S004.md) states in its own words that hard-won operational knowledge and evidence are different things.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most decision-rights columns will, before instruction, contain restatements of job titles rather than statements of what a person can settle alone. *Instrument:* the written item 1 answers, coded *title* / *right* / *mixed* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Pre-commitments written before the review movement will more often be judged externally unrecognisable than those revised after it. *Instrument:* the review movement's binary judgement, collected before and after revision.

**Prediction 3.** Learners who design a task-based probe will identify more absent decision rights than those who design a question-based one. *Instrument:* probe type against the count of stop-capable attendees identified, per learner. **If this fails, the indirect-probe requirement is decorative** and should become a recommendation rather than a standard.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the composition of a room is evidence available before the engagement starts, and that the barrier to using it is willingness rather than perception.

**What the evidence licenses.** The observation that self-report and observation differ is class 2 — direction and mechanism, no magnitude. The catalogue of warning signs is a practitioner artefact recorded from a complete reading and is evidence that experienced people believe them. That any of this improves an engagement is class 3.

**The concession worth remembering.** A reading of a room can be made to confirm whatever the reader arrived with. Nothing in this session prevents that, and the pre-commitment only limits when the reading may be revised.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That the source corpus names its facilitation antipatterns with causes, observable warning signs and mitigations — including an agenda finalised before the customer is understood, a room of managers with few hands-on engineers, no sandbox requested, and labs run on sample data rather than the customer's own — is recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md), which rests on a complete reading. So are the judgements that sessions with only leaders produce plans nobody executes, that sessions with only practitioners lack the authority to change anything, and that a stakeholder's AI literacy should be assessed indirectly. That same note records that one of the corpus's own antipatterns is contradicted by its own workshop recipe, and that no engagement, failure or outcome is measured anywhere in it.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That self-assessment of capability in front of colleagues differs systematically from observed performance; that a prediction made before an outcome is a different instrument from a judgement made after it. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The dated pre-commitment device; the requirement that a trigger be externally recognisable; the decision-rights column as the corrective to reading seniority; the claim that the barrier here is willingness rather than perception; the entire existence of Stage 1; and all three predictions in §9.
