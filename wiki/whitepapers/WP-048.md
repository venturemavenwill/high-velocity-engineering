## In plain terms

The internal corpus behind this stage contains one sentence about generated code that is sharper than anything in its own promotional material.

**Generated code accumulates comprehension debt: it exists, it passes, and nobody understands it.**

Every clause is load-bearing. *It exists*, so it cannot be ignored. *It passes*, so nothing will flag it. *Nobody understands it*, so the first person who needs to change it starts from zero — and that person usually has an incident open.

The session makes two additions to the sentence, and the second is the one that decides the anchor.

**The debt is not contracted at the moment of generation. It is contracted at the moment of acceptance**, and acceptance in current tooling is a small, frequent, dismissible act. The vendor documentation this programme anchors on records the mechanism plainly: agent mode determines its own steps and iterates rather than stopping after one response, and it requests confirmation before running a terminal command or a tool that is not built in — with that confirmation being **a dismissible default rather than a boundary**, auto-confirmable per session, per solution, or permanently. This repository's reading was made on 2026-07-30 and **has not been repeated for this paper.** **Permanently is the word to sit with**, and the vendor's own quickstart material instructs the reader to select the autopilot option, which removes approvals entirely.

**And the debt is discovered late by construction, so the design's job is to move the discovery earlier.** It produces no failing test, no incident and no review comment. It appears the first time somebody must change the thing or defend it — the moment at which finding out is most expensive. The instrument that moves the discovery forward is not a code reading, because a code reading is exactly the activity a learner has already convinced themselves they have done. **It is an evaluation: write the check that would fail if this region's behaviour were wrong, and notice which regions you cannot write one for.**

That is why the anchor is an evaluation framework. **You cannot write an evaluator for behaviour you cannot explain**, so the attempt converts an internal state into an artefact, this afternoon rather than at the incident.

Two properties of the audit are deliberate and unusual.

**Three classes, not two.** Between *I understand this* and *I do not* sits the large and under-named class of code whose effect you know and whose form you cannot account for. **That class is where most of the debt actually is**, and a two-class audit sorts it into the comfortable side.

**Hand-written code is not exempt.** Comprehension debt predates generated code — it is what a rushed engineer leaves under any tooling — and a learner who classifies it as a machine problem has misdiagnosed the mechanism and will not recognise it in their own work.

## Abstract

This paper defends a session built on a single sentence from an unmeasured corpus, on the grounds that the sentence names a mechanism the vendor documentation independently confirms the existence of and publishes no measurement for. The argument has four parts. That comprehension debt is contracted at acceptance rather than at generation, which locates it in a user-interface act rather than in a technology. That a passing check is verification and not comprehension, and conflating them is the debt's main concealment. That an account written by the process being accounted for is another output requiring the same scrutiny. And that the debt's defining property is late discovery, so the design's task is to manufacture an earlier signal — which the professional-formation literature supports at the level of mechanism, since the domains where practice volume converts least into performance are precisely those with delayed, ambiguous feedback, and professional work is the least favourable of them. The session therefore installs a three-class comprehension audit with a stated remediation price per region, a confirmation record naming standing permissions, and one evaluator per region the learner classifies as explained, with its split strategy declared and its thresholds justified. The paper concedes that the boundary between two of its three classes is a judgement rather than a criterion, that the audit is self-reported throughout, that the evaluator requirement establishes a lower bound on ignorance and not an upper bound on comprehension, and that nothing establishes how fast the debt accrues or what it costs.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

An increment passes its checks, satisfies its specification and was reviewed. Somewhere inside it is a region nobody on the team could change safely, and **nothing in the delivery process will report that**, because every instrument in the process measures whether the code is right rather than whether anybody knows why.

The problem has a shape that makes it hard to notice from inside.

**It is asymptomatic.** There is no failing test, no incident, no defect. The code works. The cost is borne entirely in the future, by whoever next needs to change it, and it is borne under time pressure because the occasions to change code you did not write are usually urgent.

**It is contracted in small, reasonable acts.** Nobody accepts a large region they do not understand. They accept a change, then another, and at some point they stop reading the confirmations because the confirmations have been right every time. **The vendor's own documentation offers *permanently* as an option** — reasonably, since the alternative is an unusable stream of prompts — and it is the correct engineering trade at the level of the tool and a cumulative liability at the level of the team.

**It is invisible to the instrument that would seem to catch it.** A test passing over a region says the region behaves. It says nothing about whether anybody could change it, and this session's hardest instructional move is separating those two things in learners who have spent a stage being told that tests are the specification.

And it is invisible to self-report, which is where the design's real difficulty lies. **Asking a learner whether they understand their increment is asking a question the answer to which is generated by the same faculty that would have to notice the gap.** §3.7 gives the reason this is not a rhetorical worry: in the closest available study, practitioners were wrong about their own throughput in a consistent direction, and doing the work did not correct the error.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** Every professional has inherited code nobody understands. What is new is the classification, the price, the confirmation record, and the requirement to write a check for what you claim to know.

It assumes [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md), which is what makes the *passing is not comprehension* distinction land — a learner who has just spent a session making tests the specification has the strongest possible prior in the wrong direction, and the session is placed to break it while it is fresh. It assumes [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md)'s review record, because what a review did not read is the first place unexplained code lives. It assumes [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md)'s instruction set, because a constraint that was not received is a common cause of a region nobody can account for.

The prior the session works against is that understanding your own increment is a given. **It is not, and item 1 demonstrates that in under a minute.**

**This is a class 3 session:** nobody supplies the regions, nobody supplies the classification boundary, and nobody says which behaviours are worth a check. The learner decides all three, and deciding that a region cannot carry an evaluator is a correct answer — it is, in fact, the session's most valuable one.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The module's affective peak. The intrinsic load is trivial; the difficulty is pointing at working, passing code of your own in front of people and saying *I cannot explain this*. **That is harder than admitting a failure**, because there is no visible problem to justify the admission.

**3.2 Why acceptance rather than generation is the moment.** Generated code that is rejected creates no debt. The liability begins when it enters the artefact, and entering is an act performed by a person, or by a standing permission that person granted earlier. **Locating the moment in an act rather than in a technology is what makes the problem addressable** — the technology is not going to change, and the act can.

**3.3 Why three classes.** A binary audit forces a judgement at a boundary most code does not sit near. The middle class — effect known, form unaccounted for — is where the majority of a working increment lives, and it is also where the debt is cheapest to service, because the reader already has the effect and needs only the reason.

**3.4 Why the debt must have a stated price.** A liability with no number never appears in a plan. The session does not require the debt to be paid; it requires one region to be priced, so that the learner discovers the price exists. **This is [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md)'s cost-ledger discipline applied to understanding rather than to evidence.**

**3.5 Why an explanation from the executor is not comprehension.** An account produced by the process being accounted for is another output of that process, subject to the same failure modes and the same unknown reliability. **The vendor publishes no measure of how often the agent reports completion on work that is wrong**, so the reliability of its self-description is unknown to the reader and, so far as this repository can establish, unpublished by the vendor.

**3.6 Why hand-written code is audited too.** The mechanism is time pressure and absent context, not generation. **Exempting hand-written code would teach the wrong causal story** and would let learners locate the problem entirely outside themselves.

**3.7 Why self-report is not an instrument here, and why an external structure is required.**

Three findings, in ascending order of how much they constrain the design.

Capability with these systems is **jagged** — the boundary between what they handle well and badly is uneven and does not track how hard a task feels to a person, and outside that boundary the failure mode is a persuasive wrong answer in the same register as the correct ones rather than an error ([research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md](/research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md)). **That note was written from an abstract; the shape of the finding is usable and no number in it is.** The consequence for an audit is that the regions most likely to be misclassified as *explained* are the ones where the output was most fluent.

In the closest available study of practitioners, experienced maintainers working in codebases they knew well were **slowed while believing they had been sped up**, and **the belief did not correct after they had done the work** ([research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md](/research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md)). **Its limits are severe and travel with it:** a very small sample, a narrow and adversarial population, self-reported time, and — decisively — the authors themselves now describe the findings as out of date and have redesigned the study because participants will no longer reliably work without the tooling. **Its follow-up results point the other way and its authors judge them unreliable. Neither set of figures is reproduced here or anywhere in this wiki.** What survives is the direction, and it is the direction that matters: perceived and actual effect diverged, and direct experience did not close the gap.

And the deepest constraint comes from the practice literature. The domain-spanning meta-analysis of deliberate practice found that how much practice volume explains about performance differs systematically **by domain**, explaining most in fields with stable rules, immediate unambiguous feedback and clear criteria, and **least in education and professional work** ([research/04-professional-formation/macnamara-deliberate-practice-limits.md](/research/04-professional-formation/macnamara-deliberate-practice-limits.md)). Professional performance is the domain it explains least, and it is the domain this programme prepares people for.

**The design's reading is not that practice does not work. It is that the structure of the domain decides whether practice converts into performance** — and the structural account of the ordering, which the note marks as interpretive rather than the authors' claim, is feedback latency, feedback ambiguity, rule stability and criterion clarity. Software work with a generative executor is unfavourable on all four. **So the curriculum's job is to manufacture the missing structure**, and an evaluation harness is exactly that manufacture: it converts a delayed, ambiguous, contested signal about whether you understood something into a fast, unambiguous one available today. **That is the whole argument for the anchor**, and it is the argument this session would still need if the platform disappeared.

**The note's own limits are heavy and must travel with it.** Its figures are variance explained from correlational studies, frequently using retrospective self-reported practice hours subject to recall bias; **range restriction is the strongest technical objection and is not fully resolvable within the design**, so the percentages are plausible lower bounds within elite samples; the professions estimate rests on the domain with the least standardised performance measurement and is the least robust of its results; the literature is actively contested; and a corrigendum exists which changed nothing substantive and is cited anyway. **No figure from it appears here.**

**3.8 Why an evaluation is the instrument, and what it can and cannot establish.**

The framework's programming model is small enough to teach in a session and structured enough to carry the discipline ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). Three types — an item wrapping a conversation and deriving a query and response via a split strategy, an evaluator that scores items, and an aggregate. Two tiers — a local evaluator running "checks locally without API calls — ideal for inner-loop development, CI smoke tests, and fast iteration", and a cloud judged tier — **mixable in a single run**, so the architecture itself encodes the cost gradient the evaluation literature argues for.

The idea the session actually depends on is the **conversation split strategy**, and the documentation states its significance in one line: "How you split determines *what you're evaluating*." Last turn, full and per-turn answer different questions about the same interaction, and **an agent that recovers well from a bad first attempt scores well under one and badly under another. Neither is wrong.** The transferable lesson is that **the unit of analysis is a choice which silently determines what a number means**, and it is the compact, unforgettable demonstration this session was arranged around.

The tool-usage catalogue supplies the vocabulary for the debt itself, decomposing failure into selection, input accuracy, call success, output utilisation and an overall accuracy. **Two of those — the right tool with wrong arguments, and a successful result ignored — produce a system that reports success and is wrong**, which is the failure class an opaque region conceals and the reason the session insists on behaviour claims rather than structural ones. Three further details are adopted directly: **repetitions exist "to detect non-deterministic behavior"**, so one sample is not a measurement; **stored responses can be evaluated "from logs or previous runs… without re-running the agent"**, so what actually happened is evaluable rather than only what you thought to ask; and **the threshold converting a score into pass or fail is a policy choice separable from the measurement**, so an inherited default is somebody else's policy applied to your situation.

**What this establishes is narrower than it looks, and the session says so.** Being unable to write a check is evidence of not understanding. **Being able to write one is not evidence of understanding**, because a narrow check can be authored by somebody who has grasped one behaviour and nothing around it. **The audit records a lower bound on ignorance and not an upper bound on comprehension.**

**And the source's silence is the most consequential fact about it. No agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold.** Most of these evaluators are model-judged. **No agreement figure is asserted anywhere in this paper or in the session.** The pages were read through search-returned excerpts rather than full fetches; most agent evaluators are marked preview; one consulted page is an explicitly superseded variant; and the surfaces differ across languages.

**3.9 Why the classes will move, and why that is the finding rather than a defect.**

The session will run into a documented circularity, and it is better met named. **Criteria drift** is the finding that "users need criteria to grade outputs, but grading outputs helps users define criteria", with the sharper corollary that some criteria are **dependent on the specific outputs observed**, which "raises serious questions for approaches that assume the independence of evaluation from observation of model outputs" ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md)). The same work names the trap the session's step 8 walks into deliberately: using a model to grade a model **relocates** the trust problem rather than solving it, because a generated evaluator inherits the failure modes of the thing it evaluates. **Its evidence is qualitative and from a small interface study; it establishes that the phenomenon is real and consequential and does not quantify its prevalence**, and it does not resolve the held-out problem it creates.

The practitioner ladder supplies the operational form. Teams need three capabilities — evaluate, debug by inspecting data, and change behaviour — and **most have only the third**, which is why they can change a system and cannot tell whether the change helped ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md)). Its decomposition into **features and scenarios** is what makes check-writing tractable for a learner who has never done it, and its break with software convention is the line the session repeats: **"unlike traditional unit tests, you don't necessarily need a 100% pass rate. Your pass rate is a product decision."** It also warns that raw agreement is misleading under class imbalance and that precision and recall should be reported separately. **It is one practitioner's essay resting on one company's case study, with no control condition, no measured improvement and no released dataset; its tooling is dated and the pattern rather than the product is what transfers.**

And if a model is used as the judge, four documented biases arrive with it — **position, verbosity, self-enhancement and limited reasoning** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/zheng-mt-bench-chatbot-arena.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/zheng-mt-bench-chatbot-arena.md)). Two bear directly. **Self-enhancement** means the learner's judge and the learner's executor are, by default, the same family, so the instrument is not independent of the thing it measures. **Limited reasoning** means a judge cannot reliably grade a problem it cannot itself solve, which is exactly the situation on an opaque region. **That work predates current models; its taxonomy has held up better than its figures, none of which is reproduced here, and agreement with human preference is not correctness in any case.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 1 is the first instrument and it is performed aloud.** *Point at a line and say why it is that line and not another.* The design's expectation is that the honest answer for most working code is *because that is what came out and the tests went green*, and the discomfort of saying it is the session's measurement.

**Opening item 5 is the second instrument and it is the one the anchor implements.** *Write the check that would fail if this region's behaviour were wrong.* Item 1 produces discomfort; item 5 produces an artefact, and the artefact is what survives the session.

**The region for item 1 is selected at random, not by the learner.** A learner-selected region is a rehearsed one, and this is the same principle [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) uses at the assessment.

**4.1 Why the evaluator is required only for the *explained* class.** Requiring one everywhere would produce ceremony. Requiring one exactly where the learner has made the strongest claim puts the cost on the claim rather than on the work, and **the regions that fail the requirement are regions that were misclassified**, which is the audit's most valuable output. The movement of a region down a class is required to stay visible in the record, because a clean audit has concealed the only evidence the session generates.

**The split strategy is declared and justified rather than accepted.** This is the single most transferable idea in the platform material and it costs one line in a file. **A team that never chose is reporting an answer to a question it did not ask**, and the justification requirement makes the choice legible to a reader who was not there.

**The confirmation record is reconstructed from the configuration rather than from memory.** Learners assume they know what they have granted. **The verifiable part of the audit is the part they have not looked at**, and standing permissions granted before the module began are the commonest finding.

**One region is priced properly rather than all of them.** Pricing everything is a project; pricing one is a discovery.

**4.2 Why the review movement measures rather than asks.** A colleague changes an *operational* region in a small specified way, and what they have to read is the region's actual extent — nearly always larger than its boundary on screen. **That difference is the interest on the debt**, and it is the only quantity in the session that is observed rather than reported, which is why the design keeps it despite the coordination cost.

**4.3 Why the instrument is used to falsify rather than to extend.** Step 8 asks the agent to produce an input for which the learner's stated claim is false. **A generative instrument asked to extend an audit adds regions; asked to falsify one, it must locate a weakness**, and locating is the harder operation. The design also requires the learner to record that this instrument shares a model family with their executor and is therefore not independent — which is §3.9's self-enhancement caution arriving as a procedure rather than as a warning.

**4.4 Why the audit now reaches the scaffold, and why the scaffold is a different debt from the accepted suggestion.**

The session as originally designed audits regions the learner accepted, one at a time. **That leaves out the largest single acquisition most projects ever make**, which arrives before any of it: a generator writes an entire project in one command.

The design treats this as a distinct case rather than as more of the same, for two stated reasons. **The first is that there is no diff.** Accepting a suggestion inside an existing file shows the reader what changed; a scaffold arrives with nothing to compare it against, so the ordinary review reflex has nothing to grip and the whole project is *operational* on arrival and stays there because nothing ever prompts a reading. **The second is where the debt sits.** The vendor's scaffold produces five directories, **two of which hold infrastructure templates and identity configuration the developer did not write** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agents-toolkit-and-sdk-family.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agents-toolkit-and-sdk-family.md)) — the two areas a source-directory reader habitually skips and the two whose failures arrive as opaque platform error codes at an unhelpful hour.

**§4's addition is a prediction step rather than a repair step**, for the same reason §4.1 requires an evaluator only where the learner has made the strongest claim. The learner writes what they expect to break, removes a specific flag from the identity action, provisions, and compares the prediction against the error. **The exercise is the prediction. A prediction that was wrong locates the boundary of what the learner actually understood about a project they had been treating as theirs**, which is the audit's most valuable output and is unavailable from reading.

**A second finding is recorded because it generalises further than the diagnostics do.** None of the three failure diagnoses the exercise relies on appears on any published documentation page: **they ship inside an editor extension, versioned separately from the documentation and undiscoverable from it.** So the material most likely to let a practitioner understand their own generated project is the material least likely to be found by looking for it. **That is a fact about where knowledge now lives, and the design requires the learner to record that they relied on a hypothesis rather than a fact.**

**And the debt has no published maturity date**, which the session makes into its own exercise. There is **no compatibility matrix, no support window for a tooling version, and no statement of how long a generated project remains buildable**; the existence of an upgrade command is the only published acknowledgement that a project can fall out of step with its generator. **A liability with no stated maturity is not a smaller liability; it is one nobody will schedule**, and the correct answer to the exercise is that the learner cannot know and can say why.

**The limits are severe and the design assesses none of the syntax.** Six pages were fetched and read in full and roughly thirty more reached only through search excerpts, so no list is exhaustive; the three diagnostics are **field-derived, unverifiable and citable only by extension version**; every principal name in the area has changed at least once and one package twice; and the ergonomics vocabulary throughout is asserted with no measurement anywhere. **No claim about work arriving in one duration rather than another is made in this paper or in the session, and nothing in that material would support one.**

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The passing unknown.** The likeliest failure and the one [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) makes more likely. *Countermeasure:* the standard's separation of *verified* from *understood*, and the warning sign — your audit classes correlate with your test coverage.

**The restated implementation.** The evaluator describes what the code does rather than what it should do, so it cannot fail unless the code is deleted. *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that an evaluator state a behaviour under a condition, and exercise 1's model-answer shape. **This is the commonest defect in first attempts and the countermeasure is a criterion rather than a check.**

**The audit that finds nothing.** *Countermeasure:* the random region for item 1, performed aloud before the standard is given, so the learner meets their own boundary before they can set it — and the evaluator requirement, which cannot be satisfied by adjusting a standard.

**The inherited threshold.** *Countermeasure:* the requirement to justify a pass mark or delete the check, which converts a silent policy into a stated one.

**The explained-by-comment.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's rule that a comment generated alongside the code is not an explanation, and the warning sign — your explanation uses the comment's own vocabulary.

**The permanent confirmation.** *Countermeasure:* the reconstruction requirement, which is the audit's only mechanically verifiable element.

**The debt attributed to the machine.** *Countermeasure:* the requirement to audit hand-written regions in the same classes with the same evaluator requirement, and the warning sign — your own code is exempt.

**The debt declared paid by a test.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's test — could you change the region without running the check to find out what you broke? **This is a thought experiment rather than an observation and §8 concedes it is weak.**

Retrieved by [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md), [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md), [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) and [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md).

[S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) meets the same debt inside an assurance chain, where an unexplained region becomes an unsourced value; [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md) asks what somebody who is not the author inherits.

The session also serves [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) backwards, and this is the more important direction. **The untestable residue produced there is, in this session's vocabulary, the set of constraints for which no evaluator can be written** — and a learner who arrives here able to name that set has the strongest possible starting point for the audit.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. The audit is submitted at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md), where one property is assessed: that every region of the increment is classified and that the unexplained regions are named rather than absent.

**The proportion of opaque regions is not assessed**, at this session or at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md). A learner with many is not marked down and a learner with none is checked against the standard's warning sign rather than rewarded. **Whether the opaque regions were eventually explained is explicitly not marked**, because the assessed competence is knowing what you did not understand and deciding about it on purpose.

**The evaluators are not assessed for coverage or for passing.** What [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) can check is that the *explained* class carries them and that a declared split strategy and a justified threshold accompany any judged check — both of which are properties of the record rather than of the system.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the whole session rests on one sentence from a corpus this programme spends most of its time discrediting, and the sentence has no measurement behind it.**

At its strongest: the collection has no citations, no failures, no defect data and no post-deployment record. This paper says so in its own evidence section. Selecting one sentence from it because the sentence is quotable and agrees with the design's scepticism is the same selective adoption [WP-043](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-043.md) §8 already conceded, committed a second time, and a programme that does it twice has a pattern rather than an exception.

**Conceded, and the repetition is acknowledged rather than explained away.** Two things distinguish this instance and neither makes it clean. **The mechanism is independently attested by vendor documentation** — the confirmation model, its dismissibility and its standing options are documented product behaviour, not a claim from the corpus — so the session rests on a documented act plus a name for its consequence, rather than on the corpus alone. And the claim is not causal: it says a liability exists and is asymptomatic, not that it is large or that generation makes it worse. **The declined fix is to teach the audit without the corpus's phrase.** It is declined because the phrase is the best available name for the thing, and a session that described the mechanism without naming it would be harder to retrieve later, which is where the retrieval matters.

**Objection 2: the middle class is undefined, so the audit is not an instrument.**

At its strongest: *I can say what it does but not why it is written this way* is a self-report about an internal state, with no observable distinguishing it from either neighbour. Two auditors will classify the same region differently; the same auditor will classify it differently on different days; and a learner who wants a favourable audit can produce one by adjusting their standard for *why*. The paper's own §3.3 admits the boundary is where most code sits, which is precisely where an unreliable classifier does the most damage.

**Conceded in full, and this is the concession the addition of the evaluator requirement was made to reduce rather than to remove.** Three mitigations, none complete. The audit's assessed property at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) is **completeness of classification rather than the distribution across classes**, so an unreliable boundary between two classes does not change what is marked. The review movement supplies one observed quantity — the region's real size under a colleague's change — which is independent of the learner's self-report. And **the evaluator requirement converts the *explained* claim from a self-report into an artefact that can fail**, which is the only part of the classification that is checkable at all. **The declined fix is to define the middle class by an operational test, such as whether the learner can predict the effect of a stated modification.** It is declined because such a test measures the learner's fluency with one modification rather than their account of the region, and because the design would rather hold an admitted judgement than a criterion that looks rigorous and measures something else.

**Objection 3: the evaluator requirement teaches the wrong inference, and the design's own honesty about it makes things worse rather than better.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the design finds hardest. The session's claim is asymmetric: inability to write a check is evidence of not understanding, but ability to write one is not evidence of understanding. **Learners will not hold that asymmetry.** They will write a check, watch it pass, and file the region under *explained* — which is precisely the *passing is not comprehension* error the session exists to break, reintroduced by the session's own instrument and now wearing the session's authority. The paper's response is to state the asymmetry in the text, and a rule stated in text is the weakest countermeasure this repository recognises. Worse, the requirement adds a genuine cost: writing checks takes the time that would have been spent reading, and reading is the activity that actually produces the understanding the audit claims to measure. **The session may be trading comprehension for a proxy for comprehension, and calling the trade rigour.**

**Partly conceded, and the second half is the part this paper cannot answer.** The rebuttal to the first half is that the asymmetry has a procedural expression rather than only a textual one: step 8 requires the learner to have the instrument falsify each *explained* claim, and every region where it succeeds is a region that must move down. **That is a check on the check**, and it is why the step exists. The rebuttal to the second half is weaker. The design's position is that reading produces understanding unevenly and unobservably, that a learner who reads and concludes they have understood generates no evidence either way, and that the discipline of stating a falsifiable behaviour is itself a form of reading rather than a substitute for one. **That is an argument and not a finding, and no source in this repository tests it.**

**The declined fix is to drop the evaluator and assess a written explanation of each region instead** — prose, marked by a second reader. It is declined for three reasons and the third is the honest one. A written explanation is scored by a human judgement whose reliability the assessment literature this programme uses is unambiguous about, and this repository would then be running an instrument it teaches learners to distrust. It would move the session's cost from the platform to the assessors, and the module cannot afford that. **And a prose explanation cannot fail**, which is the property that makes an evaluator useful and an essay comfortable. The cost of the choice is exactly what the objection names, and prediction 5 is written to detect it.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Item 1, performed on a randomly selected region, will produce an answer the learner themselves classifies as inadequate in most cases. *Instrument:* the learner's own post-hoc classification of their item-1 answer, plus a second reader's.

**Prediction 2.** A majority of learners will be unable to write an evaluator for at least one region they initially classified as *explained*, and the classes will move. *Instrument:* the audit's class distribution before and after the evaluator movement, per learner, with the movements retained in the record.

**Prediction 3.** First audits will over-populate the *explained* class relative to what the review movement's colleague test supports. *Instrument:* the audit's class distribution against the colleague's reading extent for the tested region.

**Prediction 4.** Most learners will discover at least one standing permission they had forgotten granting. *Instrument:* the confirmation record, comparing recalled permissions with reconstructed ones.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of learners will treat a passing evaluator as confirmation of comprehension in the [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) defence, despite the session stating the asymmetry and step 8 exercising it. *Instrument:* the defence transcript, coded for whether a region's *explained* status is justified by the check passing or by an account of the region. **This predicts the failure of §8's third objection's countermeasure**, and it is the prediction this paper most expects to be confirmed.

**Prediction 6.** Most first evaluators will restate the implementation rather than state a behaviour, and will therefore be unfalsifiable by any input. *Instrument:* the evaluator specifications, coded *behavioural* / *structural* by a second reader.

**Prediction 7.** Almost no learner will justify a judged threshold, and most will retain an inherited default rather than deleting the check. *Instrument:* the specification files, counting thresholds with a non-empty rationale against those without.

**Prediction 8.** Opaque regions will correlate with the portions the [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) review record marked unread. *Instrument:* the audit's opaque regions against the review record's unread boundary, paired per learner. **If this fails, the claim in §2 that unread review is where unexplained code lives has no support at this scale**, and the retrieval link from [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) should be removed.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the debt is contracted at acceptance; that a passing check is verification and not comprehension; that an account written by the thing being accounted for is another output; and that the check you cannot write is a measurement of what you do not understand.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about how much the debt costs, how fast it accrues, or whether generated code accrues it faster. The corpus supplies a name and no measurement; the vendor supplies a mechanism and no consequence data; **the evaluation documentation publishes no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, so nothing licenses treating a judged score as correct.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The instrument this session adds records a lower bound on ignorance and not an upper bound on comprehension, and §8 predicts you will forget that within a fortnight. **Watch which of the two claims you make when somebody asks whether you understand your build.**

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The evaluation framework's documentation defines three core types — an item wrapping a conversation and deriving query and response via a split strategy, an evaluator that scores items, and an aggregate of results; **two evaluator tiers, one local and running "checks locally without API calls" and one cloud and judged, which may be mixed in a single run**; a default evaluator set of relevance, coherence and task adherence with tool call accuracy added automatically when tool definitions are present; **three conversation split strategies — last turn, full and per-turn — with the quoted statement that "How you split determines *what you're evaluating*"** and a custom splitter permitted; repetitions **"to detect non-deterministic behavior"**; evaluation of pre-existing responses **"from logs or previous runs… without re-running the agent"**; a four-category evaluator catalogue including a five-way decomposition of tool usage into selection, input accuracy, output utilisation, call success and overall accuracy; a pass or fail output carrying a `reason` alongside a numeric score for scaled evaluators; and a data mapping distinguishing structured output including tool calls from plain text. Recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md), **read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches, with the page structure reconstructed rather than read heading by heading.**

The same vendor's agent toolchain scaffolds a project holding **five directories** — editor debugging configuration, manifest templates, environment files, **infrastructure templates for provisioning cloud resources**, and the source — **two of which contain material the developer did not write and will be asked to own**; it exposes an **upgrade** command whose stated purpose is to bring a generated project into line with current tooling; and its supporting extension artefact records three diagnoses reachable only by reading the generated configuration: that an identity-creation action **requires a specific flag whose absence yields an authentication error code naming nothing about the flag**, that a **missing tenant identifier in a local configuration file causes the wrong token authority to be used** and produces an authorisation failure from the connector, and that a persistent authorisation failure surviving an authentication fix may mean **the development tunnel address has been blocked.** Recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/agents-toolkit-and-sdk-family.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agents-toolkit-and-sdk-family.md), read 2026-07-31, **six pages fetched and read in full with roughly thirty more reached only through search-returned excerpts, so no list is exhaustive.** **The three diagnoses appear on no published documentation page**: they ship inside an editor extension, versioned separately from the documentation, undiscoverable from it, with no public address and no currency guarantee, and are recorded in that note **as reported rather than verified — field-derived hypotheses a practitioner can test in ten minutes, cited by extension version and by nothing else.**

**The silences that matter.** **No agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold.** That is the single figure a reader most needs, most of these evaluators are model-judged, and the independent literature is unambiguous that judges carry measurable biases which the documentation does not discuss. **No agreement figure is asserted anywhere in this paper or in the session.** The documentation also **publishes nothing about how often an evaluator's verdict changes across repeated runs on the same item**, which is the property the repetition facility exists to expose and the one a learner would need in order to interpret a single result. Most agent evaluators are marked **preview**; one consulted page is an explicitly superseded **classic** variant; and the surfaces differ across languages, so examples are not interchangeable.

**The toolchain's silences are of a different kind and bear directly on the debt this session names.** **No compatibility matrix is published, no support window is stated for any tooling version, and nothing anywhere states how long a generated project remains buildable** — so the liability a scaffold creates has no published maturity date, and the existence of an upgrade command is the only acknowledgement that breakage occurs. **Nothing is published about what happens to a deployed artefact when the toolchain that built it is renamed or superseded again.** **And there is no measure, anywhere in that material, of how much the tooling reduces time to a first working agent** — no baseline, no comparison, no sample, no study; the benefit is asserted in adjectives, and **nothing in it supports any claim about work arriving in one duration rather than another.** Every principal name in the area has changed at least once and one package twice, so the material is cited by version and date or not at all.

The host is GitHub Copilot agent mode as recorded in [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md)'s reading of 2026-07-30. **That reading has not been repeated for this paper and is carried forward as read.** Agent mode determines its own steps and iterates rather than stopping after one response, and it requests confirmation before running a terminal command or a tool that is not built in — with the confirmation being **a dismissible default rather than a boundary, auto-confirmable per session, per solution or permanently**, and with the vendor's own quickstart instructing the reader to select the autopilot option, which removes approvals entirely. The agent-mode page carried two product version ranges simultaneously at that reading. **The documentation publishes no measure of how often the self-correction loop converges, and none of how often the agent reports completion on work that is wrong** — which is exactly the frequency this session's hazard depends on, and it is unknown to the reader and unpublished by the vendor at that reading. **Every observation a cohort makes here is a property of its own recorded pin and licenses nothing about another.**
