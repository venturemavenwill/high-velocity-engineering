# WP-047 — Plan Before Implementation

**Seminar day:** [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md) · **Module:** [M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) · **Quarter:** [Q5](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 3
**Platform anchor:** GitHub Copilot custom chat modes — separating a mode that may only plan from a mode that may edit, and making the boundary a configuration rather than a habit

## In plain terms

The corpus behind this stage states a prohibition as a headline takeaway, and it is the best single rule in approximately ninety documents.

**Use the tooling to make each step better, not to skip steps.**

The same collection's customer-facing material then tells its proudest story, in which a prototype is generated on the first day from a template with no discovery activity described at all. **Read against the collection's own method, the flagship story is step-skipping presented as acceleration. Neither document notices.**

This session puts them on the same page, and then asks the question neither document asks: **what would physically have stopped it?**

Because the prohibition is a sentence, and a sentence is an intention. An intention is held by the person it constrains, which means it holds exactly as long as its holder wants it to — and the days you would break it are the days it was written for. **A mode that cannot edit is a different kind of object from a mode you have decided not to let edit**, and the difference is invisible until the decision would have gone the other way. That is the session's durable claim, and its ordinary form is already familiar: a code freeze announced in a meeting is not a code freeze, and a locked branch is.

The pinned configuration corpus supplies the process half. It separates the work into gated phases — research, plan, implement, review, follow-up — with explicit paths **back** to earlier phases rather than only forward, and it holds each phase's record on disk under stable identifiers, declared authoritative over whatever the conversation remembers. The argument is provenance and restartability: an interrupted run resumes from files, and a decision made while planning can be found by identifier while implementing.

**That establishes that the record exists and is addressable. It establishes nothing about whether the decisions in it were correct**, and the corpus says so about itself. A plan whose main effect is to make the work feel accountable is worse than no plan, because it is accountability theatre with a filing system.

And there is a third thing, which decides what the word *plan* is allowed to mean. The position paper this session rests on holds that auto-regressive models cannot by themselves plan — and, in the clause that closes the popular escape route, cannot self-verify either, **because self-verification is itself a form of reasoning.** A mode named *plan* is therefore a generator of candidate structure that has been prevented from acting. **That is a genuinely useful thing to have and it is not what the word implies to a customer**, so naming it honestly is part of the artefact.

One thing in the corpus is handed over unresolved. It also says: given a fixed time budget, run more iterations rather than fewer and more refined ones. **More iterations of a step is making the step better; fewer, longer steps is what a careful engineer does when told not to skip anything.** The two rules pull against each other and the collection never notices. The learner is required to say which one their plan implements.

## Abstract

This paper defends a session on planning built around two contradictions — one in its source and one in the word itself — rather than around a planning method. The argument runs in four steps. That a restraint enforced by intention is not a boundary, because an intention is held by the party it constrains, so the operative question about any planning discipline is what would have stopped the learner on the day they wanted to proceed. That a durable record under stable identifiers is a materially different artefact from a plan held in a conversation, because it can answer a question asked from the other end, and that provenance and restartability are the whole of what such a record buys, correctness not being among them. That the source's prohibition on skipping steps is contradicted by that source's flagship anecdote, which makes the contradiction the durable teaching content and the prohibition alone mere advice. And that a mode named *plan* is a generator of candidate structure prevented from acting, since the planning literature holds that such systems can neither plan nor verify themselves, so the correct architecture separates the proposer from the checker. The session installs two modes differing in a permission list rather than a description, a boundary test, a durable plan with resolvable identifiers, condition-shaped blockers, an exercised return path, a completion-versus-acceptance line and a skipped-step register. The paper concedes that the register invites confession rather than analysis, that no source establishes any benefit from phase separation, that the repository holds no note characterising the host surface the anchor names, and that a configured boundary can be circumvented by anybody willing to switch modes.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Planning has always been the step most easily skipped, because its output is not the thing anybody wanted.

**Cheap generation makes the skip nearly free at the moment of skipping.** An engineer who can produce a working implementation in the time it would have taken to write the plan has an economic argument for starting, and the argument is correct in the moment and wrong across the engagement. The corpus's own prohibition exists because its authors watched this happen; the corpus's own flagship story exists because its marketers watched the same thing and called it success.

There is a second problem which is specific to delegated work. **The record of why something was done now lives in a conversation** — an exchange with an executor, in a window, in a session. It is not addressable, not durable, not reviewable and not transferable. An engineer asked in Stage 8 why a component is shaped the way it is has, in the ordinary case, nothing to point at.

And there is a third, which is the one this design is organised around and which the previous two conceal. **Every remedy proposed for the first two is a rule**, and rules of this kind are enforced by the diligence of the person they constrain. Write the plan first. Do not implement during research. Return to planning when the assumption breaks. Each is sincere, each is correct, and each is abandoned under exactly the conditions it was written for. **The question the session forces is not whether the learner believes the rule but whether anything in their environment implements it**, and for almost every learner the honest answer at the start of the session is *nothing*.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** Planning is universal and identifiers are not exotic. What is new is the boundary as a configuration, the return path as an exercised mechanism, the completion-versus-acceptance separation, and the requirement to register what the tooling let the learner skip.

It assumes [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md)'s decision record, which is what the plan's identifiers must be able to reach. It assumes [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md)'s discipline about findings of fact, which is what the research record has to distinguish from assumption. It assumes [S042](/wiki/seminars/S042.md)'s blocker vocabulary, because a blocker stated as a condition can be cleared and one stated as a feeling cannot.

The prior the session works against is that a plan is a forecast. **Here it is a record with an address**, and the assessed properties are addressability and survival rather than accuracy.

**This is a class 3 session:** nobody supplies the phases, nobody supplies the permission lists, and nobody says which of the source's two competing rules the plan should implement. The learner decides all three, and deciding that one of their intentions cannot be converted into a configuration is a correct answer provided they say why.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Procedural load is high and lands with [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md)'s. Intrinsic load is low. The affective load is small and concentrated in the skipped-step register.

**3.2 Why a configured boundary is a different object from an intended one.**

The distinction is not about strength of will and it is not a moral claim. It is about who holds the restraint. **An intention is held by the party it constrains**, so its enforcement is conditional on that party continuing to want it — and the moments when they stop wanting it are exactly the moments the restraint was written for. A permission absent from a list is held by something else, and its enforcement does not vary with the mood of the person it binds.

The pinned corpus is instructive here precisely because it is honest about which side of that line it sits on ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)). Its phase gates, its return paths, its untrusted-content boundary and its prohibition on an agent recording a human's approval are **declarations**. Its own transparency statement records that it ships no model and no safety classifier, that host behaviour varies, and that human oversight remains necessary. **A phase gate written into a workflow file is a request that the executor observe a gate.** That is worth having and it is not the same object as a capability the executor does not possess.

The generalisation the session teaches is deliberately mundane: a code freeze announced in a meeting is not a code freeze and a locked branch is. **The reason to teach it here rather than in a governance session is that the delegated setting makes the substitution unusually easy to miss**, because a well-behaved executor produces exactly the same observable behaviour as a constrained one, right up until it does not.

**3.3 Why the record must be durable rather than conversational.** A conversation is ordered by time and addressed by scrolling. A file is addressed by name. **The difference matters only when somebody asks a question from the other end**, which is why the review movement is a question asked by somebody else rather than an inspection by the author. The corpus's contribution is exactly this: distinct on-disk locations for research, plans, phase details, implementation evidence, critique and review, under identifiers stable across a run, declared authoritative over session memory.

**3.4 Why completion and acceptance are separated.** A phase that ran to its end has produced output; a phase that was accepted has produced agreement. **Conflating them is how a workflow completes without anybody having said anything**, which is the failure [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md)'s authority statement addresses from the configuration side and this session addresses from the process side. The corpus names the same distinction and the design adopts it unchanged.

**3.5 Why the return path is exercised rather than described.** A path nobody has taken is a diagram. Exercising it once establishes that the mechanism exists, that somebody is entitled to declare it, and — most usefully — what it costs, which is the number that determines whether it will ever be used again.

**3.6 Why the skipped-step register must be non-empty.** Every learner skips steps and a register with no entries reports a standard rather than a week. **The requirement converts a confession into an observation**, which is the only form in which the information is professionally usable.

**3.7 Why the two source rules are handed over rather than reconciled.** More iterations and fewer, longer steps are both defensible readings of *make each step better*. The collection holds both and reconciles neither. **A curriculum that picked one would be inventing a resolution and attributing it to a source that has none** — and the collection's limits make that especially unwise: it is access-controlled, it contains approximately thirty quantitative assertions of which exactly one names a measurement instrument, and no figure from it is reproduced anywhere in this repository ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)).

**3.8 What the word *plan* is permitted to mean, and why the generator may not check itself.**

This is the subsection that supplies the session's intellectual content, and it is grounded in the literature rather than in the design's own reasoning.

The position paper rejects both prevailing camps and states its negative claim without hedging: **auto-regressive language models cannot, by themselves, do planning** ([research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md)). It then closes the escape route most agent designs reach for: they likewise cannot perform **self-verification**, *because self-verification is after all a form of reasoning*. **If a system cannot reliably plan, the plan cannot be repaired by asking the same system to check it**, since checking belongs to the class of capability that was found wanting.

The constructive half is why the paper is useful rather than merely deflationary. Models should be treated as **universal approximate knowledge sources** — broad in coverage, unreliable in correctness, and correctly positioned as sources of candidate structure, plausible decompositions and relevant constraints. The proposed architecture pairs the generator with **external model-based verifiers in a bi-directional regime**: propose, critique against something sound, return the critique, revise. **Correctness comes from the verifier and flexibility comes from the generator, and neither is trusted with the other's job.**

Three design consequences follow, and the session implements all three. The plan-only mode is described honestly as a proposer rather than a planner. The plan is required to name, for each step needing verification, the external check that supplies it — which is the LLM-Modulo discipline reduced to a documentation requirement. And the boundary between proposing and acting is made a permission rather than an instruction, because an instruction to a generator not to act is itself a request the generator evaluates.

**The paper's limits are recorded and matter.** It is a position paper rather than an experimental report; its central claims are arguments and have been contested in the subsequent literature. It targets auto-regressive models as of early 2024, and models explicitly trained for reasoning postdate the argument, so the empirical picture should be re-checked rather than assumed static. Sound verification requires a formal domain model, which is not always available; the framework reduces the formalisation cost and does not remove it, and its verifier-construction step still depends on human review. **The design adopts the architectural discipline, which is correct whether or not model reasoning improves, and adopts none of the empirical position.**

**3.9 Where the human side of the same separation is already written down.** The openly published handbook draws the proposer-versus-decider line for people, and its wording is sharper than most internal standards manage: **"The goal of a spike should be fact-finding, not decision-making or recommendation"**, and, on feasibility spikes, that the code written for a spike is not going to serve as the code for the final solution ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md), pages read 2026-07-30). Its async design-review page supplies the stopping rule the session borrows: after two round trips of question and response, move to synchronous discussion. **The handbook offers no comparative evidence for any of this and does not claim to**, its pages contradict one another in at least a dozen places, and four of its assertions are unsupported magnitude claims that this repository records as specimens and reproduces nowhere.

**3.10 The vendor framing that is worth keeping, and the two things in it that are not.** The internal platform synthesis states the industry problem in one sentence — demonstration is relatively easy and production with sustained return is the difficulty — and supplies a genuine vendor-independent taxonomy of multi-agent control flow in its five orchestration patterns, of which a plan-then-implement separation is the simplest instance ([research/06-microsoft-platform/03-accelerate-agentic-ai.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/03-accelerate-agentic-ai.md)). **It is also the best available specimen of the failure this programme exists to prevent**, containing a precise unverifiable performance figure with no benchmark or interval, a maturity model with no causal evidence, and analyst projections presented as fact. **It is internal, not publicly accessible, not citable externally, the most perishable artefact in this repository, and none of its quantitative claims is repeated anywhere in this wiki.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 4 is the instrument and it is designed to come back empty.** *While you were planning yesterday, what physically prevented you from editing code?* The honest answer for almost every learner is *nothing*, and the discovery that the answer is *nothing* is what makes the rest of the session about configuration rather than about discipline.

**Opening item 5 is the second instrument.** *Which planning step did the tooling let you skip this week, and what did skipping cost?* The design's expectation is that most learners answer *none, because it did not cost anything*, and the standard requires that answer to be treated as a claim rather than as an absence — **a cost you did not observe is not a cost you did not pay.**

**The two modes are written as permission lists and the descriptions are treated as commentary.** This is the movement that carries §3.2, and it is deliberately physical: the artefact a reader inspects is a list of capabilities, not a paragraph of intent.

**4.1 Why the boundary is crossed on purpose, and why the middle outcome is the finding.** The learner asks the plan-only mode plainly to make the edit, and records which of three things happened: it refused and the refusal is attributable to the permission list; it refused and the refusal is not attributable; it complied. **The middle outcome is the commonest and the most instructive, because it is indistinguishable from the first while everything is going well.** A mode that refuses because it was asked nicely is a mode that will comply on the prompt nobody has thought of yet. The exercise's value is that it produces that distinction as an observation rather than as a warning.

**The prohibition and the anecdote are placed in the work movement, side by side.** The learner writes down which their week resembled. **Both answers are findings**, and a learner whose week resembled the anecdote has produced the more useful one provided they can name the step and the evidence that bears on it.

**The return path is exercised on a real task.** The learner declares a return condition met, goes back to planning, and records what changed.

**4.2 Why the review movement is a question asked from the other end.** Somebody else receives the identifiers and one question — *why is this like this?* — and finds the answer without the author. **The test is not whether the record exists but whether it answers**, and a record that requires its author to interpret it has failed a property that only becomes visible after the author leaves. This is also the only property of a plan that can be measured inside a fortnight.

**The corpus's own limits are read in the same movement as its mechanism.** The learner is shown that the phases exist, that the record is addressable, and that no result corpus is stored anywhere in a repository full of graders. **The pairing is the session's methodological payload** and it is more instructive than either half.

**4.3 What the design does about a hole it cannot fill.** This repository holds no research note covering the host's custom chat mode surface — no schema, no permission model, no defaults, no characterisation of what a mode is actually prevented from doing. The session therefore teaches the surface as *shape* and refuses it as *syntax*, labels its own fenced example as shape rather than schema, and requires every observation of that surface to be recorded with a pin. **Stating the absence is the correct response and it is not a satisfactory one**, and §9's prediction 6 exists so that the gap has an instrument attached to it rather than an apology.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The intended boundary.** The failure the session is named against: the plan mode's restraint lives in its prose and in the learner's habits. *Countermeasure:* opening item 4, the permission-list requirement, and the boundary test. **The countermeasure detects the condition; it does not prevent a learner from switching modes whenever they like, which §8 concedes.**

**The conversation plan.** *Countermeasure:* the survival test — close everything, reopen, continue from files — and the standard's warning sign, that you would have to scroll to answer item 2.

**The plan written afterwards.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — every phase completed as planned — the exercised return path, which produces a mid-plan revision a retrospective plan will not contain, and the instrument prompt that asks the agent to argue the plan was written after the work.

**Completion read as acceptance.** *Countermeasure:* the required separate lines with different signatories, and at least one acceptance the executor may not record, which connects to [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md)'s authority statement.

**The skipped plan.** Implementation started first and the plan was reconstructed. *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — your first artefact of the day is code — and the skipped-step register, which makes the skip reportable rather than hideable.

**The unused identifier.** *Countermeasure:* the review movement, which fails outright if a decision cannot be traced.

**The self-verifying plan.** The plan names the generator as the thing that will check the generator's proposal. *Countermeasure:* the plan-mode instruction requiring each step needing verification to name an external check, which is §3.8's discipline reduced to a field. **A field is a weak countermeasure and prediction 5 predicts it failing.**

**The plan as ritual.** Long, complete, disconnected. *Countermeasure:* none adequate. The standard's warning sign names it — nobody returned to it after the first hour — and **§8 concedes that a session teaching a planning apparatus is exposed to exactly this failure.**

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) and [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md).

The plan's identifiers are resolved live at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md), which is the only place the property is verified rather than claimed, and the mode configuration is one of the things a candidate must be able to say caused something. **[S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) uses the same record from the other direction**, because a system model built over what was shipped has to distinguish decisions that were made from decisions that were merely recorded.

The session also serves [S042](/wiki/seminars/S042.md) backwards: the rule that scope is not added to an existing backlog item is an intention, and this session supplies the question that tests it — what, in your environment, would have stopped you?

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. The plan and the mode configuration are submitted at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md), where two properties are assessed: that a decision recorded during planning can be located from the implementation record by its identifier, demonstrated live; and that a skipped step is named with the evidence that would show whether the skip cost anything.

**The plan's quality is not assessed.** Whether the phases were the right phases, whether the research was sufficient, and whether the return path was declared at the right moment are all unmarked, and no source supplies a basis for marking them.

**The boundary test's outcome is not assessed either, only its record.** A learner whose mode complied, who recorded the compliance with its pin and said what that does and does not establish, has met the standard exactly as well as one whose mode refused.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: this teaches a five-phase workflow from one vendor's repository and dresses it as a durable idea, and the phases are neither novel nor established.**

At its strongest: research, plan, implement, review is a restatement of every process model since the 1970s. The corpus's contribution is an acronym, a directory layout and an identifier convention, none of which is evidence of anything, and the corpus itself stores no result showing that any of it works. Spending a session in a delivery stage on a rebranded waterfall with return arrows is a poor trade.

**Substantially conceded on the novelty and rejected on the trade.** The paper does not claim the phases are new — §3.3 to §3.5 locate the contribution in **durable addressable state, the completion-acceptance separation and the exercised return path**, and the session's own text says the contribution is phase separation with return paths and a durable record rather than the acronym. What is genuinely new is the condition: an executor that holds context in a session rather than in a person, so that *where is the reasoning* has an answer that used to be assumed. **The declined fix is to teach planning generically without an anchor.** It is declined for [WP-046](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-046.md) §8's reason — the properties cannot be demonstrated without something that implements them — and because a generic planning session is the part of this material a learner can get anywhere.

**Objection 2: the skipped-step register invites confession rather than analysis, and will produce a page of small admissions that teaches nothing.**

At its strongest: the requirement that the register be non-empty guarantees entries, and the cheapest entries are trivial — did not read the whole document, did not write the research note before starting. A learner who produces those has satisfied the element and examined nothing. Worse, the register frames the tooling's effect as a moral question about the learner's diligence, when the interesting question is structural.

**Conceded, and the design's mitigation is one clause.** Each entry must name **what evidence would show whether the skip cost anything** — which converts *I did not do X* into *here is how I would find out whether X mattered*, and a trivial entry cannot satisfy the clause because nobody can name evidence bearing on whether skimming a document mattered. **The declined fix is to drop the register and assess the improved steps only.** It is declined because the prohibition this session is built on is about skipping, and an assessment that marks only improvements would reproduce the source's own asymmetry — the collection that states the prohibition also tells the story that violates it, and it never records a skip anywhere.

**Objection 3: a configured boundary is trivially circumvented, so the session's headline distinction is a distinction without a difference.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the design finds hardest. The learner holds both modes. Switching between them costs one action. **A boundary the constrained party can remove at will is an intention with extra steps**, and dressing it as an enforced control is worse than admitting it is a habit, because it manufactures confidence that has no basis. The genuine configured boundaries in an engineer's life — a locked branch, a permission they do not hold, an approval gate somebody else owns — all share the property that the constrained party cannot lift them, and the mode separation shares none of it. The session may therefore be teaching learners to mistake a convenience for a control, which is precisely the error [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) will spend a whole session unpicking about advisory checks.

**Conceded on the mechanism and defended on the pedagogy, and the concession is the larger half.** The rebuttal is that the switch is *legible*, and legibility is the property the design is actually buying. An intention abandoned leaves no trace; a mode switch is an act, at a moment, that the learner performs deliberately and can be asked about afterwards. **The claim the session is entitled to is that a configured boundary makes crossing it a decision rather than a drift**, and that is a smaller claim than "cannot be crossed". The session's own standard states it in that form and the perishable table does not overstate it.

**The declined fix is to anchor the session on a boundary the learner genuinely cannot lift — a branch protection rule, or an approval owned by somebody else.** It is declined on two grounds. Such boundaries exist already in the programme and are taught where they belong, at [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md) and in the governance stage; repeating them here would teach nothing new. And the specific failure this module exists to address is one where no external party is available: an engineer alone with an executor at eleven at night, whose only available restraints are ones they configured themselves. **A design that only taught boundaries somebody else holds would have nothing to say about the situation in which most of this work is actually done.** The cost of that choice is exactly what the objection names, and prediction 4 is written to detect it.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will answer opening item 4 with *nothing* or with a statement of intention, and will still be able to name at least one convertible restraint by the end of the session. *Instrument:* the item-4 answer coded *configured* / *intended* / *nothing*, against the exercise 3 rewrite, paired per learner.

**Prediction 2.** Most learners will answer opening item 5 with *none*, and will produce a non-empty register two movements later. *Instrument:* the item-5 answer against the register's entry count, paired per learner.

**Prediction 3.** The review movement's question will be unanswerable without the author for a majority of first plans. *Instrument:* the reviewer's outcome, coded *answered from the record* / *answered after asking the author* / *not answered*.

**Prediction 4.** Among learners whose boundary test produced a refusal, a majority will be unable to attribute the refusal to the permission list rather than to the mode's prose, and a majority of those will nonetheless describe their boundary as enforced. *Instrument:* the boundary-test record coded *attributable* / *not attributable* / *complied*, against the language used about the same boundary in the [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) defence. **This predicts the failure the third objection names**, and it is included because a distinction whose misapplication is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of plans will contain at least one step whose named verification is the generator itself, despite the mode instruction requiring an external check. *Instrument:* the plans, scanned for verification fields naming the model, the agent or *review the output*. **This predicts the failure of §5's weakest countermeasure.**

**Prediction 6.** Return paths will be exercised once, during the session, and not again during the module. *Instrument:* the tracking record across [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md) to [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md), counting declared returns per learner. **If this holds, the return path is a session exercise rather than a practice**, and [M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) should say so rather than implying otherwise.

**Prediction 7.** Learners will classify their own week as resembling the prohibition more often than their skipped-step registers support. *Instrument:* the self-classification against the register, by a second reader.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a boundary enforced by intention is not a boundary; that a plan you can re-enter is a different artefact from one you remember; that completion and acceptance are different results; that a durable record buys provenance and buys nothing about correctness; and that a generator may not be its own verifier.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The corpus has graders and no results and says so; the collection's prohibition is a practitioner judgement with no measurement and is contradicted by its own author's best story; the planning literature is a contested position paper scoped to models as of early 2024. **No magnitude from any of them appears here.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The mode boundary can be crossed by anybody who holds both modes, which is you. **What it buys is that crossing becomes an act you performed rather than a standard that quietly lapsed**, and that is the whole of the claim.
