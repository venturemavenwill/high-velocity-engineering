## In plain terms

The corpus behind this stage states a prohibition as a headline takeaway, and it is the best single rule in approximately ninety documents.

**Use the tooling to make each step better, not to skip steps.**

The same collection's customer-facing material then tells its proudest story, in which a prototype is generated on the first day from a template with no discovery activity described at all. **Read against the collection's own method, the flagship story is step-skipping presented as acceleration. Neither document notices.**

This session puts them on the same page and asks the learner which one their own week resembled.

The pinned repository supplies the mechanism the prohibition needs in order to be more than a slogan. It separates the work into gated phases — research, plan, implement, review, follow-up — with explicit paths **back** to earlier phases rather than only forward, and it holds each phase's record on disk under stable identifiers, declared authoritative over whatever the conversation remembers. The argument is provenance and restartability: an interrupted run resumes from files, and a decision made while planning can be found by identifier while implementing.

**That establishes that the record exists and is addressable. It establishes nothing about whether the decisions in it were correct**, and the repository says so about itself. A plan whose main effect is to make the work feel accountable is worse than no plan, because it is accountability theatre with a filing system.

There is one more thing in the corpus that the session has to hand over unresolved. It also says: given a fixed time budget, run more iterations rather than fewer and more refined ones. **More iterations of a step is making the step better; fewer, longer steps is what a careful engineer does when told not to skip anything.** The two rules pull against each other and the collection never notices. The learner is required to say which one their plan implements.

## Abstract

This paper defends a session on planning built around a contradiction in its own source rather than around a planning method. The argument is that a durable record under stable identifiers is a materially different artefact from a plan held in a conversation, because it can answer a question asked from the other end; that provenance and restartability are the whole of what such a record buys, and correctness is not among them; and that the source's prohibition on skipping steps is contradicted by that source's flagship anecdote, which makes the contradiction the durable teaching content and the prohibition alone mere advice. The session installs a durable plan with resolvable identifiers, a research record, condition-shaped blockers, an exercised return path, a completion-versus-acceptance line and a skipped-step register. The paper concedes that the register invites confession rather than analysis, that no source establishes any benefit from phase separation, and that the pinned repository is the clearest available example of a control system with graders and no results.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Planning has always been the step most easily skipped, because its output is not the thing anybody wanted.

**Cheap generation makes the skip nearly free at the moment of skipping.** An engineer who can produce a working implementation in the time it would have taken to write the plan has an economic argument for starting, and the argument is correct in the moment and wrong across the engagement. The corpus's own prohibition exists because its authors watched this happen; the corpus's own flagship story exists because its marketers watched the same thing and called it success.

There is a second problem which is specific to delegated work. **The record of why something was done now lives in a conversation** — an exchange with an executor, in a window, in a session. It is not addressable, not durable, not reviewable and not transferable. An engineer asked in Stage 8 why a component is shaped the way it is has, in the ordinary case, nothing to point at.

The pinned repository's answer is on-disk state under stable identifiers, declared authoritative over session memory. **That is an ordinary and good engineering answer**, and its limit is exactly what the repository says it is: the record proves what was decided and when, and nothing about whether it was right.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** Planning is universal and identifiers are not exotic. What is new is the return path as an exercised mechanism, the completion-versus-acceptance separation, and the requirement to register what the tooling let the learner skip.

It assumes [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md)'s decision record, which is what the plan's identifiers must be able to reach. It assumes [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md)'s discipline about findings of fact, which is what the research record has to distinguish from assumption. It assumes [S042](/wiki/seminars/S042.md)'s blocker vocabulary, because a blocker stated as a condition can be cleared and one stated as a feeling cannot.

The prior the session works against is that a plan is a forecast. **Here it is a record with an address**, and the assessed properties are addressability and survival rather than accuracy.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Procedural load is high and lands with [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md)'s. Intrinsic load is low. The affective load is small and concentrated in the skipped-step register.

**3.2 Why the record must be durable rather than conversational.** A conversation is ordered by time and addressed by scrolling. A file is addressed by name. **The difference matters only when somebody asks a question from the other end**, which is why the review movement is a question asked by somebody else rather than an inspection by the author.

**3.3 Why completion and acceptance are separated.** A phase that ran to its end has produced output; a phase that was accepted has produced agreement. **Conflating them is how a workflow completes without anybody having said anything**, which is the failure [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md)'s authority statement addresses from the configuration side and this session addresses from the process side.

**3.4 Why the return path is exercised rather than described.** A path nobody has taken is a diagram. Exercising it once establishes that the mechanism exists, that somebody is entitled to declare it, and — most usefully — what it costs, which is the number that determines whether it will ever be used again.

**3.5 Why the skipped-step register must be non-empty.** Every learner skips steps and a register with no entries reports a standard rather than a week. **The requirement converts a confession into an observation**, which is the only form in which the information is professionally usable.

**3.6 Why the two source rules are handed over rather than reconciled.** More iterations and fewer, longer steps are both defensible readings of *make each step better*. The collection holds both and reconciles neither. **A curriculum that picked one would be inventing a resolution and attributing it to a source that has none.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 5 is the instrument.** *Which planning step did the tooling let you skip this week, and what did skipping cost?* The design's expectation is that most learners answer *none, because it did not cost anything*, and the standard requires that answer to be treated as a claim rather than as an absence — **a cost you did not observe is not a cost you did not pay.**

**The prohibition and the anecdote are placed in the work movement, side by side.** The learner writes down which their week resembled. **Both answers are findings**, and a learner whose week resembled the anecdote has produced the more useful one provided they can name the step and the evidence that bears on it.

**The return path is exercised on a real task.** The learner declares a return condition met, goes back to planning, and records what changed.

**The review movement is a question asked from the other end.** Somebody else receives the identifiers and one question — *why is this like this?* — and finds the answer without the author. **The test is not whether the record exists but whether it answers**, and a record that requires its author to interpret it has failed a property that only becomes visible after the author leaves.

**The repository's own limits are read in the same movement as its mechanism.** The learner is shown that the phases exist, that the record is addressable, and that no result corpus is stored anywhere in a repository full of graders. **The pairing is the session's methodological payload** and it is more instructive than either half.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The conversation plan.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* the survival test — close everything, reopen, continue from files — and the standard's warning sign, that you would have to scroll to answer item 2.

**The plan written afterwards.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — every phase completed as planned — and the exercised return path, which produces a mid-plan revision that a retrospective plan will not contain.

**Completion read as acceptance.** *Countermeasure:* the required separate lines with different signatories, and at least one acceptance the executor may not record, which connects to [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md)'s authority statement.

**The skipped plan.** Implementation started first and the plan was reconstructed. *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — your first artefact of the day is code — and the skipped-step register, which makes the skip reportable rather than hideable.

**The unused identifier.** *Countermeasure:* the review movement, which fails outright if a decision cannot be traced.

**The plan as ritual.** Long, complete, disconnected. *Countermeasure:* none adequate. The standard's warning sign names it — nobody returned to it after the first hour — and **§8 concedes that a session teaching a planning apparatus is exposed to exactly this failure.**

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence. The plan is submitted at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md), where two properties are assessed: that a decision recorded during planning can be located from the implementation record by its identifier, demonstrated live; and that a skipped step is named with the evidence that would show whether the skip cost anything.

**The plan's quality is not assessed.** Whether the phases were the right phases, whether the research was sufficient, and whether the return path was declared at the right moment are all unmarked, and no source supplies a basis for marking them.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: this teaches a five-phase workflow from one vendor's repository and dresses it as a durable idea, and the phases are neither novel nor established.**

At its strongest: research, plan, implement, review is a restatement of every process model since the 1970s. The repository's contribution is an acronym, a directory layout and an identifier convention, none of which is evidence of anything, and the repository itself stores no result showing that any of it works. Spending a session in a delivery stage on a rebranded waterfall with return arrows is a poor trade.

**Substantially conceded on the novelty and rejected on the trade.** The paper does not claim the phases are new — §3.2 to §3.4 locate the contribution in **durable addressable state, the completion-acceptance separation and the exercised return path**, and the session's own text says the contribution is phase separation with return paths and a durable record rather than the acronym. What is genuinely new is the condition: an executor that holds context in a session rather than in a person, so that *where is the reasoning* has an answer that used to be assumed. **The declined fix is to teach planning generically without an anchor.** It is declined for [WP-046](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-046.md) §8's reason — the properties cannot be demonstrated without something that implements them — and because a generic planning session is the part of this material a learner can get anywhere.

**Objection 2: the skipped-step register invites confession rather than analysis, and will produce a page of small admissions that teaches nothing.**

At its strongest: the requirement that the register be non-empty guarantees entries, and the cheapest entries are trivial — did not read the whole document, did not write the research note before starting. A learner who produces those has satisfied the element and examined nothing. Worse, the register frames the tooling's effect as a moral question about the learner's diligence, when the interesting question is structural.

**Conceded, and the design's mitigation is one clause.** Each entry must name **what evidence would show whether the skip cost anything** — which converts *I did not do X* into *here is how I would find out whether X mattered*, and a trivial entry cannot satisfy the clause because nobody can name evidence bearing on whether skimming a document mattered. **The declined fix is to drop the register and assess the improved steps only.** It is declined because the prohibition this session is built on is about skipping, and an assessment that marks only improvements would reproduce the source's own asymmetry — the corpus that states the prohibition also tells the story that violates it, and it never records a skip anywhere.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will answer opening item 5 with *none*, and will produce a non-empty register two movements later. *Instrument:* the item-5 answer against the register's entry count, paired per learner.

**Prediction 2.** The review movement's question will be unanswerable without the author for a majority of first plans. *Instrument:* the reviewer's outcome, coded *answered from the record* / *answered after asking the author* / *not answered*.

**Prediction 3.** Return paths will be exercised once, during the session, and not again during the module. *Instrument:* the tracking record across [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md) to [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md), counting declared returns per learner. **If this holds, the return path is a session exercise rather than a practice**, and [M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) should say so rather than implying otherwise.

**Prediction 4.** Learners will classify their own week as resembling the prohibition more often than their skipped-step registers support. *Instrument:* the self-classification against the register, by a second reader.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a plan you can re-enter is a different artefact from one you remember; that completion and acceptance are different results; and that a durable record buys provenance and buys nothing about correctness.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. The repository has graders and no results and says so; the corpus's prohibition is a practitioner judgement with no measurement and is contradicted by its own author's best story.

**The concession worth remembering.** The phases are not new and the paper does not claim they are. What is new is that the reasoning now lives somewhere it can be lost, and a record with an address is the cheapest available response to that.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The agent-configuration repository was read at commit `cf29fb457b0fe62745bb71592ec9394b834957f4`, published package version 3.3.101. **It separates research, plan, implement, review and follow-up as gated phases with explicit return paths; it carries a stable task identity across a run; and it makes on-disk state under stable identifiers authoritative over session memory, with distinct locations for research, plans, phase details, implementation evidence, critique and review records.** Its own documentation states that this establishes provenance and restartability and **establishes nothing about whether the decisions recorded were correct**; that its behaviour checks test output shape rather than correctness; that behaviour conformance is advisory by default and lacks established branch calibration; and that a check can be advisory at one tier while the nightly run is authoritative. **It contains evaluation machinery and no committed result corpus — the storage location is an empty placeholder — and the revision read was not all-green: one job failed while most were skipped.** No benefit claim from its prose is adopted anywhere in this paper.

The host is GitHub Copilot agent mode as recorded in [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md)'s reading of 2026-07-30. **That reading has not been repeated for this paper and is carried forward as read**: agent mode determines its own steps and iterates rather than stopping after one response, and **the documentation publishes no measure of how often its self-correction loop converges**, which is the property a phase-gated workflow is implicitly relied on to compensate for.
