# WP-051 — The System Model Before the Threat

**Seminar day:** [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) · **Module:** [M11](/wiki/modules/M11.md) · **Quarter:** [Q6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME · **Complexity class:** 4
**Platform anchor:** Agentic threat modelling — components, flows and trust boundaries drawn and agreed before any threat is named

> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |

> | **Information diet** | Deliberately withholding context from an agent so its output is narrow enough to be joined |
> | **Productive Failure (PF)** | A two-phase, order-dependent design: learners generate solutions to a problem they have not been taught, fail, and are then taught |
> | **Configured control** | An instruction a model or a person may follow, partly follow, or silently not follow — as distinct from a check that can fail without their cooperation |

## In plain terms

This session installs an unpopular ordering: **the system model is finished before the first threat is written down.**

The reason is mechanical rather than moral. An assurance process is a chain, and everything after the first artefact joins onto it. If a flow is described in prose, two people will describe it differently and every later join will quietly match the wrong things. If a flow has an integer, the join is exact and the reasoning problem downstream collapses into set operations.

**That is the single most transferable idea in the material this module is built on: front-load the judgement, back-load the mechanics.** All the semantic difficulty is concentrated at the front, where a person is still looking at it.

Two devices carry the ordering and both are worth more than the security content around them.

**A folder used as a blackboard is a sufficient integration contract.** No orchestration framework is required, and the contract has a property a graph does not give you for free: a missing prerequisite genuinely fails, without any model's cooperation. In a stage whose whole subject is the difference between a configured control and a demonstrated one, that matters.

**And an information diet improves a subagent's output.** Handing an agent the numbered diagram and the closed list, and withholding everything else, is a design move rather than a limitation — which inverts the reflex a learner arrives with.

The session's own trap is the third artefact. The inferred-values side-channel is where every guess is supposed to be recorded, and it is the thing [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) shows being deleted downstream. This paper concedes in §8 that the side-channel is a configured control and nothing more — which is, word for word, what this module says a control is not.

## Abstract

This paper defends opening an assurance module with model construction rather than with threat enumeration, and defends the specific form the model takes.

**First**, the argument for the ordering. An assurance process is a chain; an error at the front of a chain is not a local error; and converting prose into stable identifiers at the first stage is what makes every later stage a join rather than an interpretation. The session installs a numbered dataflow diagram, an attribute table drawn from a closed list, a provenance side-channel, an open-questions list and an evidence-bearing characteristics checklist, together with a filesystem-mediated integration contract and a deliberate information diet for any agent used. Two independent literatures support the second-order claim that a static model decays: the production machine-learning literature names **entanglement** and **configuration sensitivity** as the mechanisms, and the second is sharper, because a trust boundary implemented in configuration is a trust boundary ordinary testing does not defend.

**Second**, the argument for the session's shape. The opening items are a Productive Failure generation phase, and item 1 is constructed so that failure is immediate, unambiguous and self-evident — which satisfies a condition the error-management training literature identifies as the boundary on that whole family of designs. Where task-generated feedback cannot be readily interpreted, minimally guided training may be no better than instruction and possibly worse. **This session's opening is the rare case where the feedback is exact: two prose namings of one flow either match or they do not, and the learner can see which within a minute.**

**Third**, the argument for the platform work. Inventory is a separate problem from analysis and is usually the missing one. The vendor's workload-posture surface exists because organisations cannot enumerate their own AI estate, and its **attack path analysis over "toxic combinations"** encodes a claim this session needs: a combination is a property of a model, not of a component, and cannot be seen in a list of parts.

The paper concedes three things. That the provenance side-channel is a configured control depending entirely on the author's cooperation, which is exactly what this module argues is not a control. That its principal design source is internal, access-controlled, unquotable and uncheckable by any reader, so the paper is asking for a kind of trust it cannot discharge. And that the ordering has no outcome evidence behind it in any source available to this programme — the observations that visibly shaped the source design were never recorded, so the strongest evidence that it works cannot be cited.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A learner asked for a threat model will begin listing threats, because threats are the recognisable part and because listing them feels like progress.

**The output of that instinct is a list of things true of software in general**, attached to a picture, with no way to check whether anything was missed and no way for an engineer to act on any single line. It is also unjoinable: a later stage that tries to match controls to those threats has to interpret prose on both sides, and interpretation at scale is where a chain goes wrong quietly.

There is a second problem which is harder to see. **The security attributes of a real system are mostly not written down anywhere**, so the person building the model will infer them — from convention, from what a competent team would have done, from the fact that a diagram shows a padlock. Those inferences are unavoidable and are not the problem. **The problem is that they are indistinguishable from observations by the time anybody reads the table.**

The session's response is to make the numbering non-negotiable and to give provenance a place to live that is not the cell.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session assumes a running increment from [Stage 5](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md), the verifiable non-functional requirements from [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md), and the register of what can and cannot move from [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md), because a trust zone is a constraint before it is a boundary.

It also assumes [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) and [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md). A learner who has not yet seen how much an executor's output depends on what it was given will not accept that withholding context improves it.

**The prior it works against is that a multi-agent system needs an orchestrator.** A cohort leaving [M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) has just spent a module on agents and will reach for a graph. The blackboard contract is introduced immediately and by contrast.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Low intrinsic load, high procedural discipline. The concepts are few and independent; the difficulty is in refusing to do the interesting part first. This is a design judgement and carries no external warrant.

**3.2 The opening items are a Productive Failure generation phase, and item 1 is built to be unachievable.**

Productive Failure is a two-phase, order-dependent design in which learners generate solutions to a problem targeting a concept they have not been taught, fail, and are then taught ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). The mechanism this session leans on is the **failed generation effect** — attempting an answer before instruction improves later learning *even when the attempt is wrong*.

Item 1 is the canonical shape. *Name a dataflow. Now name it again, in a way another person would name it identically.* The second clause cannot be satisfied in prose, and learners discover that inside a minute. The argument for numbering is then made experientially rather than asserted.

Two of Kapur's design conditions bind here and are conditions rather than preferences. **The generation phase must not be scaffolded** — which is why the release rule above gates §§4, 5 and 9, and why the session states that item 1 is the one that matters without saying why. And **the consolidation phase must explicitly prompt comparison**: the book reports Loibl and Leuders finding that presenting correct and incorrect solutions side by side achieves nothing on its own, and that only the condition told to compare them learned more. Exercise 3 is that prompt, and it is mandatory rather than encouraged for that reason.

**The source's limits are substantial and travel with every use of it in this programme.** It is a trade book. Its evidence base is school, university and laboratory study; **no adult-professional study appears in its reference list**, and several of the underlying studies — including Loibl and Leuders — are reported at second hand through the book rather than read directly. Applying it to working professionals is an extrapolation this programme makes deliberately and concedes at [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md) §8.

**3.3 Why this particular generation task is defensible where many are not — the feedback-clarity condition.**

The error-management training meta-analysis converges on the same ordering from an adult workplace-training literature, and it supplies something Kapur does not: a stated boundary on when minimally guided designs work ([research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md)).

The authors' third hypothesis is that because such training withholds guidance, **"the task itself needs to provide clearly interpretable and informative feedback"** for it to be effective. Their own examples of failure are a statistics package producing output a novice cannot read, and a database search returning records whose relevance the searcher cannot judge. On tasks like those, instruction that supplies external guidance may do better.

**This session's opening satisfies that condition unusually cleanly.** Two prose namings of one flow either match another person's or they do not, and the learner can check within a minute without a rubric, a tutor or a model answer. Compare this with the alternative opening the design rejected — *what threats apply to your system?* — whose feedback is entirely uninterpretable to a novice, because every answer looks equally plausible until somebody with expertise says otherwise.

**The source's limits must be stated because they are severe and easy to overlook.** Every study in its pool compared error management training against *another* training method; **none used a no-training control**, so all effects are relative rather than absolute. **No study in the pool measured on-the-job performance** — every outcome is a post-training task in a laboratory. And the authors themselves record the feedback-clarity hypothesis as receiving **"only limited support"**, with the moderator test falling short of significance and their own coding of feedback clarity showing low interrater reliability. **The direction is used here and no magnitude from it is asserted anywhere.**

**3.4 Why identifiers must come before attributes.** A join is exact when both sides carry the same key and interpretive when they do not. Numbering is the cheapest possible act that produces a key, and it is free only before anything references the thing being numbered. **The window closes the moment the first downstream artefact exists**, which is why the session spends its first movement on naming and numbering and nothing else. This is stated as mechanism and is not a finding.

**3.5 Why one flow is attributed completely before any other.** A single finished row exercises the closed list and reveals whether it is adequate. Partially attributed rows in parallel reveal nothing and are expensive to redo. This is the same ordering argument [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md) made for spikes, applied to a table, and it is design reasoning.

**3.6 Why hedges are banned inside cells, and why the ban requires a side-channel to be honest.**

The source design bans hedging language inside table cells and gives the reason plainly: a hedge inside a cell destroys machine consumption downstream ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md)). A value reading *probably TLS* cannot be joined, counted or checked, and will be read as *TLS* by the first process that touches it.

**Relocating uncertainty is the only move that preserves both the join and the honesty.** The cell carries the value; the side-channel carries the provenance. It is also, as §8 concedes, the move that makes the honesty deletable — and the module's central session is built on watching exactly that deletion happen.

**That source is internal and access-controlled.** It is paraphrased here as original prose, quoted nowhere, attributable to nothing, and it **supplies no external warrant for any claim in this paper.** It contains operational attack detail which is deliberately not reproduced anywhere in this repository. No calibration constant in it cites a study, a measurement, a pilot or a rationale, and no figure from it appears here.

**3.7 Why a model of a running system decays, and the two named mechanisms.**

A dataflow model is a static claim about a dynamic thing. The production machine-learning literature names the two properties that make the claim decay faster than a learner expects ([research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md)).

**Entanglement** — "changing one thing changes everything" — means a change made in one region reappears in behaviour somewhere unrelated, so a boundary drawn last month may no longer be where the system's actual boundary is. **Configuration sensitivity** is the sharper one: hyperparameters, versions and feature lists live in configuration, and the slightest error there "can cause radically different model behavior that won't be picked up with traditional software tests." **A trust boundary implemented in configuration is a trust boundary that ordinary testing does not defend**, which is a first-class finding about a model rather than a footnote to it.

The same source supplies the justification for building a model at all, imported from control theory: **"You can only control a system to the extent that you can observe it"**, operationalised as *alertability* — can a failure be made unambiguous — and *actionability* — is there enough signal to find the cause. Without a model, an alert names a symptom and nobody can say which part of the system produced it.

**Its limits are heavy.** Practitioner guidance from a commercial publisher: no study design, no comparison group, no measurement that its practices outperform anything. Three of its four authors worked at one large vendor whose toolchain is its default. Some foundational claims rest on **anonymous practitioner testimony** that cannot be checked. Every quantity in it is a property of one system after one training run; **mechanism and direction transfer, magnitudes do not, and none appears here.**

**3.8 Why a stage-wise map is the thing that converts a complaint into a defect.**

The retrieval-augmented generation failure taxonomy is built on the observation that without a stage-wise vocabulary, "every failure looks like the same undifferentiated problem of the answer being wrong" ([research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md)). Its diagnostic value is spatial: a defect is located at a stage before it is explained.

That is precisely what a numbered model buys for security work. *We might have an authorisation problem* is a mood. *Flow 7 carries classified content and its authorisation column is inferred* is a defect with an owner.

The same source makes a claim that shapes the whole module's honesty: **validation of such a system "is only feasible during operation"**, because the space of queries, documents and interactions cannot be adequately anticipated before deployment, and robustness therefore evolves rather than being designed in. A model built at [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) is a hypothesis about a system, not a description of it.

**Its limits:** an experience report over three systems in three domains, with **no frequency data, no severity data and no controlled comparison**. It says what can fail, not what usually fails. It predates widespread agentic retrieval and multi-hop patterns. **Its seven failure-point names are recorded in this repository as reconstructed rather than quoted**, and are not relied on here.

**3.9 Why inventory is a separate problem, and why composition is a property of the graph.**

The workload-posture documentation names its target artefact a **generative AI Bill of Materials** — "application components, data, and AI artifacts from code to cloud" — and the existence of that feature is itself evidence that organisations routinely cannot enumerate their own AI estate ([research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md)).

The same material names **attack path analysis**, whose subject is "toxic combinations": individually acceptable configurations that compose into a breach. **A combination is a property of a model and not of a component**, and this is the strongest available argument for why the model precedes the threat rather than following it. A list of parts cannot express a composition. A graph can.

The companion argument on the data side belongs here too, because it is the reason a model is urgent rather than merely advisable. The documentation's own reasoning is that generative AI "amplifies the problem of oversharing data" by surfacing content that is obsolete, over-permissioned or ungoverned. **Deploying a capable retrieval system converts every dormant permissions error in an organisation into a live one.** The permissions were always wrong; nobody was reading. [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) is where that becomes the module's centre.

**Its limits are stated flatly and matter more than usual.** First-party and definitional. **No detection rate, no false-positive rate and no coverage measurement is published, so none is asserted here or anywhere in this wiki.** One of the two pages behind the note is explicitly labelled a classic version superseded by a newer one that was not read; the other was read through search-returned excerpts rather than fetched in full, so its structure is reconstructed. Agent-level capability is mid-licence-migration. Its stated operational bounds — scan scope, cadence and result latency — are paraphrased qualitatively throughout the session and this paper, because a number lifted from a perishable product page is the least durable thing a learner could carry away.

**3.10 Why the information diet is a design move rather than a limitation.** More context is not more signal. A subagent given the compact projections and denied the originals produces a narrower output on a narrower question, which is what a join requires. **This is asserted as mechanism and direction only; no magnitude is claimed and none is available in any source read for this paper.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The movement sequence.** Situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**4.2 Opening item 1 is the instrument.** *Name a dataflow, then name it again the way another person would name it identically.* The second clause is impossible in prose and learners discover that inside a minute. §3.2 gives the warrant for putting it before instruction, and §3.3 gives the reason this particular impasse is defensible where most are not — its feedback is immediate and unambiguous, which is the condition the error-management literature identifies as the boundary of the whole family.

**Opening item 4 has one answer.** What happens to every later stage if the first artefact is wrong is that they are all wrong, silently, and this is why the session is first in the module. Items 2, 3 and 5 are not filler: they establish the contrast that makes item 1's failure legible to the learner who wrote it, in the same way [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s confident items make its empty one visible.

**4.3 The workflow is ordered so that reversal stays cheap.** Name and number; write the closed list; attribute one flow completely by hand; then use an agent under a diet; then cross-check the inventory; then attack. The instruction to re-read a file rather than trust a table already in context looks like superstition and is not: a model will confidently reuse stale context, and in a chained process the reuse is invisible. **That instruction exists verbatim in the source design, and nobody writes an instruction like that prospectively** — it is scar tissue from an observed failure whose record does not exist.

**4.4 Why the workflow's platform step is a comparison rather than a demonstration.** Step 7 has the learner enumerate their bill of materials by hand and only then let a scanner disagree with them. The ordering is deliberate and follows the source documentation's own teaching suggestion. **The instructive disagreement is not what the scanner found that the learner missed; it is what neither could see** — a control that exists only as a tenant setting, a review that exists only as somebody's habit. That population is exactly what [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md)'s binding rule will exclude, and the learner needs to have met it before the rule is imposed.

**4.5 The attack step is an attack on the model, not on the system.** The prompt asks the agent to relocate a trust boundary and name the flow number that proves the relocation. This is chosen over *review my model* on the same grounds [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) established: a model asked to improve something adds, and a model asked to attack something must locate a weakness. Locating is the harder operation and it is the one that produces specificity.

**4.6 The stranger review uses a stranger deliberately.** The author of a model cannot tell which of its cells are legible, and cannot tell which were inferred, because they remember. Handing it to somebody who did not build the system is the only cheap test of both. Their two questions — trace one flow end to end, and ask of any cell whether it was observed or inferred — are the session's only executable check on the provenance discipline, and §8 explains why that matters more than it should.

**4.7 Why failure modes are warning signs rather than definitions.** A definition is recognised after the fact; a warning sign is noticed during. The format is taken from the source practice corpus, which uses it well, and it is one of the few places where that corpus's form rather than its content is being borrowed.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The picture.** A diagram that communicates to a human and joins to nothing. *Countermeasure:* the integers-only rule stated as an assessed property of the artefact, plus the reviewer's trace attempt, plus the instructions file that makes the rule reach the executor rather than living in the learner's head.

**The premature threat.** A learner writes threats in the first hour and then bends the model to fit them. *Countermeasure:* the workflow's explicit prohibition at step 3, and the standard's warning sign.

**The complete-looking model.** No inferred values, no open questions, everything known. *Countermeasure:* the requirement that an empty open-questions list be defended rather than accepted. **This is the design's weakest countermeasure**, because a learner who did not notice their inferences cannot be prompted into noticing them by a checkbox. Exercise 4 — running the agent once starved and once fed, then counting declared inferences in each — is the partial repair, and it works by making the disappearance visible rather than by asking the learner to introspect.

**The hedged cell.** *Countermeasure:* the named forbidden words in the instructions file, which makes the failure searchable rather than judgemental, and which can be checked by anybody without an opinion about security.

**The stale table.** *Countermeasure:* the commanded re-read, and the standard's warning sign — you cannot say when the version in front of you was last read from disk.

**The unevidenced checklist.** Traits marked present because the system feels like the kind of system that has them. *Countermeasure:* the requirement that every mark cite a component or a flow number. **This is the countermeasure the design has most confidence in**, because it is checkable by anybody, including somebody who knows nothing about the system.

**The boundary drawn where the diagram is tidy.** Trust zones follow the layout rather than the identity model. *Countermeasure:* the attack step, which asks specifically for a relocation and a flow number — the only movement in the session that pushes against layout convenience.

**The exclusions nobody stated.** *Countermeasure:* exercise 6, which is closed-book by design. The source pipeline states no scope exclusions anywhere across fourteen agent definitions, and a method that never declares its own exclusions invites the reader to assume it has none.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment evidence is generated here. The model is assessed at [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md) as a constituent of the security plan, on four properties only: that flows are numbered and labelled with integers alone; that every inferred cell appears in the provenance section with the principle that produced it; that every characteristics mark cites a component or a flow number; and that the model states what it structurally cannot represent.

**The model's completeness is not assessed here or at [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md)**, and §8's fourth objection is where that is defended rather than assumed. The short form: completeness is not observable by any instrument this programme has, and an assessment that pretended otherwise would be scoring the assessor's imagination against the learner's.

The four assessed properties share one feature that makes them defensible as entrustment evidence: **each can be checked by a reader who knows nothing about the system being modelled.** That is the practical test the entrustment literature's granularity criterion implies — could a supervisor delegate this whole thing and know whether it had been done ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). A property requiring domain expertise to check is not a property this session can assess at scale. **That note's own limits apply: it is a primer from medical education, its supervision scale and evidence standards are designed for clinical training, and its transfer to engineering practice is this programme's extrapolation rather than the author's claim.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: numbering before understanding is premature formalisation, and it destroys the thing threat modelling is actually good for.**

At its strongest: the recognised value of threat modelling is the conversation. Getting an architect, an operator and a security reviewer into a room and making them disagree about a diagram surfaces knowledge that exists in no document. A notation-first process replaces that with a data-entry exercise, and a data-entry exercise excludes exactly the people whose knowledge you needed, because they will not sit through it. **You will end up with a beautifully joinable model of a system nobody argued about.**

**Substantially correct, and the design's answer is narrow.** The session does not claim the model replaces the conversation; it claims the conversation should produce a joinable artefact rather than a photograph of a whiteboard, and that the numbering can be done during the conversation rather than after it. **The declined fix is to run a facilitated modelling workshop as a separate session before this one.** It is declined because [M03](/wiki/modules/M03.md) has already taught workshop craft and because the practice corpus behind this programme is emphatic — and inconsistent with itself — about design activity never being run as a separate pause in delivery. **The residual risk is real: a learner who works alone will build a model containing only what they already knew, and this session cannot detect that.**

**Objection 2: the provenance side-channel is a control that depends entirely on the author's cooperation, which is precisely what this module says is not a control.**

At its strongest: the module's standing claim is that a control which cannot fail without the model's cooperation is not a control. The inferred-values section is exactly that. Nothing forces an inference into it. A learner — or an agent — who does not notice an inference cannot record it, and one who would rather not record it need not. **The session teaches a discipline and calls it an instrument.**

**Conceded without qualification.** It is a configured control and the paper classifies it as one. Two things reduce the damage without repairing it: the reviewer's cell-by-cell challenge in exercise 1, which is an executable check performed by a person rather than a self-report; and [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md), which is built on the assumption that the side-channel will fail and asks what would survive its failure. **The declined fix is to require an inline provenance marker in every cell.** It is declined because it reintroduces the hedge the format exists to exclude, and the source material demonstrates the consequence: a marked cell is a cell that cannot be joined or counted. **The design accepts a fragile control over a broken format, and states that it is a trade rather than a solution.**

**Objection 3: the paper's principal source cannot be checked by anybody, and a design defended on evidence a reader cannot inspect is not defended.**

At its strongest: the artefact chain, the blackboard contract, the identifier scheme, the compact index, the information diet, the ban on hedging and the re-read instruction all come from one internal, access-controlled source. **No reader of this paper can verify a single sentence of that account.** They cannot check whether the paraphrase is faithful, whether the design is as coherent as described, or whether the failures attributed to it are real. The programme's own standing rule is that evidence is a file path and a line — and here it is a promise.

**Conceded without qualification, and it is the most serious epistemic objection to this module rather than to this session alone.** Three mitigations are offered as mitigation and not as rescue. The design ideas are structural rather than empirical, so a reader can evaluate the *argument* for numbering, dieting and relocating uncertainty on its own terms without trusting the report. The one genuinely checkable artefact in the same genre — the openly published worked threat model, with its separately stated assumptions — independently exhibits the side-channel discipline, which is weak corroboration that the pattern is not an artefact of one team. And every claim traced to the internal source is classified in the evidence status below as resting on unverifiable material, so a reader who discounts it entirely can still see what remains.

**The declined fix was to build the module on the published handbook alone.** It was declined because that handbook's threat-modelling page is a signpost of about four phases at two sentences each, with the taxonomy linked rather than explained, and because a module built on it would have a genre exemplar and no design to examine. **The cost of that decision is that the strongest content in this module rests on a source the reader must take on trust, and this paper does not pretend otherwise.**

**Objection 4: the model this session builds bakes in the module's worst blind spot at stage one, and the paper knows it.**

At its strongest: a dataflow model can only represent things that are components and flows. [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) then adopts a binding rule requiring every surviving threat to name one of each. **The composition of those two decisions systematically removes the operator, the supply chain, human process and out-of-band administration from the analysis — which is to say, it removes most of the ways real organisations are actually harmed.** Choosing this artefact shape first is not a neutral ordering decision; it is a scope decision disguised as a formatting one, made before anyone has argued about scope.

**Substantially correct and only partly answered.** The session's countermeasure is deliberate and thin: exercise 6 requires the learner to name three classes of harm their model structurally cannot represent, closed book, and the artefact list requires a written statement of exclusions. **That converts an invisible scope decision into a declared one, and it does not widen the scope at all.** The source pipeline states no scope exclusions anywhere across fourteen definitions, which is exactly the failure this exercise inoculates against, and which is also evidence of how easily competent people make it.

**The declined fix was to add a second, non-dataflow modelling artefact — an actor-and-authority model — to this session.** It was declined on load and sequencing grounds: the session's whole argument is that one artefact should be finished before the next begins, and adding a second incomplete model at the front of the chain would demonstrate the opposite of what it teaches. **The cost is that the module has one shape of blind spot from beginning to end**, and this paper records that as an accepted cost rather than a solved problem.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will put descriptive text on diagram edges at first attempt, before the integers-only rule is enforced. *Instrument:* the first submitted diagram, coded *integers only* / *mixed* / *text* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Inferred-values sections will record fewer inferences than a second reader can identify from the same source documents. *Instrument:* independent re-derivation of the attribute table by a second reader working from the same inputs, compared row by row against the learner's provenance section. **This predicts the failure of the session's central countermeasure**, and it is included because a control whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption. If it holds, the provenance side-channel is honest in intent and lossy in fact, and [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) is teaching against a defect the cohort has already committed.

**Prediction 3.** Learners who attribute one flow completely before starting others will revise their closed classification list; those who attribute in parallel will not. *Instrument:* the list's revision record against the recorded attribution order.

**Prediction 4.** Learners who hand the whole table to an agent will submit shorter open-questions lists than those who attributed a flow by hand first. *Instrument:* the open-questions list against the recorded working method, paired per learner. **If this fails, the workflow's ordering has no support and should be rewritten as a preference rather than a sequence.**

**Prediction 5.** In exercise 4, the fed agent run will declare fewer inferences in `inferred.yaml` than the starved run, while filling more cells. *Instrument:* the two generated files, compared on filled-cell count and declared-inference count. **This is the session's only direct test of the claim that more context suppresses declared uncertainty**, and a null result would mean the information-diet argument in §3.10 is asserted rather than demonstrated.

**Prediction 6.** Learners instructed to compare their item-1 answer against the taught criterion (exercise 3) will produce joinable flow identifiers more often than learners who read the same criterion without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* the submitted `dataflows.yaml`, coded for identifier stability by a second reader, across two cohorts — or, absent two cohorts, a within-cohort split on whether exercise 3 was completed. **This is the session's only direct test of the mechanism §3.2 rests on.**

**Prediction 7.** A majority of learners will find at least one component in the machine-generated inventory that is absent from their hand-built one, and a minority will record anything that neither could see. *Instrument:* the two inventories plus `open-questions.md`, coded for entries of the third kind. **The second half is the interesting half**, and if it comes out near zero the exercise is teaching inventory rather than teaching the limits of inventory.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That an assurance process is a chain, that identifiers make its later stages joins rather than interpretations, and that a folder of named artefacts with explicit refusals is enough to hold a multi-agent system together.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. **No source available to this programme reports whether a model built this way produces a better threat model, a safer system or anything else**, and the observations that clearly shaped the source design were never recorded. The teaching *method* is reasonably grounded — two independent literatures agree on the ordering, and one of them supplies the boundary condition this session's opening happens to satisfy — but that warrant is about the method, comes from populations unlike you, and says nothing about the subject matter. **Do not let the quality of the pedagogical citations transfer to the substantive thesis; they are about different things.**

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper.** That is a rule of this repository rather than an accident of what was read, and it holds even where a source states a figure in a full text that has been read.

**The concession worth remembering.** The section where your honesty is supposed to live is the one thing here that nobody can force you to fill in, and the paper defending it rests on a source you cannot open. Hold both, and read [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) knowing them.
