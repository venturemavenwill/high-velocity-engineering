## In plain terms

This session installs an unpopular ordering: **the system model is finished before the first threat is written down.**

The reason is mechanical rather than moral. An assurance process is a chain, and everything after the first artefact joins onto it. If a flow is described in prose, two people will describe it differently and every later join will quietly match the wrong things. If a flow has an integer, the join is exact and the reasoning problem downstream collapses into set operations.

**That is the single most transferable idea in the material this module is built on: front-load the judgement, back-load the mechanics.** All the semantic difficulty is concentrated at the front, where a person is still looking at it.

Two devices carry the ordering and both are worth more than the security content around them.

**A folder used as a blackboard is a sufficient integration contract.** No orchestration framework is required, and the contract has a property a graph does not give you for free: a missing prerequisite genuinely fails, without any model's cooperation. In a stage whose whole subject is the difference between a configured control and a demonstrated one, that matters.

**And an information diet improves a subagent's output.** Handing an agent the numbered diagram and the closed list, and withholding everything else, is a design move rather than a limitation — which inverts the reflex a learner arrives with.

The session's own trap is the third artefact. The inferred-values side-channel is where every guess is supposed to be recorded, and it is the thing [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) shows being deleted downstream.

## Abstract

This paper defends opening an assurance module with model construction rather than with threat enumeration, and defends the specific form the model takes. The argument is that an assurance process is a chain, that an error at the front of a chain is not a local error, and that converting prose into stable identifiers at the first stage is what makes every later stage a join rather than an interpretation. The session installs a numbered dataflow diagram, an attribute table drawn from a closed list, a provenance side-channel, an open-questions list and an evidence-bearing characteristics checklist, together with a filesystem-mediated integration contract and a deliberate information diet for any agent used. The paper concedes that the provenance side-channel is a control which depends entirely on the author's cooperation — which is exactly what this module argues is not a control — and that the ordering has no outcome evidence behind it in any source available to this programme.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A learner asked for a threat model will begin listing threats, because threats are the recognisable part and because listing them feels like progress.

**The output of that instinct is a list of things true of software in general**, attached to a picture, with no way to check whether anything was missed and no way for an engineer to act on any single line. It is also unjoinable: a later stage that tries to match controls to those threats has to interpret prose on both sides, and interpretation at scale is where a chain goes wrong quietly.

There is a second problem which is harder to see. **The security attributes of a real system are mostly not written down anywhere**, so the person building the model will infer them — from convention, from what a competent team would have done, from the fact that a diagram shows a padlock. Those inferences are unavoidable and are not the problem. **The problem is that they are indistinguishable from observations by the time anybody reads the table.**

The session's response is to make the numbering non-negotiable and to give provenance a place to live that is not the cell.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session assumes a running increment from [Stage 5](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md), the verifiable non-functional requirements from [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md), and the register of what can and cannot move from [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md), because a trust zone is a constraint before it is a boundary.

It also assumes [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) and [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md). A learner who has not yet seen how much an executor's output depends on what it was given will not accept that withholding context improves it.

**The prior it works against is that a multi-agent system needs an orchestrator.** A cohort leaving [M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) has just spent a module on agents and will reach for a graph. The blackboard contract is introduced immediately and by contrast.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Low intrinsic load, high procedural discipline. The concepts are few and independent; the difficulty is in refusing to do the interesting part first.

**3.2 Why identifiers before attributes.** A join is exact when both sides carry the same key and interpretive when they do not. Numbering is the cheapest possible act that produces a key, and it is free only before anything references the thing being numbered. **The window closes the moment the first downstream artefact exists**, which is why the session spends its first movement on naming and numbering and nothing else.

**3.3 Why one flow is attributed completely before any other.** A single finished row exercises the closed list and reveals whether it is adequate. Partially attributed rows in parallel reveal nothing and are expensive to redo. This is the same ordering argument [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md) made for spikes, applied to a table.

**3.4 Why hedges are banned inside cells.** A hedge inside a cell destroys machine consumption downstream — a value that reads *probably TLS* cannot be joined, counted or checked, and will be read as *TLS* by the first process that touches it. **Relocating uncertainty is the only move that preserves both the join and the honesty.** It is also, as §8 concedes, the move that makes the honesty deletable.

**3.5 Why the information diet is a design move.** More context is not more signal. A subagent given the projections and denied the originals produces a narrower output on a narrower question, which is what a join requires. **This is asserted here as mechanism and direction only; no magnitude is claimed and none is available.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 1 is the instrument.** *Name a dataflow, then name it again the way another person would name it identically.* The second clause is impossible in prose and learners discover that inside a minute, which is the argument for numbering made experientially rather than asserted.

**Opening item 4 has one answer.** What happens to every later stage if the first artefact is wrong is that they are all wrong, silently, and this is why the session is first in the module.

**The work movement is ordered so that reversal stays cheap** — name and number, attribute one flow completely, then use an agent under a diet, then command a re-read. The last of those looks like superstition and is not: a model will confidently reuse a table already in context rather than re-reading the file, and in a chained process the reuse is invisible. **The instruction to re-read exists in the source material, and nobody writes an instruction like that prospectively.**

**The review movement uses a stranger deliberately.** The author of a model cannot tell which of its cells are legible, and cannot tell which were inferred, because they remember. Handing it to somebody who did not build the system is the only cheap test of both.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The picture.** A diagram that communicates to a human and joins to nothing. *Countermeasure:* the integers-only rule stated as an assessed property of the artefact, plus the reviewer's trace attempt.

**The premature threat.** *Countermeasure:* the work movement's explicit prohibition, and the standard's warning sign — you wrote a threat down in the first hour.

**The complete-looking model.** No inferred values, no open questions. *Countermeasure:* the requirement that an empty open-questions list be defended rather than accepted. **This is the design's weakest countermeasure**, because a learner who did not notice their inferences cannot be prompted into noticing them by a checkbox.

**The hedged cell.** *Countermeasure:* the named words in the standard's warning sign, which makes the failure searchable rather than judgemental.

**The stale table.** *Countermeasure:* the commanded re-read, and the standard's warning sign — you cannot say when the version in front of you was last read from disk.

**The unevidenced checklist.** *Countermeasure:* the requirement that every mark cite a component or a flow number. This is the countermeasure the design has most confidence in, because it is checkable by anybody.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: numbering before understanding is premature formalisation, and it destroys the thing threat modelling is actually good for.**

At its strongest: the recognised value of threat modelling is the conversation. Getting an architect, an operator and a security reviewer into a room and making them disagree about a diagram surfaces knowledge that exists in no document. A notation-first process replaces that with a data-entry exercise, and a data-entry exercise excludes exactly the people whose knowledge you needed, because they will not sit through it. **You will end up with a beautifully joinable model of a system nobody argued about.**

**Substantially correct, and the design's answer is narrow.** The session does not claim the model replaces the conversation; it claims the conversation should produce a joinable artefact rather than a photograph of a whiteboard, and that the numbering can be done during the conversation rather than after it. **The declined fix is to run a facilitated modelling workshop as a separate session before this one.** It is declined because [M03](/wiki/modules/M03.md) has already taught workshop craft and because the practice corpus behind this programme is emphatic — and inconsistent with itself — about design activity never being run as a separate pause in delivery. **The residual risk is real: a learner who works alone will build a model containing only what they already knew, and this session cannot detect that.**

**Objection 2: the provenance side-channel is a control that depends entirely on the author's cooperation, which is precisely what this module says is not a control.**

At its strongest: the module's standing claim is that a control which cannot fail without the model's cooperation is not a control. The inferred-values section is exactly that. Nothing forces an inference into it. A learner — or an agent — who does not notice an inference cannot record it, and one who would rather not record it need not. **The session teaches a discipline and calls it an instrument.**

**Conceded without qualification.** It is a configured control and the paper classifies it as one. Two things reduce the damage without repairing it: the reviewer's cell-by-cell challenge in the review movement, which is an executable check performed by a person rather than a self-report; and [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md), which is built on the assumption that the side-channel will fail and asks what would survive its failure. **The declined fix is to require an inline provenance marker in every cell.** It is declined because it reintroduces the hedge the format exists to exclude, and the source material demonstrates the consequence: a marked cell is a cell that cannot be joined or counted. **The design accepts a fragile control over a broken format, and states that it is a trade rather than a solution.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will put descriptive text on diagram edges at first attempt, before the integers-only rule is enforced. *Instrument:* the first submitted diagram, coded *integers only* / *mixed* / *text* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Inferred-values sections will record fewer inferences than a second reader can identify from the same source documents. *Instrument:* independent re-derivation of the attribute table by a second reader working from the same inputs, compared row by row against the learner's provenance section.

**Prediction 3.** Learners who attribute one flow completely before starting others will revise their closed classification list; those who attribute in parallel will not. *Instrument:* the list's revision record against the recorded attribution order.

**Prediction 4.** Learners who hand the whole table to an agent will submit shorter open-questions lists than those who attributed a flow by hand first. *Instrument:* the open-questions list against the recorded working method, paired per learner. **If this fails, the work movement's ordering has no support and the movement should be rewritten as a preference.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That an assurance process is a chain, that identifiers make its later stages joins rather than interpretations, and that a folder of named artefacts with explicit refusals is enough to hold a multi-agent system together.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about outcomes. **No source available to this programme reports whether a model built this way produces a better threat model, a safer system or anything else**, and the observations that clearly shaped the design were never recorded.

**The concession worth remembering.** The section where your honesty is supposed to live is the one thing here that nobody can force you to fill in. Hold that, and read [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) knowing it.
