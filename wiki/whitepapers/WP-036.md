## In plain terms

You made something to show people. Everything you did to improve it made the answer you get worse.

The mechanism is specific enough to check rather than believe, and it is the best-argued claim in the whole practice corpus. **Detail drags the conversation onto the detail.** A rendered device invites an argument about the device. An assumed attribute of the workforce invites an argument about the workforce. A plausible-looking constraint invites an argument about whether the thing fits over the safety gear. **None of those arguments is about your idea**, and the room only has so many minutes.

There is a second and independent mechanism. **Polish signals investment, and people do not attack investment.** A rough thing invites alteration; a finished thing invites praise. That is why the low-fidelity artefact must be alterable while somebody is holding it, and why an artefact that takes longer to change than the conversation takes is already too finished.

The platform work exists because neither mechanism survives contact with a tool that removes the effort. **The session's response is to choose a tool for what it cannot do.** Microsoft publishes Copilot Studio's limits in its own comparison guidance — limited ability to tune API calls, actions and response formatting against a pro-code environment; a pinned card schema; thinner lifecycle tooling; slower feature availability; and a positioning for department-scale deployments. **Every one of those is a ceiling on how finished you can make the thing, supplied by somebody other than you**, and a ceiling somebody else enforces is the only kind that survives an author's good intentions. The session then requires the learner to state the objection: **the ceiling constrains controllability and constrains nothing about plausibility.** A published agent in a chat surface looks entirely decided, whatever you were prevented from tuning.

The session's problem is that the argument was built for a world in which polish had a price, and the price did the enforcement. **The corpus notices that its own tooling has made polished output nearly costless, and offers no advice.** Neither does this programme. What the session does instead is make the learner produce both versions, observe what each one makes a real person talk about, and write the sentence they would actually say to a colleague who has already made the refined one for free. **The sentence is the assessed object. The position it defends is not.**

## Abstract

This paper defends deliberately reducing the fidelity of early concept artefacts, on a mechanism rather than on an aesthetic, and then concedes that the mechanism's enforcement has been removed by the tooling. The argument is that added detail redirects discussion onto the detail and that finish signals investment which suppresses rejection, so that an artefact's persuasiveness and its evidentiary value move in opposite directions. The session installs a stripping record in which each removal is paired with the discussion it prevents, a paired-artefact comparison run on real people, and a written position on the costless-polish problem which is assessed on its argument rather than on its conclusion.

The design's one structural repair for the missing enforcement is to relocate it into the tool. **A vendor-published limit is an externally supplied fidelity ceiling**, and the platform workflow requires the learner to declare a ceiling on each of four separable dimensions before opening the tool, then to record where the published limits held the line and where only intent did. The paper argues that this converts an unenforceable rule into a checkable decision, and immediately concedes the boundary of the repair: **the ceiling is on controllability, not on plausibility**, so the artefact remains capable of looking decided while being untunable.

The paper concedes that the source corpus has no evidence base of any kind, that the countermeasure's economic enforcement is gone, that the fidelity-ceiling repair is design reasoning the documentation says nothing about, and that a cohort concluding the practice is obsolete would be reporting a real finding rather than failing the session.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

An engineer with an idea makes something to show. Every instinct they have been trained with — completeness, precision, professional finish — degrades the instrument they are building.

**The degradation is not about taste.** A concept artefact is an instrument for eliciting disagreement, and it has two failure modes. It can direct the disagreement at the wrong thing, which is what detail does. And it can suppress the disagreement entirely, which is what finish does. An artefact optimised for how it looks in a meeting is optimised against both of the things it was made for.

There is a compounding problem specific to this stage. The people being shown the artefact are frequently the same people being asked to fund it, and an artefact that looks close to done makes the funding decision feel smaller than it is. **A concept sketch asks for a conversation; a finished mock asks for a signature**, and neither the maker nor the viewer usually notices which one was asked.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** This is the second of two sessions in the stage a learner can enter without the programme behind them. The mechanism stands alone and is checkable in a single session against people who have never heard of it.

Three retrievals sharpen it. [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) supplies the leading-question problem, of which this is the artefact version: an artefact can lead exactly as a question can, and the corpus's own instrument does both. [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md) supplies the disposal discipline, and [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) supplies the practice of stating what an artefact does not establish. **A learner arriving without them loses precision rather than access.**

The prior the session works against is that fidelity is a proxy for effort, and effort is a proxy for respect. Learners believe — often correctly, about their own organisations — that showing somebody a rough thing is rude. **The session does not dispute that. It requires the cost of the politeness to be counted.**

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Low intrinsically, high evaluatively. Making the rough artefact is trivial. Deciding what the refined one cost you, and defending a position on a problem the source abandoned, is not.

**3.2 The detail mechanism.** A concrete specific is more discussable than an abstraction — it offers a surface to agree or disagree with, and people take the surface offered. The corpus's own examples are the right shape and are carried in paraphrase: the wrong equipment type, an assumed attribute of the workforce, whether a device fits over safety gear. **Each of those is a real conversation and none of them is about the idea.**

**3.3 The investment mechanism.** Visible effort creates a social cost to rejection. This is separate from the detail mechanism and has a different countermeasure — the detail problem is solved by removing detail, the investment problem is solved by the artefact being visibly alterable. **A polished artefact with very little detail in it still suppresses feedback.**

**3.4 Why more iterations beat fewer refined ones.** The corpus states the rule and does not argue it. The argument available is that each iteration is a fresh opportunity for the idea to be rejected, and rejection is the output being purchased. **This is a reconstruction of the rule's reasoning, not the rule's own warrant.**

**3.5 Why the session's own norms matter.** The learning design behind this programme holds that the surrounding norms are a layer of a failure-based design rather than a preamble to it. A cohort in which rough work is read as unfinished work will not produce rough work, whatever the session says.

**3.6 Fidelity is not one variable, and this is the correction the research literature supplies that the corpus does not.**

The corpus argues for low fidelity as a single setting. The interviewing literature splits it: appearance, behaviour, content and physical form are separate dimensions, and polish along any of them introduces its own unintended assumptions — about brand, about feasibility, about mobility, about completeness ([research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md](/research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md)). The same source treats concepts as elicitation rather than as testing: a deliberately provocative, partial or non-viable concept, presented neutrally, **without signalling authorship or emotional investment**, is used to reveal design criteria and the boundaries of desirability.

The design consequence is substantial and is why the session assesses four declared ceilings rather than one. **The right artefact is frequently high in content and low in appearance** — real terminology, real edge cases, drawn roughly — and the reflex to make everything equally rough is as unexamined as the reflex to make everything equally finished. **Limits: 2013 first edition only; a practitioner synthesis with no controlled comparison of techniques and no measured causal effect; the note records that even the book's empathy claim rests on an analogy in a footnote.**

The contextual-design method adds the operational rule for what happens after the showing, and it is the reason a high-fidelity artefact wastes work rather than merely misleading. A reaction is traced to the **highest** relevant level, and redesign proceeds downward from there — practice information changes the model, a structural mismatch changes the structure, and only a local presentation problem changes the presentation ([research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md)). **A finished artefact makes presentation the most available thing to comment on, which routes the whole conversation to the cheapest level of the hierarchy.** The same source states the artefact's purpose in a sentence the session adopts wholesale: the goal is to discover failure, not to collect approval. **Limits: a method book, second edition, whose effectiveness rests on practitioner experience rather than comparative evidence.**

**3.7 Why a published product limit is a better countermeasure than an intention, and where the argument stops.**

This is the paper's own contribution and it is design reasoning, so it is stated as such. If the enforcement mechanism for low fidelity was the price of polish, and the price has gone, then a replacement enforcement mechanism must come from outside the author. **A vendor's published capability limit is such a mechanism.** Microsoft's comparison guidance states Copilot Studio's constraints plainly — limited ability to fine-tune API calls, actions and response formatting compared with a pro-code environment; Adaptive Cards pinned to schema 1.6 and earlier; lifecycle tooling less integrated than the project-based pro-dev workflow; slower feature availability — alongside a positioning for small-scale, department-level deployments ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). A learner working inside those limits stops polishing at a point they did not choose, which is exactly the condition the old economics used to supply.

**The argument has a hard boundary and the session is built around stating it.** The published limits constrain **controllability**. They do not constrain **plausibility**: an agent published into a chat surface presents as a decided thing, in fluent prose, inside the customer's own tooling, and nothing about it announces that it is a sketch. **So the tool supplies a ceiling on one dimension of the four and none on the one that drives the investment mechanism.** A design that stopped at *choose the low-code tool* would have bought the vocabulary of the countermeasure without its effect, and would have taught learners to feel disciplined while producing exactly the artefact the mechanism warns about.

**The limits on the documentation are heavy.** First-party selection guidance whose authority is definitional; **no adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no evidence that its recommended mappings produce better outcomes**; one vendor's options compared against each other; read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, with several comparison tables truncated; and **highly perishable**, since a limitation recorded today may be closed before the session next runs. **The documentation nowhere suggests that its limits should be used as a design discipline. That inversion is this paper's, and it has no external warrant.**

**3.8 Why both artefacts are built, in the language of the learning design.**

The paired-artefact comparison is not a convenience. The learning design behind this programme requires tasks built on **contrasting cases** — at least two, ideally contrasts that can be perceptually seen — and requires the cases to be **varied**, holding some features constant while others move, so attention lands on the critical feature ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Two artefacts of one idea, shown to different people with an identical question, satisfies both conditions. One artefact and a persuasive explanation satisfies neither. **The same source's evidence is school, university and laboratory evidence, its author's claim about corporate and adult settings is uncited in his own reference list, and this application to working professionals is an extrapolation recorded as one.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The movements run situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**Opening item 1 is a one-minute hand sketch, kept.** It is produced before any instruction and it is the artefact the session goes on to use. Learners who are told what low fidelity means before producing something produce a performance of roughness instead.

**Both versions are made, deliberately, because both can now be made.** This is the design's one substantive response to the costless-polish problem: rather than asserting a rule whose enforcement is gone, it uses the new economics to run the comparison the old economics made impossible. **The rough and the refined artefact go to two different people with the same question, and what each of them talks about in the first two minutes is recorded.** That record is the session's evidence and it is reused at [S037](/wiki/seminars/S037.md) and [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md).

**The stripping record is the assessed artefact, not the concept.** Each removed detail is paired with the discussion it would have caused. *It would have been distracting* is not admissible, because the whole claim is about which specific conversation replaces which.

**The costless-polish position is assessed on the sentence.** The learner writes what they would actually say to a colleague who has produced the refined version for free — not a principle, a sentence — and **a learner who concludes that low fidelity is obsolete and argues it well has done the session's work.** The design holds this open because the source holds it open, and because a curriculum that resolved it would be asserting more than it knows.

**The fidelity ceiling is declared before the tool is opened, and this is the movement that carries the platform.** The learner writes down which of the four dimensions they are holding low, builds the same idea in Copilot Studio, and stops at the ceiling — recording every point at which they wanted to exceed it and what exceeding it would have bought. **A ceiling declared afterwards is a description of where somebody got tired**, which is why the ordering is enforced rather than suggested, and why the record has a separate section for pressure on the ceiling.

The workflow then does one thing prose cannot. **It lets the learner feel an external limit hold**, which is a different experience from resolving to be disciplined and is the whole of the design's response to the missing enforcement. And it produces the objection in the same hour: the artefact the tool constrained still looked finished to both viewers. **The session wants both of those observations and does not reconcile them**, because the honest position is that the tool solved half the problem.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The costless polish.** The likeliest failure and the session's subject. *Countermeasure:* none that is reliable. The standard's warning sign — you did not decide to polish it, you just did not decide not to — is a diagnostic rather than a control, and the paper does not claim otherwise.

**The irrelevant argument.** The room spends its time on an invented detail. *Countermeasure:* the stripping record, produced before the showing rather than after, so that the learner has already named which conversations they were trying to prevent.

**The implied commitment.** A plausibility detail is read as a decision. *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that nothing in the artefact imply a commitment not made, and the warning sign — somebody asks who chose that, and you cannot remember choosing.

**The keepsake.** The learner cannot discard the artefact and the second version is the first with amendments. *Countermeasure:* [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md)'s disposal discipline, transposed. **The design expects this to transfer badly** — [WP-031](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-031.md) prediction 4 is written against exactly this transfer.

**The performance of roughness.** A deliberately crude artefact that is nonetheless complete, closed and unalterable. *Countermeasure:* the alterability test — can it be changed while somebody is holding it — which is a property of the medium rather than of the finish.

**The tool mistaken for the ceiling.** The learner chooses a constrained platform and stops thinking, on the reasoning that the vendor has now done the discipline for them. *Countermeasure:* the four declared dimensions, which make it visible that the tool capped one of them; and the standard's warning sign — nobody who saw it asked a single question about whether the idea was right. **The paper expects this countermeasure to be the weakest of the five**, because it asks the learner to distrust a decision they have already made and been praised for.

**The stale ceiling.** The published limit the learner relied on was closed in a release, and the artefact drifted past a ceiling nobody re-read. *Countermeasure:* the requirement to date the page and record the limit as an instance rather than as a property, which is the same discipline the perishability register applies everywhere else.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the mechanism is a practitioner assertion from a corpus with no evidence base of any kind, and this programme spends a session on it because it likes the argument.**

At its strongest: the source cites nothing, measures nothing and reports no case in which fidelity was varied and feedback compared. The claim that polish suppresses honest feedback is folklore with a plausible story attached — and this programme, which spends nine stages teaching learners to demand warrant, has adopted an unwarranted claim because it is well phrased and agrees with the authors' priors. That is exactly the failure mode it accuses the corpus of.

**Substantially conceded, and the parallel is the uncomfortable part.** The rebuttal is narrow and is about testability rather than about warrant: unlike almost everything else in the corpus, **this claim can be checked in a single session by anybody**, and the session is constructed to check it rather than to assert it. The paired-artefact comparison produces the learner's own local evidence, on their own idea, with their own people. **The declined fix is to teach the mechanism as established practice and skip the comparison.** It is declined because the comparison is the only warrant the claim has anywhere in this repository, and removing it would leave a session that asserts what its source asserts. **The evidence produced is a cohort's own and is not generalisable**, and the design says so rather than pretending a classroom comparison settles the question.

**Objection 2: holding the costless-polish problem open is an abdication, and a curriculum that assesses the sentence rather than the position has declined to teach.**

At its strongest: learners come to be taught. Presenting a genuine practical dilemma, marking the eloquence of their response and declining to say which answer is right produces confident graduates with no practice — and in the field they will meet colleagues who polish everything, will have no ground to stand on, and will discover that the sentence they wrote was assessed by somebody who also did not know.

**Partly conceded.** The design does take a position — low fidelity is taught, the stripping record is required, and the artefact must be alterable — so the openness is confined to one question: whether the mechanism survives when the price is gone. **On that question the source is silent and this programme has no evidence.** The rebuttal is that asserting an answer here would be doing precisely what [Q4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md) criticises: presenting a preference as a standard. **The declined fix is to rule that the refined artefact must not be shown first, regardless of cost.** It is declined because the rule would be unenforceable, unwarranted and — if the objection is right — wrong. **A cohort that argued its way to the opposite conclusion would be producing a finding this design would want to hear**, and §9 prediction 4 is how it would be noticed.

**Objection 3: using a vendor's product limits as a design discipline is teaching learners to outsource their judgement to a commercial roadmap.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the paper takes most seriously: the limits are not there for the learner's benefit. They exist because of engineering trade-offs, packaging decisions and a segmentation strategy, and **the vendor's stated intention is to remove them** — the same documentation describes slower feature availability as a disadvantage rather than as a feature, which is the vendor saying it intends to close the gap. A discipline built on somebody else's unfinished work has an expiry date nobody controls, and a graduate who learned to stop where the tool stopped has learned a habit that will silently stop working. Worse, it teaches a bad general lesson: that a constraint is a substitute for a decision.

**Conceded in its second half and rejected in its first.** The paper does not claim the limits are durable; it claims they are *external*, and externality is the property the countermeasure lost when polish became free. **The declined fix was to teach the discipline as pure intent — declare a ceiling and hold it by will alone, on any platform.** It was declined for a reason the paper is willing to be judged on: **that is precisely the design the costless-polish problem has already defeated**, and re-issuing it would be teaching a rule whose failure mode this session exists to name. The mitigation is the one the repository uses everywhere: the limit is recorded as a **perishable instance** of a durable claim, dated at the reading, with the standard requirement to re-verify before use. **The residual cost is real and is accepted: a learner who does not re-verify will one day polish past a ceiling that has quietly moved**, and §9 prediction 6 is how that would be detected.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** In the paired comparison, viewers of the refined artefact will spend their first two minutes on details that are not the idea more often than viewers of the rough one. *Instrument:* the two-minute record, with each utterance coded *about the idea* / *about a detail* by a second reader who does not know which artefact produced it.

**Prediction 2.** Viewers of the refined artefact will offer additive feedback and viewers of the rough artefact will offer rejections. *Instrument:* the same records, coded *additive* / *corrective* / *rejecting*.

**Prediction 3.** Most learners will produce a refined artefact without having decided to. *Instrument:* a single written question administered immediately after production — did you decide to polish this — with the answer collected before the comparison is run.

**Prediction 4.** A minority of learners will argue that the mechanism does not survive costless polish, and their stripping records will be no less specific than those of learners who defended it. *Instrument:* the position, coded, against the stripping record's specificity, coded independently. **If the arguing minority produces weaker records, the design's decision to assess the sentence rather than the position is wrong**, and the session should teach a rule instead.

**Prediction 5.** Learners will name appearance as the dimension they would raise given an extra hour, more often than content, before the material is taught and still after it. *Instrument:* exercise 1's four-dial rating collected twice, once at the opening and once at the close, coded for which dial the learner would raise. **The second half of this prediction is the interesting one**: if the answer does not move, the four-dimension correction was read and not adopted, and the session has taught a distinction learners can restate and do not use.

**Prediction 6.** The platform ceiling will fail to hold for a substantial minority of learners — they will exceed the declared ceiling and record it under pressure, or reach for a pro-code path to work around a formatting limit. *Instrument:* the `ceiling_pressure` section of the stripping record, coded *held* / *exceeded and recorded* / *exceeded and unrecorded*, with the third category detectable only by comparing the record against the artefact. **This predicts the failure of the session's own countermeasure**, and it is included because the paper's central move — relocating enforcement into a vendor's limits — is untested and is exactly the kind of repair that works in a paper and not in a room.

**Prediction 7.** At least one published limit relied on in a given term will have been closed or moved by the following term. *Instrument:* the dated limit recorded in each cohort's stripping records, re-checked against the documentation at the start of the next term. **If this fails — if the limits prove stable across years — the perishability framing around this session is over-stated**, and the register should say so rather than repeating a caution that the evidence does not support.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That detail redirects a conversation, that finish suppresses rejection, that these are separate mechanisms with separate countermeasures, and that a published product limit can supply an enforcement the economics no longer do.

**What the evidence licenses.** A mechanism argument from a source that cites nothing; a fidelity taxonomy and a codesign discipline from two method books with no comparative evidence; a set of product limits from first-party documentation with no outcome data; and whatever your own paired comparison produced. **Direction and mechanism only. No magnitude is asserted and none exists.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The rule was enforced by the price of polish and the price is gone. This paper repairs the enforcement by borrowing somebody else's limits and by running a comparison instead of asserting a rule, **and it does not claim either repair works.** The borrowed limits also constrain the wrong half of the problem — they cap what you can tune and not how decided the result looks — which is stated in the session rather than smoothed over, because a countermeasure sold as complete is worse than one sold as partial.
