## In plain terms

You made something to show people. Everything you did to improve it made the answer you get worse.

The mechanism is specific enough to check rather than believe, and it is the best-argued claim in the whole practice corpus. **Detail drags the conversation onto the detail.** A rendered device invites an argument about the device. An assumed attribute of the workforce invites an argument about the workforce. A plausible-looking constraint invites an argument about whether the thing fits over the safety gear. **None of those arguments is about your idea**, and the room only has so many minutes.

There is a second and independent mechanism. **Polish signals investment, and people do not attack investment.** A rough thing invites alteration; a finished thing invites praise. That is why the low-fidelity artefact must be alterable while somebody is holding it, and why an artefact that takes longer to change than the conversation takes is already too finished.

The session's problem is that the argument was built for a world in which polish had a price, and the price did the enforcement. **The corpus notices that its own tooling has made polished output nearly costless, and offers no advice.** Neither does this programme. What the session does instead is make the learner produce both versions, observe what each one makes a real person talk about, and write the sentence they would actually say to a colleague who has already made the refined one for free. **The sentence is the assessed object. The position it defends is not.**

## Abstract

This paper defends deliberately reducing the fidelity of early concept artefacts, on a mechanism rather than on an aesthetic, and then concedes that the mechanism's enforcement has been removed by the tooling. The argument is that added detail redirects discussion onto the detail and that finish signals investment which suppresses rejection, so that an artefact's persuasiveness and its evidentiary value move in opposite directions. The session installs a stripping record in which each removal is paired with the discussion it prevents, a paired-artefact comparison run on real people, and a written position on the costless-polish problem which is assessed on its argument rather than on its conclusion. The paper concedes that the source corpus has no evidence base of any kind, that the countermeasure's economic enforcement is gone, and that a cohort concluding the practice is obsolete would be reporting a real finding rather than failing the session.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

An engineer with an idea makes something to show. Every instinct they have been trained with — completeness, precision, professional finish — degrades the instrument they are building.

**The degradation is not about taste.** A concept artefact is an instrument for eliciting disagreement, and it has two failure modes. It can direct the disagreement at the wrong thing, which is what detail does. And it can suppress the disagreement entirely, which is what finish does. An artefact optimised for how it looks in a meeting is optimised against both of the things it was made for.

There is a compounding problem specific to this stage. The people being shown the artefact are frequently the same people being asked to fund it, and an artefact that looks close to done makes the funding decision feel smaller than it is. **A concept sketch asks for a conversation; a finished mock asks for a signature**, and neither the maker nor the viewer usually notices which one was asked.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: either.** This is the second of two sessions in the stage a learner can enter without the programme behind them. The mechanism stands alone and is checkable in a single session against people who have never heard of it.

Three retrievals sharpen it. [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) supplies the leading-question problem, of which this is the artefact version: an artefact can lead exactly as a question can, and the corpus's own instrument does both. [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md) supplies the disposal discipline, and [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) supplies the practice of stating what an artefact does not establish. **A learner arriving without them loses precision rather than access.**

The prior the session works against is that fidelity is a proxy for effort, and effort is a proxy for respect. Learners believe — often correctly, about their own organisations — that showing somebody a rough thing is rude. **The session does not dispute that. It requires the cost of the politeness to be counted.**

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Low intrinsically, high evaluatively. Making the rough artefact is trivial. Deciding what the refined one cost you, and defending a position on a problem the source abandoned, is not.

**3.2 The detail mechanism.** A concrete specific is more discussable than an abstraction — it offers a surface to agree or disagree with, and people take the surface offered. The corpus's own examples are the right shape and are carried in paraphrase: the wrong equipment type, an assumed attribute of the workforce, whether a device fits over safety gear. **Each of those is a real conversation and none of them is about the idea.**

**3.3 The investment mechanism.** Visible effort creates a social cost to rejection. This is separate from the detail mechanism and has a different countermeasure — the detail problem is solved by removing detail, the investment problem is solved by the artefact being visibly alterable. **A polished artefact with very little detail in it still suppresses feedback.**

**3.4 Why more iterations beat fewer refined ones.** The corpus states the rule and does not argue it. The argument available is that each iteration is a fresh opportunity for the idea to be rejected, and rejection is the output being purchased. **This is a reconstruction of the rule's reasoning, not the rule's own warrant.**

**3.5 Why the session's own norms matter.** The learning design behind this programme holds that the surrounding norms are a layer of a failure-based design rather than a preamble to it. A cohort in which rough work is read as unfinished work will not produce rough work, whatever the session says.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 1 is a one-minute hand sketch, kept.** It is produced before any instruction and it is the artefact the session goes on to use. Learners who are told what low fidelity means before producing something produce a performance of roughness instead.

**Both versions are made, deliberately, because both can now be made.** This is the design's one substantive response to the costless-polish problem: rather than asserting a rule whose enforcement is gone, it uses the new economics to run the comparison the old economics made impossible. **The rough and the refined artefact go to two different people with the same question, and what each of them talks about in the first two minutes is recorded.** That record is the session's evidence and it is reused at [S037](/wiki/seminars/S037.md) and [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md).

**The stripping record is the assessed artefact, not the concept.** Each removed detail is paired with the discussion it would have caused. *It would have been distracting* is not admissible, because the whole claim is about which specific conversation replaces which.

**The costless-polish position is assessed on the sentence.** The learner writes what they would actually say to a colleague who has produced the refined version for free — not a principle, a sentence — and **a learner who concludes that low fidelity is obsolete and argues it well has done the session's work.** The design holds this open because the source holds it open, and because a curriculum that resolved it would be asserting more than it knows.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The costless polish.** The likeliest failure and the session's subject. *Countermeasure:* none that is reliable. The standard's warning sign — you did not decide to polish it, you just did not decide not to — is a diagnostic rather than a control, and the paper does not claim otherwise.

**The irrelevant argument.** The room spends its time on an invented detail. *Countermeasure:* the stripping record, produced before the showing rather than after, so that the learner has already named which conversations they were trying to prevent.

**The implied commitment.** A plausibility detail is read as a decision. *Countermeasure:* the standard's requirement that nothing in the artefact imply a commitment not made, and the warning sign — somebody asks who chose that, and you cannot remember choosing.

**The keepsake.** The learner cannot discard the artefact and the second version is the first with amendments. *Countermeasure:* [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md)'s disposal discipline, transposed. **The design expects this to transfer badly** — [WP-031](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-031.md) prediction 4 is written against exactly this transfer.

**The performance of roughness.** A deliberately crude artefact that is nonetheless complete, closed and unalterable. *Countermeasure:* the alterability test — can it be changed while somebody is holding it — which is a property of the medium rather than of the finish.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the mechanism is a practitioner assertion from a corpus with no evidence base of any kind, and this programme spends a session on it because it likes the argument.**

At its strongest: the source cites nothing, measures nothing and reports no case in which fidelity was varied and feedback compared. The claim that polish suppresses honest feedback is folklore with a plausible story attached — and this programme, which spends nine stages teaching learners to demand warrant, has adopted an unwarranted claim because it is well phrased and agrees with the authors' priors. That is exactly the failure mode it accuses the corpus of.

**Substantially conceded, and the parallel is the uncomfortable part.** The rebuttal is narrow and is about testability rather than about warrant: unlike almost everything else in the corpus, **this claim can be checked in a single session by anybody**, and the session is constructed to check it rather than to assert it. The paired-artefact comparison produces the learner's own local evidence, on their own idea, with their own people. **The declined fix is to teach the mechanism as established practice and skip the comparison.** It is declined because the comparison is the only warrant the claim has anywhere in this repository, and removing it would leave a session that asserts what its source asserts. **The evidence produced is a cohort's own and is not generalisable**, and the design says so rather than pretending a classroom comparison settles the question.

**Objection 2: holding the costless-polish problem open is an abdication, and a curriculum that assesses the sentence rather than the position has declined to teach.**

At its strongest: learners come to be taught. Presenting a genuine practical dilemma, marking the eloquence of their response and declining to say which answer is right produces confident graduates with no practice — and in the field they will meet colleagues who polish everything, will have no ground to stand on, and will discover that the sentence they wrote was assessed by somebody who also did not know.

**Partly conceded.** The design does take a position — low fidelity is taught, the stripping record is required, and the artefact must be alterable — so the openness is confined to one question: whether the mechanism survives when the price is gone. **On that question the source is silent and this programme has no evidence.** The rebuttal is that asserting an answer here would be doing precisely what [Q4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md) criticises: presenting a preference as a standard. **The declined fix is to rule that the refined artefact must not be shown first, regardless of cost.** It is declined because the rule would be unenforceable, unwarranted and — if the objection is right — wrong. **A cohort that argued its way to the opposite conclusion would be producing a finding this design would want to hear**, and §9 prediction 4 is how it would be noticed.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** In the paired comparison, viewers of the refined artefact will spend their first two minutes on details that are not the idea more often than viewers of the rough one. *Instrument:* the two-minute record, with each utterance coded *about the idea* / *about a detail* by a second reader who does not know which artefact produced it.

**Prediction 2.** Viewers of the refined artefact will offer additive feedback and viewers of the rough artefact will offer rejections. *Instrument:* the same records, coded *additive* / *corrective* / *rejecting*.

**Prediction 3.** Most learners will produce a refined artefact without having decided to. *Instrument:* a single written question administered immediately after production — did you decide to polish this — with the answer collected before the comparison is run.

**Prediction 4.** A minority of learners will argue that the mechanism does not survive costless polish, and their stripping records will be no less specific than those of learners who defended it. *Instrument:* the position, coded, against the stripping record's specificity, coded independently. **If the arguing minority produces weaker records, the design's decision to assess the sentence rather than the position is wrong**, and the session should teach a rule instead.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That detail redirects a conversation, that finish suppresses rejection, and that these are separate mechanisms with separate countermeasures.

**What the evidence licenses.** A mechanism argument from a source that cites nothing, plus whatever your own paired comparison produced. **Direction and mechanism only. No magnitude is asserted and none exists.**

**The concession worth remembering.** The rule was enforced by the price of polish and the price is gone. This paper repairs the enforcement by running a comparison instead of asserting a rule, and it does not claim the repair works.
