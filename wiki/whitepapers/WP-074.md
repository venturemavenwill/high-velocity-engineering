> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Authoritative register** | A record declared to be the source of truth for a fact, such that other systems derive their behaviour from its state rather than from anyone's report of it |
> | **Two-party act** | A step requiring two named roles whose interests differ, so that neither can complete it alone |
> | **Residue** | What remains inside an environment after an engagement: data, configuration, credentials, cached artefacts and access |

## In plain terms

This is the best mechanism in the corpus behind this programme, and it is worth stating precisely because most withdrawal procedures are not mechanisms at all.

Every environment is tagged to a project in the organisation's own project register. The register is declared the source of truth for project status. **Marking the project closed is what initiates decommissioning.** Nobody has to remember, nobody has to be chased, and nothing depends on the delivery team's attention at the moment their attention has already moved to the next engagement.

The design has a second property that is easy to read past. **Reusing an environment after project close is named a policy violation rather than an inefficiency**, explicitly because the asset is bound to a project identity. That single sentence forecloses the commonest drift in practice — the team that keeps a perfectly good environment for the next thing, and carries one customer's residue into another customer's engagement. Framed as efficiency, reuse is obviously sensible and will happen. Framed as identity, it is not available.

Two further controls, both of which read as having been written after somebody learned something. Access revocation is a **two-party act**, performed by the project lead together with the departing person's own manager — a design nobody adopts unless single-party revocation has been observed not to happen. And a person's departure is treated as **a security event with consequences across the organisational boundary**, triggering credential rotation inside the customer's estate, performed jointly with the customer.

Then the thing the session exists to say. The trigger is mechanised to the level of a state machine, and **there is no evidentiary requirement that any of it was performed.** No artefact, no record, nothing a person outside the delivery team could find a year later to establish that an environment was actually destroyed. The organisation has automated the firing of the process and left the proof of the process to trust.

And the larger absence, which the whole stage is built on: this mechanism answers *is our house clean?* It does not answer *can they run it without us?*

## Abstract

This paper teaches a documented withdrawal mechanism and then names its limits. The mechanism binds decommissioning to a state change in an authoritative project register, treats environment reuse after project close as a policy violation on identity grounds, makes access revocation a two-party act, and treats a person's departure as a cross-boundary security event requiring joint credential rotation inside the customer's estate. The paper argues that the design's virtue is the removal of human memory from the trigger, and that its two defects are the absence of any evidentiary requirement and the absence of any transfer step. The session adds an evidence artefact per step, which is this programme's addition and carries no external warrant. The paper concedes that the mechanism is reported with no outcome data of any kind — no incident prevented, no environment caught, no compliance measure, no case study — and that teaching it as good practice rests on its design properties rather than on evidence that it works.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Withdrawal fails because it competes with the next engagement, and it loses.

At the moment an engagement ends, the delivery team's attention has already moved. Every remaining act — destroying an environment, revoking access, rotating a credential inside somebody else's estate — is unrewarded, invisible when done and invisible when skipped. Whatever depends on the team remembering will be done unevenly and will be believed to have been done evenly.

The corpus's answer is structurally right: take the trigger out of the team's hands and bind it to a state somebody else changes for their own reasons. **A project is marked closed because a project has closed, not because somebody is thinking about environments.**

The session's job is to teach that shape, and then to make the learner notice what it does not include, in the same hour, before it has become a model they admire.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session assumes the security work of Stage 6 — a threat model bound to components and flows, and an unmitigated-risk list that survived rather than being closed — and the cost work of Stage 7, since environments are where cost accumulates after attention leaves.

It also assumes the manifest from [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md), because the specification produced here attaches to it: the trigger fires the teardown of the delivery organisation's assets and must not fire the removal of anything that has been transferred.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why a register state rather than a decision.** A decision is an event inside a person. It is unobservable, undatable and unauditable, and its absence is indistinguishable from its postponement. A register field is none of those things: it has a value, a timestamp and an actor, and its state can be read by a system that has no relationship with the delivery team at all. **This is the whole design and it is a control-engineering argument rather than an empirical one.**

**3.2 Why identity rather than efficiency.** Binding an asset to a project identity changes the category of reuse from a trade-off to a prohibition. Trade-offs are decided locally under pressure by people with good reasons; prohibitions are not decided at all. The corpus's own framing — policy violation, not inefficiency — is what makes this work, and it is worth teaching as a general move: **where a drift is individually rational and collectively harmful, the countermeasure is usually a category change rather than a stronger argument.**

**3.3 Why two parties.** A single-party act competes with that party's other work. A two-party act with a manager on the second side attaches the step to somebody whose accountability is the person rather than the project, which is a different pressure. That a serious organisation designed it this way is evidence that single-party revocation was observed to fail; **it is not evidence that the two-party form succeeds**, and the corpus reports no compliance data.

**3.4 Why the boundary matters.** A departing engineer's access does not stop at the employer's edge. Credentials issued inside the customer's estate are outside the delivery organisation's control and inside its exposure, and rotating them requires a customer-side party. Treating departure as a security event rather than an administrative one is what makes the cross-boundary step happen at all.

**3.5 Why this programme adds evidence.** A step that leaves no artefact cannot later be shown to have occurred, and the class of thing most likely to be skipped is the class nobody can check. This is an argument from auditability and **it has no external warrant**; the corpus does not require evidence, and no source behind this programme measures whether requiring it changes compliance.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**The session teaches admiration and then breaks it, in that order and within the same hour.** The mechanism is genuinely good and learners should feel that; a design taught with its criticism attached from the first sentence is a design nobody learns. But the interval is short deliberately, because the failure mode this stage exists to prevent is a team executing an excellent hygiene procedure and believing it has performed a handover.

**The three traps in the supplied situation are the session's real content.**

The untagged environment is the important one. A register-driven trigger fires reliably on tagged assets and silently on untagged ones, and **silent partial coverage is worse than a mechanism nobody trusts**, because it produces confidence proportional to the tagged fraction. The learner has to say what they do about an asset the mechanism cannot see.

The personally issued credential inside the customer's estate forces the boundary act to be designed for a party — a managed-service supplier — who attended none of the engagement and has no relationship with the departing engineer.

The evidence column forces the learner to justify an addition rather than assert it, which is the discipline this programme applies to itself.

**The review movement makes a learner play the register**, changing state and then doing nothing at all. What stalls is what the specification failed to mechanise, and it is discovered by the learner rather than told to them.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Admiration without the criticism.** The mechanism is memorable and the absence is not. *Countermeasure:* the limits movement, and the placement of the transfer half of the stage on both sides of this session.

**The trigger that is an intention.** The specification's first step is performed by the delivery team. *Countermeasure:* the register role-play, which does nothing the specification did not mechanise.

**The tagged-only specification.** The learner writes a clean mechanism and does not address the untagged environment, because it is outside the design. *Countermeasure:* the session states in advance that all three traps exist, which reduces the exercise to a search but preserves the reasoning.

**Evidence as a column of ticks.** The learner adds an evidence field and populates it with "confirmed". *Countermeasure:* the standard requires the artefact to be findable by somebody outside the delivery team, and a tick in the team's own tracker is not.

**Transfer removed by the trigger.** A learner writes a teardown that destroys something transferred. *Countermeasure:* the manifest, which the specification must attach to. **This countermeasure depends on the learner consulting the manifest and there is no detector for one who does not.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the mechanism is taught as good practice on the basis of its design, and the corpus reports no outcome for it whatever.**

At its strongest: this programme's central criticism of its own source is that the source documents practice without evidence while insisting that only measurable benefit counts. Teaching this mechanism approvingly repeats the error inside the programme that named it. There is no reported incident it prevented, no environment it caught, no compliance rate, no case in which it was applied, and no comparison with any alternative. The two-party revocation is defended above by inferring that somebody must have learned something — which is a story about a document, not a finding.

**This is conceded without qualification.** The session teaches the mechanism as a specification one organisation publishes internally, and the page says so in those words. The rebuttal is confined to the shape: removing human memory from a trigger is a control-engineering argument that does not require outcome data to be evaluated, in the way that a claim about how much better it works would. **The fix that was considered and declined** was to teach the mechanism as a specimen for criticism only, without recommending it; that was rejected because the shape is genuinely transferable and a programme that refuses to recommend anything unmeasured would recommend nothing. The rejection is a compromise and the objection is right that it is one.

**Objection 2: binding withdrawal to a register is bureaucracy that fails in the way all bureaucracy fails — people stop updating the register.**

At its strongest: the mechanism moves the dependency from memory to data hygiene, and data hygiene decays. A project register that nobody closes is exactly as ineffective as a team that nobody reminds, with the added harm that everybody believes the mechanism is working. The supplied situation's untagged environment is the objection in miniature, and it is in the brief because it is the normal case rather than an edge one.

The rebuttal is partial and should be read as partial. The register is maintained for reasons unrelated to environments — status reporting, commercials, staffing — so it decays more slowly than a record kept only for withdrawal. **That is a real asymmetry and it is not a solution.** A mechanism whose coverage equals its tagging discipline needs a coverage measure, the corpus supplies none, and this session's evidence requirement is an attempt to make coverage visible rather than a fix for it.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will produce a first-draft trigger whose first step is performed by their own team. *Instrument:* the specification's first step, coded by actor as *delivery team* / *register* / *other party*.

**Prediction 2.** The untagged environment will be missed more often than the personally issued credential. *Instrument:* presence or absence of each of the three traps in the submitted specification, tallied per learner.

**Prediction 3.** Evidence artefacts will more often be located inside the delivery team's own tooling than in a place a customer-side party could reach. *Instrument:* the evidence column, coded by location as *delivery-side* / *customer-side* / *shared* / *unstated*.

**Prediction 4.** After the register role-play, learners will add steps rather than remove them, and the added steps will name external parties. *Instrument:* the diff between pre-role-play and post-role-play specifications, with added steps coded by actor. **If learners instead add steps performed by themselves, the role-play has taught thoroughness rather than mechanisation** and should be redesigned.

**None of these has been measured.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a withdrawal trigger bound to a register state fires without anyone remembering; that binding an asset to a project identity forecloses reuse in a way an efficiency argument cannot; and that a specified step leaving no artefact cannot later be shown to have happened.

**What the evidence licenses.** That one organisation specifies these controls is verified. **That they work is not**, and nothing in the corpus reports an outcome for any of them.

**The concession worth remembering.** A team can execute all of this to a high standard and leave behind a system nobody can operate. The mechanism is excellent and it is answering the smaller question.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That every environment is tagged to a project in the organisation's project register; that the register is declared the source of truth for project status; that marking a project closed is what initiates decommissioning; that reuse of an environment after project close is named a policy violation rather than an inefficiency, on the grounds that the asset is bound to a project identity; that access revocation is a two-party act performed by the project lead jointly with the departing person's own manager; and that a person's departure is treated as a security event triggering credential rotation inside the customer's estate, performed jointly with the customer. Also verified: that no evidentiary requirement exists that any teardown was performed, and that the corpus contains no transfer of the running system, its definitions, its pipelines, its runbooks, its harness, its cost model or its monitoring, no named receiving owner, no exit criteria, no acceptance and no support taper. All recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md), which rests on a complete reading. **The corpus reports no outcome for any of these controls**, and that absence is verified too.

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That a trigger depending on human recall is performed unevenly and believed to have been performed evenly; that a prohibition framed as a category is more robust under local pressure than a trade-off framed as efficiency; and that residue in a reused environment can cross between customers. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The requirement that every step name an evidence artefact findable by somebody outside the delivery team; the treatment of silent partial coverage as worse than an untrusted mechanism; the register role-play as the session's review instrument; the requirement that the trigger attach to the manifest so that transferred items are not destroyed; the generalisation in §3.2 about category change as a countermeasure to individually rational drift; and all four predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** **This session has no platform anchor and relies on no vendor documentation.** That is a deliberate choice with a cost: every platform provides resource tagging, lifecycle policy and identity governance surfaces that could carry this mechanism, and a learner will have to map the specification onto whichever they meet. The choice is made because the mechanism's content is the binding of a trigger to an authoritative register, which is a property of an organisation's records rather than of a cloud provider's features, and an anchored session would teach the features. **The silence worth recording:** platform documentation known to this programme describes how to tag a resource, how to expire a credential and how to apply a lifecycle rule, and does not describe how an organisation establishes which register is authoritative for project status, who is permitted to change it, or what evidence a completed decommissioning should leave. Those are the three things this session is about, and no vendor publishes them. If one does, this session acquires an anchor and this paper records its version and its remaining omissions.
