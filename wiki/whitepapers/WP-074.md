> ## Vocabulary used here without definition
>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|
> | **Authoritative register** | A record declared to be the source of truth for a fact, such that other systems derive their behaviour from its state rather than from anyone's report of it |
> | **Two-party act** | A step requiring two named roles whose interests differ, so that neither can complete it alone |
> | **Residue** | What remains inside an environment after an engagement: data, configuration, credentials, cached artefacts and access |
> | **Posture estimate** | What a bounded, sampled, delayed scan produces. Distinct from an inventory, and routinely read as one |
> | **Oversharing** | Content that is over-permissioned, obsolete or ungoverned — latent until a retrieval system surfaces it |
> | **AI bill of materials** | The discovered inventory of application components, data and AI artefacts across an estate |
> | **Toxic combination** | Individually acceptable configurations that compose into a breach |
> | **Coverage statement** | This design's requirement: what the instrument looked at, on what cadence, with what caps, and what it does not support |

## In plain terms

This is the best mechanism in the corpus behind this programme, and it is worth stating precisely because most withdrawal procedures are not mechanisms at all.

Every environment is tagged to a project in the organisation's own project register. The register is declared the source of truth for project status. **Marking the project closed is what initiates decommissioning.** Nobody has to remember, nobody has to be chased, and nothing depends on the delivery team's attention at the moment their attention has already moved to the next engagement.

The design has a second property that is easy to read past. **Reusing an environment after project close is named a policy violation rather than an inefficiency**, explicitly because the asset is bound to a project identity. That single sentence forecloses the commonest drift in practice — the team that keeps a perfectly good environment for the next thing, and carries one customer's residue into another customer's engagement. Framed as efficiency, reuse is obviously sensible and will happen. Framed as identity, it is not available.

Two further controls, both of which read as having been written after somebody learned something. Access revocation is a **two-party act**, performed by the project lead together with the departing person's own manager — a design nobody adopts unless single-party revocation has been observed not to happen. And a person's departure is treated as **a security event with consequences across the organisational boundary**, triggering credential rotation inside the customer's estate, performed jointly with the customer.

Then the three things the session exists to say.

**There is no evidentiary requirement that any of it was performed.** No artefact, no record, nothing a person outside the delivery team could find a year later to establish that an environment was destroyed. The organisation has automated the firing of the process and left the proof of it to trust.

**A register can only fire on what somebody registered.** The environment created before tagging was enforced, the subscription spun up during an incident, the service principal made on a Tuesday — none is in the register, none will ever be triggered, and all will still be running in a year. This is not a hygiene problem; it is an epistemic one, and the repair is an instrument that looks at the estate rather than at the record.

**And the instrument has a resolution.** Posture tooling that discovers what is actually running is a genuine advance over a register and is still a bounded, sampled, delayed, capped observation. **It produces a posture estimate, not an inventory** — and reading its output as coverage is the specific error this session exists to prevent, because it is the error a register-shaped mind makes when handed a better register.

Underneath all of it, the larger absence the stage is built on: this mechanism answers *is our house clean?* It does not answer *can they run it without us?*

## Abstract

This paper teaches a documented withdrawal mechanism, names its two defects, supplies the instrument that addresses one of them, and then names that instrument's limits.

The argument runs in four parts. **First**, the mechanism binds decommissioning to a state change in an authoritative project register, treats environment reuse after project close as a policy violation on identity grounds, makes access revocation a two-party act, and treats a person's departure as a cross-boundary security event; its virtue is the removal of human memory from the trigger, which is a control-engineering argument rather than an empirical one. **Second**, its two defects are the absence of any evidentiary requirement and the absence of any transfer step, and the reasons nobody notices are organisational: the complacency literature names narrow functional goals, internal control systems that make every function meet its targets, and feedback arriving only through those systems — which is a description of a register reporting on itself. **Third**, first-party posture tooling addresses the coverage defect by discovering what is present rather than what was recorded, and supplies a durable claim in the process: deploying a capable retrieval system converts every dormant permissions error in an organisation into a live one. **Fourth**, that tooling samples a bounded set of the busiest locations on a delayed cadence with capped depth, at least one unsupported storage surface, expiring results, and a licence boundary that moves — so a scan bounds what may be claimed and does not establish absence.

The session produces a five-part withdrawal trigger specification whose fourth and fifth parts — an evidence artefact per step and a written discovery gap — are this programme's additions and carry no external warrant.

The paper concedes that the mechanism is reported with no outcome data of any kind; that the coverage of a register equals its tagging discipline and no source supplies a coverage measure; that **no detection rate or false-positive rate is published for any posture capability named here and none is asserted**; and that a team can execute all of it to a high standard and leave behind a system nobody can operate.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Withdrawal fails because it competes with the next engagement, and it loses.

At the moment an engagement ends, the delivery team's attention has already moved. Every remaining act — destroying an environment, revoking access, rotating a credential inside somebody else's estate — is unrewarded, invisible when done and invisible when skipped. **Whatever depends on the team remembering will be done unevenly and will be believed to have been done evenly.**

The corpus's answer is structurally right: take the trigger out of the team's hands and bind it to a state somebody else changes for their own reasons. A project is marked closed because a project has closed, not because somebody is thinking about environments.

But there is a second failure underneath, and it is the one that survives a good mechanism. **The register is an inventory of what somebody remembered to record**, and an organisation reading it will conclude the estate is clean. The conclusion will be sincere, internally consistent, and wrong by an amount nobody can state.

The session's job is to teach the shape, make the learner notice both absences in the same hour, and then hand them an instrument that finds part of what the register misses — while making them write down what the instrument misses too.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session assumes the security work of Stage 6 — a threat model bound to components and flows, and an unmitigated-risk list that survived rather than being closed — and the cost work of Stage 7, since environments are where cost accumulates after attention leaves.

It also assumes the manifest from [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md), because the specification produced here attaches to it: **the trigger fires the teardown of the delivery organisation's assets and must not fire the removal of anything that has been transferred.**

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why a register state rather than a decision.** A decision is an event inside a person. It is unobservable, undatable and unauditable, and its absence is indistinguishable from its postponement. A register field is none of those things: it has a value, a timestamp and an actor, and its state can be read by a system that has no relationship with the delivery team at all. **This is the whole design and it is a control-engineering argument rather than an empirical one.**

**3.2 Why identity rather than efficiency.** Binding an asset to a project identity changes the category of reuse from a trade-off to a prohibition. Trade-offs are decided locally under pressure by people with good reasons; prohibitions are not decided at all. The corpus's own framing — policy violation, not inefficiency — is what makes this work, and it generalises: **where a drift is individually rational and collectively harmful, the countermeasure is usually a category change rather than a stronger argument.**

**3.3 Why two parties.** A single-party act competes with that party's other work. A two-party act with a manager on the second side attaches the step to somebody whose accountability is the person rather than the project, which is a different pressure. That a serious organisation designed it this way is evidence that single-party revocation was observed to fail; **it is not evidence that the two-party form succeeds**, and the corpus reports no compliance data.

**3.4 Why the boundary matters.** A departing engineer's access does not stop at the employer's edge. Credentials issued inside the customer's estate are outside the delivery organisation's control and inside its exposure, and rotating them requires a customer-side party. Treating departure as a security event rather than an administrative one is what makes the cross-boundary step happen at all.

**3.5 Why nobody notices that the register is incomplete.**

The organisational half of this session comes from the change literature, and its diagnosis is unhedged. Complacency is the first named error, and: "This error is fatal because transformations always fail to achieve their objectives when complacency levels are high" ([research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md](/research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md)).

**Three of the named sources of complacency are measurement failures and describe an untriggered register exactly.** Narrow functional goals, where each function meets its own targets and nobody owns the whole. Internal planning and control systems "rigged to make it easy for everyone to meet their functional goals". And performance feedback arriving only through those internal systems, so that the organisation's picture of itself is assembled entirely from its own instruments. **A register is an internal system reporting on itself**: it says every registered project is in a valid state, which is true and is silent about everything unregistered.

The fourth is the sharpest and predicts what happens to the learner who runs the workflow: hostility to people who collect external data — "when enterprising young employees went out of their way to collect external performance feedback, they were often treated like lepers." **A discovery scan is external feedback about an internal system, and the person producing it should expect the reception that carries.**

**Limits, and they are substantial.** This is practitioner counsel whose own preface records that the author drew no examples or major ideas from any published source except his own writing and did not try to cite evidence to bolster his conclusions. Every case is anonymised and untraceable. Every figure is stated without method — including a leadership-to-management split derived by inspecting two of his own diagrams, and an urgency scale invented in the sentence that uses it. **None is reproduced here or anywhere in this repository.** One correction travels with the source and must not be dropped: an earlier version of this repository's note claimed the author insists on honest confrontation rather than manufactured crisis, and **the book says the opposite** — it endorses engineered crises with approving examples. That position is not this programme's and is not taught, but a reader citing him for the complacency taxonomy should know what else is in the chapter.

**3.6 Why an instrument that looks at the estate is a different kind of object.**

Posture tooling divides the problem in a way worth teaching for its own sake. Data-side posture management governs what sensitive information flows into and out of AI tools; workload-side posture management governs which AI applications exist in the estate at all ([research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md)). **The fact that these require separate products is itself the lesson**: *what is our data doing* and *what do we even have* are different questions with different instruments.

The strongest reasoning on the data side is the oversharing argument, stated precisely: "Because of the power and speed AI can proactively surface content that might be obsolete, over-permissioned, or lacking governance controls, generative AI amplifies the problem of oversharing data." **Oversharing is not new; what is new is that something now reads everything.** Over-permissioned libraries caused little damage for two decades because nobody read them. **Deploying a capable retrieval system converts every dormant permissions error in an organisation into a live one**, on the day it is switched on. That claim generalises past this product and is the durable content of this half of the session.

The remediation structure — Identify, Protect, Monitor — is worth borrowing regardless of tooling, and one of its actions states the governance dilemma in a sentence: removing a sharing link is flagged as "an action to be used sparingly because it could prevent people from accessing the item legitimately." **The safest action is the most disruptive, and somebody has to decide.**

On the workload side, the core artefact is an **AI bill of materials** — "application components, data, and AI artifacts from code to cloud" — discovered automatically and continuously across a named set of first-party and third-party model services. **Attack path analysis** targets "toxic combinations", with the specific concern that "data might be exposed during the grounding of AI models to specific data and the fine-tuning of a pretrained model on a specific dataset." **Grounding and fine-tuning are the two moments a model touches proprietary data**, and therefore the two moments it can leak by construction rather than by attack. The threat list also names a **wallet attack** — driving cost rather than downtime — which follows directly from usage-based billing and links back to [S072](/wiki/seminars/S072.md)'s quota mechanics.

**3.7 Why a scan is a posture estimate and not an inventory.**

This is the session's most important platform teaching and it comes from the documentation's own candour about its operating envelope.

**The default assessment runs on a schedule against a bounded number of the busiest locations rather than against all of them** — a sample chosen by usage, not a census. **Item-level scanning is capped at a small number of locations and is unsupported for one major storage surface.** There are **caps on items per location, above which counts are documented as possibly inaccurate.** Results arrive after a **latency measured in days**, custom results **expire**, and a newly configured policy produces nothing for an initial period.

**Read those as the resolution of the instrument, not as fine print.** A sampled, capped, delayed scan is far better than a register and is not an inventory.

**This paper states those limits qualitatively and reproduces no figure from any of them.** The reason is the rule that governs every magnitude in this wiki: the specific caps and cadences are version-bound facts about one product at one moment, and a learner who memorises them has memorised something that will be wrong before they use it. **What survives is the shape: bounded sample, capped depth, delayed and expiring results, one surface not covered.**

Two structural facts complete it. The page this repository read is explicitly labelled **classic** and states it has been replaced by a version whose improvements will not be back-ported. And **agent-level discovery moves behind a separate licence on a stated date**, after which the general posture product continues to discover accounts and projects but not agents. **A governance design that assumes a capability is permanent fails silently when the licence moves.**

One default is worth memorising because it is a trap rather than a limit: **without an explicit policy, some first-party AI experiences audit that an interaction happened and do not capture the prompts and responses.** Knowing an interaction occurred is not knowing what was said.

**The documentation's evidence base.** First-party, definitional, describing capabilities and stating operational limits — which is unusually useful. **It provides no detection rates, no false-positive rates, no measurement of how much oversharing exists in a typical estate, and no evaluation of any control's effectiveness.** The page read is superseded by a version not read; the workload-side material was read through search excerpts rather than a full page fetch, so its structure is reconstructed.

**3.8 Why this programme adds evidence, and the one literature that supports it.**

A step that leaves no artefact cannot later be shown to have occurred, and the class of thing most likely to be skipped is the class nobody can check. That is an argument from auditability and it has no external warrant in the corpus.

The nearest supporting literature is about lineage rather than teardown, and the analogy is exact. **Lineage is what makes a model reproducible, comparable across runs, and deletable on request**, and recording artefacts, executions and contexts turns provenance questions into queries rather than investigations ([research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md)). **A deletion request may force a retrain**, which makes lineage a precondition for meeting a legal obligation rather than an engineering nicety — and lineage that was not recorded cannot be reconstructed later. **A teardown with no artefact per step is the same failure with the same non-repair.** The same source supplies the cheapest control in the stage: collect less.

**Its limits:** a practitioner survey from a commercial publisher, not research; no comparative evidence for any recommended practice; the toolchain is the authors' employer's; its legal content is jurisdiction- and date-bound and is not legal advice; every number is a property of a particular system and none is reproduced here.

**3.9 The pedagogical frame.** The opening items are an unscaffolded generation phase, and exercise 3 is a comparison instruction rather than an invitation, because side-by-side presentation without an instruction to compare has been shown to achieve nothing ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). **School, university and laboratory evidence; no adult-professional study in its reference list; no magnitude asserted.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**4.1 The session teaches admiration and then breaks it, in that order and within the same hour.** The mechanism is genuinely good and learners should feel that; a design taught with its criticism attached from the first sentence is a design nobody learns. But the interval is short deliberately, because the failure this stage exists to prevent is a team executing an excellent hygiene procedure and believing it has performed a handover.

**4.2 The three traps in the supplied situation are the session's real content.**

The untagged environment is the important one. A register-driven trigger fires reliably on tagged assets and silently on untagged ones, and **silent partial coverage is worse than a mechanism nobody trusts**, because it produces confidence proportional to the tagged fraction. The learner has to say what they do about an asset the mechanism cannot see — which is what the platform workflow then answers, partially.

The personally issued credential inside the customer's estate forces the boundary act to be designed for a party — a managed-service supplier — who attended none of the engagement and has no relationship with the departing engineer.

The evidence column forces the learner to justify an addition rather than assert it, which is the discipline this programme applies to itself.

**4.3 The workflow is ordered so that the register-derived list is committed before the scan runs.** Step 1 before step 2, explicitly, because a list written after a scan is a transcription and teaches nothing. **What the workflow makes observable is the diff**, and a diff is a fact about the estate that cannot be argued with.

**4.4 Step 4 is the session, and it is the step most likely to be skipped.** Writing down what the discovery did not cover requires reading the tooling's operating envelope rather than its output, which is slower and less satisfying than reading a result. A learner who produces a diff and stops has produced a list. **A learner who produces a diff plus a coverage statement has produced a finding with a known resolution**, and knowing the resolution of your instrument is the whole of the measurement strand.

There is a second-order observation available at that point and it is the one worth carrying: the learner holds a list of unclaimed assets *and* a statement that the instrument which found them looked at a sample. **Both lists are shorter than the truth, and knowing by how much is impossible.**

**4.5 The review movement makes a learner play the register**, changing state and then doing nothing at all. What stalls is what the specification failed to mechanise, and it is discovered by the learner rather than told to them.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Admiration without the criticism.** The mechanism is memorable and the absences are not. *Countermeasure:* the limits movement, and the placement of the transfer half of the stage on both sides of this session.

**The register read as an inventory.** *Countermeasure:* the step-1-before-step-2 ordering, which makes the diff the learner's own finding rather than an assertion on a page.

**The scan read as coverage.** The learner treats a discovery result as a census, which is the register error committed against a better register. *Countermeasure:* step 4 and the specification's `discovery_gap` block. **This is a documentation countermeasure against a cognitive habit**, and §9's second prediction is about its failure.

**The trigger that is an intention.** The specification's first step is performed by the delivery team. *Countermeasure:* the register role-play, which does nothing the specification did not mechanise.

**The tagged-only specification.** The learner writes a clean mechanism and does not address the untagged environment, because it is outside the design. *Countermeasure:* the session states in advance that all three traps exist, which reduces the exercise to a search but preserves the reasoning.

**Evidence as a column of ticks.** The learner adds an evidence field and populates it with *confirmed*. *Countermeasure:* the standard requires the artefact to be findable by somebody outside the delivery team, and a tick in the team's own tracker is not.

**Transfer removed by the trigger.** A learner writes a teardown that destroys something transferred. *Countermeasure:* the manifest, which the specification must attach to. **This countermeasure depends on the learner consulting the manifest and there is no detector for one who does not.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the mechanism is taught as good practice on the basis of its design, and the corpus reports no outcome for it whatever.**

At its strongest: this programme's central criticism of its own source is that the source documents practice without evidence while insisting that only measurable benefit counts. Teaching this mechanism approvingly repeats the error inside the programme that named it. There is no reported incident it prevented, no environment it caught, no compliance rate, no case in which it was applied, and no comparison with any alternative. The two-party revocation is defended above by inferring that somebody must have learned something — which is a story about a document, not a finding.

**This is conceded without qualification.** The session teaches the mechanism as a specification one organisation publishes internally, and the page says so in those words. The rebuttal is confined to the shape: removing human memory from a trigger is a control-engineering argument that does not require outcome data to be evaluated, in the way a claim about *how much better* it works would. **The declined fix was to teach the mechanism as a specimen for criticism only, without recommending it**; that was rejected because the shape is genuinely transferable and a programme that refuses to recommend anything unmeasured would recommend nothing. The rejection is a compromise and the objection is right that it is one.

**Objection 2: binding withdrawal to a register is bureaucracy that fails in the way all bureaucracy fails — people stop updating the register.**

At its strongest: the mechanism moves the dependency from memory to data hygiene, and data hygiene decays. A project register that nobody closes is exactly as ineffective as a team that nobody reminds, with the added harm that everybody believes the mechanism is working. The supplied situation's untagged environment is the objection in miniature, and it is in the brief because it is the normal case rather than an edge one.

The rebuttal is partial and should be read as partial. The register is maintained for reasons unrelated to environments — status reporting, commercials, staffing — so it decays more slowly than a record kept only for withdrawal. **That is a real asymmetry and it is not a solution.** **The declined fix was to require a periodic manual reconciliation**, which was rejected because a manual reconciliation is exactly the memory-dependent step the mechanism exists to remove, and would decay identically. The workflow's discovery scan is the replacement, and §8's fourth objection says what that costs.

**Objection 3: adding a discovery scan replaces one instrument's blind spot with another's, and the session dresses that up as progress.**

At its strongest: the register misses what nobody tagged. The scan misses what falls outside its sample, below its cadence, above its caps, or on its unsupported surface — and unlike the register's blind spot, the scan's is invisible to the person using it, because a scan result *looks* like an enumeration. A learner who runs both and finds nothing will be more confident than a learner who ran neither, and the confidence is unearned. **The session may therefore have made its learners worse calibrated while making them better informed.**

**Conceded, and it is the sharpest objection to the session's platform half.** The mitigation is the entire content of step 4 and the `discovery_gap` block: the learner is required to state the scan's own reported scope, its caps and its unsupported surfaces, and to write the sentence that absence from register and scan alike is not absence from the estate. **The declined fix was to drop the scan and teach the register's incompleteness as an unmeasurable structural fact**, which was rejected because an unmeasurable fact produces no action and the diff produces a list of real unclaimed assets. The trade is a real list against a real risk of over-confidence, and a reader who weighs the second more heavily is making a defensible call.

**Objection 4: the evidence column is a documentation requirement invented by this programme, and documentation requirements are the classic response of people who cannot change behaviour.**

At its strongest: requiring an artefact per step adds work to the least-rewarded moment of an engagement, will be satisfied with the cheapest thing that looks like an artefact, and creates a second thing to decay. The corpus's authors are competent people who did not include it, and the programme's confidence that it knows better than an organisation with incident experience is not obviously earned.

**Partially conceded.** The failure mode named — an evidence column of ticks — is predicted in §5 and the standard's response is thin. The defence is the lineage analogy in §3.8: a record that was not made at the time cannot be reconstructed later, and the question *did this happen* is asked precisely when nobody is available to answer it. **The declined fix was to require evidence only for the cross-boundary steps**, where the question is most likely to be asked by a regulator or a customer; it was declined because a partial requirement teaches that the others do not matter, and the untagged-environment case shows that the cheap steps are the ones that silently do not happen. **This paper cannot show that the addition helps, and §9's third prediction is the closest available test.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most learners will produce a first-draft trigger whose first step is performed by their own team. *Instrument:* the specification's first step, coded by actor as *delivery team* / *register* / *other party*.

**Prediction 2.** A majority of learners will submit a discovery gap that lists what the scan found rather than what it looked at, **despite step 4 stating the requirement explicitly.** *Instrument:* the `discovery_gap` block, coded *scope stated* / *results restated* / *absent*. **This predicts the failure of the session's own countermeasure**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption.

**Prediction 3.** Evidence artefacts will more often be located inside the delivery team's own tooling than in a place a customer-side party could reach. *Instrument:* the evidence column, coded by location as *delivery-side* / *customer-side* / *shared* / *unstated*.

**Prediction 4.** The untagged environment will be missed more often than the personally issued credential. *Instrument:* presence or absence of each of the three traps in the submitted specification, tallied per learner.

**Prediction 5.** The step-1 register list will be shorter than the step-2 scan result for most learners, and the difference will be larger for learners whose engagement ran longer. *Instrument:* item counts from both steps, against engagement duration. **If the register lists are consistently complete, this session's central claim about registers does not hold for this cohort** and the material should move weight to the evidence requirement.

**Prediction 6.** After the register role-play, learners will add steps rather than remove them, and the added steps will name external parties. *Instrument:* the diff between pre-role-play and post-role-play specifications, with added steps coded by actor. **If learners instead add steps performed by themselves, the role-play has taught thoroughness rather than mechanisation** and should be redesigned.

**None of these has been measured.** No cohort has run this session.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a withdrawal trigger bound to a register state fires without anyone remembering; that binding an asset to a project identity forecloses reuse in a way an efficiency argument cannot; that a specified step leaving no artefact cannot later be shown to have happened; and that a register reports on what was recorded while a scan reports on a bounded sample, so neither tells you what exists.

**What the evidence licenses.** That one organisation specifies these controls is verified. **That they work is not**, and the corpus reports no outcome for any of them. That posture tooling discovers AI workloads across an estate, and what its operating envelope is, is first-party documentation at a moment in time — and **no detection rate, no false-positive rate and no measurement of how much oversharing exists is published anywhere in it. Assert none.**

**The claim worth carrying past every product name here** is the oversharing one: switching on a capable retrieval system converts every dormant permissions error in an organisation into a live one. That survives the tooling entirely.

**The concession worth remembering.** A team can execute all of this to a high standard and leave behind a system nobody can operate. The mechanism is excellent and it is answering the smaller question. And §8's third objection is the one to hold: running two bounded searches and finding nothing is not the same as there being nothing.

The documentation also describes how to tag a resource, expire a credential and apply a lifecycle rule, and **does not describe how an organisation establishes which register is authoritative for project status, who is permitted to change it, or what evidence a completed decommissioning should leave.** Those three are what this session is about, and no vendor publishes them.
