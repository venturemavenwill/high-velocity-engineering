# WP-074 — Tool Failure Has Four Shapes

**Seminar day:** [S074](wiki/seminars/S074.md) · **Module:** [M15](wiki/modules/M15.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](wiki/quarters/Q8.md)
**Strands:** IS · ME · SY · **Complexity class:** 5
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Agent Framework — the five tool-call evaluators · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends a four-way decomposition of tool failure whose organising criterion is **observational position** rather than stage — a tool can report on what happens inside it and on nothing else — and defends that criterion as the reason the decomposition is durable while everything around it is not. It defends supplying the bridge [WP-068](wiki/whitepapers/WP-068.md) §6 required and stated its own day could not: that a wrong-tool call emits the signal a correct call emits because the party emitting the signal is not the party that failed. It defends a contrasting set drawn from the paths this cohort failed to bound, with a payload that defeats the previous day's frame rather than the previous minute's, and defends running the platform's five tool-usage evaluators over those cases so that the room is required to classify the evaluator itself by observational position before phase 4 defines the criterion. **Section 7 reports the discharge of three rule-6 obligations and of two older forward links, including [WP-033](wiki/whitepapers/WP-033.md) §9's carried outcome-space re-probe.** Section 8 concedes that the day's durable claim has a memorable form which its own scope statement contradicts, records the **third** instance of this design placing a phase 5 instrument downstream of its own instruction — fixed here, and argued for elevation to a rule — and concedes that a taxonomy whose four names now coincide with four shipped evaluator names is easier to acquire as a product feature than as a criterion.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Every student arriving at this day holds a model of failure that has worked for eight quarters: **a failure produces evidence, and the engineering problem is to collect it.** [S027](wiki/seminars/S027.md) decomposed error into sources you can diagnose from data. [S032](wiki/seminars/S032.md) taught reasoning about events you cannot reproduce. [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) formalised detection into four parts and named three ways it fails. Every one of those days improves the collection.

**Two of the four failures in this material produce no evidence anywhere**, and no improvement in collection changes that. [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 3 got as close as its own material allowed: the signal exists, someone is watching, and it is indistinguishable from normal — a discrimination problem rather than a coverage problem. [WP-068](wiki/whitepapers/WP-068.md) §6 then said, in terms, that **S074 must supply the bridge and S068 could not**, because S068 framed the third failure as a property of a signal and the agent case is a property of an answer.

The bridge is one sentence and the room can produce it: **a tool can report on what happens inside it and on nothing else.** It validates its own arguments, so it can report a malformed call. It executes, so it can report an execution failure. It does not see the request, so it cannot know it was the wrong tool. It has already returned, so it cannot know what happened to its answer. **Selection and output utilisation happen outside the tool and no other party in the loop has the job of reporting them.** That is why the signal is indistinguishable from normal: the party emitting the signal is not the party that failed.

Getting the room to that sentence is the day's first problem, and the difficulty is not that it is hard — it is that it is easy to *hear* and easy to convert into a list. **A student who leaves with four labels has acquired a vocabulary. A student who leaves with the criterion can classify a failure in a system whose tools they have never seen**, because the question they ask is *who could have seen this*, and that question does not depend on any tool, framework or protocol. The whole durability of the material rests on which of the two they leave with, and the specification's hard requirement is written accordingly: a classification defended by label rather than by observational position fails.

**The platform has made that problem measurably worse and measurably more teachable at the same time.** The evaluation catalogue in front of these students groups five evaluators under tool usage, and four of them carry this day's four names. A student can now acquire the vocabulary from a product, run it, and receive a number for each shape — including the two the day says nobody is positioned to observe. That is the fastest available route to a label without a criterion, and it is also the sharpest available demonstration of the criterion, because the question *what did this evaluator read* has one answer and the answer is *the record the loop produced*. The day takes the second route deliberately: phase 3 runs the five before phase 4 defines anything, and requires the room to classify the evaluators themselves.

The second problem is that the students have just learned something that will get in the way. [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) spent six hours establishing the boundary of trust — the point at which content the system did not author becomes something it acts on — and it is a genuinely load-bearing object. **Output utilisation failure sits entirely downstream of it.** The value was correct, nothing external was authored, nothing crossed anything, and the system read a rate as a total. The room needs to hold the boundary as necessary and not sufficient, one day after acquiring it, which is why F4 is the payload and why it is placed against the previous day rather than against a general intuition.

The third problem is a professional one with no cognitive difficulty at all. **Output utilisation failure has no name in most organisations**, and consequently no rate. It is ordinary program error — a field read wrong, a null coerced, a unit assumed — occurring where the value's provenance makes it look authoritative. The system trusts its own tool's output more than it trusts its own input, and there is no reason for that ordering. Nobody will have told the students this, because nobody counts it. **This design does not count it either**, and the claim that it is the most frequent shape is stated in the day as a judgement rather than as a finding, because no measurement of it exists anywhere this paper could cite.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Moderate, and the module's most tractable day since [S072](wiki/seminars/S072.md).**

The taxonomy is four items and the criterion is one sentence. The technical work is step 5's discrimination, which is a comparison between two records — genuinely new as an object, and mechanically simple once its form is supplied.

The interactivity is between steps 4, 5 and 6 and it is the day's argument in operational form. Step 4 names the observing party per shape; step 5 designs a discrimination for a shape with none; step 6 declares one shape undiscriminated with its cost. **A student discovers at step 6 that the shape they are cheerfully accepting is one of the two nobody can see** — which is the artefact's most productive moment and is why the three steps are adjacent and in that order.

Management:

- **The capability, the drawing and the crossings are all carried in.** Nothing is scoped on the day.
- **Step 5's discrimination form is worked with the room.**
- **The trace log is indexed and queryable**, so step 2's instances are found rather than constructed.
- **Phase 3's four cases and traces are supplied**, pre-read, with the selection rule disclosed.
- **The four shapes are written up and remain visible**, because a student reasoning with a four-way criterion cannot re-inspect a spoken definition.

**Step 4 is unsupported.** Expertise reversal applies at point 1 for a student who has read a published agent-failure taxonomy; the design's answer is that published taxonomies are stage-based and this one is position-based, and step 4 is where the difference bites.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Diagnosing a wrong run in which every call succeeded,* item 1.
- *Naming who would have been positioned to notice, before the taxonomy exists,* item 3.
- *Enumerating the outcomes of a tool call, four quarters after doing it for a service,* item 2.
- *Cases drawn from the region your own enumeration failed,* phase 3.
- *A payload that defeats yesterday's frame,* F4.
- *Finding one real instance of each shape in a log, or saying which shape you cannot find,* step 2.
- *Writing "none" where none is true,* step 4.
- *Declaring a shape you will not discriminate, with a cost,* step 6.
- *A partner who reads the trace and says what your named party would actually have seen,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *The adversary choosing which shape to induce.* [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s, three weeks later.
- *The trajectory as an evaluation object.* [S075](wiki/seminars/S075.md)'s, tomorrow. Point 8 hands it over by name and stops.
- *Repair.* The day classifies and discriminates; what to do about a shape is a design decision that belongs with the capability, not with the taxonomy, and putting it here would produce a remediation catalogue that the register would classify as semi-durable.
- *Statistical treatment of discrimination thresholds.* [S049](wiki/seminars/S049.md)'s and [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md)'s. Step 5 requires a false-alarm rate and does not require its derivation.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form, and it is carried at point 4 on the design's own criterion.** The decomposition is **safe** as a triage for attributing a failure you can replay against the tool's own logs, and as the design frame for deciding what to discriminate. It is **unsafe** in two named ways. First, **as the claim that exactly two classes are silent**: an input failure whose arguments are valid and wrong is not observable by the tool either, because the schema accepted them, and it presents exactly as a selection failure does — silence is a property of observational position and not of the class label, and a student who quotes the headline form will under-instrument that case systematically. Second, **for a system you cannot replay**, where the trace is the only record and the trace is produced by the loop that failed; there the criterion is unanswerable because the evidence about who saw what does not exist. **A durable claim with an unnamed boundary is exactly what the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md)'s durable classification would otherwise license**, which is why the bounding is stated in the material rather than only here. §8 objection 1 argues that the bounding is nonetheless losing to the headline.

**The platform instance carries its own scope statement, at point 9.** The correspondence between the four shapes and four of the five shipped tool-usage evaluator names is **safe** as an instance — a decomposition arrived at independently by another party, useful for showing that the distinctions are operationally real, and useful for reading a catalogue you did not write. It is **unsafe** in three directions and all three are stated to students. It is **not evidence** for the decomposition: two decompositions agreeing establishes nothing about either, and a student who cites the catalogue as warrant has substituted an appeal for an argument. It is **not an observing party**: an evaluator reads the record, and for a silent shape the record carries no mark, so an evaluator named after a silent shape produces a judgement about an absence. And it is **not calibrated**, because no agreement with human judgement is published for any evaluator in the catalogue; every judged score is uncalibrated and students are required to say so. Step 4's clause requiring, for each *none* with a same-named evaluator, a statement of what that evaluator read, is the artefact element under which a student applying the correspondence outside its range would fail.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **the bridge**.

The room is asked which of [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s three detection failures a wrong-tool call is, and answers correctly and quickly. Then it is asked what would have to be different about the tool's signal for it to be one of the other two, and works out — in the room, aloud, in about ninety seconds — that **nothing about the tool's signal can be different, because the tool is not the party that failed.** That is the day's whole taxonomy, produced by students, from a day five weeks behind them. A claim in that position is stronger than any exposition could make it.

The second mechanism is **F4**, and its force comes from timing. Twenty-four hours earlier the room acquired the boundary of trust and was told it is a place. F4 has no place to mark. Nothing was authored by anybody, nothing crossed anything, the tool was right, and the outcome was wrong. The room does not get to keep yesterday's frame as sufficient, and the discomfort of that is the point: **a frame acquired the day before and left unqualified is the frame students carry out of the module.**

The third is phase 6. A tool-error rate under one percent, correct, watched, reported weekly, on the dashboard — and absent from every one of the year's worst outcomes, because the tools succeeded. The detail that carries it is that **the metric was emitted by the platform by default**, which is how most metrics are chosen, and the student disclosure is about correctness rather than about error for that reason.

Autonomy is supported at step 6, where declining to discriminate a shape is a legitimate and assessed output. Competence is supported by the criterion being one sentence and by step 5's form being worked. Relatedness is supplied at phase 7, where a partner reads the trace on the student's behalf.

**The competence risk is at step 2** and it is specific: a student who cannot find an instance of a shape in their own trace log may read that as a failure to search. The instructor states before phase 5 that a shape you cannot find is either absent or invisible, that distinguishing those is the day's method rather than a consolation, and that **the shapes hardest to find in a log are the two the log was never going to contain.**

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items. Item 2 is [WP-033](wiki/whitepapers/WP-033.md) §9's carried re-probe. **Item 3's second clause is the day's headline instrument administered before the answer is taught**, and its provenance is described in §8. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction** required by the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), and it is deliberately about the students' own instrument rather than the substrate's, because that is what the frontier map is about.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals and the bridge [WP-068](wiki/whitepapers/WP-068.md) §6 required. Its two halves are deliberate: the first is a classification the room can do, the second is what S068 could not reach.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four failures, all reporting success, **selected from the paths this cohort did not bound**, with the selection rule and the cohort's own bound errors disclosed per instrumentation rule 3. F4 is the payload and defeats [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md)'s frame. **The five tool-usage evaluators are then run over all four cases**, and the room is required to classify the evaluators themselves by observational position — before phase 4 supplies the criterion, and on the artefact most likely to substitute for it.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Point 2 is the criterion and is stated as a criterion rather than as a list. Point 4 carries the strong-form scope statement on the design's own durable claim. Point 6 is the shape with no external literature, and its incidence claim is marked as a judgement at the point of use. Point 7 is the assessed form. Point 8 hands [S075](wiki/seminars/S075.md) its subject by name. **Points 9 and 10 are the platform layer**, and point 9 is the module's most load-bearing platform point, because it is where naming a shape and observing one are separated on the artefact that conflates them.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps. **Steps 1 and 3 are the receiving elements [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) and [S071](wiki/seminars/S071.md) named.** Step 4 gains the *what did the evaluator read* clause added in §8. Step 5's independence requirement is [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 4 unchanged. **Step 7 builds and runs the discrimination. Step 4 is unsupported.** The artefact gains the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement, the last of which is required to classify the agent's own failure by shape.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A correct metric covering one shape of four, emitted by default — then an agent case in which the taxonomy is applied with a good argument and the boundary case is put in the wrong bin.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** A missed silent failure, and a named observing party checked against what the trace would have shown them.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 2 beside the [S033](wiki/seminars/S033.md) baseline; item 4 against two; **item 3 against step 4 as a movement**; **item 5 against the cohort's own frontier statements**; the [S071](wiki/seminars/S071.md) proposals and [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) crossings sorted by shape; step 7's results; and the pooled undiscriminated shapes with a summed cost.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has read a published agent-failure taxonomy — or who has simply used the platform's evaluator catalogue — will classify the four cases quickly and correctly, because stage-based taxonomies coincide with these shapes on ordinary cases. Predicted failure: **they classify by stage and never acquire the criterion**, and the difference is invisible until they meet a valid-and-wrong input, which stage-based classification puts confidently in the wrong bin. Countermeasures: the specification fails a classification defended by label rather than by observational position; step 4 requires the observing party per shape, which stage-based reasoning does not answer, and now also requires what a same-named evaluator read, which it answers even less; and phase 7's partner attacks the observation claim rather than the classification. **This is adequate for the assessed artefact and, as §8 objection 1 concedes, does not travel with the sentence students will actually remember.**

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is step 2, which requires finding four real instances in a large trace log. Countermeasures: the log is indexed and queryable and the queries for each shape are supplied as worked examples; the *shape I cannot find* route is a correct output; step 5 requires only one discrimination rather than four; and the discrimination's false-alarm rate may be estimated rather than derived, since its derivation is [S049](wiki/seminars/S049.md)'s and [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md)'s.

**Phase 3 succeeds too easily.** F1 and F3 are quick: a neighbouring tool and a stale cache both announce themselves once the trace is read carefully. **The difficulty is F2 against F1** — a valid-and-wrong argument and a wrong-tool call are indistinguishable from the tool's side, which is point 4's boundary case arriving before point 4 exists — **and F4 against everything**, because it has no crossing. A group that separates F1 from F2 *and* explains why the separation is hard has done the day's work in phase 3. **Fallback:** ask which of the four the group's own capability is most likely to produce this term, and require the discrimination they would have to build for it — which forces point 7's comparison form before phase 4 supplies it and which no group produces well on first attempt.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **fatalism**, and this is the day in the module where it peaks. The room has just been told that two of four failure shapes are structurally unobservable, that the metric they would have used covers one shape, and that the record they would consult is produced by the loop that failed. A student can reasonably conclude that agent systems cannot be operated responsibly.

Countermeasures, and the module's whole answer to this risk is concentrated here. **Point 7 is constructive and is the day's assessed object**: four discriminations exist, they are cheap, and each is a comparison between two records that should agree — an intent against a call, an argument against a request, a value against its use. **Step 5 requires the student to build one and step 7 requires them to run it over real data**, so the day ends with an instrument that finds things nothing else on the substrate finds. Phase 3's F2 and F3 are caught by the tool, which demonstrates that the boundary does work for half the space. And the instructor states once, at point 7, that the day's argument is that these systems require a different instrument rather than that they are uninstrumentable — which is the sentence the design has held back from points 1 to 6 precisely so that it lands after the difficulty rather than instead of it.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S075](wiki/seminars/S075.md) at one day (where point 8's problem is the entire subject, and where the unit of judgement is chosen against the shapes it can see), [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md) at two weeks (where an adversary selects a shape to induce, and the two silent ones are the ones worth inducing), [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md) at five weeks (where the shapes the cohort declined to discriminate are what a drift design must reach), [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md) (forensics, where the first question about an incident is which shape it was) and [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md) (the safety case, where a claim of coverage must state which shapes it covers).

**[S075](wiki/seminars/S075.md) at one day is the primary target** and it is instrumented: the step 4 observation-position shift is the instrument, and S075's phase 3 cases are selected against the shape this cohort most often mis-attributed.

**[S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md) at five weeks is the one that matters most and is the one this day cannot guarantee.** A drift design reaching a silent shape is the only mechanism by which a selection or output-utilisation failure becomes visible over time, and it requires the student to still hold the criterion rather than the labels. The link is instrumented — the pooled undiscriminated-shape list responds there — and the instrument measures the cohort's decision rather than its retention, which is a gap and is recorded as one.

Retained content: the four shapes and their positions, the observational-position criterion, the structural silence of selection and output utilisation, the fourth execution outcome, and the discrimination-as-comparison form. Five items.

**One constituent skill is scheduled for blocked practice**, which is unusual in this programme and is justified here: **classification by shape** is exercised four times in phase 3, then three more times inside phase 5 at steps 1, 2 and 3, before any interleaving. The reason is that the classification is a discrimination between four options that are confusable in exactly one direction — stage-based reasoning gives the right answer most of the time — and a skill whose errors are rare and systematic needs blocked repetition to surface them at all. It is interleaved from the between-day sets onward, alongside [S027](wiki/seminars/S027.md)'s error-source attribution and [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s detection-failure classification, which are the two operations it is most confusable with.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 5.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**No entrustment judgement**, per [Q8](wiki/quarters/Q8.md).

One specification item in IS302 with ME302. Per rule 1, the checked elements are every unmarked [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) crossing classified by shape; one instance of each shape from the trace log or a statement of which could not be found and what that means; every unanticipated [S071](wiki/seminars/S071.md) proposal classified by shape; the observing party or an explicit none per shape, with what would have to be added for each none **and, for each none carrying a same-named evaluator, what that evaluator read**; one discrimination with its false-alarm rate and independence argued; the undiscriminated shape with cost, provenance and confidence in the same field; **the built discrimination's results with its misses classified**; both phase 7 outcomes; **the prompt record**; **the intervention record**; and **the frontier statement**. **Nothing else is assessed.**

Three hard requirements: **a named observing party who would not in fact have seen the failure fails**, tested against what the trace would have shown them, **and an evaluator named as an observing party fails under the same condition**; **a discrimination computed from the record it checks fails**; and **a classification defended by label rather than by observational position fails.** A fourth, imported from the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md): **an intervention record with no entries fails** unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error, and on this day a discrimination that step 7 shows finding something over real data is the qualifying demonstration — the cleanest instance of that escape in the module. **No evaluator's output is assessed**, and a judged score reported without the statement that it is uncalibrated fails.

Integrity is observed within the activity, evidenced by whether the named parties would actually have been informed and by whether *none* was written where *none* is true.

**Rule-6 discharge. Three obligations land on this day and all three elements exist.**

- **[S071](wiki/seminars/S071.md)** named this day's phase 5 step 3 for its step 6-against-step 7 prediction accuracy, specifying that every unanticipated shadow proposal be classified by shape. **The element exists as step 3, it is checked, and phase 8 reports the cohort's proposals sorted by shape** — which converts an accuracy number into a statement about *which kind* of thing the cohort's boundaries permitted, and is more than the instrument asked for.
- **[S072](wiki/seminars/S072.md)** named this day's phase 3 for its step 2 path-bound error, specifying that the four cases be drawn from the paths the cohort did not bound. **The element exists: the cases are selected from the region between each student's stated bound and the realised distinct paths, and the selection rule together with the cohort's bound errors is disclosed at the start of the phase**, per instrumentation rule 3.
- **[S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md)** named this day's phase 5 step 1 for its crossing-count error, specifying that every unmarked crossing be classified by the shape it could produce. **The element exists as step 1, it is checked, and phase 8 reports the cohort's unmarked crossings sorted by shape with the lower-bound caveat [WP-073](wiki/whitepapers/WP-073.md) §8 requires.**

**Two older forward links discharged.** [WP-068](wiki/whitepapers/WP-068.md) §6 required this day to supply the bridge from a discrimination problem in a signal to a property of an answer and stated that S068 could not; **that is phase 2's bridge, and it is answered by the room.** [WP-033](wiki/whitepapers/WP-033.md) §6 named this day as its principal target on the claim that a tool call has [S033](wiki/seminars/S033.md)'s three outcomes plus a fourth, and §9 carried its item 1 enumeration completeness here as the outcome-space baseline; **that is point 5 and phase 1 item 2, with both counts reported together at phase 8 and the fourth outcome counted separately.**

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions. **The step 4 observation-position error rate, read as a shift against item 3**, responds at [S075](wiki/seminars/S075.md) phase 3, whose four trajectory evaluations are selected against the shape this cohort most often mis-attributed, with the selection disclosed. **The pooled step 6 undiscriminated-shape list with its summed cost** responds at [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md) phase 5, where each drift design must carry a discrimination for the shape the cohort declined here or record that it still does not and assign the cost an owner. **[WP-075](wiki/whitepapers/WP-075.md) §7 and [WP-079](wiki/whitepapers/WP-079.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

Programme instrumentation: the item 2 enumeration count against the [S033](wiki/seminars/S033.md) baseline; the item 4 dashboard-count error; the item 3 to step 4 observation-position shift; the step 2 shape-not-found distribution; and the pooled undiscriminated-shape cost.

ABET and CS2023 contribution: PO2 and PO6.

**What this day does not license anyone to conclude.** A student's classification is evidence that they can attribute a failure by observational position **on a system they can replay against tool logs**, which is point 4's safe case. It is not evidence about a system whose only record is the trace. **It is not evidence that anything was detected**, since step 7 runs the discrimination over a historical log and finds what is there rather than what is happening. And the day generates no evidence at all about the rate of any shape in any system other than the substrate's, and says so.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day's durable claim has a memorable form that its own scope statement contradicts, and the memorable form is the one that will survive.**

At its strongest: the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) records *the four shapes of tool failure* as **durable** — assessable directly, groundable in a whitepaper claim — on the reasoning that it is a decomposition and not an implementation. [M15](wiki/modules/M15.md) stated the module's central lesson, in the draft this objection was written against, as *two of the four failure classes produce systems that report success and are wrong.* That sentence is short, true-sounding, and the one every student would carry.

**It is false as stated, and point 4 says so.** An input failure whose arguments are valid and wrong is not observable by the tool either — the schema accepted them, so there is nothing for the tool to report — and it presents exactly as a selection failure does. Silence is a property of observational position, not of the class label. So the day's headline is a claim about labels, its correct content is a claim about positions, and **the two disagree precisely on the case that is hardest to instrument.** A student who leaves with *two of four are silent* will under-instrument valid-and-wrong input systematically, and will do it while believing they have applied the taxonomy correctly.

The design's countermeasures are real and they are all **inside the day**: the specification fails classification by label; step 4 asks for the observing party rather than the class; phase 7 attacks the observation claim. None of them travels with the sentence.

**The fix is available and is named.** Rename the four shapes by observational position rather than by stage — *observed by the tool* and *observed by nobody*, subdivided — so that the label carries the criterion and the headline cannot be stated wrongly. It costs nothing but a naming decision.

**It is declined, and the reason is a genuine cost that has doubled since this objection was first written.** The four-term vocabulary — selection, input, execution, output utilisation — is IS302's, and the [Course Catalogue](wiki/program/06-Course-Catalogue.md) names it in those words as the course's own content. **It is now also, name for name, four of the five tool-usage evaluators the platform ships**, which the students run in phase 3 and will meet in every dashboard they are given for the rest of their careers. A seminar day that renamed them would split the vocabulary from the concurrent course that supplies it *and* from the instrument the students operate, in a module that already carries a stated course-allocation gap on the systems strand. **The programme would trade a wrong headline for a cohort holding two incompatible sets of names for one object, one of which is printed on the tool**, which is a worse outcome and is one this design has criticised elsewhere.

**The objection is conceded and is only partly answered.** **Two of the three required corrections are made**: [M15](wiki/modules/M15.md)'s headline now states that the count of silent shapes follows from observational position rather than from the class label, and names the valid-and-wrong input case in the same sentence; [Q8](wiki/quarters/Q8.md)'s summary carries the same qualification. **The third cannot be made here.** The register is a shared page, its entry still reads as a bare decomposition, and the day therefore still ships with a durable claim whose registered form is shorter than its correct one. The required wording is given in §9 so that it can be transcribed. **And the deeper half is unanswered by any of the three**: a paragraph-length claim is not carried out of a room by the people who heard it, however carefully the pages are worded, and the design has no instrument that would tell it which form the cohort left with.

**Objection 2: the day's headline instrument was administered after the day taught its answer — and this is the third recorded instance.**

At its strongest, as it applied to this design's first draft: phase 5 step 4 asks the student to name the party positioned to observe each shape in their own system, and that measure — the observation-position error rate — is the instrument this day carries forward to [S075](wiki/seminars/S075.md). **Phase 4 point 2 has already told them that selection and output utilisation have no such party.** A student who writes *none* for those two shapes has recalled a fifty-minute expository phase. The instrument's error rate would have been concentrated entirely on the two shapes where the taught default does not apply, and the day would have reported a measure of memory as a measure of reasoning.

**This is the third instance of a pattern two prior whitepapers named.** [WP-054](wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 opened it at one instance — step 3's prediction collected after point 6 supplied the list — and stated the rule it would become: *an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.* [WP-062](wiki/whitepapers/WP-062.md) §8 and [WP-066](wiki/whitepapers/WP-066.md) §8 recorded the second. WP-054 §8 said that a third instance elevates it.

**The day is fixed.** Phase 1 item 3 now requires the observation-position claim on the supplied runs, before any taxonomy exists, and phase 8 reports **the movement between item 3 and step 4** rather than step 4 alone. The instrument carried to [S075](wiki/seminars/S075.md) is the shift. The correction is annotated in the day at the point of change.

**And the elevation is argued.** The observation has now fired three times, in three modules, on three unrelated days, and — this is the part that matters — **it was found here only because two prior whitepapers had named it.** The design did not notice the defect while writing phase 5; it noticed while writing §8, with WP-054's sentence available. That is the property that makes it worth a rule rather than a habit: **the pattern is self-detecting once written down, and undetectable otherwise.** The rule form is already stated and needs no drafting: *an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall; where a day's instrument is administered inside or after phase 4, the day must either administer it before phase 4 as well and report the movement, or state in its whitepaper that the instrument measures retention rather than reasoning.*

This paper cannot enact it. The [Seminar Day Design Pattern](wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) is a shared page, **and instrumentation rule 7 is already pending** on a different observation — that this design stages what it expects students to resist and asserts what it finds obvious once stated — which has reached three instances and three independent proposals and has not been enacted either. **No number is claimed here and nothing is renumbered.** The argument is recorded, the wording is supplied in §9, and the sequencing of two pending elevations is a matter for whoever maintains the pattern page.

**What remains wrong is conceded and it is small.** The pre-taxonomy probe at item 3 is administered on **supplied** runs and step 4 is answered on the **student's own** system, so the movement is measured across a change of object as well as across the instruction. A clean instrument would probe the student's own system at phase 1, which is impossible — the capability's traces are not read until phase 5 — and the design has taken the available approximation rather than the correct one. The shift should therefore be read as directional and not as a magnitude, and phase 8 reports it that way.

**Objection 3: the day teaches that two shapes have no observer and then hands students a tool that returns a number for them.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn, and it is the one the design finds hardest to be confident about.

At its strongest: point 2 says selection and output utilisation have no observing party. Phase 3 then has students run five evaluators, four of which carry the four shape names, and every one of them returns a result — including for the two shapes the day has just called unobservable. **A number arrived for a thing the day says nobody can see.** The day's answer is that the evaluator is a judge reading the record rather than a party positioned at the failure, and that answer is correct; but it is a distinction between two things that both produce a value, delivered to students who have spent eight quarters being taught that a value is what evidence looks like. **The demonstration's rhetorical force runs against its argument**: the room's most vivid memory of this phase will be that they measured tool failure with five evaluators, and the sentence that makes those measurements interpretable is a subordinate clause in point 9.

Worse, the specification's countermeasure is a check on an artefact and not on a belief. **A student can write *none* at step 4, pass, and go on to report `tool_selection` scores in a review the following month** without ever having held the two positions at once. The design has no instrument that would detect that and does not pretend otherwise.

**The fix is one clause and it is made.** Step 4 now requires that, for every *none* where the platform ships an evaluator of that name, the student state **what that evaluator read** in order to produce its score. The answer is always the record the loop produced, it takes one line, and it cannot be written from the taxonomy — it requires the student to trace an input. **Asserting that an evaluator is not an observer and tracing what it read are different acts, and only the second is a check on the belief.**

**Two things are conceded.** The clause tests tracing at one moment and not retention, and §6 already records that the S079 link measures the cohort's decision rather than its retention — so the module has no retention instrument for this at all. And **the deeper version of the objection is unanswered**: a programme that teaches a decomposition and then operates a product implementing it has made the product the decomposition's most available referent, and no clause in a specification changes what is most available. The design's position is that the alternative — teaching the decomposition and refusing to run the tool — is worse, because it produces students who cannot read the dashboard they will be given. **That is a choice between two bad options and it is recorded as one.**

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At item 2, the cohort's outcome enumeration will include the fourth outcome — returned something plausible and wrong — for fewer than half of students, and the count will not be higher than the same cohort's [S033](wiki/seminars/S033.md) item 1 count for the first three. *Instrument:* item 2 responses coded against the four outcomes and joined per student to the [S033](wiki/seminars/S033.md) baseline. **The second clause is the claim**: if the first three outcomes are enumerated better than at S033, four quarters of intervening material improved a retained enumeration, which this design does not expect and would be the most interesting retention result in the module.

**Prediction 2.** At item 3, more than half of the observation-position claims will name a party, and at step 4 the same students will write *none* for selection and output utilisation. The shift will be large. *Instrument:* item 3 and step 4 claims paired per student and coded party / none, by shape. **If the shift is small because item 3 already answers *none*, the day's problem is smaller than §1 claims** and the bridge is doing less work than the design credits it with.

**Prediction 3.** At step 2, the shape students most often report being unable to find in the trace log will be output utilisation, and the second will be selection. *Instrument:* step 2 shape-not-found responses tallied by shape. **This is the prediction the design is most confident of and it is the one worth being wrong about**: if students find output-utilisation instances readily, the log contains more evidence than point 2 predicts it can, which would mean the substrate has instrumentation the design has not accounted for and the criterion's boundary is drawn in the wrong place.

**Prediction 4.** More than half of step 5 discriminations will fail the independence requirement on first submission, and the modal failure will be a discrimination that compares the trace against itself. *Instrument:* step 5 submissions coded against the fail condition, before and after revision. **If independence failures are rare, [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 4 has transferred**, which would be the strongest available evidence that a remedy taught five weeks earlier survives a change of object — and this design's expectation is that it will not, because the record here looks like an external artefact in a way a monitoring pipeline does not.

**Prediction 5.** At step 4's added clause, more than a third of first submissions will describe what a same-named evaluator **computes** rather than what it **reads**, and a smaller but non-trivial number will name an evaluator as the observing party despite the specification's fail condition. *Instrument:* step 4 responses coded reads / computes / evaluator-as-party, by shape. **The computes-rather-than-reads answer is the finding**, not the outright violation: it is the form in which a student holds the taxonomy and has not held the criterion, and it is exactly what §8 objection 3 predicts the platform's availability produces. **If the clause is answered correctly at a high rate on first submission, the objection is weaker than this paper argues** and point 9 is doing more work than it is credited with.

**Prediction 6.** At item 5, the shape the cohort nominates as their agent's worst will be execution or input, and the shapes appearing most often in their own [S071](wiki/seminars/S071.md) to [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) frontier statements will be selection or output utilisation. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded by shape, joined to a blind coding of the three prior frontier statements per student. **The disagreement is the claim, and it is the module's recursion made measurable**: it would show the cohort nominating the shapes their tools report and living with the shapes nothing reports, which is point 2 recovered from two years of their own practice. If the two agree, the cohort's model of its own instrument is better than the design assumes.

**Instrumentation note, for the shared pages.** Two changes this paper argues for and cannot make.

*For the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md).* The durable entry currently reads *The four shapes of tool failure | S074 | A decomposition, not an implementation.* It should read: **"The four shapes of tool failure, decomposed by observational position rather than by stage | S074 | A claim about who is positioned to observe, independent of any implementation. The count of silent shapes is not part of the durable claim — see [WP-074](wiki/whitepapers/WP-074.md) §8."** **The corresponding qualifications on [M15](wiki/modules/M15.md) and [Q8](wiki/quarters/Q8.md) are already made**; this entry is the remainder.

*For the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md).* The elevation §8 objection 2 argues for, stated so it can be transcribed: **"An instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall. Where a day's instrument is administered inside or after phase 4, the day must either administer it before phase 4 as well and report the movement, or state in its whitepaper that the instrument measures retention rather than reasoning."** Provenance: opened at [WP-054](wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 at one instance with the rule form already stated; second instance at [WP-062](wiki/whitepapers/WP-062.md) §8 and [WP-066](wiki/whitepapers/WP-066.md) §8; third at [WP-074](wiki/whitepapers/WP-074.md) §8, found only because the prior two had recorded it. **No number is assigned here.** Instrumentation rule 7 is already pending on a separate observation and this paper does not renumber anything.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** The construction of a failure taxonomy for a composed language system, and the discipline of decomposing by where a failure occurs rather than by what it looks like, follow [research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md](research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md) in method rather than in content — that note's classes are not these classes and no mapping between them is claimed. Tool invocation surfaces, default platform metrics and trace collection, used as instances and never as assessed content, follow [research/06-microsoft-platform/](research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), including [agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md). The detection apparatus the day extends is [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s and follows [research/03-measurement-evaluation/](research/03-measurement-evaluation/collected-materials.md). The error-clinic conditions follow [research/04-professional-formation/](research/04-professional-formation/collected-materials.md), including [keith-frese-error-management-training.md](research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md). The evidentiary discipline governing this section is [research/99-source-register/source-register.md](research/99-source-register/source-register.md).

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** Points 9 and 10 are taken from Microsoft Learn's agent-evaluator and Agent Framework evaluation pages, read 2026-07-26. The catalogue groups five evaluators under **tool usage** — `tool_call_accuracy`, `tool_selection`, `tool_input_accuracy`, `tool_output_utilization` and `tool_call_success` — and four under **agent behaviour** — `intent_resolution`, `task_adherence`, `task_completion` and `task_navigation_efficiency`. The published purposes used in the day are quoted rather than paraphrased: **Tool Call Success** "evaluates whether all tool calls executed successfully without technical failures"; **Tool Selection** measures whether the agent selected the most appropriate and efficient tools; **Tool Input Accuracy** validates that tool-call parameters are correct; **Tool Output Utilization** measures whether the agent correctly interprets and uses tool outputs; and **Tool Call Accuracy** measures the overall quality of tool calls including selection, parameter correctness and efficiency. **`task_navigation_efficiency` is documented as taking `actions` and `expected_actions` with no required model parameter**, which is the basis for point 9's claim that it is the one agent-behaviour check whose result does not depend on a judge and that it requires ground truth. Point 10's defaults are documented on the same date: the cloud evaluator set runs **relevance, coherence and task adherence** by default and **adds tool call accuracy automatically when items contain tool definitions**, and the worked output example shows a `tool_call_accuracy` score of **2** against a **threshold of 3**, failing. Many of these evaluators are marked **preview**, and one page consulted for [research/06-microsoft-platform/](research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md) is a **classic** variant explicitly superseded.

**Three silences are recorded and all three are taught.** First and largest: **no agreement with human judgement is published for any evaluator in this catalogue**, no calibration data is published, and no rationale is published for any default threshold. Every judged score in this stack is therefore uncalibrated, the day says so, and the specification fails a judged score reported without that statement. Second: **two Microsoft Learn pages describe `tool_output_utilization`'s output differently** — the agent-evaluators reference lists it as a binary pass-or-fail result, and the voice-agent evaluation page groups it with the judged evaluators returning a one-to-five score converted by threshold — **and neither states which governs.** The day records the discrepancy and does not resolve it, and the students are shown that two pages were enough to find it. Third: the documentation states thresholds, required inputs and output formats throughout and **states no incidence figure for any failure shape in any system**, so the day asserts none; its only rates are computed on the substrate's own trace log, and point 6's incidence claim is marked as this design's judgement at the point of use. Consistent with the folder-wide asymmetry recorded in [research/06-microsoft-platform/](research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md), **every source consulted for this day states a bound, a format or a default and none states an accuracy.**

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** Pretesting before vocabulary is supplied, contrasting cases holding the observable fixed and varying the mechanism, blocked practice for a discrimination with rare systematic errors, expanding-interval retrieval, pre-commitment before measurement, external-observer correction of a self-made claim, and error management training are cited for **direction and mechanism only**. **No effect size is asserted for any of them.** **No magnitude is asserted for the incidence of any failure shape in any system**: the day's only rates are computed on the substrate's own trace log, phase 6's figure is one capability over one year and is marked local, and the claim that output-utilisation failure has the highest incidence is stated as a design judgement in §1 and at the point of use in the day, and is not asserted as a measured finding anywhere in the day's material.

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The four-way decomposition by observational position; the criterion that a tool reports on what happens inside it and nothing else; the identification of selection and output utilisation as the structurally silent pair and the boundary case at point 4; the four discriminations as comparisons between records that should agree; the placement of F4 as a payload against the immediately preceding day's frame; the requirement that the room classify the evaluators themselves by observational position at phase 3; the *what did the evaluator read* clause at step 4; the blocked-practice decision at §6; and all six §9 predictions are this design's own reasoning, untested. Predictions about this programme's own outcomes have no external warrant and are stated as such. **The correspondence between the four shapes and four shipped evaluator names is treated throughout as an instance and never as evidence**, and the day says so to students. **The §8 objection-1 concession is only partly answered**: the day's durable claim has a short form that its own scope statement contradicts; the naming fix is declined because it would now split the vocabulary from IS302 *and* from the instrument the students operate; the [M15](wiki/modules/M15.md) and [Q8](wiki/quarters/Q8.md) qualifications are made; the register wording is supplied in §9 for a page this paper cannot edit; and the design concedes that none of that reaches what a student carries out of the room, which it cannot measure. **The §8 objection-2 finding is fixed in the day** by adding a pre-taxonomy probe and reporting the instrument as a movement, records the **third** instance of a pattern [WP-054](wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 opened and said a third would elevate, argues the elevation with the rule wording supplied and **no number assigned**, and concedes that the corrected instrument measures a shift across a change of object and is therefore directional rather than quantitative. **The §8 objection-3 finding is fixed in the day** by the step 4 clause, and concedes both that the clause tests tracing at one moment rather than retention and that the deeper objection is unanswered — a programme that operates a product implementing its own decomposition has made the product that decomposition's most available referent, and the alternative of not operating it is worse.
