## In plain terms

**The category is not the lesson. The decision is.**

Classifying a use as sensitive is a lookup, and a learner who classifies and stops has produced a label. What makes the classification worth anything is that it forces somebody to decide something and record it, and most regimes are far clearer about how to classify than about who decides.

There are three decisions and no fourth. **Change the design. Proceed with a recorded deviation. Decline.** *Escalated* and *under review* are states, not decisions, and a record that ends in one of them has resolved nothing.

The middle option is the one worth dwelling on. **Adding a documented, approvable way to say *we cannot comply yet* is the clearest maturity signal in the regime this module draws on**, and the reason is behavioural rather than administrative: a compliance regime with no legitimate way to say that does not stop people shipping. **It stops people recording.** Making deviation visible rather than merely absent is what converts an aspiration into a control — which is the same argument [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) made about engagements, and the same one this stage has made about unmitigated threats.

Two smaller things carry disproportionate weight.

**Sensitive use usually lives where a person is the control**, which means it lives in exactly the region [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md)'s binding rule pushed out of the threat registry. That is what the exclusion register is for, and this is the session where it earns its place.

**And the dodge check is the session's most uncomfortable requirement.** Could a slightly different description of your system have avoided the classification? If yes, that is not a loophole to use — **it is a finding about the test you wrote at [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)**, and it belongs in the record.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching sensitive use as a decision-forcing device rather than as a taxonomy, and defends compressing the available responses into three. The argument is that a classification which forces no decision is a label; that a regime offering no legitimate way to declare an inability to comply produces silence rather than compliance; and that sensitive use predominantly occupies the human process around a system, which is the region a bound threat model systematically excludes. The session installs a located determination, a decision drawn from three, a named decision-maker with the material they saw, an expiry and revisit condition on any deviation, a reviewable written refusal where one is chosen, and a dodge check on the scope test. The paper concedes that requiring a named individual will meet real organisational resistance and may expose a person to blame for a collective decision, and that teaching from the corpus's single recorded refusal is reasoning from one case in a programme that spends nine stages objecting to exactly that.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

A learner who identifies a sensitive use will classify it and consider the work done, because classification is what the form asks for and because the next step is uncomfortable.

**The next step is uncomfortable because it makes somebody responsible.** A decision has an owner, a date and a consequence; a classification has none of those, and an organisation can hold classifications indefinitely without anything changing. **The commonest failure is not misclassification. It is correct classification followed by nothing.**

The second problem is that the regime's options are usually stated less crisply than they need to be, so the practical answer becomes *escalate*. **Escalation is a handoff and it terminates in the same place**, one level up, where the same three options are available and the same discomfort applies.

The third is the incentive one. **The description of a system is written by the party who benefits from the classification going one way**, and a scope test that can be avoided by rewording will be avoided by rewording — usually without anybody experiencing it as dishonesty, because a narrower description is always defensible on its own terms.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The session needs [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md)'s components and flows to locate a determination, [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md)'s exclusion register to find the places the threat model excluded, [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)'s scope test to run the dodge check against, and [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md)'s instrumentation to give a deviation somewhere to expire.

It retrieves [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) directly. **A refusal you can defend is the same artefact at a different scale**, and a learner who wrote one in week two writes a better one here.

**The prior it works against is that governance categories are somebody else's vocabulary.** They are, and the decision they force is not.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** Low intrinsic, highest interpersonal load in the stage. The session's work makes a named person responsible and asks the learner to write the naming down.

**3.2 Why the decision set has three members.** A set with an implied fourth member collapses into it, because the fourth is always cheaper. **Naming exactly three, and stating explicitly that escalation is not among them, is the only way to keep the expensive options live.** The compression is this programme's, not the source's, and §8 does not defend it as the only possible one.

**3.3 Why the absence of a legitimate deviation produces silence rather than compliance.** People under delivery pressure do not stop delivering when a rule cannot be met; they stop generating the record that would show it. **A regime that forbids saying *not yet* converts a visible gap into an invisible one**, which is worse for everybody including the regime.

**3.4 Why a deviation needs an expiry.** An unbounded deviation is a permanent exception, and the organisational learning is worse than the original gap: it teaches that the mechanism is a formality. **The expiry is what distinguishes the maturity signal from the loophole.**

**3.5 Why sensitive use hides in the human process.** The place where an output changes what happens to a person is usually a decision made by a human holding the output, and that is a process step rather than a component. **A bound threat model excludes it by construction** — [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) said so — which is precisely why the exclusion register is the right input here rather than the registry.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The seven movements run situation, artefact, standard, failure modes, work, review, limits.

**Opening item 4 is the instrument.** *Your regime has no legitimate way to say we cannot comply yet — what happens?* Every experienced person in the room knows the answer and most have never said it aloud, and saying it is what makes the deviation mechanism legible as a control rather than as leniency.

**Opening item 5 is asked before instruction because it cannot be asked afterwards.** Once a learner knows the dodge check is assessed, the honest answer becomes harder to give.

**The work movement sends the learner to the exclusion register first**, which is the module's designed payoff: an artefact built in [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) for one reason turns out to be the input to a different problem three sessions later. **A cohort that resented producing it meets it here as the only place the answer lives.**

**The refusal example is given with its shape named rather than summarised**, because a refusal is a genre and the genre is learnable: what the humans catch, why the consequence class matters, and declining the customer's own proposal rather than a hypothetical.

**The review movement asks two questions of an outsider**, and the second — when does this expire and what happens then — is aimed at the failure the design most expects.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The category exercise.** *Countermeasure:* the standard's warning sign — your artefact ends with a label — and the decision set stated as an artefact requirement rather than as advice.

**The escalation as decision.** *Countermeasure:* the explicit exclusion of escalation from the decision set.

**The absent decider.** The likeliest failure. *Countermeasure:* the requirement to record what the decision-maker saw, which a function name cannot satisfy. §8 concedes the resistance this will meet.

**The permanent deviation.** *Countermeasure:* the expiry and revisit condition as named elements, plus the review movement's second question.

**The unrecorded refusal.** *Countermeasure:* [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md)'s form, retrieved rather than re-taught.

**The scope dodge.** *Countermeasure:* the dodge check, asked before instruction. **This is a disclosure rather than a prevention**, and the design has nothing better.

No entrustment evidence. ## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: requiring a named individual is a design that organisations will refuse, and that exposes a person to blame for a decision the organisation made.**

At its strongest: committees exist precisely to distribute accountability for decisions no individual should carry, and in regulated settings the decision genuinely belongs to a body. Requiring a name produces one of two outcomes — a fictitious owner, or a real person carrying personal exposure for a collective judgement they had limited power to shape. **Personal accountability without personal authority is scapegoating**, and a curriculum should not be manufacturing it.

**Conceded, and the design accepts an adjustment rather than defending the strict form.** **A named body, with a named chair and named papers, satisfies the element.** What does not satisfy it is a function — *Governance*, *the review board*, a mailbox — because a function cannot be asked what it was looking at, and **the material the decision-maker saw is the element carrying the real information.** A decision-maker shown a one-page summary decided about a one-page summary, and if that summary was produced by the process under assessment then [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) applies. **The declined fix is to accept a function name.** It is declined for that reason. **The session states the stricter form deliberately**, because a learner offered the looser form first will always take it, and this paper records the adjustment rather than silently widening the session's text.

**Objection 2: teaching from the corpus's single recorded refusal is reasoning from one case, in a programme that spends nine stages objecting to exactly that.**

At its strongest: this programme's standing criticism of its own source material is that it generalises from experience without evidence. Here it takes the one refusal in an entire collection, holds it up as a template, and names three features of it as though they were established properties of good refusals. **That is a sample of one, presented as a genre.**

**Conceded.** Two things narrow the damage without removing it. The session uses the case as a **shape rather than as a base rate**, and says so explicitly in its limits section — one case tells you what a well-formed refusal can look like and nothing about how often refusal is right. And the three features named are defensible on their own terms without the case: a refusal that says what the humans catch is checkable, one that names the consequence class is proportionate, and one that declines the customer's own proposal rather than a hypothetical is addressed to the actual decision. **The declined fix is to remove the case and describe a well-formed refusal abstractly.** It is declined because an abstract description of a refusal has no traction — learners produce refusals that meet every stated property and would persuade nobody — and because **the scarcity of the case is itself part of the teaching: one refusal in an entire body of professional practice is a finding about the practice.** The residual risk, that a cohort over-fits to a single domain as the paradigm of sensitive use, is real and the session does not counter it.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most records will name a function rather than a person, or a body with a chair and papers. *Instrument:* the submitted record, coded *named person* / *named body with chair and papers* / *function* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most deviations will lack an expiry at first attempt. *Instrument:* a presence check for expiry and revisit condition on each deviation submitted.

**Prediction 3.** Most dodge checks will be answered in the negative. *Instrument:* the learner's answer, paired with a second reader's independent attempt to construct a narrower description of the same system that avoids the classification.

**Prediction 4.** Sensitive uses will be located inside the system more often than in the human process around it. *Instrument:* the location coded against the categories in the learner's [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) exclusion register. **If this holds, the module's claim that the exclusion register is the right input here is not being realised in practice, and the work movement's ordering should be reconsidered.**

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That a classification forcing no decision is a label; that a regime with no legitimate way to say *not yet* produces silence rather than compliance; and that a deviation without an expiry teaches an organisation that the mechanism is a formality.

**What the evidence licenses.** Almost nothing. **The regime publishes no evidence that any determination ever changed a system, and the surrounding corpus contains exactly one recorded refusal across its entire collection.** A single case is a shape, not a rate.

**The concession worth remembering.** This session asks you to write down who decided, what they saw, and whether you could have described the system differently to avoid the question. **All three are uncomfortable and the third is the one you will be tempted to skip.** It is also the only one that tells anybody anything about the regime itself.
