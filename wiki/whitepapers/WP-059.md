## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

The module has spent three days establishing that retrieval quality is a joint property of a corpus, a query population and somebody's definition of relevance. The field's answer to that is the benchmark: fix all three once, publish them, and let everyone report against the same fixed triple.

**The important thing about that answer is that it is a good one.** It is the only mechanism by which two people who have never met can compare two systems. A day that leaves you dismissing benchmarks has made you less useful, not more — and dismissal is what you will reach for, because dismissal is cheap and reading carefully is not.

So the day is arranged so that dismissal never becomes available. Everything it shows you is produced by comparing evaluations of systems that *did not change*. Four systems, four evaluations, four different rankings. There is no bad benchmark to blame and no methodological error to find. The ordering is a property of the evaluation, and you watch that happen four times in one figure. **The only conclusion left standing is the useful one: the benchmark fixed something different from what you care about.**

Then it gets personal, and this is the part that changes behaviour. Three days ago you measured how far you and a colleague diverge applying your own written definition of relevance. That number is a floor. **No difference smaller than it is observable on judgements of that kind** — not by you, and not by the people whose published improvements you have been reading. Today you compute it against a difference you have measured on your own system, and if it falls below, you report no result.

Reporting no result is not a failure here. It is an assessed outcome, it is counted, and the day says out loud that being able to produce it is what separates an engineer from somebody who has run an evaluation.

**What this paper concedes, and it is a real concession.** The day requires a re-run that anybody else could execute from its stated configuration, and **nobody ever executes one.** An assessor reads the configuration and forms a view about whether it looks runnable — which is the same act as reading a methods section and forming a view about whether a study looks replicable, the exact act the day spends fifty minutes demonstrating to be unreliable. The fix is free, the apparatus already exists, and it is not in the design.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching benchmark reading as the reading of a criterion whose authors are unnamed, and defends stating what a benchmark fixes — a corpus, a query population, a definition of relevance — as the whole of what a score licenses. It defends producing four different orderings of four unchanged systems as the day's central demonstration, because four orderings require no interpretation and cannot be attributed to any system's quality. It defends treating zero-shot as a claim about the relationship between two corpora, falsifiable by a cheap measurement nobody publishes. Its most consequential move is to convert the cohort's own measured relevance-judgement disagreement into a floor beneath which reporting a difference is forbidden, and to make reporting no result an assessed outcome. **It defends using a live vendor leaderboard as the day's platform instance on the unusual ground that the vendor states the day's argument in its own troubleshooting table, and that a disclosure which exists and is not carried with the claim is functionally a silence.** The paper reports discharge of three obligations, from S051, S053 and S056. It records **the second instance of the binding-surface question the Whitepaper Standard's compliance register left open**, repairs it locally, and argues that the local repair is itself evidence the gap is load-bearing. It concedes that the day's hard requirement is dodgeable at step 1, and that the day now demands a reproducible artefact it never checks the reproducibility of.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The module has spent three days establishing that retrieval quality is a joint property of a corpus, a query population and a relevance criterion. The field's answer to that is the benchmark: fix all three once, publish them, and let everyone report against the fixed triple.

**The problem is that the answer is a good one.** It is the only mechanism by which two people who have never met can compare two systems, and a day that teaches students to dismiss benchmarks has made them less useful, not more. What the day must produce is a reader — somebody who can take a published number, say what it is a claim about, say what it is not, and use the part that transfers.

That is harder than scepticism and it fails in a specific way. Students who have been taught that a measurement is constructed reach for a general dismissal, because dismissal is cheap and reading is not. The day's structure is built against that: every finding is produced by comparing evaluations of *unchanged systems*, so there is never a moment at which the correct response is "the benchmark is bad" rather than "the benchmark fixed something different from what I care about".

The second problem is personal and arrives at phase 5. At [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md) the cohort measured how far two people diverge applying their own written relevance criterion. That number is a floor: **no difference smaller than it is observable on judgements of that kind.** Many of the improvements the students have read about are below it, and — the part that changes behaviour — many of the improvements they are about to claim on their own systems will be too. A student who has computed their own ceiling and reported no result on their own work has learned something that no amount of instruction about benchmark construction produces.

The third problem is what [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md) opened and could not close. That day measured how many students would have reached a different conclusion from an aggregate alone — on their own ablations, where they had every incentive to look closely. **Whether that scepticism survives contact with somebody else's published table is an open question**, and it is the question phase 1 exists to answer.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Moderate technically and high dispositionally**, which is the module's only day with that profile. Nothing in the material is mechanically hard. What is hard is being required to look up who wrote a relevance criterion, to find that you cannot, and then to decline to report a difference you have measured.

The interactivity is between steps 3, 5 and 6. The licensed claim constrains what the re-run can test; the re-run produces a difference; the ceiling determines whether that difference may be reported. A student who writes step 3 loosely produces a re-run that tests nothing and a step 6 that has no claim to bound.

Management:

- **All three phase 1 comparisons and all four phase 3 evaluations are supplied**, including the overlap measurements, which students could not compute in the time.
- **The relevance judgements and their disagreement rate are [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md)'s**, unchanged, and the ceiling is arithmetic on a number they already own.
- **The [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) checklist is supplied as a checklist** and is applied rather than reconstructed.
- **Step 2 is worked with the room**, since locating a benchmark's construction and authors is genuinely difficult and failing at it quietly looks like incompetence.
- **The re-run at step 5 uses the harness from [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)**, so no new tooling appears.

**Step 4's prediction is unsupported.**

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Committing to an adoption decision on aggregates, then seeing the breakdown, then committing again,* phase 1.
- *Four orderings of four unchanged systems,* phase 3.
- *Explaining or constructing a defeating evaluation, whichever the [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) branch selects,* phase 3.
- *Being required to name a criterion's author and finding you cannot,* step 2.
- *Reporting no result,* step 6.
- *Applying a checklist to your own work and reporting its failures,* step 7.
- *Having your careful claim restated by somebody who has to act on it,* phase 7.
- *Predicting what an agent will omit rather than what it will get wrong,* item 5.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Constructing a benchmark.* Named and excluded; it is the [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) task and would double the day.
- *Significance testing procedure.* ME202's. The day uses the ceiling and the interval and does not litigate the machinery.
- *Contamination detection at scale.* Named and excluded; the overlap figures are supplied because measuring them is a research task.
- *Online evaluation.* [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md)'s, and the day says so at the point it becomes tempting.
- *Tuning the fusion constant.* Named at point 9 and excluded; changing it would give phase 3 a variable and the day's whole method is that the systems do not change.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** Points 3 and 6 are stated for evaluations whose relevance is judged by people. **Safe** for retrieval, ranking, recommendation, summarisation and every evaluation in which a person decides whether the output was right — which is the majority of what these students will read and all of what they will build this quarter. **Unsafe** where the criterion is mechanical: exact-answer sets, execution-based code evaluation, formally checked outputs. There the judgement ceiling does not apply at all, the failure modes are different, and a student who applies point 6 to an execution-based benchmark will make a confident wrong criticism in front of somebody who knows better. The mechanical case is named and its homes given — [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md) and [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md). **This day makes the boundary checkable in the artefact**, which §8 objection 1 argues is both correct and insufficient.

**The platform layer carries a scope statement whose unsafe half is a temptation rather than a misunderstanding.** Point 8 reads a live vendor leaderboard as a worked example of a disclosed construction, and point 9 names three ranking defaults that sit inside any hybrid comparison. Those are **safe** as instances of point 7's seven things — here is a comparison, here is what it disclosed, here is what it did not — and safe as an account of this product at this version. They are **unsafe** in two directions. They are unsafe as a claim about vendor benchmarks as a class: this one discloses its curation rule, its provenance mix and its normalisation, and the next one a student meets may disclose none of that, and the lesson is not *vendors are candid*. **And they are unsafe as a template for judging candour**, because the same vendor, on a different page of the same product, recommends a retrieval configuration on the strength of unnamed "benchmark testing" with no metric and no interval. **The two coexist and neither cancels the other**, which is the durable observation: candour is a property of a page and of the person who wrote it, not of an organisation, and a reader who generalises from one page to a publisher has committed point 2's error at a larger scale.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **four orderings**.

Phase 3 supplies four systems and four evaluations, and the four evaluations rank the four systems four different ways. Nothing changed about the systems between the tables. There is no methodological error to find, no bad benchmark to blame, and no interpretation to negotiate: the ordering is a property of the evaluation, and the room watches that happen four times in one figure.

The second mechanism is the overlap column at V4. The system that wins the zero-shot suite has measurable overlap with several of its datasets, and on the subset with none it loses to term matching. The word *zero-shot* was doing work it had not earned, and the measurement that would have shown it is one line of a table nobody printed.

Phase 6 supplies the affective content and is constructed so that nobody in it is wrong. Two comparisons, six weeks apart, opposite conclusions, both correctly run, both differences below a disagreement rate that was known and documented. The detail that carries the phase is the meeting: a room of competent people arguing about which of two non-results was the real one.

The competence risk is at step 6, and it is the day's largest. A student required to report no result on work they have spent a phase producing may read that as having wasted the phase. The instructor states before phase 5 that reporting no result is the day's best available outcome, that it is an assessed element rather than a failure, and that the ability to produce it is the difference between an engineer and a person who has run an evaluation. The cohort's no-result count is then reported at phase 8 plainly, without commentary and without names.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Aggregate-only conclusion, breakdown, second conclusion, all sealed, **followed by a sealed Copilot prediction placed after the S053 comparison so that it cannot contaminate it.** **The first four items are [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md)'s step 7 measurement retaken on published results**, and the within-phase design is what makes the two rates comparable. The selection of the three comparisons is disclosed immediately after sealing, per instrumentation rule 3.

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals and a bridge that delivers point 2 as a two-quarter-old consequence.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** Four evaluations, four systems, four orderings, then the overlap column, **all four run by the room on their own index.** **Then the leaderboard reading**, which is not a fifth evaluation but an exercise in reading a construction disclosure — the act point 7 will require them to perform in writing two phases later. **The phase's form is branched against [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md)'s phase 7 defeat rate**, which determines whether the defeating evaluation is supplied for explanation or must be constructed.

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Points 2 and 6 are the retained pair. **Point 8 is the perishable platform point and point 9 names the defaults**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md). Point 10's boundary is unusually important because the day's argument travels easily and corrodes on arrival.

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Seven steps, **with the re-run built as a runnable configuration rather than assembled as a table.** **Step 6 is the artefact element [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md) named** and is the day's fail condition. Step 7 discharges [Q6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md)'s reporting commitment on the [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) checklist and is deliberately self-incriminating. The three Copilot Practice Standard elements attach here, and the prompt record is diagnostic in its own right on this day.

**Phase 6 (40 min).** Two correct comparisons, opposite conclusions, one documented number nobody read, and a meeting — then an agent answer with four real, current, correctly-summarised citations and no licensing sentence.

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Restate, code against [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s two failures, adjudicate. Bilateral by construction: the partner writes and the author judges.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** The two conclusion-change rates side by side; step 4 against step 5; **item 5 coded for fabrication-versus-omission**; **the no-result count**; the checklist failures tallied by item.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who follows retrieval literature will know that learned retrievers underperform lexical baselines out of domain and will predict phase 3 correctly. Predicted failure: they treat the day as confirmation and skip step 6, because their step 1 comparison will be one they already have a view about. Countermeasure: step 6 is arithmetic on their own [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md) number and cannot be satisfied from reading, and phase 7 restates their own claim rather than the literature's. §8 objection 2 argues the countermeasure is evadable at step 1.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is step 2 — a benchmark's construction and authorship are often genuinely hard to recover, and a student who fails to find them may believe the failure is theirs. Countermeasures: worked with the room; **an explicit could-not-determine is a checked element rather than a gap**; and the instructor states that the frequency of could-not-determine across the cohort is one of the day's findings about the field rather than about the students.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** V1 and V3 are straightforward and the reordering between them is expected after three days of module material. The difficulty is V4's overlap column and, specifically, saying what should have been reported to prevent the error. Fallback if a group handles it quickly: ask **what overlap measurement would be adequate** — exact duplicates, near-duplicates, same-source documents, same-domain documents — which is a question with no supplied answer and which is where the real difficulty in contamination work lives.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **evaluative nihilism**, and it is sharper here than anywhere in the programme: four orderings, an unearned zero-shot claim, a leaderboard that is a selection process, and a ceiling that forbids most claims. A student can leave believing that nothing published is readable and nothing they measure is reportable. That would be a worse outcome than the credulity the day is correcting, because it is unfalsifiable and it ends inquiry.

Countermeasures: V3 is an evaluation the students built, on their corpus, with their criterion, and it works — the day demonstrates a usable evaluation before it demonstrates unusable ones; point 4 gives the baseline a constructive diagnostic role rather than a debunking one; point 7 is a list of seven things that make a comparison readable, which is a specification for doing this well rather than a case for despair; step 6's no-result is framed as a finding a professional produces rather than as an absence; and the instructor states once that a reader who can say what a number licenses is more useful to a room than either a believer or a sceptic.

## 6. Retention architecture

Retrieved by [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) (two days later, where a ship decision must cite the evidence it rests on), [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md) (the release gate, which is a benchmark the students own), [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) (offline against online, where the ceiling is tested as a predictor), [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md) (trajectory evaluation, where the mechanical-criterion boundary matters) and [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) (risk communication, where point 7's seven things are what a decision-maker is owed).

**S067 at roughly seven weeks is the primary target and it is the only occasion that can test the day's central claim.** This day asserts that a difference below the ceiling is not observable. S067 has the offline differences, the online outcomes and the ceilings, and can therefore ask directly whether below-ceiling differences failed to survive contact with users at a higher rate than above-ceiling ones. That is the strongest available test of point 6 and it is why the step 6 instrument responds there.

S066's retrieval is the one with the most operational weight: a release gate is a benchmark whose authors *can* be named, because the students are them, and the day is the direct answer to point 7's second addition.

Retained content: the benchmark as constructed criterion, the fixed triple and what a score licenses, zero-shot as a corpus relationship, the baseline as a distance measure, the leaderboard as selection, the judgement ceiling, and the seven things owed. Seven items, which exceeds this design's usual ceiling of six and is accepted because five of them are re-badged prior content rather than new.

Own schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 7.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement. The quarter's is at [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md).

One specification item in ME202 with IS203. Per rule 1, the checked elements are the chosen comparison; what it fixed with each part's author or an explicit could-not-determine; its kind, human-judged or mechanical; the licensed and the nearest unlicensed claim; the pre-committed prediction; **the runnable per-class re-run with its configuration**; the ceiling comparison with its no-result finding where applicable; the [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) checklist applied with failed items named; the phase 7 restatement and adjudication; **and the prompt, intervention and frontier records.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

Five hard requirements: **a claimed difference smaller than the stated ceiling, reported as a result, fails**; **a judgement ceiling applied to a mechanically-judged comparison fails**; **a re-run reported without the [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) checklist fails**; **a re-run that cannot be executed from its stated configuration fails**; and **an intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that its own design could have detected an intervention-worthy error. The accepted instance of the last is a re-run that refuses to report a difference below the student's stated ceiling — which is step 6 enforced by the artefact rather than by the student, and is the strongest thing this day can produce.

**Rule-6 discharge, first obligation — [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md)'s phase 7 defeat rate.** WP-051 §8 committed this paper to stating in §7 whether the day carries the artefact element S051 named, and where. **It does. The element is phase 3's construction rule**, which selects between two forms of the phase against that rate: a low defeat rate means the defeating evaluation is supplied and each group must explain why it defeats and what should have been reported; a high rate means groups construct their own against the V2 winner. The obligation is discharged. It is the weakest of this day's three discharges, because it changes the task's form rather than adding an assessed element, and the honest reading is that S051's instrument was the least actionable of the three it named.

**Rule-6 discharge, second obligation — [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md)'s step 7 aggregate-only counterfactual rate.** S053 named this day's phase 1 as the response occasion, specifying that the same measurement be retaken on published results rather than on the student's own work. **The element exists: phase 1 collects an adoption conclusion from aggregates, supplies the per-dataset breakdowns, collects the conclusion again, and phase 8 places the change rate beside S053's.** The obligation is discharged, and the paired display is the addition — neither rate is interesting alone and the gap between them is the day's most quoted result.

**Rule-6 discharge, third obligation — [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md)'s relevance-disagreement distribution.** S056 named this day's phase 5 as the response occasion. **The element exists, is assessed, and is the day's principal fail condition: step 6 requires the ceiling to be stated and a no-result reported where the claimed difference falls below it.** The obligation is discharged and it is the strongest of the three, because it converts a measurement taken three days earlier into a constraint on what the student is permitted to claim.

**[Q6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md)'s reporting commitment** on the [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) clustering checklist is discharged at step 7 and reported at phase 8, tallied by item.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** The step 6 no-result rate responds at [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) phase 5, where the ceiling is tested as a predictor of which offline differences survived online; the phase 1 conclusion-change gap responds at [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) phase 4, where every student is asked which published claim their ship decision relies on and what its breakdown showed. **WP-060 §7 and WP-067 §7 must report whether those elements exist**, per the commitment WP-051 §8 established.

**Frontier instrumentation.** Item 5's sealed prediction accuracy, coded for whether the student anticipated fabrication or a missing licensing sentence, is carried to the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) per instrumentation rule 9. It is deliberately the module's only frontier item whose correct answer is **an omission rather than an error**, and its value lies in the comparison with [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md)'s, whose correct answer was a fabrication. Per rule 3 the sample is stated: one cohort, one agent version, on a question chosen because a well-sourced answer with no scope statement was the likely output.

Dispositions: **integrity**, at step 6 — reporting a result that undermines your own position is the disposition's definition in [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md), and step 6 is the programme's cleanest instance of it.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the scope rule binds this paper and not this day, for the second recorded time — and the local repair is the evidence.**

At its strongest: the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s compliance register carries one open question, raised by [WP-049](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-049.md) §8. The scope rule requires a whitepaper to say where a simplification is safe and unsafe, and says nothing about whether the day's own artefact must make that boundary checkable. A day can satisfy the rule completely while assessing work that ignores it. The register states the question is left open "so that a second instance is recognised as a second instance."

**This is the second instance, and it is a worse one than the first.** Point 10 declares the judgement-ceiling argument unsafe for mechanically-judged benchmarks. As originally drafted, step 1 said only *choose a published comparison*. A student could therefore choose an execution-based code benchmark, compute a relevance-judgement ceiling that has no referent for it, report no result, satisfy every checked element in the specification, and commit precisely the error point 10 exists to prevent — while the whitepaper's scope statement sat one document away, discharging the rule.

It is worse than WP-049's instance because the consequence is a confident wrong criticism made in public rather than a mis-specified interval, and because this day's whole subject is reading a criterion correctly.

**The day is repaired.** Step 2 now requires the comparison's kind to be stated, and a ceiling applied to a mechanically-judged comparison is a fail condition. That closes the local hole.

**The repair does not answer the objection, and arguing that it does would be the mistake.** The objection is not about this day; it is about the rule's binding surface across ninety of them. [WP-049](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-049.md) recorded the gap and did not repair it; this paper repairs it here and leaves S049 as it stands. **A defect fixed where it is noticed and left standing where it was first described is exactly the propagation failure instrumentation rule 5 exists to prevent, one level up** — and the design has now produced it in its own rule-maintenance twice, since [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 recorded the same shape for rule 5 itself.

The register's stated reason for leaving the question open was that a general rule — every scope statement must appear as an assessed artefact element — would be more expensive than the four widenings that preceded it, and that one instance is not evidence the gap is load-bearing. **Two instances, on two different registers, one of which produced a repair, is a different position.** The argument for the entry, and its precise form, is carried to the programme rather than written into the shared pages, which this paper does not amend.

**Objection 2: the day's hard requirement is dodgeable at step 1, and the students who most need it will dodge it.**

At its strongest: step 6 requires the student to compare their claimed difference against their ceiling and to report no result where the difference is smaller. **Whether that situation arises is determined at step 1, by the student, when they choose which comparison to re-run.** A student who wants a finding chooses a comparison between a strong system and a weak one — where the difference is large, the ceiling is irrelevant, and step 6 is a paragraph confirming that a big number is bigger than a small one. Every checked element is satisfied. The day's central lesson is never encountered.

The students most likely to do this are the ones §5 identifies as arriving with prior knowledge, because they have views about which comparisons are interesting and those views select for large effects. The specification cannot distinguish a student who confronted the ceiling from one who arranged not to meet it, which is [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 3's failure — a discrimination the design performs and discards.

**This is conceded without qualification, and the fix is one clause.** Step 5's re-run should be required to include **the student's own pair: their [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md) retriever against their [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md) baseline, on their own query classes, whose margin they measured two days ago and which is small for a large fraction of the cohort.** That pair cannot be chosen to be safe, it is already computed, it costs no additional runtime, and it makes the ceiling confrontation unavoidable for exactly the students who would otherwise avoid it. The design did not take it, and the reason — that step 1's free choice is what makes the day feel like reading rather than an exercise — is a reason about the day's texture offered against an objection about its assessment.

**Objection 3: the day now demands a reproducible artefact and never checks that any of them reproduce.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn.

At its strongest: step 5 was a table and is now a build. The re-run must "execute end to end from a stated configuration, so that somebody else can run it and get your numbers," and a re-run that cannot be executed from its configuration fails the specification. **Nobody executes anybody's.** The assessor reads the configuration and forms a view about whether it looks runnable, which is the same act as reading a methods section and forming a view about whether the study looks replicable — the exact act this day spends fifty minutes demonstrating is unreliable.

The consequence is sharper than it looks, because the configuration was written by an agent. **A student who has never run their own configuration from clean state cannot distinguish a re-run that is reproducible from one that depends on state in their session**, and the failure mode is specific and common: an index that exists because they created it interactively, a field that was added by a prompt three days ago, a judgement file with a path that is theirs. All of that reads perfectly in a configuration listing. The day's own point 7 lists five things owed to a reader and the sixth thing — that the reader can actually run it — is the one the day has just started requiring and is the one it does not check.

The rebuttal is thin and this paper will not stretch it. Assessors reading for runnability is better than assessors reading for plausibility, and the specification's fail condition does move the standard.

**This is conceded without qualification, and the fix is free because the design already has the apparatus.** Phase 7 pairs every student with a partner for thirty minutes. **Require the partner to execute the student's re-run from its stated configuration and report whether the numbers came back the same**, before the restatement task. It costs a few minutes of a phase that is currently entirely verbal, it converts the reproducibility requirement from an assertion into a measurement, and it produces something this programme does not otherwise have: **a cohort-level rate for whether agent-authored configurations reproduce on a second machine.** That number would be worth more to the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) than anything else in this module, and it is not in the design. Until it is, the day requires reproducibility on the same evidence it teaches students to reject — the author's word.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Phase 1's conclusion-change rate on published results will be lower than [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md)'s step 7 rate on the students' own ablations, and the gap will be large. *Instrument:* the two rates, paired per student where the cohort is the same. **The claim is that scepticism does not transfer outward**, and if the gap is small the day's placement after S053 is doing more than this paper credits.

**Prediction 2.** Fewer than a third of students will report no result at step 6. *Instrument:* step 6 outcomes coded, cross-tabulated against the reported difference and the ceiling. **If the count is very low, either §8 objection 2 is correct and the requirement is being avoided at step 1, or the ceilings are being computed generously** — and the cross-tabulation distinguishes the two.

**Prediction 3.** At step 2, a majority of students will record could-not-determine for the relevance criterion's author, and far fewer for the corpus and the query population. *Instrument:* step 2 could-not-determine counts by part. **The claim is that the criterion's author is the least recoverable of the three**, which is why point 7 adds it to [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md)'s five.

**Prediction 4.** At phase 7, restatements will be coded as the hedging failure more often than as the overclaiming failure, and a majority of students will adjudicate their own wording as having invited it. *Instrument:* phase 7 restatements and adjudications coded against [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s two named failures. **This runs [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §9 prediction 2 at a second site and in writing rather than in speech**, and a consistent result across the two is the strongest evidence the programme could have that the hedging failure is a stable property of this cohort rather than of that day's format.

**Prediction 5.** At item 5, a majority will predict that the agent fabricates a citation, and the agent's actual failure will be a missing licensing sentence attached to correct citations. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded fabrication-versus-omission, against the agent outputs in each student's prompt record, **and compared against [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md)'s item 5 rates on the same cohort two days earlier.** **The claim is that a cohort taught to check sources will predict source failures and not scope failures**, which is the same asymmetry point 7 identifies in published comparisons. If the two days' rates are similar, students are predicting agent failure generically rather than from the task, and both sealed items are measuring one thing.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That a benchmark is a criterion whose authors are usually unnamed, that what it fixed — a corpus, a query population, a definition of relevance — is the whole of what a score licenses, and that dismissing benchmarks is worse than believing them. The demonstration is four evaluations ranking four unchanged systems four ways, so there is no bad benchmark to blame.

**What the evidence licenses.** Benchmark construction, the in-domain versus out-of-domain divergence, the competitiveness of lexical baselines on distant corpora, and assessor disagreement and its consequence for detectable differences are **class 1**, grounded in [research/03-measurement-evaluation/thakur-beir.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/thakur-beir.md), [research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md) and [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md). The leaderboard's curation, normalisation and standardised-datasets caveat are **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026 and marked preview; no agreement figure between any of these benchmarks and human judgement is published, and the fusion constant's justification cites experiments a reader cannot locate. The reader's list and the ceiling as a fail condition are **class 3**.

**What to do with it.**

- Say what a comparison fixed and who fixed each part, and write *could not determine* where you cannot. That answer is a finding about the field.
- Compare your claimed difference against your own judgement disagreement rate before reporting it, and report no result when it falls below.
- Candour is a property of a page and its author, never of a publisher.
- Place a published score in the five classes on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md): an observed result on somebody else's fixed triple, and that it predicts your corpus is an unsupported inference.

**The concession worth remembering.** The day requires a re-run anybody else could execute and nobody ever executes one, so reproducibility is assessed by reading a configuration and forming an impression — the exact act the day spends fifty minutes showing to be unreliable, on the author's word.

## Further reading

Only sources this paper or its seminar day already cites. Each entry carries the limit the research note itself records, so the limit travels with the recommendation.

- **[research/03-measurement-evaluation/thakur-beir.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/thakur-beir.md)** — open access; the note is verified against the arXiv listing. *Good for:* what a benchmark fixes, the in-domain against out-of-domain divergence, and the structure of the quality-against-cost trade across architectural families. *Will not support:* any current ranking. The note records that the results are a 2021 snapshot and the specific rankings have since changed, that eighteen datasets are a sample of retrieval tasks rather than a complete map, that the constituent datasets differ in the density and construction of their relevance judgements so per-dataset scores are not strictly comparable, that zero-shot is the paper's chosen condition and it says nothing about what in-domain adaptation would recover, and that the benchmark **has itself become a target of optimisation, which weakens its own zero-shot premise over time** — which is point 5's argument stated by the source's own reviewer.
- **[research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md)** — open access; **the note is built from the abstract and listing metadata, not from the full preprint.** *Good for:* the leaderboard as a selection process rather than a scoreboard, and for the reform argument that undisclosed testing and asymmetric sampling distort what a public ranking measures. *Will not support:* its numbers, or leaderboard nihilism. The note records that all allocation figures are external estimates rather than platform-disclosed values, that the findings are specific to one platform at one moment and policies can change in response to exactly this kind of audit, that its headline relative figure is a gain on that platform's own distribution and must not be restated as a general capability gain, that nothing in the source establishes that preference-based evaluation is invalid in principle, and that teaching material relying on anything beyond the abstract-level findings should go to the full text.
- **[research/03-measurement-evaluation/zheng-mt-bench-chatbot-arena.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/zheng-mt-bench-chatbot-arena.md)** — open access. *Good for:* assessor disagreement as a structural property rather than a quality failure, and the named judge biases. *Will not support:* transferring any agreement figure into your own setting. The note records that the headline agreement is for strong judges on one task family, that a judge cannot reliably grade a problem it cannot solve, that a crowdsourced preference population is not neutral, and that agreement with human preference is not correctness — a judge that faithfully reproduces a preference for confident wrong answers is a well-validated judge of the wrong thing.
- **[research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)** — open access, read in full. *Good for:* the whole of step 6 and step 7 — clustered judgements, paired comparison, and stating the smallest effect a design could detect before running it. *Will not support:* the choice of criterion, and not this day's own boundary either. The note states that the framework compares scores rather than choosing what to score, that it does not address criteria drift, judge bias or rubric design, that a tight interval around a meaningless metric is still meaningless, and — directly relevant to phase 3 — that where an eval's questions were hand-selected or curated, **the inference machinery is valid about the wrong target.**
