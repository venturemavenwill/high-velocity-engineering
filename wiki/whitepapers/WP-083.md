## In plain terms

The practice corpus behind this programme says, once, in a line of practitioner judgement, that momentum decays after the initial engagement unless there is a deliberate reinforcing rhythm.

**The remedy it proposes is a continuing engagement cadence, which is also what the delivering organisation sells.** Both of those facts are true and neither cancels the other. The observation may well be right. The party making it is the party paid to supply the answer, and the person who drafts the proposal usually has a utilisation target that the proposal satisfies.

This session's whole method is one ordering rule: **write the version of the remedy that earns your organisation nothing, and finish it, before writing anything else.** If the unpaid version turns out to be a paragraph and the paid version a schedule, that asymmetry is the finding, and it is a finding about you rather than about the customer.

There is a second reason to teach the tension rather than resolve it. The best-known account of why changes regress holds that declaring a change finished is itself the mechanism — that people who never agreed with it have been waiting for exactly that moment, and that celebrating a win is where efforts are lost rather than where they are secured. **That account is craft knowledge from an author who states plainly that he cites nothing.** It is worth having, it is recognisable, and it is not evidence.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching reinforcement design as a conflicted act rather than as a technique. The argument is that the only available claim about post-engagement decay comes from a party with a commercial interest in the remedy, that the diagnosis and the proposal are ordinarily written in one pass so that the conclusion precedes the analysis, and that the only reliable test of whether a practitioner believes a diagnosis is whether they will specify the version of it that excludes them. The session produces a two-part reinforcement design — an unpaid version with a named owner and a triggered cadence, and a conflict-of-interest statement written into the document — followed by a single recommendation which may be either. The paper concedes that no source behind this programme establishes that momentum decays, that reinforcement prevents decay, or that any engagement anywhere recommended the unpaid option, and that the session's central method is an argument with no precedent.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Three pressures converge on the same recommendation and none of them is illegitimate.

**The diagnosis is probably correct.** Changes do appear to regress; the people who objected do not usually leave; and an organisation that has just absorbed a new way of working has not yet made it ordinary. Nothing in this paper argues that decay is imaginary.

**The remedy is chargeable.** A recurring review with the delivering organisation is a real service, it is what the customer would buy if they believed the diagnosis, and it is drafted by somebody whose next quarter depends on it. The corpus behind this programme is candid enough to state the decay claim and does not anywhere separate it from the interest.

**The alternative is unowned.** The unpaid version requires a person inside the customer with the authority, the time and the standing to keep something going after the interesting part has finished. In the supplied situation that person is under-authorised, and in most real situations they are too. **The honest unpaid design is frequently harder to specify than the paid one, which is a reason it does not get written and not a reason it is wrong.**

The session's job is to make the second and third visible in the same document, and to require that the practitioner commit to one of them in their own name.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why the unpaid version first.** Written second, it becomes an argument for the paid one, and the author cannot tell that it has. This is an ordering effect on the author rather than on the reader, and the countermeasure is the ordering itself. **The session assesses that the unpaid version is a real design, not that it is chosen.**

**3.2 Why the conflict statement is a section rather than a disclosure.** A disclosure at the end of a document is read by nobody and discharges nothing. A section the reader reaches while forming their view changes what they do with the recommendation. **This programme's position is that a conflict which cannot be written into the body of a document is a conflict the author has not accepted.**

**3.3 Why reinforcement must attach to an accomplishment.** Recognition, reporting or ceremony attached to nothing achieved does damage rather than nothing: it teaches the recipients that the mechanism is decorative, and it does so faster than an absent mechanism would. This is direction and mechanism from the readiness model and carries that model's warrant.

**3.4 Why countervailing pressure is required to be named.** Whatever else the organisation rewards competes with the new behaviour and wins by default. A design that does not name the competition has assumed it away. The strongest available illustration is a practitioner case in which a change programme was executed competently and then dismantled when the team that ran it was disbanded — with the diagnosis that the team had assumed managing a change is completing the activities in a project plan.

**3.5 What is not claimed about time.** No timescale appears in this session or in any artefact it produces. The sources offer timescales; they are recollective, uncited and inconsistent with each other, and this repository does not inherit them. **Direction only.**

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**The proposal in the supplied situation is deliberately good.** It is chargeable, it is probably the right diagnosis, and it is drafted by an interested party. A situation in which the proposal was obviously self-serving would teach a much easier discrimination and would flatter the cohort.

**The unpaid version has a hard requirement: a named owner employed by the customer.** This is what makes it a design rather than a gesture, and in the supplied situation it forces a real problem — the willing operator has no authority, the technology function has authority and no willingness, and the supplier holds the credentials and is contracted for something else. **Every route out of that is a compromise, which is the point.**

**The closing question is fixed and has three acceptable answers.** *Would you still recommend this if your organisation were not paid for it?* Yes is defensible. No is defensible and is the more interesting answer. **I do not know is the most honest thing said in most rooms where this arises, and the session records it as a completed answer rather than as a failure to answer.**

**The session does not assess which recommendation is made.** It assesses whether the unpaid version was designed before the paid one was argued for, and whether the interest was written where a reader would meet it.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The diagnosis that is also the sale.** *Countermeasure:* the ordering rule, and the visible asymmetry between the two parts when it is violated.

**Reinforcement with no accomplishment.** A cadence that would run unchanged in a month where the system did nothing. *Countermeasure:* the requirement to state what the cadence would report in such a month.

**The remedy requiring the author's presence.** *Countermeasure:* the named-owner requirement, which fails immediately if the owner is not employed by the customer.

**Victory declared.** The engagement closed well, so the change is treated as settled. *Countermeasure:* partial. The session names the mechanism and cannot rehearse it, because the interval over which it operates is longer than the programme.

**Timescale imported.** A learner reads the sources and produces a schedule justified by a period one of them mentions. *Countermeasure:* explicit prohibition, restated in the artefact standard and in §3.5 of this paper.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the whole session rests on a claim that appears once, unevidenced, in a commercially interested document.**

At its strongest: the decay assertion is a single line of practitioner judgement in a corpus that reports no post-deployment record for anything it describes, contains no sustainment evidence of any kind, and has arranged its evidence so that disappointing results confirm rather than challenge its thesis. Building a session on that line is building on the least supported sentence in the least supported part of the material. **A programme that spends ninety sessions teaching learners to demand warrants has here accepted one because it is useful.**

**This is conceded without qualification.** The decay claim has no warrant, the corroborating change-management sources have no independent evaluation between them, and the session asserts no rate, proportion, magnitude or timescale. **The fix that was considered and declined** was to drop the session and treat post-engagement decay as outside the programme's scope. It was rejected for a reason that is itself an argument rather than a finding: the recommendation gets made in practice whether or not anybody has examined it, and a learner who has never separated a diagnosis from the sale attached to it will not do so the first time it matters. **The objection is entitled to say that teaching a discipline for handling an unwarranted claim gives the claim a standing it has not earned.**

**Objection 2: requiring the unpaid design first is a moral posture that makes the practitioner worse at their actual job.**

At its strongest: the practitioner is employed to deliver value to their employer as well as to the customer, and a continuing relationship is frequently the arrangement under which the customer gets sustained attention they would otherwise not receive. Insisting that the free version be designed first invites a learner to treat their own organisation's interest as suspect by default, produces documents their commercial function will not accept, and models a purity that will be trained out of them within a year.

The rebuttal is narrow and concedes the frame. **The session does not require the unpaid version to be recommended and explicitly permits the paid one.** What it requires is that the practitioner know what the free version would have been, because a recommendation made without that knowledge is not a recommendation, it is the only option that was drafted. Whether a commercial function would accept a proposal containing a conflict-of-interest section is untested, and no source behind this programme reports one being attempted.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** In most submissions the unpaid version will be shorter and less specific than the paid one, measured by the presence of named owners, triggers and artefacts rather than by length alone. *Instrument:* both parts coded for the four specificity elements, compared within submission.

**Prediction 2.** Learners who write the unpaid version first, as instructed, will more often name an owner employed by the customer than those who report writing both together. *Instrument:* the named owner against the self-reported working order.

**Prediction 3.** Most conflict-of-interest statements will describe the organisation's interest and omit the author's own. *Instrument:* the statements, coded for whose interest is named.

**Prediction 4.** The closing question will produce more *I do not know* answers than either alternative. *Instrument:* the answers, recorded verbatim and classified. **If it produces almost none, the question is being heard as a test with a correct answer** and it should be replaced rather than repeated.

**None of these has been measured.** No cohort has run this session.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That the test of a diagnosis is whether you will write the remedy that excludes you; that reinforcement attached to no accomplishment does damage rather than nothing; and that a conflict which cannot go in the body of the document has not been accepted.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about whether momentum decays, how fast, or whether anything prevents it. Every source here is a commercially interested practitioner account without independent evaluation. Everything about the session's method is this design's argument.

**The concession worth remembering.** This session is built on the weakest sentence in its source material, and §8 concedes that without qualification. If you find that unsatisfying, you have read it correctly.

## Evidence status

**Verified in this repository.** That the practice corpus asserts, as a line of practitioner judgement, that momentum decays without a deliberate reinforcing rhythm after the initial engagement, and that the same corpus reports no post-deployment record for any case, no sustainment evidence of any kind, no cost side of any claim, and no recorded failure other than one synthetic composite — recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md). That the change-management source used for the regression argument states in its own 1996 preface that it draws neither examples nor major ideas from any published source except the author's own writing and cites no evidence to bolster its conclusions; that its cases are anonymised past a reader's ability to check; that its counts are recalled rather than counted, with one paragraph moving between two denominators; and that the widely quoted change-failure statistic does not appear in it at all — recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md](/research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md) on a complete reading of the 2012 reissue. **And that the claim about effort past the blocking element being wasted is not in the readiness model's book, and was withdrawn from this repository on reading it** — recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md).

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That a change declared finished regresses, and that opposition which did not dissipate waits for the declaration; that reinforcement unattached to an accomplishment can backfire; that accountability embedded in day-to-day operation sustains a change more reliably than recognition does; and that an author who has already drafted a conclusion cannot reliably evaluate the alternative. **Direction and mechanism only; no rate, proportion, magnitude or timescale is asserted here, and none may be inherited from the sources named above.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The two-part reinforcement design; the ordering rule and the claim that the ordering acts on the author rather than the reader; the conflict-of-interest statement as a body section; the named-owner-employed-by-the-customer requirement; the treatment of *I do not know* as a completed answer; the assessment position that the recommendation itself is not assessed; and all four predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** **This session has no platform anchor and relies on no vendor documentation for its content.** The silence worth recording is a commercial one rather than a technical one: **no vendor documentation known to this programme describes what happens to a customer's use of a product after a delivering team disengages, publishes a definition of a post-engagement support taper, or states what a customer must hold in order to sustain a delivered system without the party that built it.** Product documentation describes what a platform does; delivery material describes what a team does while present; the interval after both is documented by nobody with an interest in documenting it. **That is the same silence recorded at [WP-080](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-080.md), observed one step further along.** If any vendor publishes a sustainment or taper definition, this session acquires an anchor and this paper records its version and what it still omits.
