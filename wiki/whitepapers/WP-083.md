## In plain terms

**What this paper argues, before the formal version.**

You have been trained for four quarters to say more. A number needs an interval; an interval carries a population; a criterion carries a provenance; a claim carries a scope. Every one of those lessons is right, and every one of them pushes the same way.

**This day is about where that road ends.** A statement can be true, complete, correctly qualified — and undecidable. Nobody can do anything with it. It reads as rigour, it will be praised, and on the substrate's own record it produced six weeks in which nothing was decided and then the thing everyone was worried about happened. That is not a failure of honesty. It is the natural output of a well-trained engineer with a hard risk and a decision-maker they respect.

The room's second belief is harder to shift than the first. **Everybody thinks a good risk communication is one that produces the right decision.** The design's position is that it produces an *informed* decision made by the person entitled to make it — and that this can be the worse outcome, and still be the success. You cannot be told that; you will file it as consolation for having been ignored. So it is shown, on a record, with the outcomes attached, and the case that wins the ranking is the case that lost the argument.

Then it becomes real. A panel of people who actually run the service, with authority and no technical background, hears the student's live risk and **decides, that afternoon, in front of them.** Some of those decisions go against the student. A decision against the recommendation is explicitly not a failure, and the specification says so in writing — because the alternative is a room that learns to shade a risk until the answer comes out right.

**What this paper concedes, and it is a real concession.** A day whose whole discipline is subtraction has been given a build that produces material: thirty minutes and an agent to turn one number into five, immediately before a ten-minute encounter where the skill is leaving things out. The paper has no evidence that a better number makes a more decidable statement, and it has a live mechanism suggesting the opposite. The fix that would tie the number to the decision was declined because it would penalise the student who honestly said *I do not have it yet*. **And the fix the paper would actually take — drop the build from this day — is forbidden by a programme rule written for other days, which the paper records rather than works around.**

## Abstract

This paper defends the programme's closest rehearsal of EA-3, and defends separating the success condition of a risk communication from the outcome it produces. The target competence is stating a live risk in the units of a decision that is not yours, to a real decision-maker with authority and no technical background, on a day the decision is made. The mechanism the design leans on hardest is a contrasting-case set in which the best of four communications produced a decision against the engineer and a worse outcome, and the one that produced the right outcome is the worst of the four. **A fifth communication is added and the room generates it: the agent's, produced from the same record in under a minute, complete, correctly qualified and undecidable — so that the day's characteristic failure is something the cohort did four minutes ago rather than something other engineers did.** The principal risk is that a cohort trained for four quarters in qualification will produce statements that are true, complete and undecidable, and will believe the duty discharged. **Section 7 reports the second reading of the [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) entrustment distribution, which is reported and not compared, per instrumentation rule 2.** Section 8 records the third instance of the compliance register's binding-surface question, takes the one-clause fix, argues an entry, and faces the objection that a day about decidability has been given a build that produces completeness.

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

The cohort's difficulty here is caused by their training, and by the part of it that worked.

Since [M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md) the programme has taught, correctly and repeatedly, that a number without an interval is not a result, that an interval carries a population, that a criterion carries a provenance, and that a claim carries a scope. [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) added the professional half: say what the measurement does not license, to a party who wants it to license more, in language they can act on. Every one of those lessons is right and every one of them pushes in the same direction — **toward saying more.**

**The failure this day is about is the endpoint of that direction.** A statement that is true, complete, correctly qualified and undecidable is the natural product of a well-trained engineer with a hard risk and a decision-maker they respect. It reads as rigour. It will be praised. And on the substrate's own record it produced six weeks in which nothing was decided, followed by the event.

The difficulty source is therefore not a missing concept. It is **a counterintuitive result held in place by a rewarded habit**, which is the hardest combination the [Learning Science Foundations](/wiki/program/02-Learning-Science-Foundations.md) recognises, because the habit supplies a confident wrong answer at the moment of use.

A second difficulty is that the day's success condition is not the outcome. The room's criterion — shared by every student, most staff and all of their future managers — is that a successful risk communication produces the right decision. **The design's position is that it produces an informed decision made by the person entitled to make it, and that this can be worse.** A student told this will file it as consolation for being ignored. It has to be shown, on a record, with the outcomes attached, which is what phase 3 is for and why the payload is the case that lost.

The third is affective and is specific to a rehearsal that is real. Every student will be overruled at some rate this afternoon, in front of a person with authority, on a risk they have worked on. [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s audiences went away. This one decides.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**The intrinsic load is a unit conversion and nothing else.** Everything hard about this day is in the delivery and in accepting the success condition.

The interactivity is between steps 2, 3 and 5. The four decision units determine which measurements are relevant; the interval must be expressed in those same units, which frequently reveals that the measurement was taken on a different population from the one the decision concerns; and step 5 asks what was left out, which is only answerable once steps 2 and 3 have forced a choice. A student who does step 5 first writes a disclaimer.

Load reductions:

- **The risk is the student's own and is already characterised** by [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) or [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md).
- **The four units are supplied as a form** at step 2, so no time is spent on structure.
- **The unit conversion is worked once with the room** on a supplied measurement at step 3.
- **The delivery is ten minutes**, which is short enough that a student cannot recover by adding material — the day's characteristic failure has no room to operate.

Expertise reversal applies at phase 4 for any student who has communicated risk professionally, and the design's answer is that such a student holds points 2, 3 and 5 and does not hold point 1. Professional experience of this act mostly teaches people to get the decision they wanted, which is the belief phase 3 exists to disturb.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Writing the sentence you would actually say, before knowing which decision it serves,* item 1 against item 2.
- *Being asked the asymmetry cold,* item 3.
- *Predicting a real person's real decision,* item 4, resolved the same day.
- *A payload that is the best communication and the worse outcome,* R4.
- *A ten-minute delivery,* which forecloses the completeness strategy.
- *Stating what you left out,* step 5, unsupported.
- *A real decision-maker who decides, and may decide against you,* step 6.
- *Recording your own losing position in the decision's own record,* step 7.
- *A partner who must name a decision rather than a paraphrase,* phase 7.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *A hostile or incompetent decision-maker.* Point 8's safe case assumes competence and good faith, and the day holds to it. A decision-maker who cannot be reasoned with is [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md)'s and the four moves are named rather than practised.
- *Multiple audiences.* [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) varied the audience four ways. This day varies nothing and puts everything into one real encounter, because the variability lesson is already held and the reality is not.
- *Written-only delivery.* The statement outlives the conversation at [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) and [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md); today it is spoken and recorded.
- *Quantifying the decision-maker's other inputs.* Point 8 names them; weighing them is not the engineer's act and simulating it would teach the opposite of the point.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** Point 8's one-input position is **safe where the decision-maker is competent, is acting in good faith, and the harm is bounded and reversible.** That is the substrate's condition and the day states it as a property of the substrate rather than of the world. It is **unsafe where the harm is irreversible or falls on people with no recourse.** The failure mode is exact and is not a matter of emphasis: a student who accepts *I am one input, they decided, I recorded it* in a case where the harm is permanent and the affected party has no channel has used a professional position that is correct in the bounded case to discharge a duty that the bounded case is what makes discharged. The homes are named — [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) point 7's four moves, and the safety-case and disclosure obligations at [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) and [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) — and, unusually for this design, **the boundary is a checked artefact element**: step 1's second clause requires the student to state whether the harm is bounded and reversible and, where it is not, to say that the day's form does not apply. §8 objection 1 explains why this day binds its artefact where the rule as written binds only this paper, and what that costs.

Point 2's enumeration also carries a scope statement: the four decision units omit regulatory and contractual exposure, reputational and second-order effects, and anything with a legal character. All three are outside the programme and all three are inputs to real decisions.

**The platform instance carries a third, at point 9, and it is bound to the artefact.** Reading a cost or exposure figure from the platform's near-real-time surfaces is **safe for steering a decision inside the current period, where the decision-maker will act on direction and magnitude and will not later reconcile the figure against an invoice.** It is **unsafe for reconciliation and unsafe in front of anybody who will see the settled account.** The failure is exact and the vendor states the mechanism itself: Foundry's estimated cost and Microsoft Cost Management's invoiced charges differ through ingestion timing and aggregation, there is roughly a five-hour delay from a billing event to its appearance in cost analysis, and Foundry costs are only a subset of a solution's costs. **A student who quotes the near-real-time figure to a budget holder has not made an error of arithmetic; they have answered a reconciliation question with a monitoring instrument**, and the number will be contradicted later by a number that is also correct. **The artefact element that binds it is step 8's requirement that the figure carry its as-of time and its named source of record in the same field**, per specification rule 2, with a fail condition for its absence — which is the same binding §8 objection 1 argues for at step 1, applied a second time in the same day.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **the decision being made.**

Every prior communication occasion in this programme ended when the conversation did. [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md)'s partner said what they understood. [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s role-players walked out. [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md)'s payers were real and the ask was real, and that day is the nearest thing to this one — but a cost argument is a request, and a request that is refused leaves the requester's position intact. **Here the student's risk goes into a decision that is made this afternoon and the student watches it happen.**

Competence support is at item 4, which is a prediction about another person that the student can check by six o'clock, and at the phase 8 report of the cohort's overrule rate, which converts a personal experience into a base rate. Autonomy is at steps 4 and 5, both unsupported and both judgements. Relatedness is supplied by the decision-maker being a colleague of the institution the service belongs to.

**The affective risk is concentrated and is the day's largest.** A student overruled by a real authority on a real risk, having predicted at item 4 that they would not be, is in a position that the programme's own dispositions framework calls humility and that a twenty-one-year-old will experience as being dismissed. Countermeasures: point 7 states what being overruled means and does not mean, before phase 5; a decision against the recommendation is not recorded as a failure and the specification says so in writing; the cohort's overrule rate is reported at phase 8 with the design's statement that it expects the figure to be substantial; and the phase 6 clinic is placed immediately after the encounter, opened with a case in which the engineer was right, was overruled, and was later blamed — which names the worst version of the experience before any student has to name it themselves.

## 4. Phase-by-phase design and its warrant

**Phase 1 (25 min).** Five sealed items on a real, unresolved risk. Item 2 is the opening finding; item 4 is the only same-day prediction about another person's behaviour in the programme. **Item 5 is the sealed Copilot prediction and its expected answer is that the agent will produce R1's shape in forty seconds.**

**Phase 2 (20 min).** Four retrievals at two years, four quarters, one quarter and one quarter, closing with a bridge that produces point 4 before phase 3 runs.

**Phase 3 (45 min).** **Six minutes executed, then thirty-nine demonstrated.** The executed part is R5, generated by the room from the same record. The demonstrated part is four communications of one risk to one decision-maker, sampling disclosed, adjudicated by the decision and the outcome; they cannot be run and are **not assessed**. Payload R4; R2 placed second because it splits the room. **R5's placement near R1 is the phase's second payload and costs nothing to obtain.**

**Phase 4 (50 min).** Ten points. Points 1 and 7 are reached from R4; point 4 is reached from the bridge and is made the room's own error by R5. Point 2 carries the enumeration scope statement, point 8 the strong-form one, and point 8's final clause is an artefact element. **Points 9 and 10 are the platform instance, placed after the argument per this module's convention; point 9 carries the third strong-form statement and point 10 names the default that is about to be mis-promised this afternoon.**

**Phase 5 (90 min).** Eight steps on a live risk with a real decision-maker who decides today. Steps 4 and 5 unsupported. Step 1's applicability clause and step 7's record location are checked elements. **Step 8 is the build, timeboxed to thirty of the ninety, performed first and listed last, with the panel's time timetabled ahead of it and an explicit permission to deliver without the figure.**

**Phase 6 (40 min).** A good communication, an overrule, a bad outcome, a record in the wrong place — **and an agent-assembled cost statement whose number was right and whose instrument was wrong.**

**Phase 7 (30 min).** Decidability rather than comprehension, deliberately replacing the [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md) protocol whose degradation [WP-024](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-024.md) §8 predicted at this day.

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Item 2's rate as the headline and the branch driver; item 4 against the real decisions; **item 5's two rates and the cohort's R5 ranking**; the step 8 provenance outcomes; the [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) second reading, reported and not compared.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has briefed a manager professionally will deliver fluently and will produce a good four-unit statement. The predicted failure is **at step 4**: they will omit the direction-of-error sentence, deliberately, because in professional practice stating a preference is how you get held to it. Countermeasure: it is the day's single hard specification requirement, it is stated as a fail rather than a deduction, and point 6 states in advance that declining to give a requested judgement is not neutrality. The fluent student is the one this requirement exists for.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is a statement in the four units that is nonetheless a summary — the form filled in, the conversion unattempted, and the interval carried over in parameter units. Countermeasures: the parameter-unit interval is a named fail condition; the conversion is worked once with the room; and phase 7's partner must name a decision, which a summary cannot support.

**Phase 3 is too easy.** Some groups will rank R4 first before the outcomes are supplied, because R4 is visibly the most careful artefact. That does not defeat the phase, because the ranking is not the payload — **the payload is that R4 lost.** The difficulty is relocated to the task-after: state what the decision-maker could do after each communication that they could not do before. R2 is the hard one there, since it produced the right decision and enabled nothing, and a group that answers R2 correctly has done the day's central discrimination. Fallback if a group answers R2: ask what R2's author should have done differently given that their decision-maker acted correctly and promptly on the sentence supplied, which has no clean answer and previews point 3's unsettled boundary.

**Affective failure.** Two.

The first is **being overruled**, described in §3.3 and countered there. The countermeasures are real and partial.

The second is quieter and worse: **a student who is not overruled and reads it as success.** They will be the confident students, the fluent ones, and the ones whose statements most resembled a recommendation — which is R3. Countermeasure: point 1's success condition is stated in terms of the decision-maker's engagement rather than the outcome, phase 4 point 7's last sentence says that a student who has never been overruled has probably been making the decisions, and the phase 7 protocol asks a partner to name the decision the statement drove, which catches R3's shape directly. **This countermeasure is weaker than the first and the design knows it**: the student who was agreed with has no experience today that contradicts them, and the correction is delivered verbally, which is the mode this paper's own §1 says does not dislodge a rewarded habit.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md). **EA-3 is assessed at PE301, CAP390 and SG390, and this day is a rehearsal**, as the [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) records.

One specification item, in ME390 with PE390. Per rule 1 the checked elements are the risk and its decision with the decision-maker and date, the applicability statement, the four decision units, the interval in the decision-maker's units with provenance and population in the same field, the direction-of-error sentence with its reason, the selected-out material with its reason, the outcome in the decision-maker's own words in the decision's own record with the residual risk and the re-raising condition, the phase 7 result, **the shipped figure with its as-of time and named source of record in the same field or the statement that it was unavailable, the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

Three hard requirements: **a statement with no direction-of-error sentence fails**; **an interval stated in parameter units fails**; and **a figure delivered without its as-of time and named source of record fails.** A decision made against the student's recommendation is explicitly not a failure and the specification says so in writing, and **a figure not delivered at all does not fail** provided the student said so and named when it would be available — which is itself a decidable sentence and is the day's subject rather than an exception to it.

**What this day does not license anyone to conclude.** It generates evidence that a student can put a risk into a decision-maker's units and can record an outcome they lost. It generates none about EA-3, and the assessors' brief says so: EA-3 requires three assessors across multiple occasions and this is one occasion with a specification check. It also generates no evidence about the student's conduct where the harm is irreversible, because the substrate has no such risk, and the applicability statement at step 1 exists precisely so that the boundary of the evidence is visible in the artefact rather than only in this paper.

### The second reading of the S065 entrustment distribution

**This paragraph is the report [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md)'s instrumentation block requires.**

[S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md)'s first rule-6 instrument — the supervision-level distribution and the phase 4 least-defensible-number responses — named [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) as its response occasion for the EA-1 half and stated: "**A second reading is taken at [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) for the EA-3 half.**" **The reading is taken.** The S065 distribution is shown at phase 8 beside this day's specification outcomes, and the room is told what it is.

**The two readings are reported and are not compared.** This is instrumentation rule 2 applied at the point it was written for: the elicitation S065 and this day share — a real counterparty, a prepared position, a self-nominated weakest element, a defence — is the same protocol on its second administration, and [WP-065](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-065.md) §6 stated the consequence in advance: "**The two occasions must not be compared as instances of one instrument, per rule 2, because the protocol is shared and the second reading would measure familiarity.**"

That instruction is honoured in a specific and slightly awkward way, and the awkwardness is worth stating. **The design shows both figures to the room and tells the room not to draw a line between them.** A cohort shown two distributions from two occasions will draw the line whatever they are told, which is a known property of presenting paired numbers. The alternative — not showing the S065 figure at all — would have discharged rule 2 more cleanly and would have withheld from students a result about themselves that the programme's own transparency commitments say they are owed. **The design has chosen transparency over instrument hygiene, has said so in the room, and records here that rule 2's requirement is satisfied in the report and is not satisfiable in the reading.**

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments. **The item 2 decision-identification rate** responds at [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md) phase 3 as a branch: a low cohort rate causes S085 to supply each counterparty's decision and date as given material rather than requiring the student to establish them, so that an entrustment occasion does not turn on a competence this day found absent. **The step 6 and step 7 outcomes** — the overrule proportion, and the proportion of records made in the decision's own record in the decision-maker's own words — respond at [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md), whose residual-risk statement must be written for a reader who decided against the engineer and must carry one risk the student raised and lost. **[WP-085](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-085.md) §7 and [WP-088](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-088.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the day binds its own artefact to its scope statement, which no other day does, and doing it once makes the programme's open question harder to see rather than easier.**

At its strongest: the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s compliance register carries one entry on the scope rule's *binding surface* — [WP-049](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-049.md) §8 — and states plainly that the question was left open because "a rule requiring every scope statement to appear as an assessed artefact element would be substantially more expensive than the four widenings that preceded it, and because one instance is not yet evidence that the gap is load-bearing. It is stated here so that a second instance is recognised as a second instance." [WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 supplied the second, argued its entry, and explicitly declined the one-clause fix at its own day: "**It is not taken here because taking it at one day and not at the other twenty-three would make the register's open question harder to see rather than easier, and the question is worth more open than quietly answered at a single site.**"

**This day takes the fix. Step 1's applicability clause is a checked element, and this paper is therefore doing exactly what WP-064 declined to do and gave a reason for.**

The objection has real force and the paper answers it in three parts rather than dismissing it.

**First, this is the third instance and the register's stated reason for leaving the question open no longer applies.** The reason was that one instance is not evidence that the gap is load-bearing. It is now at three, across three modules and three registers — an evaluation criterion at WP-049, a commercial break-even at WP-064, and a professional position at this day. The entry is argued here in the register's own terms:

> \| [WP-083](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-083.md) §8 — [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) restricts its one-input position to bounded, reversible harm with a competent good-faith decision-maker, and **binds the boundary as a checked artefact element at step 1**, departing from the register's two prior instances \| Scope rule, strong form \| **Recorded** \| Third instance of the binding-surface question opened at [WP-049](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-049.md) §8 and continued at [WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8. The departure is a design judgement the whitepaper is arguing for, not a correction, and the register's criterion assigns judgements to *recorded* \|

**Second, WP-064's reason was about visibility and this day's case is not symmetric with it.** WP-064's unbound scope statement concerned a break-even calculation: a student who applies it outside its conditions computes a number that does not decide the question, and the cost is a bad recommendation the design can inspect. **This day's concerns whether an engineer treats a duty as discharged when the harm is irreversible and falls on people with no recourse.** The cost is not a bad artefact and the design cannot inspect it, because the case does not occur on the substrate — the substrate's harm is bounded by construction, which is what makes the day safe to run and also what makes it silent about the case it most needs to bound. A rule left open for cost reasons is defensible; leaving this particular statement unbound because a general rule would be expensive is not the same decision.

**Third, and conceding what should be conceded: taking the fix at one site does damage the register's question, exactly as WP-064 said.** The programme now contains one day whose scope statement is an assessed element and eighty-nine whose are not, and a reader reconstructing the compliance state will find an inconsistency rather than an open question. **The design has traded the legibility of a programme-level question for the safety of one day's professional claim, and it is not obvious that was the right trade.** What would make it right is closing the question — binding every strong-form scope statement to an artefact element, at whatever cost — and that is a decision for whoever ratifies the register, not for this paper. **What this paper will not do is leave a student holding "I am one input, they decided, I recorded it" with no artefact element asking whether they were in the case where that is true.**

**Objection 2: the day predicts a substantial overrule rate, tells students it expects one, and thereby manufactures the finding it reports.**

At its strongest: phase 8 reports the proportion of decisions made against the students' recommendations, and the design states in the room that it expects the figure to be substantial and does not treat it as a failure rate. The decision-makers are institutional service owners who know the day's purpose, know they are part of a teaching exercise, and know that a room of students has been told being overruled is normal. **A decision-maker in that position has been given permission to decline**, and the resulting figure measures the briefing at least as much as it measures the communications.

The rebuttal is partial. The decisions are real and are made on the substrate, so a service owner who declines a recommendation carries the consequence of declining it, which is a stronger constraint than a role-player faces. The decision-makers are briefed to decide as they would decide, and the briefing is not *decline more*.

**But the objection is conceded on the point that matters, which is what the design does with the number.** The figure is used at phase 8 as a countermeasure to the affective risk — to show a student who was overruled that this is the base rate — and a manufactured base rate performs that function perfectly well while being worthless as evidence. **The paper's §9 prediction 3 is therefore stated with this contamination named**, and the design does not claim the rate measures communication quality.

The cheap fix is available and is declined. **The decision-makers could be given no information about the day's purpose beyond the risk and the decision**, which would remove the permission effect at the cost of asking a colleague to participate in something they have not been told the point of. It is declined on that ground: the programme requires the substrate's users to be told about deliberate disturbance, per the disclosure obligation [WP-035](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-035.md) §8 established, and asking a member of staff to make a real decision inside an assessment exercise without telling them it is one is the same failure in the other direction. **The design has chosen to contaminate an instrument rather than to withhold a material fact from a colleague, and it prefers that trade, but the instrument is contaminated and the number should not be reported to external reviewers as a measure of anything.**

**Objection 3: this is a day about decidability, and the design has given it a build that produces completeness.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn.

At its strongest: the day's argument is that a statement can be true, complete, correctly qualified and useless, and that the cohort's failure mode is adding material. **Step 8 adds material.** It gives every student thirty minutes and an agent with which to produce a figure, its provenance, its population, its as-of time and its source of record — five things where they previously had one — and it does so immediately before a ten-minute encounter whose whole discipline is subtraction. A student who arrives with a well-built number is more likely to say it, and R1 is what saying everything looks like. **The day has, in effect, resourced the failure it is trying to prevent, and the resource came from a standard written for days whose problem is that students do not have enough evidence.**

The rebuttal has two real parts and neither is complete. The delivery is ten minutes and cannot absorb five extra sentences, which is the design's standing defence against the completeness strategy and is unchanged. And the build's failure condition is not about quantity: what is checked is that the figure carries its as-of time and its source of record **in the same field**, which is specification rule 2 and is a compression requirement rather than an expansion one — *seventy a week, invoiced basis, as at Tuesday* is shorter than the two sentences it replaces.

**The objection is conceded on the part the rebuttals do not reach.** The design has no evidence that a student holding a better number produces a more decidable statement, and it has a live mechanism suggesting the opposite: the phase 3 payload exists precisely because good material and good decisions are uncorrelated in the cases the substrate has recorded.

**The cheap fix is named and is declined.** It would be to make the shipped figure a **required element of step 7's record** — the decision's own record must name the number the decision-maker was given — which would tie the build to the decision rather than to the artefact and would cost one clause. **It is declined for a reason the design believes and states so it can be disagreed with: the day explicitly permits a student to deliver without the figure and say so**, and requiring the number in the record would penalise exactly the student who made the honest call. **The alternative fix — dropping the build from this day and leaving the cost surfaces to [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md) — is the one the paper would take if the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) permitted it, and it does not, because a day with no build does not exist in this programme.** That is a rule constraining a design judgement it was not written for, it is recorded here rather than worked around, and §9 prediction 5 is the test.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** At item 2, fewer than a third of students will be able to name both the decision their risk bears on and its date. *Instrument:* item 2 responses coded for a named decision and a named date, separately. **The separation is the claim**: naming a decision without a date is a different and lesser failure than naming neither, and only the second means the student is writing into a vacuum.

**Prediction 2.** At phase 3, groups will rank R2 above R3 and will rank both below R1, and the modal reason given for R1 will be its completeness. *Instrument:* rankings with free-text justification, coded for whether the justification names completeness, decidability or outcome. **If R1 is ranked below R3, the cohort already prefers a recommendation to a qualified statement** and the day's problem is R3's rather than R1's, which changes what phase 4 needs to weight.

**Prediction 3.** More than a quarter of decisions at step 6 will go against the student's recommendation, and item 4's predictions will under-estimate that rate. *Instrument:* step 6 outcomes against item 4 predictions, paired. **This prediction is contaminated by the briefing effect §8 objection 2 describes and is reported with that contamination attached**; the interpretable half is the item 4 gap, which is a claim about students' expectations rather than about decision-makers' behaviour.

**Prediction 4.** At phase 7, more than half of partners will be unable to name a decision the statement drove, and the failure will concentrate on statements whose step 3 interval was carried over in parameter units. *Instrument:* phase 7 outcomes coded for whether a decision was named, cross-tabulated against the step 3 interval's unit. **The cross-tabulation is the claim**: if decidability failures are evenly distributed across interval units, the unit conversion is not the mechanism the day says it is, and point 5 is a stylistic preference rather than a functional requirement.

**Prediction 5.** Groups will rank R5 above R4 before the outcomes are supplied, at a rate above half; **and students who completed the step 8 build will deliver statements no more decidable, at phase 7, than students who delivered without the figure.** *Instrument:* R5 rankings with justification; and phase 7 decision-naming rates cross-tabulated against whether the student's figure was ready at step 6. **The second half is §8 objection 3's test and the design expects to lose it in one of two directions.** If build-completers are *less* decidable, the build is resourcing the failure and should be moved out of the day. If they are *more* decidable, the objection is answered and the paper conceded too readily. **A null result is the least useful outcome and is the most likely**, because the sample is one cohort and the effect, if any, is small — which is itself worth stating in advance, per [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md)'s own discipline applied to this design.

## What a student should take from this paper

**What the design is claiming.** That a risk statement can be true, complete, correctly qualified and undecidable, and that this is the natural product of everything the programme has trained into you. The success condition is not the right decision; it is an informed decision made by the person entitled to make it, and that can be the worse outcome.

**What the evidence licenses.** Writing for a party who will act, interval construction and what a number licenses are **class 1**, grounded in [research/05-fde-craft/palantir-fde-practitioner-writing.md](/research/05-fde-craft/palantir-fde-practitioner-writing.md) and [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md). **So, as of 30 July 2026, is point 5's addition that a comparison establishes an ordering and not an absolute.** The cost surfaces are **class 4**, read on 26 July 2026: no hard budget cap, an estimate that is not the invoiced figure, a documented ingestion delay, and **no figure anywhere for how often an alert arrives before the money is spent.** **The four decision units and the success condition are class 3.**

**What to do with it.**

- Put the interval in the decision-maker's units. An interval carried over in parameter units fails the specification and, more to the point, decides nothing.
- Give the direction-of-error sentence. Declining a requested judgement is not neutrality.
- Carry a figure's as-of time and named source of record in the same field, or say you have neither. **An observed result is what was reported, at that revision, on that host** — see the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md).
- Record the outcome in the decision's own record, in the decision-maker's words, including when you lost.

**The concession worth remembering.** The paper concedes that a day about subtraction has been given a build that produces material — an agent and half the phase to turn one number into five — immediately before a ten-minute encounter whose whole discipline is leaving things out. It has no evidence that a better number produces a more decidable statement; the fix that would tie the number to the decision was declined because it would penalise an honest non-delivery; and **dropping the build is forbidden by a programme rule written for other days.**
