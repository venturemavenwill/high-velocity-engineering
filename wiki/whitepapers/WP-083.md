>
> | Term | Meaning |
> |---|---|

> | **Reinforcement design** | This session's artefact: an unpaid version, a conflict-of-interest statement, and one recommendation |
> | **Countervailing pressure** | Anything the organisation rewards that competes with the new behaviour, and which wins by default |
> | **Quota** | A policy limit on what may be requested. Costs nothing, guarantees nothing |

> | **Drift** | The distance between the workload a commitment was sized against and the workload as it now is |
> | **Graceful degradation** | A fallback with a configured trigger, a stated behaviour and a recovery interval set by the failing party |

## In plain terms

The practice corpus behind this programme says, once, in a line of practitioner judgement, that momentum decays after the initial engagement unless there is a deliberate reinforcing rhythm.

**The remedy it proposes is a continuing engagement cadence, which is also what the delivering organisation sells.** Both of those facts are true and neither cancels the other. The observation may well be right. The party making it is the party paid to supply the answer, and the person who drafts the proposal usually has a utilisation target that the proposal satisfies.

This session's whole method is one ordering rule: **write the version of the remedy that earns your organisation nothing, and finish it, before writing anything else.** If the unpaid version turns out to be a paragraph and the paid version a schedule, that asymmetry is the finding, and it is a finding about you rather than about the customer.

Underneath the commercial argument sits a claim about mechanism, and it is the one worth carrying: **momentum decays because the conditions that produced it were specific and the world moved.** Momentum is not a substance that drains. It is several conditions holding at once — somebody senior kept asking, the alternative was still painful, the builders were reachable, the workload still looked like the workload the thing was designed for — each with its own half-life and none correlated with the others. Nothing decays. Several independent things change, and the conjunction stops holding.

**The platform makes that concrete in a form nobody can argue with.** A capacity commitment is sized against a traffic model: request shape, output-to-input ratio, cache rate. Six months later a team adds a caching layer, another switches the assistant from summarising to drafting, a third onboards a second application. **The commitment did not change, because a commitment is a document and a workload is a population of behaviours** — and nothing in the platform emits an event when a sizing assumption stops being true. The organisational version of that failure is invisible and disputable. The capacity version is arithmetic.

There is a second reason to teach the tension rather than resolve it. The best-known account of why changes regress holds that declaring a change finished is itself the mechanism — that people who never agreed with it have been waiting for exactly that moment, and that celebrating a win is where efforts are lost rather than where they are secured. **That account is craft knowledge from an author who states plainly that he cites nothing.** It is worth having, it is recognisable, and it is not evidence.

## Abstract

This paper defends teaching reinforcement design as a conflicted act rather than as a technique, and defends anchoring it on a capacity commitment because the mechanical case makes the organisational one legible.

The argument runs in four parts. **First**, the only available claim about post-engagement decay comes from a party with a commercial interest in the remedy; the diagnosis and the proposal are ordinarily written in one pass so that the conclusion precedes the analysis; and the only reliable test of whether a practitioner believes a diagnosis is whether they will specify the version of it that excludes them. **Second**, momentum decays because a conjunction of specific conditions stops holding, not because a quantity drains — which means a design that reinforces *the change* in general reinforces nothing, and a design that names one condition, its holder and its failure signature can be executed by somebody who was not there. **Third**, a capacity commitment sized against last quarter's traffic is the same failure with arithmetic attached: the sizing inputs are facts about past behaviour, ordinary engineering decisions move them, and no mechanism connects the person who improves a cache hit rate to the person holding the commitment. **Fourth**, the platform supplies the correct shape for a reinforcement design's failure branch — a fallback with a configured trigger fired by an observed error rather than by a forecast, and a recovery interval stated by the failing party rather than guessed at.

The session produces a two-part reinforcement design — an unpaid version with a named owner and a triggered cadence, and a conflict-of-interest statement written into the document — together with a decay prediction, a capacity re-derivation and a configured degradation, followed by a single recommendation which may be either.

The paper concedes that no source behind this programme establishes that momentum decays, that reinforcement prevents decay, or that any engagement anywhere recommended the unpaid option; that the session's central method is an argument with no precedent; and that the analogy between capacity drift and organisational decay is this design's own and may be exactly the false precision the programme warns about elsewhere.

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Four pressures converge on the same recommendation and none of them is illegitimate.

**The diagnosis is probably correct.** Changes do appear to regress; the people who objected do not usually leave; and an organisation that has just absorbed a new way of working has not yet made it ordinary. Nothing in this paper argues that decay is imaginary.

**The remedy is chargeable.** A recurring review with the delivering organisation is a real service, it is what the customer would buy if they believed the diagnosis, and it is drafted by somebody whose next quarter depends on it. The corpus behind this programme is candid enough to state the decay claim and does not anywhere separate it from the interest.

**The alternative is unowned.** The unpaid version requires a person inside the customer with the authority, the time and the standing to keep something going after the interesting part has finished. In the supplied situation that person is under-authorised, and in most real situations they are too. **The honest unpaid design is frequently harder to specify than the paid one, which is a reason it does not get written and not a reason it is wrong.**

**And the decay nobody proposed anything about is the one with a receipt.** A capacity commitment made against a traffic model is drifting from the moment it is signed, because the model is a description of past behaviour and the behaviour is a population that keeps changing for good reasons. **Nobody is at fault and no event fires.** That case is included because it removes the temptation to treat decay as a fact about people's commitment, and because it is the only instance in the session where the drift can be computed rather than asserted.

The session's job is to make the second, third and fourth visible in the same document, and to require that the practitioner commit to one recommendation in their own name.

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Why the unpaid version first.** Written second, it becomes an argument for the paid one, and the author cannot tell that it has. This is an ordering effect on the author rather than on the reader, and the countermeasure is the ordering itself. **The session assesses that the unpaid version is a real design, not that it is chosen.**

**3.2 Why the conflict statement is a section rather than a disclosure.** A disclosure at the end of a document is read by nobody and discharges nothing. A section the reader reaches while forming their view changes what they do with the recommendation. **This programme's position is that a conflict which cannot be written into the body of a document is a conflict the author has not accepted.**

**3.3 Why reinforcement must attach to an accomplishment.** Recognition, reporting or ceremony attached to nothing achieved does damage rather than nothing: it teaches the recipients that the mechanism is decorative, and it does so faster than an absent mechanism would. This is direction and mechanism from the readiness model and carries that model's warrant.

**3.4 Why countervailing pressure is required to be named.** Whatever else the organisation rewards competes with the new behaviour and wins by default. A design that does not name the competition has assumed it away. The strongest available illustration is a practitioner case in which a change programme was executed competently and then dismantled when the team that ran it was disbanded — with the diagnosis that the team had assumed managing a change is completing the activities in a project plan.

**3.5 What is not claimed about time.** No timescale appears in this session or in any artefact it produces. The sources offer timescales; they are recollective, uncited and inconsistent with each other, and this repository does not inherit them. **Direction only.**

**3.6 Why decay is taught as a conjunction failing rather than as a quantity draining.**

The metaphor matters because it determines what a design can contain. A quantity that drains implies a design that tops it up — a cadence, a refresher, a reminder — and such designs are unfalsifiable, because there is no state at which anybody could say the topping-up had failed.

**A conjunction failing implies a design that names each condition, its holder, and what its failure looks like.** That design can be executed by somebody who was not there, can be checked by somebody who does not trust you, and can be reported against. It is also considerably harder to write, which is why the first metaphor persists.

**This is design reasoning and carries no external warrant.** No source behind this programme decomposes momentum into conditions, and the decomposition is this paper's own.

**3.7 Why a capacity commitment is the worked instance.**

Provisioned capacity "holds a fixed amount of processing capacity exclusively for your deployment's use, whether or not requests are being made" ([research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md)). Sizing rests on three inputs: request shape, output-to-input ratio, and cache rate — where **"cached tokens don't consume PTU capacity, so a higher cache rate reduces the PTUs required."**

Three properties make this the right teaching object and each transfers.

**Every sizing input is a fact about past traffic.** The commitment is therefore drifting from the moment it is signed, and the drift is caused by correct engineering decisions made by people who do not know the commitment exists. **No mechanism connects them**, and a register that reads as an accusation will be ignored by exactly the people who could close that gap.

**Permission is not availability.** Quota is "a policy limit enforced by Azure, and it has no associated cost"; capacity is "the actual amount of PTUs per model version that's available to be deployed"; and the documentation states the consequence directly — **"Having PTU quota doesn't guarantee that capacity is available."** Availability moves through the day, and releasing capacity offers "no guarantee the same capacity is available" later. **The escape hatch is a one-way door that looks reversible**, which is the shape of most organisational momentum decisions and is worth carrying out of the platform entirely.

**And the correct commitment sequence is stated outright:** reservations "don't guarantee capacity. First create deployments to confirm that capacity is available, then purchase the reservation to lock in the discounted rate." **Deploy, verify, then commit.** The intuitive sequence — commit, then deploy — is exactly the sequence a reinforcement proposal follows when it is signed before anybody has checked that the customer can execute it.

**The source's limits.** First-party documentation, definitional rather than empirical; single-vendor, with no comparison against alternative capacity models; per-model ratios, minimum sizes and prices live on companion pages that were not fetched and are reproduced nowhere; names, defaults and portal paths change frequently; and the choice architecture is the vendor's, which is worth remembering in a session about proposals with an interest attached.

**3.8 Why the degradation branch is copied from the platform rather than invented.**

**Spillover** redirects requests to a standard deployment when a provisioned deployment is saturated and returns a non-success response. The trigger is an error response rather than a prediction: the system does not forecast saturation, it discovers it, and has already decided what to do. At the gateway, priority-based routing sends traffic to reserved capacity first with overflow elsewhere — the documentation names that case explicitly for provisioned endpoints — and the circuit breaker "features dynamic trip duration, applying values from the `Retry-After` header provided by the backend" ([research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md)), **letting the failing service state its own recovery time rather than being guessed at.**

Three properties transfer to the reinforcement design and together they define the standard: **an observable trigger rather than somebody noticing; a fallback stated in advance rather than improvised; and a recovery interval set by the party who knows it rather than by the party writing the plan.** A reinforcement design whose failure mode is *somebody will notice* has no failure mode.

**Limits:** policy names, tier availability and preview status change frequently; individual policy reference pages were not fetched; the gateway page does not discuss the privacy consequences of the logging it recommends.

**3.9 Why the opening items are unscaffolded, and why exercise 3 exists.**

The opening items are a Productive Failure generation phase: learners generate answers to a problem they have not been taught, fail, and are then taught ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). **The generation phase must not be scaffolded**, which is why §4's account of what a real unpaid design contains is gated. **The consolidation phase must explicitly prompt comparison** — presenting a correct answer beside a learner's wrong one achieves nothing on its own, and only the condition instructed to compare them learned more — which is why exercise 3 requires the learner to return to their item-2 answer and name the difference before rewriting it.

**Its limits are conceded in §8 of [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md) and hold here unchanged:** every study behind the design is a school, university or laboratory study; the source is a trade book; no adult-professional study appears in its reference list.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

**The proposal in the supplied situation is deliberately good.** It is chargeable, it is probably the right diagnosis, and it is drafted by an interested party. A situation in which the proposal was obviously self-serving would teach a much easier discrimination and would flatter the cohort.

**The unpaid version has a hard requirement: a named owner employed by the customer.** This is what makes it a design rather than a gesture, and in the supplied situation it forces a real problem — the willing operator has no authority, the technology function has authority and no willingness, and the supplier holds the credentials and is contracted for something else. **Every route out of that is a compromise, which is the point.**

**The closing question is fixed and has three acceptable answers.** *Would you still recommend this if your organisation were not paid for it?* Yes is defensible. No is defensible and is the more interesting answer. **I do not know is the most honest thing said in most rooms where this arises, and the session records it as a completed answer rather than as a failure to answer.**

**The session does not assess which recommendation is made.** It assesses whether the unpaid version was designed before the paid one was argued for, and whether the interest was written where a reader would meet it.

**What the platform workflow is for.** It is not a capacity-planning tutorial, and a learner who leaves able to size a deployment and unable to write an unpaid remedy has taken the wrong half.

It exists to make one thing physically observable: **the drift, as a figure the learner derived rather than a worry the learner has.** Nobody argues productively about whether momentum decays. Everybody can see that the cache rate assumed at signature and the cache rate measured this week differ, that the difference was caused by a named engineering decision, and that **nobody told the person holding the commitment because there is no mechanism by which they would have.**

**The second-order finding is the one worth carrying and it cannot be delivered by argument.** No individual was careless. Every decision that moved an input was correct engineering. **A learner who has watched a sizing assumption go stale with nobody at fault stops looking for someone to blame in the organisational case**, and starts looking for the missing mechanism — which is the only thing a reinforcement design can actually supply.

**The degradation step is deliberately executed rather than described.** The learner is required to drive the deployment past its limit and watch the overflow take the traffic, because a fallback that has never carried a request is a paragraph, and because the user-visible behaviour during overflow is a decision the design has usually deferred to whoever is on call without saying so. **Graceful degradation is not invisible degradation.**

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The diagnosis that is also the sale.** *Countermeasure:* the ordering rule, and the visible asymmetry between the two parts when it is violated.

**Reinforcement with no accomplishment.** A cadence that would run unchanged in a month where the system did nothing. *Countermeasure:* the requirement to state what the cadence would report in such a month.

**The remedy requiring the author's presence.** *Countermeasure:* the named-owner requirement, which fails immediately if the owner is not employed by the customer.

**The sizing model nobody kept.** The commitment cannot be re-derived because its inputs were never written down. *Countermeasure:* the workflow requires this to be reported at the top of the document rather than worked around, which is the only honest response and is also the finding.

**Quota reported as capacity.** The allocation exists and the thing cannot be deployed. *Countermeasure:* the drift register separates them into different fields with different timestamps, which makes the conflation impossible to write down.

**Degradation described rather than configured.** *Countermeasure:* the requirement to trigger the fallback at least once. **Partial**, because a learner without a sandbox can only record the intent, and §9 predicts that this is where the workflow most often stops.

**Victory declared.** The engagement closed well, so the change is treated as settled. *Countermeasure:* partial. The session names the mechanism and cannot rehearse it, because the interval over which it operates is longer than the programme.

**Timescale imported.** A learner reads the sources and produces a schedule justified by a period one of them mentions. *Countermeasure:* explicit prohibition, restated in the artefact standard and in §3.5 of this paper.

**The analogy over-extended.** A learner begins computing organisational half-lives from a mechanical analogy. *Countermeasure:* the session's limits section states plainly that the analogy is used because it makes an invisible failure visible and not because the two are the same kind of thing. **That is naming rather than preventing.**

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

No specification item and no entrustment level. This session generates observation for the entrustment decision at [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md), and it is the occasion where the professional-engagement strand is most visible.

**What assessors record**, as narrative: whether the unpaid version was a design or a gesture; whether the named owner exists and has the authority claimed; whether the conflict statement was in the body; whether the drift register separated permission from availability and named the decision that moved an input; whether the degradation was configured or described; whether the recommendation committed; and what the learner said to the closing question.

Recording this as reasoned narrative rather than as a mark follows the assessment literature this programme's architecture rests on: the useful information is *why* an assessor judged as they did, and assessors reaching the same outcome often reach it by different reasoning that a score conceals ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)).

**Nothing else is assessed.**

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the whole session rests on a claim that appears once, unevidenced, in a commercially interested document.**

At its strongest: the decay assertion is a single line of practitioner judgement in a corpus that reports no post-deployment record for anything it describes, contains no sustainment evidence of any kind, and has arranged its evidence so that disappointing results confirm rather than challenge its thesis. Building a session on that line is building on the least supported sentence in the least supported part of the material. **A programme that spends ninety sessions teaching learners to demand warrants has here accepted one because it is useful.**

**This is conceded without qualification.** The decay claim has no warrant, the corroborating change-management sources have no independent evaluation between them, and the session asserts no rate, proportion, magnitude or timescale. **The fix that was considered and declined** was to drop the session and treat post-engagement decay as outside the programme's scope. It was rejected for a reason that is itself an argument rather than a finding: the recommendation gets made in practice whether or not anybody has examined it, and a learner who has never separated a diagnosis from the sale attached to it will not do so the first time it matters. **The objection is entitled to say that teaching a discipline for handling an unwarranted claim gives the claim a standing it has not earned.**

**Objection 2: requiring the unpaid design first is a moral posture that makes the practitioner worse at their actual job.**

At its strongest: the practitioner is employed to deliver value to their employer as well as to the customer, and a continuing relationship is frequently the arrangement under which the customer gets sustained attention they would otherwise not receive. Insisting that the free version be designed first invites a learner to treat their own organisation's interest as suspect by default, produces documents their commercial function will not accept, and models a purity that will be trained out of them within a year.

The rebuttal is narrow and concedes the frame. **The session does not require the unpaid version to be recommended and explicitly permits the paid one.** What it requires is that the practitioner know what the free version would have been, because a recommendation made without that knowledge is not a recommendation, it is the only option that was drafted. Whether a commercial function would accept a proposal containing a conflict-of-interest section is untested, and no source behind this programme reports one being attempted.

**Objection 3: the capacity anchor is a category error, and the session is using arithmetic to lend borrowed rigour to a claim that has none.**

At its strongest, and this is the objection the paper takes most seriously: a sizing assumption going stale is a mechanical fact with a computable answer, a stated method and a vendor-published divisor. Organisational momentum is none of those things. Placing them side by side invites the learner to believe the second is tractable in the way the first is, and the session's own vocabulary — drift, half-life, conjunction — encourages exactly that. **A programme that warns against precision computed from a guess has here built a whole session around an analogy between something computable and something that is not.** The suspicion is sharpened by the fact that the analogy is load-bearing: remove it and the session is an ethics seminar with no workflow.

**Partially conceded, and the concession is larger than the rebuttal.** The analogy is this design's own, no source draws it, and a learner computing organisational half-lives has taken it further than it goes. The session says so in its limits.

The rebuttal is that the analogy is used for one specific property and not for its arithmetic. **What transfers is that a commitment drifts because independent parties made correct decisions and no mechanism connected them** — and that property is observable in the capacity case and merely arguable in the organisational one. The learner is being shown a legible instance of an illegible failure, not being given a formula. **The declined fix was to anchor the session on the organisational case alone**, which would have been more honest about the evidence and would have left the workflow with nothing executable — and a session whose workflow cannot be run is the defect this programme's own review found across an earlier build. **The cost is that some learners will over-extend the analogy**, and this paper records that as an accepted cost rather than a solved problem.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** In most submissions the unpaid version will be shorter and less specific than the paid one, measured by the presence of named owners, triggers and artefacts rather than by length alone. *Instrument:* both parts coded for the four specificity elements, compared within submission.

**Prediction 2.** Learners who write the unpaid version first, as instructed, will more often name an owner employed by the customer than those who report writing both together. *Instrument:* the named owner against the self-reported working order.

**Prediction 3.** Most conflict-of-interest statements will describe the organisation's interest and omit the author's own. *Instrument:* the statements, coded for whose interest is named.

**Prediction 4.** In most estates the sizing model a commitment was derived from will not be recoverable, and the learners who cannot recover it will more often report it as an obstacle than as the finding. *Instrument:* the drift registers, coded for whether `sizing_record_found` is false and, where it is, whether the absence appears in the document's opening or in a limitations section.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of learners will complete the drift register and stop before triggering the degradation, recording the fallback as configured rather than as observed. *Instrument:* the submitted registers checked against the sandbox's request logs for an actual overflow event. **This predicts the failure of the session's own countermeasure for described-rather-than-configured degradation**, and it is included because a countermeasure whose failure rate is unmeasured is an assumption. If it holds, the triggering step should become the assessed artefact rather than a workflow step.

**Prediction 6.** Learners instructed to compare their item-2 answer against the criterion (exercise 3) will more often produce an unpaid design satisfying all four specificity elements than learners who read the same material without the comparison prompt. *Instrument:* a within-cohort split on whether exercise 3 was completed, against the four-element coding from prediction 1. **This is the session's only direct test of the mechanism §3.9 rests on.**

**Prediction 7.** The closing question will produce more *I do not know* answers than either alternative. *Instrument:* the answers, recorded verbatim and classified. **If it produces almost none, the question is being heard as a test with a correct answer** and it should be replaced rather than repeated.

**None of these has been measured.** No cohort has run this session, and this repository's prediction register is machinery rather than a result — which is [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s finding applied to this page.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That momentum decays because a conjunction of specific conditions stops holding; that the test of a diagnosis is whether you will write the remedy that excludes you; that reinforcement attached to no accomplishment does damage rather than nothing; that a conflict which cannot go in the body of the document has not been accepted; and that permission, availability and commitment are three different things which imply nothing about each other.

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about whether momentum decays, how fast, or whether anything prevents it. Every organisational source here is a commercially interested practitioner account without independent evaluation. The capacity material is first-party documentation that is definitional rather than empirical and publishes no performance claim anybody could check. Everything about the session's method is this design's argument.

**No magnitude appears anywhere in this paper**, including from the capacity material, which states several. That is a rule of this repository rather than an accident of what was read.

**The concession worth remembering.** This session is built on the weakest sentence in its source material, and §8 concedes that without qualification. The anchor that makes it teachable is an analogy this design invented, and §8's third objection concedes that too. **If you find both unsatisfying, you have read them correctly.**

That the change-management source used for the regression argument states in its own 1996 preface that it draws neither examples nor major ideas from any published source except the author's own writing and cites no evidence to bolster its conclusions; that its cases are anonymised past a reader's ability to check; that its counts are recalled rather than counted, with one paragraph moving between two denominators; and that the widely quoted change-failure statistic does not appear in it at all — recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md](/research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md) on a complete reading of the 2012 reissue. **And that the claim about effort past the blocking element being wasted is not in the readiness model's book, and was withdrawn from this repository on reading it** — recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md).

**Cited from general knowledge, not verified here.** That a change declared finished regresses, and that opposition which did not dissipate waits for the declaration; that reinforcement unattached to an accomplishment can backfire; that accountability embedded in day-to-day operation sustains a change more reliably than recognition does; that an author who has already drafted a conclusion cannot reliably evaluate the alternative; and that judgements of this kind are better recorded as reasoned narrative than as a score. **Direction and mechanism only; no rate, proportion, magnitude or timescale is asserted here, and none may be inherited from the sources named above.**

**Design reasoning with no external warrant.** The two-part reinforcement design; the ordering rule and the claim that the ordering acts on the author rather than the reader; the conflict-of-interest statement as a body section; the named-owner-employed-by-the-customer requirement; the decomposition of momentum into a conjunction of independently-decaying conditions; **the entire analogy between capacity drift and organisational decay**, which no source draws and which §8's third objection concedes may be exactly the false precision this programme warns against; the transfer of the platform's degradation properties — observable trigger, stated fallback, recovery interval set by the failing party — into an organisational design standard; the treatment of *I do not know* as a completed answer; the assessment position that the recommendation itself is not assessed; and all seven predictions in §9.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded.** The definition of provisioned capacity as held whether or not requests are being made; the four deployment categories against billing, latency guarantee and workload type; the definitions of quota as a policy limit with no associated cost and capacity as what is actually deployable, together with the quoted call-out that quota does not guarantee capacity; intra-day capacity movement and the absence of any guarantee on re-creation after release; the three sizing inputs including the statement that cached tokens do not consume capacity; spillover as redirection on a non-success response; and the reservation guidance that reservations do not guarantee capacity, so deployments should be created to confirm availability before a reservation is purchased — all recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md), from a page **fetched and read in full**. Separately, priority-based load balancing toward provisioned endpoints and the circuit breaker's dynamic trip duration taken from the backend's own header are recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md), also **fetched and read in full**, with the individual policy reference pages not fetched.

**What the documentation does not say, recorded because it is the more important half.** The capacity page **makes no performance claim that could be independently checked**, publishes no reliability observation, and does not situate its capacity model against any alternative — its authority is definitional and it should be cited as evidence of how this platform works and never as evidence of what is architecturally best. **Every concrete magnitude it depends on — per-model unit-to-throughput ratios, minimum deployment sizes, default quota allocations, prices and regional availability — lives on companion pages that were not fetched, and none is reproduced here or anywhere in this wiki.** The gateway page does not discuss the privacy consequences of the prompt-and-completion logging it recommends. And **no accuracy, effectiveness or outcome figure appears in either source**, consistent with every platform note this programme holds: each states a latency or a bound and none states an accuracy.

There is a commercial silence too, and it is the session's real subject: **no vendor documentation known to this programme describes what happens to a customer's use of a product after a delivering team disengages, publishes a definition of a post-engagement support taper, or states what a customer must hold in order to sustain a delivered system without the party that built it.** Product documentation describes what a platform does; delivery material describes what a team does while present; **the interval after both is documented by nobody with an interest in documenting it.** That is the same silence recorded at [WP-080](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-080.md), observed one step further along. If any vendor publishes a sustainment or taper definition, this session's opening is due for revision and this paper should record its version and what it still omits.
