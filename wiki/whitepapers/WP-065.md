# WP-065 — The Cost Argument You Present to Someone Who Pays

**Seminar day:** [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) · **Module:** [M13](/wiki/modules/M13.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md)
**Strands:** ME · IS · SY · **Complexity class:** 5
**Platform anchor:** The capacity stack of S061–S064, read through Microsoft Cost Management · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Abstract

This paper defends an entrustment occasion in which the counterparties are two real people with incompatible interests and the authority to refuse, on the ground that a cost argument delivered to an audience that cannot say no has not been delivered. It defends forming the level from the artefact and the two encounters before the defence, per rater discipline rule 7, and defends publishing five failure conditions in advance of which the first two are symmetric — asking for money you do not need, and fitting the budget by silently accepting a degradation nobody was told about. **It defends making the ask specific in the platform's own terms** — a quantity, a kind of capacity, a commitment shape, a scope and a date — because there is no configuration in which somebody buys "more capacity", and a vague ask is therefore not a communication failure but an incoherent one. It defends a fifth phase in which the substrate's realised bill is opened from Cost Management in front of the room and is explicitly not assessed, discharging a commitment made at S061 rather than announced here. It reports discharge of three rule-6 obligations inside the entrustment artefact, and closes the forward link WP-050 §8 recorded as missing. It concedes that the judgement is partly a re-reading of work already assessed, that three of its five failure conditions are compliance checks that will drive the level, that **the one place the quarter could have made a deferred consequence consequential is the place it deliberately removed the stakes**, and that **the four builds' agent records now enter the assessment room as evidence the design declares to be non-evidence.**

## 1. The instructional problem this day must solve

Every student in the room can produce the argument. Four days have supplied the parts and the parts are correct.

The problem is that the argument they will produce is **an argument for the model**, and the model is the one thing neither counterparty wants. [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) established that nine quarters ago, from a real decision-owner's own mouth: they want the margin and the assumption. Nothing since has required the cohort to act on it, because every audience between S020 and here has been technical — evaluators, peers, examiners, other engineers. Under a technical audience the model *is* the argument, and six quarters of that is a strong training signal in the wrong direction.

The second problem is that this is a request for money, and requests for money have a structure students have never been taught and have strong instincts about. **Two failure modes are available and only one is socially punished.** Over-asking is recognised as a fault everywhere. Under-asking — fitting the budget by quietly accepting a degradation nobody was told about — reads as prudence, is rewarded in every prior context these students have been in, and is the failure this cohort will produce. It is the same asymmetry [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) found between overclaiming and unusable hedging, in a different currency.

The third problem is **the refusal branch**. An engineer with no prepared answer to "what if I say no" is asking permission rather than making a recommendation, and the difference is audible. The programme has never asked for one, because no prior task had a counterparty who could decline.

The fourth problem is temporal and is the module's whole reason for existing. **The consequence of a cost decision arrives after the decision has left everyone's attention**, and the record usually cannot join them. Phase 7's clinic is that failure exactly, and §8 argues the day flinches from its own conclusion.

## 3. Theoretical basis for the design

### 3.1 Load profile and element interactivity

**Low during the day and high before it.** Nothing new is taught before the assessed activity, and the artefact is committed before phase 3 begins. The interactivity is entirely in the artefact's construction: the ask depends on the binding quantity, which depends on the capacity figure, which carries a divergence band from a model whose own limits statement determines what the check date can mean. Seven elements, and five of them are constrained by one another.

Management:

- **Every measurement the artefact requires already exists** from the module's four preceding days. Nothing is re-measured.
- **The artefact's seven elements are enumerated in advance** and are the same seven the specification checks.
- **The five failure conditions are published before phase 1**, not discovered at phase 4.
- **The defence questions are not published**, which is a departure from [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md)'s practice and is deliberate: this cohort has defended four times and no longer needs ambush removed.
- **Phase 5 requires no preparation** and is deliberately placed after the assessed phases so that nothing in it can affect the level.

Expertise reversal is not a live concern on this day, since the activity is a performance rather than an acquisition.

### 3.2 Desirable difficulties selected, and those deliberately not

**Selected.**

- *Writing the sentence you would actually say, before polishing it,* item 1.
- *Predicting which of your own numbers you will soften, and to whom,* item 3.
- *Producing two materially different and simultaneously true arguments from one body of evidence,* phase 3.
- *Meeting the plain audience first and the technical attack second,* the fixed encounter order.
- *Preparing the refusal branch,* artefact element 7.
- *Naming the number you are least willing to defend,* phase 4.
- *Receiving the bill for a model you committed four weeks ago,* phase 5.

**Deliberately not selected.**

- *Forecasting.* ME301's. The demand change is supplied throughout the module.
- *Negotiating the quota.* Not staged anywhere in Q7; [WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 objection 2 records this as a gap and puts [WP-085](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-085.md) on notice.
- *Contract and procurement mechanics.* Perishable under the register and excluded from assessment entirely.
- *The ethics of selective true reporting.* Named at phase 6 point 2 and deferred to [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md), which is where [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) sent the same question one quarter ago.

**Scope, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), strong form.** The day teaches a request structure — ask, four quantities, cheapest capacity, who waits, provenance, expiry, refusal branch. It is **safe** for a bounded operating request inside an organisation the student belongs to, where the counterparty has authority and the amounts are within an existing budget envelope. It is **unsafe** in two settings the day does not touch. Where the ask crosses an organisational boundary — to a vendor, a funder or a customer — the counterparty's interest is not aligned with the student's employer and the transparency the day rewards can be exploited; that case is [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md)'s. And where the amount is large enough to require a business case rather than an operating request, the seven elements are necessary and not sufficient, and a student who presents them as a complete case will be asked for things this day has not taught. **The first is the more dangerous, because the structure looks the same from inside.**

**Scope of every number in the argument, in the same form, and its unsafe condition is a date.** The figures the artefact carries — a rate, a break-even, a capacity requirement, a monthly projection — are **safe** only on the day their inputs were retrieved and for the traffic mix they were measured on. They are **unsafe at any other date, without exception**, and this is not a caution about precision. A published rate on this platform carries no version number and changes without notice; a capacity requirement moves when the mix moves, which [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) situation D establishes happens with nobody touching anything; and a commitment discount is a term price that can be renegotiated by the party offering it. **A figure whose inputs cannot be re-derived on the day the recommendation becomes checkable cannot be vindicated and cannot be held against anybody**, which is the same defect from both directions.

The artefact element under which a student applying the simplification outside its safe range fails is the fourth published failure condition, extended at this day: **provenance includes the retrieval date of every commercial rate a figure depends on, in the same field as the figure.** That is the widening recorded on the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) applied at the occasion where a number is most likely to travel — out of the room, into a spreadsheet, and into a decision six weeks later.

### 3.3 Engagement and motivational mechanism

The day runs on **payer A being real.**

Every prior communication occasion in the programme used a constructed audience: role-players at S050, peers at S024, staff playing decision-owners at S020. Here the person can decline, and the money is the substrate's. That single fact changes the preparation more than any instruction could, and it is why [WP-024](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-024.md) §8 named this day as the place where a real counterparty was available and unused.

The second mechanism is **payer B's interest.** B is not sceptical for pedagogical reasons; B loses the money. A student who has only ever been questioned by people who want them to succeed meets, for the first time, a competent technical interrogator with a reason to prefer a different answer.

The third is **phase 5**, and its force comes from its placement rather than its content. The bill arrives after the assessment is over, from a decision made four weeks ago, and it cannot change anyone's level. That is the class-5 experience in its purest available form and §8 objection 3 argues the design bought it at too high a price.

The competence risk is severe and concentrated. A student who is refused by payer A in front of two assessors will experience it as a failed assessment, and it is not one — a refusal may be the correct outcome of a correct argument. **The instructor states before phase 3, and it is written into the specification, that the level is formed from the argument and not from the answer**, and that a well-argued ask that is declined and a well-argued ask that is granted are indistinguishable in the judgement. Payer A is briefed to decline at least one ask on its merits, so that the statement is demonstrably true rather than reassuring.

Relatedness is supported by phase 7, whose disclosure prompt asks for an absence rather than a mistake and is the softest in the programme by design.

Seven committed artefact elements, two real payers, twenty-five minutes each, A before B. **The day departs from the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](/wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) as assessment-bearing days may**, so it has no contrasting cases and no learning task; the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s build requirement is met by **the four preceding builds arriving with their three records attached**, which §8 objection 4 examines.

**Phase 4 (60 min).** Defence after artefact, per rule 7. Eight questions, the last two asked of everyone.

**Phase 5 (35 min).** The bill, opened from Cost Management in front of the room. Three categories, category 3 split. Discharges S061's declared deferral. Not assessed, and said so at S061.

**Phase 6 (35 min), assessors absent.** Nine points, each indexed to an encounter that has just happened. **Point 8 is the perishable platform point and point 9 the defaults point**, placed here because this day's expository phase is phase 6.

**Phase 7 (40 min), assessors absent.** A correct decision, a later incident, and no artefact joining them — then **an agent-drafted cost summary whose every figure was right and whose qualification was one sentence too far from its number.**

**Phase 8 (20 min).** Sealed items; **item 5 opened and the module's five-day frontier series shown as a series and not aggregated**; levels against predictions; **the S050 and S060 distributions beside today's**; category 3; the module's three rule-6 chains reported to the room; the S010 series, eighth administration.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**Students with more relevant prior knowledge.** A student who has presented a budget request professionally will handle payer A comfortably and will find the seven elements familiar. Predicted failure: they produce a polished ask and skip element 3, because in commercial practice the cheapest capacity is somebody else's job. Countermeasure: element 3 is the first named failure condition, published before phase 1, and payer B is briefed to ask for it if the student does not offer it — which converts a habit into a visible omission in front of an assessor.

**Students with less relevant prior knowledge.** The risk is the encounter itself rather than the artefact. Countermeasures: the artefact is committed in advance, so nothing has to be composed live; the failure conditions are published, so the standard is not a matter of inference; the aggregate-model substitution is offered in advance for students whose S061 work was weak; and the encounter order places the plain audience first, which is the easier of the two for an unpractised student.

**The activity is too easy.** If both payers accept every ask, the occasion has measured nothing. This is a real risk because the substrate's serving costs are modest and most asks will be affordable. Countermeasure: **payer A is briefed to decline at least one ask on its merits and payer B is briefed to have a specific alternative use for the money**, stated in a sentence, so that the money is genuinely contested. Where the whole cohort's asks are trivially affordable, the substrate's monthly ceiling is used as the constraint, which is the mechanism [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) already carries and which makes every ask rival.

**Affective failure.** The characteristic risk is **humiliation in front of an assessor.** A refusal from a real person with real authority, observed by two people forming a judgement, is a materially different experience from a failed exercise, and a student who reads it as a verdict on themselves may not recover inside the day.

Countermeasures: the decoupling of level from outcome is stated before phase 3 and written into the specification; payer A's briefed decline makes it demonstrable rather than asserted; the assessors are absent for phases 6 and 7, so the recovery happens outside their presence; phase 7's disclosure prompt asks for a consequence never learned rather than a mistake made, which is the least exposing prompt in the programme; and the S010 instrument at phase 8 is the eighth administration in a series the cohort has watched since Q1, which is the design's only longitudinal check on whether any of this is working.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**Entrustment judgement.** Activity: *present a cost argument to someone who pays for it.* Ordinal level only. **Expected 2b; 3a available and rare.** Narrative substantiation mandatory, citing specific moments from the two encounters. Per rule 7 the level is formed from the artefact and the encounters, and the defence tests it.

Five failure conditions, published before phase 1. **The level is formed from the argument and not from whether the ask was granted**, and payer A is briefed to decline at least one ask on its merits so that this is demonstrable.

One specification item in IS301 with ME301. Per rule 1, the checked elements are the seven artefact elements, **the appended prompt records, intervention records and frontier statements from S061 to S064**, what each payer wanted the argument to say and what was said instead, the phase 4 least-defensible number or an explicit declining, **the phase 4 answer on what was checked about an agent-produced figure**, and one number the student will not present again without its provenance. **Nothing else is assessed.**

**The three records are checked for presence and integrity, not for quality**, and **a record edited after its day fails.** They were assessed on their own days and are not re-assessed. §8 objection 4 argues that the distinction between reading them and assessing them is one the design asserts and cannot enforce.

**No commercial figure, product name, deployment category, commitment shape or platform mechanism is assessed.** All are perishable under the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md). What is assessed is the structure of the ask, the separation of the quantities, the provenance discipline including retrieval dates, and the expiry condition.

**Phase 5's result is not assessed**, and the decoupling appears in this specification and in [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md)'s.

**Error-clinic firewall.** Assessors absent for phases 6 and 7 and given no account.

**Rule-6 discharges, all three reported as required by [WP-051](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-051.md) §8.**

- [S062](/wiki/seminars/S062.md) named this day for its step 5 waiting-class statement. **The element exists as artefact element 4, is a checked element, and phase 4 examines whether the affected party was told.** Discharged.
- [S063](/wiki/seminars/S063.md) named this day for its step 3 reserved-and-unused figure. **The element exists as artefact element 3 and is the first named failure condition of the entrustment judgement** — the strongest form a receiving element takes in this programme, since it can put a student below the expected level. Discharged.
- [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) named this day for its phase 3 classification error rate. **The element exists as artefact element 2 and is the third named failure condition.** Discharged.

**Discharge of the S061 deferral.** [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) declared that this day would report the realised cost against every model built there, in three categories, unassessed. **Phase 5 is that report, the categories are as declared, and category 3 is additionally split into named and unnamed omissions to serve [WP-061](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-061.md) §9 prediction 4.** Discharged.

**Two competences the programme previously named as assessed here are assessed here.** [WP-012](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-012.md) named the stating of a recommendation's expiry condition as directly assessed at this day; it is the fifth failure condition. [WP-013](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-013.md) named the production of two true and materially different reports from one body of evidence as assessed here; the two-payer construction requires it and phase 4's second question tests it.

**Instrumentation rule 1 is satisfied by construction**, and this closes an omission the programme recorded against itself. [WP-024](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-024.md) §8 found that a real requester was available at S024 and at this day and was not used, calling it the more interesting of its two omissions. Both counterparties here are real people present for their own reasons, and the observation of whether the argument worked is theirs.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments, both responding outside this module:

- **The supervision-level distribution and the phase 4 least-defensible-number responses** respond at **[S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md)**, where the same students make a release decision carrying a cost position before a real audience and the two distributions are reported together. **This closes the forward link [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §8 recorded as missing** — that paper conceded it had produced an entrustment level with no comparison against a real audience, and named S070 as the comparison it had not arranged. ## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the level is formed from an artefact that is largely four already-assessed specification items in a folder.**

At its strongest: the artefact's seven elements are outputs of S061 through S064. Element 2 is S064 step 2. Element 3 is S063 step 3. Element 4 is S062 step 5. Element 5 is specification rule 2 applied to all of them. Each was assessed in its own course, pass or fail against specification. **The entrustment level is then formed from that same material, which is averaging by another name in a programme whose architecture explicitly forbids averaging.**

The consequence is concrete. A student whose S063 measurement was poor arrives with a weak reserved-and-unused figure and cannot honestly satisfy the first failure condition. Their level is depressed by a measurement failure that has already been graded, and the assessors cannot separate *this student cannot argue* from *this student's inputs were bad.*

The rebuttal is that rule 7 directs the assessor to the argument, not to the measurements, and that a student presenting a wide band honestly, with its provenance, is arguing well by the day's own standard. **That rebuttal is genuine and it is not complete**, because the failure conditions are stated over the content of the elements and not over the quality of the argument about them.

**The fix costs one prepared dataset and was declined.** A common, staff-produced measurement set could be offered as an alternative basis for the artefact, at the student's election, with the election recorded on the artefact. It decouples the communication judgement from the measurement quality for anyone who needs it, and would be taken by few. The design declined it because the substrate's whole value is that the student is arguing about their own system — **a real reason, and one that an opt-in election does not damage**, since the students who would elect it are precisely those for whom the alternative is arguing about a system they measured badly.

**Objection 2: three of the five failure conditions are compliance checks, and they will drive the level.**

At its strongest: [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) named two failure conditions and they were symmetric, substantive and hard — letting a claim stand stronger than the evidence, and hedging into language nobody can act on. This day keeps that pair and adds three: three numbers with three owners, provenance in the same field, a check date and a falsifying number.

Each of the three encodes something real. **All three are also checkable by inspection in under a minute, and rater discipline rule 4 states that the variance in this class of judgement lives in the assessor's implicit theories rather than in the rubric.** Give an assessor three things they can point at and two things they must judge, and the level will be anchored on the three. An occasion that can be failed for an omission visible on inspection is an occasion whose ordinal output will track the inspection.

This is conceded on the weighting rather than on the content. **The fix is one paragraph and was declined:** separate the five, so that conditions 3 to 5 are a specification gate the artefact must pass before the encounter begins, and conditions 1 and 2 are the entrustment content. The gate would be binary, pre-encounter, and administered by a specification check rather than by an assessor, which is exactly what the [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) says specification grading is for. The design declined it so that students would see one standard rather than two, **which is a presentational reason, and a presentational reason should not shape an assessment architecture.** §9 prediction 3 is the test.

**Objection 3: the quarter's central claim is that consequences arrive later and are answerable for, and this day removes every consequence from the only place it delivers one.**

At its strongest: [Q7](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md) states that class 5 requires the student to own a system across time and be answerable for decisions whose consequences arrive weeks later. Phase 5 is where such a consequence arrives — the realised bill against a model committed four weeks earlier. **The design then strips it of every stake:** not assessed, results private, aggregate to the cohort, and the decoupling published in advance so that no student ever believed otherwise. Answerability with no consequence attached is not answerability, and the quarter's own claim is therefore delivered nowhere except in a sentence.

The rebuttal is [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s and it is a good one: assessing the check makes the prediction strategic, students would list every conceivable cost to keep category 3 empty, and the considered statement the module wants would stop being produced. That argument is correct and this paper does not dispute it.

**The concession is that the design has chosen one branch of a genuine dilemma twice and has never tested the other.** S050 chose it for failure-mode statements; this day chooses it for cost models; and no cohort has ever run the alternative. The claim that assessment destroys the prediction is a prediction itself, made by this design, with no evidence and no instrument.

There is no day-level fix, and that is the point. **The available test is programme-level: run the check unassessed for one cohort and assessed for the next, and compare category 3's size and its composition.** The design has no mechanism for running such a comparison, no place to record it, and no whitepaper that would own it. That absence is the finding, and it is recorded rather than repaired, because repairing it means building a cross-cohort experimental apparatus that this programme's self-instrumentation table gestures at and does not specify.

**Objection 4: the day brings four builds' agent records into the assessment room and then declares them not to be evidence.**

This is the objection that bites on the practical turn, and it is new at this revision because the records are.

At its strongest: the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement from each of S061 to S064 are appended to the entrustment artefact. They are available to payer B, who is briefed to ask where a number came from and who produced it, and they are available to the two assessors, who are forming an ordinal level. The specification says they are checked for presence and integrity and not for quality.

**An assessor cannot read a three-line intervention record covering four weeks of work and then form a judgement as though they had not read it.** Rater discipline rule 4 states that the variance in this class of judgement lives in the assessor's implicit theories rather than in the rubric, which is precisely the mechanism by which unassessed material becomes assessed material. So the design has introduced, into an occasion whose whole defensibility rests on the level being formed from named things, **a body of unnamed things that speak directly to the question the occasion is about** — whether the student directed the work they are defending.

Two rebuttals are available and neither is sufficient. The first is that the records were already assessed, at their own days, against published fail conditions, so nothing new is being judged. That is true of the records and false of the assessor: a record that passed a specification check can still read as thin, and thinness is exactly what an implicit theory attaches to. The second is that a student who cannot answer for their own work should be marked down for it, which is true and is an argument for assessing the records rather than for reading them without assessing them.

**The concession is that this is the worst of the three available arrangements and the design has chosen it.** Assessing the records would be defensible. Withholding them from the assessors would be defensible. Reading them while declaring them irrelevant is the arrangement with the contamination and none of the accountability.

**The cheap fix is one sentence and it is not taken, for a reason that is itself a dilemma.** The fix is to give the records to payer B only — who has a use for them, is briefed to probe with them, and is forming no level — and to withhold them from the assessors, who would then learn what they need from the student's answer to phase 4's agent question rather than from the documents. It costs a line in the specification.

It is not taken because **the phase 4 agent question asks the student to account for work an agent did on their behalf, and an assessor with no access to the record cannot tell an honest account from a fluent one.** Withholding the evidence would protect the judgement from contamination and would blind it to the thing it is judging. The design has no third option and has not found one; what it has is a preference, stated here so it can be argued with: **contaminated and informed is less bad than clean and blind, at an occasion whose subject is ownership of work.** That preference is untested, it contradicts the arrangement this programme uses everywhere else, and it is recorded as the objection's resolution rather than as its answer.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** The modal awarded level will be 2b, and among sub-2b awards the modal cited failure condition will be the second — fitting the budget by silently accepting an untold degradation — rather than the first. *Instrument:* awarded levels coded against the five published failure conditions. **The direction is the claim**: under-asking reads as prudence and the cohort will import it from everywhere else they have been.

**Prediction 2.** Fewer than half the cohort will produce two materially different arguments; most will deliver one argument twice with the vocabulary adjusted. *Instrument:* the two encounters coded independently by the assessors, before the defence, for material difference in what was selected. **If most deliver one argument twice, the competence [WP-013](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-013.md) named as assessed here is not being produced, and the two-payer construction is doing less than §3.3 claims.**

**Prediction 3.** Assessor divergence will be low on artefacts that fail a published failure condition and high on artefacts that pass all five. *Instrument:* independent pre-conferral levels, with the divergence rate reported against failure-condition status. **This is §8 objection 2's test.** The predicted pattern is the signature of a level anchored on inspection where inspection is available and unmanaged where it is not; the contrary result — divergence roughly equal in both groups — would show the checklist is not doing the anchoring.

**Prediction 4.** Category 3 at phase 5 will be dominated by costs the [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) scope statement had already named as omitted, rather than by costs nobody named. *Instrument:* category 3 items coded named against unnamed, per [WP-061](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-061.md) §9 prediction 4. **The two results call for different repairs**: unnamed omissions indicate a modelling failure at S061, and named omissions indicate a scope statement that was delivered, understood, and not acted on — which is the failure the strong form of the scope rule was adopted to prevent and would be evidence that the strong form is not sufficient on its own.

**Prediction 5.** A majority of item 5 sealed predictions will state that the agent's draft ask omits something, and fewer than one in five will name the refusal branch specifically. *Instrument:* item 5 responses coded for which of the three omissions — owner, expiry, refusal branch — is named, against the agent's actual output. **The refusal branch is the prediction.** An owner and an expiry are elements of a specification the cohort has been drilled on; a prepared answer to *what if I say no* is a professional posture rather than an artefact element, and it is the one thing on this day that no specification anywhere in the programme has ever required. If a substantial number name it, the posture has formed without being taught, which would be the most encouraging single result in the module.

**Prediction 6.** Assessor divergence on artefacts passing all five failure conditions will be higher for students whose appended intervention records are short than for those whose records are long, controlling for encounter quality as coded independently. *Instrument:* pre-conferral level divergence against appended-record length, with encounter quality coded blind. **This is §8 objection 4's test and it is the only one available.** A relationship in either direction is evidence that the records are being read into the judgement; no relationship is evidence that the declared separation holds, and would be the first empirical support this design has for a firewall it asserts in several places.
