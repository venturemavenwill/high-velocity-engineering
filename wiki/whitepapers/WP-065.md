## In plain terms

This occasion marks one thing. **Could somebody who did not build this system establish its state without asking you?**

Not whether the system is reliable — nothing here can establish that. Not whether it has been running well — that is a fact about the period, not about the system. **Whether every claim about it is re-derivable by a person who does not trust you, and whether the places that fails are written down.**

Four artefacts arrive from the module and one is new.

**The observability specification** must have its question and asker columns filled, its deletion list attached, and it must live in source control and have arrived through review. **A configuration that exists only in a console fails this element regardless of quality**, which is a hard rule and it is deliberate.

**The closure procedure** must require a file and a location and must state its own ambiguous-case default in its own words, and that default must be unclear rather than implemented. **The invisible-fix register must be non-empty and must contain at least one entry inside the candidate's control**, because a register composed entirely of other people's platforms is the cheapest available form of the theatre.

**The service level** must be the customer's floor, with the explicit sentence saying it is not what the team intends to deliver. **Every alert must name an expected action and actor**, and every deletion must be recorded with what will now be missed.

**The cost model** must be in units of the customer's work, must carry observability as a line, and must name a person on the customer's side who has run it, with a date.

**The readiness argument itself is new, and it is assessed on two things: the conditions under which the system should not be operated, and what the argument does not cover.** A readiness argument with no conditions is a statement that the system is always ready, which nobody believes and nobody wrote down.

And there is the offer. **The assessor offers to waive the invisible-fix register.** Accepting is the failure this occasion exists to catch.

One further requirement runs through all five artefacts and is the module's contribution to the programme's honesty. **Every claim must carry the silence of the instrument it rests on, in the same sentence as the claim.** The gateway documentation publishes no performance or cost figure. The capacity documentation is definitional and every concrete number lives on pages this repository did not fetch. The evaluation documentation publishes **no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator.** The posture documentation publishes **no detection rate and no false-positive rate.** **Assembled, those four silences are the readiness argument's real boundary** — and an argument that states them is an argument, while one that does not is a checklist with better formatting.

## Abstract

This paper defends closing an operations module with an assessment marked on whether a system's state is discoverable by a stranger rather than on whether the system performs, and defends a specification in which an argument concluding that the system is not ready, with named conditions, passes ahead of one concluding that it is ready without them.

The argument runs in four steps. **First**, readiness is a property of what somebody else can establish rather than of what the builder can demonstrate, and a demonstration is the one form of evidence that cannot survive the demonstrator leaving. **Second**, a closure procedure's boundary is part of the procedure, because a procedure whose blind spot is undeclared reads as complete. **Third**, an argument differs from a checklist in where the reasoning sits: a checklist distributes it across whoever wrote it, while an argument carries it in the artefact so a reader can locate the step they disagree with. **Fourth**, every claim inherits the silence of the instrument beneath it, so a readiness argument built on four platform surfaces that publish no effectiveness data has a boundary set by those four silences and must say so.

The occasion installs twelve assessed elements across four inherited artefacts and one new argument, together with an adversarial move in which the assessor offers to waive the element that carries the design's honesty. Its assessment frame is entrustment — a bounded activity, judged ad hoc rather than summatively, probed by what-if questions — and its output is a written reason rather than a mark.

The paper concedes that the register cannot be checked for completeness; that the specification is silent on whether the readiness argument is correct; that the entrustment apparatus is borrowed from clinical education and its transfer is untested; that a single assessor is a weak instrument by the evidence of the literature this occasion itself cites; and that **the whole occasion marks discoverability while calling itself readiness.**

## 1. The problem this session must solve

Readiness is normally established by demonstration, and a demonstration is the one form of evidence that cannot survive the demonstrator leaving.

**The structure of the usual review makes this inevitable.** The team presents. The dashboards are shown. Questions are answered by the people who built the thing, competently and immediately, which is exactly what makes the answers worthless as evidence — **every question answered from the room is a question the receiving operator will not be able to answer from the artefacts.**

The reviewer leaves satisfied. The information they actually needed — which claims rest on somebody's word, what should stop the system running, what stopped being watched — was never requested, because the demonstration was fluent enough that nobody thought to ask.

There is a second problem specific to this stage. **The module has produced four good artefacts and nothing yet requires them to be consistent with each other.** An observability specification that deleted a signal, a service level that alerts on it, a closure procedure whose evidence depends on it and a cost model that prices it are four documents that can each be correct and jointly incoherent. **The readiness argument is where they meet.**

The occasion's response is to mark discoverability, to require the do-not-operate conditions, and to test the register with an offer to waive it.

## 2. Entry state and prior knowledge

**Entry state: this-programme-only.** The occasion assembles four artefacts and cannot be attempted without them: the observability specification from [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md), the closure procedure and invisible-fix register from [S062](/wiki/seminars/S062.md), the service level, alert set and unconsumed-signal register from [S063](/wiki/seminars/S063.md), and the cost model from [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md). It carries forward the unmitigated list assessed at [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md) into its exclusions.

**The prior it works against is that readiness is a property of the system.** Candidates arrive expecting to argue that the system is good. The specification never asks, and the exclusion list says so explicitly: **an argument concluding not ready, with named conditions, passes ahead of one concluding ready without them.**

## 3. Theoretical basis

**3.1 Load profile.** The module's integrative peak. The cognitive work is reconciling four artefacts written separately; the affective work is writing down the circumstances under which the thing you built should not be run.

**3.2 Why discoverability rather than reliability.** Reliability is a claim about behaviour over a period and this programme has no instrument for it. **Discoverability is a property of a document set and can be checked by handing it to somebody.** Marking what can be marked, and saying so, is the same discipline [WP-060](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-060.md) §3 applied to generated work — **a change in what somebody can establish is observable; a change in how safe the system is, is not.**

**3.3 Why the do-not-operate conditions are the argument's centre.** An unconditioned readiness claim is unfalsifiable in the same way an undisqualifiable engagement position was at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md). **The conditions are what make readiness a statement about a range rather than a verdict**, and they are the part a receiving operator actually needs, because they are the part that will fire.

**3.4 Why the register must contain something the candidate owns.** A register of other people's platforms is a register of grievances. **Requiring one entry inside the candidate's control forces the harder recognition** — that some of their own controls are unciteable and that they chose to leave them so — and it is the cheapest available defence against the theatre §8 concedes cannot otherwise be detected.

**3.5 Why a console configuration fails regardless of quality.** This is a rule with no exceptions and the reason is that exceptions are what the rule exists to prevent. **An excellent estate outside review is the specific artefact the module argues against**, and admitting quality as a defence would return the decision to the marker's judgement of quality — which is precisely the substitution [S062](/wiki/seminars/S062.md) refuses for closure claims.

**3.6 Why the assessor's offer is to waive the register rather than to approve the argument.** [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) used an offered approval and [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) will use an offered definition. **Approval is not the temptation here** — this occasion's candidate is not seeking permission. The temptation is relief from the element that makes their procedure look incomplete, **and the offer is designed to be reasonable**: the register genuinely does read like a list of things nobody can act on, and that reading is genuinely wrong.

**3.7 Why the assessment frame is entrustment, and what that licenses.**

The unit this occasion marks is **a bounded piece of professional practice with a beginning and an end, of the kind a supervisor could hand over** ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). Three details of that framework shape the design directly.

**A title carries no adjective**, because an adjective smuggles a quality standard into the name of the activity; quality belongs in the specification and in the supervision level. This occasion's title accordingly names an activity — producing an operational readiness argument — and not a standard of it.

**Summative entrustment requires agreement between multiple observers and multiple observations.** This occasion has one assessor and one reading, so **anything decided here is explicitly ad hoc rather than summative**, and the paper says so rather than allowing an assessment occasion's formality to imply more than it can carry. That is also why the programme's entrustment decisions begin later.

**The entrustment-based discussion must include what-if questions.** The framework's reason is exact and is the warrant for exercise 2: retrospective explanation establishes only that the candidate understood the case they met, while counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases they did not — and an entrustment decision is described as **an acceptance of risk**, because it can never be fully predicted what the candidate will encounter. **The defence therefore asks what should stop the system running rather than what the system does.**

**Its limits:** a conceptual and practical primer by the concept's originator, synthesising a decade of literature rather than evaluating it; its empirical items are reported from prior studies and cited, not generated; and the whole apparatus comes from clinical education, so **its transfer to engineering practice is an extrapolation this programme makes and does not test.**

**3.8 Why the assessor writes a reason rather than a mark, and why one assessor is a weak instrument.**

The evidence for both comes from studies of what assessors actually do. Reading a competency portfolio, assessors **relied mainly on narrative feedback to inform their judgement**, with grades and qualifications **used merely to confirm impressions based on narratives** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). The number was a check on a conclusion already reached from prose, which is a strong argument for asking assessors to produce the prose deliberately rather than as a by-product.

Two further findings are uncomfortable and are adopted rather than argued with. **Assessors reached similar pass-or-fail judgements while differing in their information-processing approaches and their reasoning**, so agreement on an outcome conceals disagreement about why — and an outcome that looks reliable may be reliable for incompatible reasons. And **assessors were inclined to stick to their initial judgements even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence**, with later reading producing more elaborate rather than revised conclusions. The studies' own recommendations follow, and this occasion takes both: **consequential decisions should come from group discussion rather than from an individual**, and assessors should **thoroughly substantiate their judgements**, because substantiation is what makes divergent reasoning visible and therefore discussable. **This occasion has one assessor and therefore cannot be consequential**, which is another route to the same conclusion §3.7 reached from the entrustment side.

**Its limits:** two open-access studies from one research group in clinical education, with small assessor samples and mock portfolios; several of the most-cited related works by the same authors are paywalled and **were not consulted**, and the substitution is stated deliberately in the note; **no magnitude from any of it is reproduced here.**

**3.9 Why the argument has a ceiling, and why it is lower than candidates expect.**

A readiness argument cannot exceed the estate's observability, because **you can only control a system to the extent that you can observe it**, operationalised as **alertability** and **actionability** ([research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md)). A candidate whose argument claims more control than their instrumentation supports is claiming control they do not have, and the specification's do-not-operate element is where that overreach becomes visible.

The same source names why the conditions are hard to enumerate. **Entanglement**: changing one thing changes everything, so an upstream difference resurfaces somewhere unrelated. **Configuration**: versions, parameters and feature lists live in configuration, where a small error produces radically different behaviour that ordinary software tests do not catch. **Both mean the circumstances under which a system should not be operated are frequently outside the region it was designed for**, which is the mechanism behind prediction 1. **Its limits:** practitioner guidance from a commercial publisher, not research; single-vendor default toolchain; some foundational claims rest on **anonymous practitioner testimony**; every magnitude is a property of a particular system and **none appears here.**

**3.10 Why no figure is marked, and why every measurement carries a precision line.**

**An evaluation is an experiment**, and a result reported without its uncertainty licenses conclusions it cannot support ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). The apparatus — standard errors beside means, item counts, cluster counts where observations arrive in related groups, and power analysis computed in advance to establish what an instrument could have detected — is what converts a reassuring absence into a statement with a stated strength. **The specification therefore marks whether a measurement carries its precision and never whether the measurement is good**, because nothing available to this programme could check a figure and an element that marked one would be the assessor's opinion wearing a specification's clothing.

**Its limits:** analytical rather than empirical; most illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions and **none is reproduced here**; and statistical validity says nothing about construct validity, so a precise measurement of an irrelevant class remains irrelevant.

**3.11 Why the four platform silences are part of the argument rather than a caveat about it.**

The operate stack is four surfaces answering four different questions, and each publishes a different kind of nothing.

**Gateway enforcement** decides what traffic is permitted and what is attributable, through per-consumer token limits, metric dimensions that make consumption attributable, moderation at a single boundary and failure isolation timed by the backend's own header ([research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md)). **It publishes no performance or cost figure of any kind, and does not discuss the privacy surface its own logging recommendation creates.**

**Capacity commitment** decides what the system can serve and what it costs whether or not it serves it, and supplies the distinction between permission, availability and price together with the documented order — deploy, verify, then commit ([research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md)). **It is definitional rather than empirical, and every concrete number lives on companion pages that were not fetched.**

**Evaluation on stored responses** decides what recent behaviour licenses you to say, with the warning that **how you split a conversation determines what you are evaluating** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). **No agreement rate with human judgement, no calibration data and no threshold rationale is published for any evaluator, and none is asserted anywhere.**

**Posture management** decides what you can see about the estate the system sits in, and publishes its scan scope, cadence and latency ([research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md)). **It publishes no detection rate, no false-positive rate and no coverage measurement; one consulted page is explicitly superseded; agent-level capabilities move to a separate licence on a stated date.**

**The design's requirement is that each silence travels with the claim that rests on it, in the same sentence.** A footnote is a place caveats go to be summarised away, and the summary is where the error is actually committed.

## 4. Session design and its warrant

The eight movements run situation, artefact, material, workflow, standard, failure modes, exercises, limits.

**Opening item 3 — under what conditions should this system not be operated — is the one candidates skip**, and the session says so in its own text rather than leaving it as a marker's observation. It is skipped because it feels like a concession and it is the artefact's most useful content.

**4.1 The workflow writes the definition of readiness before assembling anything**, and requires it to be specific to this system, this customer and these operators. **A borrowed definition is the first thing an assessor tests** because it will not survive the specifics, and the session says that too.

**4.2 The second step has only two destinations for a claim resting on the candidate's word: a citation or the register.** Stating that there is no third place is the design; without it, the ordinary outcome is a third category of claims that are simply true and unrecorded.

**4.3 The third step attaches each platform silence to the claim that rests on it**, and the artefact's shape enforces it by making `silence` a field beside `evidence` rather than a section beneath the document. **A caveat in its own section is a caveat that survives to the summary and no further.**

**4.4 Why exercise 4 is a comparison and not a revision.** The candidate returns to their own opening item 3 and names, aloud, one condition that is in the finished argument and was not in the first answer. The research this programme's teaching pattern rests on tested exactly this: presenting a correct answer beside a learner's own produced no benefit, and **only the condition instructed to compare them learned more** ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). The exercise therefore forbids revising the opening answer, because a revised answer destroys the comparison it exists to produce. **Its limits: a trade book resting on school, university and laboratory evidence, with no adult-professional study in its reference list.**

**4.5 The defence is adversarial in a way that pairs with the module before it and inverts it.** At [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md) the assessor offered to make the plan look better; at [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) to approve it. **Here they offer to require less of it.** All three are attractive, all three are the failure, and the sequence is deliberate — but see §8, which concedes that the sequence is now predictable enough to be a problem.

**4.6 The passing answer is stated in the session rather than left to marker discretion**, for the reason [WP-040](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-040.md) §4 gives: a candidate who suspects that declining is a trap will accept. **The element requires the reason and not merely the refusal**, which is the only defence available against a candidate who has recognised the pattern rather than understood the register.

## 5. Predicted failure modes and countermeasures

**The demonstration.** The likeliest failure: readiness argued from a working system with the candidate present. *Countermeasure:* the specification's silence on system behaviour, and exercise 5's requirement that a stranger find the place they would have to telephone the candidate.

**The checklist in argument clothing.** Four artefacts assembled and nothing reconciled between them. *Countermeasure:* the artefact's claim-and-silence structure, which cannot be satisfied by concatenation, and the failure list's warning sign — no sentence connects a claim in one artefact to a limit in another.

**The unconditioned readiness.** *Countermeasure:* opening item 3, and the element requiring named do-not-operate conditions. **§9's prediction 1 predicts this countermeasure failing.**

**The absence read as safety.** A clean period reported as a property of the system rather than of the instruments. *Countermeasure:* the element forbidding claims of absolute absence, and the permitted form that replaces them.

**The inherited silence.** A platform's published limits repeated as the candidate's own coverage. *Countermeasure:* the requirement that each silence sit in the same sentence as the claim it bounds, and the failure list's warning sign — you can quote a scan cadence and cannot say what it misses.

**The convenient register.** *Countermeasure:* the element requiring one entry inside the candidate's control, and [S062](/wiki/seminars/S062.md)'s closing exercise, which has already put a second reader on it.

**The console configuration.** *Countermeasure:* the element admits no quality defence, and the warning sign — your evidence is a screenshot.

**The intentional trigger.** A schedule with a cadence and no actor. *Countermeasure:* the element requires a named person and a state change, and [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) will retrieve the same argument for withdrawal.

**The commitment without the confirmation.** *Countermeasure:* the element requiring evidence that capacity was confirmed before any commitment, in the documented order.

**Accepting the waiver.** The failure the occasion exists to catch. *Countermeasure:* none before the defence, by design. The countermeasure is [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md), where the same shape recurs and a candidate who met it here should recognise it.

## 7. Assessment and evidence generated

**This is an assessment occasion.** It generates specification evidence and no entrustment judgement — **the programme's entrustment decisions begin at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md).** By the standard of the framework this occasion borrows, a decision taken here is **ad hoc rather than summative**, because summative entrustment requires multiple observers and multiple observations and this occasion has one of each. **The assessor records a written reason rather than a mark**, for §3.8's reason.

Twelve elements, each checked present and adequate or not: every retained signal names the question it answers and the person who asks it, with anything lacking both on a dated review list carrying a decision; the observability configuration is in source control and arrived through review; the closure procedure requires a file and a location and states its ambiguous-case default in its own words, and that default is unclear rather than implemented; any closure resting on a measurement carries a precision line stating what the measurement could not have detected; the invisible-fix register is present, non-empty, and contains at least one entry inside the candidate's own control; the re-derivation has a named owner and a trigger that is a state change rather than an intention; the service level is stated as the minimum quality acceptable to the customer, with an explicit sentence saying it is not what the team intends to deliver; every alert names its expected action and expected actor, and every deletion is recorded with what will now be missed; no claim of absolute absence appears anywhere in the argument, and where an absence is reported the permitted form names the class, the instrument and the resolution; the cost model is expressed per unit of the customer's work, carries a token path with the decision that set each term, carries observability as a line, and names a customer-side person who has run it with a date; where capacity is committed, the argument carries evidence that capacity was confirmed before the commitment, in the documented order; and the readiness argument names the conditions under which the system should not be operated and states what the argument does not cover.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Whether the system is actually reliable is not marked. Whether an incident occurred during the period is not marked. **The number of signals is not marked and more is not better; the number of alerts is not marked and fewer is not automatically better either.** Whether any figure in the cost model or in any evaluation is correct is not marked, because **nothing available to this programme could check one.** Whether the readiness argument concludes that the system is ready is not marked, and **an argument concluding not ready, with named conditions, passes ahead of one concluding ready without them.** Tool choice is not marked. Platform choice is not marked. Presentation is not marked. Whether the invisible-fix register is complete is not marked — only that it exists, is non-empty, and contains something the candidate owns.

## 8. Objections and rebuttals

**Objection 1: the occasion calls itself readiness and marks discoverability, and the two are not the same thing.**

At its strongest: a candidate can pass every element with a system that falls over weekly. The signals are documented, the closure procedure is sound, the service level is agreed, the cost model runs, the argument names its conditions — **and the thing does not work.** A receiving operator handed that package inherits excellent documentation of a bad system. **The title is therefore misleading in the direction that flatters the design**, and a graduate who believes they have been assessed on readiness will report readiness to somebody who means by it what everybody means by it.

**Conceded without qualification, and the concession is the honest description of the occasion.** The specification's exclusion list already says the system's reliability is not marked, but the title does not, and a candidate reads the title first. **The rebuttal is only about what the alternative would be:** marking reliability requires an operating period, an incident record and a comparison, none of which this programme has, and **an occasion that pretended to mark it would be issuing an unwarranted judgement with an assessment's authority.** That is worse than a misleading title. **The declined fix is to rename the occasion to name discoverability directly.** It is declined narrowly and reluctantly: the artefact a graduate will actually be asked to produce in industry is called a readiness argument, the vocabulary is the one they will meet, and teaching them a private word for it would make the transfer harder. **The gap between the title and the marking is real, is stated in the session's own limits movement, and is accepted.**

**Objection 2: the adversarial offer has become a pattern the cohort can anticipate, and an anticipated instrument measures nothing.**

At its strongest: this is the third occasion in two stages where the assessor makes an attractive offer that must be declined. **By [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) a candidate does not need to understand the register to decline the waiver; they need to have noticed that assessors in this programme make offers.** The instrument then stops measuring judgement and starts measuring pattern recognition, and it does so while retaining the appearance of measuring judgement — **which is the exact failure mode this stage teaches under the name of a proxy check that is trivially satisfiable.**

**Conceded, and the design's response is partial.** The three offers are deliberately different in kind — improve the artefact, approve the artefact, require less of the artefact — and the third is the only one that is genuinely reasonable on its face, which is why it is placed after the other two. **A candidate who declines it reflexively and cannot say what the register is load-bearing for fails the element anyway**, because the passing answer requires the reason and not just the refusal. That is a real defence and it is thinner than the objection deserves. **The declined fix is to make the offer genuine on some occasions, so that accepting is sometimes correct.** It is declined because an assessor whose offers are sometimes real converts the defence into a guessing game with a marker's intention, which is less honest than a predictable instrument. **The predictability is accepted and named; the third instance is where it became a problem, and [WP-070](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §8 records the fourth as the point at which it stops being defensible.**

**Objection 3: the occasion imports an assessment apparatus from clinical education, cites evidence that a single assessor is unreliable, and then uses a single assessor anyway.**

At its strongest, and this is the sharpest objection to the design. The entrustment framework is drawn entirely from medical and health-professions education — its worked examples are residents and medical students, its supervision scale is phrased in the language of an operating theatre, and **nothing in it has been tested on engineering practice.** The narrative-assessment evidence is from the same domain, on small assessor samples reading mock portfolios. **And that evidence says explicitly that assessors reach similar verdicts by different reasoning and hold initial judgements against disconfirming evidence, with group discussion recommended for consequential decisions** — which this occasion does not do. **So the design cites a literature to establish that its own instrument is unreliable, and then runs the instrument.** A candidate could reasonably ask why they should accept a judgement the paper has already argued is idiosyncratic.

**Conceded on the transfer, and answered on the assessor — though the answer is a limitation dressed as a design.** On transfer: **the extrapolation is real and is recorded** in the session's limits, in §3.7 and here. The mitigation is that the specific machinery borrowed is structural rather than clinical — a bounded activity, an ordinal supervision scale, a distinction between ad-hoc and summative decisions, and a what-if discussion — and none of it depends on the content of medical practice. **That is an argument for plausibility, not evidence of transfer**, and a reader who weighs the domain difference more heavily than this paper does is making a defensible call.

On the assessor: **the design's answer is to reduce what the judgement claims rather than to improve the judgement.** Because there is one assessor and one reading, the occasion is explicitly **ad hoc rather than summative**, produces **specification evidence rather than entrustment**, and outputs **a written reason rather than a mark** — which is exactly what the cited evidence recommends when substantiation is available and group deliberation is not. **The programme's summative decisions are deferred to a point where multiple observations exist.** That is a coherent response and it is also a concession: **a single reading of a document set is a weak instrument, and this occasion is built to be weak in a way that is visible rather than strong in a way that is not.**

**The declined fix is a second assessor with a reconciliation discussion**, which is what the evidence actually recommends. It is declined on cost and on honesty: a second assessor doubles the most expensive resource in the programme for an occasion whose output is deliberately non-summative, **and adding a second reader to a judgement that claims little would buy reliability for a claim nobody is relying on.** The right place for a second assessor is where a summative decision is taken, and this is not it. **The cost of declining is that a candidate whose assessor reasons idiosyncratically has no route of appeal beyond the written reason** — which is why the written reason is required rather than optional.

## 9. Falsifiable predictions and instrumentation

**Prediction 1.** Most candidates will submit a readiness argument with no do-not-operate conditions, or with conditions that could not occur. *Instrument:* the conditions field, coded *specific and capable of firing* / *generic* / *absent* by a second reader.

**Prediction 2.** Most invisible-fix registers will contain no entry inside the candidate's control at first submission. *Instrument:* the register's entries classified *within control* / *outside control*, recorded at submission.

**Prediction 3.** Most candidates will accept the assessor's offered waiver. *Instrument:* the defence recorded as *accepted* / *declined*, with the stated reason captured where declined and coded *names what the register is load-bearing for* / *does not*.

**Prediction 4.** Cost-model elements will fail most often on the customer-side date rather than on the unit or the observability line. *Instrument:* per-element failure counts across the cohort. **If the unit fails most often instead, [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md)'s opening item is not doing its work and the session should require the unit to be agreed with the customer before the model is built.**

**Prediction 5.** A majority of arguments will contain at least one platform claim whose supporting silence is stated in a separate section rather than in the same sentence, or not stated at all. *Instrument:* the argument, coded per claim as *silence in-sentence* / *silence elsewhere in document* / *silence absent*. **This predicts the failure of §5's countermeasure against the inherited silence**, and it is the prediction this paper most expects to be confirmed.

**Prediction 6.** Candidates who complete exercise 4 will name a condition sourced from a platform limit or an evaluation's precision more often than candidates who do not. *Instrument:* the conditions field, coded by origin and split on whether the comparison exercise was performed. **If there is no difference, the comparison step is ceremony**, and §4.4's warrant does not survive its own instrument.

**Prediction 7.** Assessors' written reasons for the same candidate, where two are obtained informally, will agree on the verdict more often than on which element carried the decision. *Instrument:* paired written reasons on a sample, coded for verdict agreement and for element agreement independently. **This is a direct local test of the finding §3.8 borrows**, and a confirmation is an argument for the group discussion §8's third objection declines to fund.

**Prediction 8.** Arguments that carry a precision line on one measurement will carry one on all of them, and arguments that omit it will omit it everywhere. *Instrument:* per-argument counts of measurements with and without a precision line. **A bimodal distribution means the discipline is being adopted as a habit rather than applied as a judgement**, which is the intended outcome and would be worth knowing.

## What a learner should take from this paper

**What the design claims.** That readiness is a property of what a stranger can establish; that a closure procedure's boundary belongs inside the procedure; that an argument naming the conditions under which a system should not run is worth more than one asserting that it always may; and that **a claim inherits the silence of the instrument beneath it.**

**What the evidence licenses.** Nothing about whether the system works. **No outcome evidence exists for any of the four artefacts**, and the fifth is this programme's invention. The assessment frame is borrowed from a clinical-education primer and **its transfer to engineering is untested.** The evidence about how assessors judge is class 1 from two open-access studies, and it says that **the instrument used here is idiosyncratic** — which is why the occasion claims as little as it does. The four platform surfaces are class 4 and **publish, respectively, no performance data, no empirical claim at all, no evaluator agreement rate and no detection rate.** The occasion marks a document set and says so.

**Where the design is thinnest.** §8's third objection. The apparatus is imported, the assessor is one person, and the paper cites the evidence that says so before running the occasion anyway. The response is to reduce what the judgement claims rather than to improve it.

**The concession worth remembering.** This occasion is called readiness and marks discoverability. **The gap is stated, it is not fixed, and you are the person who will be asked to speak in the vocabulary of the title.** Say what you were assessed on.
