# M04 · Managing Memory, Time and Contention

**Quarter:** [Q2](/wiki/quarters/Q2.md) · **Seminar days:** [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md)–[S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) · **Complexity class:** 2
**Strands:** SY · CF · MS · PE
**Platform anchor:** Foundry embeddings and prompt caching; Foundry deployment types, quota and rate limits · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## The central difficulty

M03 asserted repeatedly that constant factors have physical causes and deferred the explanation four times. This module pays the debt, and in doing so it changes what a performance model is.

The difficulty is that students hold a model of computation in which operations cost what the operation is — an addition costs an addition, a memory read costs a read. That model is the one S002 gave them and it is wrong by two or three orders of magnitude in ways that depend entirely on **where the data is and who else wants the machine**. Those two questions — locality and contention — are the module's whole subject.

The stance being installed: **performance is a property of a system under load, not of a program in isolation.** A number measured on an idle machine with warm caches is not a prediction about production, and treating it as one is the most common performance error in the industry.

## The practical turn: contention is a bill

The module used to make its argument on a machine the students were not paying for. That was the weakness. A student can accept that contention exists, agree that latency is not throughput, and still treat every limit as a soft one, because nothing in the room ever refused them.

This module now runs on a metered service. **The limits are real, they are enforced in code, they are metered, and somebody pays.** A student who asks a Foundry deployment for more than its allocation does not get a slow answer; they get HTTP 429 with a `retry-after-ms` header, and the request that failed still consumed the budget that caused it to fail. A student who wants headroom on a provisioned deployment finds it billed hourly whether or not a single token passes through it. A student who wants more quota discovers that quota is a policy limit with no cost attached — and that having it does not mean the capacity exists.

That last distinction is the module's practical payload and the whole of [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md). **Quota is not capacity.** It is written down by the vendor in those terms, it is the first thing every capacity argument in this programme has to survive, and it cannot be learned by being told.

The module is therefore also where the programme's four-layer platform map is entered from the bottom. Capacity is the layer under everything else, and its failures present at the application as something else entirely — which is the argument [research/06-microsoft-platform/](/research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md) exists to support and which this module makes concrete before any of the layers above it are named.

## What the module operates

Every day is executed with GitHub Copilot in agent mode against a real Foundry deployment, through the **Azure MCP Server** in read configuration and the **Microsoft Learn MCP Server** for grounding. The Azure MCP Server publishes a read-only annotation on each tool, and part of the module's discipline is that students check it rather than assume it: the day they stop checking is the day they run something destructive by accident.

Two capabilities are **demonstrated rather than operated**, because the programme does not hold the licence or the invitation, and neither is assessed:

- **Provisioned throughput.** Students read a capacity check and the hourly billing model; they do not create a provisioned deployment.
- **Priority processing,** which is in preview and available by invitation.

The distinction is stated to students on the day it applies, because a student who cannot tell what they ran from what they were shown will report both as experience.

## Why this module sits here

**It closes four deliberate open loops.** S011 point 4, S012 point 4, S013 caller C and S014 point 5 each named a mechanism and deferred it. The module resolves all four, and the resolution is the first time the programme has made a promise at that scale and kept it visibly.

**It is where class 2 becomes routine.** S015 effected the transition on one day with heavy support from a live caller. M04 makes choosing-and-defending the ordinary mode across five consecutive days, so that by S021 it is unremarkable.

**It must precede M06.** Measurement of a system under load requires knowing what load does. S027's error structure and S029's instrument design both assume the student knows that a measurement perturbs and that variance has physical sources.

## Task-class variability plan

At class 2 the student chooses the method, so variability is in **the constraint that makes one method preferable**.

- **S016** varies the access pattern. Same data, four patterns, four best layouts.
- **S017** varies the workload mix. Interactive, batch and mixed, with a different policy winning each.
- **S018** varies the working set relative to available memory, which is the only day where the same policy is best and worst depending on one number.
- **S019** varies the target. Throughput, mean latency and tail latency give three different operating points on one curve.
- **S020** varies nothing. It is the assessment occasion and requires all four.

The platform supplies a second axis of variability the module did not previously have. Each day's four contrasting cases are four configurations executed against the same deployment, so the cases differ in exactly the dimension the day is about and in nothing else — which is the controlled contrast the design has always wanted and could only approximate on supplied apparatus.

## Load profile

The module is the programme's highest-load stretch so far and the design does not pretend otherwise.

S016 and S019 are the peaks. S016 because the memory hierarchy has many interacting levels and the interaction is the content; S019 because queueing behaviour is non-linear and human intuition about it is reliably wrong.

Load management is structural rather than local: **the same substrate component is used on all five days.** Students meet it at S016 and are still arguing about it at S020, which means comprehension load is paid once for the module and every measurement is comparable to every other.

Fading is retained around the methods and removed from the choices, per the position stated at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md) — the arithmetic is supported throughout, the decision is not.

## Module-level predicted failure modes

**The hierarchy learned as trivia.** Cache line sizes and level counts are memorable and useless. Countermeasure: every day's artefact requires a prediction that the numbers alone cannot produce, and all specific figures are marked perishable.

**Queueing intuition never forms.** S019's material is genuinely counter-intuitive — utilisation and latency are not linearly related — and one day is thin. Countermeasure: S013's caller C planted the phenomenon two weeks earlier, S019 resolves it, and S020 requires it. Three encounters is the most the timetable allows.

**The capacity argument becomes arithmetic.** S020's risk is that students produce a number with a calculation attached and call it an argument. Countermeasure: the defence is to a person who will act on it, and the specification requires the assumption that would most change the answer to be named.

**Idle-machine measurement.** The module's target error, reproduced by the module's own students. Countermeasure: every day's measurements are taken under a supplied background load, and one day's are taken both ways so that the difference is observed rather than asserted.

**The agent supplies the number and the student ships it.** The module's new failure mode, and the one the platform turn introduces. Copilot will produce a plausible capacity calculation, a plausible batch size and a plausible retry policy on request, and every one of them can be wrong in a way that only shows up under load. Countermeasure: each day seals a prediction at phase 1 about what the agent will get wrong, checks it at phase 8, and requires an intervention record in the artefact. **An intervention record with no entries fails unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.**

## Assessment contribution

**No entrustment judgements.** S020 is assessment-bearing and produces a specification decision and a dispositions observation.

Specification items:

- **S016** — a layout chosen for an access pattern, with a predicted and measured effect.
- **S017** — a scheduling policy chosen for a workload mix, with the workload it serves worst named.
- **S018** — an allocation policy with its working-set assumption stated and the point at which it fails.
- **S019** — an operating point chosen against a stated target, with the curve and the sensitivity.
- **S020** — the capacity argument: a quantitative claim about a forthcoming load change, made before observation, defended orally, with its most load-bearing assumption named.

Every one of the five specification items additionally carries the three artefact elements the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) requires: the **prompt record**, the **intervention record** and the **frontier statement**. They are not separate deliverables. They travel with the build, and they are what distinguishes a student who directed the work from one who accepted it.

Dispositions observed: **integrity** at S020, in whether the argument's uncertainty is stated to the person relying on it.

## The S020 follow-through

S020's capacity arguments are about a real forthcoming load change. Staff record the cohort's predictions and compare them against **what actually happens**, and the comparison is reported back in Q3.

This is the programme's first long-horizon prediction adjudication and the first time students' work is checked against reality after they have stopped thinking about it.

**The record now carries a second series.** Alongside the capacity claim, each student's S020 frontier statement is recorded — one thing the agent did well and one thing it did badly on this build. Both series are read at the same Q3 occasion, and both are retrieved again at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md), [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md). The capacity series answers whether the cohort's estimate of a system was right. The frontier series answers whether the cohort's estimate of its own principal instrument was right, which is the harder question and the one the programme has the weaker claim to.

The two are kept as separate series and are not combined into a score. A student may be well calibrated about the platform and badly calibrated about the agent, and the design's expectation is that this combination will be the common one.

## Perishability register

| Durable | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| Data moves between levels in fixed-size blocks | Cache hits occur for every 128 additional identical tokens after the first 1,024 |
| Locality is a property of an access pattern | Prompt-cache prefix matching and its retention window |
| Every allocation policy has a victim | Deployment types, and Global Batch's separate enqueued quota |
| Working set is not allocation | The rate-limit estimate counts `max_tokens`, not tokens produced |
| Non-linear saturation is a class | The 429 boundary and its retry feedback |
| A limit is a policy someone set | Quota is a policy limit with no cost, and is not capacity |

The left column is assessed. The right column is the vehicle, is re-verified before every offering per the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), and every specific figure in it is expected to change.
