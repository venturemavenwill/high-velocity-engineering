## The switch this module makes, and why it is announced rather than assumed

For forty-five sessions the agent has been an adversary.

[S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) set the disposition in the first hour: attack your own position with it, and **an agent that agreed with you was not used as an instrument.** Every stage since has used it that way — to argue an engagement should be declined, to enumerate failure causes nobody in the room wanted to name, to argue that a commitment should be withdrawn. The learner's job was to be harder to persuade than the thing persuading them.

**From [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) the agent does work that ships**, and the disposition that made the first forty-five sessions work is the wrong one for the next five. An instrument you are trying to defeat is not an instrument you can delegate to. An instrument you delegate to is not one you can afford to stop checking.

**Nothing in the source material signposts that switch**, and the omission is the module's opening problem rather than a gap in the sources. The corpus that supplies the delivery model treats the tooling as an accelerant throughout and never distinguishes the two uses. The playbook barely addresses generated code at all — its one page on the subject is a commit-authorship convention, distinguishing the case where the agent is the author and a person is recorded as supervising from the case where a person is the author and the assistance is recorded on a separate trailer. That distinction is useful and it is a bookkeeping rule, not a practice.

So the module states the switch, and it states the thing that does not switch: **you remain accountable for the output either way.** The adversarial use protects your reasoning. The instrumental use produces work. Neither of them moves the defence off your desk, and [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) is where that becomes concrete rather than rhetorical.

## Why this module's anchor is its subject, and why the earlier refusals were reversed

**This module was for a long time the exception.** [Stage 2](/wiki/quarters/Q2.md), [Stage 3](/wiki/quarters/Q3.md) and [Stage 4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md) refused to name an instrument in the header, and [M09](/wiki/modules/M09.md) kept the refusal. Their reason was sound and it was the same reason each time: naming the instrument decides the instrument before the question is stated, and the choice was the assessed work. **That reasoning has since been reversed, and the reversal is recorded on each of those stage pages rather than erased.** The short account is that each stage decided independently, six of the nine argued themselves out of an anchor, and the aggregate they jointly produced — seventy-nine of the ninety sessions with no platform workflow at all — was nobody's intention. The decision had been taken at the wrong level: a per-stage judgement cannot see the total it is producing.

**What did not change is this module's own reason, which was never the same one.** Elsewhere the instrument was a choice about how to do the work. **Here the instrument is the question.** You cannot teach that a constraint file is a specification without a host that loads one. You cannot teach that a green check is a claim about a check rather than about the work without a check that goes green. You cannot teach comprehension debt on a whiteboard, because the debt is contracted at the moment somebody accepts a change they did not read, and that moment is a user-interface event.

The anchor is therefore taken, and it is taken with three conditions attached.

**It is pinned, not current.** A commit, a package version, a host, a model and a date. Every session records them; every cohort re-records its own. The programme's account of this repository rests on a machine-read of a single revision, and **a claim about that revision is not a claim about the project.**

**It is taught as one vendor's opinion.** The separation of agent behaviour into four kinds of authored artefact — automatically applied constraints, user-invoked entry points, orchestrating agents, and reusable procedures — is a genuine and teachable distinction. It is also a product decision, and the durable idea underneath it is smaller and older: **agent behaviour is an engineering artefact, and if it is an artefact it should be authored, versioned, reviewed, packaged and distributed like any other.**

**Its benefit claims are excluded.** The repository's prose uses causal and outcome vocabulary — accelerate, prevents, proven — and **none of it is established by anything in the repository.** It contains evaluation machinery and no committed results: a grader exists, a result corpus does not. Its own documentation states that its behaviour checks test output shape rather than correctness, that some checks are advisory rather than authoritative depending on where they run, and that human oversight remains necessary. The revision this module pins is not all-green, and the failing check is part of the evidence.

**That combination is exactly why it is the right anchor.** It is a large, sincere, carefully engineered control system published by the vendor whose platform the programme teaches, in which nothing is careless and nothing is demonstrated. A straw example would teach the same lesson worse.

## The claim the module is built on, and where it came from

**Generated code accumulates comprehension debt: it exists, it passes, and nobody understands it.**

That sentence is the corpus's own, and it is the sharpest thing in approximately ninety documents about the model those documents exist to promote. [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) is built on it entirely.

It has a companion, buried deeper, which [M09](/wiki/modules/M09.md) already used and which this module inherits. On a mature production codebase the assistant **could not** be asked to add a feature end to end, because the generated code reused outdated patterns and ignored architectural constraints; the workaround was to make failing tests the specification and put most of the human effort there. That account is held in speaker notes while the same collection's headline stories describe end-to-end feature delivery as the model's signature achievement.

**The module teaches the contradiction rather than choosing a side, and the reason is that the testimony is the better-evidenced half.** It names a codebase property, a specific failure mechanism, and a workaround that costs something. The headline names none of those. A learner who leaves able to say *this worked here and not there, and here is the property that distinguishes them* has the competence; a learner who leaves believing either story unconditionally has a slogan.

The third piece is a prohibition and it is the module's spine: **use the tooling to make each step better, not to skip steps.** It is stated as a headline takeaway in the source's own training material, and it is contradicted by that source's proudest customer-facing anecdote, in which a prototype is generated on the first day with no discovery activity described at all. **Neither document notices.** [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md) puts them side by side.

## Why the module sits here

**It needs [M09](/wiki/modules/M09.md) in front of it.** A learner who cannot state what *done* means for one behaviour cannot delegate that behaviour, and a learner who has never recorded what a review did not read has no instrument to point at an executor's output. [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) and [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) are the two prerequisites and both are artefacts rather than concepts.

**It must precede [Stage 6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md).** Threat modelling a system nobody understands produces a document rather than a finding, and the comprehension audit from [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) is what tells the assurance stage where its system model is guessing.

## Load profile

The module's intrinsic load is the highest in the stage and it is front-loaded, which is deliberate.

[S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) and [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md) carry the setup cost of the anchor — a host, a configuration, a tracking convention — and that cost lands once, on those two sessions, and on none of the three after. **A cohort that spreads the setup across five sessions will spend the module on tooling**, which is the failure this module is least able to survive, because the thing being taught is a disposition towards tooling rather than a tool.

[S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) is the affective peak and it is uncomfortable in a specific way. It requires a learner to point at a region of their own working, passing increment and say *I cannot explain this*, in front of people, about code that works. **That is harder than admitting a failure**, because there is no visible problem to justify the admission.

[S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) inverts the module's own instrument and is the point at which the adversarial disposition from [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) is deliberately re-installed on top of the instrumental one, rather than replacing it.

## What the module hands forward

**Delegating the work does not delegate the defence.**

Installed at [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) as the constraint an executor actually receives, made durable at [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md), priced at [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md), bounded at [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md), and assessed at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) by asking the candidate to defend a region chosen by somebody else.

Assumed thereafter by [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md), which builds a system model of something the learner may only partly understand; by [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md), which asks whether a governance scope test survives agentic delivery; and by [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md), which asks what dependency the engineer created.
