## The central difficulty

M09 measured a model against a criterion. The criterion arrived from somewhere and nobody asked where.

That question is this module's whole subject, and asking it moves the programme to **complexity class 4: the problem arrives ambiguous and the student formulates it, including what would count as an answer.** Until now every task has had a stated success criterion. Here there is none, and constructing one is the work.

The difficulty is that a criterion feels like a fact. Accuracy, relevance, helpfulness, quality — each sounds like a property of the output rather than a decision somebody made about what to value. Once a criterion is written down it acquires authority quickly, it is optimised against, and the thing it was standing in for is forgotten.

The module's stance: **a criterion is constructed, by named people, from evidence, for a purpose — and it drifts, its judge is biased, and the interval you report around it is usually wrong.**

## Where the argument becomes concrete

This module is where the platform stops being an illustration and becomes the subject. **A Foundry evaluator is a constructed criterion with an unpublished judge**, and every one of the module's five claims has a place in the service where a student can operate it rather than accept it.

Ask where a criterion came from and the answer, on this platform, is unusually legible: **somebody chose the evaluator, somebody chose the threshold, and somebody chose the split.** None of those three people is the student, none of them is the affected party, and all three decisions are recoverable from the documentation in an afternoon.

- **Somebody chose the evaluator.** The catalogue ships evaluators named after constructs — relevance, coherence, task adherence, customer satisfaction. A rubric evaluator can be auto-generated from an agent's own instructions, which is criterion construction with the constructing party's own description as the only evidence.
- **Somebody chose the threshold.** Quality evaluators score one to five and pass at three. Content-safety evaluators score zero to seven and pass at or *below* three. Rubric evaluators return a weighted average normalised to zero-to-one and pass at 0.5. Three different scales, three different directions, one word — `passed`.
- **Somebody chose the split.** How a multi-turn conversation is divided into query and response determines what the score is about. The documentation says so in its own words: **"How you split determines what you're evaluating."** The default is the last turn.

**No agreement-with-human data is published for any Foundry evaluator.** That single fact carries more of this module than any other: it means every judged score the students produce across five days is uncalibrated, that they must say so in every report, and that [S048](wiki/seminars/S048.md)'s judge is not a metaphor but the thing actually producing their numbers. Where the module says *characterise your instrument*, the platform's answer is that nobody has published a characterisation of theirs.

**Every day of this module produces a build** except [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md), which is assessment-bearing and carries the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement into the defence instead. The full discipline is in the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

## Why this module sits here

**It requires M06 and M09 entirely.** Construct validity, error structure, sampling, generalisation and selection are all load-bearing.

**It requires M08's problem construction.** A criterion arrives the way a request does — as somebody's conclusion — and S036's apparatus applies directly.

**It requires M09's deployed model.** The module's criterion is constructed for a system that is running, judged by people who use it, and S050 reports its actual behaviour against what students said it would do badly.

**It is the last module before the language-model material.** Every day of M11 and M12 depends on a student who knows that a benchmark is a criterion somebody built.

## Task-class variability plan

At class 4 the student formulates the problem, so variability is in **what makes the criterion contested**.

- **S046** varies who is asked: four groups with legitimate and incompatible views of what good means.
- **S047** varies the passage of time: the same criterion applied at four points, drifting in a different way each time.
- **S048** varies the judge: human, model, rubric-guided and unguided, disagreeing systematically.
- **S049** varies the sampling structure: the same result with four dependency structures and four honest intervals.
- **S050** varies the audience: the same finding, four parties, each wanting it to say something different.

## Load profile

High throughout and highest at S049, which combines unfamiliar inferential machinery with the module's least intuitive content.

The module's distinctive load is not cognitive but **dispositional**. Every day requires the student to hold that a thing which looks like a fact is a decision, and to keep holding it while other people treat it as a fact. That is tiring in a way the programme has not previously demanded.

The standing load reduction is that **one criterion runs through all five days**, constructed at S046 for the deployed model from M09, and defended at S050 to the people it affects.

## Module-level predicted failure modes

**The criterion becomes the engineer's.** The module's defining failure. Countermeasure: S046's criterion must be traceable to named people, and a criterion no stakeholder recognises fails.

**Validation of the validator skipped.** S047's risk, and the one that produces the most damage later. Countermeasure: the artefact requires a check the criterion could fail, not a review it will pass.

**Judge bias treated as noise.** S048's risk, and S027's error in a new domain. Countermeasure: the day's artefact requires a systematic component to be identified and its direction stated.

**The interval reported without its dependency structure.** S049's risk. Countermeasure: the specification fails an interval computed as though observations were independent, unless independence is argued.

**The platform instance is mistaken for the durable claim.** New with the module's practical turn, and the risk the design watches hardest. A student who leaves able to configure a Foundry evaluation and unable to say what a criterion is has learned a product. Countermeasure: every day carries a perishability register separating the durable claim from the platform instance, and every specification assesses the reasoning rather than the configuration. **Where a capability cannot be run — because it requires a licence or tenant privilege the programme does not hold — the day states that it is demonstrated rather than operated, and does not assess it.**

## Assessment contribution

**One entrustment judgement, at S050.** The activity is *state what an evaluation does not license, to a party who wants it to license more* — the closest precursor yet to EA-3. Expected 2b.

Specification items:

- **S046** — a criterion traceable to named people, with what each group wanted and what was traded.
- **S047** — a validation the criterion could fail, run, with its result.
- **S048** — a judge's error structure with a systematic component and its direction.
- **S049** — an interval with its dependency structure argued, and the claim it supports.
- **S050** — the statement, delivered, with what the audience wanted it to say.

Every specification item from S046 to S049 additionally carries the **prompt record**, the **intervention record** and the **frontier statement**. S050 carries the same three into the defence rather than into an artefact, because it has no build.

Dispositions observed: **integrity** at S047 and S050; **humility** at S048.

## Perishability

The module's durable content is the five claims in the stance above and nothing else. Everything platform-specific is perishable and is marked on the day that teaches it.

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| A criterion is a decision, traceable to people | A rubric evaluator's dimensions, weights and generation inputs |
| A criterion drifts, and cumulative adjustment has no culprit | Evaluator versions sharing a stable `id` across arbitrary edits |
| A judge is an instrument with an error structure | The judge model named in `deployment_name`, and the published model ranking |
| An interval is a property of a procedure applied to a structure | `sampleCount`, `standardDeviation` and the portal's t-test legend |
| A score whose agreement with human judgement is unpublished is uncalibrated | No Foundry evaluator publishes it |

**If Microsoft publishes evaluator agreement data, the durable claims are unchanged and the module re-anchors.** That is the test the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) applies before every offering.
