# M07 · Cheap Evidence First

**Strands:** PE · ME

## The central difficulty

A design has been written. Somewhere inside it is a thing that might not work, and everybody in the room can feel which thing it is without being able to say what would settle it.

The default response is to start building and find out. That response is not stupid — it is the only one that reliably produces an answer, and it is usually the fastest route to *a* result. Its defect is one of timing rather than of method: **the answer arrives after the commitment it was supposed to inform.** By the time the thing that might not work has been shown not to work, the team is staffed, the date is public, and the finding has become a problem to be managed rather than a decision to be taken.

The module's response is the spike, and the spike is a more disciplined object than the word suggests. It is not a rough first attempt at the system. It is an instrument pointed at one uncertainty, with a stated question, a declared bound, a written finding and a disposal rule — and the disposal rule is what stops it becoming the thing it was supposed to prevent.

## What the open playbook supplies here, and why it is the module's spine

The openly published engineering playbook this programme draws on has a page on engineering feasibility spikes that is the most operationally prescriptive thing in its whole design section, and the protocol it gives is complete rather than suggestive. Three parts of it are carried into this module directly.

**The spike opens with a pre-mortem**, framed by a single question: imagine this has already failed — what caused it? That is a generative act placed deliberately before any investigation, and [S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md) teaches it as one.

**The spike closes with a written finding in a fixed shape** — goal, method, evidence, conclusions, next steps — and the shape is not decoration. Separating evidence from conclusions in the artefact is what lets a reader disagree with the conclusion without disturbing the evidence, which is the same discipline [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) installed for observation records and [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) assesses here.

**And two standing rules constrain what the spike may be used for.** Its goal is fact-finding rather than decision-making or recommendation. Its code is not the product's code. Together those two are what stop a spike becoming an unreviewed prototype in production, and neither is negotiable in this module.

**A fourth item is carried as an example of a rarer virtue.** The playbook's feasibility-study material — on pages whose footers place them in the most uniformly maintained part of the site — sets a bounded investigation window and then explicitly relaxes its own engineering rigour inside it, stating that not all experimentation there requires full test coverage and review. **A standard that names the conditions under which it does not apply is more useful than one that does not**, and it is the cleanest available demonstration that a time box is a licence as well as a limit.

## The durations problem, quarantined

The playbook attaches specific durations to the pre-mortem, to the recurring share-out, and to the outer bounds of a feasibility study.

**None of those numbers is reproduced in this module, in this programme, or anywhere in this repository**, and the reason is the same mechanism [M04](/wiki/modules/M04.md) quarantined specimen answers for. A duration lifted out of one organisation's cadence and printed in a curriculum stops being that organisation's choice and becomes a norm. A learner who has read it will hear their own time box as long or short against a scale nobody validated, and will not notice that the scale came from somebody else's calendar.

What transfers is the *shape*: the pre-mortem is fixed-length and short, the share-out recurs and is shorter, and the study has a declared outer bound. **Each session requires the learner to set their own and defend it against the decision waiting on it**, which is [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md)'s stopping-rule discipline applied to a technical question rather than a conversational one.

## What the practice corpus supplies, and the thing it buries

The internal corpus is thinner here than the playbook and contributes two things.

**One is a prohibition it states clearly and then contradicts:** use the tooling to make each step better, not to skip steps. Its own customer-facing material's proudest anecdote is a prototype generated on the first day from a reusable starting point with no discovery activity described at all. Read against the method the same organisation publishes, that is step-skipping presented as acceleration, and **neither document notices.** [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md) hands the contradiction over rather than resolving it, because a spike and a day-one prototype are genuinely hard to tell apart from the outside and the difference is entirely in the charter.

**The other is the most trustworthy passage in the corpus, and it is in speaker notes.** On a mature production codebase the assistant could not be asked to add a feature end to end: the generated code reused outdated patterns and ignored architectural constraints, and the workaround was to make failing tests the specification and spend most of the human effort on the tests. That directly contradicts the collection's own headline claims about speed, it is the closest thing anywhere in the collection to a recorded technical limit, and **it is a feasibility finding in everything but name.** [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md) teaches it as one.

## The four repairs this module makes to the source

Grouped by what the learner has to do about them.

- **The corpus reports no cost side of any kind.** Every claim in it is a gross benefit; the cost of the investigation itself is never counted. This module counts it, at every session, because the stage's question has a price clause in it.
- **The corpus reports no failure.** Its single failure narrative is synthetic and fictional by its own admission, so it cannot supply a case in which cheap proof averted anything. The module's cases are constructed and say so.
- **The playbook offers no comparative evidence** that its spike protocol beats an alternative — true of the whole document. The module teaches the protocol as a protocol and never as a finding.
- **Neither source treats data as a feasibility question.** The corpus's discovery instrument has no data block at all: nothing about provenance, access, correctness, labelling or consent. [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md) exists because of that absence and is the module's addition rather than its inheritance.

## Why the module sits here

**It needs [Stage 3](/wiki/quarters/Q3.md) in front of it.** A spike without a design is a hobby. The question a spike answers has to be a question some decision turns on, and until a design exists at the scope of its decision there is no decision for it to turn on. A learner who spikes before framing produces an interesting investigation of something nobody was going to act on, which is the module's second-commonest failure and is named at [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md).

**It supplies [M08](/wiki/modules/M08.md).** The disposal rule installed at [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md) is what makes a low-fidelity prototype possible at all: a learner who cannot throw away code cannot throw away an artefact either, and the whole of [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md) depends on having practised the disposal on something that was easier to give up.

## Load profile

Moderate at [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md) and [S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md), high at [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md), highest at [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md).

[S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md) is the intrinsic high point. Converting a felt uncertainty into a question that has an answer, choosing an instrument that could produce that answer, and doing both before knowing what the answer is, does not decompose into simpler steps — and it must be done for several uncertainties at once, which is where the load actually comes from.

[S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) is the evaluative high point, and its load is the requirement to report a negative result in writing to somebody who was hoping for a different one.

## What the module hands forward

One sentence, and [M08](/wiki/modules/M08.md), [Stage 5](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md) and [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) all assume it has landed:

**An investigation that could only have produced one answer was not an investigation.**

That is stated about spike charters at [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md), about question design at [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md), about prototype tests at [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md), and about measurement much later. It is [M03](/wiki/modules/M03.md)'s claim about instruments, pointed at your own work rather than at somebody else's.
