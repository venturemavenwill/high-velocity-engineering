# M09 · Delivery Under Constraint

**Quarter:** [Q5](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md) · **Sessions:** [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md)–[S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md) · **Complexity class:** 3
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none

---

## The central difficulty

Every rule in this module is a rule about what you do when you are behind.

That is not a rhetorical framing; it is the only condition under which the rules are load-bearing. Nobody breaks scope discipline in week one. Nobody skips the tests on the change they are proud of. Nobody rubber-stamps a review when the queue is empty. **The discipline is invisible while it is cheap, and the module's entire subject is the fortnight in which it stops being cheap.**

The constraint itself is inherited rather than chosen. A forward-deployed engineer builds inside somebody else's estate, against an architecture decided before they arrived, through a review process they do not own, on a date somebody sold. The practice corpus behind this programme has one genuinely transferable idea about that condition, and it is worth stating in its own terms: **the space is classified by how much each part of it can move, and even in an almost entirely frozen space there is something that can be changed.** Constraint is not an excuse for doing nothing. [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) installed the classification; this module is where it is spent.

## The module's argument, in one sentence

**A rule you cannot tell whether somebody followed is not a rule.**

The open playbook is the best available material for making that argument, because it is unusually good at the mechanical end and unusually candid at the other, and the two sit on adjacent pages in the same flat declarative register.

At the mechanical end: code is incomplete if it is not accompanied by tests; tests are committed in the same change as the code they cover, and *I will add tests next* is stated as not acceptable; new work becomes a new backlog item and never a bigger old one; every changed line is read; an in-scope change may not be deferred by its author; an out-of-scope issue may not block the change in front of you. **The pull request template is nine fields and every one of them is checkable by somebody who has the pull request and nothing else** — a work-item link, a description, reproduction and validation steps, a six-item checklist, an explicit breaking-change question, a testing section naming the operating system and the scenarios, the relevant logs, and the known dependencies.

At the other end, the same document declines to say when a change is too large — *it is not a matter of setting a code line limit, but rather using common sense* — on two separate pages, and **a substantial minority of the items on its own engineering checklist are not falsifiable as written.** One of its sections stops asserting conditions partway down and starts asking questions instead, so items in the same list are doing different grammatical work.

**Neither end is a defect and the pairing is the teaching material.** A learner who takes the playbook as a coherent standard will be caught out by anyone who reads two adjacent pages; a learner who has been shown the pairing has acquired the skill of reading an internal standard, which is the document they will actually meet in their first job.

## The tension the module cannot resolve

The rules above were written for a world in which producing the code was the expensive part.

Every one of them is a constraint on output at the point of production. Tests with the code, scope in its own item, every line read. **When production becomes cheap and review does not, the constraints stop binding at the same rate**, and the corpus that supplies the delivery model does not notice.

That corpus rests its entire claim on expert human validation. It says the tooling amplifies the operator rather than levelling the field, and it says a team member must be somebody you would have trusted on a purely human team. It therefore makes reviewer competence load-bearing — and across approximately ninety documents it **never asks what happens when the volume arriving at the reviewer changes.** Review capacity is the binding constraint on the model and it is not discussed once.

**This module has no answer.** [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) does not pretend to one: it requires the learner to state the review capacity their own rate of production demands, to name who supplies it, and to write down what they let through when the demand exceeded the supply. **The statement is what is assessed, not the capacity it reports.** A learner who concludes that their team's rate of production is already beyond what its review can absorb, and says so with the evidence, has done the session's work.

## Why the module sits here

**It needs [Stage 4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md) in front of it.** A slice is only minimal against a proof; without one, *smallest valuable increment* collapses into *whatever we can finish*. [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md) cuts the slice out of the commitment obtained at [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md) and priced at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md), and a learner who never established what the commitment turned on has nothing to cut against.

**It must precede [M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md).** The specification written as failing tests at [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) is what makes delegation defensible four sessions later, and the review record at [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) is the instrument [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) points at an executor. Reversing the order teaches learners to configure a delegate before they can say what they would have wanted done.

## Load profile

The module's intrinsic load is low and its affective load is concentrated at [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md).

[S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md) to [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) are craft sessions whose difficulty is precision rather than volume: naming a recipient, refusing an addition, writing a check that could fail. **[S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) is different in kind**, because it requires a learner to record in writing that they approved something they had not fully read, in a professional culture where that admission is career-relevant, and then to argue that the alternative was worse.

The session is placed fourth so that [S042](/wiki/seminars/S042.md)'s boundary vocabulary and [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md)'s specification are both available. A reviewer who can say *that is a new item* and *that is not what the specification asked for* has two ways to decline that are not personal, and both of them come from the sessions immediately before.

[S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md)'s load is different again: it is the requirement to defend a small thing in front of somebody who expected more, and to argue that the smallness was a decision.

## What the module hands forward

**Work you would ship is work you can say the boundary of.**

Installed at [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md) as the slice, enforced at [S042](/wiki/seminars/S042.md) as the ledger, expressed at [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) as a check that could fail, tested at [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) against what a reader had time to read, and assessed at [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md).

Assumed thereafter by [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md), which asks the same question about an executor who was not in the room, and by [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md), which asks whether the boundary is legible to somebody who inherits it.
