# M01 · The Engagement Before It Starts

**Quarter:** [Q1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md) · **Sessions:** S001–S005 · **Complexity class:** 1
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** GitHub Copilot agent mode, at [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) and [S003](/wiki/seminars/S003.md); none elsewhere
**Assessment:** [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md) ⊘

## The module's central difficulty

Learners arrive able to start work and unable to decline it. That is not a deficit of courage. It is the correct behaviour for almost every professional situation they have been in, where accepting the task was the job and questioning it was somebody else's role.

The module's purpose is to install an alternative that is available in the moment, which means it has to be **written down before it is needed**. A refusal improvised in the room is a refusal that will not happen; the sponsor is better prepared, has more at stake, and has already answered the objection once.

**The hard part is not persuading learners that qualification matters — they agree immediately.** The hard part is that agreement costs nothing and produces a disqualifying condition that could never fire. Almost the entire module is arranged around that single failure.

## The five sessions

| Session | Title | What the learner does | What the learner produces |
|---|---|---|---|
| [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) | Why this programme is shaped like an engagement | Writes a position before being taught anything, then has an agent attack it | An engagement position with a disqualifying condition |
| [S002](/wiki/seminars/S002.md) | What a forward-deployed engineer is for | Separates what they are accountable for from what they may only propose | A role boundary statement |
| [S003](/wiki/seminars/S003.md) | The problem behind the request | Strips the technology out of a request and looks for the difficulty underneath | A problem restatement with its evidence |
| [S004](/wiki/seminars/S004.md) | Reading a room you were invited into | Reads attendance as evidence, and commits in advance to what would change the plan | A room reading with a pre-commitment |
| [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md) ⊘ | The qualification decision | Decides, and defends the decision against challenge | A qualification decision, defended live |

The sequence is a narrowing. S001 asks whether refusal is available at all. S002 asks who is entitled to decide. S003 asks what is actually being decided about. S004 asks what the room tells you. S005 makes the decision.

## Why the module is class 1

Task specified, method given. Every session supplies the artefact's shape, and the learner supplies the content and the judgement. **Nothing in this module asks a learner to choose an approach**, because a learner choosing an approach in week one is a learner choosing the approach they already had.

Variation is introduced in surface only: the same brief is read four times through four different instruments — a position, a boundary, a restatement, a room. Holding the material constant while changing the instrument is what makes the instrument visible.

## Assessment contribution

**No entrustment evidence.** One assessment occasion, [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md), with a closed specification.

The specification checks whether the grounds for a decision were stated in a checkable form and whether the learner could hold them under questioning. It does not check the decision. **A defended decline and a defended proceed pass identically**, which is the design decision most likely to be misread by learners and is stated to them explicitly at S001.

## Predicted failure modes for the module as a whole

- **Qualification as agreement.** The module's argument is easy to accept and easy to accept without changing anything. *Countermeasure:* none available inside this module. The condition written at S001 is tested at [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) against a brief built to fire it, and the interval is deliberate.
- **The decorative condition.** A disqualifying condition that could never occur. *Countermeasure:* the review movement at S001, the specification check at S005, and the fact that both are known in advance — which converts the failure from a trap into a standard.
- **Authority confusion.** A learner concludes that the module is teaching them to make a decision that in most organisations they will not own. *Countermeasure:* S002 makes the boundary the artefact, and states plainly that the module teaches recognition rather than authority. Whether recognition without authority is useful is untested and the module says so.
- **The restatement that renames.** At S003 a learner removes the product name and leaves the solution intact underneath. *Countermeasure:* the restatement is checked for a datable occurrence and a named workaround performer, neither of which a renamed solution can supply.
- **Fluency mistaken for evidence.** An agent produces a well-formed problem statement and the learner adopts it. *Countermeasure:* on both agent-bearing sessions the learner's own text is committed before the agent is invoked, and the comparison is what gets read out. **The countermeasure is procedural and therefore defeatable**, and there is no detector for a learner who defeats it.

## The evidence position

**Class 3 throughout.** The practice corpus behind this programme contains no declined engagement, no non-synthetic failure narrative and no exclusion criteria, so the module's existence is inferred from an absence rather than derived from a finding.

Three things inside the module have better standing and are marked separately in the papers: the direction of the retrieval effect behind asking the opening questions before teaching; the direction of the difference between behavioural recall and hypothetical prediction; and the definitional account of the role itself, which comes from the organisation that named it and is primary evidence of that organisation's stated model and of nothing else.
