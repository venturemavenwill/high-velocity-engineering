# M08 · Prototypes That Fail Well

**Quarter:** [Q4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md) · **Sessions:** [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md)–[S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md) · **Complexity class:** 3
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none

---

## The central difficulty

[M07](/wiki/modules/M07.md) asked whether the system could work. M08 asks whether the idea survives a person, and the two failures look nothing alike. A technical uncertainty resolves against a machine and the machine does not care how it is asked. An idea resolves against somebody who can see that you made the thing, who would rather not upset you, and who will tell you it looks great.

**The module's whole subject is that the artefact you show determines the answer you get**, and that most of the ways of improving an artefact make the answer worse.

This is the module where the practice corpus is at its best, and it is worth saying so plainly given how much of this programme is spent taking that corpus apart. Its argument for low fidelity is not asserted, it is derived: adding detail to an early concept artefact is **actively harmful**, because the detail drags the conversation onto things that are not the idea — the wrong equipment type, an assumed attribute of the workforce, whether a device fits over safety gear — and away from the thing being tested. Polish suppresses honest feedback. **Therefore strip it on purpose.**

That is a mechanism, it is checkable against your own experience the first time you run it, and it is the best-reasoned claim anywhere in the collection.

## The tension the corpus notices and cannot answer

Having derived the mechanism, the same collection observes that **its own tooling makes polished output nearly costless**, and offers no advice on holding the line when the refined version costs the same as the rough one.

That observation is correct and the absence of advice is honest. It is also the module's hardest problem, because the low-fidelity argument was constructed for a world in which polish had a price and the price did the enforcement. Remove the price and the countermeasure has to be carried entirely by intent, which is a much weaker thing to rest a practice on.

**This programme has no answer either.** [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md) does not pretend to one: it requires the learner to produce both versions, to state what each would cause a stakeholder to talk about, and to write the sentence they would actually say to a colleague who has already produced the refined one at no extra cost. **The sentence is what is assessed, not the position it defends.** A learner who concludes that low fidelity is no longer defensible and argues it well has done the session's work.

## The second tension, which the corpus does not notice

The same collection proposes **using a model's simulation of a user in place of a user** when users are unavailable, and offers no discussion of validity — in a body of work whose opening warning is against building for an imagined user.

[S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) refused the substitution and recorded that the refusal was an argument rather than a finding. [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md) does not repeat the refusal. It requires the learner to construct the substitution's best case — that the practical alternative is frequently not a real informant but no informant at all — and to state the one circumstance in which they would accept it and what they would write down when they did. **A prohibition that has never met its strongest objection is a preference wearing a rule's clothing**, and this module's position is that the learner should be the one holding it, not the curriculum.

## Failing on purpose, and the pedagogy it borrows

The module's second claim comes from the corpus in one line: **a cheap prototype that fails immediately is an instant win**, and stakeholders are to be actively coached into reading it that way rather than as a setback.

The coaching clause is the interesting half. It concedes that the reading is not natural — that a stakeholder's default interpretation of a failed prototype is *this went badly* — and it makes converting that reading part of the engineer's job rather than part of the stakeholder's disposition.

**This programme's teaching design rests on the same mechanism from a different literature, and the parallel is deliberate.** The learning-sciences source behind this curriculum's ordering argues that failure should be designed for rather than waited for; that the awareness of a gap is what makes the subsequent explanation land; that the phase which converts failure into learning is the explicit, prompted comparison between what you produced and what turned out to be right; and — the finding most often dropped — that **helping learners towards the answer during the generation phase reduces the failure and reduces the learning with it.**

The module uses that as a design warrant for its own sequencing and hands the same structure to the learner as professional technique at [S037](/wiki/seminars/S037.md), where the stakeholder is the one being carried through the comparison. **Two limits ride with it and are stated at every use.** The source's evidence is school, university and laboratory evidence; its author's claim that the cycle works in corporate and adult settings is asserted in his own voice and supported by no citation. And **the source's own book and its own meta-analysis disagree about how far the design generalises** — the book claims the design principles are domain-general and names writing and business among successful applications, while the meta-analysis abstract reports a reversal favouring instruction-first for the learning of domain-general skills. Only the abstract has been read. **The disagreement is unresolved, it bears directly on a module made largely of domain-general craft, and it is carried forward rather than resolved.**

## Whose answer counts

The module's third claim is about who is in the room, and it repairs an absence rather than inheriting a strength.

The corpus's discovery instrument asks the customer to endorse the interviewer's own solution, ships specimen answers that narrow the response before it is given, and contains no question asking who loses if the thing succeeds. **A prototype test run on that population, by that person, with that artefact, cannot produce a disconfirming answer**, and [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md) exists to make that unavailable.

The customer-conversation handbook this repository holds supplies the vocabulary: compliments, generic self-description and unexamined feature requests are the three classes of bad data; **commitment** means the other party gave up something they valued and **advancement** means the relationship reached a defined next stage; and a friendly contact who neither commits nor rejects is the worst outcome available, because it looks like progress. That handbook establishes no effect and no threshold and says so. **It is a diagnostic vocabulary and a set of inspectable behaviours, and that is what the module teaches.**

## Why the module sits here

**It needs [M07](/wiki/modules/M07.md) in front of it.** A learner who has not yet discarded a spike's code will not discard an artefact they made for a person. The disposal is easier to practise on code, which has no audience, than on a concept that somebody praised.

**It closes the stage into [Stage 5](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md).** Building begins with a commitment, and a commitment that nobody could have refused is not one. [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md) is where the stage's evidence is finally converted into an ask, and [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md) is where the ask has to be defended on price.

## Load profile

The module's difficulty is affective rather than intrinsic and it is concentrated at [S037](/wiki/seminars/S037.md).

Showing somebody an artefact you know will break, declining to repair it first, and then talking them out of the interpretation they naturally arrived at, is uncomfortable in a way that nothing in [M07](/wiki/modules/M07.md) is. It is placed second so that [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md)'s stripping discipline is available — a rough artefact is much easier to let fail than a finished one — and so that [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md) immediately puts the same skill in front of a population who did not commission the work.

[S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md)'s load is different again: it is the requirement to argue that your proof was cheap, in front of somebody entitled to name a cheaper one.

## What the module hands forward

**An artefact that could not have been rejected has not been tested, and a commitment that cost nothing has not been given.**

Installed at [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md), enforced at [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md), assessed at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md), and assumed by [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md), [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) and [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md).
