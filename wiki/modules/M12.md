# M12 · Finding the Right Thing

**Quarter:** [Q6](wiki/quarters/Q6.md) · **Seminar days:** [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md)–[S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** IS · ME · SY · PE
**Platform anchor:** Azure AI Search — keyword, vector, hybrid and semantic retrieval over the substrate corpus · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (Search, write) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server · Foundry MCP Server

---

## The central difficulty

Students arrive holding an ordering. Lexical retrieval is old, dense retrieval is new, new is better, and the engineering question is which library to use.

The ordering is not invented. It is what the published comparisons say, it is what the tooling assumes, and on the material the students have read it is frequently correct. Its problem is that it is an ordering of *methods*, and **retrieval quality is not a property of a method.**

It is a joint property of three things the method does not control: the distribution of queries the system actually receives, the corpus it searches, and the criterion by which an answer counts as an answer. Change any one and the ordering changes. The published comparison that produced the students' ordering fixed all three at values that belong to somebody else.

The module's second difficulty is sharper and is the reason it sits at class 4. **At class 4 the student formulates the problem including what counts as an answer, and in retrieval that is not a metaphor.** The student must write down what relevance means. They must then watch two competent people apply that written criterion to the same twenty documents and disagree. That disagreement is a ceiling on every measurement the module takes, it is not reducible by better methods, and almost every retrieval improvement the students have read about is smaller than it.

The module's stance: **the lexical baseline is not the naive option, the benchmark is a constructed criterion, and a retrieval system's quality is a property of your queries and your corpus rather than of your method.**

## Why this module sits here

**It requires M10 entirely.** Every comparison in this module is an evaluation, and the students will be asked to reject most of the published ones. That is only defensible from a cohort that knows a criterion is constructed by named people ([S046](wiki/seminars/S046.md)), that a judge carries a systematic component ([S048](wiki/seminars/S048.md)), and that an interval without its dependency structure is not a result ([S049](wiki/seminars/S049.md)). A student without M10 who is told that benchmarks mislead learns cynicism instead of method.

**It requires M11 immediately and specifically.** [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md)'s *similar-to-what* is the whole of [S057](wiki/seminars/S057.md)'s content in advance; [S052](wiki/seminars/S052.md)'s soft lookup is what a dense retriever is; [S053](wiki/seminars/S053.md)'s aggregate-hides-class finding is [S058](wiki/seminars/S058.md)'s and [S059](wiki/seminars/S059.md)'s central mechanism. The two modules are one quarter because the gap could not be tolerated.

**It requires M03 and M04's structural material.** [S013](wiki/seminars/S013.md)'s hashing and amortised claim types, [S014](wiki/seminars/S014.md)'s graph shape, and [S019](wiki/seminars/S019.md)'s operating point are not analogies for [S058](wiki/seminars/S058.md) — they are its content, arriving four quarters later with vectors in place of keys.

**It requires M09's [S041](wiki/seminars/S041.md).** A dense retriever evaluated against negatives drawn as its training negatives were drawn is a leak, and it is the leak class students find hardest to see because nothing was copied.

**It is not placed later** because Q7 needs a real system. [S061](wiki/seminars/S061.md) to [S064](wiki/seminars/S064.md) profile, batch and budget something, and the design's position is that it must be the students' own and must already have users.

## The platform this module runs on

This is the module where the programme's practical claim is most directly demonstrable, and the reason is that **every abstraction in the paragraphs above has a switch on one service.** [Q6](wiki/quarters/Q6.md)'s anchor is the Foundry model catalogue and Azure AI Search vector and hybrid retrieval; IS203's is *Azure AI Search: vector, hybrid, semantic ranking, the chunking trap*. The five days operate one search service over one substrate corpus, and the system that exists at the end of [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md) is serving the substrate's real users.

The mapping is exact and it is the reason the module is not a reading course:

| Module claim | Where it becomes a switch |
|---|---|
| The lexical baseline is not the naive option | A real BM25 index with `k1` and `b` the student did not set, that they must beat and often cannot |
| Relevance is a judgement, not a property | Relevance judgements the students write, apply and disagree over, against results a service returned |
| The training signal is the relevance definition | Four vector profiles over four embedding constructions, queried through one index |
| The operating point is a decision somebody made | `m`, `efConstruction`, `efSearch`, and a quantization configuration with an oversampling default |
| A benchmark fixes a triple that is not yours | The model leaderboard's own caveat that published figures **"might not reflect performance on your specific data"** |
| Approximation loss concentrates | The documents a compressed index stops returning, listed by identity rather than counted |

The students operate all of it **by prompting GitHub Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server**, grounding every claim about the service through the Learn MCP Server, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md). The module's position is that this is not a convenience: an index built from prompts and then characterised is the same engineering act as an index built by hand and then characterised, and the characterising is the part the programme assesses.

**Nothing about the service's numbers is taught as durable, and one silence is taught explicitly.** The documentation states parameter defaults, ranges, score ranges, token limits and chunk counts throughout, and **states no recall figure anywhere for approximate search at any parameter setting.** Students are required to notice that, because it is the exact quantity [S058](wiki/seminars/S058.md) is about and the exact quantity a vendor has no incentive to publish.

## Task-class variability plan

At class 4 the student formulates. Variability is therefore in **what makes the formulation contestable**, and in every case the method is the thing held fixed.

**In this module the contrasting cases are executed, not read.** Every day's four cases are four configurations of one search service, issued against one corpus, and the payload is the case whose measured behaviour defeats the room's committed prediction. A case that cannot be operated — because it needs a licence, a managed-compute deployment or a tenant privilege the programme does not hold — is stated as **demonstrated rather than operated**, and the specification does not assess it.

- **S056** varies the query population: one corpus, one pair of rankers, four query sets — and the winner changes with the set. **The payload set is the real one.**
- **S057** varies the training signal: one architecture, four negative constructions, four decision boundaries — one of which is best on the supplied evaluation because the evaluation was built the way it was.
- **S058** varies the operating point: one corpus and one retriever, four index parameterisations, four positions on a recall–latency curve — and one whose recall loss is concentrated rather than spread.
- **S059** varies the evaluation: one set of retrievers, four evaluation constructions, **four different orderings of the same systems.**
- **S060** varies nothing. It is the module's only day with a single case — the substrate, its real users, and one decision — and that is the variability plan's endpoint rather than a departure from it.

## Load profile

**High and evenly distributed**, which distinguishes this module from [M11](wiki/modules/M11.md)'s front-loaded shape. No day here is technically light.

The module's distinctive load is that **three quantities move at once.** Query distribution, corpus and relevance criterion each change the answer, and attributing an observed difference to the method requires holding all three still. Students who have only ever compared methods on a fixed benchmark have never had to do this, because the benchmark did it for them and did not say so.

The standing load reduction is that **the corpus, the query log and the relevance criterion carry across all five days.** The student writes the criterion once at [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md) and every subsequent day evaluates against it, so the criterion is a fixed cost paid on day one rather than a recurring one. This is also why [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md) is the module's heaviest day for its position.

The second reduction is that no day requires a student to implement an index. Index structures at [S058](wiki/seminars/S058.md) are supplied by the service, parameterised and instrumented; what the student decides is where to sit on the curve, which is the class-4 decision and the only part that transfers.

**The third is that the service is operated through an agent.** Nobody memorises a schema. Index definitions, vector profiles, skillsets and queries are produced by prompting Copilot through the Azure MCP Server and then read, and the module's position is that reading a definition the agent produced is a lower-load and more transferable act than authoring one from a portal. The cost is that a student can ship a configuration they cannot account for, which is what the **intervention record** exists to detect and is why it is a fail condition on every day.

## Module-level predicted failure modes

**The method is treated as the variable.** The module's defining failure and the one that survives correct answers: a student can state that quality depends on the query distribution and still, when asked to improve retrieval, reach for a different model. Countermeasure: every phase 3 in the module holds the method fixed and varies something else, and **every artefact element requires reporting by query class rather than in aggregate.** [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md)'s phase 3 must produce a case in which the older method wins on the real query distribution, and if it does not, the phase has failed.

**The configuration is treated as the system.** The module's new failure and the one the practical turn introduces: a student who has shipped a working index by prompting can mistake a running service for an understood one. Countermeasure: **every day requires one platform default the student did not choose to be named, with what it cost them**, and no day accepts a parameter value as an answer where a decision was asked for. This is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s requirement, and in this module it is load-bearing rather than decorative because the service has a default for every quantity the module is about.

**Relevance treated as a property of a document.** The failure that makes every subsequent measurement uninterpretable, because a student holding it will read judge disagreement as annotator error. Countermeasure: a written relevance criterion is a required artefact element from [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md) onward, peer disagreement against it is measured rather than assumed away, and the measured disagreement is carried forward as a stated ceiling at [S059](wiki/seminars/S059.md).

**Recall against exact search mistaken for accuracy against relevance.** [S058](wiki/seminars/S058.md)'s risk, and the one with the worst consequences in production, because a 98 per cent recall figure reads as a small loss and can be the whole of the gain that justified the system. Countermeasure: the artefact requires both quantities and an explicit statement of which one the promise to the caller is about.

**A published comparison read as transferable.** [S059](wiki/seminars/S059.md)'s risk and the module's longest half-life — a student who cites a leaderboard position in a design review three years from now will do so on this module's authority. Countermeasure: **every claim taken from a published comparison must be labelled by what that comparison fixed — corpus, query population, or relevance criterion — and defended as a claim about that.** An unlabelled transfer fails the specification.

**Retrieval nihilism.** The affective failure, and the module creates conditions for it: no method is best, the benchmark is constructed, the judgements disagree, and the approximation eats the improvement. A student can conclude that no retrieval claim can be made. Countermeasure: the lexical baseline is a real, defensible, cheap and checkable result that the module treats with respect throughout; every day ends with a claim the student is willing to make and to be held to; and [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md) ships.

## Assessment contribution

**One entrustment judgement, at [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md)** — *ship a retrieval system into production and own the consequence.* Precursor to EA-1, expected **2b–3a**, and the quarter's only occasion. The reduction to one is [Q6](wiki/quarters/Q6.md)'s decision and its reason is stated there.

Specification items:

- **S056** — a shipped keyword index, a written relevance criterion, a query set with its sampling disclosed, measured peer disagreement, and the query class on which the lexical baseline wins.
- **S057** — a shipped vector index with its positives and negatives stated, probed on the respects the cohort could not predict at [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md), and the class on which it loses to the baseline.
- **S058** — an operating point with its recall reported per class, and a checkable promise to the caller with its failure behaviour.
- **S059** — a published comparison re-read against the student's own corpus, with the transferable claim separated from the untransferable one and the judgement ceiling stated.
- **S060** — the ship decision, its failure-mode statement, and the user-facing promise.

**Every specification item in this module additionally carries the three artefact elements the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) requires** — the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement. At [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md) the three are carried into the release record and its defence rather than into a separate build. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that its own design could have detected an intervention-worthy error; the module's standing example of such a design is an index whose recall against exhaustive search is asserted by the build itself rather than by the student.

Dispositions observed: **humility** at [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md) and [S059](wiki/seminars/S059.md); **reliability** at [S058](wiki/seminars/S058.md) and [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md); **integrity** at [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md).

## Perishability

**Every number this module touches on the platform is perishable and every claim it assesses is not.** The separation is carried in each day's own register and is summarised here because the module is the wiki's densest concentration of vendor-specific values.

| Durable, assessed | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| Term weighting is a claim about informativeness | `k1` and `b`, and the algorithm's availability by service age |
| Relevance is a judgement against a criterion | The judgements the cohort wrote this year, on this corpus |
| Negatives are the decision boundary | Which embedding deployments the tenant holds |
| The operating point is a decision | `m`, `efConstruction`, `efSearch`, oversampling, quantization kind |
| Approximation loss concentrates | Every recall and latency figure the cohort measures |
| A benchmark licenses a claim about its own triple | The leaderboard's composition, and which models it covers |

Re-verification runs before every offering, per the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md). Where a parameter is renamed or a default changes, the day is re-anchored and the durable claim is unchanged; if a re-anchoring would require the durable claim to change, that is a finding about the claim and is recorded as one.
