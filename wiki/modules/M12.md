# M12 · Responsible AI Inside the Process

**Quarter:** [Q6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md) · **Sessions:** [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md)–[S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** one in every session — the build-path comparison extended with the option the vendor does not sell at [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md), Foundry prompt agents against hosted agents at [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md), Microsoft Purview DSPM for AI at [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md), Defender AI security posture management at [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md), and Microsoft Agent Framework evaluations bound to the assessment's open items at [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md)

## The central difficulty

Responsible-AI governance is usually met as a form that arrives late, is filled in by somebody who was not on the build, and clears a gate. By the time it appears, every decision it could have influenced has been made. **It is then filed, and it describes a system that stops existing within weeks.**

This module's position is that the failure is structural rather than cultural. It is not that engineers are careless about harm; it is that **the artefact is placed where it cannot change anything, given no mechanism to notice that it has gone stale, and closed by a signature rather than by work.** Each of those three is fixable, and the module fixes them one at a time.

The material behind it is the governance regime of a customer-embedded engineering organisation within a large technology vendor, which was found in two generations. **Comparing the generations is more instructive than either alone**, and the module is largely built on the comparison.

## The standing first question

**Can this be solved without AI at all?**

That question sits at the front of the regime, and it is the module's opening session because it is the only point at which the answer is still cheap. Asked after a model has been selected it is theatre; asked after a prototype exists it is an insult to the people who built it.

It is also the question the surrounding practice corpus is least equipped to ask. That corpus's organising thesis is excellent — a delivery can satisfy every agreed criterion, use good architecture, follow textbook fundamentals, ship to production **and still produce nothing of value** — and everything short of realised business value is reclassified as activity rather than impact. But the same corpus arrives at every engagement selling an AI-accelerated delivery model, and its readiness criteria are composed almost entirely of customer dispositions. **A practice whose failure explanation is always the customer's readiness has arranged its evidence so that a disappointing result confirms rather than challenges the thesis**, and it will not often conclude that the tooling was the wrong instrument.

[S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md) therefore requires the non-AI alternative to be **designed, costed and rejected on the record** rather than gestured at. A rejection with no design behind it is not a rejection, and this programme has already taught that pattern under a different name at [S008](/wiki/seminars/S008.md).

## The best governance idea in the collection

**The scope test moved from effort to surface, and that is the change worth carrying.**

The older test asked, in effect, whether the team had built or fine-tuned a model. The newer one asks whether **an artefact capable of AI behaviour crossed the boundary into the customer's control**, such that they can execute it, test it or operationalise it.

That is precisely the redefinition agentic and low-code delivery force. When the deliverable is a configured agent rather than a trained model, an effort-based test lets the highest-velocity work escape governance entirely — the team did no model work, so nothing applies, and the customer is now running something that acts. **The newer test closes that hole cleanly** and it survives the next shift in how systems are assembled, because it asks about the boundary rather than about the technique.

**It then draws its line at authored artefacts, and excludes configuration using existing controls.** That is defensible on ownership grounds and indefensible on harm grounds, and the module says both. A carelessly configured off-the-shelf agent placed in a hiring workflow can do exactly the damage the regime exists to prevent, and under the newer test it is out of scope.

[S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md) requires the learner to write their own scope test in the artefact-transfer form and then **to name one case it wrongly excludes.** The named case is the assessed work. A test whose author cannot find a hole in it has not been read by its author.

## The requirement that is asserted everywhere and instrumented nowhere

**The regime insists the assessment is a living document.** Refresh at least annually. Refresh on every new intended use. Refresh before every release stage. Refresh at every milestone. A re-screen written into the team's own working cadence.

Against all of that: the signature block has one row shape and no mechanism for re-signing; the revision history carries no version number and no change description; the submission is one-shot with no expiry; and the completed assessment is filed in a document repository rather than in the system of record where the work lives.

**The per-sprint re-screen is the best-designed control in the collection and the least observable — no artefact, no checkbox, nowhere at all to record that it happened.** In practice the assessment is written once to clear a commercial gate and thereafter describes a system that no longer exists.

The module's response is not to add more insistence. **A living-document requirement with no version field, no re-sign-off row and no place to record the review is not a requirement**, and [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md) requires the learner to build the missing instrumentation for their own artefact and to state what it costs the team every time it fires. An instrument nobody will run is the same as no instrument, and the cost is the part that determines which one it becomes.

## The maturity signal, and the refusal it makes possible

**Adding a documented, approvable non-conformance state is the clearest maturity signal in the whole regime.**

A compliance regime with no legitimate way to say *we cannot comply yet* is a regime that gets quietly ignored. People do not stop shipping; they stop recording. **Making deviation visible rather than merely absent is what converts an aspiration into a control**, and it is the same move [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) made for engagements — a refusal that can be stated is a refusal that can be reviewed.

[S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md) is built on this. Sensitive use is not interesting as a category; it is interesting because identifying one **forces a decision that somebody has to make and record**, and the available decisions include proceeding with a stated deviation, changing the design, and declining.

The corpus supplies exactly one worked refusal, and it is the most trustworthy content in the collection: replacing human reviewers with AI in a legal-review process was judged irresponsible, because those reviewers catch issues with legal consequences, and the customer's own proposed solution was declined on those grounds. **It is the only place in the entire collection where the answer to a customer request is no**, and the module uses it as the reference case rather than inventing a cleaner one.

## What was dropped between the generations, and why that is the finding

Three things went, and the dropping is more informative than anything that stayed.

**The genre changed from statute to routing table.** The older document contained the actual clause-by-clause duties; the newer one points at a standard held elsewhere. That is better for currency and worse for self-containment, and **a team reading only the current material learns process rather than duty** — which is precisely the population that most needs the duty.

**A candid admission disappeared.** The older document conceded that no compliance timeline had been established and explained why its goal set had been reduced. Those admissions are gone and no replacement timeline appears.

**The confidence went up and the enforcement did not.** That sentence is the module's summary of the comparison and it generalises well past this regime.

## Where the module is strongest

**One part of the assessment template is directly reusable and the module says so plainly**, because most of the criticism above is structural and this is not.

A handful of its fields force a claim to become falsifiable: a named measurement, a stated threshold, a verification method, and an accountable owner. Those four turn *the system will be monitored for bias* into something that can be true or false, and they are the same discipline [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) installed for requirements and [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md) installed for non-functional ones. **The rest of the template is form-filling and the module treats it as such.**

## Why the module sits here

**It needs [M11](/wiki/modules/M11.md) in front of it.** A harm assessment is written about a system, and a learner who cannot yet state what the components are, where the trust boundaries fall and which flows carry which data has nothing to write it against. The system model from [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) is the input.

**It closes the stage into [Stage 7](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md)** by producing the only governance artefact this programme regards as legitimate: **one that generates work rather than permission.**

## Load profile

The module's intrinsic load is low and its interpersonal load is the highest in the stage.

[S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md) requires a learner to argue against the premise of their own engagement in front of people who are paid to deliver it. [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md) requires them to name a decision and a decision-maker, which makes somebody responsible who would rather not be. **Neither is a technical difficulty and both are the reason governance artefacts are usually written by somebody who was not in the room.**

[S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md)'s load is different again: defending an assessment on the work it generated, in front of an assessor who will read a clean assessment as a failure.

## What the module hands forward

**A governance artefact that produced no work has governed nothing.**
