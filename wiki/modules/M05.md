# M05 · From Problem to Requirement

**Quarter:** [Q3](/wiki/quarters/Q3.md) · **Sessions:** [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md)–[S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) · **Complexity class:** 2
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** one in every session — Microsoft Agent Framework evaluations and test sets at [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md) and [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md), the Azure API Management AI gateway as the enforcement point for non-functional requirements at [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md), an agent configuration corpus read at a pinned commit at [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md), and the Foundry prompt-against-hosted build path at [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md)

## The central difficulty

A requirement is the point at which a discovery either survives or evaporates, and the evaporation is almost never noticed.

What survives is whatever somebody can be held to. Everything else — the texture of the observation, the contradiction between two informants, the thing the sponsor said once and did not repeat — passes into the requirement document as an adjective and dies there. **"The system must be responsive." "Reporting should be timely." "The solution needs to be secure."** Nobody disagrees with any of these, and that is the diagnosis rather than the achievement: a sentence nobody can disagree with is a sentence nobody can fail.

The module's whole content is the difference between that and a commitment. **A commitment names what would show it had been missed, who would notice, and how.** That is harder to write, unpleasant to write, and it is the only writing in this stage that anybody downstream can use.

There is a second failure running alongside, and [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) already named its parent: the requirement that is the solution the customer arrived with, restated in the numbered-list voice. It is the commonest single defect in requirement documents and it is invisible in the document itself, because a restated solution reads exactly like a requirement. **It is only visible against the discovery it was supposed to come from**, which is why this module sits immediately after [Stage 2](/wiki/quarters/Q2.md) and not anywhere else.

## What the source is unusually good at

The openly published engineering playbook this module rests on is the most specific material in this programme's whole source set, and its specificity is of a particular kind: **it says what fields an artefact has.**

That matters more than it sounds. Most engineering guidance is advice, and advice cannot be marked. A field list can. A document either has the field or it does not, and the argument moves from whether the team did a good job to whether the sentence in the field says anything.

Three specifications transfer directly and are taught as content.

**The requirement capture form, and the two fields that carry it.** The form has eight fields. Six of them are administrative — a name, a description, a priority, constraints, an owner, dependencies. **Two of them are the requirement: the measurement, and the verification method.** This module marks only those two, and the reason is the module's own thesis: a requirement that cannot name how it would be verified has not been written. The page also ships a large catalogue of example thresholds — response times, availability figures, coverage targets — **which carries no citation of any kind.** That catalogue is used here as an example bank and is explicitly forbidden as a benchmark, and [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md) is built around the difference. Page read 2026-07-30.

**The decision record, with a status vocabulary and a review state.** An ascending number and title, a date, a status drawn from a fixed set, a context that is required to be a value-neutral description of the forces at play, a decision sentence that begins with a commitment, and consequences. Two properties are worth more than the field list. The record is stored with the code rather than in a document repository, and **it enters review as a proposal by pull request rather than arriving already decided** — which makes the reasoning reviewable at the moment reviewing it could still change something. Page read 2026-07-30.

**The value-neutral context requirement is the sharpest single instruction in the module.** A context section written so that a reader cannot tell which way the decision went is a genuinely difficult thing to produce, is mechanically checkable by handing the section to somebody, and catches the most common defect in written engineering judgement — reasoning composed after the conclusion. [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md) marks against exactly that and against nothing else.

## What the source cannot help with, and is taught as material

The playbook is a serious document that fails its own standards in visible places, and **the failures are more useful to a learner than the successes**, because every engineer will meet an internal standard shaped exactly like this.

**Its status vocabulary is never fully exercised.** Neither of its own worked decision records ever reaches the superseded state. A status nobody uses is decoration, and [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md) therefore requires one record in the set to be superseded by another that names it.

**Its threshold catalogue is unsourced and reads exactly like its best-argued pages.** The register is identical; only the bibliography differs, and the bibliography is not on the page. [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md) hands learners the catalogue and requires every number they adopt from it to be justified independently or declared as borrowed.

**One of its own sections has no index page at all**, while the pages beneath it remain reachable. A reader navigating by menu concludes the enumeration is smaller than it is. [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md) uses that as its closing exercise: find the category the enumeration does not cover, and add it.

**It contradicts itself in more than a dozen places and never notices.** This is the normal residue of a document written by many hands over several years without a reconciliation pass. It is handed to learners as material rather than hidden, on the same reasoning [M03](/wiki/modules/M03.md) used for the contradiction it found in the internal corpus: **a standard that has stopped reading itself is a thing you must be able to recognise, and recognising it is transferable to every standard a learner will later be handed.**

## Why the module sits here

**It requires [Stage 2](/wiki/quarters/Q2.md) and supplies [M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md).**

[S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) produced a discovery packet that separates observation from report from inference, records an absence, and routes uncertainties a conversation cannot settle. **Every one of those properties is a precondition for writing a requirement that is not a restated solution**, and a learner who arrives here without them will write requirements from the brief rather than from the discovery, which is the failure the module exists to prevent.

Downstream, [M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md) cannot start until there is something to hold against the constraints. A constraint is only interesting in relation to a commitment it forecloses, and until the commitments are written the constraint conversation is abstract.

The module also supplies the vocabulary discipline the rest of the programme assumes. The requirement is written in the customer's own language, sharpened — not in the engineer's, and not in the customer's unaltered, because specialist jargon carries ambiguities and contradictions that a requirement cannot afford. **The customer keeps a veto over the sharpening.** That pairing is taken from the domain-modelling literature and is the reason [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md) requires a glossary with at least one recorded disagreement about a word.

## Load profile

Low at [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md), rising sharply at [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md), highest at [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md).

[S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) is the module's hinge and the load is intrinsic rather than affective: writing a verification method requires knowing what instrument exists, who owns it and when it runs, and most learners discover at that moment that their requirement was a preference. [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md) is heavier still because the properties in question were never volunteered by anybody and must be produced from an enumeration rather than from a conversation.

[S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md) is deliberately lighter, and sits fourth so that the assessment at [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) follows a session the cohort found manageable rather than one that exhausted them.

## What the module hands forward

One sentence, and [M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md) and everything after it assume it has landed:

**A requirement that cannot name how it would be verified has not been written.**

It is stated about business outcomes at [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md), about system properties at [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md), and about constraints at [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md), where it becomes: a constraint that cannot name who would have to agree to move it has not been established. **The same claim, three times, at three scopes.**
