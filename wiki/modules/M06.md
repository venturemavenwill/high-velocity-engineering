# M06 · Evidence, Error and Measurement

**Quarter:** [Q3](wiki/quarters/Q3.md) · **Seminar days:** [S026](wiki/seminars/S026.md)–[S030](wiki/seminars/S030.md) · **Complexity class:** 2
**Strands:** ME · MS · SY · PE
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Foundry evaluators — local and cloud · Azure AI Search knowledge index · **MCP servers:** Microsoft Learn MCP Server · Azure MCP Server (read)

---

## The central difficulty

Students treat measurement as reading. You want to know something, you measure it, and the number is the answer. Under that model a measurement can be *wrong* — the apparatus failed, the calculation slipped — but it cannot be *about the wrong thing*, and it cannot license less than it appears to.

Both failures are the normal case. A measurement is an instrument applied to a question, and there are two joints where it can fail before any arithmetic occurs: the instrument may not measure the construct you named, and the sample may not support the claim you make. Neither failure produces a wrong number. Both produce a right number about something else.

The module installs the stance the entire measurement strand rests on: **a number is evidence for a specific claim under specific conditions, and stating which claim and which conditions is part of reporting it.**

## The instrument this module characterises

Every day of this module runs against **Microsoft Foundry's built-in evaluators**, and the choice is not decoration. They are the instrument the rest of the programme will use to decide whether an AI system is good enough to ship, and they have the two properties this module exists to make visible.

**They are named after constructs and they score something narrower.** An evaluator called `relevance` scores how well a response addresses a query. It does not score whether the response is true. A fluent, on-topic fabrication passes it. That is not a defect in the evaluator — it is doing exactly what its documentation says — and it is the construct-instrument gap in a form students can run and watch.

**Their agreement with human judgement is published nowhere.** Microsoft documents scales, thresholds, default behaviour, sampling rates and hourly limits with precision. It publishes no figure for how often any evaluator agrees with a competent human on the same output. Microsoft's own AI playbook states that LLM-based evaluation *has not yet been systematically studied* and lists position, verbosity and self-enhancement bias among its known reliability problems. **Every judged score in this stack is therefore uncalibrated, and students are taught to say so in the sentence that reports it.**

This is the strongest available real-world instance of the module's entire argument, and it is why the module is anchored here rather than on a laboratory apparatus. A student who can state that a `groundedness` score of 4 is an uncalibrated ordinal judgement binarised at a threshold nobody chose has understood construct validity in the only register that will matter to them professionally.

## Copilot is the module's second instrument

The [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) maps three of this module's days onto the students' own primary tool, and the module carries that commitment rather than referring to it.

| Module content | Applied to Copilot |
|---|---|
| [S026](wiki/seminars/S026.md) construct versus instrument | What you asked for versus what the agent optimised toward |
| [S027](wiki/seminars/S027.md) four error sources | The agent's systematic component does not average out over more prompts |
| [S029](wiki/seminars/S029.md) validating an instrument | A prompt that has never failed has never been tested |

The third is the sharpest. A prompt file is a procedure whose output varies, whose behaviour drifts when the model behind it changes, and which almost nobody validates. **A prompt file with no review date is S029's unvalidated instrument in a new costume**, and the module says so at the point where the student has just built one.

## Why this module sits here

**It pays a five-whitepaper debt.** [WP-011](wiki/whitepapers/WP-011.md), [WP-012](wiki/whitepapers/WP-012.md), [WP-016](wiki/whitepapers/WP-016.md), [WP-018](wiki/whitepapers/WP-018.md) and [WP-019](wiki/whitepapers/WP-019.md) each recorded that Q2 required measurement practice ahead of its justification, and [WP-019](wiki/whitepapers/WP-019.md) §8 concluded the sequencing was a curriculum-level error rather than a series of local compromises. S026 opens by revisiting those measurements explicitly.

**It mirrors M05.** M05 taught the boundary of what can be computed; M06 teaches the boundary of what can be established. The two are deliberately consecutive and the parallel is drawn at S026.

**It carries the programme's first entrustment judgement,** at S030, and everything before it is preparation for that judgement being defensible.

## Task-class variability plan

The module varies **the joint at which the measurement fails**, so that failure is understood as located rather than general.

- **S026** varies the construct-instrument gap: same evaluator, several named constructs, and it measures at most one.
- **S027** varies the dominant error source: same evaluation, several conditions, and a different source dominates each.
- **S028** varies the sample: same evaluator, several sampling schemes, and different claims are supported.
- **S029** varies the question: same system, several questions, and each demands a different instrument.
- **S030** varies nothing. It is the entrustment occasion.

**Every day's phase 3 is executed rather than read**, against a real Foundry project, and every day's phase 5 ships something. Two cases across the module cannot be operated — continuous evaluation and trace-based intelligent sampling are both preview capabilities requiring production traffic and an Application Insights connection the programme does not hold for a student project. Those two are **demonstrated rather than operated**, and neither is assessed.

## Load profile

High throughout, peaking at S028.

S028 is the module's hardest day because inferential reasoning is genuinely counter-intuitive, the arithmetic is unfamiliar, and the day's conclusions are almost all negative — a sample supports less than students expect, in every direction. It is placed third with S027 and S029 as buttresses rather than in a trough, because the module cannot afford to defer it.

The module's standing load reduction is that **all five days measure the same substrate property**, chosen so that it is genuinely uncertain, matters operationally, and has never been measured. Students meet the question at S026 and are defending an answer to it at S030.

## Module-level predicted failure modes

**Construct validity learned as a phrase.** The module's most likely failure. Countermeasure: every day's artefact requires the construct and the instrument to be stated separately, and S026's specification fails any artefact where they are the same sentence.

**Error treated as noise.** Students absorb that measurements vary and treat all variation as random. Countermeasure: S027's phase 3 supplies four conditions in which a different systematic source dominates, and random error is the smallest term in three of them.

**Intervals produced without meaning.** S028's risk: the arithmetic is learnable and the interpretation is not. Countermeasure: the day's artefact requires a sentence stating what the interval does and does not license, judged by whether a non-specialist reader draws the intended conclusion.

**The platform instance mistaken for the durable claim.** A student who leaves believing that *Foundry evaluators are uncalibrated* has learned a fact with a shelf life. The claim is that **a judged score whose agreement with human judgement is unpublished is uncalibrated, whoever publishes it** — and that if Microsoft publishes agreement data next year, the durable claim is unchanged and this module re-anchors. Countermeasure: every day carries a perishability register separating the two, and the specifications assess the durable column.

**The entrustment judgement anchored on the artefact rather than the reasoning.** S030's risk, and an assessor-side one. Countermeasure: the supervision level must be substantiated by a narrative naming a specific observed action, per the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md), and a level assigned without one is returned to the assessor.

## Assessment contribution

**The programme's first entrustment judgement, at S030.**

The entrustable activity is bounded: **conduct a measurement of a stated quantity and report what it does and does not establish.** It is not one of the three terminal activities; it is a precursor to EA-1 and EA-3. The expected level at this point is 2a.

Specification items:

- **S026** — a construct and an instrument stated separately, with the gap between them characterised.
- **S027** — an error budget with the dominant source identified and its mechanism named.
- **S028** — a claim, its interval, and a sentence stating what it does not license.
- **S029** — an instrument designed for a stated question, with what it cannot detect.
- **S030** — the measurement, its result, its limitations, and the oral defence.

**Every specification item from S026 to S029 additionally carries three elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md): the **prompt record** — what you asked the agent for, in order, including what you abandoned; the **intervention record** — every correction you made, which **fails with no entries** unless the artefact demonstrates the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error; and the **frontier statement** — one thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you caught. S030 does not add a build; it defends S029's, and the three records are evidence in that defence.

Dispositions observed: **humility** throughout, and **integrity** at S030 in whether the limitations stated are the real ones.
