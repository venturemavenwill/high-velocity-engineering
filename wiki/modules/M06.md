# M06 · The Frozen and the Fluid

**Quarter:** [Q3](/wiki/quarters/Q3.md) · **Sessions:** [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md)–[S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) · **Complexity class:** 3
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** one in every session — Microsoft Purview DSPM for AI at [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md), MCP and OpenAPI composition at [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md), Microsoft Foundry basic-against-standard setup at [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md), the three-way build-path trade study at [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md), and an architecture decision record whose rejected option the agent is made to argue at [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md)

## The central difficulty

Nobody designs on open ground. Every design a forward-deployed engineer produces sits inside decisions somebody else took, usually before the engineer arrived, often for reasons nobody present can reconstruct.

The two ways of getting this wrong are opposite, and most practitioners have only ever been warned about one of them.

**The first is treating everything as negotiable.** It produces the design that is correct and unavailable — an architecture that would work if the data could leave the customer's estate, if the platform team would take a dependency, if the contract signed last year said something else. It is the failure engineers are warned about, it is embarrassing in public, and it is comparatively rare.

**The second is treating nothing as negotiable, and it is the common one.** The constraint list arrives, nobody asks who wrote it, and the design shrinks to fit. The engineer's own judgement disappears into somebody else's decisions and the eventual disappointment is attributed to the constraints, which is convenient, unfalsifiable and usually partly true. **This is the failure the module is built against**, and the rule it takes from its source material is a rule against exactly it.

## The idea the module is built on, and what is wrong with it

The internal corpus behind this module proposes classifying every element of a problem space by how much it can move — **a frozen-to-fluid axis** — and attaches a rule: even in a space that is almost entirely frozen there is always something that can be changed, and constraint is not an excuse for doing nothing.

**This is the most transferable original idea in that material, and it is entirely unevidenced.**

That statement is precise and every session in the module repeats it. There is no worked instance in the corpus of a problem space actually classified. There is no engagement in which the classification changed a decision. There is no record of the classification being wrong, and no account of what it costs to produce. The idea is stated as a method and then never used in anything that was read. **The corpus contains no reference, footnote, study or citation of any kind anywhere in it**, so there is nothing behind the assertion but the assertion.

The rule attached to it is in the same condition, and worse: **the same body of material, elsewhere, explains disappointing delivery results by pointing at the customer's own organisational constraints.** A practice whose method says constraint is not an excuse, and whose prepared answers to objections use constraint as the explanation, has not reconciled itself. The module hands that contradiction to the learner at [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md) rather than resolving it, because it cannot be resolved from the source and because noticing it is the more useful skill.

**It is taught anyway, and the reason is stated plainly: it is cheap to test and it names a failure mode nothing else in the programme names.** A learner who classifies a constraint list and finds that a third of it was assumed rather than stated has spent an afternoon and learned something checkable. That is a good enough reason to teach a method. It is not a reason to believe it works.

## Where the module borrows warrant, and what that warrant covers

Because its central idea has none, the module borrows structure from two sources that are better evidenced without being evidence of anything about this practice.

**The domain-modelling literature supplies the boundary vocabulary.** A model applies inside an explicit boundary; a map of those boundaries must describe reality first, including its political contours, and must not be redrawn until reality has changed; conforming to somebody else's good model is a real option that is refused more often than it should be, for emotional rather than technical reasons; a translating layer between two models is genuine engineering and is expensive, so the wall must be costed before it is built; and not integrating at all is frequently the right answer. [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md) is built on these directly.

That source's evidence is **named, followed-up personal experience across identified projects, including two that failed and one where the author's own design was replaced.** It makes no quantitative claim of any kind, and it declares two prerequisites — iterative development, and a continuing close relationship with the people who understand the domain — without which it claims nothing at all. Both facts are carried forward into every session that uses it.

**The negotiation literature supplies the vocabulary for moving a constraint.** A stated constraint is a position; the need behind it is an interest, and the interest can often be met another way. Before the conversation, know what you will do if it fails, and set a threshold that forces you to stop and re-plan rather than drift into a bad commitment. Argue from standards independent of either side's will rather than from will. Make the decision easy for the person who has to justify it to people who were not in the room.

That source is case-based and its authors state plainly that it establishes no effect and that its examples illustrate mechanism rather than isolate cause. **No claim in this module about whether a constraint conversation is likely to succeed is warranted by it, and none is made.**

## The specification the module ends on

[S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) uses the open playbook's design-review templates, which exist at four scopes: a task, a feature or story, a milestone or epic, and a technical spike. Each has an explicit heading list, and each is marked as in progress while it is in progress.

**The escalation from story scope to milestone scope adds exactly one heading, and that heading concerns operation.** That is a precise, teachable and falsifiable claim about what changes when a design leaves the scope of a single piece of work: **it acquires a runtime owner.** It is the sharpest thing the playbook says about design scope, it can be checked against any design document in minutes, and it is one organisation's convention rather than a finding. Pages read 2026-07-30.

The assessment at [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) therefore marks the **choice** of scope and its defence, not the design. That is what makes the session class 3: nobody supplies the standard, the learner selects it, and selecting it wrongly is the failure.

## Why the module sits here

**It requires [M05](/wiki/modules/M05.md), and it supplies [Stage 4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md).**

A constraint is only interesting against a commitment it forecloses. Run before [M05](/wiki/modules/M05.md), the constraint register is an inventory of facts about the customer's estate; run after it, it is a list of things that make specific written requirements unbuildable, which is a different and much shorter list.

Downstream, everything this module produces is an input to proving feasibility. The unavailable design from [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md) is the thing a spike would have to justify unfreezing; the boundary decisions from [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md) are where integration risk actually lives; and the trade study from [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md) is the artefact whose per-option experiments are the experiments a proof stage would run.

The module also closes a loop opened in [Stage 1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md). [S006](/wiki/seminars/S006.md) asked what constraints you cannot see yet; [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) asks which of the constraints you can now see you were ever actually told.

## Load profile

Moderate at [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) and [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md), highest at [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md), evaluative at [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md).

[S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md) is the module's difficult session and the load is affective. Writing down the design you are not allowed to build, in enough detail that somebody could build it, and then starting on something smaller without sulking, is uncomfortable in a way the other sessions are not. **It is placed third so that the register and the boundary decisions are available to make the argument concrete, and so that [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md) immediately supplies a mechanism — a comparison with real experiments — for making the case the session asks a learner to make.**

## What the module hands forward

Two sentences.

**A constraint with no named authority is not a constraint; it is a rumour.** That is the operational half, it is checkable, and it is what [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) marks.

**Constraint is not an excuse, and this claim has no evidence behind it whatsoever.** That is the disposition half. It is carried forward as a stated position rather than as a finding, and every session that uses it says so in its own words.
