# M15 · Handover as an Engineered Act

**Quarter:** [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md) · **Sessions:** S071–S075 · **Complexity class:** 5
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** one in every session, and what is anchored is the versioned artefact rather than the conversation that produced it — an agent configuration corpus read at a pinned commit at [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md), APIM AI gateway policies and Foundry deployment configuration at [S072](/wiki/seminars/S072.md), Microsoft Agent Framework evaluations at [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md), Microsoft Purview DSPM for AI at [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md), and the whole operate stack handed to a named individual at [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md)
**Assessment:** [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md) ⊘

## The module's central difficulty

Learners arrive able to finish and unable to leave.

Finishing is familiar: the work meets its criteria, the increment ships, the incident count is acceptable, and the team's attention moves. Leaving is a separate act with its own artefacts, its own failure modes and its own evidence, and almost nobody has been made to do it deliberately. The default is departure — the team stops appearing, and whatever it built becomes somebody's problem by attrition.

**The difficulty is not that learners think handover is unimportant.** They agree at once, and the agreement is worthless, because handover is scheduled last and everything scheduled last is cut when the date holds and the work does not.

The module's answer is to make handover an object with a shape: a manifest, an owner per line, an acceptance condition per line, a trigger bound to a register rather than to somebody's intention, and an artefact left behind by every step. **A handover that produces no artefact cannot be shown to have happened**, which is exactly the defect the source corpus has: it mechanises the trigger for teardown and requires no evidence that the teardown occurred.

## The five sessions

| Session | Title | What the learner does | What the learner produces |
|---|---|---|---|
| [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md) | What actually transfers | Enumerates the transfer object line by line and finds the line nobody will take | A transfer manifest with an unaccepted item on it |
| [S072](/wiki/seminars/S072.md) | Runbooks, and the operator who is not you | Watches somebody else execute their runbook while not being allowed to speak | An executed runbook with the execution record attached |
| [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) | The evaluation harness they inherit | Transfers not the harness but the authority to act on its output | A harness handover record with threshold provenance |
| [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) | Estate hygiene, triggered by a register | Binds withdrawal to a state change somebody else can observe | A withdrawal trigger specification with evidence per step |
| [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md) ⊘ | A handover with a named receiving owner | Assembles the four and defends the transfer against a recipient who does not want it | A defended handover |

The sequence goes from **what** to **who**. S071 fixes the object. S072 and S073 take the two items most likely to be orphaned — the operating procedure and the instrument that says whether the system still works — and gives each a receiver. S074 supplies the mechanism that fires without anyone remembering. S075 puts a name against every line and has that name argued with.

## Why the module is class 5

The task is not specified and the method is not given. The learner is handed a running system, a fixed end date, a set of receivers with mismatched authority and appetite, and no template for what a handover contains.

**Two things in particular are left to the learner and are the reason for the class.** Deciding what a receiver must be able to do unaided is a judgement with no correct answer available in advance. And deciding what to do with an item nobody will accept — transfer it anyway, remove the capability it serves, or record it as unaccepted and name the consequence — is a genuine choice between defensible options with different costs.

## Assessment contribution

**One assessment occasion, [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md), with a closed specification.** It contributes evidence towards the entrustment decision at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) but is not itself one.

The specification checks the presence and the checkability of things: an owner per item, an acceptance condition a recipient could evaluate without the learner present, a trigger stated as a register state change, an evidence artefact per teardown step, a taper with an end date on it. **It does not check whether the handover would have succeeded**, because nothing available to the programme could establish that.

## Predicted failure modes for the module as a whole

- **Repository access mistaken for transfer.** The learner grants permissions and considers the object moved. *Countermeasure:* S071 requires an acceptance condition per line, and a permission grant satisfies none of them. **The countermeasure works on the artefact and not on the belief**, and learners who hold the belief will produce a conforming manifest anyway.
- **Documentation substitution.** Every unaccepted item acquires a document instead of an owner. *Countermeasure:* S072 makes a non-author execute the document while the author stays silent, which is the cheapest available demonstration that a document is not a receiver.
- **The tidy teardown.** The learner produces an excellent hygiene plan and no transfer at all — which is precisely what the source does, and it will read as competence. *Countermeasure:* S074 teaches the mechanism and then names the absence in the same session, and S075's specification puts the transfer items ahead of the teardown items.
- **The willing recipient.** The learner selects the receiver who is enthusiastic rather than the one with authority, and the handover is accepted warmly by somebody who cannot act on it. *Countermeasure:* the supplied situation in [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md) is built so that willingness and authority sit in different people, and S075's challenger is instructed to be the one with authority.
- **The provenance gap that is invisible to its author.** Thresholds, selection rules and routing decisions made during delivery are transferred as values with no reasons, because the person transferring them still remembers the reasons and cannot see that the memory is not in the artefact. *Countermeasure:* S073 requires a provenance line per threshold and treats "set during tuning" as a failed line.

## The evidence position

**Split, and the split is the module's teaching point.**

The withdrawal mechanism at S074 is **documented practice from a customer-embedded engineering organisation within a large technology vendor**: it is reported as what that organisation specifies, with no claim that it improves any outcome, because the corpus reports no outcome. The register-triggered trigger, the two-party revocation and the cross-boundary credential rotation on departure all sit in that class.

Everything the module adds — the manifest, the acceptance condition, the named receiving owner, the evidence requirement per teardown step, the taper with an end — is **design reasoning with no external warrant**, constructed from what the source omits.

**Nothing in this module rests on a measured effect, and no source behind it reports whether a handover of any kind made a delivered system survive.**
