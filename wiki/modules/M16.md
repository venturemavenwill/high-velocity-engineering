## The central difficulty

The adversary and the accident are the same engineering problem approached from two directions, and the programme has spent two years approaching it from one of them.

Everything the students hold about failure was built on the assumption that failure is sampled. [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md) decomposed error into sources that behave like distributions. [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md) put intervals around quantities on the assumption that the next observation resembles the last. [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) broke a working system by constructing conditions, and the conditions were constructed to be representative. [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) measured a gap between two populations. [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) designed detection for failures that start. Every one of those is correct and every one of them assumes nobody is choosing.

**An adversary is an accident with a chooser, and an accident is an adversary with no motive.** The consequence is not that the students must learn a second discipline. It is that the two problems diverge in exactly one place and coincide everywhere else, and the students will assume the reverse.

Where they diverge is the tail. [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md)'s phase 2 bridge already gave the room the sentence: an attacker chooses the worst case rather than sampling from it. That makes every probability estimate the programme has taught unavailable in the adversarial setting, because a probability is a statement about a distribution and an adversary is not one. It follows that the *defences* differ, the *forecasts* differ, and the acceptance decisions of [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) point 6 rest on a quantity that cannot be estimated the same way.

Where they coincide is the evidence. **An attack and an accident produce the same record**, and they produce it through the same components, at the same resolution, in the same log. The thing that separates them is not visible in the trace; it is provenance and population, and both are recording decisions made before anything happened. A student who can tell them apart afterwards can do so because somebody decided, in advance, to write down a quantity that had no operational use at the time.

That is the module's payload and it lands on the second day, in a phase 3 case that is not an attack at all.

The consequence for the module's own subject is exact. **The detection design the student built at [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) is the only sensor their security posture has, and it was tuned on a distribution the adversary is not drawing from.** Every threshold the cohort set was set against observed variation; an adversary reads the threshold as a budget. This is why drift, alerting and forensics belong in a security module and not in an operations one, and it is why [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) and [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) sit inside M16 rather than inside [M14](/wiki/modules/M14.md).

## What class 6 means here

At class 6 the student decides **what to hand over, to whom, with what warranty.** This is the programme's first class-6 module and the transition is the sharpest of the five, because class 5 asked a question the student could answer alone and class 6 does not.

The five days each produce a handover, and each one is a document that will be acted on by somebody who was not present and cannot ask a follow-up question.

- [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) hands over **a content boundary** — which sources this component treats as instruction, which as data, which component enforces the distinction, and what happens when it is bypassed. The receiver is whoever is on call when the component does something odd.
- [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) hands over **an exposure statement** — what an adversary would have to do to the function, the answer channel and the capacity, what it would cost them, and what the system records. The receiver is the staff member accountable for the serving path.
- [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md) hands over **an authorisation position** — who can see what, where the check runs, and what is said about what was withheld. The receiver is the substrate's data owner.
- [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) hands over **an alerting contract** — a promise to a named person about what they will be told, when, and what they are expected to do. The receiver is the on-call rota.
- [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) hands over **a forensic account and three recommendations with owners**, to an implementer who was not in the incident. This is the module's entrustment occasion and the programme's first rehearsal of **EA-2**.

**The warranty is the part students will not supply unless it is demanded.** A boundary statement with no failure behaviour, an exposure statement with no acceptance list, an alerting contract with no latency, a recommendation with no owner — each is a handover with the warranty removed, and each is a named failure condition on its day. The class-6 discrimination is not whether the student can produce the artefact. It is whether they will state what they are standing behind when it is wrong.

## What the platform makes exact

A security module can be taught entirely in the abstract, and the result is a student who can recite attack names. This one is run against named services, and the reason is that **the abstractions this module teaches have documented instances in which the abstraction is not a metaphor but the thing itself.**

Four are load-bearing and each anchors a day.

**Document-level authorisation at retrieval time is a real four-way choice, and the documentation names the trade.** [Azure AI Search](/research/06-microsoft-platform/azure-ai-search-and-acl-patterns.md) supports four approaches to enforcing per-document permissions. **The generally available one is the one the documentation itself calls a workaround** — a string comparison whose trust boundary sits in the application rather than in the service — and the three that move the boundary into the service are in preview. Enforcement runs against **permission metadata already stored in the index rather than against live source permissions**, so a revocation does not bite until a synchronisation happens, and the documentation states that a timing lag occurs and gives no figure for it. And **chunking silently discards permissions unless they are projected onto every chunk**, which is a failure a student can produce, observe and then close. [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md) is built on all three facts.

**Indirect prompt injection has a named product surface whose defaults are visible.** Content Safety's Prompt Shields separate a *user prompt* shield from a *document* shield, which encodes the vendor's own belief that provenance is knowable — and the assembler is what decides which content is which. **Spotlighting, the sub-feature that marks retrieved content as less trustworthy, is off by default.** [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) runs on that.

**The wallet attack is in a vendor's published alert catalogue.** Defender for Cloud names two of them, plus ASCII-smuggling indirect injection, jailbreak attempts blocked and jailbreak attempts detected-but-not-blocked, anomalous tool invocation and LLM reconnaissance. **The catalogue publishes names and severities and no detection rate and no false-positive rate for any of them**, which is the exact silence [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) and [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) teach students to notice and to say out loud.

**Monitoring is sampled and says so.** Foundry's continuous evaluation runs on sampled agent responses at a rate you set, skips runs when an hourly limit is reached, and publishes guidance thresholds nobody in the student's organisation decided. [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md)'s subject is a signal that stops, and the platform supplies three documented ways for one to stop while the dashboard stays green.

**Two rules govern all of it and are stated to the cohort at [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md).**

**Nothing adversarial runs against live users.** Every attack in this module is executed against a disposable copy of the student's own component, or inside an announced disturbance window, and never against the production path. The [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page's disclosure covers deliberate disturbance during announced windows and instrument adoption; **an adversarial demonstration causing the live service to do something its operator did not intend is not covered by it**, and [WP-076](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-076.md) §8 concedes what running on a copy costs. Where a capability requires a licence or a tenant privilege the programme does not hold — several Purview and Defender capabilities do — the day states that it is **demonstrated rather than operated** and the specification does not assess it.

**No vendor detection claim is taught as fact.** The [Microsoft AI Platform Map](/wiki/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md) prohibits asserting any Purview or Defender detection or false-positive rate, because none is published. In a security module that prohibition is not a caveat; **it is content**, and it is the reason [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) point 3 and [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) point 2 both require the student to say what the absence of an alert licenses them to conclude.

## What the module's late start costs it

**Security is not distributed throughout this programme and first appears at Q5.** The [Outcomes and Accreditation Map](/wiki/program/01-Outcomes-and-Accreditation-Map.md) records this against the ABET Criterion 5 requirement for exposure to security concerns throughout, in the programme's own words: *"Throughout" is the criterion's word and the programme does not fully meet it.* The [Module Map](/wiki/program/07-Module-Map.md) names the strand's six-module count as the design's thinnest area.

M16 is where the bill arrives, and it arrives in four specific forms rather than as a general weakness.

**The cohort has had one seminar day of adversarial reasoning.** [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) is thirty-two seminar days behind this module. It established that a threat is an actor with access and a goal, it supplied four attack families, and its own point 3 explicitly deferred attacks specific to generative components to [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md). The module therefore opens by discharging a deferral placed on it eight months earlier, to a room that has practised the underlying move once.

**Adversarial reasoning is not a habit and the module cannot assume it is one.** Every day in M16 opens with a phase 1 item that requires the student to name who could do something, because a module that assumes the actor requirement has been internalised will get hazards instead of threats. This is a real cost in phase 1 time on all five days and it is spent deliberately.

**SG301 and SG302 carry more than a concurrent course should.** [SG301](/wiki/program/06-Course-Catalogue.md) in Q7 supplies document-level authorisation at retrieval and permission-metadata staleness; SG302 supplies prompt injection, tool abuse, poisoning, extraction, exhaustion and the safety case. The seminar days recruit that material heavily, and where they do, the cross-course deferral rule of the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) applies in full: each day carries an artefact element requiring the student to state which course material they used. Three of the five days carry such an element.

**The module cannot teach what a two-year exposure would have produced.** A student who had met adversarial framing in Q2 would arrive here with the reflex of asking *who benefits* about every design decision, and M16 would be about judgement rather than about acquisition. It is about both, and the second is the reason the module is five days long for four subjects. This is stated to the cohort at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) phase 6 point 6 rather than concealed.

## Why this module sits here

**It requires [M15](/wiki/modules/M15.md) immediately before it.** An agent that acts is the object the module's attacks are aimed at. [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md) established what changes when a system can act, [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) established that a tool call crosses a boundary of trust, and [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) established that two of the four tool-failure shapes report success and are wrong. That last is the same finding [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) reaches from the adversarial side, and a cohort that has not met it will read the security material as paranoia.

**It requires [M14](/wiki/modules/M14.md), and specifically [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) and [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md).** [M14](/wiki/modules/M14.md)'s own page states the dependency in the other direction: *S079's drift and alerting material is S068 at a longer horizon with an adversary present.* The link is instrumented rather than asserted. [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) named this module's [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) under instrumentation rule 6 for two obligations — an alerting case in which the signal itself stopped, and the behaviour of the shipped changes against each student's stated detection commitment — and [WP-070](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §7 requires WP-079 §7 to report whether the elements exist. **That is the module's principal inbound link and it is the mechanism by which a promise made in week 70 acquires a measured latency in week 79.**

**It requires [M12](/wiki/modules/M12.md)'s shipped retrieval system.** [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5 opened a question and named two homes for its two halves. [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md) takes the authorisation half; [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) takes the other. A cohort that has not owned a corpus cannot be asked whose documents are findable.

**It requires [M13](/wiki/modules/M13.md)'s cost work.** [Q7](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md) states the dependency explicitly: *S077's resource-exhaustion material is an adversary deliberately moving the operating point, and it is unintelligible to a student who has not chosen one and paid for it.* [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md)'s separation of capacity, quota and budget is the whole apparatus of [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) point 4.

**It requires the substrate to have had real incidents.** The [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page lists *real incidents, not injected ones* as a requirement and names [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) and [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) as the days that depend on it, and its withdrawal commitment names both days among those that are not taught if the substrate cannot be delivered. **[S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) is the only entrustment occasion in the programme whose material cannot be constructed**, and the module says so rather than holding a simulation in reserve.

**It is not placed later** because [M17](/wiki/modules/M17.md) and [M18](/wiki/modules/M18.md) both consume it. [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) receives the corpus half of [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5 and the per-class results [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md) produces; [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) communicates a risk that [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) measured; [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md)'s safety case rests on the detection commitments and forensic accounts this module produces, and the [Seminar Day Index](/wiki/program/08-Seminar-Day-Index.md) records S087 as retrieving S076–S079 as a block.

## Task-class variability plan

At class 6 the student decides **what to hand over, to whom, with what warranty.** Variability across the five days is therefore in **who receives the handover and what they are able to do with it**, because holding the class constant while varying the receiver is what prevents the handover calcifying into one document shape.

- **S076** varies the write path: four pieces of content reaching one agent's context by four routes, **one of which originates inside the system and has no outsider in the loop at all.** All four are sent through a disposable copy of the student's own assembler and the agent's real behaviour is the adjudicator.
- **S077** varies the quantity under attack: three attacks on the function, the answer channel and the capacity, plus a fourth trace that **is not an attack and is indistinguishable from one at the resolution supplied.** Two of the three are run on the disposable copy; the third is bounded by arithmetic against a bill.
- **S078** varies where the check runs: four placements of one authorisation decision over one corpus, **one of which appears strictly safest and preserves the leak.** All four are configured and queried against a real index, and a fifth result — the unprojected chunk — is produced by the student rather than supplied.
- **S079** varies the failure of the signal: four alerting designs over one component's six-month record, **one of which is healthy for eleven weeks because the signal stopped.**
- **S080** varies nothing supplied. The incident is real, the record is whatever the substrate wrote, the on-call member who handled it is in the room, and the recommendations go to somebody who will implement them.

## Load profile

**Low technical, extreme evidential.** Nothing in this module is difficult to compute. A boundary statement is a list of sources, an exposure statement is three paragraphs, a change-point test is a library call, and a timeline is a table. A student who finds the module hard is not finding the material hard.

The module's distinctive load is **reasoning about a system whose record is itself in question.** Every prior module gave the student evidence and asked what it licensed. This module gives the student evidence, asks what it licenses, and then asks what the evidence would look like if somebody had chosen it — which is a second-order question the programme has posed once, at [S042](/wiki/seminars/S042.md), about the student's own model selection. Here it is posed about the logs.

Three interactivities are irreducible and are the reason the days are ordered as they are.

**Provenance, capability and detection interact and cannot be held separately.** What reached the system determines what it could have been made to do; what it was authorised to do determines the harm; and what was recorded determines whether either is knowable. [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) supplies the first, [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md) the second, [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) the third, and [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) requires all three at once. A student who meets them in a different order builds a control for a capability they have not bounded.

The standing load reduction is that **one component, carried since [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md), runs through all five days.** The student is not choosing a system; they are asking five questions about one they already operate, and four of the five artefacts are inputs to the fifth.

The second reduction is that **[S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) builds nothing.** The entrustment occasion is an account and a handover, not a construction, for the reason [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §3.1 gave and [WP-070](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §3.1 accepted unchanged: a judgement about reasoning under pressure is invalid if the student is simultaneously constructing the evidence.

## Module-level predicted failure modes

**Security read as a checklist.** The module's defining failure and the commonest professional one. A student who leaves with a list of attacks and a list of mitigations has acquired something that will be obsolete before they graduate and will not notice when it is. Countermeasure: **every day's phase 4 carries a strong-form scope statement naming the conditions under which its repertoire does not apply**, every specification checks a boundary rather than a control, and the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) entry for adversarial technique catalogues — *the attack classes are more stable than the specific exploits* — is read to the cohort at [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) phase 4 rather than left in a register.

**The adversary imagined as a genius.** Students overestimate adversarial sophistication and underestimate adversarial patience, which produces defences against the interesting attack and none against the cheap one. Countermeasure: **[S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) step 2 requires a cost to the adversary for every attack**, and [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md)'s actor D — the careless insider with no goal — is retrieved at [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) phase 2 specifically so that the highest-expected-harm actor in the room is the one with no intent at all.

**The accident read as an attack.** The module's own thesis turned into a defect. A cohort five days into adversarial framing will attribute every anomaly to an adversary, which is expensive, unfalsifiable and flattering. Countermeasure: **[S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s phase 3 payload is not an attack**, the misclassification rate is measured, and [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md)'s artefact requires the discriminating evidence for whichever answer the student gives — including *undecided*, which is a pass.

**Blameless read as consequence-free.** A student who concludes that a blameless review means nobody is accountable has learned the opposite of [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) and will produce reviews that change nothing. Countermeasure: **[S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) phase 6 point 2 states the distinction directly** — blameless is a claim about what a review is *for*, and a review that names a person changes one person's behaviour and no system's — and **every recommendation in the [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) artefact requires a named owner**, so that the occasion produces accountability in the only place it survives the person's departure.

**The naming reflex.** The specific behavioural form of the previous failure, and the one that will appear in the artefacts. Under time pressure, a mechanism is easiest to state as a sequence of things people did. Countermeasure: **[S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) item 4 requires every "X did Y" to be rewritten as "the system permitted Y, and here is what made Y reasonable at the time", and naming a person as the cause is a published failure condition stated before phase 1.**

**Security read as somebody else's strand.** The cost of the late start, expressed as a disposition. A student who treats M16 as the security module rather than as five questions about their own system will not carry the questions into [M17](/wiki/modules/M17.md) or [M18](/wiki/modules/M18.md). Countermeasure: **no day in this module supplies a system.** Every artefact is about the component the student has operated since [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md), and [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md)'s safety case is built from these five artefacts rather than from new material.

**The control the agent wrote.** The module's newest failure and the one its own method creates. Every day is executed in agent mode, and a security control is the artefact class on which an agent is most convincing and least checkable: a filter that reads correctly, a policy that deploys cleanly, and an authorisation check that enforces nothing. **The four tool-failure shapes [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) established apply to the agent as a tool, and two of them report success and are wrong.** Countermeasures: **every phase 5 artefact carries an intervention record**, which fails empty unless the artefact demonstrates the design could have caught an intervention-worthy error; **every phase 6 error clinic carries an agent case** in which a security control was produced confidently, passed review and did not hold; and the specifications check a boundary, a measurement or a stated non-coverage rather than the presence of a control, so a well-formed control the student cannot bound purchases nothing. The characteristic instance is at [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md), where the agent will produce the generally available string-filter approach — because it is the one with the most documentation and the most examples — and will not say that the documentation calls it a workaround.

## Assessment contribution

**One entrustment judgement, at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md)** — the quarter's only one, and the module's. Activity: *conduct a blameless forensic analysis of a real incident and report it.* Expected **2b–3a**. It is a precursor to **EA-2**, which is assessed at PE390 and CAP390, and it touches **EA-3**; it is neither.

Specification items:

- **S076** — a content-boundary statement naming which sources the component treats as instruction, which as data, the component that enforces the distinction and its failure behaviour; a write-path map including paths the student does not control; a demonstration on a disposable copy with a statement of what it does not establish; a capability bound naming one removed action; the recorded on-call narration; and the prompt, intervention and frontier records.
- **S077** — an exposure statement covering the function, the answer channel and the finite resource, each with the cheapest attack and its cost carrying provenance and confidence in the same field; a recording-gap list; one control shipped to the disposable copy with its explicit non-coverage and its measured effect; an exhaustion cost computed against the student's own [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) model and [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) commitment; an acceptance list with owners; the recorded accountable-owner narration; and the prompt, intervention and frontier records.
- **S078** — a principal model; a placement with the failure accepted from it and the three rejected; a per-class recall measurement naming the least-privileged class; a chunk-projection result, produced and closed on a disposable index; a measured revocation-to-effect latency in [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s four-part form; a capability bound for requests carrying untrusted content; a disclosure position with a named entitled owner or an explicit none; the data owner's answer to the phase 5 question; and the prompt, intervention and frontier records.
- **S079** — the [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) commitment set against what happened; a reference with its date and provenance in the same field; a statistical detector with what its parameter costs in the other error and the ME302 material used; a liveness property on every signal with its dead-signal latency; one recording change that would separate attack from accident, or an explicit none; **a discrimination for the [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) tool-failure shape the cohort declined, or a record that the design still does not carry one with the cost assigned an owner**; the alerting contract's human half including what happens if the recipient does not act; the recorded rota narration with the mute answer; and the prompt, intervention and frontier records.
- **S080** — the sourced timeline with inferences marked; the detection latency against the commitment in force; the attack-or-accident position with its discriminating evidence; the mechanism stated as system properties; the decisions with what made each reasonable; blast radius with its duration attributed; the disclosure entitlement; three recommendations with owners plus what is not recommended and why; and the three agent records, defended rather than built on.

Dispositions observed: **integrity** at [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) and [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), in whether the stated costs and the timeline's inferences match the student's own evidence rather than their conclusion; **humility** at [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md) and [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), where the honest answers are *the least-privileged user gets worse answers and cannot tell* and *the record does not decide*; **reliability** at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), where a commitment made in week 70 is checked against a measured latency and the student is answerable for the gap.

**Platform material in this module is the vehicle and never the ground.** Every day is executed against named services and **no design claim in this module rests on one.** The [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) requires the separation and the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) classifies adversarial technique catalogues for language and agent systems as perishable per delivery. The distinction is operational rather than rhetorical: **each day's perishability register writes its durable content as boundary reasoning and measurement obligations rather than as facts about named attacks or named products**, and each day's specification checks the durable column. A student who can state where their check runs, what it costs and to whom, and what its absence licenses has learned something that survives every service on this page being renamed.

Each whitepaper carries the fourth evidence class the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) requires — **grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded** — and in this module the silences are unusually load-bearing. The access-control documentation states a timing lag and no latency; the alert catalogue states names and severities and no detection rate; the red-teaming tooling defines a success rate and publishes no reference value for it. **A security module that filled those silences would be teaching the opposite of its own subject.**
