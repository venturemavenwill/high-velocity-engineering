# M11 · Threat Modelling as an Engineered Artefact

## The central difficulty

Threat modelling is usually taught as a workshop. People gather, a diagram appears on a wall, somebody prompts the room with a mnemonic, sticky notes accumulate, and a document is written afterwards by whoever volunteered. The output is a record of a conversation.

**This module teaches it as an engineered artefact instead**, and the difference is not stylistic. An engineered artefact has named parts, stable identifiers, explicit refusals, and a contract about what each stage may read and write. It can be re-run. It can be wrong in a locatable place. **A record of a conversation can only be disagreed with.**

The material behind the module is a working chained agent pipeline for threat modelling, built inside a customer-embedded engineering organisation within a large technology vendor. It is a good system. Its design decisions are the module's first three sessions and its failures are the last two, and the failures are worth more.

## The four design ideas the module carries forward

These are the transferable part. None of them requires anyone to accept a claim about efficacy, and none of them depends on the tooling that produced them.

**Front-load judgement, back-load mechanics.** The pipeline converts prose into stable identifiers as early as it possibly can. Dataflows acquire integers at the first stage and every subsequent stage refers to a flow only by its number. Threats and controls acquire prefixed identifiers as they are created. **By the join stage the reasoning problem has become a join over two identifier sets, and by the assembly stage it is set resolution and text splicing.** All the semantic difficulty is deliberately concentrated at the front, where a human is still looking.

This is the single most transferable idea in the artefact and it generalises far past security. It is also the reason the pipeline's own instructions insist that an error at the front of a chain is not a local error: it silently corrupts everything downstream, which makes front-stage formatting discipline a security property rather than a tidiness one.

**A folder used as a blackboard is a sufficient integration contract.** Each stage takes one folder path, reads named artefacts, writes exactly one named artefact, and is forbidden to touch anything else. Stages never call each other. A stage that finds a prerequisite missing returns a named error and says which earlier stage produces it. **The chain therefore cannot be run out of order by accident**, because the artefact-presence precondition genuinely fails — it is one of the few things in the whole design that can fail without the model's cooperation.

No orchestration framework is involved. [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) makes the point directly, because a cohort that has just finished [M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) will assume that a multi-agent system requires a graph.

**The compact index is a design artefact, not an optimisation.** Every rich output also emits a deliberately lossy projection of itself — a few columns per entry — so a downstream stage can perform a join without loading the full text. The subagents doing the mapping are given the projections and forbidden the originals.

**Imposing an information diet on a subagent is a design move.** That sentence is the module's most reusable single line, and it inverts the reflex a learner arrives with, which is that more context produces better output.

**Remove a judgement the model is bad at rather than prompting it to make it well.** Risk maps to work-item priority through a fixed table the agent is explicitly forbidden to adjust. That is better design than any amount of instruction about how to weigh severity, and it is the pattern to reach for whenever an agent is asked to be consistent about something it has no stable basis for.

## The rule that defines the artefact, and the cost that comes with it

**A threat survives only if it can be bound to at least one named component and at least one numbered flow.** Anything that cannot be is discarded, with the reason logged.

This is a genuinely good discipline. It is what stops a threat model becoming a list of things that are true of software in general, and it is what makes every entry actionable, because an entry that names a component and a flow tells an engineer where to go.

**It also removes, systematically, everything about the operator, the supply chain, human process and out-of-band administration** — each discarded with an entirely plausible logged justification that nobody reads. The binding rule and its blind spot are the same rule. You cannot keep the first without accepting the second, and the only honest response is to make the discard visible.

[S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) therefore requires two artefacts: the bound registry, and the register of what binding excluded. **The second is the assessed one.**

## The second rule, which is about language

**Generic mitigations are worse than absent ones, because they cannot be assessed, implemented or falsified.**

A plan that says *implement appropriate access controls* has produced a line that no engineer can act on, no reviewer can check and no status process can ever close — while occupying the space where a real mitigation would have gone and reading, to anyone scanning, as coverage. An absent mitigation at least leaves a hole somebody might notice.

Three related prohibitions ride with it and the module teaches all three.

**Hedging language is banned inside table cells.** Uncertainty is relocated to a separate section naming the principle that supports the inference, never qualified in place, because a hedge inside a cell destroys machine consumption downstream. This rule is correct and it is also the first step of the failure the module spends [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) on.

**Component overlap is not relevance.** A shared name proves nothing, and a control credited because it appears near a threat is not credited.

**A control that protects the wrong layer or the wrong direction must not be credited.** Channel against payload, ingress against egress, data plane against management plane — these are distinctions practitioners routinely collapse, and collapsing them produces a plan that looks covered and is not. **A credited control that a documented alternative path bypasses is not a control at all.**

## The best thing in it: closure re-derived rather than asserted

**A threat's status is not something a person ticks. It is re-derived from the workspace.**

The status stage searches source, pipeline definitions, container files, infrastructure definitions in several dialects, configuration and documentation for evidence that each mitigation exists, and a claim of *implemented* must cite a file and a location. Re-run it after a fix and it finds the evidence, flips the status, and nothing further is generated for that item.

**Evidence in the repository is the closure criterion, and evidence means a file path and a line rather than an assertion that something is secure.** Most organisations do not have this, and a learner who takes nothing else from the module should take this.

**Three caveats belong with it and are taught alongside it, never after it.** Nothing schedules the re-run. A fix that is real but invisible in the repository — a setting applied in a console, a policy applied at tenant level — will never close and will regenerate indefinitely. And the acceptance criteria a person might close a ticket against were written by the same model that wrote the mitigation.

The instruction governing the whole stage is the right one and it is the module's standing tie-break: **when in doubt, classify as unclear, because a false claim of implementation is strictly worse than a failure to notice a real one.**

## Then it goes wrong, and that is why the module exists

Three failures, in ascending order of how much a graduate will reproduce them.

**The scores measure documents, not systems.** One constraint inside the design says so plainly. The presentation contradicts it everywhere — a score, a named tier, severity colours, a findings table — and every one of those signals posture measurement. What is actually measured is prose conformance. **A team that learns the rubric can raise the score without touching the system**, and most of the checks reduce to whether some mitigation somewhere mentions a thing, so **naming a technology you have not deployed scores full marks.**

**Recall is unmeasured and the design declines to measure it.** The evaluator explicitly refuses to deduct for threats that are missing, listing them separately as unscored suggestions. That is a defensible choice — a scorer that penalises absence of the unknowable is unusable — and it also means **the score is silent on the pipeline's most consequential failure mode.** Precision, meanwhile, is elaborately instrumented: signal hierarchies, semantic gates, anti-junk-drawer rules, a stated preference for leaving a threat unmapped. **Nothing comparable guards against under-mapping, and an unmapped threat still appears in the plan and still reads as handled.**

**And the pipeline grades its own guess.** That is [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) and it is the sharpest thing in the programme.

## Two absences the module makes learners find rather than telling them

**There is no approval gate anywhere.** Human decisions exist as inputs — which control frameworks apply, whether recommendations are in scope, and the credential for the final write. One machine gate exists, and it refuses to proceed while any threat is unevaluated: a gate on a machine-written field, satisfied by an earlier stage having run, not by a person agreeing with anything. There are placeholders shaped like sign-off — a reviewer row with blanks for reviewer, date and disposition — but it is a form to fill in, not a precondition for anything. **Open questions are carried forward as prose from stage to stage, and every downstream precondition is whether the file exists and parses.** A system model full of unresolved ambiguity flows unimpeded into a plan, a status assessment and finally a team's live backlog, with nobody required to have read any of it. **The one place a person is unavoidably present is authenticating the write — and that person is being asked for a credential, not for a judgement.**

**There is no adversarial-input handling.** Every input stage reads untrusted repository content, feeds it to a model that writes files, and the chain terminates in an authenticated write to an external system. Nothing anywhere addresses content in the analysed repository attempting to influence the analysis. **For a pipeline whose entire subject is threat modelling, that is the most useful thing in it to make a learner notice**, and [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md) requires them to design both the attack and the control, having been told that the source contains neither.

## Why the module sits here

**It needs [M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) in front of it.** A learner who has not yet met comprehension debt, or written down what an agent did that they could not explain, has no reason to distrust a well-formatted output. This module's entire method is distrusting one.

**It needs to precede [M12](/wiki/modules/M12.md).** The governance half asks whether a system should exist and what its harms are. That question is answerable only by somebody who can already say what the system is made of and where its trust boundaries are, and [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) is where that becomes writable.

## Load profile

The module's difficulty is not intrinsic — the concepts are few and each is simple on its own. **It is evaluative, and it is concentrated at [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md).**

Tracing a single value from the moment it was guessed to the moment it was counted as evidence requires holding a chain of stages in mind at once and resisting the reading each stage offers of itself. Every individual step is defensible. The composition is not. **That is exactly the shape of the failure a graduate will meet in their own systems**, and it is why the session comes before the assessment rather than after it.

[S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md)'s load is affective instead: submitting a plan whose threats are mostly unmitigated, to an assessor, and being marked on having left them that way.

## What the module hands forward

**A configured control is not a demonstrated one, and a plan that cannot fail is not an assurance.**
