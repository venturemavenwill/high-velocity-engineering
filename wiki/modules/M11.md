# M11 · Language, Attention and Representation

**Quarter:** [Q6](wiki/quarters/Q6.md) · **Seminar days:** [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md)–[S055](wiki/seminars/S055.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** IS · ME · MS · PE
**Platform anchor:** Foundry model catalogue — embedding deployments, reasoning summaries, preference fine-tuning · Azure AI Search vector and hybrid retrieval · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server · Azure MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## The central difficulty

Every student in the room has used these systems for years. They arrive with a folk model.

The folk model is not stupid and it is not empty. It is built from thousands of interactions and it makes decent predictions about ordinary cases. Its problem is that it is a model of *behaviour under typical use*, assembled with no access to mechanism, no controlled comparison, and no negative cases — and it therefore fails in exactly the places an engineer needs it to hold: at the boundaries, under distribution shift, and when something must be said about what the system will do that it has not yet done.

**Displacing a folk model is harder than building a model where none exists.** A student with no prior belief about a hash table accepts the mechanism. A student with four years of chat experience meets the mechanism, agrees with it, and continues to reason from the folk model, because the folk model is faster and has never obviously failed them.

The module's stance: **the mechanism is genuinely simple, the behaviour genuinely is not, and the gap between them is where every engineering error lives.** No day in this module can be taught by exposition alone, because exposition is exactly what a folk model survives.

## The instrument is the subject

One fact changes the shape of this module and it is worth stating before anything else. **The students' own primary working tool is an instance of the thing being studied.** They have spent five quarters directing GitHub Copilot in agent mode; they are about to spend five days learning what is inside it.

That makes the displacement both easier and more urgent.

Easier, because every abstraction in the module has a referent the student has personally operated. *Similar to what* is a question they can ask of the retrieval that fills their own context window. *Weight is not influence* is a claim about the agent's own stated reasoning, which they read every day and have never once tested. An ablation is a configuration change they can make and measure. The module does not have to construct motivation; it has to point at something already in the room.

Urgent, because the folk model here is not idle belief — it is the operating theory under which they accept or reject an agent's work. A student who thinks the agent's explanation of its change is the reason for its change will approve the wrong changes, and will do it faster than they could have done it by hand. **The most expensive misconception in this module is one the students are acting on several times a day.**

The consequence for the design is that this module's phase 6 agent cases are not illustrations borrowed from elsewhere. Each is the day's own error, produced by the day's own tool, on the day's own material.

## Why this module sits here

**It requires M10 entirely.** Every claim in this material is an evaluation claim. A student who does not know that a benchmark is a constructed criterion, that a model judge has a systematic error component, and that an interval needs its dependency structure argued, will believe whatever they are told about these systems — and most of what they are told is measured badly.

**It requires M03 and M04's representation and memory work.** S051 is an embedding-space day and it is unintelligible without the structure material; S053's assembly is a composition-and-cost argument before it is anything else.

**It requires M09's generalisation stance.** S054's preference optimisation is a selection process, and S042's apparatus is the one that applies.

**It is not placed later** because M12 needs it immediately, and M13's serving economics needs a mechanical account of where the time goes.

## Task-class variability plan

At class 4 the student formulates. Variability is therefore in **what makes the account contestable**. Every set of four is executed against a real service, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), and where a case cannot be run the day says it is demonstrated rather than operated and the specification does not assess it.

- **S051** varies the notion of similarity: **four real deployed models over the substrate's own corpus**, four geometries, four sets of nearest neighbours, all defensible. One of the four is the same weights as another at a reduced dimension count, and it still disagrees.
- **S052** varies the input: one mechanism, four inputs, four attributions — one of which is confidently wrong — with the model's own **stated reasoning** as the fourth artefact and an input intervention as the adjudicator.
- **S053** varies the ablation: **the same retrieval and inference system with four components removed at query time**, and four behavioural consequences the folk model does not predict. The weight-level ablations that motivate the day are demonstrated rather than operated, because serverless deployments do not expose weights.
- **S054** varies the annotator population: the same preference optimisation over **four preference datasets in the platform's own training format**, producing four differently-shaped deployments.
- **S055** varies the failure: the same apparent limitation demonstrated four ways, **one of which is not a limitation at all but a measurement artefact.**

## Load profile

High and front-loaded. S051 to S053 are the module's technical mass; S054 and S055 are dispositionally heavier and technically lighter.

The module's distinctive load is **displacement rather than acquisition.** Students are not filling an empty slot; they are holding a new account alongside an old one that keeps answering first. That is a different kind of effort and it is why every day in this module contains a case the folk model gets wrong, stated as a prediction before the mechanism is given.

The standing load reduction is that **the corpus, the index, the deployments and the substrate are the same across all five days.** S051 builds the Azure AI Search index over the substrate corpus and the four embedding deployments; S052 adds one reasoning deployment; S053 and S055 reuse both; S054 adds four preference-tuned deployments that S055 reuses as its model-swap arm. Nothing is rebuilt and nothing new is introduced except the question. The platform learning cost is paid once, on day one, and the module's remaining four days spend it.

**The build is not additional load; it replaces load.** The alternative — supplying result tables and asking students to reason about them — costs the same working memory to interpret and produces no artefact anybody can attack.

## Module-level predicted failure modes

**The folk model survives the mechanism.** The module's defining failure, and the one that is invisible because the student can state the mechanism correctly. Countermeasure: every day's phase 1 elicits a folk-model prediction in writing before the mechanism arrives, and phase 8 reports how many were wrong. **S053's ablation must produce at least one consequence the folk model gets confidently wrong.**

**Attention read as explanation.** S052's risk, and the most widespread error in the surrounding literature. Countermeasure: the artefact requires a case in which a stated reason and a causal influence diverge, produced by the student, on a running deployment.

**Alignment read as "making it good".** S054's risk. Countermeasure: the artefact requires the student to name something the optimisation selected for that nobody asked for, on their own data.

**Limitations asserted as permanent properties.** S055's risk, and the one with the longest half-life — a student who writes "these systems cannot do X" in a published statement has made a claim that may be false in a year. Countermeasure: **every claimed limit must be labelled architectural, training-derived, or measurement artefact, and defended as that kind.** An unlabelled limit fails the specification.

**The agent's account is accepted as the module's account.** New in this module and specific to it. A student who asks Copilot what an embedding is, what attention does, or why an ablation had the effect it did will receive a fluent, mostly-correct answer built from the same public commentary the folk model came from — and will have replaced one unexamined source with a faster one. The risk is sharpest here because the agent is genuinely good at this material and the student has no independent means of checking it. Countermeasures: every day's phase 1 seals a prediction about what the agent will get wrong *before* it is asked; every phase 5 requires an artefact whose claims are defended by a measurement the student ran rather than by an account anybody supplied; and the phase 6 agent case is chosen so that the wrong answer would have passed the review the student actually performs. **The countermeasure is not scepticism, which is cheap. It is that the assessed claim must be traceable to an intervention.**

## Assessment contribution

**No entrustment judgement.** The quarter's is at [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md). This is the second module in the programme with none, and the reason is stated on the [Q6](wiki/quarters/Q6.md) page: the design declines to judge entrustment in the module carrying the largest volume of unfamiliar technical material.

Specification items:

- **S051** — a similarity construction, with what it makes near and what it makes far, on the substrate's corpus, in a working index.
- **S052** — an attribution, and a case where a stated reason and a causal influence diverge.
- **S053** — an ablation with a pre-committed prediction, a pre-run sensitivity statement, and the observed consequence.
- **S054** — something the optimisation selected for that nobody asked for, evidenced.
- **S055** — a published limits statement, every claim labelled by kind and defended.

Every one of the five additionally carries the three artefact elements the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) requires: a **prompt record**, an **intervention record** — which fails with no entries unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — and a **frontier statement**.

Dispositions observed: **humility** at S052 and S055; **integrity** at S054.

## Perishability

**This module's platform layer is the most perishable in the programme, and every one of its five days says so in its own register.**

The reason is not that the services are unstable but that M11's anchors sit at the newest edge of the platform. Model leaderboards are preview. Reasoning summaries are documented as not guaranteed to be generated. Preference fine-tuning is preview on a named and moving list of models. Two of the five days rely on parameters available only in preview API versions. Any of that can move between one offering and the next, and some of it moved while these pages were being written.

The response is the one the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](wiki/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md) requires and is structural rather than hopeful. Each day names its durable claim and its platform instance separately; **the durable claim is assessed and the instance is the vehicle**; and where a re-anchoring would require the durable claim to change, that is a finding about the claim and is recorded rather than absorbed.

The five durable claims are: similarity is constructed and recoverable by probing; weight is not influence; load-bearing is a property of a configured system on a class of inputs; optimisation selects for what the signal rewards rather than what anyone wrote down; and a limit claim has a kind, an expiry condition and an audience. **None of the five names a vendor.**
