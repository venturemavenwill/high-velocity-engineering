# M18 · The Next Engagement Starts Here

**Quarter:** [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md) · **Sessions:** S086–S090 · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none, in any session of this module
**Assessment:** [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) ⊘ — an entrustment decision, and the programme's closing occasion

## What the module is about

An engagement produces four things that can outlive it: an asset somebody could start from, a report about a product that friction was found in, an honest account of what was and was not established, and an amendment to the practice that produced all three.

**Only the first is named in the practice corpus behind this programme, and it is named without a mechanism.** Reusable starting points carried between engagements are one of the four things the corpus argues must hold together for accelerated delivery to produce value rather than activity. Nothing anywhere describes how one is built, what provenance it must carry, how it is versioned, who owns it, or when it is retired. It is a pillar with nothing under it.

The second is documented and the documentation stops in a revealing place. **Engineering feedback is specified as a writing standard for a single report** — a title, a summary, a categorisation block, reproduction steps, notes — in an openly published engineering handbook from a customer-embedded engineering organisation within a large technology vendor. It teaches an engineer how to write a report a product team could act on. **It describes no routing, no triage, no ownership, no service-level expectation and no closure loop, anywhere.** It does not claim to. It is not an institutional loop, and the source nowhere describes one.

The third and fourth are this programme's own. **That gap is the module's material.**

## The module's central difficulty

**Everything worth carrying forward decays, and the decay is invisible at the moment of carrying.**

A starter repository is at its most attractive on the day the engagement closes and at its most dangerous on the day it is reused, because by then two thresholds inside it have no provenance, a selection rule inside it was never written down, and the customer it was built for is not the customer it is being used on. A feedback report is written by somebody who has moved on and read by nobody in particular. A register of limits is written when everybody involved is tired of the subject. An amendment to the practice competes with the next engagement for the same person's afternoon.

**The module's answer is to attach a fate to each thing rather than a quality bar.** Not *is this good enough to carry* but *who receives it, what happens to it, and what would tell us it did not arrive*. That question is answerable for all four, and the corpus answers it for none.

## The five sessions

| Session | Title | What the learner does | What the learner produces |
|---|---|---|---|
| [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) | Reusable starting points | Decides what may be carried to a different customer, with provenance, and what must be retired | A reuse ledger with retirements in it |
| [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) | Engineering feedback that goes somewhere | Writes the report to the published standard, then specifies the loop the standard omits | A feedback item, and the routing the source does not describe |
| [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) | What this engagement did not establish | Sorts every claim the engagement made into the four evidence classes and reports the ones that damage it | A limits register, including a result that undermines the learner's position |
| [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) | The practice improving itself | Amends the written practice where it was wrong, from the position of somebody who used it | One proposed amendment, and one declined |
| [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) ⊘ | Entrustment: the final defence | States what they should not yet be trusted to do unsupervised, before hearing what the programme concluded | An entrustment judgement, and the programme's closing statement of its own limits |

The sequence is an outward movement. S086 carries an artefact to the next engagement. S087 carries a report to a product team. S088 carries the truth to whoever scopes next. S089 carries a correction back into the standard. **S090 carries the learner out of the programme, and requires them to say what they cannot yet be trusted with.**

## Why the module is class 6

Because four of the five sessions require the learner to produce something that costs them.

Retiring a starting point costs the next engagement's head start. Reporting a limit costs the engagement's account of itself. Amending a practice costs an afternoon and invites the argument that the team was doing it wrong. Stating what you cannot yet be trusted to do costs the reading a stranger would otherwise have taken.

**None of these is technically hard and none of them has a rule.** That is the class.

## The evidence position, stated before the sessions rather than after

**One thing in this module is verified from a public, page-dated source and can be cited as description:** the engineering-feedback field set, and the absence of any routing, triage, ownership, service level or closure around it. The handbook it comes from carries no single date — its pages are individually dated and the dates run across several years, unevenly, so a citation must be pinned to the page rather than to the site. **The feedback guidance page carries the oldest footer date on the whole site.** That is a fact about maintenance, and it is the kind of fact this programme requires a reader to notice.

**One further thing is verified and is unusually good:** the same handbook obliges an engineer to fix the standard if it is broken, and its amendment route is a public change request against a public repository. **The right is real and it is exercised selectively**, which the uneven page dates demonstrate. [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) is built directly on that, and it is the only session in Stage 9 whose central move has a published precedent rather than an argument.

**Everything else is class 3.** The reuse ledger, the retirement obligation, the routing specification, the limits register, the four-class sort applied to an engagement rather than to a paper, and the entrustment occasion at S090 are all this design's arguments.

## Assessment contribution

**[S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) is an entrustment decision and it closes the programme.**

The judgement is: **can this learner be trusted to judge the limits of their own competence, in a form somebody else can act on?**

Its instrument is a calibration. The learner lodges, before the occasion, a statement of what they should not yet be trusted to do unsupervised, with the observation supporting each item. The assessors have independently formed levels from across the programme. **The two are compared, and what is assessed is the fit — in both directions.** A learner who has understated their own limits and a learner who has overstated them have made the same error, which is the error this programme has been about since [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md).

**It is deliberately not a challenge dialogue and it contains no offer for the learner to decline.** The reason is recorded in [WP-090](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md) §8: this programme has used the decline-the-offer device on consecutive assessment occasions, and had begun conceding that rather than correcting it.

See [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) for the scale and the rater discipline.

## Predicted failure modes for the module as a whole

- **Carrying by default.** Everything the engagement produced goes into the ledger, because nothing in it is obviously wrong. *Countermeasure:* S086 requires at least one retirement and treats a ledger with none as unread.
- **Provenance replaced by authorship.** The learner records who wrote a starting point instead of what its contents rest on. *Countermeasure:* S086 assesses per item, and an item whose provenance is a name fails.
- **The report as the loop.** A well-written feedback item is produced, filed, and treated as the mechanism. *Countermeasure:* S087 separates the two explicitly and requires the learner to state what would tell them the report was never read.
- **The limits register that flatters.** Every limit named is one the learner had already accounted for. *Countermeasure:* S088 requires at least one result that undermines the learner's own position, and treats its absence as the finding.
- **The amendment nobody asked for.** The learner rewrites the practice to match what they would have preferred rather than what they found. *Countermeasure:* S089 requires each amendment to name the occasion on which the practice was followed and produced the wrong thing, and requires a second candidate amendment to be declined with a reason.
- **Modesty performed.** At S090 the learner names limits that cost nothing to admit. *Countermeasure:* the calibration comparison, which reads understatement and overstatement identically, and a mandatory narrative naming a specific observed action.

## What this module does not establish

**That any of it is done anywhere.** No source behind this programme describes a reuse ledger, a retirement decision, a feedback routing loop, a limits register or a practice amendment arising from an engagement. Three of those five are inferred from an omission, which is a weak basis, and the objection is conceded in every paper in this module.

**And there is no outcome evidence for any of it.** The practice corpus reports no defect, incident, security-finding, maintainability or post-deployment record for any case it describes, and no sustainment record of any kind. **Whether compounding an engagement into the next one produces anything is unknown to every party with an interest in knowing.**
