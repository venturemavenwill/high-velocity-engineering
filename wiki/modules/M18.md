# M18 · Entrustment and Handover

**Quarter:** [Q9](wiki/quarters/Q9.md) · **Seminar days:** [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)–[S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** all seven — CF · MS · SY · ME · PE · IS · SG
**Platform anchor:** the whole stack, handed over — the substrate's Bicep and `azure.yaml` infrastructure, its deployment stack, its Foundry project and deployment identities, its agent registry entries, its Application Insights trace store, its evaluation harnesses, its instructions and prompt files · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read and write) · Foundry MCP Server (preview) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## The central difficulty

A system you cannot hand over is a system you have not finished.

That is not a claim about documentation quality. It is a claim about what finishing means. For three years the students have been the reason their components work. They know which log line matters, which test is load-bearing and which is decoration, which of the system's oddities is a bug and which is the only thing standing between the substrate and a class of failures nobody has written down. None of that is in the repository. It is in them, and in eleven weeks it leaves.

The module's stance is that this is not an unfortunate side-effect of staffing. **It is a property of the artefact, and it is measurable on the receiver.** A handover succeeded if a team that has never met the author can operate the system, diagnose it when it misbehaves, and change it without breaking something they could not have known about. It failed otherwise, and the volume of what was written down is close to uncorrelated with which of those two happened.

The second half of the module's stance is the harder one. **The artefact that transfers is not the one you wrote for yourself.** Every representation the students have built across three years was built for a reader who already held the context: their own notes, their own runbook, their own naming. [S015](wiki/seminars/S015.md) taught them to choose a representation for a caller they had met. This module asks them to choose one for a caller they will never meet, who will arrive after they have gone, and who will read it exactly once under time pressure, in an incident, at the moment it matters most.

Three objects carry the weight, and each is a class-6 version of something the programme has already built at a lower class.

**A safety case** ([S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md)) is [S024](wiki/seminars/S024.md)'s duty to say so and [S004](wiki/seminars/S004.md)'s proof-as-communication assembled into a bounded claim: *this system is acceptably safe for this use in this context, on this evidence, under these assumptions.* It is not a certificate and it is not a test report. It is an argument, addressed to someone entitled to disbelieve it, whose strength is in its boundary rather than in the volume of evidence behind it.

**A residual-risk statement** ([S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md)) is what the safety case does not cover, written for the person who will bear it and who cannot ask a follow-up question. It is [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s two symmetric failures in permanent written form, and it is where the programme finally answers a question it opened in week thirteen: **when does selecting true things to say become deceiving the person you say them to.**

**A handover artefact with a warranty** ([S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md)) is [S034](wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s consistency contract at class 6 — what you may assume, what you will observe, what you must do — with an expiry, an exclusion set, and obligations that run in both directions. It is the only artefact in the programme whose reader is guaranteed to be absent when it is written.

None of the three is technically hard. All three are difficult for the same reason: **each requires the student to state a boundary that makes them look less capable than an unbounded version would**, in writing, to someone who will keep it.

## What is actually being handed over

The module used to be able to describe a handover without ever producing one. It cannot now, and the change sharpens the module rather than complicating it, because every object above has a concrete counterpart that either works in the receiving cohort's hands or does not.

The substrate transfers as a specific list, and the list is checkable:

- **Infrastructure as code.** The substrate is declared in Bicep and its agent projects in `azure.yaml`, per [Substrate and Infrastructure](wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md). A receiving team either can provision from that repository or cannot, and the distance between those two states is not a matter of opinion.
- **A deployment stack**, whose `actionOnUnmanage` setting and deny settings are the closest thing on the platform to a warranty with terms: what is protected, from whom, and what happens to a resource that stops being managed.
- **Deployment and agent identities.** A Foundry project's managed identity, the agent identities that hold the roles, and the role assignments themselves. None of these is a person, all of them have owners, and the departure of the person does not change the identity.
- **Agent registry entries**, which are the platform's own inventory of what exists and who owns it — including a count, on the tenant's own dashboard, of the agents that no longer have owners.
- **Evaluation harnesses**, whose thresholds are decisions somebody made and whose judged scores are uncalibrated, because no evaluator's agreement with human judgement is published for any of them.
- **Instructions files and prompt files**, which are specifications in executable form per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), and which the receiving cohort will discover to be defective the first time they produce non-conforming work.
- **The trace store**, which is one Application Insights resource per Foundry project, with a retention policy and a portal window that are not the same number.

Two consequences run through all five days.

**[S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)'s central claim becomes a measurement rather than an argument.** *Transfer is a property of the receiver* was previously demonstrated by a stranger's twelve-minute reading. It is now also demonstrated by whether the receiving cohort can run the thing. A reconstruction that produces a correct description and a failed provision is a specific and legible result, and it is one the author cannot talk their way out of.

**[S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md)'s warranty acquires real terms, because the platform's own guarantees change hands.** On 1 July 2026 the agent security capabilities covering Copilot Studio and Foundry agents moved to requiring a Microsoft Agent 365 licence, the agent-inventory table in Advanced Hunting was replaced, and real-time protection rules set to block stopped enforcing unless they were redefined under a new policies experience. Nobody in a receiving organisation chose any of that, and nobody re-warranted the system afterwards. It is a documented, dated instance of the thing the module is about, and the day uses it as one.

## What it means that this is the last module

Every earlier module could be followed by a correction. If [M09](wiki/modules/M09.md) taught generalisation badly, [M10](wiki/modules/M10.md) had five days to notice. If a student left [M14](wiki/modules/M14.md) believing a gate is an answer, [M16](wiki/modules/M16.md)'s adversary would disabuse them. This module has nothing downstream. Whatever a student believes on the evening of [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md) is what they leave with, and the design has no further occasion to find out that it was wrong.

Three consequences follow, and the module is built around them rather than around a closing ceremony.

**The module cannot defer.** The [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s deferral rule requires a deferring day to name the occasion that discharges the deferral and to check that the named day can carry it. From [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) onward the supply of later occasions is five, then four, then three. The programme's two longest open questions — the ethical half of selective true reporting, opened at [S013](wiki/seminars/S013.md) and half-answered at [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md), and the status of a system optimised on a criterion nobody recorded, raised at [S054](wiki/seminars/S054.md) — both come due at [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md), because there is nowhere after it to send them. **A deferral that reaches [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md) undischarged is not deferred; it is dropped**, and the module's whitepapers are required to say which of the two happened.

**The module inherits contradictions rather than creating them.** [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) must resolve a conflict [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) created and did not flag: S069 fails an arrangement whose owner is named as a function rather than a person, and a person-named owner is precisely what does not survive a handover. [WP-069](wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §6 identified this and assigned the resolution here. There is no later day to assign it to.

**The exit judgement is judged on its own.** [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md) is the tenth entrustment occasion of the programme, after S030, S040, S045, S050, S060, S065, S070, S080 and S085. **No prior level is carried forward** — a rule every previous occasion has also observed, and which means something different here, because there is no eleventh occasion at which a low reading could be revised upward. The design accepts that cost deliberately and argues for it in [WP-090](wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md) §8, where the strongest objection to the whole architecture is stated and conceded.

The module also closes the programme's own instrumentation. The paragraphs students wrote in week five at [S005](wiki/seminars/S005.md) — *what do you now believe about programs that you did not believe five days ago* — have been held for three years and are returned at [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md). The long-horizon prediction checks opened at [S020](wiki/seminars/S020.md), [S040](wiki/seminars/S040.md), [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md) and [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) are closed there, and the design states what it learned from each, including where it learned that a prediction it made was wrong.

## Why this module sits here

**It requires the students to be leaving.** Every rehearsal of a handover in which the author remains available is a rehearsal of something else. The substrate rota ends with this cohort's graduation and the receiving party — the next cohort, plus the staff who will operate the system in the interval — is real, named on the [Substrate and Infrastructure](wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page, and does not have the students' phone numbers afterwards. This is the only module in the programme whose central condition cannot be created and must simply arrive.

**It requires [M16](wiki/modules/M16.md) in full.** A safety case whose assumption set does not include an adversary is a reliability argument wearing the wrong name. [S076](wiki/seminars/S076.md)–[S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md) supply the attack classes, the authorisation boundary and the detection horizon that [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md)'s assumptions are drawn from, and [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md)'s forensics supply the only evidence in the programme of what a case's assumptions look like after they have failed.

**It requires [M17](wiki/modules/M17.md) immediately before it.** [S081](wiki/seminars/S081.md) established that the organisation is part of the system, [S083](wiki/seminars/S083.md) rehearsed risk communication to a decision-maker who will act, and [S084](wiki/seminars/S084.md) established that adoption cannot be mandated. A handover is a change-management act performed on people the engineer will not be present to persuade, and without M17 this module reads as documentation practice.

**It requires [M14](wiki/modules/M14.md)'s release apparatus.** The safety case cites a gate as evidence, and [WP-066](wiki/whitepapers/WP-066.md) §6 named [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md) as the day at which citing a gate whose error structure was never characterised becomes a defect the student can name. The residual-risk statement is [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s accepted-failure list rewritten for a reader who was not in the room. The warranty's owner clause is [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s arrangement surviving the person who negotiated it.

**It is not placed earlier** because the three artefacts are all boundaries on claims, and a student who has not yet made claims they were answerable for has nothing to bound. It is not placed later because there is no later.

## Task-class variability plan

At class 6 the student decides **what to hand over, to whom, with what warranty.** Variability across the five days is therefore in **who the receiver is and what they can do about what they are given**, because holding the class constant while varying the receiver is what prevents the handover collapsing into one rehearsed document shape.

- **S086** varies the transfer medium: four completed substrate handovers, differing in what the departing engineer produced, **one of which produced no new document at all and transferred the most.**
- **S087** varies the argument: four safety cases for one change, differing in claim breadth, evidence quality and assumption discipline, **one of which is the shortest, claims least, and is the only one still true.**
- **S088** varies the selection: four residual-risk statements from one safety case, all true, differing in what they omit, **one of which discloses its own omissions and is the only one whose reader could have found the rest.**
- **S089** varies the warranty: four handover artefacts, differing in scope, expiry and ownership, **one of which places obligations on the receiver and is the only one that survived contact with a receiving team.**
- **S090** varies nothing supplied. The system, the safety case, the residual-risk statement, the handover artefact and the receiving party are the student's own, built across the preceding four days and across three years, and the receiver is real.

**Where a case can be run, it is run.** [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)'s four handovers, [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md)'s four cases and [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md)'s four warranties are all read as documents and then tested against the system they describe — a provision attempted, a cited piece of evidence produced by command, a deny setting checked against what it actually prevents. [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md)'s four residual-risk statements are the exception and cannot be run, because what adjudicates them is a person's decision rather than a system's behaviour; the day states that they are **demonstrated rather than operated**, and its specification assesses the student's own statement and the shipped expiry check rather than the four cases.

## Load profile

**Low technical, extreme representational and dispositional.** Nothing in this module is difficult to compute and almost nothing is built. The safety case is a page, the residual-risk statement is shorter, and the warranty is a table with four columns.

The module's distinctive load is **writing for an absent reader while being assessed by a present one.** Every prior artefact in the programme was defended orally within hours of being written, and a student who wrote something unclear could repair it in the next sentence. Here the reader is absent by construction: [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)'s reconstruction test hands the artefact to someone who has not met the author and who says nothing until they have finished, and [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md)'s warranty is read by a receiving party who will act on it after the cohort has gone. **The repair channel is closed, and closing it is the module's whole point.**

The second load is ethical rather than cognitive and it is concentrated at [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md). A student who has learned that every true statement is selected from a set of true statements, and who is now asked when that selection becomes deception, is being asked to draw a line they will be held to. The day is placed third of five deliberately, so that the line is drawn before [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) requires them to write a document under it and before [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md) requires them to defend one.

The standing load reduction is that **one substrate component runs through all five days.** The student picks it at [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) and does not pick again; each day adds one object to the same handover — a measured transfer gap, a safety case, a residual-risk statement, a warranty — and [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md) assembles them. Nothing is introduced except the next question, and [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md) builds nothing at all, for the reason [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §3.1 gives and this module accepts unchanged.

## Module-level predicted failure modes

**Handover read as documentation.** The module's defining failure and the one every prior habit points toward. A student who responds to [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) by writing more will have done the thing their instincts, their employers and most of their prior coursework reward. Countermeasure: **[S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)'s payload case produced no new document and transferred the most**, the day's specification checks the reconstruction gap rather than the artefact's completeness, and no day in the module assesses a document's coverage.

**The safety case read as a completeness exercise.** Students will attempt to make the case unassailable by widening it, which weakens it, because a broader claim needs more evidence and gets less. Countermeasure: **[S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md)'s payload is the shortest case with the narrowest claim**, and the day's specification fails a case whose claim is not bounded in use, context, population and time.

**Residual risk written for the author's protection.** The commonest professional form of this failure is a statement that is complete, accurate, unreadable, and constructed so that no reader can later say they were not told. It is a defensive artefact wearing an honest one's clothes. Countermeasure: **[S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md)'s statement is read cold by a non-technical recipient who states what decision they think they are being asked to make**, and a statement whose recipient cannot say that fails regardless of its accuracy.

**The warranty read as a promise rather than a conditional.** A student who writes a warranty with no conditions on the receiver has written an unlimited liability, and will either write it and mean it or write it and not mean it. Countermeasure: **[S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md)'s payload places obligations on both parties**, and the specification requires the receiver's obligations and the consequence of their not being met.

**Closure read as celebration.** The module's affective risk, and it is subtler than the others. A cohort at the end of three years wants the last week to be a summation, and a summation is precisely what [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md) is not: it is an assessment, on the student's own three-year substrate work, with named failure conditions and an exit target the design expects a substantial share of the cohort not to reach on every activity. Countermeasure: **the judgement is stated before phase 1**, as at every prior entrustment occasion; the failure conditions are named in advance; and the module's closing instrument is not a ceremony but the [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) psychological-safety series shown across three years with whatever it actually says.

**The build read as the point.** The module's new failure mode and the one the practical turn introduces. Four of the five days now ship something, and a cohort that has spent three years being assessed on artefacts may conclude that a handover is complete when the infrastructure provisions. It is not: [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) point 10 and [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) point 8 both say that a receiving team who can run everything and has decided not to own the system has received a complete handover that failed. Countermeasure: **no day in the module assesses whether the build works**, and every day says so in writing; what is assessed is the transfer, the boundary, the selection and the warranty, and the build is what makes those checkable rather than what makes them good.

## Assessment contribution

**One entrustment judgement, at [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md)** — the programme's tenth and final occasion, carrying **all three entrusted activities on one occasion**: EA-1 (make and defend a bounded ship / no-ship decision), EA-2 (hand over a running system to a receiving organisation) and EA-3 (communicate technical risk to a non-technical decision-maker who will act on it). **Exit target 3b** for all three, per the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md).

Two assessors judge independently before conferring and **form their levels from the artefacts before the defence**, per rater discipline rule 7. **No prior level is carried forward.** That three activities are judged on one occasion by two assessors is a direct tension with rater discipline rule 1, and it is stated and conceded in [WP-090](wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md) §8 rather than smoothed.

This module is the principal seminar-side rehearsal of **EA-2**, which the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) names as rehearsed at S086, S088 and S089 and assessed at PE390 and CAP390.

**The programme's third and final unassisted occasion is at [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md)**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) and the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s unassisted-work provision. It is a **bounded unassisted build** — thirty minutes, announced, documentation available and no agent — and it is compared against the same student's [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) and [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md) records. **It is reported to the student and to the programme and it is not used to award a level**, and no assessor of the three entrusted activities sees it. It is a build rather than a judgement exercise because [WP-045](wiki/whitepapers/WP-045.md) §8 objection 3 recorded that S045's unassisted hour tested judgement and left construction untested, and named this day as the occasion that would take the other half. [WP-090](wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md) §7 discharges that commitment explicitly.

**Every specification item in this module carries the build record** — a prompt record, an intervention record and a frontier statement — under the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), assessed on the binary checks the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) enumerates and on nothing else. [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md) produces no build of its own beyond the unassisted one and instead **carries the module's four sets of records into its defences**, where a student who cannot account for an instruction in their own prompt record has produced a record they did not write or did not read.

Specification items:

- **[S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)** — a predicted retention statement; a three-part transfer inventory carrying the not-written-down enumeration with a medium classified for each item, the [S081](wiki/seminars/S081.md) moved-work item or an explicit not-found statement, and the substrate's weekly usage series with the [S084](wiki/seminars/S084.md) two-week predicted point marked; a transfer artefact with at least one item moved into the system, **shipped to the substrate's own repository**; a reconstruction test run with a receiver who has not met the author, **including the receiver's attempt to run one operation from the artefact**; the measured gap between prediction and reconstruction; and an explicit list of what the student has decided not to transfer with the cost of each.
- **[S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md)** — a safety case with a claim bounded in use, context, population and time; its argument; its evidence **with the command that produces each item** and each item's error structure named; its assumptions separated into environment, operator and adversary; its defeaters; and its expiry condition.
- **[S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md)** — a residual-risk statement for a named non-technical recipient, with its selection stated, its recoverability route named, its quantities carrying their confidence in the same field per specification rule 2, its expiry **shipped as a check that fails when the expiry event occurs**, and the recipient's cold reading of what decision they are being asked to make.
- **[S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md)** — a handover artefact in [S034](wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s three-part form with a four-part warranty, obligations on both parties, a named owner at handover and a named owner after it with the transfer's date and mechanism, **the deployment stack whose settings the warranty's terms are checked against**, and a discoverability clause telling the reader how to find what the artefact does not say.
- **[S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md)** — the three activities, their defences, the four sets of build records carried into them, the bounded unassisted build, and the closing checks.

**Each of the first four items also carries the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement.** They are assessed on the checks in the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) and on nothing else; **an intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

Dispositions observed: **integrity** at [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md), in whether the omissions the student names match the omissions their own safety case implies; **humility** at [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md), where the reconstruction gap is a measurement of the student's own self-assessment and is frequently unflattering; **reliability** at [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md), in whether the warranty is one the student would have been able to honour.

## Perishable content in this module

**Durable, and assessed:** what a receiving team must be able to do; transfer measured on the receiver; the bounded claim and its four boundaries; evidence inheriting its error structure; the three conditions on legitimate selection and the deception boundary; the recording duty preceding the disclosure duty; the warranty's four parts; slot-and-occupant ownership; obligations running both ways; discoverability as an author's requirement; and entrustment as a decision rather than a score.

**Perishable, and never assessed:** every service name, table name, role name, command, default, threshold, retention window and licence boundary named in the module. All of it is a vehicle. The Agent 365 transition is taught because it is a **dated, documented instance of a guarantee changing hands**, not because the dates matter; when it is superseded the day is re-anchored to whatever has by then replaced it, and the durable claim is unchanged. The [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) carries the verification.

**A specific perishability warning for this module.** Its platform anchors are the fastest-moving in the programme: the Foundry role names were renamed while the underlying role IDs stayed the same, the agent registry is converging from several portals into one, and the licence boundary for agent security moved during the period this module was written. **A day that teaches the current arrangement as though it were the arrangement has taught the thing the module argues against.** What survives is the shape — that inventory, identity and guarantee are separate objects with separate owners, and that all three outlive the person who set them up.
