## The stage's question

[Stage 2](/wiki/quarters/Q2.md) ended with a discovery you can act on. Acting on it is not the same as building from it.

Between a finding and a buildable thing there are two operations, and this stage is each of them once.

The first is **turning a problem into a requirement somebody could fail.** A finding describes a situation. A requirement is a commitment, and a commitment that cannot be checked has not been made. [M05](/wiki/modules/M05.md) is that operation, and its whole content is the difference between a sentence everybody agrees with and a sentence somebody could be shown to have missed.

The second is **establishing what cannot move.** Every design is built inside somebody else's decisions — systems that already run, policies nobody in the room owns, contracts signed before you arrived, and a great many things that are frozen only because nobody has asked. [M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md) is that operation, and its difficulty is that the two obvious failures are opposite: treating everything as negotiable, and treating nothing as negotiable.

**The stage's spine is one sentence, and it belongs to the open engineering playbook rather than to this design: a requirement that cannot name how it would be verified has not been written.** Everything in [M05](/wiki/modules/M05.md) is that claim applied to a different kind of requirement, and [M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md) applies it to constraints — a constraint that cannot name who would have to agree to move it has not been established either.

## The asymmetry of warrant, which is this stage's real lesson

The two halves rest on sources of very different quality, and the difference is visible rather than hidden.

**[M05](/wiki/modules/M05.md) rests on an openly published engineering playbook.** Anybody can read it. Its artefact specifications are exact — named fields, a storage location, a status vocabulary, a review state — at a level of detail a team can be marked against. **These are the strongest verified material available anywhere in this programme's sources**, because a specification is checkable in a way a claim about outcomes is not: either a document has the fields or it does not.

It is also a document that contradicts itself, carries thresholds with no source at all in the same flat register as its best-argued pages, and in one place fails its own specification in its own worked example. **Its pages were written across several years and its navigation gives no indication which is which.** Every citation in this stage is therefore pinned to a page and to the date that page was read — never to the playbook as a whole, because the playbook as a whole has no date.

**[M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md) rests on access-controlled internal material nobody outside can check**, and the idea it takes from there is the most transferable original thing in that material and **is entirely unevidenced**. There is no worked instance of it, no engagement in which it changed a decision, and no record of it being wrong. It is taught anyway, and every session in [M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md) says so in its own words.

**A learner who leaves this stage able to say which half they can check has learned the thing the stage is actually for.**

## Why this stage was written with an anchor in three sessions only, and why that was reversed

**The original position, at its strongest.** This stage once named an instrument in three sessions and refused it in seven, and the argument was a real one. **Framing is writing, and writing is exactly where a generative instrument is both most useful and most dangerous.** Useful, because a specification's only real test is whether somebody who was not in the room can build from it, and an agent is the cheapest such reader available — literal, unwilling to fill a gap out of politeness, and it will not tell you your requirement was clear when it was not. Dangerous, because the same instrument will produce a complete-looking specification from an incomplete understanding, at no cost, and the polish is indistinguishable from the work. The stage therefore admitted the agent only where it could be turned **against** the learner's own artefact:

- [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) — satisfy this requirement in a way I would reject.
- [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) — build from this specification and nothing else.
- [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md) — argue for the option I am about to discard.

Platform content is also the most perishable material in this repository, and restraint looked like the conservative reading.

**What actually happened.** Each stage reached its own judgement independently, and six of the nine argued themselves out of an anchor by reasoning of that shape. The aggregate — seventy-nine of the ninety sessions carrying no platform workflow at all — was nobody's intention and contradicted the programme's own stated purpose. This stage's contribution to that total was seven sessions.

**The defect was the level, not the reasoning.** A per-stage judgement cannot see the aggregate it is producing, and no stage page was ever asked to read the other eight. That is the transferable finding here, and a reader gets more from it than from the original argument.

**The resolution.** Anchors are assigned centrally now, as a designed spine across all ninety sessions, and every session in this stage carries one. The perishability concern is handled where it belongs — in each session's `## Perishable content in this session` table, which pairs a durable claim with the version-bound instance illustrating it, and in [09 · Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md). **The adversarial use survives as the discipline it always was.** The three sessions above still turn the agent against the learner's own artefact; the seven that now carry an anchor use it to give a requirement an enforcement point, a verification instrument or a named build path, which is a different job from writing the requirement.

## The complexity class falls back, and that needs defending

[S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) reached class 3: the learner chose what would count as success and defended the choice with no supplied criterion. **This stage opens at class 2 again, and holds there for five sessions.**

The reason is that complexity class in this programme is a property of a session's artefact and not a property of the learner's seniority. **A new artefact type arrives with a supplied specification, and while the specification is supplied the session is class 2** — the learner chooses the method, fills the fields and defends the filling, but the standard against which the artefact is judged was published before they started. That is exactly the condition in [M05](/wiki/modules/M05.md), where the field lists come from an external document and the assessment at [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) checks the fields.

[M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md) removes the specification. Nobody supplies the classification of a problem space; nobody supplies the scope at which a design should be written; and at [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) the learner **chooses the standard they will be marked against** and defends the choice. That is class 3 by this programme's own definition and it is where the stage ends.

**The objection is obvious and is not dismissed.** A cohort that has already worked at class 3 will read a return to class 2 as regression, and there is no evidence that per-artefact class reset is better than a monotonic climb. It is asserted here because a learner meeting a specification for the first time is not exercising the same judgement as one who has met a dozen, and marking them as though they were seems worse. Nothing has tested it.

> **A recorded inconsistency with a page this stage may not amend.** [Stage 4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md) states, in its own account of why it holds at class 3, that [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) crossed the boundary and **Stage 3 consolidated it.** That is true of where this stage ends and not of where it begins: [M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md) is class 3 throughout and [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) is the strongest class 3 occasion in the programme so far, but [M05](/wiki/modules/M05.md) is class 2 and consolidation is not what happens there.
>
> **The two pages disagree and the disagreement is left visible rather than smoothed.** Stage 4's inheritance list — a design at the scope of its decision, a register of what can and cannot move, a set of requirements that could be shown to be false — is exactly what this stage produces, so nothing downstream is broken by the difference. What is at issue is the description of the path, and this page's description is the one written by the stage that walked it. **A later reader who reconciles them should amend one page and annotate the other, not delete either.**


## The confidentiality discipline, stated once

Part of the material behind [M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md) is **access-controlled internal material belonging to a customer-embedded engineering organisation within a large technology vendor.** This repository is public.

**No organisation, programme, site, team, tool, checklist, individual, customer or partner from that material is named anywhere in these twenty-two files, and none of it is linked.** The stage teaches the durable structure — the classification, the sequencing, the failure modes — in original prose, and refuses the labels. The standing rule is recorded at [research/99-source-register/source-register.md](/research/99-source-register/source-register.md).

**This is a real limit on what the stage can teach.** A reader cannot check the paraphrase against the source, and no claim resting on it alone can be externally warranted. Where a claim rests on that material only, the papers say so and classify it accordingly.

**The engineering playbook is a different case and is treated differently.** It is openly published, may be discussed normally, and is cited here **by page and by the date that page was read**. It is never cited as a whole, because its pages were written across several years and it has no single currency. Every citation in this stage that carries a date carries it for that reason.

## What this stage does not establish

Nothing here shows that a well-specified requirement produces a better system.

The playbook offers no comparative evidence that its practices outperform alternatives and does not claim to. Its own examples include a comparison document that does not follow its own template, and a threshold catalogue presented without a single citation — material this stage uses as an example bank and explicitly forbids as a benchmark. The internal corpus behind [M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md) contains no reference, footnote, study or citation of any kind, and its central classification has no recorded instance of use. The book this stage takes its boundary vocabulary from makes **no quantitative claim at all**, states two prerequisites without which it claims nothing, and reports its own failures. The negotiation framework this stage uses for constraint conversations is case-based and its authors say plainly that it establishes no effect.

**The strongest honest claim available is about checkability, not about outcomes.** A requirement that names a measurement and a verification method can be shown to have been missed; one that does not, cannot. That is a property of the sentence rather than a finding about delivery, and it is what this stage teaches.
