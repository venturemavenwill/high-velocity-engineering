# Q4 · Prove — is it feasible, and how cheaply can you find out?

## The stage's question

[Stage 3](/wiki/quarters/Q3.md) produced a design at the scope of its decision. Stage 4 asks whether the thing can be done, and it attaches a second clause that is the whole discipline: **how cheaply can you find out?**

Both halves are load-bearing and the second is the one learners drop. A feasibility question can always be answered by building the system; the answer arrives after the money has been spent, which is to say it arrives after the decision it was meant to inform. **Proving is establishing feasibility before commitment**, and the measure of a proof is not how convincing it is but how little it cost to obtain.

The stage runs in two halves that use the same discipline on different material.

**[M07](/wiki/modules/M07.md) is cheap evidence about the system.** Its central artefact is the spike: a time-boxed investigation, opened by imagining the failure that has not happened yet, closed by a written finding of fact, and followed by the deliberate disposal of its own code. The half also covers the two things a spike most often discovers — that the question was not answerable as asked, and that the data promised at discovery does not exist in the form it was promised in.

**[M08](/wiki/modules/M08.md) is cheap evidence about the idea.** Its central artefact is a prototype built at deliberately low fidelity, tested with people who cannot buy it, and offered as the basis of an ask that somebody can refuse.

## Why this stage was written without a platform anchor, and why that was reversed

**The original position, at its strongest, and it had two limbs.** First, **naming the instrument in the header decides the instrument before the question is stated.** The stage's whole discipline is choosing the cheapest test that could answer a particular uncertainty; a learner who arrives knowing which tool the session runs on has had that choice made for them, and the choice is the assessed work. Second, **anchoring a proving stage to a generation capability would install the failure the stage exists to teach against.** The practice corpus argues, better than it argues most things, that adding detail to an early concept artefact is actively harmful — detail drags the discussion onto irrelevancies and away from the idea being tested, and polish suppresses honest feedback — and then notices, without resolving, that **its own tooling makes polished output nearly costless.** A stage that fixed a generation capability in its header would be teaching the mechanism while demonstrating the counter-example. Platform content is also the most perishable material in this repository, and abstention looked like the safe reading.

**What actually happened.** Each stage reached its own judgement independently, and six of the nine argued themselves out of an anchor by reasoning of that quality. The aggregate — seventy-nine of the ninety sessions carrying no platform workflow at all — was nobody's intention and contradicted the programme's own stated purpose. No stage argued for that total; every stage contributed to it.

**The defect was the level, not the reasoning.** A per-stage judgement cannot see the aggregate it is producing, and no stage page was ever asked to read the other eight. That is the transferable finding, and a reader gets more from it than from the original argument.

**The resolution.** Anchors are assigned centrally now, as a designed spine across all ninety sessions, and every session in this stage carries one. The perishability concern is handled where it belongs — in each session's `## Perishable content in this session` table, which pairs a durable claim with the version-bound instance illustrating it, and in [09 · Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md). **The first limb survives as an exercise rather than as an omission**: the anchor names the instrument the session runs on, and the sessions still require the learner to select and defend the cheapest test for their own uncertainty, several of the strongest answers to which are not software at all. The second limb survives at [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md), where the costless-polish tension is the session's own subject and the anchor is a tool chosen precisely because its limits are published.

Agents appear throughout the ten sessions as instruments rather than as ceremonies: to attack a spike charter, to enumerate failure causes nobody in the room wanted to name, to produce the refined artefact whose cost the learner must then argue about. **They are never the evidence.**

## The two tensions this stage teaches rather than resolves

Both are properties of the source material, and in both cases the honest teaching move is to hand the tension over rather than to pick a side on the learner's behalf.

**The costless-polish tension.** The mechanism for low fidelity is well argued and the countermeasure it implies — strip detail on purpose — was designed for a world in which the refined version cost more than the rough one. That world is gone. The corpus records the observation and offers no advice, and this programme has none either. [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md) requires the learner to write the sentence they would actually say to a colleague who has produced the polished version at no extra cost, and marks the sentence rather than the position.

**The simulated-informant tension.** The same corpus proposes substituting a model's simulation of a user for a user when users are unavailable, and offers no discussion of whether the simulation is valid — in a body of work that opens by warning against building for an imagined user. [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) refused the substitution and said the refusal was an argument rather than a finding. [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md) requires the learner to argue the other side properly, because a refusal that has never met its best case is a preference.

## Why the stage holds at complexity class 3

At class 2 the learner chooses the method and defends the choice. At class 3 the learner also chooses what would count as success. [S020](/wiki/seminars/S020.md) crossed that boundary and Stage 3 consolidated it.

**Stage 4 does not advance the class, and the reason is worth stating rather than leaving as an omission.** What varies here is not how much of the criterion the learner supplies — it is all of it, in every session — but what the criterion is *about*. In Stage 2 the criterion was sufficiency: when have I learned enough. Here it is adequacy of proof at a price: what evidence would settle this, and what is the least I can spend to get it. Those are the same degree of autonomy pointed at a different object.

The stage's one internal escalation is the object of the defence at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md), which is **the cheapness rather than the finding.** A learner who proved feasibility expensively has satisfied the question and failed the clause.

## What this stage inherits and what it hands forward

**Inherited from [Stage 3](/wiki/quarters/Q3.md), and not re-derived:** a design stated at the scope of its decision; a register of what can and cannot move; a set of requirements written so that they could be shown to be false. Stage 4 is entitled to assume these exist and entitled to send them back if the design has no decision in it, because a proof of something nobody was going to decide is not a proof of anything.

**Handed to [Stage 5](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md):** a written finding of fact with its instrument named and its time box recorded; a data reality record separating what was promised from what was inspected; a prototype whose failure is documented rather than repaired; and one commitment obtained in a currency the giver felt. Build begins from those four or it begins from optimism.

## The confidentiality discipline, stated once

The practice material behind this stage is **access-controlled internal material belonging to a customer-embedded engineering organisation within a large technology vendor.** This repository is public.

**No organisation, programme, site, team, tool, checklist, individual, customer or partner is named anywhere in these twenty-two files, and no internal material is linked.** The stage teaches the durable structure in original prose and refuses the labels. The standing rule is recorded at [research/99-source-register/source-register.md](/research/99-source-register/source-register.md).

**The openly published engineering playbook is a different object and is treated differently.** It is public, it is open to external contribution, and it may be discussed. It is cited here **by page**, never as a whole, because it has no single date: its pages carry individual footer dates spanning several years, the unevenness is invisible in its navigation, and a reader who cites the site rather than the page has cited nothing datable. Where this stage relies on a page whose individual footer date was not recorded during the repository's reading, the papers say so and pin the citation to the reading date instead.

## What this stage does not establish

Nothing here shows that cheap proof produces better outcomes than expensive proof, or than none.

The practice corpus reports **no failed engagement**, no cost side of any kind, and no post-deployment record. Its single failure narrative is a synthetic composite and is fictional by its own admission. It therefore cannot supply a case in which a spike saved a project, because it cannot supply a case in which a project was in trouble.

The open playbook supplies the spike's protocol in unusual operational detail and **offers no comparative evidence that the protocol outperforms an alternative**, which is true of the whole document and is stated plainly in this repository's reading of it. In at least four places the playbook reaches for a magnitude and warrants none of them; **none of those figures is repeated anywhere in this stage, and none of the corpus's many unsourced claims about speed is repeated either.**

**The strongest honest claim available is a claim about ordering, not about outcomes:** evidence obtained before a commitment can change the commitment, and evidence obtained after it cannot. That is close to a tautology, and the stage says so rather than dressing it as a finding. Everything more than that — that low fidelity produces more honest feedback, that a fast failure is a win, that a disposable spike stays disposable — is mechanism, argument or practitioner judgement, and each session states which.
