## The quarter's theme

Q4 is where the student stops being told what success looks like.

At class 2 the method was chosen by the student and the success criterion was supplied. At class 3 **both are the student's**, and the quarter's two modules attack this from opposite ends. M07 works systems in which correctness itself is negotiable — concurrency, partial failure, consistency — where the criterion is a contract you choose and must defend. M08 works the situation before any system exists, where the request you received is not the problem you should solve, and finding the real one is the work.

The two halves meet in the same claim: **a specification is something you construct and are answerable for, not something you receive.**

Q4 is also the first quarter in which students change production code.

## The write turn

This is the quarter in which the Azure MCP Server moves from read to write, and it is the largest single change in what a student can do to something that is not theirs.

For three quarters the agent has inspected. From S031 it creates, configures and deletes. A prompt now has a resource group on the other end of it, and the difference between a good prompt and a careless one is a bill, an outage or a deleted database. **Bicep is the second half of the turn and it is the half that makes the first half survivable**, because it converts an architecture from something the agent did into something a person can read before it happens.

The quarter's governing sentence, stated at S031 and retrieved through S035:

> **An architecture you can describe and cannot deploy is a diagram. An architecture the agent deploys that you cannot review is a liability.**

Both halves are taught, and the second is the harder one. It is taught concretely rather than as an exhortation, because the review instrument the platform supplies — `what-if` — is itself an instrument with false positives and false negatives that the documentation states plainly, and characterising it is [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md)'s discipline applied to the tool a student would otherwise trust without checking. That work is done at [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md).

## What advances to complexity class 3

At class 3 **the student chooses the method and the success criterion, and must defend both.**

- **S031 to S034** each require a correctness criterion to be stated before any implementation, with the criterion's cost to the caller named.
- **S035** requires the student to decide what "working" means for a system nobody has specified, and then to falsify it.
- **S036 to S039** require the problem itself to be constructed from evidence rather than received.
- **S040** requires the student to hold an unresolved problem for six hours without converging.

What does *not* advance is the decision to proceed. Whether the work should be done at all is class 5 and arrives at M13.

## Substrate role

**Q4 is the first quarter in which student changes reach production**, beginning at S035 — and the first in which students write to Azure at all, beginning at S031.

The two are deliberately separated, and the separation is the quarter's principal safety measure.

- **S031 to S034** deploy to **disposable subscriptions the programme owns**, not to the substrate. Every build is created and torn down within the day. The substrate is analysed and specified against; no substrate change ships.
- **S033** is the quarter's full deploy-and-destroy day: a gateway backend pool with a circuit breaker, authored with Copilot in agent mode, deployed by Bicep, exercised until it trips, and removed with its resource group before phase 6.
- **S035** ships to the substrate. Students break a real component in a controlled window, observe the consequences, and repair it. Staff hold a rollback and do not use it unless a user is affected.
- **S036 to S039** work with real users on a real unaddressed need, with the resulting requirements entering the substrate's backlog.
- **S040** works on a problem staff have not solved.

The escalation from S030's adopted instrument to S035's production change is deliberate and is the largest single increase in consequence in the programme.

**Disposable is not the same as consequence-free.** A disposable subscription still bills, still holds a real quota, and still permits a deletion nobody intended. The design's position is that the disposable environments teach the write discipline and the substrate teaches the write *consequence*, and that reversing the order would be indefensible.

## What the agent may do, and where

Stated once, at quarter level, so no day has to restate it.

| Environment | Agent write access | Human gate |
|---|---|---|
| Disposable subscription, S031–S034 | Yes, through the Azure MCP Server | Deployment reviewed against `what-if` before it runs |
| Substrate, S035 | **No.** The change is authored with the agent and applied by the student | Criterion committed in writing; staff veto without appeal |
| Substrate, any other day | No | — |

**The agent does not hold the substrate window.** A student may use Copilot to write the change, to reason about the blast radius and to draft the report; the application of the change at S035 is a human act by a named person under a named accountable staff member. This is not a claim that agent-applied changes are unsafe in general. It is a claim that the day whose subject is *consequence you cannot delegate* must not delegate the consequence.

## Entrustment occasions

**One, at S040.** The activity is *characterise a problem from evidence and defend the characterisation to someone who asked for something else* — a precursor to EA-3. Expected level 2a to 2b.

The Q3 judgement at S030 is not carried forward; it is re-formed. Per the [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md), levels do not accumulate.

## Reports due in this quarter

- **The adopted instruments from S029** — operational status, which of them are still running, and which were withdrawn at their review date.
- **The S020 load-change comparison** was reported in Q3; students are asked at S035 to revisit what they got wrong about it.

## What Q4 must achieve for Q5 to be possible

1. **Correctness as a chosen contract.** Q5's machine-learning material requires a student who can specify what a system must do before evaluating whether it does it.
2. **Tolerance for unresolved problems.** S040's endurance exercise is a precondition for M10's criteria work, where the criterion genuinely does not exist until someone builds it.
3. **Real user contact as routine.** S041 onward assumes contextual inquiry is a practised skill rather than an event.
4. **Production consequence experienced once.** S045 and S060 both require students who have shipped something that mattered.
5. **Infrastructure as a reviewable artefact.** Q5 onward assumes a student who can read a deployment before it happens and who knows what their review tool does not show them. Without it, every later quarter's build is an act of faith in the agent.

An agent invoking tools concurrently is a distributed system; a tool that times out has S033's three outcomes plus a fourth; a tool boundary is S034's contract with a caller that cannot read documentation.

Its documentation and observability frame — the five-part report, the estimated-and-measured blast radius, and the cohort's grouped unpredicted observations entering the substrate's documentation — is load-bearing for all three and is not to be weakened.

## Perishability

Every policy name, attribute, default, tier restriction and version threshold used in M07 is **perishable** and is marked as such in each day's register. What is durable is that a shared finite resource without per-consumer accounting is consumed by the least careful consumer; that a limit enforced by distributed counters is approximate; that a cache keyed on similarity trades correctness for cost; that a review instrument has both false positives and false negatives; and that a default is an opinion somebody else held.
