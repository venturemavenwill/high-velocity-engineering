## The stage's question

[Stage 4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md) closed with a proof somebody funded. Stage 5 is where the money starts being spent, and the question has a clause in it that learners drop as reliably as they dropped the cost clause: **under real constraint.**

Delivery in an unconstrained setting is an exercise. Delivery in a forward-deployed engagement happens inside somebody else's estate, on somebody else's schedule, with an architecture you were not consulted about and a review queue you do not control. The practice corpus behind this programme has one genuinely good idea about that condition and this stage takes it seriously: **even in an almost entirely frozen space there is something that can be changed, and constraint is not an excuse for doing nothing.**

The stage runs in two halves and the halves are not variations on each other. They are the same discipline applied to two different executors.

**[M09](/wiki/modules/M09.md) is delivery under constraint.** Its artefacts are the slice, the scope ledger, the specification written as failing tests, and the review record. Its argument is that every rule worth having is a rule about what you do when you are behind, and that a rule you cannot tell whether somebody followed is not a rule.

**[M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) is working with an agent**, and it is the first place in this programme where the agent is treated as an instrument that does work rather than as an adversary that attacks your position. That switch is the stage's largest single move and it is argued for at [M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) rather than announced.

## Why the first half has no platform anchor and the second half does

[Stage 4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md) refused an anchor in every session, on two grounds: that naming the instrument in the header decides the instrument before the question is stated, and that anchoring a proving stage to a generation capability would install the failure the stage exists to teach against. **[M09](/wiki/modules/M09.md) keeps the refusal and adds a third reason.**

Everything M09 requires must hold whoever or whatever does the work. A slice is valuable or it is not, regardless of who cut it. A specification is a specification or it is prose, regardless of who reads it. **A specification that only works when a human reads it is not a specification**, and fixing an executor in the header would let a learner satisfy the module by writing something a particular model happened to follow that term.

**[M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) takes an anchor because the instrument has become the object of study.** You cannot teach the configuration of an executor without a configured executor, and you cannot teach that a green check is a claim about a check rather than about the work without a check that goes green. The anchor is therefore taken deliberately, and it is taken **pinned rather than current**: a named commit, a named package version, a named host, a named model and a date, recorded in every session that uses it and re-recorded by every cohort.

The honest cost of that decision is stated here once and repeated in each M10 paper. **This is the most perishable content in the programme.** File paths, artefact counts, plugin names and package versions all moved during the period the underlying reading describes, and will be wrong soon after it. **Pinning does not slow the decay. It makes the decay visible**, which is the only property available, and it means that a cohort running these five sessions must record their own pin before they record anything else.

## The two tensions this stage teaches rather than resolves

Both are properties of the source material. In both cases the honest move is to hand the tension over.

**The validation-capacity tension.** The delivery model behind this stage rests entirely on expert human validation. It says so, and it says so as a strength: the tooling amplifies the operator rather than levelling the field, so an experienced engineer gets usable assets out of it and an inexperienced one generates noise. Reviewer competence is therefore load-bearing, and a team member must be somebody you would have trusted on a purely human team to hold the bar.

**The corpus then never asks what happens when generation accelerates and validation does not.** Review capacity is the obvious binding constraint on the whole model, and across approximately ninety internal documents it is not discussed once. This programme has no answer either. [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) requires the learner to state the review capacity their own production rate demands and to name who supplies it, and marks the statement rather than the answer.

**The buried-testimony tension.** The same collection's headline achievement is end-to-end feature delivery. Its own practitioner testimony, held in speaker notes, says that on a mature production codebase the assistant could not be asked to add a feature end to end, because the generated code reused outdated patterns and ignored architectural constraints — and that the workaround was to **make failing tests the specification and spend most of the human effort there.**

The two accounts sit in the same collection and neither notices the other. **This stage teaches the second.** [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) is built directly on it, and [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) marks the consequence the testimony implies and the headline conceals: that the human effort moved rather than disappeared, and that nothing in the collection prices where it moved to.

A third contradiction is smaller and is taught in one session rather than across the stage. The corpus's standing prohibition — **use the tooling to make each step better, not to skip steps** — is contradicted by the customer-facing material's proudest anecdote, in which a prototype is generated on the first day from a template with no discovery activity described at all. [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md) puts the rule and the anecdote on the same page and asks which one the reader's own week resembled.

## Why the stage holds at complexity class 3

[Stage 4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md) argued that class 3 means the learner supplies the criterion as well as the method, and that a stage does not advance the class merely by changing what the criterion is about. Stage 5 makes the same argument for the same reason, and the change here is worth naming because it looks like an escalation and is not.

What changes is **who did the work the learner is accountable for.** In every prior stage the learner defended their own act. From [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) the learner defends an act performed by a delegate, and the defence must be as specific as if they had performed it — line by line, at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md).

That is the same degree of autonomy pointed at a delegated object. It is harder, and difficulty is not class. **The stage's one internal escalation is the object of the defence at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)**, which is neither the increment nor its speed but the candidate's account of which steps the tooling made better and which it let them skip.

## What this stage inherits and what it hands forward

**Inherited from [Stage 4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md), and not re-derived:** a proof pack with its cost ledger; an unproven list; a commitment obtained in a currency the giver felt, or a recorded refusal; a data reality record separating what was promised from what was inspected. Stage 5 is entitled to assume these exist and entitled to send the work back if the commitment nobody could have refused turns out to be the only one on offer.

**Handed to [Stage 6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md):** a shipped increment with its exclusions still visible; a specification expressed as tests that arrived with the code; a review record stating what was *not* read; a comprehension audit naming the regions nobody can explain; an instruction set attached to the work rather than filed near it; an inexplicability register; and a recorded pin. **Assurance begins from those or it begins by threat-modelling a system whose parts nobody understands**, which is the commonest way a security review produces a document instead of a finding.

## The confidentiality discipline, stated once

The practice material behind this stage is **access-controlled internal material belonging to a customer-embedded engineering organisation within a large technology vendor.** This repository is public.

**No organisation, programme, site, team, tool, checklist, individual, customer or partner is named anywhere in these twenty-two files, and no internal material is linked.** The stage teaches the durable structure in original prose and refuses the labels. The standing rule is recorded at [research/99-source-register/source-register.md](/research/99-source-register/source-register.md).

**The openly published engineering playbook is a different object and is treated differently.** It is public, it is open to external contribution, and it may be discussed. It is cited **by page**, never as a whole, because it has no single date: its pages carry individual footer dates spanning several years and the unevenness is invisible in its navigation. Where a page's own footer date was not recorded during this repository's reading, the papers say so and pin the citation to the reading date instead.

**The agent-configuration repository used in [M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) is also public** and is treated as a third kind of object again: an open-source artefact whose mechanisms are craft and whose surface is product detail. It is cited only with its commit, its package version, the host and the model attached, and never live.

## What this stage does not establish

Nothing here shows that any of it produces better software.

The playbook supplies the stage's normative content in unusual operational detail — code is incomplete without tests, tests arrive in the same change as the code, new work becomes a new backlog item rather than a bigger old one, every changed line is read — and **offers no comparative evidence that any of those practices outperforms an alternative.** That is true of the whole document and is stated plainly in this repository's reading of it. In at least four places it reaches for a magnitude and warrants none of them, including an assertion on its own code-review measures page that review costs less than nothing, offered with no measurement, no baseline and no source. **None of those four is repeated anywhere in this stage.** One of them is put in front of learners at [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md) as a specimen, which is the only use this repository permits.

The internal corpus supplies the stage's most quotable human judgements — comprehension debt, the amplification claim, the prohibition on skipping steps — and supplies **no failure, no cost side, no defect data, no post-deployment record and no sustainment evidence.** Its single failure narrative is a synthetic composite and is fictional by its own admission.

The agent-configuration repository supplies the stage's platform anchor and is candid about its own limits in a way the rest of the material is not. It contains evaluation machinery and **no committed results** — a grader exists, a result corpus does not. Its own documentation states that its behavioural checks test output shape rather than correctness, that some checks are advisory rather than authoritative, and that human oversight remains necessary. **The revision this stage pins is not all-green**, and that failing check is part of the evidence rather than noise to be tidied away.

**The strongest honest claim available is a claim about accountability, not about outcomes:** work you cannot explain is work you cannot defend, and delegating the production of it does not delegate the defence. That is close to a definition, and the stage says so rather than dressing it as a finding.
