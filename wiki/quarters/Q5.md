## The quarter's theme

Q5 is where the programme's subject matter arrives.

Four quarters have built a stance — correctness is chosen, evidence is scarce, measurement is an instrument, problems are constructed — on material where the answers are eventually knowable. Machine learning is where that stance becomes load-bearing, because a learned component's behaviour is not derivable from its construction and the only access is measurement.

M09 asks what a model does. M10 asks **where the criterion came from**, and that question moves the quarter to complexity class 4: the student formulates the problem from an ambiguous brief, including what counts as success.

## This is where the practical claim becomes fully real

The programme claims that a graduate can architect and ship a working AI system on the Microsoft AI Platform principally by prompting GitHub Copilot, and can say what the result does badly. Four quarters have built toward that. **Q5 is where it is discharged in full.**

By the end of week forty-five each student has, through Copilot agent mode against the [Foundry MCP Server](/wiki/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md): chosen a model from the Foundry catalogue with the catalogue's own benchmark data in front of them and a reason for distrusting it; created a deployment and read back its quota and status; scored their component with Foundry cloud evaluation and stated in writing that every judged score it produced is uncalibrated; and either shipped that deployment to real substrate users behind a feature flag or defended a decision not to.

**The Foundry MCP Server is introduced this quarter and is the specific mechanism behind the claim.** It exposes 38 tools across 10 categories, and the three that matter here are `model_deploy`, `model_deployment_get` and `model_benchmark_get`. It is in public preview, which is stated to students on the day they first use it, and which is itself the quarter's first lesson about the difference between a capability and a supported capability.

## What advances to complexity class 4

At class 3 the student chose the method and the success criterion **within a stated problem**. At class 4 **the problem itself arrives ambiguous and the student formulates it**, including what would count as an answer.

- **S046** is the hinge. The criterion does not exist; it is constructed from evidence and defended.
- **S047 to S049** require the criterion to be validated, its judge examined, and its statistical claims bounded.
- **S050** requires the student to state what their own evaluation does not license, to someone who wants it to license more.

M08 prepared this: a student who has held an unresolved problem for six hours can hold an unresolved criterion for a module.

## Substrate role

Q5 is where student models reach users. **It is the first quarter in which something a student built, using an agent, serves a request from a person who did not consent to be part of a course.**

- **S041 to S043** train and evaluate on the substrate's real data, offline, with every evaluation run through Foundry cloud evaluation so that the judged scores are on file and their uncalibrated status is stated in the artefact.
- **S044** produces a threat model for a component that is deployed, and runs the four Defender IaC checks against the deployment template that will actually be used.
- **S045** deploys one student model behind a feature flag, to a fraction of traffic, under the [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page's disclosure conditions.
- **S046 to S049** construct and validate the criterion by which that model is judged.
- **S050** reports the result to the people affected, and **reports each deployed model's actual behaviour against that student's stated failure modes.**

### The deployment conditions

S045's deployment is bounded on four sides and every bound is stated to students before phase 1.

1. **Behind a feature flag, to a bounded fraction of traffic.** The flag is the withdrawal mechanism and the student is the one who can pull it.
2. **Under the substrate's disclosure conditions.** Affected users are told a student-built component is in the path, and one of them attends the defence.
3. **On a deployment the student can name.** The Foundry deployment name is the value that appears in the `model` parameter of every routed request; a student who cannot say which deployment served a response cannot investigate one.
4. **With a withdrawal signal and a threshold, decided before the deployment exists.** This is the element the specification weights, and it is why the deployment follows the judgement at phase 5 rather than preceding it.

**A defended decision not to deploy is a full pass. A deployment with no stated failure modes is not.** That is the programme's position and it is stated before the day begins so that no student optimises toward shipping.

### The unassisted-work provision

S045 is **one of the three occasions in the programme** — with [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) — **at which a bounded portion of the assessed work is performed without agent assistance.** At S045 the bounded portion is phase 3's decision hour.

The [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) states the reason plainly: a student who has only ever built with an agent may be unable to build without one, and the programme cannot currently detect that. The comparison between assisted and unassisted work is reported to the student and to the programme. **It is not used to award a level.** It exists so the trajectory is visible while there is still time to act on it.

## Entrustment occasions

**Two.**

- **S045** — *train, evaluate and deploy a learned component, and state what it will do badly.* Precursor to EA-1. Expected 2a–2b.
- **S050** — *state what an evaluation does not license, to a party who wants it to license more.* Precursor to EA-3, and the closest precursor yet. Expected 2b.

Neither carries forward from Q4. Per the [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md), levels are re-formed each occasion. At both occasions two assessors judge independently and **form their level from the artefact before the defence**, per rater discipline rule 7.

## The commitment this quarter makes to itself

**S045 commits S050 to reporting the deployed models' actual behaviour against each student's stated failure modes.** This is not a summary of the quarter; it is an adjudication of a prediction each student made in writing on the day they shipped, and it is the programme's third long-horizon prediction check after S020 and S040.

The instrument is on file: each student's three failure modes, each with an affected party and a frequency, recorded at S045 phase 3 step 3. The comparison at S050 is against the deployment's Foundry monitoring metrics and the substrate's own incident record over the intervening five weeks. **A student whose model failed in a way they named is in a different position from one whose model failed in a way they did not**, and the day's whole content is that the difference is not luck.

The deployments run for the quarter. Withdrawal at any point on the student's own stated signal is a result, not a failure, and is reported as such.

## What Q5 must achieve for Q6 to be possible

1. **Generalisation as the default question.** Q6's language-model material is unintelligible to a student who evaluates on training data.
2. **A constructed criterion, defended.** S054's alignment material and S059's benchmark material both assume the student knows criteria are made rather than found.
3. **Judge bias as a live concern.** S048 is the direct precursor of every model-as-judge decision from S067 onward.
4. **A deployed model with a stated failure mode.** S060 and S070 both require students who have shipped something learned.
5. **A working relationship with the Foundry MCP Server.** Q6 assumes a student can list, deploy, inspect and delete a model deployment by prompting, and can tell when the agent has done something other than what they asked.

## Perishability

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| A held-out estimate is an estimate for the population the set was drawn from | The catalogue leaderboard's normalised index, and its own warning that standardised datasets "might not reflect performance on your specific data" |
| Selection is search, and a maximum is not an estimate | Foundry cloud evaluation runs against a versioned dataset, and how many times a student consults it |
| A capacity choice is a structural hypothesis | Fine-tuning method choice — `supervised`, `dpo`, `reinforcement` — and the `auto` hyperparameters the service resolves for you |
| A threat is an actor with access and a goal | The four Defender IaC checks, and Defender CSPM's plan and licensing boundary |
| A deployment decision is not an evaluation decision | The Foundry deployment name in the `model` parameter, and the feature flag in front of it |
| Every judged score is uncalibrated until its agreement with human judgement is published | Every Foundry evaluator, on the date of reading |

**The whole platform layer of this quarter is perishable and one part of it is perishing on a published date.** From 1 July 2026, AI agent discovery and security posture for Foundry agents require a Microsoft Agent 365 licence; Defender CSPM continues to discover Foundry accounts and projects. That is a capability moving out from under a course anchor on a date the vendor announced, and it is taught at S044 as an instance of what perishable means rather than hidden as an inconvenience.

Verification runs before every offering, per the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md). Where a capability cannot be run for licensing or tenant reasons, the day states that it is **demonstrated rather than operated**, and the specification does not assess it.
