# Q1 · Stage 1 · Qualify

**Sessions:** [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)–[S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) · **Modules:** [M01](/wiki/modules/M01.md), [M02](/wiki/modules/M02.md)
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** one in every session — GitHub Copilot agent mode with the Microsoft Learn MCP Server, the Copilot Studio and Foundry build-path comparison, Microsoft Purview DSPM for AI, Azure AI Search security trimming, the Azure API Management AI gateway with provisioned throughput, and Microsoft Agent Framework evaluations
**The question this stage answers:** should this work happen at all, and can you say no?

---

## What this stage is for

A forward-deployed engineer is handed a solution, a date and a room. The stage exists to insert one act between being handed those things and starting: **deciding, on stated grounds, whether the work should happen.**

The decision has three permitted outcomes and the stage treats them as equal. Proceed. Proceed on a named condition. Decline. What the stage assesses is never which one a learner reached, only whether the grounds were stated in a form that could be checked and whether the learner could hold them under challenge.

## Why it comes first

Three reasons, in order of weight.

1. **The decision is made under the worst information the engagement will ever have, and it constrains everything afterwards.** A misjudged qualification cannot be recovered by good delivery; it can only be delivered efficiently in the wrong direction.
2. **Refusal is structurally unavailable unless it has been rehearsed.** By the time an engineer notices the disqualifying condition, the customer has spent political capital, the delivery organisation has committed commercially, and the engineer has been selected for competence and reads hesitation as a failure of it. None of those pressures is illegitimate. Together they are decisive.
3. **A practice that cannot refuse work cannot be said to exercise judgement.** That is an argument rather than a finding, and this stage says so on every page.

## What it hands to Stage 2

Stage 2 · Discover assumes it has been given three things and does not re-derive them.

- **A problem stated without a technology in it**, with a datable last occurrence and a named person who performs the current workaround. Produced at [S003](/wiki/seminars/S003.md), assessed at [S005](/wiki/seminars/S005.md).
- **A map of who is affected and who was absent**, including at least one party that bears a cost and holds no purchasing power. Produced at [S007](/wiki/seminars/S007.md).
- **A register of what can and cannot move**, with the source of each belief recorded and the untested beliefs marked as untested. Produced at [S006](/wiki/seminars/S006.md).

Discovery without those three is a workshop about a solution. **Stage 2 is entitled to assume they exist and is entitled to send them back if they do not.**

## The strands active here

Two, and only two. Both run the length of the programme; the stage uses them narrowly.

- **PE — professional engagement.** What the engineer is accountable for, to whom, and what happens at the boundary where their judgement meets somebody else's authority.
- **ME — measurement and evidence.** What a claim rests on, who would produce the number again, and who is responsible for establishing that an observed change was caused by the work.

**This stage is where ME is at its most negative.** Almost everything it teaches is what a number does not license.

## The supplied briefs

**Both briefs below are invented for this programme.** No organisation, customer, sector body or individual described in them exists, and nothing in them is drawn from any engagement. They are constructed to contain specific defects, which is the only reason they are usable.

### Brief A — used from [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) to [S009](/wiki/seminars/S009.md)

A regional operator maintains building systems — ventilation, water, fire suppression — under scheduled contracts for several hundred sites it does not own. Field engineers visit sites, do the work, and write free-text notes on a handheld application. A back-office team reads those notes and completes a compliance record for each visit, which an external assessor samples.

The sponsor is the operations director. Their request, as given: *"We want an AI agent that reads the engineers' job notes and fills in the compliance record automatically. We've seen it demonstrated. We'd like it live before the next assessment window."*

Also true, and not volunteered:

- Records are frequently returned by the assessor and reworked. Nobody present can say what proportion, and no one has looked.
- The back-office team believes the returns are caused by engineers omitting things from notes. The engineers have not been asked.
- The record's field structure is described by everyone as "set by the regulator". One person thinks it was set by a predecessor to match an old system.
- The compliance team's size has been raised twice in leadership meetings this year.
- The assessor has never been in a conversation with the operator's technology function.

### Brief B — introduced at [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md)

The same operator, some months later. The sponsor has a second request: use the same notes to produce a ranking of the subcontractors who perform a share of the visits, for use in renewal decisions.

The conditions attached, all stated by the sponsor as fixed:

- The performance signals must come from the incident-reporting stream, which was gathered under an explicit safety-reporting purpose and told contractors it would not be used for commercial assessment.
- Subcontractors are not the operator's employees and will not be approached, because the sponsor does not want the ranking known before renewal.
- The ranking is to be applied without a human review step, because a review step is what the sponsor is trying to remove.
- The safety function is not to be included, on the grounds that this is a commercial exercise.

**Brief B exists to fire a well-written disqualifying condition.** A learner whose condition from [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) does not fire here has learned something more useful at [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) than one whose does.

## What this stage does not establish

Nothing here shows that qualification improves outcomes, and no source behind this programme measures it.

The practice corpus this material rests on refuses work exactly once, in a note attached to a slide. It records no engagement it declined, no non-synthetic failure narrative, and no criteria under which a customer would be unsuitable. **The stage is therefore built on an absence in its own source, and that is a weak basis for a practice.** It is included because the alternative — a programme that teaches delivery and never teaches the decision to deliver — makes a stronger implicit claim on even less.

**Every session in this stage is class 3: design reasoning with no external warrant.** The interviewing and evidence techniques inside the sessions have better standing than the stage that contains them, and the papers separate the two.
