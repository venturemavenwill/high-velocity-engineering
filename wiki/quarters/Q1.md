# Q1 · Machines, Programs and Proof

**Year 1, quarter 1 · 10 teaching weeks · 10 seminar days (S001–S010) · Complexity class 1**
**Platform anchor:** GitHub Copilot agent mode · **MCP servers:** Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## Theme

The quarter establishes two habits that everything after depends on: **reasoning about behaviour before executing it**, and **treating a claim as something that requires warrant**. It also does something unusual — it teaches students the instructional design they are subject to, on day one, before they have been subject to it long enough to resent it.

No *subject* in this quarter is artificial intelligence. That is deliberate, and the original reason stands: a programme whose distinctive claim is production judgement about intelligent systems cannot begin with intelligent systems, because judgement requires something to judge against.

**The instrument, however, is one from the first hour.** Every day of this quarter is executed with GitHub Copilot in agent mode against the Microsoft Learn MCP Server, and the distinction between the two sentences above is the quarter's first lesson. A machine trace, a representation, a proof and a loop invariant are all things whose truth does not depend on what an agent says about them — which makes them the only honest material on which to learn what an agent is worth. **A student who first meets Copilot on a problem they cannot check has no way to find out that it was wrong.** Q1's subjects were chosen, before this quarter had a platform anchor at all, precisely because the student can check every one of them by hand.

That is the quarter's structural position and it is not repeatable. From Q2 the answers stop being hand-checkable, and the habit of checking has to already exist.

## The toolchain this quarter establishes

Q1 is where the instrument the next three years are operated with is installed, characterised and written down. Three artefacts exist by the end of week five and are carried forward unchanged in kind, if not in content, to [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md).

**A configured repository.** Every student holds a repository containing `.github/instructions/*.instructions.md` files with `applyTo` globs, and — from [S003](wiki/seminars/S003.md) — at least one instruction whose effect on the agent's output the student has personally demonstrated. The instructions file is introduced here as what it is: **a specification whose conformance is checked by execution rather than by reading.** The programme assesses specifications from [S006](wiki/seminars/S006.md); this quarter establishes that the two objects are the same object.

**A grounding server.** The Microsoft Learn MCP Server is configured at [S001](wiki/seminars/S001.md) and used on every subsequent day. It requires no authentication and carries no charge, which is why it and not a provisioned service is the quarter's anchor — the whole cohort can operate it on day one, and the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](wiki/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md)'s open question about what a course lab can actually run does not arise here. It arises from Q2.

**A frontier map.** Each day's phase 5 artefact carries a frontier statement. **M01's five are collated at [S005](wiki/seminars/S005.md) phase 8 into one page**, and that page is carried to [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) as the baseline against which the quarter's first unassisted occasion is read. The map is a sample from one cohort on five hand-checkable problems, and the quarter says so. Its reader is next year's cohort, via the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md).

What is *not* established here is any competence with the platform's own services. The Azure MCP Server is demonstrated at [S002](wiki/seminars/S002.md) and operated from Q4; Foundry appears from Q5. A student leaves Q1 able to direct an agent and say what it did badly, and unable to deploy anything.

## Complexity class

**Class 1 — the student decides nothing.** Tasks are fully specified and the method is given. This is not a lack of ambition; it is the load-management position. Element interactivity is at its highest relative to the student's schema base in the first quarter, and asking a novice to select a method as well as execute it is the standard way to produce a cohort that learns nothing while feeling challenged.

Class 1 persists through M03. The first method choice arrives at S016.

**Class 1 is not weakened by the agent, and this is the quarter's most easily missed point.** An agent that can produce a method makes it *easier* for a student to skip method selection, not harder, and the class-1 tasks are specified tightly enough that the agent's method choice is not the student's decision either. What the student decides at class 1 is what to check and against what. That is why every phase 5 in this module carries a hand-produced oracle — a trace, a committed pre-statement, a proof — against which the agent's output is compared.

## Entrustment occasions

None. Q1 generates no entrustment evidence. The dispositions record opens at S010 and runs continuously thereafter.

This is a deliberate refusal: an entrustment judgement in the first quarter would be a judgement about prior background, not about the programme.

## Substrate role

Students **observe** the substrate; they do not touch it. Two contacts:

- **S001** — students are shown the live system, its current user count, its current monthly cost and its most recent incident, and are told they will be responsible for it. Nothing is asked of them.
- **S009** — the testing day uses a real defect from the substrate's history as its worked case.

The purpose of both is anticipatory: the substrate must be present as a fact from week one, or the class-5 days in year three arrive as a genre shift rather than a progression.

**Builds ship to a disposable environment, not to the substrate.** Every phase 5 in this quarter produces something that runs — a configured repository, a machine simulator, an instructions file with a demonstrated effect, a property check — and all of it is disposable by construction. Substrate *data* is used at [S003](wiki/seminars/S003.md); substrate *deployment* is not available until the student can be trusted with it, which is a judgement this quarter deliberately declines to make.

## Perishability

The quarter's durable content is unchanged by the platform anchor and remains what it was: determinism, representation, argument, induction, and the design the student is subject to. **Everything about the tooling is perishable and is marked as such on each day.** Named modes, file paths, front-matter keys, tool names, approval defaults and MCP configuration syntax will all move; two of them moved between the first and second drafts of this page.

The separation is enforced per day in each day's perishability register and it is not decorative. A student who can state what an instructions file is for can rebuild one when the path changes. A student who has memorised `.github/instructions/` has learned a directory name.

## Retrieval position

Q1 is the quarter in which the spacing mechanism is weakest, because there is almost nothing old to retrieve. Days S002–S006 retrieve only from the preceding fortnight. The design compensates with denser within-day generation rather than pretending the schedule is working, and this limitation is stated to students at S001 as a worked example of the honesty the programme asks of them.

## What Q1 must achieve for Q2 to be possible

- A student who can state what a program will do before running it, and be embarrassed when they are wrong rather than surprised.
- A student who has been wrong in public at least once without consequence, and knows from experience rather than assurance that the error clinic is safe.
- A student who understands why the programme's schedule feels inefficient, and can articulate the argument for it well enough to disagree with it specifically.
- A shared vocabulary of invariant, decomposition, warrant and counterexample.
- **A student who can direct an agent to a specified task, and produce a record of what they asked, what they corrected and where the agent was wrong.** Q2's tasks are not hand-checkable, and a student who has not built the checking habit on material where checking is cheap will not build it on material where it is expensive.
- **A student who has personally observed the agent be confidently wrong at least twice**, on a problem they could adjudicate. The jagged frontier is taught at S001 and is not believed until it has been seen; the quarter's design assumes one demonstration is insufficient.
