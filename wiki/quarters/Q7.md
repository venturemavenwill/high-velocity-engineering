## The quarter's theme

Q6 ended with a retrieval system in production. Q7 is the quarter in which somebody sends the bill for it.

That is not a joke about money. It is the structural fact the quarter is built on. Every decision a student made in Q6 — which index, which model, which recall target — was defended on evidence and against a baseline, and none of it was defended against a cost. A system that is correct, evaluated, and unaffordable has not been engineered. A system whose operator cannot say what it will cost next month has not been engineered either, and that is the more common condition.

M13 supplies the model. Inference is not one workload; it is at least two, with opposite bottlenecks, and where the time and the memory go is derivable from the assembly [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md) built. Batching converts latency into throughput and decides who waits. Memory management decides how many requests may be in flight, and the decision has a cliff. Capacity, quota and budget are three different numbers owned by three different parties, and confusing them is the quarter's most expensive error.

M14 supplies the gate. A release decision on a system with a cost position, an evaluation result, a monitoring plan and a deadline — which is the shape of the first entrusted activity.

**The quarter's stance:** latency, throughput and cost are the same decision viewed from three positions, and a promise about one is a constraint on the others. An engineer who has promised a latency target has already made a cost commitment and usually does not know it.

## Why this quarter is the platform's best fit

The redesign's difficulty everywhere else in the programme is finding a place where a durable idea becomes concrete. Here the problem is the reverse: **the platform states this quarter's central claims in its own documentation, in its own units, with its own consequences attached**, and the design's job is to stop students memorising them.

Three of the four things M13 teaches are documented facts on Microsoft Learn rather than analogies for them.

- **Cost per request is a function of shape.** The published sizing method for a provisioned deployment takes three inputs — request shape, an output-to-input ratio, and a cache rate — and states that output tokens require more processing capacity than input tokens and that cached input tokens consume none. That is [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md)'s claim, in the vendor's arithmetic, with a divisor per model.
- **Reserving against the worst case costs you concurrency.** A managed deployment estimates the cost of an in-flight request from its prompt tokens plus the `max_tokens` the caller declared, and the documentation states that leaving `max_tokens` unspecified can produce lower concurrency than expected. `max_tokens` is [S063](/wiki/seminars/S063.md)'s reservation, it is one line of the request, and almost nobody sets it deliberately.
- **Quota is not capacity.** Not a slogan. The documentation carries it as a call-out: having quota does not guarantee that capacity is available, and if regional capacity is insufficient the deployment fails. A separate sentence adds that a reservation is a discount on a billing meter and does not hold anything either. **A student can be refused for one reason and then refused again for a different one**, which is [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) executed rather than described.

The fourth — that a decision in week 61 is a bill in week 68 — the platform supplies by billing hourly from the moment a deployment is created until the moment it is deleted, whether or not any request is served.

## What the cohort operates, and what it is only shown

Stated at the top of the quarter because it bounds everything below, per the open question on the [Microsoft AI Platform Map](/wiki/program/11-Microsoft-AI-Platform-Map.md).

**Operated.** Standard deployments and their full token accounting. The gateway: token limits and quotas, per-consumer metrics with dimensions, backend pools with priority routing, circuit breakers, semantic caching. Azure Monitor metrics and their splitting dimensions. Cost Management. The model-capacities read API and the portal capacity calculator. A deployment attempt that is refused. Foundry cloud evaluation, including the evaluation GitHub Action, which is in preview.

**Demonstrated rather than operated.** Creating a provisioned deployment; spillover and its response headers; the provisioned utilisation metric under real load; purchasing a reservation. All four require quota, capacity and money the programme does not hold, and **none of them is assessed anywhere in this quarter.** What that costs the quarter is set out on each day's page and argued in [WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8.

**Every commercial figure in this quarter is perishable and is dated at the point of use.** No price, rate, ratio, minimum size or discount appears in any page of M13 or M14 as a taught fact. Where a student needs one they retrieve it themselves through the Learn MCP Server and record the retrieval date beside it, and the date is a checked element of the specification.

## What advances to class 5

[Q6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md) held class 4 for a full quarter and said why it would not advance: class 5 requires the student to **own a system across time — to make decisions whose consequences arrive weeks later and to be answerable for them** — and Q6's material could not supply that without teaching autonomy and transformers simultaneously.

Serving supplies it without contrivance, and the mechanism is arithmetic rather than pedagogy.

- A cost model built at **week 61** produces a monthly figure. The billing period closing in **week 68** is what says whether the figure was right.
- A commitment shape chosen at **week 64** — pay-per-use against a reserved rate — has a break-even utilisation. Whether the student cleared it is knowable in **week 68** and not before.
- An admission policy chosen at **week 63** determines which users waited during the traffic peak the substrate happens to get, and the substrate's peaks are not scheduled by the timetable.

**The platform does not merely permit that structure; it enforces it.** A provisioned deployment's meter starts when the deployment is created and stops when it is deleted, and the documentation states that the deployment cannot be paused. A reservation is a one-month or one-year commitment against a meter, and scaling a deployment down does not shrink it. There is no configuration on this platform under which a capacity decision becomes checkable inside the day it is made.

Class 4 asked the student to formulate the problem. Class 5 asks whether to proceed at all, and the distinguishing feature is not difficulty but **latency of feedback**. Every prior class let the student find out whether they were right inside the day. This one does not, and the discomfort of that is the content.

Two consequences follow, and both are structural rather than tonal.

**The artefact must carry its own expiry.** A recommendation made under uncertainty and checked six weeks later must state, at the time it is made, the date at which it becomes checkable and the number that will show it was wrong. This is the competence [WP-012](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-012.md) named as assessed here, and every specification in M13 requires it.

**A prior level cannot be carried forward.** [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) and [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) judge occasions, not people. A student who was 2b at S060 has established nothing about a decision whose consequence has not yet arrived.

## Substrate role

Q7 is the quarter in which the substrate's operating cost becomes a taught object rather than a background fact.

- **The hard monthly ceiling and its degradation path are shown to students in week 61**, per [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md). Students have known the ceiling exists since Q1; this is the first quarter in which they are the ones deciding what happens as it is approached.
- **S061 to S063** profile, batch and allocate against the substrate's own serving path, under real traffic. There is no synthetic load in this module except where a controlled measurement requires it, and every such case is marked.
- **S064** produces a capacity commitment for the substrate's forthcoming term, with a named owner and a named review date.
- **S065** presents that commitment to the person who signs for it, who is a real person with real authority to refuse.
- **S066 to S070** put a release gate in front of the serving path and then use it.

**The degradation path is the quarter's most important substrate property.** The programme does not run out of money and go dark; it falls back to a cheaper serving path with worse behaviour. That makes the cost constraint visible as a service-quality decision rather than as an administrative one, which is exactly what a student needs to argue about.

**Two substrate components are added for this quarter and stay for the rest of the programme.**

- **A gateway in front of the serving path.** Every request the cohort's systems make from week 62 onward passes through an API Management instance the students configure, and each student team is a distinct consumer of it with its own counter key. That is what makes contention between students a measured quantity rather than an anecdote, and it is what makes [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md)'s agent traffic attributable when it arrives.
- **A cost line the cohort can read.** Per-consumer token metrics are emitted with dimensions from week 62, and Cost Management is readable by every student from week 61. **A student who cannot see the bill cannot argue about it**, and until this quarter none of them could.

The gateway is also the quarter's answer to a problem the design would otherwise have no answer to. Provisioned capacity is the thing the programme cannot buy — but **priority routing across a backend pool is the same decision at a layer the programme can operate**, and the day that teaches it says plainly which half is real.

## Entrustment occasions

**Two**, which returns the quarter to Q5's rate after Q6's single occasion.

- **[S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md)** — *present a cost argument to someone who pays for it.* Precursor to EA-1 and touching EA-3. Expected **2b**.
- **[S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md)** — *make and defend a bounded ship / no-ship decision.* The first direct rehearsal of EA-1. Expected 2b–3a.

The two are separated by five weeks on purpose. S065 commits to a cost position; S070 defends a release decision against, among other things, that same position with five weeks of realised spend attached. **A student who overcommitted at S065 meets it at S070**, which is the quarter's whole argument compressed into two occasions.

**Neither carries a level forward from Q6.** Specification items continue at every day.

Aggregate to the cohort, individual results privately. **Not assessed**, and that decoupling is published at S061 as well as at S065.

- **The S010 psychological-safety series** — eighth administration taken at S065, with the full series across six quarters shown to the cohort.

## What Q7 must achieve for Q8 to be possible

1. **A cost model the student built and was billed against.** [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s resource-exhaustion material is an adversary deliberately moving the operating point, and it is unintelligible to a student who has not chosen one and paid for it.
2. **Capacity, quota and budget held apart.** S064's distinction is retrieved at S077 and again at [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md), where scope is negotiated under exactly these three constraints with a counterparty. A student who still treats them as one number will negotiate the wrong one.
3. **A release gate that exists and has refused something.** [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md)'s agent material adds a component that acts, and the gate is the only thing standing between an agent and the substrate's users. It must be real before Q8, not designed during it.
4. **One decision whose consequence the student has already met.** This is the class-5 acquisition and it cannot be taught in Q8. S065 and S070 are where a student first finds out, in public, that a position they defended was wrong — and the professional composure that requires is the entry condition for M16's incident work.
5. **A gateway the cohort configured, with per-consumer accounting already running.** [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s wallet attack is an adversary spending somebody else's token budget, and it is not detectable — let alone attributable — without a counter key that was in place before the attack. Building the accounting after the incident is the failure that day exists to prevent, and the only way to avoid teaching it that way is to have built the accounting a quarter earlier.
