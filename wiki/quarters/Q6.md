# Q6 · Assure — is it safe, and how would you know?

**Stage 6 of 9** · Sessions [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md)–[S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md)
**Modules:** [M11](/wiki/modules/M11.md) · [M12](/wiki/modules/M12.md)
**Complexity class:** 4 throughout
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none, in any session — see below

---

## The stage's question

[Stage 5](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md) shipped something. Stage 6 asks whether it is safe, and it attaches a second clause that is the entire discipline: **how would you know?**

The first clause is what every assurance process claims to answer. The second is the one it usually cannot, and the gap between them is where this stage lives. A security plan exists. It has sections, a table of threats, a set of mitigations, a score and possibly a colour. **None of that is evidence that the system is secure, and most of it is not evidence of anything except that a document was produced.**

The stage runs in two halves that apply the same test to different objects.

**[M11](/wiki/modules/M11.md) is the artefact.** It teaches threat modelling as something engineered rather than facilitated — a system model that converts prose into stable identifiers before anything else happens, a threat that must bind to a named component and a numbered flow to survive, a mapping that refuses the junk drawer, and a closure criterion that is re-derived from the repository rather than ticked by a person. It then teaches, at greater length, where that design fails, because the failures are more instructive than the successes and they are the ones a graduate will reproduce.

**[M12](/wiki/modules/M12.md) is the process around the artefact.** It teaches a governance regime whose best idea is a scope test that survives agentic delivery, whose worst property is that its central requirement is asserted everywhere and instrumented nowhere, and whose standing first question — can this be solved without AI at all — is the question the rest of the industry has stopped asking.

## What this stage is built on, and why it is unusual

Most of this programme rests on a practice corpus that is thoughtful, experienced and entirely unwarranted. **This stage rests on something better: a working multi-agent system, built by people solving a real problem under real constraint, whose design decisions can be examined one at a time.**

It is a chained agent pipeline for threat modelling, produced inside a customer-embedded engineering organisation within a large technology vendor. It is genuinely good. It is also the sharpest available specimen of the confusion this programme exists to name — **the confusion between a control that is configured and a control that has been demonstrated to work** — and it fails in ways the earlier stages have already given learners vocabulary for.

Nothing built for a classroom would be this good or fail this instructively. That is why the stage teaches from it rather than around it.

**The material is access-controlled and this repository is public.** The confidentiality discipline is restated at the end of this page and it is not negotiable.

## Why this stage has no platform anchor

Every stage in this programme that names a platform capability does so because the work depends on it. **This stage names none, and as in [Stage 4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md) the omission is an argument rather than a gap** — but it is a different argument.

**First, the suite this stage learns from is bound to a specific knowledge source, a specific set of control frameworks and a specific tracking platform, and none of those bindings is the lesson.** The pattern is durable; every concrete integration in it is a recipe. A header that fixed one of them would teach the recipe and lose the pattern, and the pattern is what survives the next procurement decision.

**Second, and more seriously: anchoring an assurance stage to a named capability installs the failure the stage exists to teach against.** The whole subject of [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) is that naming a technology is not evidence that it is deployed, protecting anything, or configured the way you assume. A stage that opened every session by naming one would be demonstrating the error in its own header while marking learners down for it.

Agents appear constantly across the ten sessions — the stage would make no sense without them — and they appear as objects of study and as instruments, never as the evidence. **A learner is expected to run one, read what it produced, and then establish independently whether what it produced is true.** That is the stage's whole method.

## Why the stage advances to complexity class 4

At class 2 the learner chooses the method and defends the choice. At class 3, established in [Stage 3](/wiki/quarters/Q3.md) and held through [Stage 4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md), the learner also chooses what would count as success.

**At class 4 the learner supplies the evidentiary standard: what would count as evidence that the criterion was met, and what would count as its absence.**

That is the correct escalation for an assurance stage and it is not an arbitrary one. The material this stage teaches from sorts every claim it makes into a configured control, an executable check, an observed result, outcome evidence or unsupported inference — and the exercise of sorting is only possible for somebody who has already decided what each category requires. **A learner who cannot say what would count as evidence cannot classify anything, and will read a well-formatted document as a measurement.** Every session in this stage requires the standard to be written down before the artefact is produced, and several of them mark the standard rather than the artefact.

The escalation is stated here rather than derived per session, because it applies uniformly and because the two assessment occasions both turn on it.

## The two tensions this stage teaches rather than resolves

Both belong to the source material, and in both cases the honest move is to hand the tension over rather than to settle it on the learner's behalf.

**The precision–recall tension in threat selection.** The rule that a threat must bind to a named component and a numbered flow is a genuinely good discipline: it is what makes a threat model about *this* system rather than about systems in general. It also removes, systematically and invisibly, everything concerning the operator, the supply chain, human process and out-of-band administration — each dropped with an entirely plausible logged justification that nobody reads. **The rule and its cost are the same rule.** [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) requires both the bound registry and the register of what binding excluded, and marks the second.

**The over-inclusion tension.** The same practitioners hold two opposite instincts and both are right. When *choosing which security domains to consider*, over-inclusion is cheap and under-inclusion is catastrophic. When *mapping controls to threats*, the identical instinct produces the junk drawer — broad hardening attached to everything, which is the characteristic failure of coverage-maximising practitioners. **Nothing in the source says when to switch**, and neither does this stage. [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md) requires the learner to state where their own switch happens and to defend it.

## What this stage inherits and what it hands forward

**Inherited from [Stage 5](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md), and not re-derived:** a shipped increment; a record of what an agent did that the learner could not explain; and the comprehension debt that came with it. Stage 6 is entitled to assume a running system exists and entitled to send the work back if nobody can say what it is made of, because a threat model of a system nobody understands is a threat model of a diagram.

**Handed to [Stage 7](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md):** a system model with numbered flows and marked provenance; a threat registry with its exclusion register attached; a set of closure criteria expressed as evidence discoverable in the repository; an unmitigated list that has not been shortened; and a governance assessment that generated work items with owners rather than a signature. **Operations begins from those five or it begins from a document nobody will reopen.**

## What this stage does not establish

Nothing here shows that threat modelling of any kind produces more fixed vulnerabilities than any alternative, including doing none.

**No outcome evidence exists for the pipeline this stage learns from.** There is no comparison against a human-authored threat model, no defect-escape record, no time-to-plan, and no coverage against a known vulnerability set. The observations that clearly drove its design are visible only as scar tissue in its own instructions — a stage told to re-read a file rather than trust the table already in front of it, a writing strategy designed for interruption — and **the observations themselves are nowhere recorded, so the strongest evidence that the design works cannot be cited.**

**Not one calibration constant anywhere in that material cites a study, a measurement, a pilot or a rationale.** Every weighting, band boundary, threshold and cut-point is a designer's judgement rendered as a number. **No figure from any of them is reproduced anywhere in this stage or anywhere in this repository**, and the stage's position is that a score presented with a tier and a colour will be read as an observation when it is an assertion.

**The governance half is weaker still.** The regime it teaches from carries no evidence that any assessment ever changed a system, and its central living-document requirement has no version field, no re-sign-off row and nowhere to record that the review happened. This programme has not fixed that; [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md) requires the learner to fix it for their own artefact and to say what it cost them.

**The strongest honest claim available is a claim about what can be checked, not about what is safe:** a mitigation whose implementation can be found in the repository is one you can argue about, and one whose implementation cannot be found is not a mitigation yet. That is close to a definition, and the stage says so rather than dressing it as a finding.

## The confidentiality discipline, stated once

The primary material behind this stage is **access-controlled internal material belonging to a customer-embedded engineering organisation within a large technology vendor.** This repository is public.

**No organisation, programme, site, team, tool, suite, individual, customer or partner is named anywhere in these twenty-three files, and no internal material is linked.** The stage teaches the durable structure in original prose and refuses the labels. The standing rule is recorded at [research/99-source-register/source-register.md](/research/99-source-register/source-register.md).

**A second restriction applies to this stage and to no other. The source contains operational attack detail.** None of it is reproduced. **These sessions carry threat *categories* and control *classes* only** — what a class of attack targets, never how to perform one — and a learner's own work is held to the same rule. That restriction has a teaching cost, it is accepted deliberately, and [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) states it in the session rather than leaving it as an unexplained thinness.
