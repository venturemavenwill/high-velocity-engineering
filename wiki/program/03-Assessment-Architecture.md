# Assessment Architecture

> The entrustment spine. It is placed at the centre of the programme rather than at its margin because it is the only element that touches every strand, and because the gap this programme exists to close is the inability to certify judgement. Design inherited from [research/01-course-structure/10-gap-analysis-and-course-architecture.md](/research/01-course-structure/10-gap-analysis-and-course-architecture.md) and [research/08-assessment-epas/](/research/08-assessment-epas/collected-materials.md).

## The decision this architecture makes

Conventional assessment answers *how much of the material has this student acquired*. This architecture answers a different question: **what would we allow this student to do unsupervised, and on what evidence?**

That is a decision, not a measurement. It has a decision-maker, a body of evidence, a standard of proof, and consequences for being wrong. Treating it as a measurement — converting it to a percentage and averaging it — destroys exactly the information that makes it useful.

The distinction is imported from the entrustment literature, where it is stated sharply: competencies are possessed by a person; activities are entrusted to a person. See [research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md).

## The three entrusted activities

Sizing guidance in the source literature is twenty to forty activities for an entire professional programme, with a reported per-learner completion average well below the number defined. A three-year undergraduate programme should therefore define **few**. This design defines three.

### EA-1 · Make and defend a bounded ship / no-ship decision

The student is given a system change, an evaluation result, a cost position and a deadline, and must decide whether to release. The decision is defended orally against what-if perturbation: *what if the interval were twice as wide; what if the affected population were the one that cannot complain; what if you are wrong.*

- **Rehearsed at** S070, S065, S050.
- **Assessed at** ME390, across at least four occasions with at least three distinct assessors.
- **Target level at exit:** 3b.

### EA-2 · Hand over a running system to a receiving organisation

The student produces and defends a handover: what transfers, to whom, with what warranty, what the receiving organisation must be able to do without them, and what the residual risk is.

- **Rehearsed at** S086, S088, S089.
- **Assessed at** PE390 and CAP390.
- **Target level at exit:** 3b.

### EA-3 · Communicate technical risk to a non-technical decision-maker who will act on it

The student explains a real risk on the substrate to a decision-maker with authority and no technical background, in a way that supports a decision without either overclaiming certainty or hiding behind qualification.

- **Rehearsed at** S083, S065, S082.
- **Assessed at** PE301, CAP390, SG390.
- **Target level at exit:** 3a–3b.

**These three are provisional and have not been ratified.** None has yet been written to the seven-part activity template the source literature specifies. This is named as an open question in the [Programme Charter](/wiki/program/00-Program-Charter.md).

## The scale

Output is an **ordinal supervision level**, never a percentage.

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Present and observing; not permitted to act |
| 2a | Acts with direct, proactive supervision in the room |
| 2b | Acts with direct supervision available on request |
| 3a | Acts unsupervised with all findings reviewed before consequence |
| 3b | Acts unsupervised with findings reviewed after the fact |
| 3c | Acts unsupervised; review on exception only |
| 4a | Acts unsupervised and may sign off routine cases |
| 4b | Acts unsupervised and may supervise others |

The programme targets **3b** at exit for EA-1 and EA-2. Levels 4a and 4b are not attainable in an undergraduate programme and are listed so that the scale is not mistaken for a ceiling.

**Why sub-levels.** Level 3 without sub-levels collapses the three distinct decisions — pre-review, post-review, exception-only — that are exactly where undergraduate progression happens.

## Narrative substantiation is mandatory

**No level may be recorded without a narrative that would justify it to a colleague who did not observe the occasion.**

The warrant is direct. Where assessors are given both narrative and numeric ratings, they reason from the narrative and use the score to confirm an impression already formed; and idiosyncratic rater variance accounts for a substantial share of variation in performance ratings. A number recorded without narrative therefore preserves the least informative part of the judgement. See [research/08-assessment-epas/govaerts-narrative-assessment-literature.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/govaerts-narrative-assessment-literature.md).

A conforming narrative states: what the student was asked to do; what they actually did, including at least one specific observed action; what the assessor inferred from it; and what would have to change for the next level.

Narratives that do not name a specific observed action are rejected by the moderation process, not the assessor.

## Rater discipline

Six rules, each with a reason.

1. **Multiple observers, multiple occasions.** No entrustment level derives from a single observation by a single assessor. Rater variance is too large for a single observation to be defensible.
2. **Independent written judgement before deliberation.** Assessors record level and narrative independently before any discussion. Assessors reaching the same level frequently reason from incompatible premises, and deliberation-first destroys the evidence that this has happened.
3. **Divergence is investigated, not averaged.** Where independent judgements differ by more than one sub-level, the disagreement is documented and resolved by re-observation where possible, not by splitting the difference.
4. **Assessors are trained on their own theories.** Rater training targets the assessor's implicit performance theories and inference habits, not the rubric wording, because the rubric is not where the variance lives.
5. **The error-clinic firewall.** Nothing surfaced by a student in a seminar day's error clinic may enter an assessment judgement about that student. Ever. If this is breached once the mechanism is dead for the cohort.
6. **Published reliability position.** The programme publishes, to students and to external reviewers, that rater variance in this class of judgement is large and that human agreement in comparable judgement tasks has a documented ceiling. Assessment claims are made at coarse granularity because that is what the reliability supports.
7. **Form the level from the artefact; use the defence to test it.** Where an occasion produces both a written artefact and an oral defence, the assessor's provisional level comes from the artefact, and the defence is used to confirm, revise or overturn it. The reverse ordering — forming an impression in conversation and reading the artefact for support — is what rule 4 names as the site of variance, and a defence supplies narrative while an artefact supplies evidence. Added after [WP-040](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-040.md) §8 found the instruction absent from the assessor brief for the second entrustment occasion running, in a programme whose own material warns that assessors reason from narrative.

   **Amended after [WP-060](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-060.md) §8.** Where the artefact records a **prospective** decision rather than a completed action, it must carry **at least one rejected alternative with its reason** — the operating point not taken, the promise not made, the condition not imposed. Rule 7's justification is that an artefact supplies evidence and a defence supplies narrative; for a prospective decision that premise inverts, because the artefact contains only what the student intends and the reasoning lives in the conversation. A rejected alternative is the only element that restores it. The amendment binds every ship / do-not-ship occasion — [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md), [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md), [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md) and all of EA-1 — and the register entry recording the defect stands, so that the reason for the amendment remains legible.

## Course-level grading

Courses are graded **pass / fail against specification**, with no partial credit.

- Each course publishes a specification bundle: the artefacts required, the standard each must meet, and the number required for a pass.
- Specifications are binary and public. An artefact meets the specification or it does not.
- **Revision is expected**, not exceptional. See the token economy below.

This removes point-chasing, which is the principal mechanism by which extrinsic contingency displaces the internalised motivation a three-year programme depends on. It also removes the averaging that makes a bare pass and a strong pass indistinguishable in a transcript — which is a real cost, accepted deliberately, and mitigated by the narrative record.

### Three rules on how specifications are written

All three were added after whitepapers found the same defects recurring across unrelated days.

**1. A specification must state what is actually checked.** Where the design applies a small number of binary checks, the specification must list them and say that nothing else is assessed. Wording that implies a quality judgement the design cannot make will produce one, because assessors reason from narrative and unstated criteria drift — a finding this programme teaches and had, in its own specifications, ignored. Identified at [WP-028](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-028.md) §8 for the licensing sentence and again at [WP-032](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-032.md) §8 for the evidence argument; recorded twice before being acted on, which is itself part of the record.

**2. A qualification must travel with the quantity it qualifies.** Where a specification requires a number, it must require the number's confidence and provenance **in the same field** — not in a separate step, not in an appendix, not by convention. A limitation recorded once and detached from the figure it bounds will be separated from it, and the figure will travel alone. Identified at [WP-033](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-033.md) §8 as the third instance of one failure mode: [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) phase 6's metric attrition, the [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md) instrument adoption problem now handled in [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md), and [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md) step 5's stripped confidence. Two of the three were in this programme's own specifications, telling students to do what the specifications did not.

**3. A discrimination the day performs must not be discarded before assessment.** Where a phase produces a judgement that separates good work from work that merely satisfies the form — a peer's construction, an expert's stumble, a cohort comparison — the specification must require its outcome to be recorded and acted on, not merely to have occurred. Identified at [WP-038](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-038.md) §8 as the third instance in one module: [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md)'s cohort grouping of unpredicted observations, [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md)'s reverse-inference test, and [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md)'s stumble record. In each case the day generated the evidence that would distinguish substantive from perfunctory work and the specification checked only that the activity had happened.

The pattern is worth naming precisely. It arises because the discriminating phases are conversational and the specifications are documentary, so the discrimination lives in a register the assessment does not read. The remedy is not to assess the conversation but to require its outcome in the document.

**When these rules took effect, and what that leaves visible.** Rule 1 was identified at [WP-028](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-028.md) §8 and again at [WP-032](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-032.md) §8 before being acted on; rules 2 and 3 followed at [WP-033](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-033.md) §8 and [WP-038](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-038.md) §8. All three bind from [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md) forward. **The specifications for [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) to [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md) are not retrospectively amended and do not carry the "nothing else is assessed" sentence** — the same treatment the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) gives its scope-rule widenings and the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](/wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) gives instrumentation rule 6. A reader who checks will find days whose specifications imply a quality judgement the design cannot make. That is not an oversight; it is the first third of the programme preserved in the state that produced the rule, and a reader who cannot see the defect cannot evaluate the correction.

> **Correction, recorded on audit rather than resolved.** The paragraph above invites the reader to check, and a mechanical check does not return what it promises. The sentence *"Nothing else is assessed."* is present in **36 of the 40** days from S001 to S040; the four that lack it entirely are S037, S038, S039 and S040. The sentence originally read *"forty days"*, and that number is wrong, so it has been changed to "days".
>
> The presence is not uniform in kind, which is why this is recorded rather than repaired. In **S001–S009** the sentence follows the frontier statement and belongs to the **build record**, which the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) governs on every day of the programme — its presence there is not a retrospective amendment and contradicts nothing. Elsewhere in the range it sits closer to the day's own specification, where the claim above says it should not be.
>
> **What is unresolved is which artefact should change.** Either the days carry a sentence rule 1 says they should not, or this paragraph overstates a boundary that was always about specifications and never about build records. Both readings are live; the first would mean amending assessment specifications, and the second would mean rewriting a passage of the self-correction record. Each is a design decision, and an audit is not the place to take it.

## Assessing the build record

Phase 5 of every seminar day produces a build, and three elements travel with the artefact under the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md): a prompt record, an intervention record and a frontier statement. They are assessed. They are also the elements most easily satisfied in form and not in substance, so what is checked is stated exactly, per specification rule 1.

**A fourth element attaches to the same artefact and adds no new one**, under the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md): each conclusion the artefact draws is placed in exactly one of five classes — **configured control, executable check, observed result, outcome evidence, unsupported inference**. **A conclusion in no class fails.** The check is mechanical and the judgement is not: an artefact whose every conclusion sits at *observed result* or above, with nothing in the fifth class, is returned once with the question *what did you conclude that you cannot support?* — because the fifth class is the one the discipline lives in, and an artefact that never uses it has usually not looked.

**The prompt record.** Three binary checks: that it records the instructions given and the order they were given in; that it states why, for each; and that it includes the instructions the student abandoned. **Nothing else is assessed.** In particular it is not assessed for brevity or elegance, and a build produced in fourteen prompts is not worse than one produced in one — they are different engineering events, and the record exists to make them distinguishable rather than to rank them.

**The intervention record.** Two binary checks: that every correction of the agent is present together with what the correction was, and that the record is not empty. **An empty intervention record fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — the same sensitivity discipline [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) established and [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 corrected. The escape clause is load-bearing and is not a softening. Without it the fail condition becomes a quota on interventions, and a quota on interventions produces invented ones, which is worse than an empty record because it is not detectable.

**The frontier statement.** Two binary checks: that both halves — one thing the agent did well, one thing it did badly — are specific, and that both are drawn from this build rather than from the student's general impression of the tool. **Whether the judgement is correct is not assessed, and cannot be**, because the programme holds no ground truth for the frontier. That is the reason the statements are being collected at all.

**Which rules bind these three.** Specification rule 2 binds the frontier statement's first half: *faster than I could have done it* is a comparison against a counterfactual that was not run, and the qualification must appear in the same field as the claim rather than in a preamble, exactly as a confidence interval must travel with its estimate. Specification rule 3 binds the intervention record, which is precisely a discrimination the day performs — it separates the student who directed the work from the one who accepted it — so the specification requires its outcome and not its occurrence. Rater discipline rule 7 applies in its original form rather than its [WP-060](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-060.md) amendment, because a build is a completed action and not a prospective decision: the level is formed from the build and its records, and the oral defence tests it. **A student who cannot account in conversation for an instruction in their own prompt record has produced a record they did not write or did not read**, and that is the specific thing the defence looks for.

**The concession, which is substantial.** All three elements are self-reported, and instrumentation rule 1 says not to use self-report where an external observer is already present. An observer is present here: the agent session is loggable, and a full transcript would settle every one of these checks mechanically. **The design does not require the transcript.** A mandatory process log converts a record of reasoning into surveillance of exploration, and the error-clinic firewall — the single mechanism this architecture protects most strongly — depends on students believing that what they surface while getting something wrong is not evidence against them. Trading that for the verifiability of three artefact elements is a bad trade. It is nonetheless a rule-1 departure taken deliberately, and it makes the build record **the weakest evidence class in this architecture**. It is never used alone to form an entrustment level.

## The token economy

Adapted from the CMU exemplar, whose structure is verified at [research/01-course-structure/cmu-17-645-ml-in-production-ai-engineering.md](/research/01-course-structure/cmu-17-645-ml-in-production-ai-engineering.md).

- **Eight individual and eight team tokens per quarter.**
- **One token buys one day of extension.** Without a token, late work is not accepted rather than penalised, because a penalty reintroduces the point-arithmetic that specification grading exists to remove.
- **Three tokens buy a resubmission** of a failed specification, with the improvement credited in full for individual work and at a modest discount for team work.
- Tokens do not carry across quarters, so that hoarding does not substitute for planning.

## Dispositions

Integrity, reliability and humility are assessed **within** activities, never as activities. Each is evidenced by observed behaviour recorded in narrative:

- **Integrity** — reporting a result that undermines your own position; correcting a claim you previously made.
- **Reliability** — doing what you said, when you said, or saying early that you will not.
- **Humility** — acting on a correction; asking for help before the cost of not asking is incurred; declining to opine outside your evidence.

This layer supplies the professional-dispositions evidence required by the proposed accreditation criteria. See [Outcomes and Accreditation Map](/wiki/program/01-Outcomes-and-Accreditation-Map.md).

## Oral verification

Every entrustment occasion includes an oral component, and AI tools are prohibited only there. This is the safeguard that makes the permissive tool policy on produced work coherent: if the student cannot reconstruct and defend the reasoning live, the artefact does not certify them regardless of who or what produced it.

Oral verification is discharged as an ordinary rubric item within a bounded window, at office hours, rather than as a set-piece examination event. This is a deliberate anxiety-management decision and it has a cost: it consumes a large amount of staff time, and **the staffing feasibility of it is an unresolved open question.**

## The unassisted-work provision

Every day's produced work is built with an agent, which raises a risk the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) states plainly: **a student who has only ever built with an agent may be unable to build without one, and this programme cannot currently detect that.** The design's response is one structural measure and no exhortation.

**At [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md), [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md), a bounded portion of the assessed work is performed without agent assistance.** The three are already assessment-bearing days, closing M02, M09 and M18, so the provision sits at the end of the first quarter, at the midpoint and at exit without adding an occasion.

What it does:

- **It produces a comparison, not a level.** The assisted and unassisted work on the same day are reported side by side to the student and, in aggregate and unattributed, to the programme. No entrustment level derives from the unassisted portion, and none may. A single occasion observed once is exactly what rater discipline rule 1 forbids as a basis for entrustment, and the provision does not get an exemption from a rule the architecture applies everywhere else.
- **It is bounded and announced.** Students know which portion, in advance. Concealing it would make it a test of composure rather than of capability, and would put it in tension with the psychological-safety conditions the error clinic depends on.
- **It is not comparative across students.** No ranking, no distribution shown, consistent with the feedback position in [Learning Science Foundations](/wiki/program/02-Learning-Science-Foundations.md) that damage concentrates where feedback directs attention to the self.

**What its result changes**, since instrumentation rule 4 requires an instrument to say so and rule 6 requires a deferred response to name its occasion. A student whose unassisted work at S010 falls substantially below their assisted work is not sanctioned; the gap is carried forward and is read again at S045, and S045's is read again at S090. The response is the student's own, supported in advising, and the programme's response is at the level of the cohort: a cohort-wide widening gap is a finding about the fading schedule in the [Module Map](/wiki/program/07-Module-Map.md), not about the students. **Its reader**, per instrumentation rule 9, is the student and the programme's annual design review, and the annual review is the party that publishes what it did about it.

**Whether three occasions is enough is unknown, and is recorded as unknown.** Three points across three years can show a trajectory and cannot characterise one. The provision is a detector with a coarse resolution, chosen over a finer one because a finer one would mean routinely assessing students under conditions the programme has otherwise told them are not how the work is done.

## What assessment does *not* do here

- It does not rank students against each other. There are no leaderboards on assessed work.
- It does not assess perishable content. See the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md).
- **It does not assess the platform.** Every build is produced on a named Microsoft service, and the judgement is about the engineering rather than about the service. A specification that requires a student to recall a service name, a tier name or a portal path is rejected by the same check that rejects any other perishable requirement.
- **It does not assess prompting as a skill in itself.** The prompt record is evidence about how the work was directed; there is no rubric for a good prompt, because the programme has no defensible standard for one and inventing a house style would be a quality judgement the design cannot make.
- It does not treat the seminar day's pretest or error clinic as evidence.
- It does not produce a numeric grade point at any level.

## Programme self-instrumentation

The programme measures itself against the predictions made in the whitepapers.

| Instrument | What it tests | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Delayed cumulative retrieval performance | Whether the spacing schedule is producing retention rather than familiarity | Every phase 2 |
| Calibration gap | Predicted versus actual delayed performance, per student | Every phase 8, confronted later |
| Transfer tasks in unfamiliar surface structure | Whether whole-task variability is producing transfer | Once per module |
| Assessor divergence rate | Whether rater discipline is holding | Every entrustment occasion |
| Error-clinic disclosure rate | Whether psychological safety is real or nominal | Every phase 6 |
| Substrate incident behaviour | Whether taught practice survives contact with consequence | Continuous |
| Copilot prediction accuracy | Whether students' models of the agent's capability are calibrating, or whether the frontier is being assumed rather than mapped | Every phase 1, checked at phase 8 |
| Assisted-against-unassisted comparison | Whether capability is forming independently of the tool, and whether the fading schedule is doing anything | S010, S045, S090 |
| Intervention-record density | Whether builds are being directed or accepted. A cohort trend toward empty records is a finding about the complexity class, not about diligence | Every phase 5 |

Instrumentation rule 9 binds this table: **a design that instruments more than it can read has produced a literature, not a measurement.** The programme publishes, before each offering, which of these it will actually analyse that year. The three rows added with the toolchain are subject to that discipline and not exempt from it.

Where a cohort's data contradicts a whitepaper's §9 prediction, the seminar day is redesigned and the whitepaper amended with the contradicting evidence retained rather than deleted.

## Open questions

- **How many independent assessor-occasion pairs can staffing support per student?** Until this is answered, no judgement here is honestly summative.
- **Are the three entrusted activities the right three?** None has been written to the seven-part template; none has been ratified.
- **Can oral verification scale?** No source in this repository addresses it.
- **Does the error-clinic firewall survive contact with a genuinely serious disclosure?** The protocol says yes. It has not been tested.
- **Is a self-reported build record worth assessing at all?** The transcript that would verify it exists and is deliberately not required. If the records turn out to be uniformly compliant and uninformative, the elements should be withdrawn rather than tightened, because tightening them without the transcript can only add form.
