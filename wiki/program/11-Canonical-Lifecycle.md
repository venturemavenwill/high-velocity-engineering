# Canonical Lifecycle — the eight steps, and how this programme's nine stages map onto them

> **The lifecycle vocabulary is the vendor's. The stages, questions, modules and assessment occasions are this programme's.** This page holds the join between them, and records what the join cost.

## The canonical eight steps

GitHub and Microsoft publish **Hypervelocity Engineering** as an eight-step delivery lifecycle:

**Discover → Define → Decompose → Plan → Implement → Review → Deliver → Operate**

Beneath the eight runs a single arc labelled *continuous feedback and learning*, and its return edges are drawn into the **first four** steps rather than into the first alone. **The published lifecycle is a loop over its own front half, not a pipeline**, and this programme's arc is shaped the same way for the same reason.

The same source names four classes of **reusable accelerator asset** that compound across engagements — **instructions, prompts, agents and skills**. This programme builds all four and assesses three of them.

**What the source is, and what it is worth:** a first-party programme graphic, read in full on 2026-08-05, recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/hypervelocity-engineering-lifecycle.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hypervelocity-engineering-lifecycle.md). It is **evidence class 4 — grounded in vendor material, with its silence recorded.** It is authoritative for vocabulary and for nothing else. It reports no measurement, carries no date or version, states no exclusions, and no Microsoft Learn article carrying this sequence was located on the day of reading. **Its eight benefit claims license nothing and appear nowhere in this programme's teaching material.**

## The mapping

Nine stages, eight canonical names, one stage retained.

| Stage | Canonical name | Previously | The question the stage answers | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | [Discover](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md) | Qualify | Should this work happen at all, and can you say no? | **approximate** |
| **2** | [Define](/wiki/quarters/Q2.md) | Discover | What is the real problem, and who actually has it? | close |
| **3** | [Decompose](/wiki/quarters/Q3.md) | Frame | What can be built, against what that cannot move? | approximate |
| **4** | [Plan](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md) | Prove | Is it feasible, and how cheaply can you find out? | approximate |
| **5** | [Implement](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md) | Build | How is it delivered under real constraint? | **exact** |
| **6** | [Review](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md) | Assure | Is it safe, and how would you know? | close |
| **7** | [Deliver](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md) | Operate | Does it work in production, and what does that measurement license? | approximate |
| **8** | [Operate](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md) | Transfer | Can they run it without you? | close |
| **9** | [Compound](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md) | Compound | What is better next time — the system, the people, or the practice? | **no canonical equivalent** |

**Nothing moved except the labels.** No session changed stage, no module changed stage, no assessment occasion moved, and every stage answers the question it answered before. A rename that altered the arc would be a redesign wearing a vocabulary change as a disguise.

## Where the fit is only approximate, stated rather than hidden

Four joins are worth naming, because a reader who assumes a clean mapping will misread the stage they land in.

**Stage 1 is the sharpest.** The canonical lifecycle opens at Discover and assumes throughout that the work is happening; **there is no step in it at which the answer is *do not build this*.** This programme's first stage exists to make refusal available, and it keeps that content under the canonical name. The label is the vendor's; the refusal is not.

**Stage 3 and stage 4 carry more than their names suggest.** *Decompose* here includes the whole framing act — requirements a reader could falsify, the register of what can and cannot move, and a design stated at the scope of its decision. *Plan* here is a plan somebody funds, built out of spikes and prototypes rather than out of estimates, and its discipline is the cost clause: how cheaply can you find out.

**Stages 7 and 8 exchange the word *operate*, and this is the join most likely to confuse.** Stage 7 was called Operate and is now **Deliver**: it is where the system reaches production and the delivered result is measured for what it licenses. Stage 8 was called Transfer and is now **Operate**: its question is whether somebody else can run it without you. The canonical lifecycle does not say who operates. **This programme's answer is that the recipient does, and the whole of stage 8 is the argument for that reading.**

## Why nine stages and not eight

**Stage 9 keeps its name because the canonical lifecycle has no equivalent for it.** The published arc closes through a feedback edge rather than through a named step, and a feedback edge is not a place where anyone is accountable for anything. This programme makes it a stage because compounding — maturity, adoption, reusable starting points, feedback that reaches somewhere, and an honest register of what the engagement did not establish — is work somebody has to do, and work nobody is scheduled to do is work that does not happen.

**Two stages remain written past their sources, and now past two sources rather than one.** [Stage 1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md) infers a qualification practice from a corpus that records a single refusal and no declined engagement. [Stage 9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md) infers a cycle the corpus implies everywhere and states as a model nowhere. **The canonical lifecycle is silent in exactly those two places** — it has no refusal and no named compounding step.

That convergence is worth stating precisely, because it is easy to over-read. **Two sources being silent in the same two places is not evidence that this programme's answer is correct.** It is evidence that the gap is real and is not an artefact of one corpus. Both stages remain **class 3 — design reasoning with no external warrant** — and every session in them still says so.

## The rename is recorded, not erased

This programme's stages were named from the practice corpus and from its own design reasoning, and carried those names through ninety sessions before the canonical lifecycle was read. **The previous names are kept in the table above** for the same reason [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md) still records that it was once called *Withdraw*: a reader who cannot see the earlier position cannot evaluate the change.

**What the rename bought:** a reader arriving from the vendor's own material can locate themselves in this programme without a translation step.

**What it cost:** the word *Qualify* named the thing stage 1 is actually about, and *Discover* does not. The stage's question is doing that work now. **A label that is legible to more people and less precise about its own content is a real trade and not a free one**, and the honest statement is that it was made for legibility.

**What is perishable here.** Vendor lifecycle vocabulary decays in months and it decays quietly, because a renamed step breaks no build and fails no test. This page is verified before every offering, along with the rest of the [platform layer](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md).

| Durable claim | Canonical instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| A delivery lifecycle that cannot refuse work cannot be said to exercise judgement | The published eight steps, which open at Discover and contain no refusal |
| A loop that closes through a feedback arrow makes nobody accountable for closing it | The *continuous feedback and learning* arc, and its return edges into the first four steps |
| Reusable assets are the unit that compounds across engagements | Instructions, prompts, agents and skills as the four named asset classes |
| A named benefit with no instrument attached licenses nothing | The eight benefit tiles, none carrying a baseline, period or denominator |
