# S054 · Inference Laundering

**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md)

**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

This session traces one value through an assurance chain, and it is the most important hour in the programme.

**A stage is told to infer missing security properties from best practice. It is forbidden to mark those inferences in the table. It is told to resolve conflicts in the more secure direction. The provenance survives in a separate justification section — which the assembly step is explicitly instructed to leave out when it embeds the table into the delivered plan. And the review step then scores the plan by counting non-empty cells in that same table.**

Read that again slowly. **The chain fills in the answers and later grades itself on them.** A plan can score well on encryption because an earlier stage assumed encryption.

No step in that sequence is unreasonable. Every one of them is defensible in isolation and each was written by somebody solving a real problem. **The composition is the defect, and no participant can see it from where they stand.**

Answer these before instruction.

1. Point at a cell in your attribute table from [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md). Did you read that value or infer it? **How would a reader of your finished plan know?**
2. When your artefacts are assembled into one document, which section gets dropped? Who decided?
3. Something is going to assess your plan. What is it actually counting?
4. If a cell is non-empty because an earlier step filled it in, what has that assessment measured?
5. What is the **smallest** change that would break the loop?

**Item 5 is the assessed thinking and the rest of the session is preparation for it.** Anyone can propose a redesign. The skill is finding the one small change that makes a self-grading chain stop grading itself.

## What you must produce

**A provenance trace** of one value in your own artefacts, step by step, from the moment it was produced to the moment it was counted. Name the step where the marker is lost, and name the step that consumes the unmarked value as evidence.

**A two-defect statement.** The trace contains two distinct failures and they need separating, because they have different fixes.

- **Marker loss** — provenance existed and was discarded at a named step.
- **Optimistic resolution** — where two sources disagreed, the more secure reading was adopted, so the unmarked value is not merely unsourced but **biased in a known direction.**

**A double score.** Assess your plan twice — once counting every filled cell, once counting only cells you can trace to an observation. Report both. **The gap is the artefact.**

**The smallest change**, with its cost stated.

## What good looks like

- **The trace names steps and artefacts**, not tendencies. *The model made things up* is a failed trace.
- **The loss point is a single step**, identified precisely enough that somebody could go and change it.
- **The two defects are stated separately**, and the trace says which fix addresses which.
- **The double score shows a gap**, or the trace explains why your artefact has no inferred cells — which usually means you never marked any.
- **The smallest change is genuinely small**, and its cost is stated in the currency it is paid in: a wider table, a longer document, a slower assembly, a reader who has to read more.
- **Any self-verification checklist in your own artefact is classified as a configured control**, not an executed one.
- **Nothing in your submission claims your score means anything about the system.**

## How it goes wrong

- **The general complaint** — you write that generated content is unreliable and stop. *Warning sign:* your trace has no step numbers in it.
- **The big fix** — you redesign the chain. *Warning sign:* your fix requires more than one team to agree.
- **The single-defect reading** — you find the dropped section and miss the optimistic resolution, or the reverse. *Warning sign:* your fix is a single change and you have not asked what it leaves behind.
- **The clean re-score** — both scores are identical, and you conclude your artefact is sound. *Warning sign:* your provenance section was empty when you started.
- **The audit-trail costume** — your deliverable renders your own verification checklist, ticks and all. *Warning sign:* it looks like evidence and it is a self-report wearing a uniform.
- **The score as measurement** — you report a number, with a tier or a colour, and let the reader take it as posture. *Warning sign:* you would be uncomfortable writing *this measures the document, not the system* directly underneath it.

## Do it

**First, trace one value, not the class.** Pick a single cell. Establish where it came from. Follow it through every artefact your chain produces, and write down which artefact still carries its provenance and which does not. **The step where the answer changes is the defect's address.**

**Second, separate the two defects and say why they need separate fixes.** Restoring the dropped section fixes marker loss and leaves the optimistic bias intact: you would then have visible provenance on values that still lean systematically towards the more secure reading. Making conflict resolution neutral fixes the bias and leaves the values unmarked. **Neither fix is sufficient and neither is a superset of the other**, which is why a single change is nearly always the wrong answer to item 5 — and why finding one that is not is the exercise.

**Third, score twice.** Count every filled cell. Then count only what you can trace to an observation. **The second number is what your evidence actually supports and the first is what your artefact will be read as claiming.**

**Fourth, design the smallest change and price it.** Not a governance process. Not a review board. **A change to one artefact or one step**, whose cost you can state, and which somebody could make on a Tuesday. Then say what it does not fix.

**Fifth, turn the same instrument on yourself.** Find every self-verification checklist in your own artefacts — identifier uniqueness, referential integrity, arithmetic reconciliation. **These are prompt-engineering devices that improve output quality and they are not controls**, because the thing being checked and the thing doing the checking are the same. If any of them is rendered into your deliverable with its ticks intact, you have made a configured control wear the visual language of an audit trail, and **that single move is the whole session in miniature.**

Finally, notice what your assessment is actually testing. Most checks of this kind reduce to whether a mitigation somewhere mentions a thing — **so naming a technology you have not deployed scores full marks.** Any team that learns the rubric can raise the score without touching the system.

## Show it

Hand your assembled plan — the delivered version, not your working files — to somebody who has not seen your model.

**One question: which of these values did the author observe?**

If they cannot tell, the laundering happened in your artefact, and the fact that you can tell is not a defence. **You will not be reading it in eighteen months and the person who is will not have your working files.**

Then ask a second: **what would you now do differently having read this plan?** If the answer is nothing, the plan has produced a score and no work, which is [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md)'s subject arriving early.

## What this does not establish

**Nothing here measures how often inference laundering occurs, how much it distorts a score, or whether it has ever led to a system being harmed.** No such measurement exists in any source available to this programme.

**The chain this session traces has never been calibrated against human judgement in any form** — no gold standard, no held-out sample, no inter-rater statistic, no pilot, no statement that any produced score was ever compared with an expert's assessment of the same plan. **More than one review agent operates on the same document, on different scales, applying different working definitions of what counts as specific, without referencing each other and with no rule for what happens when they disagree.** Their self-verification verifies computation rather than validity. **The chain can prove it added its own numbers up correctly, and can prove nothing else about them.**

**Every weighting, band boundary, threshold and partial-credit fraction in that material is a designer's judgement rendered as a number, and not one of them cites a study, a measurement, a pilot or a rationale.** None is reproduced here. **The point is not that the numbers are wrong — it is that they are assertions presented in the visual grammar of observations**, and a score with a tier and a colour will be read as a measurement by every reader who did not build it.

**The smallest-change exercise is this programme's invention** and it has no warrant beyond the argument that a fix nobody can afford is not a fix.
