# S038 · Testing with People Who Are Not the Buyer

**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-038](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-038.md)

**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

The sponsor has seen the prototype and likes it. That is not evidence, and it is not evidence for a reason that has nothing to do with the sponsor's honesty: **they will not use it.**

The person who will use it has not seen it, may not know it exists, and in several organisations you will meet has been told not to worry about it.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. List everybody your idea touches. Mark each as user, buyer, budget holder, or blocker. Some are more than one.
2. Which of them can tell you that this would not help them? Which of them cannot?
3. Somebody says *this would be really useful*. What have you learned?
4. You cannot get access to a real user. A model can produce a plausible one. What is your position, and what is the best argument against it?
5. Who loses if this succeeds, and have they seen it?

**Item 3 has a specific answer and most learners do not have it.** You have learned that they were polite. A compliment is the commonest class of bad conversational data and it is evidentially empty.

## What you must produce

A **prototype test record** in which the populations are kept apart.

- **Who was tested**, by role rather than by name, with their relationship to the work: uses it, buys it, funds it, can block it, loses from it.
- **What each was and was not able to tell you.** A budget holder cannot tell you whether the workflow is right; a user cannot tell you whether it will be funded.
- **What each said**, with compliments marked as compliments and generic self-description marked as such.
- **What each did** — where the artefact was misread, where they reached for something that was not there, where they stopped.
- **Who was not tested**, and why, and what that costs the record.

## What good looks like

- **The buyer's response is recorded separately from the user's and is never merged into a finding.** Mixed answers across unlike populations are not one voice; they are several, and averaging them destroys the only thing they were good for.
- **At least one participant could have said this would not help them.** If nobody could have, the record says so, and that is the finding.
- **Compliments are marked and not counted.** They may be recorded — they are data about the conversation — but they do not appear in any conclusion.
- **Something was observed rather than only reported.** [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md)'s discipline transfers directly: watch them use it, on their own case, and note where they stop.
- **A person who loses from this has been identified**, and the record says whether they were shown it and what happened.
- **The record names what a positive result here would not license**, which is almost everything about funding, adoption and scale.

## How it goes wrong

- **The demonstration to the sponsor** — the only test was with the person who commissioned the work. *Warning sign:* your record has one population in it.
- **The merged segment** — users, buyers and blockers are aggregated under a customer heading. *Warning sign:* your finding says *they* and means four different groups.
- **The compliment as validation** — the record's evidence for the idea is that people liked it. *Warning sign:* your strongest quotation is an adjective.
- **The coached participant** — you explained the idea until they agreed, then recorded the agreement. *Warning sign:* you spoke more than they did.
- **The test that could not fail** — the artefact answered every question and offered no way to reject it. *Warning sign:* no participant said no to anything.
- **The simulated participant** — the user was unavailable, so a model produced one. *Warning sign:* nobody in your record has ever done the work.

## Do it

**Segment before you recruit.** A test run across a population that shares a job title and nothing else produces contradictions that look like disagreement about your idea and are actually disagreement about whose problem you asked about. **Pair each population with the place you can actually reach them**, because a segment you cannot reach is a segment you will substitute for.

**Then get the artefact away from you.** Hand it over and stop talking. The low-fidelity artefact from [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md) earns its place here: it can be altered while the person is holding it, which converts the conversation from an assessment of your work into a joint alteration of a rough thing. **That is the mechanism, and it is why a polished artefact gets you compliments — nobody redraws a finished thing.**

**Then repair the questions before you ask them.** [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) established this and the temptation returns in full force here: with an artefact in front of you it is nearly irresistible to ask whether it would help. **Ask instead about the last time they did the thing it addresses, what they did, what it cost them, and what they tried before.** The corpus's own instrument asks the customer to endorse the interviewer's solution in every section — the failure is not an accident of one question, it is the template — and you are holding the artefact version of the same defect.

**Then handle the substitution properly, because [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) refused it and refusal is not an argument.** The corpus proposes using a model's simulation of a user in place of a user when users are unavailable, and offers no validity discussion, in a collection that opens by warning against building for an imagined user. **This programme's position is that a simulated informant is not an informant**, and its reason is narrow: the whole case for observation is that practitioners cannot articulate their own tacit steps, and a model trained on articulations has access only to the articulated layer.

**Your job today is to build the best case against that position**, in writing, and it has real material in it — the practical alternative is frequently not a real informant but no informant at all, the refusal has no more warrant than the proposal it refuses, and a preference stated as a standard is exactly what this programme criticises the corpus for. **Then state the one circumstance in which you would accept the substitution, and exactly what you would write in the record when you did.** That last clause is the assessed part: a substitution recorded in a test record becomes an unrecorded one by the time the record is summarised, and naming the words that survive summarisation is the only defence available.

## Show it

Hand your record to a peer with the role column removed.

**Their job is to reconstruct which population each response came from.** If they cannot, you have written one voice, and the separation that makes the record useful exists only in your memory of the sessions.

Then ask them the harder question: **which response in here could have stopped the work?** If none could, the test could not fail, and it is [M07](/wiki/modules/M07.md)'s sentence returning — an investigation that could only have produced one answer was not an investigation.

## What this does not establish

Nothing here shows that testing with non-buyers improves products.

The handbook that supplies the vocabulary — compliments, generic self-description and unexamined requests as the three classes of bad data; commitment and advancement as the two things worth recording — **reports no controlled comparison, no measured effect and no validated threshold, and says so.** Its suggested conversation counts are the author's own experience and are explicitly not saturation rules. **Direction and mechanism only. No magnitude is asserted.**

The observation technique comes from a method that **states plainly that its effectiveness rests on practitioner experience rather than comparative evidence.**

**The corpus's defects are verified and its remedy is not.** That its question bank asks the customer to endorse the interviewer's solution in every block, ships specimen answers that narrow the response, contains no observation of any kind and never asks who loses if the thing succeeds — all recorded from a complete reading of access-controlled material **that no public reader can check.** That repairing those defects produces better tests is this programme's argument.

**The refusal of the simulated informant remains as unwarranted as the proposal it refuses**, and this session exists partly to make that symmetry uncomfortable rather than to resolve it.
