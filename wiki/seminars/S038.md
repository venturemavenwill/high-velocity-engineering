# S038 · Testing with People Who Are Not the Buyer

**Module:** [M08](/wiki/modules/M08.md) · **Quarter:** [Q4](/wiki/quarters/Q4.md) · **Complexity class:** 3
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** Copilot Studio published to a Teams channel for group use, against a declarative agent designed for one person — the initiation model decides who can tell you it is wrong
**Whitepaper:** [WP-038](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-038.md)
**Retrieves from:** S007, S016, S017, S036
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

The sponsor has seen the prototype and likes it. That is not evidence, and it is not evidence for a reason that has nothing to do with the sponsor's honesty: **they will not use it.**

They are also the person who spent political capital getting this approved, who described the problem to you in the first place, and who will be asked in six months whether it worked. **Every one of those facts makes their answer worse**, and none of them makes them dishonest. They are the buyer, and the buyer is the worst available test subject for the thing they are buying.

The person who will use it has not seen it, may not know it exists, and in several organisations you will meet has been told not to worry about it.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught. Nobody is going to help you.

1. List everybody your idea touches. Mark each as user, buyer, budget holder, or blocker. Some are more than one.
2. Which of them can tell you that this would not help them? Which of them cannot?
3. Somebody says *this would be really useful*. What have you learned?
4. You cannot get access to a real user. A model can produce a plausible one. What is your position, and what is the best argument against it?
5. Who loses if this succeeds, and have they seen it?

**Item 3 has a specific answer and most learners do not have it.** You have learned that they were polite. A compliment is the commonest class of bad conversational data and it is evidentially empty. Item 5 is the one almost nobody answers concretely, and the discovery instrument this stage's practice corpus supplies **never asks it at all.**

## What you must produce

1. **A segmentation** — each population paired with the place you can actually reach it, and the person who loses named as one of them.
2. **A declared initiation model** — how the artefact gets invoked, and the written statement of who that excludes from ever forming a view.
3. **An initiation log** — who was exposed, who invoked it, who did not, and what the non-invokers did instead.
4. **A prototype test record in which the populations are kept apart**: who was tested by role, what each was and was not able to tell you, what each said with compliments marked as compliments, what each **did**, and who was not tested and what that costs the record.
5. **The best case against this programme's refusal of the simulated informant**, in writing, with the one circumstance in which you would accept it and the exact words you would put in the record.

## The material

### The buyer's answers flatter twice, and only one of the reasons is politeness

Fitzpatrick's central claim is that people will lie to you — not maliciously, but because you asked a question whose answer they can see would please you ([research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md)). The repair is to ask about **past behaviour rather than future intention** and about **specifics rather than generalities**, and to treat three classes of response as contaminated: **compliments**, which are socially pleasant and evidentially empty; **fluff**, meaning generic self-description, future promises and hypotheticals; and **ideas**, meaning feature requests, which should be recorded but never obeyed. The repairs are **deflect, anchor and dig.**

That is the general problem. The buyer is the special case, and it is worse than the general one for a second and independent reason. **The buyer has already committed.** They have argued for this internally, they have a budget line with their name near it, and a finding that the idea is wrong is a finding about a decision they defended. **Politeness explains why a stranger flatters you. Investment explains why a sponsor does**, and investment does not decay when you tell them to be honest — the same source names this from the interviewer's side as the **Pathos Problem**, where exposing your own emotional stake causes people to protect you even when invited to be candid. The sponsor has the identical stake pointing the same way.

The source's third contribution is the one this session is built on. **Segmentation makes evidence comparable**, and mixed answers across unlike populations must not be aggregated as one customer voice — because a set of contradictions that looks like a divided market is frequently one conversation each with several different markets. **Customer slicing** alternates among organisational type, motivation, current behaviour and location, and the output is a **who-where pair**: a specific kind of person joined to the place or behaviour through which they can actually be reached. And the coverage requirement is explicit: users, buyers, budget owners, administrators, partners, and anybody able to block adoption. **Seniority is not a substitute for representativeness.**

**The limits are heavy and travel with every use.** It is a founder-oriented practitioner handbook reporting **no controlled comparison, no sampling method, no measured effect and no validated threshold**; its conversation counts are the author's own experience and explicitly not saturation rules; and a set of conversations can expose mechanism and meaning but **establishes no prevalence**, so repeated anecdotes must not become frequency claims. The local copy carries **no publisher, year, edition or ISBN** — its package identifier is an all-zero placeholder — so it cannot be cited by edition, and the note records that explicitly.

### Recruiting is data, and a segment you cannot reach is one you will substitute for

Portigal makes access part of the method rather than a logistics problem before it. **A screener must establish both the participant and the context** — a superficially eligible person may lack the relevant device, task, environment, authority or relationship at the interview site, and a study that discovers this in the room has spent the session. **Difficulty finding the population is itself data** about the team's conception of the market ([research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md](/research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md)).

Two of his findings bear directly on what you are about to do this afternoon. **A participant who appears wrongly recruited usually reveals ambiguity in the screener or in your own category** rather than a mistake by the recruiter — complete the session, learn what is available, then revise the criteria. And **multiple participants at once introduce conformity and power effects**, so some topics require separate sessions or separate portions of one. **You are about to publish an artefact into a channel where a manager and their reports can all see each other's messages**, and that is the multiple-participant condition in its purest form.

**Limits: 2013 first edition only, a practitioner synthesis with no controlled comparison of techniques, no sampling framework and no measured causal effect.**

### Watch rather than ask, because people cannot narrate what they do

The contextual-design method supplies the reason observation is not merely a nicer form of interviewing. People know their own activities intimately but **do not ordinarily articulate their structure**: familiar action becomes tacit, retrospective accounts omit detail, general questions invite general answers, and **requests for features elicit solutions shaped by the tools people already know** ([research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md)).

The stance is master and apprentice, disciplined by four principles — **context**, staying close to ongoing action and specific past events; **partnership**, investigating with the person rather than receiving a tour; **interpretation**, stating your emerging reading aloud so it can be corrected while the context is present; and **focus**, using the project's concern to choose what to pursue while treating surprises as corrections to that focus. And the governing rule for the artefact, which is the same one [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md) ended on: **the goal is to discover failure, not to collect approval.**

One further mechanism transfers, and it is the reason your record is a team artefact rather than a personal note. If one person attends every conversation and reports what customers supposedly said, that person becomes a **learning bottleneck** and can use customer authority to direct engineering work. The remedy in both sources is the same: shared preparation, first-hand exposure for the people making decisions, retrievable notes separating verbatim language from interpretation, and prompt review. **Limits: a method book whose effectiveness rests on practitioner experience rather than comparative evidence.**

### Agreement conceals divergence, which is why populations are never merged

There is a finding from the assessment literature that transfers to this session exactly, and it is more uncomfortable than it first looks.

When assessors judged the same portfolios, they **reached similar final judgements while differing in their information-processing approaches and in the reasoning behind them** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). Convergence on the outcome concealed complete divergence in why. The same work found that judges relied on **narrative** to form the judgement and used scores only to confirm impressions already formed from prose.

Apply that to your test record. Two participants both say the idea would help. If you record two ticks, you have recorded agreement. If you record what each of them said, you may find one meant *this removes a handover I hate* and the other meant *this makes the case for the headcount I have been refused* — **and those two answers point at different products.** A merged finding destroys the only information the responses contained. **This is the same argument as the segmentation rule, arriving from a literature that measured it on human judges rather than asserting it.**

**Limits: two studies from medical education at one institution, with a specific portfolio type and competency framework; transfer to prototype testing is an argument rather than a given, and it is stated here as one.**

### The initiation model is a recruitment instrument you did not know you had chosen

This is where the platform stops being a delivery detail and becomes the sampling frame.

Microsoft's guidance splits agents on an axis most teams never discuss. **Declarative agents** are **"designed to be used by individuals"**, are **"limited to Copilot's orchestrator and models"**, do **not** support proactive interactions and **"rely on user-initiated interactions"**, are **"integrated into Microsoft 365 apps"** and are hosted there. **Custom engine agents** **"support both individual use and group collaboration"** and **"enable agents to trigger actions automatically, even without direct user input"**. Among the published triggers for choosing the second is group productivity, where **"multiple users can collaborate with or benefit from the same agent in a Teams channel or meeting"** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)).

Read that as a statement about evidence rather than about architecture. **A user-initiated, individually-scoped agent can only be evaluated by somebody who knows it exists and chooses to invoke it.** That is a sampling frame, and it is a biased one in a specific and predictable direction: the people who invoke a new tool in their first week are the people already disposed towards it. **The person most likely to tell you the idea is wrong is the person who will never type its name.** You did not recruit that bias. You inherited it from a build-path decision, probably made for reasons of cost.

Publishing the same artefact into a Teams channel inverts the exposure. The channel's membership becomes your sample, including people who did not opt in, including the person who loses if this succeeds — and including a manager and their reports in the same thread, which is the conformity condition the interviewing literature warns about. **Neither arrangement is correct. They are two different instruments, and this session's claim is that you must choose one deliberately and write down who it silences.**

Two further published facts constrain the exercise honestly. Copilot Studio is **"designed for small-scale deployments, such as department-level solutions"** — which is roughly the size of a channel, so this is a defensible platform for a test and not for a rollout. And **"these options aren't mutually exclusive"**, which means the real decision is frequently per component, and a group-initiated surface can front an individually-scoped component.

**The limits go with every use.** First-party selection guidance with definitional authority; **no adoption, cost, failure-rate or outcome data**; one vendor's options compared against each other; read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, with several comparison tables truncated; and **highly perishable** — the initiation boundaries described here are exactly the kind of thing that moves between releases.

### The instrument this stage inherits, and the question it never asks

The practice corpus behind this stage supplies a large discovery question bank, and it is the item that most repays comparison against the interviewing literature ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). It does several things well — a mandatory breadth-first block before any deep dive, prior-attempt questions, threshold questions, and a closing pair in every section asking what good would look like and how improvement would be measured.

And it has four diagnosable failures, all of which you are about to be tempted by with an artefact in your hands. **It asks the customer to endorse the interviewer's own solution category, in every block** — the central prohibition in the question-quality literature, present here as the template rather than as an occasional slip, and asked by the person who has come to deliver that solution. **Its specimen answers lead the witness**, because appending examples narrows the response set. **It contains no observation of any kind** — nobody is asked to demonstrate anything while being watched. And it provides **no consolidation**, offering instead a lookup table that converts a single utterance from a single person into a prescribed intervention.

**It also never asks who loses if the thing succeeds**, in a body of commercial material that elsewhere makes an explicit claim about headcount. That absence is the reason opening item 5 exists.

The same corpus proposes **using a model's simulation of a user in place of a user** when users are unavailable, and offers no validity discussion at all — in a collection whose opening warning is against building for an imagined user. [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) refused the substitution and recorded that the refusal was an argument rather than a finding. **This session does not repeat the refusal; it makes you argue against it**, because a prohibition that has never met its strongest objection is a preference wearing a rule's clothing. The strongest objection has real material in it: the practical alternative is frequently not a real informant but **no** informant, and the refusal has no more external warrant than the proposal it refuses.

What this programme can say is narrow and worth stating precisely. The whole case for observation is that practitioners cannot articulate their own tacit steps. **A model trained on articulations has access only to the articulated layer**, which is the layer the method exists because you cannot trust. That is an argument about what is representable, not a measurement, and the confident-wrong-output problem makes it worse rather than better: outside its capability boundary a model does not refuse, it produces a plausible informant ([research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md](/research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md)). **That note rests on an abstract and licenses direction only.**

## The workflow

**Publish the artefact where a group can see it, then watch who does not use it.**

**Step 1 — segment, then pair each segment with a place.** Write the who-where pairs before you approach anybody. A segment you cannot reach is a segment you will silently substitute for, and the substitute will be whoever answers your messages.

**Step 2 — declare the initiation model and write what it excludes.** One sentence: *this artefact is invoked by an individual who chooses to, therefore my sample is people already disposed towards it, therefore the following named person will never form a view.* Do this before publishing.

**Step 3 — publish the [S036](/wiki/seminars/S036.md) Copilot Studio artefact to exactly one Teams channel whose membership you can enumerate.** One channel, not the tenant. You need the membership list because it is your sampling frame, and because the difference between exposed and engaged is the finding.

**Step 4 — say nothing for a working day.** No announcement beyond the minimum consent notice, no walkthrough, no encouragement. **You are measuring invocation, and an invocation you prompted is not one.**

**Step 5 — log initiation, including the non-events.** Who was exposed, who invoked, who did not, and what the non-invokers did instead — asked a colleague, used the old route, ignored it. **The people who did not invoke it are your most valuable population and they will not appear in any telemetry as anything other than absence.**

**Step 6 — run sessions in which you hand it over and stop talking.** Ask for the last time they did the thing it addresses, what they did, what it cost them, and what they tried before. **Do not ask whether it would help.** With an artefact in front of you that question is nearly irresistible, and it is the corpus's own defect in its most concentrated form.

**Step 7 — record the populations apart, in a file, with compliments marked.**

```yaml
# docs/prototypes/shift-handover/test-record.yaml
initiation_model:
  chosen: "group-initiated — published to one Teams channel"
  excludes: >
    Nobody outside the channel. But channel membership includes the line manager,
    so junior responses are given in front of their manager. Conformity risk is
    NOT controlled here; two sessions below were run separately for that reason.
  counterfactual: >
    An individually-scoped, user-initiated agent would have sampled only people
    who chose to type its name. P4 would never have appeared in this record.

exposure:
  enumerated_members: "channel roster, captured 2026-07-31"
  invoked: [P1, P2, P5]
  did_not_invoke: [P3, P4, P6]
  what_non_invokers_did_instead:
    P3: "asked P1 in a direct message instead — did not want to be seen trying it"
    P4: "used the paper log; said the channel is for announcements"
    P6: "unaware it existed despite being a member"

participants:
  - id: P1
    relation: [buyer, budget_holder]
    can_tell_me: ["whether it gets funded", "whether it survives procurement"]
    cannot_tell_me: ["whether the workflow is right", "whether the log has the causal detail"]
    said: "This is exactly what we need."
    marked: COMPLIMENT              # recorded; MUST NOT appear in any conclusion
    did: "Did not open the shift log to check the output against it."
  - id: P4
    relation: [user, loses_if_it_succeeds]
    can_tell_me: ["whether the log contains what the next shift needs"]
    cannot_tell_me: ["whether it gets funded"]
    said: "The log is where you put what you can prove. The rest you say out loud."
    marked: BEHAVIOURAL             # about a past event, not about the artefact
    did: "Reached for a page of the paper log that has no digital equivalent."
    could_have_stopped_the_work: true

not_tested:
  - who: "night-shift agency staff"
    why: "not in the channel; not in the directory"
    cost_to_the_record: >
      The population most affected is entirely absent. Any claim about adoption
      in this record excludes the people who do most of the handovers.

simulated_informants_used: none      # if any, the exact words that must survive summarisation go here
```

**Step 8 — attack your own record with the model, twice.** First: **build the best case against this programme's refusal of the simulated informant**, then have the model strengthen it, then state the one circumstance in which you would accept the substitution and the exact words you would write in the record. Second: ask the model to **produce a participant response your record could not distinguish from a real one.** If it can, your record's defences are the field labels rather than anything you wrote in them, and that is worth knowing before somebody summarises it.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** That exposure and engagement are different populations, and that the gap between them is legible in a single day. And that the initiation model — a decision made for cost — selected who was allowed to disagree with you, which is a sentence nobody believes until they have the roster in one column and the invocation log in the other.

## What good looks like

- **The initiation model is declared and its exclusions are named**, with a specific person who would never have formed a view under the alternative.
- **Exposure and engagement are recorded separately**, and the non-invokers are treated as a population rather than as missing data.
- **The buyer's response is recorded separately from the user's and is never merged into a finding.** Mixed answers across unlike populations are not one voice; they are several, and averaging them destroys the only thing they were good for.
- **At least one participant could have said this would not help them.** If nobody could have, the record says so, and that is the finding.
- **Compliments are marked and not counted.** They may be recorded — they are data about the conversation — but they do not appear in any conclusion.
- **Something was observed rather than only reported.** [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md)'s discipline transfers directly: watch them use it, on their own case, and note where they stop.
- **A person who loses from this has been identified**, and the record says whether they were shown it and what happened.
- **The record names what a positive result here would not license**, which is almost everything about funding, adoption and scale.

## How it goes wrong

- **The demonstration to the sponsor** — the only test was with the person who commissioned the work. *Warning sign:* your record has one population in it.
- **The self-selecting sample** — everyone who formed a view chose to invoke it, so you tested enthusiasm. *Warning sign:* your record has no row for anybody who did not use it.
- **The channel as a room with a manager in it** — junior responses were given where their line manager could read them. *Warning sign:* the most junior participant agreed with the most senior one, in public, quickly.
- **The merged segment** — users, buyers and blockers are aggregated under a customer heading. *Warning sign:* your finding says *they* and means four different groups.
- **The compliment as validation** — the record's evidence for the idea is that people liked it. *Warning sign:* your strongest quotation is an adjective.
- **The coached participant** — you explained the idea until they agreed, then recorded the agreement. *Warning sign:* you spoke more than they did.
- **The test that could not fail** — the artefact answered every question and offered no way to reject it. *Warning sign:* no participant said no to anything.
- **The simulated participant** — the user was unavailable, so a model produced one. *Warning sign:* nobody in your record has ever done the work.

## Exercises

**None of these is assessed.** The test record is carried into [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md), where a response becomes a commitment, and into [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md), where the populations must still be separated in the assembled pack.

**1 · Sort six statements into evidence and noise.** For each, say what it is data about.

- *This would be really useful.* · *We usually do it manually.* · *Could you add a filter for site?* · *Last Tuesday I rang the day shift because the log said nothing.* · *I'd definitely use this every day.* · *I'd have to check with compliance, they blocked the last one.*

> **Answer key.** Compliment · fluff · idea · **behavioural, and the only one worth anything on its own** · fluff dressed as commitment · **behavioural about a blocker, and the most valuable line in the set** because it names a person with authority to stop the work and a precedent for using it. Repairs: **deflect** the compliment and return to specifics; **anchor** the fluff to a particular past episode; **dig** beneath the request for the job it serves rather than accepting or refusing it. **If you marked the fifth as evidence because it contains the word *daily*, note that a frequency claim about a hypothetical future is still a hypothetical.**

**2 · Run the initiation counterfactual on the platform.** Using the comparison guidance, state what your artefact could and could not do under each initiation model, and name one person whose view you would have lost under the other one.

> **What you should find.** The documentation states that declarative agents are designed for individuals and **rely on user-initiated interactions**, with no proactive capability, while custom engine agents support group collaboration and can trigger without direct user input, with a Teams channel or meeting given as the group-productivity example. **The consequence is evidential, not architectural: a user-initiated agent samples people who already wanted it.** If you could not name a lost person, your channel and your opt-in population were the same people, and the exposure log was not doing any work. **If the boundary you found no longer matches the documentation, record that** — this material is marked highly perishable and initiation boundaries move.

**3 · Go back to your own item 3, then break it.** Find what you wrote when somebody said *this would be really useful*. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference.

> **Criterion.** A passing answer says **what the compliment is evidence about** — the conversation, the relationship, the person's willingness to be pleasant — and then names the next move, which is to deflect and return to a specific past episode. **The commonest failing answer is a hedge**: *it's weak evidence, but it's something.* It is not something. Evidence that cannot discriminate between a good idea and a bad one is not weak evidence; it has zero discriminating power, and treating it as weak-but-positive is how a record accumulates a favourable impression made entirely of politeness. **If your answer kept the compliment in the plus column at any weight, that is the difference to name.**
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**4 · Find two people who agreed for incompatible reasons.** From your record, take two positive responses from different populations and write what each of them actually meant.

> **What to notice.** If both said yes and you cannot reconstruct two different reasons, either you did not ask why or you merged them at the point of writing. The assessment literature found exactly this shape in trained judges: **similar final verdicts resting on entirely different reasoning**, invisible in the outcome and visible only in the prose. **A record of ticks cannot contain this finding at all**, which is the argument for narrative over scores in a sentence.

**5 · Argue against this programme.** Write the strongest case for the simulated informant, then state your one acceptable circumstance and the exact words you would put in the record.

> **Scoring criterion.** The strong case has three parts and most learners find two: that the alternative is frequently **no** informant rather than a real one; that the refusal has **no more external warrant than the proposal it refuses**, which is a fair charge against this programme; and — the one usually missed — that a simulated informant can legitimately be used to **generate questions** rather than to supply answers, which is a different claim and a much better one. **The assessed part is the words you would write in the record**, because a substitution noted in a test record becomes an unrecorded one by the time somebody summarises it, and naming the sentence that survives summarisation is the only available defence.

**6 · Hand the record over with the role column removed.** Give it to a peer and ask them to reconstruct which population each response came from. Then ask them which response could have stopped the work.

> **What to notice.** If they cannot reconstruct the populations, you have written one voice and the separation exists only in your memory of the sessions. If no response could have stopped the work, the test could not fail — and [M07](/wiki/modules/M07.md)'s sentence returns: an investigation that could only have produced one answer was not an investigation.

## What this does not establish

Nothing here shows that testing with non-buyers improves products.

The handbook that supplies the vocabulary — compliments, generic self-description and unexamined requests as the three classes of bad data; segmentation as the precondition for interpretable evidence; commitment and advancement as the two things worth recording — **reports no controlled comparison, no measured effect and no validated threshold, and says so.** Its suggested conversation counts are the author's own experience and are explicitly not saturation rules. Its local copy carries **no publisher, year or edition**. **Direction and mechanism only. No magnitude is asserted.**

The interviewing and observation techniques come from method books whose **effectiveness rests on practitioner experience rather than comparative evidence**, one of them read as a **2013 first edition only**. A set of conversations can expose mechanism and meaning; it **establishes no prevalence**, so nothing in your record licenses a claim about how common anything is.

**The assessment finding is borrowed across a large gap.** It comes from two studies in medical education at a single institution, with a specific portfolio type and competency framework, using think-aloud methods that by the authors' own account cannot capture fast or automatic processing and risk participants constructing plausible explanations for the researcher. **That agreement conceals divergent reasoning in prototype testing is this programme's transfer of that finding, not the finding itself.**

**The platform layer establishes the initiation boundary and nothing about its consequences.** The declarative and custom-engine distinctions, the group-collaboration trigger and the department-level envelope are first-party selection guidance: definitional authority, **no adoption, cost, failure-rate or outcome data**, one vendor compared against itself, read through search excerpts with several tables truncated, and **highly perishable**. **The claim that the initiation model functions as a recruitment instrument is this design's argument** — the documentation says nothing about who a build path silences, because that is not what it is for.

**The corpus's defects are verified and its remedy is not.** That its question bank asks the customer to endorse the interviewer's solution in every block, ships specimen answers that narrow the response, contains no observation of any kind, provides no consolidation and never asks who loses if the thing succeeds — all recorded from a complete reading of access-controlled material **that no public reader can check.** That repairing those defects produces better tests is this programme's argument.

**The refusal of the simulated informant remains as unwarranted as the proposal it refuses**, and this session exists partly to make that symmetry uncomfortable rather than to resolve it. The jagged-frontier note that sharpens it **rests on an abstract**, licenses direction only, and no number from it appears here.
