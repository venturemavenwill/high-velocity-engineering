# S024 · The Decision Record

**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** An agent configuration corpus read at a pinned commit — configuration as a decision record, citable only with its version, package, host and model attached
**Whitepaper:** [WP-024](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-024.md)
**Retrieves from:** S021, S022, S023
**Entry state:** either

## The situation

Some months from now, an engineer who was not here will look at something in this system and ask why it was done that way.

The people who know will have moved on. What is left will be the code, which says what was done and never says what was rejected.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. Name a decision already taken in this engagement that nobody wrote down.
2. Who took it? Not which team — **who.**
3. What would have to change for it to be reversed?
4. What did you decide against, and what did that cost you?
5. Where would a new engineer look for the answer, and would they find it there?

**Item 1 is easy and item 2 is not**, and the gap between them is the session. Decisions in delivery are mostly taken by accretion: somebody tried something, it worked well enough, nobody objected, and by the time it was a decision it had no author.

## What you must produce

A **decision record** for each architecturally significant decision your requirement set has already forced, using the published field list: an ascending number and title, a date, a status from a fixed vocabulary, a context, a decision, and consequences. Stored with the code. Page read 2026-07-30.

Plus a **decision log** that indexes them: a one-sentence summary, the date, what else was considered, a short rationale, a link to the record, who decided, and the work item it relates to.

**One record in your set must be superseded by another**, and the superseding record must name what it replaced.

## The material

### Decisions are not taken; they accrete

Somebody tries something under time pressure. It works well enough. Nobody objects, because objecting requires an alternative and nobody has one ready. Within weeks the thing is load-bearing, and by the time anyone calls it a decision it has no author, no rejected alternative and no stated cost.

**It cannot be revisited because there is nothing to revisit.** A decision with no recorded reasoning can only be reversed by re-deriving the whole situation, which nobody has time for, so it is not reversed. This is why item 2 of the opening is hard and item 1 is easy: naming a decision is trivial and naming its author is often impossible.

### The specification, and the two properties that do the work

An openly published engineering playbook from a customer-embedded engineering organisation supplies the field list ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md)). Page read 2026-07-30. An ascending number and a title; a date; a status drawn from a fixed vocabulary of proposed, accepted, deprecated and superseded; a context that must be "a value-neutral description of the forces at play"; a decision whose sentence begins "We will…" or "We have agreed to…"; and consequences. Stored with the code, and **"added as pull request in the proposed status".** Where several are written in parallel, the collision rule is to give each record the identifier of the work item it relates to.

**The field list is not what makes this specification good. Two properties are.**

**The record enters review as a proposal, before the thing is built.** That is a claim about timing rather than content, and it is the difference between reasoning that could still change something and a minute of a decision already taken. A record written after the code is not a decision record; it is an explanation.

**The context section must be value-neutral.** A reader given the context alone cannot tell which way the decision went. This is genuinely difficult to produce, it is mechanically checkable by handing the section to somebody, and it catches the commonest defect in written engineering judgement: **reasoning composed after the conclusion, to fit it.**

**The playbook's limits.** Its pages carry individual footer dates spanning several years and it has no single currency; pin every claim to a page and that page's own date. It offers no comparative evidence that any of its practices outperform alternatives. Roughly half of it is substantive and the rest is recipe, stub or link list, with nothing in the navigation distinguishing them. **And it reaches for a magnitude in several places and warrants it in none — a cost claim about code review, a velocity claim about pairing, a defect-cost multiple attributed through a link that can no longer be followed, and vendor efficiency figures with unfalsifiably wide bands. None of the four is reproduced here or anywhere in this wiki.**

### A record that lists only the chosen option is a description, not a decision

This is the session's central claim and it is worth stating flatly. **The value of a decision record is in what it says was rejected, and why.**

A record naming only the chosen option tells a later reader what was done, which the code already tells them. What the code cannot tell them is the shape of the space the decision was made in: which alternatives were live, which were considered and dismissed, which were never available, and what the chosen option cost. **A reader who does not know what was rejected cannot tell a considered decision from an unexamined default**, and the two are indistinguishable in the running system.

The consequences section is where this becomes checkable. **Every real decision costs something.** A consequences section that is a list of benefits is an advertisement, and the reader six months from now cannot use it — not because it is dishonest, but because it does not tell them what they are now paying, which is exactly what they need to know before changing anything.

The related failure is the record nobody could have disagreed with: a documented choice that was never genuinely open. That is not a decision record; it is a note. Its warning sign is an alternatives section listing options nobody proposed.

### Assessors anchor early, which is why value-neutrality has to be a rule

The requirement that the context reveal nothing is usually justified as good style. It has a better warrant than that, and it comes from research on how people actually judge complex written evidence.

Studies of assessors reading portfolios and thinking aloud found a three-phase cyclical process — acquiring, organising and integrating evidence — and then a return to the remaining material to look for confirming or disconfirming evidence. The finding that matters here is what happened next: **"assessors were inclined to stick to their initial judgments even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence."** Their final judgements were more elaborate and detailed than their preliminary ones but not substantially different ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). A companion study reports that most raters began building an impression of the person the moment they began observing.

**Read that as a claim about your reader.** A context section that leaks its conclusion in the first sentence does not merely fail a style test; it sets the reader's judgement before they have read the forces, and the rest of the record then elaborates a conclusion rather than being weighed against it. **A value-neutral context is a defence against a reader's anchoring, not a courtesy.**

The same literature supplies the reason a decision record is prose rather than a score: **narrative was what assessors actually used to form judgements, and numerical ratings were used only to confirm impressions already formed from the prose.**

**Its limits, and they are real.** This is a clinical-education literature, studied through think-aloud protocols on mock portfolios in a single institution and one portfolio type. Think-aloud methods cannot capture fast or automatic processing and carry a risk that participants confabulate explanations for the researcher. The reported magnitudes in that note concern rater variance in performance ratings and are not reproduced here. **The transfer to engineering decision records is this repository's, not the source's.**

### Configuration is a decision record, and it decays whether or not you notice

Now the part that makes this session about engineering rather than about documents.

An openly published corpus of agent configuration — instructions, prompts, agent definitions and skills, authored, versioned, reviewed, packaged and distributed like any other artefact — is available and was read at a pinned commit ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)). **Its organising claim is that a coding agent's behaviour is an engineering artefact**, and everything else in it follows from taking that seriously.

Three of its properties are decision records wearing different clothes.

**Maturity and retirement are expressed through membership and metadata rather than inferred from a file's age.** That is a status vocabulary, exercised.

**Durable state on disk is declared authoritative over session memory**, with stable identifiers so that a decision made in one phase can be found in a later one by name. The stated purpose is provenance and restartability. **It establishes that the record exists and is addressable. It establishes nothing about whether the decisions recorded in it were correct**, and the corpus does not claim otherwise.

**Execution completion and acceptance outcome are treated as different results.** A phase that ran to completion has not thereby been accepted. That distinction is exactly the one a consequences section makes and a benefits list does not.

**Now the discipline this repository applies to it, which is the teachable instance.** That corpus is cited here only with its commit, its published package version, the host it ran on and the model it ran against — because every one of those changes what the artefacts do, and an artefact is inert without a host that loads it. **Pinning does not slow the decay. It makes the decay visible.** A citation with a commit attached will one day be obviously stale; a citation without one will simply be quietly wrong, and nothing in it will say so. That is the same property a dated, superseded decision record has, and it is why the status vocabulary matters more than the field list.

**Its limits, and they are severe.** Perishability is extreme: file counts, paths, plugin names, package version and collection membership all changed during the period the reading describes. Host and model dependence is total. The repository contains its own marketing — benefit vocabulary appears in its prose without support — and **no committed study, baseline, dataset or outcome corpus was found at the revision read, by its own evaluation machinery or anybody else's.** Its own documentation states that its behavioural graders test output shape rather than correctness, and that some checks are advisory rather than authoritative. At the pinned revision one build check was failing, which the reading records rather than rounding away. **A grader is not a result**, and this corpus is the clearest available worked example of the difference at scale.

### The status vocabulary exists to be exercised

The published specification's own examples do not exercise it: **neither of its worked records ever reaches the superseded state.** A status vocabulary nobody uses is decoration, and a set in which every record is accepted has adopted a field with no content.

That is why this session requires one record to be superseded by another that names it, and why the superseded record stays rather than being edited. **A record you can no longer read is a record you cannot learn from**, and the whole point of the apparatus is that the reasoning outlives the reasoner.

## The workflow

**Read a piece of agent configuration as a decision record, find what it rejected, and write the record its author did not.**

Steps 1 to 5 need only a browser and a text editor. Step 6 needs an agent. **Nothing here requires you to run the corpus**, and you should not try to evaluate it by running it — that is the mistake the session is about.

**Step 1 — pin the corpus before you read a line of it.** Record the commit, the published package version, the host you would run it on and the model you would run it against. **A reading with none of those attached is not reproducible and is not a citation.**

**Step 2 — pick one instructions file or one agent definition and read it as a decision.** Every constraint in it forecloses something. Ask of each: what would this have permitted if it were absent, and who decided that was unacceptable?

**Step 3 — write down what the file does not say.** The rejected alternative is almost never in the artefact. Configuration records the winner of an argument and discards the argument, which is precisely the failure this session exists to correct — **and it is the same failure in a file that a benefits-only consequences section is in a document.**

**Step 4 — write the decision record the configuration implies**, in the published field list, with a context you would fail if you could guess the outcome from it.

```markdown
# 0007 — Untrusted fetched content is treated as data, never as instruction

Date: 2026-07-31
Status: Proposed
Work item: <the identifier of the item this record relates to>

## Context

Agents in this workflow read material they did not author: fetched pages,
uploaded documents, source repositories, tool output and issue bodies. That
material can contain text addressed to the agent. Two positions are available.
Treating fetched material as instruction lets an agent follow guidance that
is genuinely useful and is often authored for exactly that purpose. Treating
it as data means an agent may ignore an instruction its operator wanted it to
follow, and the operator will not be told. Whether either position holds in
practice depends on the host attaching the constraint and the model complying
with it, and neither can be guaranteed by the file that states it.

## Decision

We will treat all fetched, uploaded and tool-returned content as data. An
embedded instruction is reported to the operator and is not obeyed.

## Consequences

Useful embedded guidance is lost, and operators must supply it themselves.
The constraint is a declaration and not an enforcement: it holds only where
the host attaches it and the model complies, and we have no measurement of
either. Reported instructions add operator work on every run that meets one.

## Provenance of the reading behind this record

Corpus commit: <sha> · package version: <x.y.z> · host: <client and version>
· model: <name and version> · read on: <date>
```

**Step 5 — hand the context section, alone and unlabelled, to one person and ask one question: which way did this decision go?** If they can tell, the context is advocacy and you rewrite it. If they cannot tell but can name the forces the decision had to balance, it is a context section and it will still be useful when nobody remembers the meeting.

**Step 6 — attack your own record with the agent.** Give it the context section only, with one instruction: *argue for the option this context rejects, using only what the context says.* Then give it the whole record and ask a second question: *what does this record claim that its own consequences section does not pay for?*

**A context that cannot support an argument for the other side is not value-neutral**, whatever a human reader guessed. And a record whose consequences section survives the second question without a concession has recorded a preference.

**Step 7 — supersede one of your records.** Write the replacement, name what it supersedes, and change the status of the original rather than editing it. Then write one sentence saying what changed in the world — not in your mind — that made the original wrong.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** That a configuration file is a decision record with the reasoning deleted, and that you can tell exactly what was deleted by trying to reconstruct it. It also makes the pinning discipline physical: you will notice, while writing the provenance line, how many things have to be true for your reading to mean anything — and every one of them will be false within a year.

## What good looks like

- **The context is value-neutral.** A reader given the context alone cannot tell which way the decision went. This is the session's central requirement and it is mechanically checkable: hand the section to somebody and ask them to guess.
- **The decision sentence is a commitment**, phrased as something the team will do or has agreed to do. Not a preference, not an assessment, not a recommendation. A record whose decision section says the team *prefers* an approach has recorded an opinion.
- **The consequences include something that got worse.** Every real decision costs something. A consequences section that is a list of benefits is an advertisement, and the reader six months from now will not be able to use it.
- **The record entered review as a proposal**, before the thing was built, so that reviewing it could still change the outcome. A record written after the code is a minute, not a decision.
- **One record is superseded, and the superseding record names it.** The status vocabulary exists to be exercised. If nothing in your set ever reaches that state, you have adopted a field with no content.
- **Each record is tied to the work it came from**, so that parallel authorship does not collide and so that a reader can get from the code to the reasoning without knowing the record exists.

## How it goes wrong

- **The advocacy context** — the context section is written to make the decision look inevitable. It usually is inevitable, by then. *Warning sign:* the context contains a comparative adjective.
- **The fait accompli** — the record documents something already built, entering review with nothing at stake. *Warning sign:* the record's date is later than the first commit that depends on it.
- **The consequence-free record** — everything about the decision is good. *Warning sign:* your consequences section has no sentence beginning with a concession.
- **The unexercised status** — nothing is ever deprecated or superseded, so the vocabulary is decoration. **The published examples this specification comes from have the same defect: neither of them ever reaches the superseded state.** *Warning sign:* every record in your set is accepted.
- **The orphan decision** — the record exists, is well written, and is attached to no work item and no owner. Nobody will find it. *Warning sign:* the record is in a folder nobody has reason to open.
- **The record nobody could have disagreed with** — it documents a choice that was never genuinely open. That is not a decision record; it is a note. *Warning sign:* the alternatives section lists options nobody proposed.

## Exercises

**None of these is assessed here.** One thing from this session is checked at [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md): that at least one decision record is present whose context does not reveal which way the decision went, tested by handing the section to the assessor.

**1 · Guess the outcome.** Take these three context openings and say, for each, which way the decision went:

- *The team needed a durable store and evaluated three options, none of which met every requirement.*
- *Given the obvious limitations of the existing approach, the team considered alternatives.*
- *Two teams own systems that use the same term for different objects, and both are shipping this quarter.*

> **Model answers.** The first and third are value-neutral: the first states an unresolved trade, the third states a collision without implying a resolution. The second is advocacy — *obvious limitations* announces the conclusion in the second clause. **The reliable tell is a comparative adjective.** *Better*, *cleaner*, *obvious*, *unmaintainable* and *legacy* are verdicts wearing descriptions.

**2 · Write the rejection.** Take a decision already made in your engagement and write only the alternatives section: what was live, what was dismissed, what was never available, and why in each case.

> **Criterion.** A passing section distinguishes three states — considered and rejected, considered and unavailable, never considered — and gives a reason for each rejection that a reader could disagree with. **A section listing only the chosen option is a description, not a decision**, and the commonest failure is listing alternatives nobody actually proposed, which is a record of a choice that was never open.

**3 · Find the concession.** Take a consequences section you have written or read and identify the sentence that names something that got worse. If there is not one, write it.

> **What you should find.** Most consequences sections are benefit lists, and the missing concession is usually one of: an increase in operating cost, a capability that is now foreclosed, a dependency that is now load-bearing, or an obligation somebody else has silently acquired. **A consequences section with no concession is an advertisement**, and the reader six months from now cannot use it, because they need to know what they are currently paying before they change anything.

**4 · Go back to your own item 4, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 4 — what you decided against, and what that cost you. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then write it properly, as an alternatives section and a consequences section.

> **Criterion.** A passing answer names a specific alternative that was genuinely available, states why it was rejected in terms a reader could contest, and names a concrete cost the chosen option imposes that the rejected one would not have. "We chose the managed service rather than building it, which cost some flexibility" fails on the third: *some flexibility* is not a cost anybody can act on. "We chose the managed service; we can no longer run the pipeline in the customer's own tenant, which the security function has not yet been asked about" passes.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**5 · Read a configuration file as a decision.** Using the workflow, take one instructions file or agent definition from the pinned corpus and write down what it forecloses, and what its author must have rejected to write it that way.

> **What you should find.** The constraint is stated and the argument is gone. That is the normal state of configuration, and it is why a repository full of well-written instructions is not a repository full of decisions. **Note also what your own reading depends on**: the commit, the package version, the host and the model. If you did not record all four, your finding is about nothing in particular.

**6 · Supersede something, and say what changed in the world.** Write the replacement record, name what it supersedes, change the original's status, and write the sentence explaining what changed outside your own head.

> **Scoring criterion.** A passing sentence names an external event — a constraint that appeared, a measurement that came in, a party who arrived, a capability that was withdrawn. "We realised the first approach was wrong" fails: it records a change of mind and gives a later reader no way to tell whether the original was wrong then or only wrong now. **Note that the published specification's own worked examples never reach this state**, which is the defect this exercise exists to correct.
>
> **Then compare.** Look back at your answer to opening item 3 — what would have to change for the decision to be reversed. Is it the same kind of thing you just wrote, or was your original answer a change in somebody's opinion? **A reversal condition that is a change of mind is not a reversal condition**, for the same reason a disqualifying condition that could never fire is not a condition.

## What this does not establish

**Nothing shows that teams keeping decision records make better decisions, or that engineers arriving later actually read them.**

**The field list is a specification from a published page read on 2026-07-30, and a specification is not a finding.** The publication offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices and does not claim to. Its pages were written across several years and it has no single currency, which is why this session cites a page and a date rather than the document. It also reaches for a magnitude in several places and warrants it in none; **none of those figures is reproduced anywhere in this wiki.**

**The specification is also not followed in its own examples**, which is taught here rather than hidden: neither published record exercises the full status vocabulary, and neither refers to a documented comparison although the parent page instructs that a data-driven decision should refer to one. **A standard that its own author does not follow is the normal condition of standards, and recognising it is the transferable skill.**

**The anchoring evidence is borrowed from a different field and the transfer is this repository's.** The studies behind the value-neutral context requirement are clinical-education research using think-aloud protocols on mock portfolios in a single institution. Think-aloud methods cannot capture fast or automatic processing and risk participants confabulating explanations. **No magnitude from that literature is reproduced here**, and nothing in it concerns engineering decision records.

**The claim that a value-neutral context is achievable is contestable and is not established anywhere.** Writing an account of forces without revealing your conclusion may be a skill, or may be an appearance produced by careful omission, and this session's check — whether a reader can guess — cannot distinguish the two. The agent step at workflow 6 narrows the gap and does not close it.

**The configuration corpus establishes architecture and configuration claims and nothing else.** At the revision read there was no committed study, baseline, dataset or outcome corpus supporting any benefit claim attached to it, including by its own evaluation machinery; its own documentation states that its behavioural graders test output shape rather than correctness; some of its checks are advisory rather than authoritative; and one build check was failing at that revision. **Its perishability is extreme and its dependence on host and model is total.** A result observed on one client and one model is not a result about the corpus.
