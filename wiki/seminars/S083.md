# S083 · Communicating Risk to Someone Who Will Decide

**Module:** [M17](/wiki/modules/M17.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** PE · ME · SG
**Whitepaper:** [WP-083](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-083.md)
**Depends on:** S028, S050, S079
**Re-tests:** S065
**Platform anchor:** the substrate's real cost line — Foundry model-deployment cost monitoring, Azure Cost Management, and a budget that alerts and does not cap · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (monitor, pricing) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

At [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md), four quarters ago, the cohort said what a measurement did not license to four audiences who wanted it to license more. That day was constructed: the audiences were trained role-players, the pressure was calibrated, and when the twelve minutes ended nothing had happened. It was the closest precursor the programme had to EA-3 and it was not EA-3, and it said so before phase 1.

**This is the closest day the programme has, and it is not EA-3 either.** EA-3 is assessed at PE301, CAP390 and SG390. Today is the rehearsal, and the difference from S050 is not the difficulty of the reasoning. It is that today's decision-maker is a real person with authority over the substrate's service, today's risk is live and currently unresolved, and **a decision is made this afternoon that would not have been made the same way if the student had said something else.**

The shape of the problem inverts. At S050 the audiences wanted the finding to say *more* than the evidence supported, and the student's task was to hold a line. **Here the decision-maker wants less.** Less detail, fewer conditionals, one number, and an answer by Thursday. Holding the line by supplying more is the failure, and it is the failure this cohort is built to commit, because four quarters of measurement training have taught them that the qualification is the professional part.

It is, and it is not sufficient. A statement that is true, complete, correctly qualified and undecidable has discharged nothing. **The decision gets made anyway, on worse information, and the engineer who produced the complete statement will believe they did their job.**

The target: **say what a live risk means for a decision that is not yours, in the units of that decision, in a way that changes what the decision-maker can do — and accept that what they then do may not be what you recommended.**

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

A live, unresolved risk from the substrate's own [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) drift and alerting record is supplied, stated technically, as it appears in the record.

1. **Write what you would say to the person who signs for this service. One sentence, in the form you would actually say it.**
2. **What decision does that person have to make, and by when?**
3. **Which direction would you rather be wrong in?**
4. **Predict: will the decision-maker do what you recommend?**
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly give the agent this same risk record and ask it for a statement to a non-technical decision-maker. **Write down what it will produce, and name the one thing that will be missing from it.**

Item 2 is the day's opening finding and most students will not be able to answer it. **Producing a risk communication without knowing which decision it bears on is the normal professional condition and it is what makes item 1's sentence unactionable regardless of how well it is written.**

**Item 5's expected result is the day's argument in miniature.** The agent will produce something fluent, correctly qualified, structurally complete and undecidable — R1's shape, in forty seconds — because it has the risk record and does not have the decision, and nothing in the record contains one. **The prediction that the agent will do well is not a prediction; naming the absence is.**

**Warrant.** Pretesting a communicative act on a real risk before any framework exists, with item 1 captured before it can be polished. Item 3 is asked cold because the asymmetry is the sentence engineers omit and the day needs to know whether they hold it privately and suppress it or do not hold it at all. Item 4's prediction is checked against a real outcome six hours later, which no other day in the programme can do. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 and does not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**The fifth communication first, executed (6 min).** Each group gives the agent the same [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) record and asks for a statement to a non-technical decision-maker. Call it **R5**. It arrives in under a minute, it is nine or ten sentences, it is correctly qualified, and it is on the table for the rest of the phase.

Then groups receive **four communications of the same risk to the same decision-maker**, each with what the decision-maker decided afterwards and what happened. **These four are demonstrated rather than operated** — they are historical records with outcomes attached and there is nothing to run them against — **and they are not assessed.** R5 is executed and is what step 8 improves on.

**Sampling is disclosed**, per instrumentation rule 3: three are from the substrate's own record and one from a partner institution; they were selected because their outcomes differ while the underlying risk and the decision-maker's authority do not; and the outcomes were known to the staff who selected them. Students are told before they begin.

- **R1** — complete and correctly qualified. Nine sentences, three conditionals, an interval with its population stated. Nothing in it is wrong. **The decision-maker deferred and asked for more analysis. Nothing was decided for six weeks and the risk materialised in week four.**
- **R2** — one sentence, no qualification, no interval. **The decision was made immediately and it was the right decision.** The substrate's record shows why it was right, and the reason is not in the sentence: the risk's actual shape was not what the sentence implied, and the same sentence on a different draw would have produced the wrong call.
- **R3** — framed as a recommendation, with the reasoning attached. The decision-maker accepted it without engaging and said, eight weeks later, that they had not realised they were deciding anything. **The engineer made the decision and the decision-maker carried it.**
- **R4** — **the payload.** The risk in the units of the decision: what changes, for whom, by when, and what it costs to be wrong in each direction, with the asymmetry stated. The decision-maker engaged with all of it, asked two questions the engineer could answer, **and decided against the recommendation.** The outcome was worse than the recommendation would have produced. **On the substrate's record this is the best of the four communications.**

Groups rank all five and commit. **Then the decisions and the outcomes are supplied.**

The payload is **R4**, and it defeats the room's expectation about what a successful risk communication is. The room's criterion is that the right thing happens. R4 produced the wrong outcome and is the best communication in the set, because the success condition of a risk communication is that **the decision was made by the person entitled to make it, on the best available information, and they were able to weigh it against the other things they were weighing.** R2, which produced the right outcome, is the worst of the four.

**R5 will be ranked near R1 and it belongs there.** It is complete, it is qualified, it is what a competent engineer with the record and no decision would write, and **it is the shape the room is about to be told is a failure.** The room produced it in one minute without noticing, which is the point.

Task after: **for each of the five, state what the decision-maker could do after hearing it that they could not do before.** For four of the five the answer is nothing.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases holding the risk, the decision-maker and the authority constant and varying only the communication, adjudicated by the decision that followed rather than by a judgement about quality. R4 is the payload because it separates the day's success condition from the outcome, which is the claim the room will resist hardest and which cannot be delivered by assertion — a student told that being overruled can be success will file it as consolation. R2 is placed second because it is the case that will split the room, and the split is the phase's most useful minute. **R5 is the executed case and is generated by the room rather than supplied, which is what turns the day's characteristic failure from something other engineers did into something the room did four minutes ago.**

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **A risk communication is an input to somebody else's decision.** Its success condition is that the decision is better informed, not that it goes your way. R4. This is [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 3 — authority is not expertise — arriving as a communicative standard rather than as a governance one.

2. **The decision-maker's units.** Four things: **what changes, for whom, by when, and what it costs to be wrong in each direction.** That is what a decision needs and it is not what a risk register contains. **This enumeration omits three registers this day does not handle: regulatory and contractual exposure, reputational and second-order effects, and anything with a legal character. All three are real inputs to real decisions, all three are outside this programme, and a student who presents the four units as the complete set to somebody weighing the other three will be correctly ignored.**

3. **Reduction is selection, and selection is a claim.** [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md)'s question, [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) point 3 and [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) point 2, arriving where the authority is somebody else's: the set you did not say determines what they are able to decide. **The boundary between legitimate selection and misrepresentation is still not settled and returns at [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md).**

4. **Completeness is not a defence.** R1 was true, complete, correctly qualified, and produced six weeks of nothing followed by the event. **A statement that is understood and cannot be decided on has discharged nothing.** This is the failure this cohort is at risk of and it is [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s second symmetric failure with a date attached — it looks like rigour, it is rewarded everywhere else, and here it has a body count.

5. **The interval in the decision-maker's units.** [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md)'s licensing discipline expressed as a range of outcomes rather than a range of parameters. *Between forty and two hundred people a week, most likely around seventy* is decidable. *A rate increase of 3.2 percentage points, 95% interval 1.1 to 5.3* is not, and both may be the same measurement. **The conversion is not simplification and it is not approximation. It is the measurement stated in the unit of the decision, and it carries its provenance in the same field**, per specification rule 2.

   **The commonest undecidable statement you will be handed is a comparison, and the measurement literature says so in this day's own words.** A practitioner account of evaluating foundation-model systems states of the ranking methods it has just spent a chapter recommending: *comparative evaluation tells us which model is better. It doesn't tell us how good a model is or whether this model is good enough for our use case.* That is point 4 arriving from the measurement side rather than the communication side. **A comparison can be true, well constructed, correctly qualified and still decide nothing**, because the decision is about a threshold and the comparison is about an ordering, and the two do not convert into each other without a further measurement nobody has taken. The same source records that a small change in a win rate produces a large operational change in some applications and almost none in others, **which is why no conversion factor exists and why the engineer, not the decision-maker, is the party who has to do the arithmetic or say that it cannot be done.** See [research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md); direction and mechanism only, and **no magnitude is carried from it.**

6. **Say the direction you would rather be wrong in.** Every risk has an asymmetry and the decision-maker cannot see it. Stating which error you would prefer is the single most useful sentence you can give them, and it is the sentence engineers omit, because it looks like advocacy and because it is the only part of the statement that is unambiguously a judgement rather than a finding. **It is a judgement, you are being asked for it, and declining to give it is not neutrality.**

7. **Being overruled.** What it means: the decision was theirs and they made it. What it does not mean: that you failed, or that you were unclear, or that you should have pressed harder. What you do next: **record it — at the moment, in the decision's own record and not in your mail — state the residual risk in that record, and name the condition under which you would raise it again.** [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 5's discipline, applied to your own losing position. **A student who has never been overruled has probably been making the decisions.**

8. **You are one input.** Cost, timing, three other risks and a person's judgement about their own organisation are also inputs, and the decision-maker is integrating them. **Your job is to make yours legible, not to make it decisive. Safe where the decision-maker is competent, is acting in good faith, and the harm is bounded and reversible — which is the substrate's condition and is stated as a property of the substrate rather than of the world. Unsafe where the harm is irreversible or falls on people with no recourse: there, being one input among several is not a sufficient professional position, the four moves at [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) point 7 apply, and the safety-case and disclosure obligations at [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) and [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) do too. This day does not answer that case and the artefact at step 1 requires you to say whether you are in it.**

**Points 9 and 10 are the platform instance and come after the argument, per this module's convention.**

9. **Where the number came from, and what it is a number of.** The figure you quote this afternoon has two sources of record and they do not agree. Foundry's model-deployment monitor shows a total and an estimated-cost chart for a date range; Azure Cost Management shows invoiced charges. The documentation is explicit about the division of labour: **use estimated cost for near-real-time monitoring and Microsoft Cost Management and invoiced charges for financial reconciliation**, because token and request charts can temporarily differ from estimated cost through ingestion timing and aggregation. It also states that there is **roughly a five-hour delay** from a billing event to its appearance in Azure portal cost analysis, and that **your Foundry costs are only a subset of the solution's costs** — storage, networking, monitoring and marketplace items are elsewhere. **So the number on your slide at two o'clock is not the number the finance office will see, and both are correct.** That is [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md)'s provenance discipline arriving as two systems of record rather than as a caveat, and it is why step 3 requires the as-of time in the same field as the figure. **Safe** for steering a decision this week. **Unsafe** for reconciliation, and unsafe as a figure quoted to somebody who will later see an invoice — there, the invoiced number is the only one you may name, and if you do not have it yet the honest sentence is that you do not have it yet. **Perishable:** the five hours, the portal paths, the two surfaces' names. **Durable:** a near-real-time estimate and a settled account are different instruments and a decision-maker must be told which one they are holding.

10. **A default the platform chose for you, and it is the one you were about to promise.** Somebody will ask whether the cost is capped. The documentation answers it directly: **while OpenAI has an option for hard limits that prevent you from going over your budget, Azure OpenAI does not currently provide this functionality.** An Azure budget **notifies**; you may start automation from action groups as part of the notification, and the documentation says that **requires additional custom development.** So the true sentence is not *we have a ceiling*; it is *we have an alert, and somebody wrote the thing that acts on it.* On this substrate that somebody was a previous cohort, and the ceiling is enforced at the gateway by per-consumer token policies precisely because the platform does not enforce it — see [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md). A second default worth naming: **you cannot see the cost at all without two role assignments**, Cost Management Reader and Foundry User, at the right scope. Cost visibility is an access decision somebody made about you. And the silence to carry: **there is no published figure for how often a budget alert arrives before the money is spent**, and nothing in the tooling would produce one.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Points 1 and 7 are the day's two claims with engineering consequences and both are reached from R4 rather than asserted; point 4 is produced by the phase 2 bridge before phase 3 runs, and R5 makes it the room's own error rather than a warning. Point 2 carries the enumeration's scope statement and point 8 carries the strong-form one. **Point 8's final clause is a checked artefact element rather than a statement in this plan only**, which is a departure from the day's own precedents and is argued in [WP-083](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-083.md) §8. **Point 9 carries its own strong-form scope statement and is the day's perishable platform point; point 10 is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required naming of a default the platform chose, and it is chosen because it is the specific false promise a competent student is about to make this afternoon.**

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 6. The decision is not the student's, and it is made today.**

Each student takes one live, unresolved risk from the substrate's [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) record or from their own [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) detection commitments.

1. **Identify the risk and the decision it bears on.** Name the decision-maker and the date the decision is made. **Then state whether the harm is bounded and reversible.** If it is not, state that this day's form does not apply and name what you would do instead under [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) point 7.
2. **State the risk in the four decision units:** what changes, for whom, by when, and what it costs to be wrong in each direction.
3. **State the interval in the decision-maker's units**, with its provenance and its population in the same field.
4. **State the direction you would rather be wrong in, and why.**
5. **State what you are not saying, and why.** The material you selected out, and the reason you selected it out.
6. **Deliver it.** Ten minutes, to a real decision-maker with authority over the service and no technical background, who makes a real decision on the day. **They may decide against you.**
7. **Record the outcome in the decision-maker's own words**, in the decision's own record, with the residual risk stated and the condition under which you would raise it again.
8. **Ship the figure.** Thirty minutes, with Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server. Produce the query that returns the exposure behind step 3 in the decision-maker's unit — monitor metrics, cost analysis, gateway token metrics, whichever the risk actually needs — **carrying its provenance, its population and its as-of time in the same field as the number**, per specification rule 2. Say which of the two systems of record it came from. **If it is not ready by the encounter, deliver without it and say so**: *I do not have the settled figure and will have it on Thursday* is a decidable sentence and is not a failure.

**Step 8 is performed first in the room and is listed last because it is the statement's evidence rather than the statement.**

**The decision-makers are real and are drawn from a panel of institutional service owners. No student faces the party they faced at [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md)**, and the assignment method is stated to students, per instrumentation rule 3.

**The build is bounded and the encounter is not.** Step 8 is timeboxed to thirty of the ninety minutes. **The panel's time is timetabled first and the build is what compresses**, because the decision is made this afternoon whether or not the query is finished.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave, in order, including the ones you abandoned, each with why. **The instruction that converted a parameter into a decision unit is the one to record most carefully**, because it is the only one the agent will not produce unprompted.
- **Intervention record.** Every correction and what it was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a query that refuses to emit a figure without its as-of time and its source of record is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have and one thing it did badly that you had to catch, both from this build.

**Fading:** step 2's four units are supplied as a form; step 3's unit conversion is worked once with the room on a supplied measurement. **Steps 4 and 5 are unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 6, with a real counterparty holding real authority, which satisfies instrumentation rule 1 — the person who can say whether the communication supported a decision is the one making it. Step 1's second clause is the applicability statement point 8's scope requires and is a checked element; [WP-083](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-083.md) §8 records why this day binds its own artefact where the rule as written binds only the paper. Step 4 is unsupported because the asymmetry is a judgement and a supplied form would collect a hedge. Step 5 is unsupported because a student who cannot say what they left out has not selected; they have summarised. **Step 8 exists because a decision unit is only decidable if the number under it is real, and because point 9's two systems of record cannot be understood by being described** — a student who has watched the estimate and the invoiced figure disagree about their own service has the distinction and a student who has read about it has a caveat.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate risk communicated well, understood, and decided against. The outcome was bad and the engineer was later held to have failed to press hard enough. **The engineer had a record.** It was in a message thread, it was accurate, it was contemporaneous, and it was not in the decision's own record, so at the review nobody found it and the engineer could not produce it in a form anybody accepted. Attention is directed at the location of the record and not at the communication, which was good.

Then the agent case: a Copilot-assembled cost statement that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. Asked for the month's spend on a deployment, it queried the Foundry monitor, returned the estimated-cost figure for the range, labelled it correctly as an estimate, and put it in front of a budget holder. The reviewer checked the number against the portal and it matched exactly. **Both numbers were right and the reviewer's check could not have failed**, because the check consulted the same surface the figure came from. The invoiced charge, when it settled, was materially higher — a different aggregation, five hours of ingestion behind, and excluding the storage and monitoring meters that point 9 says live elsewhere. Attention goes to the shape: **the agent used the source the documentation names for near-real-time monitoring to answer a question about financial reconciliation**, and nothing in the artefact recorded which question was being asked.

Then students, in threes: **a time you were right and it did not matter.** What did you do next, and what would you do now?

**Warrant.** Error management targeting point 7, using a case in which the communication is exemplary and the failure is entirely in where the record was kept — the only construction that makes the record-location duty part of the act rather than administrative tidiness. It is [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 5 read from the losing side. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen because the review that would have caught it is not a review of the number**, which is the only review a student knows how to perform; it is point 9's distinction, which nobody holds until this afternoon. The student disclosure is placed here rather than earlier because a room that has just been overruled by a real person needs somewhere to put it, and because the second question converts the disclosure from a grievance into a procedure.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student delivers **their step 2 four-unit statement** to a partner **who has not read the underlying risk**.

The partner's task: **state the decision they would make and name the specific part of the statement that drove it.** Not whether they understood it — what they would do.

The student then says whether that is the decision the statement was meant to enable, and where the difference came from. Both students perform both roles, and the outcome is recorded per specification rule 3, including a partner who cannot name a decision at all, which is a result about the statement.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with bilateral production, testing decidability rather than comprehension. **The comprehension form of this protocol was used at [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md) and [WP-024](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-024.md) §8 predicted its degradation across repeated administrations, naming this day specifically.** That warning is honoured: the demand here is a decision and a named driver, not a restatement, and a student who has anticipated the test by making the decision conspicuous has done the thing the day wants rather than gamed it — which is the property the comprehension form lacked.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in ME390 with PE390. Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the risk and the decision it bears on, with the decision-maker and the date; **the applicability statement — whether the harm is bounded and reversible, and if not, what the student would do instead**; the four decision units; the interval in the decision-maker's units with its provenance and population in the same field; the direction the student would rather be wrong in, with its reason; what was selected out and why; the outcome recorded in the decision-maker's own words in the decision's own record, with the residual risk and the re-raising condition; the phase 7 result; **the shipped figure with its as-of time and named source of record in the same field, or the statement that it was not available; the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

**A statement with no direction-of-error sentence fails.** This is the day's single hard requirement, and it is a fail rather than a deduction because the sentence is the one the cohort will omit and because omitting it is a decision to withhold a judgement that was requested.

**A decision made against the student's recommendation is not a failure and is not recorded as one.** The specification says so in writing. What is recorded is whether the outcome was captured in the decision's own record in the decision-maker's own words — which is the checked element, and which a student can satisfy having lost the argument entirely.

**An interval stated in parameter units fails**, per point 5 and specification rule 2.

**A figure delivered without its as-of time and its source of record fails**, per point 9 and the same rule. **A figure not delivered at all does not fail**, provided the student said so and named when it would be available.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**The four historical communications are not assessed**, because they are demonstrated rather than operated. R5 is executed and is the object step 8 improves on.

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md), and **EA-3 is assessed at PE301, CAP390 and SG390 and not here.**

**Rule-6 discharge.** One obligation lands on this day and **the element exists.**

- **[S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md)** named this day for a second reading of its supervision-level distribution and its phase 4 least-defensible-number responses, "for the EA-3 half." **The reading is taken and reported at phase 8, beside this day's own outcomes.** Per instrumentation rule 2 and per [WP-065](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-065.md) §6, **the two readings are reported and are not compared**, because the protocol they share would make a second administration a measure of familiarity with it. The room is told this rather than shown two numbers and left to draw a line between them.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions:

- **The item 2 decision-identification rate** — how many students could name the decision their risk bears on, and its date, before the day began. **Response occasion: [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md), phase 3, where a scope statement must name each counterparty's decision and its date. Where this cohort's rate is low, [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md) supplies the decision and date as given material and assesses the scope against them; where it is high, the student establishes both.** The branch is stated so that the weaker cohort does not fail an entrustment occasion on a competence this day found missing.
- **The step 6 and step 7 outcomes** — the proportion of decisions made against the student's recommendation, and the proportion of students whose record captured the outcome in the decision-maker's own words, in the decision's own record. **Response occasion: [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md), whose residual-risk statement must be written for a reader who decided against the engineer, and must carry one risk the student raised and lost.** [WP-088](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-088.md) §7 must report whether that element exists.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** A risk communication is an input to somebody else's decision, and it succeeds when the decision is made by the person entitled to make it. **Completeness is not a defence**: a statement that is true, correctly qualified and undecidable has discharged nothing. State the risk in the decision's units — what changes, for whom, by when, what it costs to be wrong each way — and say which direction you would rather be wrong in.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** That the audience's vocabulary is part of the claim is verified at [research/05-fde-craft/palantir-fde-practitioner-writing.md](/research/05-fde-craft/palantir-fde-practitioner-writing.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md); what a number licenses at [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md). **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted for how often a qualified statement delays a decision.** The cost documentation names which surface is for monitoring and which for reconciliation, and **publishes no figure for how often an alert arrives before the money is spent.**

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Name the decision and its date before you write the sentence.** A statement without one is unactionable however well written.
- **Carry the as-of time and the source of record in the same field as the figure.** Two systems can disagree and both be right.
- **Put the outcome in the decision's own record, in the decision-maker's words**, with the re-raising condition.
- **Provenance is not correctness.** Per the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md), citing a number's source does not establish that it answers the question asked.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Reading an overruled recommendation as a failure. The decision was theirs; what you owe afterwards is the record, in the decision's own file.
