## The situation

The evaluation harness is the only thing the customer will have that can tell them the system has stopped working properly. It is also the item on your manifest least likely to find an owner, because operating it is nobody's job, understanding it requires knowing why it was built the way it was, and its output is uncomfortable.

Answer these in writing before the session opens.

1. Where did the evaluation set come from? Name the selection rule, not the source.
2. Who chose each threshold, on what basis, and is that basis recorded anywhere but in your memory?
3. Suppose the harness runs after you leave and the result is bad. Name the person who is obliged to do something, and name what they are obliged to do.
4. What would make this harness stop being a test of the live system without anybody noticing?
5. Is the cost model in the same class of object as the harness? Say why or why not.

**Item 3 is the session.** A harness whose failing result obliges nobody is a decoration, and you can transfer a decoration perfectly.

## What you must produce

A **harness handover record** covering six things: the dataset and the rule by which it was selected; the criteria applied and who wrote them; each threshold with its provenance; the procedure for running it; the rule for interpreting the output; and the obligation a bad result creates, with the named person who holds it and the authority they need.

**Item five of the manifest travels with this one.** The cost model is the same class of object — an instrument, built by you, that the customer will need after you disengage and that nobody on their side currently operates — and it gets the same six-part treatment.

## What good looks like

- **Every threshold has a provenance line**, and none of them says "set during tuning". A value with no reason cannot be revised, because nobody can tell whether the reason has expired.
- The selection rule for the evaluation set is stated well enough that somebody else could extend the set without changing what it measures.
- The interpretation rule distinguishes *the system got worse* from *the world moved*, and says what evidence separates them.
- **The obligation is a specific act by a specific role**, with the authority that act requires. If the person obliged to act cannot deploy, cannot pause a rollout and cannot commission work, say so on the record.
- Somebody is named as permitted to change a threshold, and the record says what they must do when they change one.

## How it goes wrong

Five failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **The frozen set** — the evaluation data was assembled once, the live distribution moved, and the harness now passes reliably while measuring something that no longer occurs. *Warning sign:* nobody can say when the set last changed.
- **The threshold with no parent** — values transferred as numbers, with the reasoning left in the head of the person who set them on a busy afternoon. *Warning sign:* you can state the value instantly and the reason slowly.
- **The consequence-free result** — the harness runs, the result is recorded, and nothing in the organisation is arranged to respond to it. *Warning sign:* your answer to "who acts?" is a team rather than a role.
- **The harness that requires you** — running it involves a step only you can perform, an environment only you can reach, or a judgement only you can make. *Warning sign:* the run procedure contains the word "then check".
- **The orphaned instrument** — the harness and the cost model are handed to the most technically capable receiver rather than to the one whose work depends on their output. *Warning sign:* the named owner has never asked to see a result.

## Do it

Work from the supplied situation in [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md).

The evaluation set there was assembled from records the external assessor previously returned, by a rule nobody wrote down. **Reconstructing that rule is the first task, and you should expect to find that you cannot reconstruct it exactly.** Say so on the record; a stated uncertainty about how the set was built is more useful to the recipient than a confident description that is wrong.

Then do the same for the two routing thresholds with no recorded provenance, and for the cost model that lives on a laptop.

Finally, take the obligation. Write who acts on a bad result and what authority they need — and if the honest answer is that the person who would notice cannot act and the person who could act will not look, **that is a finding and it belongs on the record**, not in a mitigation you invent to make the document tidy.

## Show it

State your interpretation rule aloud, then have another learner propose a result and ask you what it obliges.

They are instructed to propose the awkward case: a result that is worse but not badly worse, at a moment when the assessor's sampling window is close. **A rule that only resolves clear cases has not been written.**

## What this does not establish

That a transferred harness gets run. Nothing behind this programme reports whether any delivered evaluation apparatus was operated after its authors left, or whether any customer ever acted on one.

**The strongest evidence in this area is negative and it comes from the source corpus itself.** That corpus contains a value-conversation framework that instructs its reader to establish a baseline and never exhibits one being established, catalogues metric definitions and reports none of them measured, and ships model workbooks with no populated values. It is a number-generating apparatus containing no numbers. The lesson this session takes from it is not about evaluation quality; it is that **an instrument can be transferred in full working order and still never produce a number anybody acts on**, and that the difference is the obligation rather than the instrument.
