# S073 · The Evaluation Harness They Inherit

**Module:** [M15](/wiki/modules/M15.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md) · **Complexity class:** 5
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Agent Framework evaluations — a harness the recipient cannot run has not been transferred, whatever the handover document says
**Whitepaper:** [WP-073](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-073.md)
**Retrieves from:** S066, S068, S070, S071
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

---

## The situation

The evaluation harness is the only thing the customer will have that can tell them the system has stopped working properly.

It is also the item on your manifest least likely to find an owner. Operating it is nobody's job. Understanding it requires knowing why it was built the way it was. Its output is uncomfortable. And — the part that decides the outcome — **running it requires a credential, a machine, an environment or a judgement that currently belongs to you.**

You will transfer it anyway. The repository will be shared, the documentation will be written, the walkthrough will happen, and everybody will believe the harness has changed hands. **The test of whether it has is a single observation: the recipient runs it, alone, and reads the result.** Nothing else counts, and almost nobody performs that test, because performing it risks discovering that the answer is no on a day when the schedule cannot absorb it.

Answer these in writing before the session opens.

1. Where did the evaluation set come from? Name the selection rule, not the source.
2. Who chose each threshold, on what basis, and is that basis recorded anywhere but in your memory?
3. Suppose the harness runs after you leave and the result is bad. Name the person who is obliged to do something, and name what they are obliged to do.
4. What would make this harness stop being a test of the live system without anybody noticing?
5. Is the cost model in the same class of object as the harness? Say why or why not.

**Item 3 is the session.** A harness whose failing result obliges nobody is a decoration, and you can transfer a decoration perfectly.

## What you must produce

A **harness handover record** in seven parts:

1. **The dataset**, and the rule by which it was selected — stated well enough that somebody else could extend it without changing what it measures.
2. **The criteria**, and who wrote them.
3. **Every threshold with its provenance line.** "Set during tuning" is a failed line.
4. **The run procedure**, executable by the recipient with every credential, environment and permission it requires named.
5. **The interpretation rule**, distinguishing *the system got worse* from *the world moved*, and naming the evidence that separates them.
6. **The obligation** a bad result creates, with the named person who holds it and the authority that act requires.
7. **The run record** — the recipient ran it, on their machine, with their credentials, and read the output.

**Item five of your manifest travels with this one.** The cost model is the same class of object — an instrument, built by you, that the customer needs after you disengage and that nobody on their side currently operates — and it gets the same seven-part treatment.

## The material

### An instrument transfers when somebody else can run it, and not before

Start with the observation that decides everything else, because the rest of the session is downstream of it.

**A harness that requires your credentials, your machine or your knowledge is not inherited.** It is visible to the recipient, readable by them, and unavailable. This is the [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md) criterion — an object transfers if a stranger can find it without asking anyone — extended by one clause: *and can operate it without becoming you*.

The distinction between knowledge and demonstrated capability is stated most precisely in the change-management literature, where **ability** is "turning knowledge into action" and is achieved "when a person or group has the demonstrated capability to implement the change at the required performance levels" ([research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md)). The named barriers include available time and available support resources, and a harness handover is where both bite: nobody's time was allocated to receiving it, and the support arrangement ends the day you do.

**Take the distinction and nothing else from that source.** It is a trademarked practitioner model published by the firm that sells its training; its ordering is argued from word meanings; its quantitative claims all trace to its own publisher's practitioner surveys; and no independent evaluation of it appears anywhere in it.

### The platform will build you a complete harness and tell you nothing about whether it is right

The evaluation surface is small, well designed, and instructive precisely because of what it does not publish.

The framework's programming model is three types: `EvalItem`, "a single item to evaluate — wraps the full conversation and derives query/response via a split strategy"; `Evaluator`, "a provider that scores items"; and `EvalResults`, "aggregated results from an evaluation run" ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). Three types is a good sign: evaluation is a function from an interaction to a judgement, aggregated, and everything else is a detail of who judges.

The two-tier design is the structural idea and it is the one your recipient inherits. `LocalEvaluator` "runs checks locally without API calls — ideal for inner-loop development, CI smoke tests, and fast iteration". The cloud tier connects to the service "for cloud-based LLM-as-judge evaluation". **The tiers differ in cost, speed and what they require the recipient to hold**, and a harness composed entirely of the second tier is a harness the recipient cannot run without a subscription, a quota and a credential — which is the transfer failure disguised as an architecture choice.

Three details on that page are the ones your handover record must carry.

**The split strategy is the most consequential and most easily missed decision in the whole surface**, and the documentation flags it correctly: "Multi-turn conversations must be split into query and response halves for evaluation. How you split determines *what you're evaluating*." Last turn scores response quality at a point; Full scores task completion and trajectory; Per-turn scores each exchange. **An agent that recovers well from a bad first attempt scores well under Full and badly under Per-turn.** Neither is wrong. They answer different questions, and a recipient who inherits a number without the strategy has inherited an answer to a question nobody stated.

**Thresholds are policy, separable from measurement.** Output is pass or fail with a `reason`, plus a numeric score for scaled evaluators. **Moving a threshold changes the pass rate without changing the system**, which is why item 3 of your record exists: an inherited threshold with no provenance can only be obeyed or ignored, never revised.

**Repetitions exist because one sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement.** If your harness runs each case once, the recipient will read run-to-run variation as regression, and will do it on their first bad morning.

There is also a mechanism that decides whether the harness stays connected to reality: **pre-existing responses from logs can be evaluated "without re-running the agent"**. A harness fed from production traces tests what happened; a harness fed from a synthetic set tests what you thought to ask.

**Now the silence, and it is the important part.** The documentation is definitional and accurate about what the tooling computes. **It publishes no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold** — and most of these evaluators are model-judged. **Assert no agreement figure for any of them, here or anywhere.** That is a statement about what you may claim and not a claim that the evaluators are poor; they may be excellent, and neither you nor anyone quoting their output has any way to know.

**Its further limits:** read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, with structure reconstructed; most agent evaluators are marked preview; one consulted page is an explicitly superseded variant.

### Who validates the validators, and why the criteria will not hold still

If the recipient inherits a judge, they inherit the judge's problems.

The human-computer-interaction literature is exact: using a model to grade a model **relocates the trust problem rather than solving it**, because generated evaluators "simply inherit all the problems of the LLMs they evaluate, requiring further human validation" ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md)). The repair is alignment: have a person grade a subset directly, then prefer the evaluator whose verdicts best reproduce the person's grades. **That is the only route in this repository from machinery to evidence, and it requires a person on the recipient's side, not on yours.**

The finding that makes the handover harder is named and defined: **criteria drift** — "users need criteria to grade outputs, but grading outputs helps users define criteria." That is a genuine circularity rather than an inefficiency. A person cannot fully specify what counts as a good output before seeing outputs, because seeing outputs is how they discover which dimensions vary and matter. The sharper form is that some criteria appear **dependent on the particular outputs observed**, which the authors say "raises serious questions for approaches that assume the independence of evaluation from observation of model outputs."

**The consequence for a handover is uncomfortable and worth stating plainly: your criteria are partly a record of what you happened to see.** The recipient will see different outputs, and their criteria will drift away from yours — correctly. A handover record that presents the criteria as fixed is transferring a snapshot as though it were a standard.

**Its limits:** the drift evidence is qualitative, establishing that the phenomenon is real and consequential rather than how often; the user study is small by construction; the tool is a research prototype. It addresses criteria alignment and says nothing about statistical validity.

### The set will stop being a test of the live system, and nothing will announce it

The production-ML literature supplies the mechanism and the vocabulary.

**Data drift** is a change in the distribution of the inputs; **concept drift** is a change in the relationship between inputs and labels, which invalidates the mapping rather than merely moving the inputs ([research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md)). **Model decay** is the degradation of a deployed model's usefulness as the world moves away from its training data. And the design decision your record must state: **the rate at which ground truth changes determines the labelling strategy** — change over months or years permits a curated set, change over days or hours forces direct feedback or weak supervision.

Two further points bear directly. **Retraining policy is a design decision with no correct answer**, and a scheduled trigger "assumes a steady rate of change in the world which frequently does not hold" — so a handover record that names a cadence must name the assumption the cadence rests on. And **aggregate metrics conceal subgroup failure**, with the choice of slices being a domain question rather than a statistical one: a harness reporting one number will not fire for the failure destroying one segment, and the recipient is the person who knows which segments exist.

**Its limits:** a practitioner survey from a commercial publisher, not research; no study design, no comparison group, no evidence its practices outperform alternatives; the toolchain is the authors' employer's; every number in it is a property of a particular system and none is reproduced here.

### The machinery is not the result, and the obligation is not the machinery

You met the first half of this at [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md). An openly published agent configuration corpus, read at a pinned commit, contains substantial evaluation machinery — graded behavioural checks, conformance lanes, baseline-equivalence comparisons, validators — and **no committed result corpus**, with the storage location an empty placeholder ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)). It also documents that its behavioural checks test **output shape and not correctness**, and that **a check can be advisory at one tier while the nightly run treats the same check as authoritative.**

That last distinction is a handover object in its own right. **A recipient reading a green summary cannot tell whether the check that passed was one that could have stopped anything**, and if you do not write down which of your checks are authoritative, the recipient will treat them all as decoration or all as gospel, and both are wrong.

**Its limits are extreme:** perishability total on paths, counts and versions; host and model dependence absolute; benefit vocabulary in its prose that its own machinery does not support; the pinned revision not all-green.

Now the second half, which is this session's addition. **An instrument that produces a result nobody is obliged to act on has been transferred as a decoration.**

The change-management literature is unusually good here, and its best material is a failure case. A bank deployed a quality programme well and then disbanded the implementation team; within a year the programme was dead. The diagnosis is the sentence to carry: **"The first mistake the implementation team made was to assume that managing change is essentially completing activities in a project plan"** ([research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md)). The same source names accountability systems as "one of the strongest forms of reinforcement", and requires that reinforcement be meaningful to the recipient, tied to a real accomplishment, and free of countervailing negative consequences.

**Read the last of those three against your own harness.** If a bad result creates work for the person who reads it and no consequence for anyone else, the countervailing incentive is to stop reading. That is not a character flaw and no amount of documentation fixes it. **It is fixed by an obligation attached to a role with the authority to discharge it, or it is not fixed.**

## The workflow

**Build the harness so that the recipient can run it, then have them run it while you watch.**

**Step 1 — split the harness by what it requires.** List every check. Against each, record what a runner must hold: a credential, a subscription, a quota, a network path, a machine, a judgement. **Any check requiring something the recipient does not hold is not transferred**, whatever the record says.

**Step 2 — move what you can to the local tier.** A deterministic check that runs without an API call is inheritable by anyone with the repository. Every check you move from judged to deterministic is a check that survives the loss of a subscription. **You will not be able to move most of them**, and knowing which is the finding.

**Step 3 — write the split strategy down as a decision.** Not a default. State which strategy is in use and what question it answers, because a strategy chosen by omission is an unstated question.

**Step 4 — give every threshold a provenance line, in the file.**

```jsonl
{"id":"tc-014","question":"...","expected_tool_calls":["lookup_policy"],"slice":"renewals"}
```

```yaml
# eval/thresholds.yaml  — the record travels WITH the value, in the same file.
# A threshold with no provenance can be obeyed or ignored. It cannot be revised.
split_strategy: "full"          # scores task completion and trajectory.
                                # "per_turn" would score recovery differently. State the question.
repetitions: "<n>"              # more than one. A single sample is not a measurement.
source: "production traces, sampled weekly"   # synthetic sets test what you thought to ask

evaluators:
  - name: tool_call_accuracy
    tier: judged                # requires a subscription + quota. RECIPIENT MUST HOLD BOTH.
    threshold: "<cut point>"
    authority: authoritative    # a failure here stops a release
    provenance: >
      Chosen 2026-05-14 after the renewals incident. Set at the lowest value that
      would have caught it, checked against a hand-graded sample. Recorded by <role>.
      REVISE IF: the renewals flow changes, or hand-grading disagrees on the sample.
  - name: task_navigation_efficiency
    tier: deterministic         # no model parameter. Runs anywhere. INHERITABLE.
    authority: advisory         # a failure here opens a question, it does not stop anything
    provenance: "Expected action sequence from the runbook at S072. No judgement involved."

obligation:
  on_authoritative_failure:
    who: "<role, and the person holding it>"
    act: "pause the weekly release and open an incident"
    authority_required: "release pause"
    authority_held: false        # <- state it. Do not invent a mitigation to tidy the record.
```

**The `authority_held: false` line is the one that matters.** If the person who would notice cannot act and the person who could act will not look, that is a finding and it belongs on the record.

**Step 5 — write the interpretation rule against the awkward case.** Not *if the score falls, investigate*. State how the recipient distinguishes a system regression from a moved world, and name the evidence. If you cannot, say so: an honest "we cannot currently distinguish these" is worth more to the recipient than a rule that resolves only clear cases.

**Step 6 — the recipient runs it.** Their machine, their credentials, their network, you silent. Record where it failed to start. **A harness that ran for you and not for them was never transferred**, and this step is the only way to learn that before you have left.

**Step 7 — attack your own record with the agent, and do not let it validate the harness.** One prompt: *here is my harness handover record and here is the recipient's actual access. For every check, name what the recipient must hold to run it and flag anything they do not. Then argue that each of my thresholds is arbitrary, and name the one whose provenance line would not survive a challenge.*

Read it for the access gaps and the weak provenance line. **Record what you rejected and why.** Note that this instrument is model-judged and shares a family with the judged tier of your own harness, so it is not independent of the thing it is auditing — an agent that endorses your evaluators has agreed with itself.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** Step 6. There is no argument about whether a harness has transferred once somebody else has failed to start it, and no amount of documentation quality substitutes for the observation. **The gap between "I have given you this" and "you can run this" is invisible from both sides until somebody tries.**

## What good looks like

- **Every threshold has a provenance line**, and none says "set during tuning". A value with no reason cannot be revised, because nobody can tell whether the reason has expired.
- **Each check is marked by tier and by authority** — deterministic or judged, advisory or authoritative — so a green summary can be read correctly.
- The split strategy is stated as a decision with the question it answers.
- Repetitions are set, and the record says why a single run is not a measurement.
- The selection rule for the set is stated well enough that somebody else could extend it without changing what it measures, and the record says whether the set is fed from traces or from synthesis.
- The interpretation rule distinguishes *the system got worse* from *the world moved*, and says what evidence separates them — or states honestly that it cannot.
- **The obligation is a specific act by a specific role**, with the authority that act requires, and the record states whether that authority is held.
- Somebody is named as permitted to change a threshold, and the record says what they must do when they change one.
- **The recipient has run it and the run is recorded**, including what they could not do.

## How it goes wrong

- **The frozen set** — the data was assembled once, the live distribution moved, and the harness now passes reliably while measuring something that no longer occurs. *Warning sign:* nobody can say when the set last changed.
- **The threshold with no parent** — values transferred as numbers with the reasoning left in somebody's head. *Warning sign:* you can state the value instantly and the reason slowly.
- **The unrunnable inheritance** — every check is judged, and the recipient has no subscription. *Warning sign:* you have never seen anybody but you start it.
- **The unstated split** — the strategy was a default, so the number answers a question nobody asked. *Warning sign:* you cannot say what would score differently under a different strategy.
- **The consequence-free result** — the harness runs, the result is recorded, and nothing is arranged to respond. *Warning sign:* your answer to "who acts?" is a team rather than a role.
- **The uniform green** — advisory and authoritative checks look identical in the summary. *Warning sign:* your record does not distinguish them, and neither will theirs.
- **The single sample read as regression** — no repetitions, so ordinary variation reads as decay. *Warning sign:* your harness runs each case once and you have never seen it disagree with itself.
- **The orphaned instrument** — the harness and the cost model go to the most technically capable receiver rather than the one whose work depends on the output. *Warning sign:* the named owner has never asked to see a result.

## Exercises

**None of these is assessed.** The harness handover record and the run record are carried to [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md), where the obligation is assessed.

**1 · Sort seven statements into machinery, observation, obligation and unsupported.**

- *The harness contains a tool-call accuracy evaluator.* · *I ran the harness on Tuesday and three cases failed.* · *This harness tells us the system is working.* · *A failure on the authoritative lane pauses the weekly release.* · *The evaluator agrees with human judgement.* · *The set is sampled from production traces weekly.* · *The recipient ran the harness on their own machine and read the output.*

> **Model answers.** Machinery: the evaluator's existence and the sampling rule — facts about what is configured. Observations: the Tuesday run and the recipient's run, both bound to a date and a runner. Obligation: the release-pause rule, which is the only statement that says what anyone will do. Unsupported: *this harness tells us the system is working*, which is an outcome claim no harness establishes; and *the evaluator agrees with human judgement*, which **no vendor publishes for any of these evaluators and which you must therefore never assert.** **The general rule: a statement is machinery if it would still be true with the system switched off, an observation if somebody had to run something, an obligation if it says what a named role will do, and unsupported if it asserts a correspondence nobody has measured.**

**2 · Run step 1 and count what the recipient cannot hold.** For each check, name what a runner must have. Tally the checks the recipient could not start.

> **What you should find.** That the judged checks — the ones carrying most of the harness's apparent value — require a subscription, a quota and a credential, and that the deterministic checks the recipient could run unaided are the ones you thought least of. **The one deterministic agent-behaviour check in the platform catalogue takes an expected action sequence and requires no model parameter**; find it, and notice that it is the only check in the set whose result does not depend on a judge. **If your tally is zero, you have listed what you run and not what running requires.**

**3 · Go back to your own item 3, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 3 — who is obliged to act on a bad result, and what they are obliged to do. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then write the honest version, including the `authority_held` line.

> **Criterion.** An obligation passes if it names **a role, a specific act, and the authority that act requires**, and states whether that authority is held. *"The platform team investigates"* fails on all three. *"The service owner pauses the weekly release and opens an incident; this requires release-pause authority, which they do not currently hold"* passes — **and it passes precisely because it records a gap rather than closing one.** An obligation that resolves cleanly on a first draft was probably invented to make the document tidy.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**4 · Change the split strategy and re-read the number.** Take one evaluation result and state what it would be under a different split strategy, and what question each answers.

> **What to notice.** That a system which recovers from a bad first attempt looks competent under one strategy and incompetent under another, and that **neither number is wrong.** The recipient inherits whichever you chose, usually by default, along with a mental model of the system's quality that the choice created. **State the strategy in the same place as the number, permanently.**

**5 · Write the provenance line for your worst threshold.** Pick the threshold you can least defend and write its line: who, when, on what basis, and under what condition it should be revised.

> **Criterion.** A pass names a person or role, a date, an observable basis, and a revision condition. **The revision condition is the part everyone omits and the part that makes the value inheritable**, because it tells the recipient when your reasoning has expired. If your honest line is *"chosen on a busy afternoon, basis not recorded"*, write that — a stated gap can be closed by the recipient and a fabricated justification cannot.

**6 · Take the awkward result.** Have another learner propose a result that is worse but not badly worse, at a moment when the sampling window is close, and ask what it obliges.

> **Scoring criterion.** **A rule that only resolves clear cases has not been written.** A pass either resolves the awkward case with named evidence, or states that it cannot be resolved with the evidence available and names what would resolve it. Notice that criteria drift makes this permanently harder: your criteria are partly a record of the outputs you happened to observe, and the recipient will observe different ones. **A record that presents its criteria as fixed is transferring a snapshot as a standard.**

## What this does not establish

**That a transferred harness gets run.** Nothing behind this programme reports whether any delivered evaluation apparatus was operated after its authors left, or whether any customer ever acted on one.

**No evaluator's agreement with human judgement is published, and none is asserted here.** The platform documentation supplies no calibration data and no rationale for any default threshold, and most of its evaluators are model-judged. That is evidence about what you may claim and no evidence at all that the evaluators are poor. **They may be excellent. You have no way to know, and neither does anyone quoting their output at you.**

**The strongest evidence in this area is negative and it comes from the source corpus itself.** That corpus contains a value framework instructing its reader to establish a baseline and never exhibits one being established, catalogues metric definitions and reports none measured, and ships model workbooks with no populated values. **An instrument can be transferred in full working order and still never produce a number anybody acts on**, and the difference is the obligation rather than the instrument.

**The seven-part record and the tier-and-authority marking are this design's inventions.** They are derived from verified structural observations — that a corpus documents advisory and authoritative tiers for the same check, and that thresholds are separable policy — but no source establishes that recording them improves anything.

**The criteria-drift finding is qualitative.** It establishes that the phenomenon is real and consequential, not how often it occurs or how much it costs, and its user study is small by construction.

**One run by one recipient on a prepared morning is a demonstration and not a measurement.** It establishes that the harness started, or that it did not. It establishes nothing about whether it will be run again.
