# S012 · Readiness, and the Workshop You Should Not Run

**Module:** [M03](/wiki/modules/M03.md) · **Quarter:** [Q2](/wiki/quarters/Q2.md) · **Complexity class:** 2
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Learn MCP Server — pre-checking the platform premises a workshop would otherwise spend its time discovering
**Whitepaper:** [WP-012](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-012.md)
**Retrieves from:** S005, S010, S011
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

---

## The situation

You have a charter. The session is in eleven days. The customer's sponsor is enthusiastic, the room is booked, and two of the four people your charter names as necessary have not replied.

Your charter's decision assumes three things about the platform that neither you nor the customer has checked: that the connector you intend to demonstrate exists for their system of record, that the deployment topology you will recommend is available in their region, and that the thing you plan to publish can in fact be published the way you plan to publish it.

Somebody asks whether you are ready.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. List every condition that would have to be true for this session to be worth running. Now mark each one **customer**, **us** or **platform**.
2. Look at your marks. How many are **us**?
3. The session runs badly. Write the sentence your organisation will use to explain why. Whose fault does it assign?
4. Your sponsor says a condition cannot be met but asks you to proceed anyway. Under what circumstances is that the right answer, and what gets recorded?
5. What is the difference between a condition and a preference?

**Item 2 is the session.** Most readiness lists point outward. A list that points only outward cannot fail in a way that implicates the person holding it — and a list with no platform row cannot fail at all until the room is watching.

## What you must produce

A **readiness gate** for your charter, with four parts.

1. **Conditions**, each stated so that a person who was not there could check it. At least one must be about your own organisation — your preparation, your understanding of the customer, your access, your staffing.
2. **Platform premises**, each written as a claim about the product that your charter's decision depends on, each with a named check, a date, and a record of what the documentation does **not** say about it.
3. **A waiver rule**: who may proceed without a condition, and what is written down when they do.
4. **A stop position**: the one condition you will not waive, and the sentence you would say to the sponsor.

## The material

### A gate that can only find fault with somebody else is not a gate

Start with the specimen, because it is the clearest example this programme has of a competent organisation building an instrument that cannot fail.

The practice corpus behind this stage carries a readiness rubric for engagements. **Almost every condition in it describes the customer**: their engagement, their openness, their willingness to invest ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). Alongside it sits a brief of anticipated objections, and the objection the material arms its engineers against most heavily is that the tooling improved and delivery did not speed up. **The prepared answer reattributes the shortfall to the customer's own organisational constraints.**

Put the two artefacts side by side and derive the property that neither shows alone. If the engagement goes well, the model worked. If it goes badly, the customer was not ready. **There is no observation left that counts against the thesis** — and nobody had to intend that for it to be true.

The corpus does something else that matters here. **Its own prerequisites are stated and then immediately followed by an offer to proceed regardless.** Three conditions are named for the model to work, and then the reader is told the work can go ahead anyway. A condition that comes with its own waiver attached in the same breath is a preference wearing a condition's clothes, and that is the whole of opening item 5.

**Its limits, and they are the module's standing caveat.** Access-controlled internal material held only as a white-labelled paraphrase that no public reader can check. A stratum written by many hands over years, contradicting itself in more than a dozen places. **No declined session anywhere. No exclusion criteria of any kind — no domain, scale or regulatory limit, and no criteria for a customer being unsuitable. No failure recorded, ever.** The argument above is an argument about the *structure* of a rubric, not a finding about outcomes, and it must be held as one.

### The refusal, one level down

[S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) established that a practice which cannot decline work cannot be said to exercise judgement. This session is the same claim at a smaller scale, and the scale change is the point: declining an engagement is rare and dramatic, declining a session is ordinary and frequent, and **a person who can do the first but not the second has learned an instance rather than a skill.**

The distinction [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) supplies is the one that does the work here. A **control** is an instruction somebody may follow. A **check** is something that fails without anybody's cooperation. Nearly every readiness rubric in circulation is a list of controls: the sponsor is committed, the team is aligned, the data is available. **None of those can fail without a person deciding that it has.**

### Why an unfalsifiable condition also fails as teaching

There is a second argument for checkability, and it comes from a place learners do not expect.

The error-management training literature identifies a boundary condition on learning by exploration that transfers directly. When the task itself supplies clearly interpretable feedback, exploratory training with error encouragement outperforms guided instruction. **When the task's feedback cannot be readily interpreted — output a novice cannot read, results whose relevance they cannot judge — the advantage disappears** ([research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md)). The authors' own verdict is that this hypothesis received only limited support and that it cannot be concluded the method works only under clear feedback; the moderator test did not reach conventional significance and the coding of feedback clarity was the least reliable in the paper.

Take the direction only, and it says something useful about gates. **A condition that cannot answer back teaches nobody anything.** "The customer is engaged" produces no feedback at any point: not before, when you cannot check it, and not afterwards, when any outcome is consistent with it. A gate made of such conditions cannot improve, because nothing it contains can ever be found wrong.

**Further limits.** All effects in that literature are relative to another training method — no study used a no-training control. **No study in the pool measured on-the-job performance**; every outcome is a laboratory task, almost all of them software training with students. Nothing here licenses a claim about workshops.

### A condition needs a specification, a limitation and an expiry

The assessment literature supplies the shape of a well-formed condition better than any delivery source does.

An entrustable professional activity is described with a **specification and limitations** capturing "no more and no less than what is exactly meant", with explicit restrictions written in; with named **sources of information** that would support the decision; and with an optional **expiry date**, because if the activity has not been practised for a long time the decision may lapse ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). The same source prohibits adjectives in the title of an activity, on the grounds that an adjective smuggles a quality standard into the name of the thing.

Every one of those transfers. A condition needs its limits stated, or it silently expands to cover cases nobody intended to gate. It needs a named source of evidence, or checking it becomes a conversation. **And it needs an expiry, because a platform premise verified four months ago is not a verified premise** — which most readiness rubrics never admit.

**Its limits.** A primer by the concept's originator: guidance, not evaluation. It reports adoption rather than effectiveness, it is a clinical model in which supervisors carry legal responsibility and a course does not, and the full evidence standard it describes is expensive enough that any adaptation is a reduced version and should say so.

### The premises you will otherwise discover in the room

Now the third column, which is the one nobody writes.

Every charter decision rests on claims about the platform, and those claims are checkable in advance at almost no cost. They are also the ones most likely to be wrong, because they are the ones a practitioner holds from the last engagement rather than from this one.

The vendor's own comparison material is full of premises worth checking before you stand in front of anybody ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). The rapid setup topology "lacks network isolation", where the controlled one offers "fine-grained control over data, security, and networking" — so a demonstration built on the fast path is not a demonstration of the thing the customer would run. Distribution is constrained by build choice: "Only agents built with the Teams SDK, Microsoft 365 Agents SDK, or Foundry can be published to the Microsoft Commercial Store with the Agents Toolkit." Adaptive Cards are "limited to schema 1.6 and earlier" in one of the tools. And the whole family is explicit that the options "aren't mutually exclusive" and compose through MCP, so the correct question is per component rather than per system.

**Each of those is a premise a workshop discovers on day two if nobody checked it on day minus eleven.**

### A documentation server is data, not authority

The instrument for checking them is a documentation MCP server, and it carries a hazard that must be taught in the same breath as the capability.

The durable idea is that documentation becomes queryable by the same agent that is helping you draft the gate, so the check happens where the claim is written rather than in a different tab an hour later. The hazard is that the returned text then sits in the model's context looking exactly like an instruction.

The strongest available statement of the correct posture comes from a published agent-configuration corpus, which treats fetched pages, uploaded documents, source repositories, tool output and issue bodies as **data rather than authority**, and requires embedded instructions to be reported rather than obeyed ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)). The same corpus supplies the sentence this whole session is about: **the existence of a control is not evidence that the control works.** Its untrusted-content boundary is a declaration whose effectiveness depends on the host attaching it and the model complying, neither of which the file can guarantee — and its own evaluation lanes are candid that a check may be advisory at one tier and authoritative at another, which looks identical in a green summary.

**Its limits.** Read at a pinned commit, at which one of its own checks was failing. Perishability is extreme: file counts, paths and package versions changed while it was being read. It contains benefit vocabulary its own evidence does not support, and it commits no results at all — **a grader exists, no result corpus does.** Nothing in it licenses a claim about learning.

**And one absence you must carry.** This repository holds **no research note on the Microsoft Learn MCP Server itself.** Its behaviour, its coverage, its freshness and its failure modes are unverified here. Treat everything it returns as a pointer to a documentation page you then read, and record the page rather than the answer.

## The workflow

**Turn your charter's platform assumptions into checked premises, then attack your own gate.**

You will use the Microsoft Learn MCP Server from an agent host that supports MCP tool registration. If none is available, steps 2 to 6 can be run against the documentation directly in a browser; the artefact is the same and step 6 is the one this session most wants you to have done.

**Step 1 — list the platform claims your charter's decision depends on.** Not the ones you are interested in. The ones that, if false, make the decision unavailable. Most charters have between three and six and most learners find one they did not know they were assuming.

**Step 2 — register the documentation server and confirm which tools it exposes.** Typically a search tool returning excerpts and a fetch tool returning a full page as markdown. **Note which one you used for each premise**, because an excerpt is a weaker read than a page and the difference belongs in the record.

**Step 3 — query one premise at a time, and write the query down.** A premise checked by a question you cannot reproduce has not been checked.

**Step 4 — record the silence.** For every premise, write what the documentation does **not** say. This is the field learners skip and the one that pays. A page that describes a capability and never states its limits has told you less than it appears to.

**Step 5 — give every premise an expiry and a named checker.**

```yaml
# readiness-gate.yaml — illustrative shape, not a product artefact.
charter: "Two named engineers committed to a six-week pilot on their own claims data"
session_date: 2026-08-11

conditions:
  - id: C1
    scope: customer
    text: "The four named engineers have accepted the invitation."
    checkable_by: "anyone with the meeting response list"
    state: unmet
  - id: C2
    scope: us                    # REQUIRED. A gate with no us-row cannot implicate its holder.
    text: "We have read the customer's published architecture standard and can name
           two places our proposal conflicts with it."
    checkable_by: "any colleague, by asking for the two places"
    state: unmet

platform_premises:
  - id: P1
    claim: "The rapid setup topology is adequate for the pilot we will propose."
    check: "docs search: setup options network isolation"
    finding: "Documentation states the basic setup lacks network isolation;
              the standard setup provides fine-grained network control."
    read_as: excerpt          # excerpt | full-page. An excerpt is a weaker read.
    verified_on: 2026-07-31
    expires: 2026-09-30       # premises decay. So does this whole file.
    silence: "No guidance on migrating a workload from basic to standard, which is
              the transition our proposal actually requires."
    consequence: "Demonstration must run on the standard topology or be labelled
                  as not representative of production."
  - id: P2
    claim: "What we build in the workshop can be distributed the way we will promise."
    check: "docs search: publish agent commercial store toolkit"
    finding: "Publishing to the Commercial Store is constrained by build choice."
    read_as: excerpt
    verified_on: 2026-07-31
    expires: 2026-09-30
    silence: "No statement of what happens to an agent already built on a path that
              cannot publish. Migration is not addressed."
    consequence: "Build-path decision must be taken in the session, not after it."

waiver_rule:
  who_may_waive: "engagement lead, named in the record"
  what_is_written: ["condition id", "name of person waiving", "date", "what changes as a result"]
  note: "A waiver with no name is recorded as an unmet condition."

stop_position:
  condition: C2
  sentence: "We have not read your architecture standard. Running this on the
             eleventh would produce a proposal you would have to reject, so we
             should move it by a week."
```

**Step 6 — attack your own gate with the agent.** Give it the gate and one instruction: build the strongest case that this session should go ahead with every condition unmet. Then ask it to name the single observation that would settle the disagreement. Then ask the harder question — **which of my conditions could be true and false at the same time without anybody noticing?** Record what you kept and what changed.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** That a premise has a date. You will watch a claim you have been repeating for months turn out to be either superseded, or narrower than you thought, or absent from the documentation altogether — and the third case is the interesting one, because **an absence is not a denial and you will be tempted to treat it as one.** You will also see, in your own silence column, how much of what you believed about the platform came from somewhere other than the platform.

## What good looks like

- Every condition is **checkable by somebody else**, on a date, without a conversation. "The customer is committed" is not checkable. "The named engineers have accepted the invitation" is.
- At least one condition points at you and could plausibly be unmet. If none can be, the gate is decoration.
- Every platform premise carries a check you could re-run, a date, an expiry and a recorded silence.
- The waiver rule **produces a record**, and the record names who waived rather than saying that a waiver occurred.
- The stop position is genuinely disqualifying — it would stop the session, not merely complicate it. [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) established the difference and this is where it is applied one level down.
- The gate distinguishes **a condition for the session to be useful** from a condition for it to be comfortable.
- No condition contains an adjective doing the work of a standard.

## How it goes wrong

- **The disposition gate.** *Warning sign:* your gate would let you explain any bad outcome without changing anything you do. Every condition describes the customer's attitude — engaged, open to change, willing to invest. Attitudes are unfalsifiable in advance and infinitely available as explanations afterwards.
- **The waived prerequisite.** *Warning sign:* the document that lists the conditions also contains the words *even if*. Conditions are stated and then followed by an offer to proceed regardless. **They are therefore not conditions.**
- **The unrecorded waiver.** *Warning sign:* you can say a waiver happened but not who owns it. Everybody remembers agreeing; nobody wrote down who decided.
- **Readiness theatre.** *Warning sign:* the gate has never once come back unmet. It is completed because completion is required, by the person whose session it would block.
- **The gate that only points outward.** *Warning sign:* nothing in your gate could be fixed by doing more work yourself.
- **The undated premise.** *Warning sign:* you can state the platform constraint and cannot say when you last checked it or where. A premise carried from the last engagement is a memory, and memories do not expire visibly.
- **Absence read as denial.** *Warning sign:* your finding column says the documentation does not support something, when what you observed is that the documentation does not mention it. **A silence bounds your knowledge, not the product.**

## Exercises

**None of these is assessed here.** The gate is assessed at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md), where an unmet condition is introduced on the morning of the session and you must decide and record.

**1 · Sort eight conditions.** Mark each *checkable* or *disposition*, and for the checkable ones name who could check it and how.

- *The sponsor is bought in.*
- *The four named engineers have accepted the invitation.*
- *The organisation is ready for AI.*
- *A sandbox subscription exists and we have been added to it.*
- *The team is open to new ways of working.*
- *We can name two places our proposal conflicts with their published architecture standard.*
- *There is executive appetite.*
- *The connector we will demonstrate is listed for their system of record, checked this month.*

> **Model answers.** Checkable: the invitations, the sandbox, the two conflicts, the connector. Disposition: bought in, ready, open, appetite. **Note the asymmetry that makes the disposition list dangerous — every one of them is available afterwards as an explanation, and none of them is available beforehand as a test.** Note also that the two-conflicts condition is the only one that points inward, and that it is the only one you can fix without asking anybody for anything.

**2 · Write the two failure sentences.** Write *this went badly because…* twice — once for a customer-caused failure and once for an us-caused one. Then check that your gate contains a condition that would have caught the second. If it does not, add it.

> **Scoring criterion.** A passing us-sentence names something you could have done differently before the date. "We did not understand their environment" passes only if your gate contains the condition that would have found that out. **If your second sentence is a version of the first with a softer subject, you have written one sentence twice.**

**3 · Read the specimen for its structure, not its intentions.** You have the corpus's readiness rubric and its prepared objection brief. Write, in three sentences, the property of the model that neither artefact shows on its own.

> **Model answer.** A model whose readiness criteria are the assessed party's attitudes, and whose prepared explanation for disappointment is the same party's constraints, has arranged its evidence so that a disappointing result confirms the thesis rather than challenging it. There is no outcome left that counts against it.
>
> **What a passing answer does not say.** That the organisation was dishonest. **A structure that cannot be disconfirmed does not require anybody to have intended it**, and a learner who reads this as an indictment has learned the wrong transferable skill — the point is that thoughtful, experienced, competent people build these without noticing, which is why you check your own.

**4 · Check three premises and record three silences.** Using the documentation server, verify three platform claims your charter depends on. For each, record the query, whether you read an excerpt or a full page, the finding, and what the documentation does not say.

> **What to notice.** The silence column will be the most useful thing in your gate and the hardest to write, because the natural response to a page that does not mention something is to move on. **At least one of your three will turn out to be a claim the documentation neither supports nor contradicts**, and that is the premise most likely to be discovered in the room — it is also the one you cannot resolve by reading, only by asking the customer or by building something.

**5 · Go back to your own item 2.** Find the count you wrote at opening item 2 — how many of your conditions were marked **us**. **Do not change it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then rewrite your gate.

> **Criterion.** A passing gate contains at least one inward condition that could plausibly be unmet on the day, that is checkable by a colleague without a conversation, and whose remedy is work you could do yourself before the date. A gate whose only inward condition is that the team is prepared fails on all three: unfalsifiable, uncheckable, unfixable.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer beside their own produced no benefit, and only the group **instructed to compare them** learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference is. **Its limits: school, university and laboratory evidence, with no adult-professional study in its reference list.**

**6 · Hand your gate to a peer.** Ask them one question: **"What would have to happen for you to tell me not to run this?"**

> **What to notice.** If they cannot answer, the gate is not a gate. If their answer is something that has never happened to anyone, it is still not a gate. **The useful failure is a peer who names a condition you have and would waive**, because you have then discovered your stop position is somewhere other than where you wrote it.

## What this does not establish

Nothing here shows that gated sessions go better than ungated ones.

The corpus supplies **no session it declined to run**, no exclusion criteria of any kind, and no domain, scale or regulatory limit on the model. Its prerequisites are stated and immediately waived on its own pages. The argument that a disposition-only rubric is unfalsifiable is **an argument about the structure of the rubric, not a finding about outcomes**, and it should be held as one. It is also access-controlled, so no public reader can check the paraphrase against its source.

The claim that a recorded waiver behaves differently from an unrecorded one is **design reasoning with no external warrant.** It is included because an unrecorded decision cannot be reviewed, which is a property of records rather than evidence about behaviour.

**The error-management material establishes a boundary condition and its own authors do not claim it firmly.** The feedback-clarity moderator did not reach conventional significance, the coding of it was the least reliable in the paper, and the authors state that it cannot be concluded the method works only under clear feedback. Every effect in that literature is relative to another training method — there was no no-training control anywhere in the pool — and **no study measured on-the-job performance.** Its transfer to readiness gates is this session's inference, not the paper's claim.

**The assessment material is guidance from an advocate.** It reports adoption rather than effectiveness, it comes from a clinical setting in which supervisors carry legal responsibility, and its full evidence standard is expensive enough that any adaptation is a reduced version.

**The platform documentation describes what is offered and supplies no evidence that following its recommendations produces better outcomes.** It was read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches; several comparison tables were truncated; it compares Microsoft options only with each other; and its product names are in active flux, so a limitation recorded today may be closed tomorrow.

**The agent-configuration corpus was read at a pinned commit at which one of its own checks was failing**, and it commits no results at all — it has graders and no result corpus, and says so. Its untrusted-content boundary is a declaration whose effectiveness depends on the host and the model, and is not demonstrated anywhere.

**Most importantly: this repository holds no research note on the Microsoft Learn MCP Server itself.** Its coverage, freshness, ranking behaviour and failure modes are entirely unverified here. Nothing in this session establishes that a documentation server returns the right page, the current page, or any page at all for a given premise — which is why the workflow records the query and the read depth rather than the answer.

**No effect size is asserted anywhere in this session.** Direction and mechanism only.
