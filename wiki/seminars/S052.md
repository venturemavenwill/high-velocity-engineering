# S052 · A Threat Bound to a Component and a Flow

**Module:** [M11](/wiki/modules/M11.md) · **Quarter:** [Q6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-052](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md)
**Retrieves from:** S007, S032, S042, S051
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

---

## The situation

You have the model from [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md). Now the threats, under one rule that will do more work than anything else in the module.

**A threat survives only if it can be bound to at least one named component and at least one numbered flow. Anything that cannot be is discarded, and the discard is logged with its reason.**

Answer these in writing before instruction.

1. Write a threat you believe applies to your system. Which component? Which flow number?
2. If your answer to item 1 was *all of them*, what did you actually write down?
3. Name a way your system could be harmed that involves no dataflow at all.
4. Where did your list of candidate threats come from, and what was that source unable to know about?
5. What would a threat model with no open questions and no discarded threats be evidence of?

**Item 3 is the session.** Everything you produce there is real, is a threat to the customer, and will be excluded by the rule you are about to adopt. Item 5 has one honest answer and it is not *thoroughness*.

## Before you begin: a restriction that applies to this session and no other

**Threat categories and control classes only.** You may state what a class of attack targets and what property it violates. **You may not write down how to perform one**, and neither may anything you submit.

The material this module is derived from contains operational detail and worked examples. **None of it is reproduced anywhere in this programme, and the omission is a decision rather than a gap.** The restriction has a real teaching cost — you will produce a thinner artefact than a practitioner would — and it is accepted deliberately, because this work is public and because a threat model that reads as a manual has changed what it is.

## What you must produce

**Two artefacts, and the second is the assessed one.**

**A threat registry**, in which every entry carries an identifier, a bound component, a bound flow number, the property it violates, and a ranking. Alongside it: a compact index — a deliberately lossy projection, a few columns per entry — for the next stage to join against, and a reverse index from component to threat.

**An exclusion register.** Every candidate you discarded, its logged reason, and — the part that does not exist in the source material — **a grouping of those discards into the categories they fall into.** Not a list of individual losses. The shape of what your rule cannot see.

## What good looks like

- **Every surviving threat names a component and a flow number**, and a reader can go to that component.
- **No threat is true of software in general.** If the entry would survive being pasted into another system's model unchanged, it is not a finding about this system.
- **The compact index is genuinely lossy** and you can say what it dropped.
- **The exclusion register is grouped**, and the groups are named as categories rather than as incidents. Operator action. Supply chain. Human process. Out-of-band administration. Whatever else yours contains.
- **Each discard reason is one you would defend out loud**, because the failure mode here is a reason that is individually plausible and collectively catastrophic.
- **The registry states where its candidates came from**, and what that source could not have known.
- **Nothing in it is operational.**

## How it goes wrong

- **The general threat** — an entry that is true of all software, bound to a component because something had to go in the column. *Warning sign:* the entry would survive being copied into another system's model.
- **The plausible discard** — every reason in your log is defensible on its own, and the pile of them removes an entire class of risk. *Warning sign:* you never re-read the log after writing it.
- **The catalogue ceiling** — your threats are exactly the threats your source knew about, and its blind spots are now yours, invisibly. *Warning sign:* you cannot say how old the source is.
- **The empty exclusion register** — the rule excluded nothing, which means it was not applied. *Warning sign:* you feel good about coverage.
- **The forced binding** — a real threat about people or process is attached to a component so that it can survive. *Warning sign:* the flow number is arbitrary and you know it.
- **The operational drift** — the entry starts explaining how. *Warning sign:* somebody could follow it.

## Do it

**First, enumerate against the characteristics checklist, not against a mnemonic.** The checklist from [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) is the routing key: it says which architectural traits your system has, with evidence, and therefore which classes of threat are in play at all. A mnemonic asks the same questions of every system. **The checklist asks the questions your system earned.**

**Second, bind or discard, and never bend.** For each candidate, name the component and the flow. If you cannot, discard it and write the reason. **Do not attach it to something adjacent so that it survives** — a forced binding produces an entry an engineer will investigate and find nothing behind, and [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md) will then map a control to it.

**Third, re-read your discard log adversarially**, which nobody in the source material ever does. Read it as somebody who wants the system to be harmed. Ask of each reason: *is this true, or is it just formatted like a reason?*

**Fourth, group the discards into categories and write the exclusion register.** This is the artefact the source does not have, and its absence there is why nobody notices what the rule removed. **A method that never states its own scope exclusions invites the reader to assume it has none.**

**Fifth, date your source.** Whatever supplied your candidate threats — a catalogue, a standard, a model's training — record what it is and when it was current. Nothing in the pipeline this session is derived from records this anywhere in any output, and **a stale catalogue produces a confident, well-formatted, out-of-date threat model containing no signal that this happened.**

Use an agent to enumerate, then use a second one, given only the compact index, to argue that your registry is missing something. Record what you kept.

## Show it

Hand somebody **only your discard log** — not the registry.

Their job is to find the threat you should not have dropped, and to say what it would have cost you. **A reviewer given the surviving threats will validate them, because reading a list of plausible entries is not a test of anything.** The discards are where the information is.

Then ask them the second question: **what class of harm is missing from both lists?**

## What this does not establish

**Recall is unmeasured here and it is unmeasured in the source, which explicitly declines to measure it.** The evaluator in the material behind this module refuses to deduct for threats that are absent, listing them separately as unscored suggestions. That is defensible — a scorer that penalises the absence of the unknowable is unusable — and it means the resulting score is silent on the most consequential failure mode available.

**This session does not fix that.** The exclusion register makes one class of omission visible: the class your own binding rule removed, which you saw go. **It cannot make visible the threats nobody thought of**, and no artefact in this programme can.

**Nothing establishes that a bound threat model finds more real vulnerabilities than an unbound one.** The binding rule is a practitioner judgement carried from experienced people, and its consequence — that everything about operators, supply chain, human process and out-of-band administration disappears — is a structural fact rather than a measurement. The rule and its blind spot are the same rule, and this session's position is that the only honest response is to write the blind spot down.

## Perishable content in this session

| Durable claim | Perishable instance |
|---|---|
| A threat that binds to nothing is not a threat for this system | The catalogue or standard supplying your candidate threats this term |
| The discard log is where the information is | Current tooling's willingness to emit a discard log at all |
| A source's blind spots become yours, invisibly | The currency of whatever knowledge base is in use now |
| A model with no discards and no open questions is suspect | Present reporting conventions, in which absence reads as weakness |
