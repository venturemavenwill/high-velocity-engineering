# S088 · What This Engagement Did Not Establish

**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-088](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-088.md)

**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

Somebody is scoping the next engagement now. They were not on this one. Whatever you write down is what they will have.

The baseline covers only part of the compliance record and was established later than it should have been. The assessor's next sampling window will not produce anything comparable before the scoping decision is made. **Nobody is going to ask you for this document**, and its absence will not be noticed.

Answer these in writing before the session opens.

1. List three things this engagement is now believed to have shown. For each, say who would say it, and on what.
2. Which of those three is an activity dressed as an outcome?
3. What did you find that argues against your own account of this engagement's value?
4. Who bears the cost if the next team assumes something you did not establish?
5. What is outside the scope of everything you produced, and where is that written down?

**Item 5 is the one nobody does.** A method that never states its own exclusions invites every reader to assume it has none, and that assumption is made silently and immediately.

## What you must produce

A **limits register** for the engagement, sorted into the four classes this programme uses everywhere and nothing else.

- **Verified here** — established by this engagement, with the artefact that establishes it and the person who could reproduce it.
- **Cited, not verified here** — believed on general grounds. **Direction and mechanism only. No magnitude, ever.**
- **Design reasoning with no external warrant** — the engagement's own arguments, including the ones it acted on.
- **Grounded in vendor documentation** — what the products' own documentation says, at what version, and what it does not say that somebody relying on it would need.

Plus two mandatory entries with nowhere else to live: **the scope exclusions**, and **at least one result that undermines your own position.**

## What good looks like

- Every claim in the register is placed in exactly one class, and the placement is arguable. **A claim that could be in two classes is two claims.**
- The verified class is small. It is always smaller than the account everyone has been telling, and the gap between the two is the register's reason for existing.
- **No magnitude appears in the cited class.** Not a proportion, not a multiple, not a range. The direction and the mechanism are what a general claim licenses and they are all it licenses.
- The vendor class records a version and a silence. **What documentation omits is more useful to the next team than what it contains**, because the omission is what they will otherwise assume.
- The undermining result is genuinely undermining. It is not a limitation you had already priced in and it is not a caveat about scope.
- The register names who bears the cost of each unestablished thing being assumed true. That is the field that makes it a professional document rather than a modest one.

## How it goes wrong

Five failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **Activity reported as outcome** — the register lists what was built, deployed, delivered and attended. *Warning sign:* the verified class is the longest one. **A body of work whose central claim is that only realised benefit counts, supported almost entirely by measures of activity, is the specimen this session exists to stop you becoming.**
- **The framework as furniture** — recognised measurement frameworks are named, and no measurement from any of them appears. *Warning sign:* you cited an approach and reported no result from it.
- **The specimen that became a norm** — an illustrative figure from somewhere is now the scale against which real numbers are read. *Warning sign:* somebody described a customer's figure as high or low without naming what it was high or low against.
- **The unfalsifiable account** — every disappointing result is explained by the customer's readiness, so no result could disturb the account. *Warning sign:* your explanation for the thing that went badly is about them. **This is the single most instructive failure available in this material and it is committed by experienced people.**
- **The register written for the reader's comfort** — every limit is one you can afford to admit. *Warning sign:* nothing in the document would change the next scoping decision.

## Do it

Work from the supplied situation in [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md), and from everything this programme has produced since [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md).

Start with the account being told about this engagement, not with the artefacts. **Write the three sentences somebody on your side would say about it in a room**, and then sort those three sentences. That is a faster route to the register than working forwards, and it puts the flattering claims where the work is.

Then the baseline. It is partial and late. Decide what that permits anybody to say, and say it in the register in the exact words a scoping conversation could quote — because the alternative is that somebody quotes the number and drops the qualification, which is how a limitation becomes a benchmark.

Then the exclusions. What did nobody look at? Whose interests were never represented? What would have shown up if somebody had?

## Show it

Read the undermining result. Not the register, not the summary — the one result that makes your engagement look worse.

Then answer one question: **who would be harmed if this document were never written, and would they know?**

## What this does not establish

**That anybody reads a document like this.** No source behind this programme describes one being produced, and no engagement anywhere in the material closes with an account of what it did not establish. The four classes are this repository's own apparatus, applied to itself, and extended here to an engagement on nothing but the argument that a practice which cannot state its own limits will be believed past them.

**The corpus's own position is the counter-example rather than the precedent.** It opens on the excellent claim that everything short of realised benefit is activity, and then supports itself almost entirely with activity measures. It devotes space to externally published measurement frameworks and reports no measurement from any of them. It carries figures inside illustrative answer menus that will function as norms for anyone who reads them. It prepares an answer to the objection that its tooling improved and delivery did not, and that answer reattributes the shortfall to the customer. **Taken with a readiness rubric composed of customer dispositions, the evidence has been arranged so that a disappointing result confirms the thesis rather than testing it.**

That is not dishonesty and it is not incompetence. It is what happens to a practice that never writes down what it did not establish. **This session is the countermeasure and there is no evidence that it works.**
