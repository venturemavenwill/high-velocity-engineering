## The situation

Somebody is scoping the next engagement now. They were not on this one. Whatever you write down is what they will have.

The baseline covers only part of the compliance record and was established later than it should have been. The assessor's next sampling window will not produce anything comparable before the scoping decision is made. **Nobody is going to ask you for this document**, and its absence will not be noticed by anybody.

**Answer these in writing before reading further.** Item 5 is the one almost nobody does, and it is what this session is about.

1. List three things this engagement is now believed to have shown. For each, say who would say it, and on what.
2. Which of those three is an activity dressed as an outcome?
3. What did you find that argues against your own account of this engagement's value?
4. Who bears the cost if the next team assumes something you did not establish?
5. What is outside the scope of everything you produced, and where is that written down?

Most people answer 1 fluently, hesitate on 2, produce a caveat rather than a finding for 3, and leave 5 empty. **A method that never states its own exclusions invites every reader to assume it has none**, and that assumption is made silently and immediately by somebody who will never meet you.

This session is the programme's most self-implicating, and you should know that before you start. **The apparatus you are about to apply to your engagement is the same apparatus every paper in this wiki applies to itself**, and by the end of the session you will have applied it to the programme too. **A graduate who can perform this discipline on a vendor's documentation and not on their own work has not learned this programme's actual subject.**

## What you must produce

A **limits register** for the engagement, sorted into the four classes this programme uses everywhere and nothing else.

1. **Verified here** — established by this engagement, with the artefact that establishes it and the person who could reproduce it.
2. **Cited, not verified here** — believed on general grounds. **Direction and mechanism only. No magnitude, ever.**
3. **Design reasoning with no external warrant** — the engagement's own arguments, including the ones it acted on.
4. **Grounded in vendor documentation** — what the products' own documentation says, at what version, and **what it does not say that somebody relying on it would need.**

Plus two mandatory entries with nowhere else to live: **the scope exclusions**, and **at least one result that undermines your own position.**

## The material

### The four classes, and why the fourth is the one that carries the value

Every whitepaper in this wiki closes by sorting its claims into four classes. The sort is not a formality and the classes are not degrees of confidence — they are **different kinds of thing**, and mixing them is the defect the apparatus exists to prevent.

**Verified here** means somebody in this engagement produced an artefact that establishes the claim, and can name who could reproduce it. The test is reproduction, not conviction.

**Cited, not verified here** means the claim rests on general grounds that were not checked on this engagement. **The rule attached to this class is absolute: direction and mechanism only, never a magnitude.** A claim you did not verify cannot carry a number, because the number is the part that would have required verification.

**Design reasoning with no external warrant** is the engagement's own argument. It is not a lesser class — much of what a forward-deployed engineer does is design reasoning, and most of it is correct. What it may not do is borrow the authority of the classes above it.

**Grounded in vendor documentation, with its version and its silence recorded** is the class most people write wrongly, because they record what the documentation says and stop. **The value is in the silence.** What the documentation omits is worth more to the next team than what it contains, because the omission is what they will otherwise assume away.

Here is a worked example you can check. The evaluation framework's documentation for this platform is precise and useful: three core types, two evaluator tiers, three conversation split strategies, repetitions to detect non-deterministic behaviour, a four-category evaluator catalogue, and pass-or-fail output carrying a `reason`, a `threshold` and a `passed` field ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). It states the load-bearing warning plainly: **"Multi-turn conversations must be split into query and response halves for evaluation. How you split determines *what you're evaluating*."**

**And it publishes no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold.** Most of those evaluators are model-judged. **Assert no agreement figure for any of them, here or anywhere** — none is published, and a team that stands up the full harness, produces a dashboard of scores and believes those scores correspond to what a competent human would say has no basis for the belief.

**The documentation's own limits:** read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, with structure reconstructed; most agent evaluators are marked preview; one consulted page is an explicitly superseded variant. **Re-verify before teaching any specific.**

### An apparatus described without its outputs is the commonest failure in this field

The cleanest specimen available is not a bad document. It is a good one.

A first-party engineering blog describing the knowledge service behind the platform's documentation server records that the team built a **"golden dataset"** of questions, approved answers and best source articles, and used it for before-and-after tests on indexing changes ([research/06-microsoft-platform/learn-mcp-server-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/learn-mcp-server-docs.md)). It records that root-cause analysis of a single unsatisfactory response could take substantial time. It describes chunking, embedding, query rewriting, expansion, clarification, re-ranking, chunk expansion, filtering and compression.

**It publishes no result from any of it.** No recall figure, no ranking metric, no groundedness score, no before-and-after difference. **The apparatus of measurement is described in detail and its outputs are withheld.**

The same family's general-availability announcement is worth reading as an evidence claim, because it is the only one the source makes: the server added its endpoint and **"saw usage grow without issues."** That is an availability-and-adoption argument. **It says nothing about whether the answers were right.**

Read this as the shape of your own engagement's account rather than as a complaint. **The authority of that documentation is definitional and it is evidential nowhere.** It tells you with total authority what the thing is, and gives you no reason to believe it makes anything better. That distinction — definitional against evidential — is the one your register exists to preserve about your own work.

**Its limits:** first-party throughout; extremely perishable, with dated changes across nine months and a developer reference reserving the right to change the tool list without notice; collapsed sections on three pages were not read; the Terms of Use was not read. **Its repository's outcome claims are marketing and are prohibited in this wiki in every form, including paraphrase.**

### The failure is not lying. It is the drift by which activity becomes outcome

The corpus behind this programme is the clearest available specimen of the drift, and **it is a specimen because it is good rather than because it is sloppy** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)).

Its organising thesis is excellent and is put to an engineering audience deliberately, because it is uncomfortable: **everything short of realised benefit is activity** — including the team, the architecture, the fundamentals and the deployment. That is the correct claim and it is hard to argue with.

Then almost every figure the corpus offers in its own support measures activity: elapsed time, headcount, proportions of code generated, minutes to add a feature, backlog readiness. It devotes space to externally published measurement frameworks and **reports no measurement from any of them**. **It fails its own test in the same documents that state it, in the same register, without noticing.**

And the arrangement compounds. Its prepared answer to the objection *your tooling improved and our delivery did not* reattributes the shortfall to the customer. Its readiness rubric is composed almost entirely of customer dispositions. **Taken together, the evidence has been arranged so that a disappointing result confirms the thesis rather than testing it.**

**The corpus's limits are the material.** Internal, paraphrased here and never named or quoted. **No declined engagement. No non-synthetic failure — every case study succeeds and the single failure narrative is a synthetic composite, fictional by its own admission. No exclusion criteria of any kind: no domain limit, no scale limit, no regulatory limit, no description of a customer who would be unsuitable. No cost side anywhere. No defect, incident, security-finding or maintainability data. No sustainment evidence — whether any delivered system survived is not reported once. Not one reference, study or citation appears anywhere in it.** Its prerequisites are stated and immediately waived, which means they are not prerequisites.

The neighbouring failure is easier to live with and worth the contrast. The openly published engineering handbook **asserts four unsupported magnitude claims** — figures with no method, no baseline and no citation ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md)). **This repository records those four as specimens and reproduces none of them, here or anywhere**, because naming the pattern is the teaching and repeating the figures would install the hazard. **An unsupported figure is locatable, dateable, sourceable and refutable.** A claim produced by an apparatus that asserts nothing offers no such purchase. **The handbook's failure is worse manners and a better epistemic position**, and it must be cited by page and by that page's own footer date.

### A number without an interval has not told you whether anything was found

The statistical treatment behind this module opens by attacking the convention every one of these specimens exemplifies: results are reported with a highest-number-is-best mentality, in which a leading figure is highlighted **"but not necessarily to test that result for any kind of statistical significance"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)).

Two of its conclusions bear directly on your register. **A comparison on a small evaluation set frequently cannot distinguish two systems at all**, and the correct response is to determine in advance what difference the instrument could have detected rather than to report the difference it happened to show. And **where items arrive in related groups rather than independently, an uncorrected interval is too narrow**, which makes a measurement look more precise than it is.

For this session that yields one sentence, and it belongs in your register verbatim wherever a comparison appears: **an apparatus that emits a number and no interval has not told you whether it found anything.** Your baseline covers part of the record and was established late. **Say what that permits anybody to say, in the words a scoping conversation could quote**, because the alternative is that somebody quotes the number and drops the qualification, which is how a limitation becomes a benchmark.

**Its limits:** an analytical paper rather than an empirical one, with most illustrative magnitudes resting on stylised assumptions; its machinery is valid only where the items genuinely sample the population you care about; and **statistical validity says nothing about construct validity — a tight interval around a meaningless metric is still meaningless.** **No figure from it is reproduced here.**

One governance point completes it. The external number you might borrow to fill your own gap has its own provenance problem, one layer further away and harder to inspect: an audit of the de facto public ranking of frontier models found scores distorted by undisclosed platform policy, producing **"biased Arena scores due to selective disclosure of performance results"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md)). **Its allocation figures are external estimates by its own account and no figure from it is reproduced here.**

### A practice states its limits only where saying so is survivable

One mechanism explains why registers of this kind are rare, and it is not laziness.

Writing down what your own engagement did not establish is speaking up when silence is cheaper, which is the construct Edmondson studies and Frazier synthesises ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md); [research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)). Edmondson's account of the mechanism is that people make a quick, not entirely conscious, risk calculation, **discounting the future** — underweighting the consequence that plays out slowly and overweighting the response that arrives immediately.

That is exactly the calculation in front of you. **The cost of the undermining result is immediate and lands on you; the cost of its absence is delayed and lands on somebody you will never meet.** The register is a device for making the second cost visible at the moment the first one is being paid, and **the field that does the work is the one naming who bears the cost.**

**Their limits:** the Edmondson note covers a 2019 trade book read in full and a 1999 paper that was **not read**, with case studies almost entirely secondary. The Frazier note is written from the official abstract of a closed-access meta-analysis. **Direction and mechanism only, and no magnitude from either appears here.**

## The workflow

**Sort the account, not the artefacts — then turn the same instrument on this programme.**

**Step 1 — write the account before you write the register.** Write the three sentences somebody on your side would say about this engagement in a room. Not what is true; **what would be said.** That is a faster route to the register than working forwards from artefacts, and it puts the flattering claims where the work is.

**Step 2 — sort those three sentences into the four classes, and defend each placement.** A claim that could sit in two classes is two claims and must be split. **A sentence you cannot place is a sentence nobody should be repeating.**

**Step 3 — strip every magnitude out of class 2.** Anything believed on general grounds carries direction and mechanism only. If a number survives into class 2, either move the claim to class 1 with the artefact that establishes it, or delete the number.

**Step 4 — write the vendor class properly, which means writing the silences.** For each product this engagement relied on: what does its documentation say, at what version, read on what date — and **what does it not say that somebody relying on it would need?** Record where you looked. An absence is evidence only if the search was competent.

**Step 5 — write the baseline sentence a scoping conversation could quote.** The baseline is partial and late. State what that permits anybody to say, in quotable words, in the register.

**Step 6 — write the exclusions.** What did nobody look at? Whose interests were never represented? What would have shown up if somebody had? **Then name who bears the cost of each exclusion being assumed away.**

**Step 7 — write the undermining result.** One finding that damages your own account of this engagement's value. Not a caveat about scope; not a limitation you had already priced in.

**Step 8 — record it in the register's own shape.**

```yaml
# .evidence/limits-register.yaml   <- the SAME four classes every paper in this wiki uses.
# The register is addressed to somebody who was not here and will not ask for it.

verified_here:
  - claim: "<what this engagement established>"
    artefact: "<the thing that establishes it>"
    reproducible_by: "<named person or role>"

cited_not_verified_here:
  - claim: "<direction and mechanism only>"
    magnitude: null          # MUST be null. A number here is a defect.
    basis: "general grounds; not checked on this engagement"

design_reasoning_no_warrant:
  - claim: "<the engagement's own argument>"
    acted_on: true           # record the ones you ACTED on, not just the ones you held

vendor_documentation:
  - product: "<name>"
    version: "<version or page date>"
    read_on: "<YYYY-MM-DD>"
    says: "<what it establishes; definitional authority only>"
    does_not_say: "<the silence somebody relying on it would need>"
    searched: ["<where you looked for the silence>"]

scope_exclusions:
  - excluded: "<what nobody looked at>"
    whose_interest_unrepresented: "<who>"
    cost_borne_by: "<who pays if this is assumed away>"   # the field that makes it professional

undermining_results:
  - finding: "<the one result that makes this engagement look worse>"
    why_it_undermines: "<in one sentence, without softening>"

self_audit:                  # the same instrument, turned on the programme teaching it
  predictions_written: "hundreds, each naming an instrument"
  predictions_resolved: 0
  reason: "no cohort has run"
  therefore: "This programme's prediction register is machinery. It is not a result."
```

**Step 9 — turn the instrument on this programme, in writing.** Open any whitepaper in this wiki, find its §9, and place the prediction register in one of the four classes. Then write the programme's own limits register entry, using the concessions in the section below. **This is not a rhetorical flourish.** A learner who can perform the sort on a vendor's documentation and not on the material teaching them has learned a manner rather than a method.

**Step 10 — attack your own register.** One prompt: *for each item in my `verified_here` class, argue that it belongs in `design_reasoning_no_warrant` instead — then find the claim I have placed in `cited_not_verified_here` that still carries an implied magnitude.* Read it for the placement you defended too easily. Keep what is better than your own reasoning and record what you rejected. **Record separately that the instrument is not independent of the tooling under discussion**, and that an agent which endorsed your register was not used as an instrument.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** The length of the `verified_here` list beside the account you wrote in step 1. **They sit on one screen, they were produced half an hour apart, and the first is always shorter.** No argument produces that.

## What good looks like

- Every claim sits in exactly one class, and the placement is arguable. **A claim that could be in two classes is two claims.**
- **The verified class is small.** It is always smaller than the account everyone has been telling, and the gap between the two is the register's reason for existing.
- **No magnitude appears in the cited class.** Not a proportion, not a multiple, not a range. Direction and mechanism are what a general claim licenses and they are all it licenses.
- **The vendor class records a version, a date and a silence, and says where you looked.** What documentation omits is more useful to the next team than what it contains.
- The undermining result is genuinely undermining. It is not a limitation you had already priced in and it is not a caveat about scope.
- **The register names who bears the cost of each unestablished thing being assumed true.** That field is what makes it a professional document rather than a modest one.
- The programme is in the register too, sorted by the same rules as everything else.

## How it goes wrong

- **Activity reported as outcome** — the register lists what was built, deployed, delivered and attended. *Warning sign:* the verified class is the longest one. **A body of work whose central claim is that only realised benefit counts, supported almost entirely by measures of activity, is the specimen this session exists to stop you becoming.**
- **The framework as furniture** — recognised measurement frameworks are named and no measurement from any of them appears. *Warning sign:* you cited an approach and reported no result from it.
- **The specimen that became a norm** — an illustrative figure from somewhere is now the scale against which real numbers are read. *Warning sign:* somebody described a customer's figure as high or low without naming what it was high or low against.
- **The unfalsifiable account** — every disappointing result is explained by the customer's readiness, so no result could disturb the account. *Warning sign:* your explanation for the thing that went badly is about them. **This is the single most instructive failure available in this material and it is committed by experienced people.**
- **The register written for the reader's comfort** — every limit is one you can afford to admit. *Warning sign:* nothing in the document would change the next scoping decision.
- **The vendor class written as a summary** — what the documentation says is recorded and what it omits is not. *Warning sign:* your `does_not_say` field is empty and you did not search.
- **The exemption** — the register is applied to the engagement and not to the method that produced it. *Warning sign:* your register has no entry about the apparatus you used to write it.

## Exercises

**None of these is assessed.** The limits register is carried to [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md), where it is one of the artefacts the forward brief rests on, and it crosses into [Q1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md) so that the next qualification decision is made against a known gap rather than a good feeling.

**1 · Sort seven statements into the four classes.** For each, say what it licenses.

- *We ran the harness and three artefacts failed.* · *The evaluator's threshold is three, and the documentation does not say why.* · *Grounding improves answer accuracy.* · *Splitting a conversation at the last turn measures response quality at a point.* · *Reviewers catch things the model does not, so the review step stays.* · *The vendor publishes no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator.* · *This approach is proven.*

> **Model answers.** The first is **verified here** — somebody ran something and recorded what happened, bound to a pin. The second and fourth are **vendor documentation**, and the second is the better entry because it records a silence alongside the fact. The third is **not placeable at all**: it is asserted nowhere in the documentation, it is a marketing claim in the associated repository, and **it is prohibited in this wiki** — the correct handling is to strike it and record that no measurement of it is published. The fifth is **design reasoning with no external warrant**, and it may well be right. The sixth is **vendor documentation recorded as a silence**, and it is the most valuable entry in the list. The seventh is an outcome claim with nothing behind it. **The general rule: a statement is verified if somebody could reproduce it, cited if you believe it on general grounds and have stripped its number, design reasoning if it is your argument, and vendor-grounded only when its version, its date and its silence travel with it.**

**2 · Write one vendor entry properly, using the platform.** Take one product this engagement relied on. Record what its documentation establishes, at what version, read on what date — and then spend the same effort finding what it does not say.

> **What you should find.** For the evaluation tooling: a complete, precise, well-organised catalogue — and **no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold**, with most evaluators marked preview and at least one consulted page superseded. **Assert no agreement figure. If you find one published, that is a real finding and this session's material needs revising** — record the page and the date and file it as a report through [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md).

**3 · Go back to your own item 5, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 5 — what is outside the scope of everything you produced, and where that is written down. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then write the honest version.

> **Criterion.** A pass names something specific that nobody looked at, names whose interest was therefore unrepresented, and names who bears the cost if the next team assumes it was covered. *"We did not evaluate for accessibility"* is half a pass — it names the exclusion and not the bearer. *"Nobody sampled the sites outside the original subset, so the two sites added late have no comparable record; the next team will read the aggregate as covering them, and the cost falls on whoever is asked to defend the compliance position for those sites"* passes. **If your answer to item 5 was blank, write that down and keep it: an empty answer to the question nobody asks is this session's most honest artefact.**
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**4 · Turn the instrument on this programme.** Open any whitepaper in this wiki, find its §9, and place the prediction register in one of the four classes.

> **Scoring criterion.** A pass places it in **design reasoning with no external warrant** and states that **not one prediction in this repository has been resolved, because no cohort has run.** A fail places it in *verified here*, which is the error this session is about, committed on the material teaching it. **The purpose is not humility.** It is to establish that the error is structural: this repository was written by people who knew the distinction, stated it in every paper, and still built an apparatus and no results. **If it can happen here it will happen to you.**

**5 · Write the undermining result and read it aloud.** One finding that makes your engagement look worse. Then answer one question in front of the room: **who would be harmed if this document were never written, and would they know?**

> **What to notice.** That the answer to the second half is almost always *no*. **An unwritten limits register harms somebody who cannot detect the harm**, which is why nothing in the incentive structure produces one and why the discipline has to be installed rather than expected.

**6 · Find the arrangement that makes a disappointment into a confirmation.** Take any account of a method — the corpus behind this programme, a vendor's, or your own — and find the place where a bad result is pre-explained by something about the recipient.

> **What to expect.** You will find it quickly and it will not look like a defence when you find it. It looks like realism. **A readiness rubric composed of the customer's dispositions, plus a prepared answer that reattributes shortfall to the customer, is an arrangement in which no outcome can disconfirm the thesis** — and nobody had to intend that for it to be true.

## What this does not establish

**That anybody reads a document like this.** No source behind this programme describes one being produced, and no engagement anywhere in the material closes with an account of what it did not establish. The four classes are this repository's own apparatus, applied to itself and extended here to an engagement on nothing but the argument that **a practice which cannot state its own limits will be believed past them.** That argument is class 3.

**The corpus is the counter-example rather than the precedent**, in the terms set out above: an excellent organising thesis, supported almost entirely by activity measures, in the same documents, without noticing. **That is not dishonesty and it is not incompetence.** It is what happens to a practice that never writes down what it did not establish. This session is the countermeasure and **there is no evidence that countermeasures of this kind work.**

**And now this programme's own register, stated plainly because there is nowhere else it belongs.**

**Nothing in this programme has been measured.** Its papers close with hundreds of falsifiable predictions, each naming an instrument, each with its coding scheme specified and its artefacts defined — and **not one has been resolved, because no cohort has run.** The apparatus is complete. **It is machinery, and it is not a result**, and a reader who mistook the existence of the prediction register for evidence about this programme's effectiveness would be making exactly the error this session exists to prevent.

**Stage 1 and Stage 9 are class 3 throughout, and both are written past their source.** Stage 1 teaches qualification from a corpus that records one refusal and no declined engagement. Stage 9 teaches compounding from a cycle the corpus implies in hundreds of scattered references and states as a model nowhere. Both are arguments. Neither is a finding.

**The pedagogical design rests on studies of school and university learners.** Every study behind the generation-first pattern used in all ninety sessions is a school, university or laboratory study. **Its author states that the cycle works in corporate training and adult education and cites nothing in his own reference list for that sentence.** This programme's learners are working professionals on multi-week engagements. **The extrapolation is the most serious methodological objection to the whole teaching pattern**, and the convergent warrant from error-management training is a partial mitigation rather than a rescue.

**The practice corpus reports no declined engagement, no non-synthetic failure and no outcome data of any kind** — no cost side, no defect or incident data, no sustainment evidence, and not one reference or citation anywhere in it.

**And the prediction this programme makes about itself: most of its own predictions will never be measured.** Not some. Most. **That is the design's largest weakness and it should be read as a weakness rather than as charming humility.** A body of work that specifies its own instruments and never applies them has built the most elaborate possible apparatus for not finding out. **Hold that beside the golden dataset with no published result and notice that they are the same object.**
