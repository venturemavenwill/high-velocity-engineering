# S088 · Residual Risk, Stated Plainly

**Module:** [M18](/wiki/modules/M18.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** PE · SG · ME
**Whitepaper:** [WP-088](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-088.md)
**Depends on:** S050, S083, S087
**Re-tests:** S028
**Platform anchor:** the Microsoft Agent 365 licence transition of 1 July 2026, as a documented instance of a guarantee changing hands; and Foundry evaluator thresholds, as a criterion nobody recorded · **MCP servers:** Microsoft Learn MCP Server · Azure MCP Server (read) · Foundry MCP Server (preview)

---

## What this day is for

Yesterday produced a bounded claim. Everything outside the boundary is somebody's problem now, and today they are told whose and what it is.

The engineering half of this is short. The residual risk is the complement of the safety case: what the mitigations do not cover, what the assumptions cost if they fail, what the detection commitment will not see, and what the uncharacterised evidence might be hiding. A student with yesterday's artefact can enumerate it in twenty minutes.

The rest of the day is the part the programme has been carrying for two years.

**Every true statement you make is selected from a set of true statements, and the set you did not say is part of what you communicated.** [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) phase 7 raised that in week thirteen — two true reports from one body of evidence, and the question of whether the concealment was legitimate — and the instructor declined to answer, naming [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) and this day. [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) phase 6 point 3 answered the epistemic half, stated that the ethical half — **when selection becomes deception** — was not answered that day, and sent it here. [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) phase 6 point 2 gave a partial condition and sent the boundary here too. [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) raised a harder version — a system optimised on a criterion nobody recorded, deployed to people who never saw it — and [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §6 named this day as where it becomes an ethical object rather than only an engineering one.

**All of it comes due today.** There is no later day, and a question that reaches [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) unanswered has been dropped rather than deferred.

And the platform supplies both hard cases in a form nobody can dismiss as hypothetical. **The criterion nobody recorded is sitting in the substrate's own evaluation configuration**, in the shape of a pass threshold that a default chose and no human decided. **The guarantee that changed hands is dated 1 July 2026**, when agent security capabilities for Copilot Studio and Foundry agents stopped being covered by the licences that had covered them, block rules stopped enforcing unless somebody redefined them, and an inventory table was replaced. Nobody selected any of that away. It is the residual risk of a system whose warranty was written by a party who is not in the room.

The target: **write what remains, to the person who will bear it, in words they can act on — and be able to say where the line is between selecting and deceiving, and which side you are on.**

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Eight items on material not yet taught. Confidence attached. Never recorded.

1. **Write the residual risk of your own component in three sentences**, addressed to a person with no technical background.
2. **What did you leave out of those three sentences?** List everything you considered and did not include.
3. **For each item in your list, why did you leave it out?** One reason each.
4. You tell someone five true things and omit a sixth. **Under what condition is that deception?** In your own words, before anyone tells you.
5. A system was optimised on a criterion. **Nobody wrote the criterion down, and nobody affected by the system has seen it.** Is that a technical problem, an ethical one, or both — and who would you say has been wronged, if anyone?
6. Your statement in item 1 will be read once, in six months, by somebody you will never meet. **What will they do with it?**
7. Predict: **when a non-technical reader reads your item 1 statement cold, what decision will they think they are being asked to make?**
8. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to turn your full residual-risk enumeration into a one-page statement for a named non-technical reader. Write down what it will produce, and **name the one thing it will quietly drop.** Sealed with items 1 to 7 and opened at phase 8.

Items 2 and 3 are the day's honest items and are the material phase 4 point 3 works on directly. Item 4 is the question the programme opened two years ago, taken cold from every student in the room and collected verbatim.

**Warrant.** Pretesting the ethical question before any framework exists, in the students' own words, so that phase 4's answer corrects positions the room already holds rather than filling a vacuum. Item 3 is constructed so that the reasons — length, complexity, uncertainty, self-protection — are separable when read back, and the read-back is phase 8's. Item 7 is a numeric-free self-prediction resolved four hours later against an observed reader. Item 8 is the day's contribution to the running frontier map and is sealed at phase 1 per instrumentation rule 8; it is deliberately about **omission**, because summarisation is where an agent's selection is invisible and the day's whole subject is invisible selection.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

Groups of four receive **four residual-risk statements written from one safety case** — [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md)'s C4, which the room read last week — addressed to the same non-technical recipient, by four authors. **Every sentence in all four is true.** What the recipient did afterwards is withheld.

**These four are demonstrated rather than operated, and the specification does not assess them.** The [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) requires phase 3's cases to be run wherever the subject permits, and this subject does not. What adjudicates a residual-risk statement is a person's decision, that decision was made once, by a recipient who has since read all four, and there is no operation that re-runs it. The design states that rather than inventing a proxy — a simulated recipient would be a different instrument measuring a different thing, and the day would then be assessing a simulation of the one judgement it exists to teach.

**One thing on this day is run, and it takes eight minutes.** Before the four statements are handed out, each group opens the substrate's own evaluation configuration and answers two questions: **what criterion decides whether a run of this evaluator passes, and who decided it.** They find `tool_call_accuracy` scoring one to five with a pass threshold of three, and they find that nobody in three years of cohort records chose three. They also find, at the same time, that no agreement figure between that evaluator and a human judge is published anywhere, so the threshold is a cut on an uncalibrated scale. **That result is written down before R3 is read**, and it is the case R3 stages, standing in the substrate the students are about to hand over.

**The sampling is disclosed, per instrumentation rule 3.** As with last week's case set, these four were commissioned rather than found; the authors knew they were being compared; and the recipient's subsequent decision is known to staff and is why this case was chosen. The room is told this before reading, and is told that the same objection [WP-087](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-087.md) §8 raised about last week's corpus applies here unchanged.

- **R1 · The complete one.** Every residual risk from the case, in the case's vocabulary, with intervals. Accurate, exhaustive, four pages. The recipient read the first paragraph and asked a colleague what it meant.
- **R2 · The usable one.** One page, plain, actionable, correctly prioritised — and it omits the one risk that would have changed the recipient's decision. The author judged it too unlikely to be worth the space. **The judgement was defensible and the omission was not disclosed.**
- **R3 · The honest one that is missing something nobody could ask for.** Complete, usable, well-prioritised. The system's ranking criterion was never recorded anywhere, the recipient has never seen it, and no risk arising from it appears — because there is nothing to write about a criterion nobody wrote down. **The room has, eight minutes earlier, found the same shape in the substrate's own evaluator threshold.**
- **R4 · The short one that discloses its own selection.** Half a page of risks, then four sentences: *this is what I have told you; this is the class of thing I have left out and why; here is the complete list and where it is; here is who to ask.* Nothing in it is more accurate than R2 and its risk list is shorter than R1's.

Groups rank the four on *which statement they would rather have received, as the recipient* and **commit before the outcome is supplied.** Then the outcome is supplied.

The payload is **R4**, and what it defeats is the room's assumption that the ethical question is about what you said. It is not. **It is about whether the reader could find out what you did not say.** R4's author omitted more than R2's author did and deceived nobody, because R4 told the reader that an omission existed, what kind it was, and how to close it. R2's author omitted one thing, in good faith, on a defensible judgement, and the recipient made a decision they would not have made — and had no way to know a decision was being made for them.

R3 is the day's second finding and it is the one with no author to blame. **Nobody omitted the criterion. There was nothing to omit, because it was never recorded**, and the recipient cannot ask for what does not exist.

Task after: **for each statement, name what the recipient could have found out and how.** Then: **what does R4 cost, and who pays it?**

The last question has an answer. R4's disclosure invites requests, and a recipient who asks for the complete list receives R1, which they cannot read. R4 does not solve the problem; it moves the unreadable artefact behind a door and puts a sign on the door. That is a real improvement and it is not a resolution, and a cohort that leaves believing otherwise will write signs instead of statements.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases matched on safety case, recipient and moment, varying only what was selected and whether the selection was disclosed, with a real decision as adjudicator and the corpus's construction disclosed. Commit-before-outcome makes the ranking a generation act. R4 defeats the expectation the day exists to defeat, R3 supplies the [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) case in a form with no villain, and the final question bounds the payload before phase 5 turns it into a template. **The four statements are demonstrated rather than operated and the day says so; the eight-minute configuration check is the one element that is run, and it is placed before R3 rather than after so that the room meets the unrecorded criterion in their own system before they meet it in somebody else's story.** A case a cohort has found for itself is harder to file under *that would not happen here*.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **What a residual-risk statement is.** The complement of the safety case: what the mitigations do not cover, what each assumption costs if it fails, what the detection commitment will not see, and what the uncharacterised evidence may be concealing. It is written **for the person who will bear the risk**, not for the person who wrote the case, and its test is whether that person can act on it. **This decomposition follows yesterday's case structure and omits risks that arise from the handover itself — the receiving organisation's capability, funding and continuity — which are real, are frequently the largest, and are [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md)'s.**

2. **The two symmetric failures, in writing, permanently.** [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s pair arrives here in its hardest form. Overclaiming is a failure. Stating a limitation in language the reader cannot act on is the same failure, and R1 is what it looks like when the author is scrupulous. **The difference from [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) is that there is no next sentence.** A spoken hedge can be repaired when the listener's face changes; a written one is read once, in six months, by somebody who cannot ask.

3. **Three conditions on legitimate selection, and all three are required.** [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) point 2 supplied the first two and this day supplies the third and tightens the first.
   - **Recoverability.** The omitted material is available to the reader *and the reader knows it exists.* Availability alone is not recoverability, which is what phase 2's bridge established: material behind a door nobody has been told about is not available in any sense the reader can use.
   - **Service.** The selection serves the reader's decision rather than the author's position. This is [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md)'s second condition unchanged, and the test for it is whether the omitted items are randomly distributed with respect to how they reflect on you. They will not be. Look.
   - **Counterfactual disclosure.** Anything whose disclosure would have changed the reader's decision is not omissible, whatever the other two conditions say. R2 satisfied recoverability and service and failed here.

4. **When selection becomes deception.** The question [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) opened in week thirteen and [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) sent here. **Selection becomes deception at the point where the author knows which omission would change the reader's decision, and omits it without disclosing that an omission exists.** Three parts to that, and each is doing work. *Knows* is what distinguishes deception from error. *Would change the decision* is condition three and is why the standard is the reader's decision rather than the author's sense of importance. *Without disclosing that an omission exists* is R4's move and is the reason R4 could omit more than R2 and deceive nobody.
   **And the knowledge condition has a professional floor.** You are required to look. After the first occasion on which you have been told that omissions have a distribution and that yours will not be random, *I did not realise which one mattered* is a description of a failure to perform a duty, not a defence against having failed it. **This duty applies where you can identify the reader's decision. Where you genuinely cannot — a statement published to an unknown readership — the duty converts into an obligation of disclosure rather than of selection: state the selection rule you used, because you cannot state the omission's relevance to a decision you cannot see.**

5. **The criterion nobody recorded.** [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md)'s question, and R3's case, answered. A system optimised on a criterion that was never written down cannot have that criterion disclosed, and its subjects cannot ask for what does not exist. Under point 3 this fails recoverability **structurally rather than through anyone's choice** — which means the wrong was committed at the moment of not recording, by people who were not thinking about disclosure at all. **Therefore the recording duty precedes the disclosure duty.** Writing down what a system is being optimised for is not documentation hygiene; it is the precondition of every obligation this day imposes, and a system whose criterion is unrecorded has been placed permanently outside the reach of the ethics. That is the answer to [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md)'s question: yes, it is an ethical object and not only an engineering one, and the ethical failure is located earlier than the deployment.

6. **Quantities carry their qualification in the same field.** [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md)'s discipline and specification rule 2, in a document that will be quoted. A probability in a residual-risk statement carries its horizon ([S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md)) and its confidence, in the same sentence, because a figure separated from its bound will travel alone — and in this artefact it will travel to a reader who has no way to reconnect them.

7. **Every stated risk carries its not-met procedure.** [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) step 5's promise form, applied. A residual risk stated without what to do when it occurs, and who to tell, is an item of information delivered to someone who cannot use it. **The procedure has a named person, and [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) is where that name survives the author's departure.**

8. **Where this duty does not apply, and where it applies to somebody else.** The disclosure duty is not unconditional, and the exception is the most abused clause in professional practice, so its conditions are stated rather than gestured at. **Where disclosing a residual risk to the general reader creates the risk** — a live, exploitable weakness — the duty is redirected, not suspended: it runs to a party who can fix it, on a stated timetable, with a stated point at which broader disclosure follows. **Redirection requires all three: a named recipient with the capability to act, a date, and a default to disclose if the date passes.** An exception claimed without all three is not the exception; it is the omission the exception is used to justify, and it is the form in which almost every real failure of this duty is dressed. This is SG390's material and the seminar states the conditions so that the course's framework is met with the boundary already drawn.

9. **A guarantee can change hands without anybody in the room deciding anything, and that is residual risk too.** The clearest worked instance available to this cohort is dated. **On 1 July 2026 the agent security capabilities covering Copilot Studio and Microsoft Foundry agents began requiring a Microsoft Agent 365 licence and stopped being covered by the Defender for Cloud Apps and Defender for Cloud licences that had covered them**; tenants without an eligible licence lose those capabilities on that date. Three consequences are the shape worth carrying. **Saved queries break**: the agent-inventory table in Advanced Hunting is replaced and the old one deprecated, so a detection somebody wrote and forgot returns nothing rather than failing. **Enforcement lapses silently**: real-time protection rules set to block stop enforcing and must be redefined under a new policies experience to preserve blocking. **And one switch turns everything off**: the capabilities consolidate under a single Security for AI Agents toggle, and when it is off, all of them are disabled. **None of that is a defect and nobody did anything wrong.** A supplier changed the terms of a guarantee that a system depends on, on a published date, and the residual-risk statement that a departing engineer wrote in June is silently wrong in July. **The durable claim is that a residual-risk statement inherits every guarantee its system rests on, including guarantees written by parties who will never read it — and that the statement's expiry must therefore name events in other people's systems.** The dates and the table names are perishable and will be superseded; the shape is not.

10. **A criterion nobody recorded is a defaults problem before it is an ethics problem, and today's substrate has one.** Phase 3's eight-minute check found `tool_call_accuracy` scoring one to five with a pass threshold of three, chosen by nobody, deciding whether runs pass, on a scale for which **no agreement with human judgement is published anywhere**. A second sits beside it: an evaluation of a multi-turn conversation is split before it is scored, the split defaults to the last turn, and how you split determines what you evaluated. **Both are R3, in the substrate, today.** Under point 5's finding the wrong was committed at the moment of not recording, which here means the moment somebody accepted a default without writing down that they had accepted it — and the remedy is correspondingly cheap and correspondingly unglamorous: **write down the criterion, including when the criterion is a default you did not choose.** A handover that names its evaluator's threshold and says *this was the default and we did not change it* has done everything the ethics of this day require. One that omits it has placed the criterion permanently outside the reach of the person it will be applied to.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to the four statements, with the room's ranking and the recipient's decision in front of them. Points 1, 4 and 8 carry the scope statements required by the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), point 8 in the normative-claim form with its conditions stated on the day rather than deferred. Point 4 discharges the ethical half of the question [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) opened, which has been outstanding for two years and is the longest deferral in the programme. Point 5 discharges [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md)'s. Both discharges are stated to the room as discharges, with the dates, because the programme's own two-year loop is itself a demonstration of what carrying a question rather than closing it costs and buys. **Points 9 and 10 are the day's perishable platform content and its named defaults, required by the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), and each is placed to serve a point rather than to satisfy a rule**: point 9 is the residual risk nobody in the handover chain chose, which is what phase 5 step 2's enumeration will otherwise miss, and point 10 is point 5's abstract answer standing in the students' own configuration. **Point 10 also carries a documentation silence and names it: no agreement figure between any evaluator on this platform and a human judge is published, so every judged score in the students' own harnesses is uncalibrated, and the residual-risk statement must say so.**

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 6. The selection is the student's, the reader is real, and the reading is cold.**

Each student writes the residual-risk statement for **the safety case they built at [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md)**, on the component they chose at [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md).

1. **Name the recipient** — a specific non-technical person connected to the substrate — and state, in one sentence, **the decision they will make with this.** **Where your [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) decision-maker decided against your recommendation, they are the recipient**, and where they did not, the recipient is a party who has previously decided against you on this component. **The statement is written for a reader who has already overruled you once.**
2. **Enumerate the residual risk in full**, from the case: uncovered by mitigation, cost of each assumption failing, invisible to the detection commitment, and concealed by uncharacterised evidence. **A fifth heading is added today: guarantees you inherited and did not write**, per point 9 — a licence term, a retention setting, a default threshold, a preview capability, a table somebody else will rename. **One entry under it is fixed and is the same for every student: the criterion the room found at phase 3, named with its value and with the fact that nobody chose it.** One further entry is fixed: **the risk you raised at [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) and lost**, carried with what you said at the time and what was decided. This list is working material and is not the deliverable.
3. **Write the statement.** One page maximum, in the recipient's language, with each risk carrying **its horizon, its confidence and its not-met procedure with a named person, in the same field as the risk itself.**
4. **Write the selection disclosure**, per R4: what you have said, what class of thing you have left out and why, where the full list from step 2 is, and who to ask.
5. **Apply the three conditions to your own omissions.** For each item in step 2 that did not reach step 3: is it recoverable, is the selection serving the reader, and **would its disclosure have changed the decision you named at step 1?** Any item where the third answer is yes goes back into step 3.
6. **Read it cold.** The recipient — or, where the named recipient is unavailable, a non-technical member of substrate staff who has not seen your component — reads the statement alone and states: **what decision they think they are being asked to make; what they would do on Monday; and one thing they still do not know that they need.** You say nothing until they finish.
7. **State the expiry, and ship the thing that notices it.** When does this statement stop being true, by event and by date, and who is responsible for noticing. Per [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) point 5 and [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) point 8. **Then build the check.** Using Copilot in agent mode, add to the substrate's repository **one check that fails when the expiry event occurs or the expiry date passes** — a test, a scheduled query, a pipeline step, whatever the event admits — and merge it. This is [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) point 4 applied to a document: **an expiry written into a statement decays silently, and an expiry written into the system fails loudly.** Where the event genuinely cannot be detected from inside the substrate — point 9's class, where the event is in somebody else's system — the check is a dated reminder to a named person and the artefact says that it is one, which is weaker and is honest.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned. **The instruction that told it what to leave out is the one the assessors will ask about**, because it is the only place where the student's selection rule becomes a written object rather than a private judgement.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — here the step 7 check itself is the obvious example and is accepted, because a statement that ships its own expiry detector has built the detection the record would otherwise have to evidence.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch. Both specific, both from this build. **A student whose statement was drafted with the agent is expected to name the compression as the first half and something the compression lost as the second**, and a frontier statement whose two halves are unrelated to each other on this day has not been written about this build.

**Readers are scheduled in two waves.** Eight minutes each.

**Fading:** step 2's four-way enumeration is supplied as headings from yesterday's case structure; step 4's disclosure form is supplied as R4's four sentences; step 7 reuses [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md)'s event-and-date requirement and the check has a supplied skeleton. **Steps 3 and 5 are unsupported** — the selection and the audit of it are the day's whole judgement.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 6 with the measurement taken on a real non-technical reader rather than on a peer, per instrumentation rule 1. Step 5 is the day's distinctive construction: the student applies the three conditions to their own selection, in writing, while the omitted items are still in front of them at step 2 — which is the only moment at which the audit is possible, because a week later the discarded items are gone and the author remembers only what they kept. **Step 7's shipped check is the day's build and it is deliberately the smallest one in the module.** The day's subject is a document, the document is the deliverable, and a build bolted on for the sake of having one would be exactly the failure [WP-088](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-088.md) §8 objection 3 examines. What the check does is convert the one part of the statement that is mechanically checkable — its own expiry — into an artefact that behaves like [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) argued artefacts should, and it exposes in passing which expiry events are detectable from inside the system and which are not, which is point 9's content arriving as a property of the student's own work. **Steps 1 and 2's [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) clauses are the artefact elements that day named under instrumentation rule 6**, and they are placed at the task's opening rather than as an addendum because they change who the whole statement is addressed to: a reader who has already overruled the author once is the hardest recipient in the module, and a residual-risk statement written to a receptive reader is a different artefact. The silent cold read at step 6 reuses [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md)'s reconstruction structure and is **contingent on that day's reported outcome** per instrumentation rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: **a residual-risk statement on this substrate that was accurate, complete, disclosed and ignored.** It named the risk, it named the horizon, it named the person to tell, and the recipient — who was competent and who read it — took no action, because the statement arrived four days before a deadline and the action it implied was expensive. The risk occurred. Attention is directed at the fact that **the author had discharged every duty this day imposes**, and at what they did afterwards, which was to conclude that the recipient was not serious, and at what else could have explained it.

Then the agent case: **a Copilot-written residual-risk statement that was confidently wrong and passed review.** A student gave the agent a twenty-two-item enumeration and asked for one page in a named reader's language. It produced a page that was clear, correctly prioritised, and better written than the student's own draft. **Every quantity in it had lost its horizon and its confidence.** *A small chance of degradation* replaced *roughly one week in twelve, on a measurement whose interval we have never computed*; *rare* replaced a figure with a stated bound. The reviewer read it, found it clearer than the enumeration, and approved it. Attention goes to why: **the agent was asked for a one-page statement in a non-technical reader's language, and stripping qualifications is what makes prose read like that.** The instruction was followed exactly and specification rule 2 was destroyed by the following of it, and nobody wrote an instruction saying the qualification travels in the same field because nobody thought a summariser needed telling.

Then students, in threes: **something you decided not to say this year, and the reason you gave yourself at the time.**

The firewall applies and is stated: **nothing surfaced here reaches any assessment judgement about the student who surfaced it.** The instructor states this before the prompt rather than after, because the prompt is the most self-incriminating in the programme and the cohort is three days from an entrustment occasion.

**Warrant.** Error management on the day's residue. The instructor case is chosen because the author is faultless under the day's own framework, which prevents the clinic becoming a reinforcement of phase 4 and keeps it about the failure the framework does not cover — [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) phase 7's finding at class 6, where the statement is written and permanent and the misreading is a decision rather than an inattention. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen because the agent performed a selection the day has spent fifty minutes calling deception when a human performs it, and did it invisibly, in response to a correct instruction** — which is the sharpest available demonstration that an instructions file is a specification and that a specification which does not state its qualification rule will produce work without qualifications. The student prompt is deliberately the item 3 material given a self-justification column, which is [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md) phase 6's construction two years later and with the students' own current conduct as the subject.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs, structured so that both produce.

Each student hands their partner **the step 2 full enumeration and the step 3 statement together** — which no other reader in the day sees jointly — and the partner must:

- **name the omission they judge most consequential**, and say what decision it would change;
- **classify it under the three conditions**: recoverable or not, serving the reader or the author, decision-changing or not;
- **say whether the step 4 disclosure covers it.**

The author then states whether they agree, and where the pair disagrees on the third condition the disagreement is recorded rather than resolved, with both positions written.

Per specification rule 3, the partner's classification, the author's response and any unresolved disagreement are elements of the artefact.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with the only reader in the design who can see both what was said and what was not. Step 5 is a self-audit and self-audits of one's own omissions are the least reliable instrument in the day; the partner is the external observer instrumentation rule 1 requires, and they are supplied with the one document pair that makes the judgement possible. The instruction to record rather than resolve disagreement on condition three is deliberate: whether a specific omission would have changed a specific reader's decision is frequently not settleable, and a pair that reaches agreement quickly has usually agreed to the author's framing.

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- **Item 7 resolved:** the decision the student predicted their reader would take from the statement, against what the reader said at step 6. Reported as a cohort agreement figure and returned privately.
- **Item 8 opened.** The sealed Copilot predictions are read against what the agent actually dropped, and the cohort's hit rate is recorded. **Both halves count**: predicting that a summariser would drop something is easy, and naming what is the measure. **The cohort's most-predicted and most-observed omission classes are reported side by side**, because a room that predicts *a risk* and observes *every confidence interval* has learned something specific about the instrument it uses every day.
- **Item 3's reasons read back anonymously**, sorted into the categories the room supplied — length, complexity, uncertainty, self-protection — with the cohort's distribution shown and the most common named.
- **The two-year loop closed.** The cohort's [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) phase 7 legitimacy positions, recorded in week thirteen and held since, are returned in aggregate and set against item 4's answers from this morning. **The direction of movement is the finding and is stated plainly, including if there is none.** Per [WP-013](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-013.md) §9 prediction 4, no change would indicate that neither day moved anything.
- The room's phase 3 rankings against the recipient's decision: **how many groups ranked R2 first**, which is the defensible statement that deceived.
- **The step 5 audit result:** across the cohort, how many items moved from step 2 back into step 3 after the three conditions were applied. This is the day's headline instrument.
- The phase 7 unresolved-disagreement total on condition three.
- Each student writes **one omission they have made in the past year that they now judge to have been deception**, and keeps it. It is not collected, it is not seen by anyone, and this is stated before it is written.
- Retrieval schedule: next morning, day 3, week 1, week 2.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in SG390 with PE390. Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the named recipient with the decision they will make, and the record that they have previously decided against the student on this component; the full step 2 enumeration under its five headings, including the inherited-guarantee entry naming the phase 3 criterion with its value and the fact that nobody chose it, and including the [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) risk the student raised and lost, with what was said at the time and what was decided; the statement, one page maximum, with each risk carrying its horizon, its confidence and its not-met procedure with a named person in the same field; the step 4 selection disclosure in its four parts; the step 5 three-condition audit with every item's three answers and every item that moved back; the reader's three cold-read answers as recorded by the reader; the expiry by event and date with a responsible party; **the merged expiry check, or the dated reminder with the statement that the event is not detectable from inside the substrate**; the phase 7 partner classification with the author's response and any recorded disagreement; **the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

**Five hard requirements.** A statement with **no selection disclosure fails.** A statement in which **any item the step 5 audit marked decision-changing was not returned to step 3 fails** — this is the day's single ethical requirement and it is binary. A **risk stated without its horizon and confidence in the same field fails**, per specification rule 2. A statement **addressed to a recipient who has never decided against the student on this component fails**, and is rewritten for one who has. **An expiry with no shipped check and no dated reminder fails**, and an expiry whose check was written and not merged is not a check. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**The four phase 3 statements are demonstrated rather than operated and nothing about them is assessed**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s provision for cases that cannot be run. What is assessed is the student's own statement, its audit, and its check.

**The length of the omission list is not assessed, and neither is the size of the step 5 movement.** A student who moved eight items back has not performed worse than one who moved none, and the specification says so in writing. Assessing the movement would make step 5 strategic in the one place in the programme where a strategic answer is indistinguishable from a dishonest one.

**A reader who cannot state the decision is a finding about the statement**, and the finding is recorded rather than penalised, unless the statement also fails one of the four hard requirements.

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md).

**Error-clinic firewall.** Phase 6 is unobserved by anyone contributing to a specification judgement, nothing from it is recorded, and phase 8's kept sentence is not collected.

**Discharge of the programme's longest deferrals.** Five questions were deferred to this day by name and **all five are answered.**

- **[S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) phase 7**, week thirteen, opened whether concealment through selection is legitimate and was explicitly left unresolved with [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) and this day named. **[S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) phase 6 point 3 answered the epistemic half and stated that "the ethical half — when selection becomes deception — is not answered today and returns at S088." That is phase 4 point 4**, and it is the day's single binary specification requirement at step 5. The deferral was outstanding for two years and is the longest in the programme.
- **[WP-013](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-013.md) §7** committed that "the phase 7 legitimacy positions are recorded in aggregate and returned to the cohort at S088." **That is phase 8's third item**, with item 4 supplying the re-elicitation [WP-013](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-013.md) §9 prediction 4 requires and the direction of movement reported as the finding.
- **[S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) phase 6 point 2** stated that selection is legitimate when the omitted material is available on request and serves the audience's decision, and that "the boundary between that and misrepresentation is not settled today and returns at S088." **That is phase 4 point 3**, which finds the condition necessary and insufficient, tightens availability into recoverability, and adds the counterfactual-disclosure condition. Phase 2's bridge produces the insufficiency from the room before the instruction supplies it.
- **[WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §6** named this day for the question whether "a system optimised on a criterion nobody recorded, deployed to people who never saw it, is an ethical object and not only an engineering one." **That is phase 3 case R3 and phase 4 point 5**, which answers yes, locates the wrong at the moment of not recording rather than at deployment, and derives from it that the recording duty precedes the disclosure duty.
- **[S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) phase 4 point 3** restated the question at the point where the authority is somebody else's — the set you did not say determines what they are able to decide — and stated that "the boundary between legitimate selection and misrepresentation is still not settled and returns at S088." **That is phase 4 points 3 and 4**, which settle it. This is the third day to defer the same boundary here, after [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) and [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md), and it is the last.

Two further inbound obligations are also discharged: **[WP-025](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-025.md) §6** named this day as where the promise-with-a-not-met-procedure becomes a residual-risk statement, which is **point 7 and step 3's not-met requirement**; and **[WP-012](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-012.md)** identified the expiry-condition competence as assessed at [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) and again here, which is **step 7 and is a checked element.**

**Rule-6 discharge.** One obligation lands on this day and **the element exists.** [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) named this day as the response occasion for its step 6 and step 7 outcomes — the proportion of decisions made against the student's recommendation, and whether the record captured the outcome in the decision-maker's own words — specifying that the residual-risk statement "must be written for a reader who decided against the engineer, and must carry one risk the student raised and lost." **That is phase 5 steps 1 and 2, both are checked elements, and the recipient requirement is one of the day's five hard requirements.** The element is placed at the task's opening rather than appended to it because it changes who the whole statement addresses: a reader who has already overruled the author once is the hardest recipient in the module, and a residual-risk statement written to a receptive reader is a different artefact.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions:

- **The step 5 audit movement** — how many items the cohort returned to the statement after applying the three conditions, and which condition triggered each. **Response occasion: [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) phase 4, where EA-3 is judged and the assessors' published perturbation set includes, for every student, one item drawn from their own step 2 enumeration that did not reach step 3, asked as: *why does this person not need to know that?*** The cohort figure is reported at S090 phase 8.
- **The item 7 against step 6 decision-agreement rate** — whether a student can predict what a non-technical reader will take their statement to be asking. **Response occasion: [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) phase 4, where the same prediction is made against a real decision-maker on a live decision, and the two rates are compared as the programme's only measurement of whether this capability improved between a constructed reader and a real one.** Reported at S090 phase 8.
