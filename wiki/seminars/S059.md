**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** Defender AI security posture management alongside the one recorded refusal in the practice corpus — declining to automate a review step whose reviewers catch issues with legal consequences
**Whitepaper:** [WP-059](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-059.md)
**Retrieves from:** S010, S056, S058
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

Every responsible-AI regime carries a heightened category — uses that consequentially affect a person's opportunities, rights, access or safety, and that therefore require more than the ordinary process.

**The category is not the interesting part.** Classifying something as sensitive is a lookup. **What matters is that the classification forces a decision somebody has to make and record**, and most regimes are much clearer about the classification than about the decision.

There are three decisions available and only three.

- **Change the design** so the sensitive use goes away.
- **Proceed with a recorded deviation** — a documented, approvable statement that you cannot comply yet.
- **Decline.**

This is the one session in the programme with a real worked example of the third, and it is worth saying plainly how thin that is. **The entire body of practice material behind this programme contains exactly one refusal.** A proposal to replace human reviewers with AI in a legal-review process was judged irresponsible, because those reviewers catch issues with legal consequences, and the customer's own proposed solution was declined on those grounds. **It is the only place in approximately ninety documents where the answer to a customer request is no.**

**That refusal is real, it is correct, and one instance is a shape rather than a base rate.** A programme that built a general stance on it would be doing exactly what this repository exists to prevent. What one instance can support is a form to imitate; what it cannot support is any claim about how often refusal is warranted, how often it happens, or what follows from it. This session uses it for the first and says so at every point.

Answer these before instruction.

1. Where in your system does an output affect what happens to a person?
2. Who decides whether that proceeds? Name them, not their role.
3. What are they looking at when they decide?
4. Your regime has no legitimate way to say *we cannot comply yet*. What happens?
5. Could you have described your system slightly differently and avoided the classification? Be honest.

**Item 4 has an answer everybody in the room has seen.** People do not stop shipping. **They stop recording.** Item 5 is the one nobody volunteers, and the willingness to answer it is the disposition this session is for.

## What you must produce

**A sensitive-use determination and the decision it forced, with a named decision-maker and a record somebody can review.**

1. **An inventory of what the system is actually made of** — components, dependencies, data sources and endpoints — built by hand before any tool is consulted.
2. **Where the sensitive use is**, located in a component and a flow from [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md), or in a category from the exclusion register at [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md).
3. **The decision**, chosen from the three, with reasons.
4. **The decision-maker**, named, with what was in front of them when they decided.
5. **If a deviation: an expiry and a condition for revisiting it.** A deviation with neither is a permanent exception wearing a temporary one's clothing.
6. **If a refusal: a written refusal somebody could review**, in the form [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) established, and carrying the three properties of the one recorded refusal.
7. **The dodge check** — whether a different description would have avoided the classification, answered honestly.

## The material

### One refusal, read closely, and what a single instance supports

The recorded refusal has three properties and each is doing work ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)).

**It names what the humans catch.** Not *humans are important* but *these reviewers catch issues with legal consequences*. The refusal is grounded in a specific class of thing the automation would stop detecting, which is what makes it arguable rather than a preference.

**It names why the consequence class matters.** Legal consequences are not merely severe; they are the kind of error that is discovered late, by somebody else, at a cost the delivery team does not pay. **A harm whose cost falls outside the team that caused it is the harm a delivery incentive is least likely to price**, which is why it is the one worth naming explicitly.

**And it declines the customer's own proposal** rather than a hypothetical. That is the hard part. Declining a strawman is free; declining the thing the person in front of you brought is the act the session is about.

**Now the honest accounting.** That refusal sits inside a collection with **no exclusion criteria of any kind** — no domain limit, no scale limit, no regulatory limit, and no description of a customer who would be unsuitable. Its readiness rubric is composed almost entirely of customer dispositions. **It records no failure anywhere**; every case study succeeds and the single failure narrative is a synthetic composite, fictional by the material's own admission. Its prepared answer to the objection that tooling improved while delivery did not **reattributes the shortfall to the customer's own organisational constraints.**

**So the corpus can refuse and does not document doing so.** One instance establishes that the practice is capable of the act. It establishes nothing about frequency, nothing about consequence, and nothing about whether the refusal was accepted. **The whole of this programme's stance on refusal is built on a wider argument — that a practice which cannot say no cannot be said to exercise judgement — and that argument is design reasoning with no external warrant.** It is stated in every session in Stage 1 and it is restated here.

**Further limits.** The corpus is access-controlled and paraphrased here, never quoted. It carries no citation of any kind across approximately ninety documents, and it contradicts itself in more than a dozen places. No figure from it is reproduced anywhere in this repository.

### Sensitive use hides where a person is the control, and that is exactly what gets dropped

The most useful thing this programme knows about *where* sensitive use lives comes from watching a well-built threat-modelling pipeline systematically lose it.

That pipeline has an excellent discipline: **a threat survives only if it can be bound to at least one named component and at least one numbered flow**, and anything that cannot is discarded with a logged reason ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md)). That rule is genuinely good — it is what makes a threat model about *this* system rather than about systems in general.

**It also removes, silently and by construction, everything about the operator, the supply chain, human process and out-of-band administration**, each discarded with an entirely plausible logged justification that nobody reads. **Recall is unmeasured and the design declines to measure it**: the evaluator explicitly refuses to deduct for threats that are missing, listing them separately as unscored suggestions.

**Read that as a map of where to look.** Sensitive use lives at the point where an output changes what happens to somebody — what they are offered, what they are refused, which queue they enter, what a human decision-maker sees before deciding. **That is frequently the human process around the system rather than the system**, which is precisely the region a component-and-flow binding rule pushes out. **The exclusion register from [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) exists for this**, and this is the session where it earns its place.

The same pipeline supplies the second finding. **There is no approval gate anywhere in it.** Human decisions exist as inputs; there are placeholders shaped like sign-off, with blanks for a reviewer, a date and a disposition, and **they are a form to fill in rather than a precondition for anything.** The one unavoidably present human authenticates a write to a live tracking system — **and is being asked for a credential, not for a judgement.** That distinction is the whole of this session's second element, and it was found in a system built by people who plainly knew what they were doing.

**Its limits.** Internal and access-controlled, paraphrased here and never quoted; **it cannot supply an external warrant for any claim.** No outcome evidence exists for any of it. **The source contains operational attack detail that is not reproduced here and should not be.** Every concrete integration in it is perishable.

### Refusing is a voice act, and voice is conditional on something you do not control

What this session asks a learner to rehearse is not primarily a cognitive act. **It is saying *this should not be built* to a room that has already decided to build it**, which is speaking up when silence is cheaper.

That is the construct the psychological-safety literature studies, and its definition is narrower and better than its popular version: **"the belief that the work environment is safe for interpersonal risk taking"**, where the interpersonal risks are appearing **"ignorant, incompetent, or disruptive"** ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)). The mechanism is a calculation rather than a disposition, and it is a bad one: **voice benefits the organisation, after a delay, with low certainty; silence benefits oneself, immediately, with high certainty.** Silence is rational under that payoff structure, which is why moralising about it does not work.

Four clarifications from the same source matter here and are routinely mangled second-hand. **It is not about being nice** — the author says "you could say it's the opposite", and that it is "about candor, about making it possible for productive disagreement and free exchange of ideas." **It is not comfort.** **It is not lowered standards**; safety and performance standards are two separate dimensions rather than a trade-off, and the quadrant the author says keeps her up at night is **high standards with low safety.** And **sanction is inside the model rather than an exception to it**: a fair, thoughtful response to a genuinely blameworthy act is held to reinforce psychological safety rather than damage it.

**The consequence for this session is uncomfortable and is stated rather than hidden.** If refusal is conditional on safety, **teaching refusal to an individual is treating an environmental variable as a personal skill.** A learner who writes a defensible refusal and takes it into a team where dissent is costly has been given a capability they cannot exercise. **This session does not solve that.** It requires the refusal to be written so that somebody else could carry it, which is the only mitigation available to a person with no authority over their environment.

**The limits are important and this programme insists on them.** The book's leadership framework is **untested** — no trial of it appears anywhere in it, the author does not claim one, and it is drawn partly from consulting experience. Its narrative review is not a systematic review and reports no disconfirming study. **Its several magnitudes may not cross into this wiki**, and none does. For synthesis rather than advocacy, the meta-analytic review is the better instrument ([research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)): it aggregates a large body of independent samples, ranks antecedents by relative strength rather than listing them, tests incremental validity against named comparators, and tests whether the construct behaves equivalently at individual and group levels rather than assuming it. **That note was written from the paper's abstract; it reports no numerical effect sizes and none is asserted here.** Its evidence base is overwhelmingly cross-sectional and self-report, so **causal direction is not established by it.**

### What a posture tool can tell you, and the two moments it points at

The platform anchor is on the workload side rather than the data side, and it is here because it answers the first question this session asks: **what is this system actually made of?**

The workload posture surface builds a **generative AI Bill of Materials** — "application components, data, and AI artifacts from code to cloud" — and discovers it automatically and continuously across several first-party and third-party model services ([research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md)). It scans source for infrastructure misconfigurations and container images for known vulnerabilities in generative-AI library dependencies. Its named minimum baseline for a hosted model endpoint is worth memorising because most of it is about removing keys and network exposure: **private endpoints, restricted endpoints, managed identity, identity-based authentication.**

Two of its ideas transfer directly into a sensitive-use determination.

**Attack path analysis targets what the documentation calls "toxic combinations"** — individually acceptable configurations that compose into a breach. **A sensitive use is frequently a toxic combination of exactly this kind**: a model that is fine, a data source that is fine, a workflow step that is fine, and a person downstream who treats the composite as authoritative. Nobody made a bad decision anywhere in the chain.

**And the documentation names the two moments a model touches proprietary data**: it flags the concern that "data might be exposed during the grounding of AI models to specific data and the fine-tuning of a pretrained model on a specific dataset." **Grounding and fine-tuning are therefore the two moments data can leak by construction rather than by attack**, and they are the first two places to look when asking where a person's information enters a system that will act on it.

**Its limits, and they are the reason this is an anchor rather than an authority.** The workload-side material was **read through search-returned excerpts rather than a full page fetch**, so its structure is reconstructed and should be re-verified. **No detection rates, no false-positive rates and no coverage measurements are published anywhere in this documentation — assert none.** Agent-level discovery and posture are moving to a separate licence, which changes what is available under which plan. **It is single-vendor: the concepts transfer, the mechanisms and portal paths do not.** And a course lab may not be able to satisfy its administrative prerequisites at all.

**One more caution, and it belongs to this session more than to any other.** Do not ask the system to classify its own sensitivity. The planning literature holds that these models cannot self-verify **"because self-verification is after all a form of reasoning"** ([research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/kambhampati-llms-cant-plan.md)), and a sensitivity determination is a verification claim about the system's own effects. **Correctness comes from an external verifier**, and here the external verifier is a person with a name. That paper is a position paper whose claims are arguments and have been contested, scoped to auto-regressive models as of early 2024; the architectural discipline is what transfers.

### Record the decision as reasoned narrative, not as a verdict

The last element is how the decision is written down, and there is a literature on this that most engineering practice ignores.

When assessors judge complex evidence, **they rely on narrative to form the judgement and use scores only to confirm it** — grades were "merely used to confirm impressions based on narratives" ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). Two further findings bear directly on a sensitive-use decision. **Assessors anchor early and then seek confirmation**, sticking to initial judgements even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence, with final judgements more elaborate than preliminary ones but not substantially different. **And convergence on an outcome conceals divergence in reasoning** — assessors reached similar decisions for incompatible reasons, which is invisible in the outcome.

**So a record that states only the decision has recorded the least informative part of it.** The remedy the same literature recommends is that high-stakes decisions **should not be made individually but should result from group discussion**, and that judgements should be **thoroughly substantiated** — because substantiation is what makes divergent reasoning visible and therefore discussable.

**Its limits.** These are studies of clinical assessors judging portfolios and trainee performance, in a single institutional setting, using think-aloud methods that cannot capture fast or automatic processing and that risk participants confabulating explanations for the researcher. **The transfer to an engineering governance decision is this design's inference, not the authors'.**

## The workflow

**Enumerate the system by hand, find where an output changes what happens to a person, and force the decision to have a name attached.**

**Step 1 — build the bill of materials by hand, before you open any tool.** Every component, dependency, data source, model endpoint and outbound integration. **Do this first and badly.** The manual pass is where the surprises happen, and a learner who starts from a generated inventory has skipped the only part that teaches anything.

**Step 2 — then look at what a posture tool discovers, and diff it.** Record what the discovery found that you missed, and — more usefully — what you knew that it does not cover. Record which page version you read and the date.

**Step 3 — mark grounding and fine-tuning on your own inventory.** Those are the two moments the model touches proprietary data. **Anywhere a person's information enters at either point is a candidate sensitive use before you have thought about it at all.**

**Step 4 — find one toxic combination in your own system.** Two or three individually acceptable configurations that compose into something you would not have approved as a whole. **Then check whether any of them are individually out of scope under your [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md) test.** If they are, you have found a hole in your own test with a live example attached, which is worth more than the hypothetical you wrote last session.

**Step 5 — locate the sensitive use where a person is affected, not where the diagram is thickest.** What is offered, what is refused, which queue somebody enters, what a human sees before deciding. **Check your [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) exclusion register**, because a binding rule that requires a component and a flow will have pushed the human-process cases out of the threat model, and this is where they come back.

**Step 6 — name the decision, name the person, and record what they saw.**

```yaml
# assessment/sensitive-use.yaml   <- SHAPE, NOT SCHEMA. Your regime's fields will differ.
located_at:
  component: "<from S051>"
  flow: "<numbered flow, or the S052 exclusion category if it lives in the human process>"
  effect_on_a_person: "<what is offered, refused, queued, or shown to a decision-maker>"
  entered_via: "grounding | fine-tuning | neither"   # the two by-construction exposure moments

decision: "change_design | deviate | decline"       # THREE. 'Escalated' is a state, not a decision.

decided_by:
  name: "<a person>"                                 # not a board, not a function, not a mailbox
  role: "<their role, for context only>"
  what_they_saw: "<the artefact in front of them, by name and version>"
  produced_by_the_process_being_assessed: false      # if true, S054 applies. Say so.
  discussed_with: ["<others present>"]               # a decision reached alone is recorded as alone

reasoning: >
  The substantiation, not the verdict. Two assessors can reach the same decision for
  incompatible reasons, and only the reasoning makes that visible. Write enough that a
  reader who disagrees knows exactly what to disagree with.

if_deviation:
  expires: "<condition or date>"                     # BOTH absent = a permanent exception
  revisit_when: "<state change, with an actor>"      # reuse the S058 trigger shape
  approved_by: "<name>"

if_refusal:                                          # the three properties of the one recorded refusal
  what_the_humans_catch: "<the specific class of thing automation would stop detecting>"
  why_that_consequence_class_matters: "<who pays, how late it is discovered>"
  declines_their_proposal_not_a_hypothetical: true
  reviewable_by_someone_who_disagrees: true

dodge_check:
  could_a_different_description_have_avoided_this: "<yes / no>"
  if_yes_what_wording: "<the wording that would have worked>"
  # A 'yes' is a FINDING ABOUT THE TEST, filed against S057. It is not a route to take.
```

**Step 7 — if you deviate, bound it; if you decline, write it so it can be argued with.** **Adding a documented, approvable way to say *we cannot comply yet* is the clearest maturity signal in the whole regime this module draws on**, because a regime with no legitimate route for that does not produce compliance — **it produces silence, and silence is indistinguishable from compliance in every report.** An unbounded deviation gives that mechanism away again.

**Step 8 — run the dodge check honestly.** Could a slightly different description have avoided the classification? **If yes, that is a finding about your regime's test rather than a loophole to use**, and it belongs in the record beside the determination. [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md) built the test; this is where you find out whether it survives somebody with an incentive.

**Step 9 — attack your own decision from the side you are least able to see.** If you declined, one prompt: *argue that this refusal is unnecessary and that the risk is being overstated — make the strongest version of the case, using only what is in this record.* If you deviated or changed the design, the mirror: *argue that this should have been declined.* Then read the output as an argument to answer rather than a verdict to accept, **and record separately that a system you are governing is not an independent judge of whether it should be governed.** **An agent that agreed with your decision was not used as an instrument.**

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** That a system's real inventory is larger than its diagram, and that the gap is where the sensitive uses are. That the composition of individually acceptable configurations is the normal shape of a serious problem, and that no single step in the chain looks like a mistake. And, if you have a real decision to record, **that naming a person is the part everybody resists** — not because it is hard to find out who decided, but because writing the name makes somebody responsible who would rather not be.

## What good looks like

- **The inventory was built by hand first**, and the diff against what a tool discovered is recorded in both directions.
- **The sensitive use is located, not asserted.** A component, a flow, or a named category of harm. **Where a person is the control is the commonest place it hides**, and that is exactly the region [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md)'s binding rule pushed out of the threat registry.
- **The decision is one of the three**, and not a fourth thing. *Escalated to governance* is not a decision, it is a handoff.
- **A person is named.** Not a board, not a function, not a mailbox.
- **What they saw is recorded**, by name and version. A decision-maker shown a summary decided about a summary.
- **The reasoning is substantiated, not just stated.** A reader who disagrees can tell exactly what to disagree with.
- **A deviation has an expiry and a revisit condition with an actor.**
- **A refusal carries the three properties**: what the humans catch, why that consequence class matters, and that it declines the actual proposal rather than a hypothetical.
- **The dodge check is answered**, including when the answer is yes, and a yes is filed against the scope test.

## How it goes wrong

- **The category exercise** — you classified it and stopped. *Warning sign:* your artefact ends with a label.
- **The absent decider** — the decision exists and nobody made it. *Warning sign:* your record names a function.
- **The credential mistaken for a judgement** — somebody authorised the change, and you recorded that as approval. *Warning sign:* the person you named was asked for access rather than for an opinion.
- **The permanent deviation** — recorded, approved, never revisited. *Warning sign:* it has no expiry, and nobody minded.
- **The escalation as decision** — you passed it upwards and treated the passing as resolution. *Warning sign:* nothing changed and nobody said no.
- **The unrecorded refusal** — you declined, verbally, in a meeting. *Warning sign:* nobody outside the room could find out that it happened.
- **The self-classified system** — you asked the system whether its own use was sensitive. *Warning sign:* your determination's evidence is an output of the thing being determined about.
- **The scope dodge** — you narrowed the system's description until the classification did not apply. *Warning sign:* the description changed and the system did not.

## Exercises

**None of these is assessed.** The determination and its decision are carried into [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md), where the whole assessment is defended on the work it produced.

**1 · Take the refusal apart.** Write down the three properties of the one recorded refusal in the practice corpus, then apply each to a system you have worked on.

> **Model answer.** It **names what the humans catch** — issues with legal consequences, a specific class rather than a general appeal to human judgement. It **names why that consequence class matters** — the errors are discovered late, by somebody else, at a cost the delivery team does not pay. And it **declines the customer's own proposal** rather than a strawman. **The exercise fails if your version says the equivalent of *a human should stay in the loop***, which names nothing, is unarguable, and will lose to anybody who wants to ship. **And record the accounting: this is one instance across approximately ninety documents in a collection with no exclusion criteria and no recorded failure.** It supports a form to imitate and no claim about how often refusal is warranted.

**2 · Build the bill of materials by hand, then diff it.** Using the workflow above, enumerate your system manually before consulting any discovery tool, then compare.

> **What you should find.** In both directions. The tool will find dependencies and endpoints you had forgotten; **you will know about data sources, human process steps and outbound integrations it does not cover.** Record both, because the second list is where sensitive uses live. Then mark grounding and fine-tuning on your inventory — the documentation names those as the two moments a model touches proprietary data and therefore the two moments it can leak by construction rather than by attack ([research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md)). **That documentation publishes no detection rate and no false-positive rate — assert none** — and its workload-side pages were read through search excerpts rather than fetched in full, so re-verify anything structural.

**3 · Go back to your own item 2, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 2 — who decides whether the sensitive use proceeds. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then produce a real name, and record what that person was looking at.

> **Criterion.** An answer passes if it names **a person** and states **what artefact was in front of them, by name and version.** It fails if it names a function, a board, a mailbox or a process. **It also fails, more subtly, if the person named was asked for a credential rather than for a judgement** — the difference is whether they could have said no and had that mean something. The worked pipeline this session studies has exactly this failure at its most consequential point: the one unavoidably present human authenticates a write to a live backlog, and **is being asked for access rather than for agreement** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md)).
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**4 · Price the refusal for the person who has to make it.** Write down what it would cost you, personally, to state a refusal in your current team: to whom, in front of whom, and what you expect to happen next.

> **What to notice.** That the calculation is asymmetric and that the asymmetry is structural rather than a failure of nerve: **voice benefits the organisation, after a delay, with low certainty; silence benefits you, immediately, with high certainty** ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)). **Silence is rational under that payoff structure**, which is why this exercise asks you to price it rather than to resolve to be braver. Note the source's own corrections — the construct is not niceness, not comfort, and not lowered standards; safety and performance standards are separate dimensions, and the dangerous quadrant is high standards with low safety. **Note also that its leadership framework is untested: no trial of it appears in the book, and the author does not claim one.** For synthesis rather than advocacy, the meta-analytic review is the better instrument, and **it reports no effect sizes in the form held here** ([research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)).
>
> **This programme cannot fix your environment and does not pretend to.** What it can do is require the refusal to be written so that somebody else could carry it, which is the only mitigation available to a person with no authority over the room.

**5 · Substantiate, then hand it over.** Give your determination to somebody outside your team and ask two questions: **who decided this, and what were they looking at?**

> **Scoring criterion.** A pass is when both halves are answerable **from the document**. If not, you have a classification rather than a decision. Then ask a third question of anybody who chose a deviation: **when does this expire, and what happens then?** **A deviation whose expiry passes with nothing occurring has taught your organisation that deviations are permanent**, which is more damaging than the original non-compliance. And notice what the record's reasoning section is for: judgements of this kind are formed from narrative and only confirmed by verdicts, assessors converge on outcomes for incompatible reasons, and **substantiation is what makes the divergence visible** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). Those are studies of clinical assessors judging portfolios; **the transfer to an engineering decision is this design's inference and not the authors'.**

**6 · Find one toxic combination, and check it against your own scope test.** Two or three individually acceptable configurations in your system that compose into something you would not approve as a whole.

> **What you should find.** That no single step looks like a mistake, which is what makes the class hard. **Then check whether any of the components is individually out of scope under your [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md) test.** If one is, you have found a hole in your own test with a live example attached — file it against the test rather than around it. Note that the vendor's framing of this idea is **definitional rather than empirical**: it names the phenomenon and publishes nothing about how often such combinations occur or how many are found.

## What this does not establish

**Nothing here establishes that identifying a sensitive use changes an outcome.** The regime this session draws on publishes no evidence that any determination it made ever altered a system.

**The single refusal is the evidential centre of this session and one instance is a thin base for a programme's stance.** It establishes that the practice is capable of the act. **It establishes nothing about frequency, nothing about consequence, and nothing about whether the refusal was accepted or what happened to the engagement.** It sits inside a collection with no exclusion criteria of any kind, no recorded failure, a readiness rubric composed of customer dispositions, and a prepared answer that reattributes disappointing results to the customer. **The programme's wider stance — that a practice which cannot say no cannot be said to exercise judgement — is design reasoning with no external warrant, and this session inherits that status rather than escaping it.**

**This session enumerates no sensitive-use categories and no thresholds.** Which uses are heightened is a matter for the learner's organisation, their customer, their regulator and their jurisdiction — **none of which this programme knows.** Nothing here is legal or regulatory guidance, and a learner who treats it as such has misread it.

**The three-decision structure is this programme's compression** of a regime that presents its options less crisply. It is offered because a decision set with three members is usable and one with an implied fourth is not, and **the compression may be wrong for a regime the learner actually meets.**

**The psychological-safety material is the strongest-evidenced thing in the session and it does not license what learners will want it to.** The construct is well supported by a large synthesis that ranks its antecedents, tests incremental validity against named comparators and tests homology across levels — and that evidence base is **overwhelmingly cross-sectional and self-report, so causal direction is not established by it**, and the note held here rests on the paper's abstract and reports no effect sizes. **The leadership framework in the popular account is untested**: no trial of it appears in the book, and its author does not claim one. **Nothing in this session shows that a team can be made safer, or that a safer team refuses more.**

**And the session's own position is uncomfortable.** If refusal is conditional on an environment the learner does not control, **teaching refusal to an individual is treating an environmental variable as a personal skill.** The session does not solve that. It requires the refusal to be written so that somebody else could carry it, and that is a mitigation rather than an answer.

**The posture surface is definitional, not evaluative.** The documentation describes capabilities and names phenomena — the bill of materials, toxic combinations, the grounding and fine-tuning exposure moments — and publishes **no detection rates, no false-positive rates and no coverage measurements.** **Assert none.** It was read through search excerpts rather than a full page fetch, so its structure is reconstructed; agent-level capabilities are mid-licence-migration; and it is single-vendor, so the concepts transfer and the mechanisms do not.

**The pipeline specimen has no outcome evidence of any kind**, its implied observed failures are nowhere recorded, and the operational attack detail it contains is deliberately not reproduced here.

**The narrative-assessment findings come from clinical assessors judging portfolios**, in a single institutional setting, using methods that cannot capture fast or automatic processing. **The transfer to an engineering governance decision is this design's inference and not the authors'.**

**The one claim this session makes without hedging is definitional.** A classification that forces no decision is a label, and a person asked for a credential has not been asked for a judgement. Both are true of the regime, of the pipeline studied here, and of whatever process a learner meets next.
