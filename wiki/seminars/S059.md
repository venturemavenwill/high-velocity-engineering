# S059 · Sensitive Use, and the Decision It Forces

**Module:** [M12](/wiki/modules/M12.md) · **Quarter:** [Q6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-059](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-059.md)
**Retrieves from:** S010, S056, S058
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

Every responsible-AI regime carries a heightened category — uses that consequentially affect a person's opportunities, rights, access or safety, and that therefore require more than the ordinary process.

**The category is not the interesting part.** Classifying something as sensitive is a lookup. **What matters is that the classification forces a decision somebody has to make and record**, and most regimes are much clearer about the classification than about the decision.

There are three decisions available and only three.

- **Change the design** so the sensitive use goes away.
- **Proceed with a recorded deviation** — a documented, approvable statement that you cannot comply yet.
- **Decline.**

Answer these before instruction.

1. Where in your system does an output affect what happens to a person?
2. Who decides whether that proceeds? Name them, not their role.
3. What are they looking at when they decide?
4. Your regime has no legitimate way to say *we cannot comply yet*. What happens?
5. Could you have described your system slightly differently and avoided the classification? Be honest.

**Item 4 has an answer everybody in the room has seen.** People do not stop shipping. **They stop recording.** Item 5 is the one nobody volunteers, and the willingness to answer it is the disposition this session is for.

## What you must produce

**A sensitive-use determination and the decision it forced, with a named decision-maker and a record somebody can review.**

- **Where the sensitive use is**, located in a component and a flow from [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md), or in a category from the exclusion register at [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md).
- **The decision**, chosen from the three, with reasons.
- **The decision-maker**, named, with what was in front of them when they decided.
- **If a deviation: an expiry and a condition for revisiting it.** A deviation with neither is a permanent exception wearing a temporary one's clothing.
- **If a refusal: a written refusal somebody could review**, in the form [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) established.
- **The dodge check** — whether a different description would have avoided the classification, answered honestly.

## What good looks like

- **The sensitive use is located, not asserted.** A component, a flow, or a named category of harm. **Where a person is the control is the commonest place it hides**, and that is exactly the region [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md)'s binding rule pushed out of the threat registry.
- **The decision is one of the three**, and not a fourth thing. *Escalated to governance* is not a decision, it is a handoff.
- **A person is named.** Not a board, not a function, not a mailbox.
- **What they saw is recorded.** A decision-maker shown a summary decided about a summary.
- **A deviation has an expiry and a revisit condition.**
- **A refusal is written down** and could be reviewed by somebody who disagrees.
- **The dodge check is answered**, including when the answer is yes.

## How it goes wrong

- **The category exercise** — you classified it and stopped. *Warning sign:* your artefact ends with a label.
- **The absent decider** — the decision exists and nobody made it. *Warning sign:* your record names a function.
- **The permanent deviation** — recorded, approved, never revisited. *Warning sign:* it has no expiry, and nobody minded.
- **The escalation as decision** — you passed it upwards and treated the passing as resolution. *Warning sign:* nothing changed and nobody said no.
- **The unrecorded refusal** — you declined, verbally, in a meeting. *Warning sign:* nobody outside the room could find out that it happened.
- **The scope dodge** — you narrowed the system's description until the classification did not apply. *Warning sign:* the description changed and the system did not.

## Do it

**First, look where sensitive use actually lives, which is not where the diagram is thickest.** It lives at the point where an output changes what happens to somebody: what they are offered, what they are refused, what queue they enter, what a human decision-maker sees before they decide. **Frequently it lives in the human process around the system rather than in the system**, which means it lives in the categories your [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) exclusion register named — the ones the binding rule pushed out of the threat model. **That is why that register exists.**

**Second, name the decision and the person, and record what they were looking at.** A decision-maker who saw a one-page summary made a decision about a one-page summary, and that is not a criticism of them. **If the summary was produced by the process being assessed, [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) applies and you should say so.**

**Third, choose among three, and do not invent a fourth.** *Under review* and *escalated* are states, not decisions. **A decision changes what happens next.**

**Fourth, if you deviate, bound it.** An expiry and a condition for revisiting. **Adding a documented, approvable way to say *we cannot comply yet* is the clearest maturity signal in the whole regime this module draws on** — because a compliance regime with no legitimate way to say that is a regime people quietly ignore, and **making deviation visible rather than merely absent is what converts an aspiration into a control.** An unbounded deviation gives that mechanism away again.

**Fifth, if you decline, write it so it can be argued with.** The practice material behind this programme contains exactly one refusal in its entire collection: **replacing human reviewers with AI in a legal-review process was judged irresponsible, because those reviewers catch issues with legal consequences, and the customer's own proposed solution was declined on those grounds.** It is the only place in the whole body of work where the answer to a customer request is no, and it is the most trustworthy content in it. **Notice its shape.** It names what the humans catch, why the consequence class matters, and it declines the customer's own idea rather than a hypothetical. Yours should do the same three things.

**Sixth, run the dodge check.** Could a slightly different description of your system have avoided the classification? **If the answer is yes, that is not a loophole to use — it is a finding about your regime's test**, and it belongs in the record beside the determination. [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md) built the test; this is where you find out whether it survives somebody with an incentive.

## Show it

One question to somebody outside your team, holding your record.

**Who decided this, and what were they looking at?**

If they cannot answer both halves from the document, you have a classification and not a decision.

Then the harder one, for anybody who chose a deviation: **when does this expire, and what happens then?** A deviation whose expiry passes without anything occurring has taught your organisation that deviations are permanent, which is more damaging than the original non-compliance.

## What this does not establish

**Nothing here establishes that identifying a sensitive use changes an outcome.** The regime this session draws on publishes no evidence that any determination it made ever altered a system, and **the surrounding practice corpus records exactly one refusal across its entire collection.** One case is a shape to imitate, not a base rate, and this session treats it as the former.

**This session enumerates no sensitive-use categories and no thresholds.** Which uses are heightened is a matter for the learner's organisation, their customer, their regulator and their jurisdiction — **none of which this programme knows.** Nothing here is legal or regulatory guidance, and a learner who treats it as such has misread it.

**The three-decision structure is this programme's compression** of a regime that presents its options less crisply. It is offered because a decision set with three members is usable and one with an implied fourth is not, and **the compression may be wrong for a regime the learner actually meets.**
