# S059 · Zero-Shot Generalisation and the Benchmark Trap

**Module:** [M12](/wiki/modules/M12.md) · **Quarter:** [Q6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** ME · IS
**Whitepaper:** [WP-059](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-059.md)
**Depends on:** S041, S049, S056
**Re-tests:** S050
**Platform anchor:** the Foundry model leaderboard and model-card benchmarks (preview) · Azure AI Search hybrid and semantic ranking · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server · Azure MCP Server (Search) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

Three days ago the students learned that which retriever wins is a fact about the query population. Today they meet the industry's answer to that problem, which is to fix a query population, a corpus and a relevance criterion once, publish them, and let everybody report against them.

That answer is not stupid. It is the only mechanism by which two people who have never spoken can compare two systems, and without it there is no field. **Its cost is that every number it produces is a claim about somebody else's triple**, and almost nobody who cites one says so.

**The day has a platform instance so exact that it is almost embarrassing.** The Foundry portal ships a model leaderboard with published benchmark scores, comparative charts and a normalised quality index. Its own troubleshooting table contains the line a student will spend today learning to write for themselves: *benchmark scores differ from my results* — **"Public benchmarks use standardized datasets and might not reflect performance on your specific data."** The vendor has written down the day's whole argument, in its own documentation, next to the leaderboard, and it is still the leaderboard people cite in design reviews. **The problem is not that nobody says it. The problem is that saying it changes nothing about how a number gets used**, and that is what today is for.

The day's second half is worse and belongs to the students personally. They measured, at [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md), how far two people diverge applying their own written relevance criterion. That divergence is a floor: **no difference smaller than it is observable, by anyone, on judgements of that kind.** A large share of the improvements the students have read about — and a large share of the improvements they are about to claim — are below it.

The target: **a benchmark is a criterion constructed by named people who fixed a corpus, a query population and a definition of relevance; a score on it licenses a claim about that triple and no other; and a reported difference smaller than your own judges' disagreement is not a small result but no result.**

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

**Three published retrieval comparisons** are supplied with **aggregate scores only.** Five items, sealed and opened at phase 8.

1. **Which system would you adopt for the substrate, and why?**
2. **What do these results tell you about your own corpus?**
3. **What would have to be different for your answer to item 1 to change?**

Then, without discussion, **the per-dataset and per-domain breakdowns for all three are supplied**, and:

4. **Answer item 1 again.**

Then, sealed separately:

5. **Copilot prediction.** You will shortly ask the agent to recommend a retrieval configuration for the substrate and to justify it with evidence. Write down **what evidence it will cite, and what it will not say about that evidence.**

The item 1 to item 4 change rate is the day's opening measurement. It is placed beside [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md)'s step 7 rate at phase 8 — the same question about the students' own ablations — so the cohort can see whether they apply to published work the scepticism they apply to themselves.

Item 5's design expectation is that the agent will cite real, correct, current, well-sourced comparisons — and will not state what any of them fixed. **The agent's failure on this day is not fabrication. It is fluent, accurate citation with the licensing sentence missing**, which is precisely the behaviour the cohort is here to stop performing themselves.

**Disclosure**, stated immediately after items 4 and 5 are sealed and before anything else: **the three comparisons were selected by the design because their aggregate and per-dataset conclusions differ**, from a larger set in which many do not, and the selection is disclosed here in the same terms the day demands of students at step 2. [Instrumentation rule 3](/wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) applies to this day's own evidence.

**Warrant.** This is the day's discharge of the obligation [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md) placed on it under instrumentation rule 6: the aggregate-only counterfactual retaken on published results rather than on the student's own work. The within-phase design — conclude, then breakdown, then re-conclude — is what makes the two rates comparable, since S053's was also taken inside a single reveal. **Item 5 is sealed here and may not migrate into phase 5**, per instrumentation rule 8, and is placed after item 4 so that it cannot contaminate the S053 comparison.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative retrieval set (20 min)

Free recall, no notes.

- [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md): what a held-out estimate estimates, and the licensing sentence's three parts.
- [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md): the unit of analysis, the design effect, and the five things owed to a reader.
- [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md): the two symmetric failures, and why unusable hedging is the one this cohort produces.
- [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md): the four terms in order of size, and your own measured disagreement rate.
- **Bridge:** [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md) said a held-out estimate estimates performance on data drawn as the evaluation set was drawn. **A benchmark is an evaluation set drawn by somebody else, over somebody else's corpus. What does a score on it estimate?**

The room answers: performance on that corpus, for that query population, under that definition of relevance. Which is a claim about the benchmark and not about anybody's system in use.

**Warrant.** Expanding-interval retrieval that delivers the day's central claim as a two-quarter-old consequence rather than as new content. The bridge is the highest-value minute of the day and it is answered by the room.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups have **four systems — the cohort's [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md) retriever, the [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md) keyword baseline, and two supplied retrievers, all four queryable as fields and profiles on one index — and they run four evaluations.**

- **V1** — each system's own in-domain held-out set.
- **V2** — a public zero-shot suite, reported in aggregate.
- **V3** — the substrate's query log with the cohort's own [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md) relevance judgements.
- **V4** — the same public suite reported per dataset, **with the measured overlap between each dataset's corpus and each system's training corpus.**

**The four evaluations produce four different orderings of the same four systems.** Groups commit to an explanation for the reordering before V4's overlap figures are shown.

The payload is **V4**. The system that wins V2 has substantial measurable overlap with several of the suite's datasets; on the subset with no overlap it is beaten by the keyword baseline. **"Zero-shot" turns out to be a property of the reporting rather than of the model**, and the quantity that would have revealed it was never published.

**A fifth reading is taken from the platform and is not an evaluation.** Alongside the four tables, groups open the Foundry model leaderboard through the Foundry MCP Server and read three things: which models carry benchmark results at all, who produced them, and the troubleshooting note quoted at the top of this page. **The leaderboard is a preview feature, it covers a curated selection rather than the catalogue, some of its figures are the vendor's and some are the provider's, and its scores are normalised indexes rather than metric values.** Every one of those is a construction decision, all four are disclosed, and the disclosure sits somewhere nobody reads. Groups state which of point 2's three fixed terms each disclosure bears on.

**Construction rule.** Which form phase 3 takes is **selected against [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md)'s phase 7 defeat rate**, which measured whether the cohort could construct cases that defeat a chosen system. If that rate was low, the defeating evaluation is supplied as V4 and each group's task is to **explain why it defeats** and to state what would have had to be reported to prevent it. If the rate was high, V4's overlap figures are withheld and each group must **construct its own defeating evaluation** for the V2 winner, with the supplied version revealed afterwards for comparison. Which branch runs is decided from the S051 data before delivery.

Task after: **for each of the four evaluations, state what it fixed — corpus, query population, or relevance criterion — and therefore what a score on it is a claim about.**

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across evaluation constructions with the systems held fixed, so that four orderings of four unchanged systems is the phase's entire finding and requires no interpretation. **The four evaluations are run by the room rather than supplied as tables**, which matters because V3 is the students' own and its result is the only one they can defend. The leaderboard reading is the platform instance and is deliberately not a fifth evaluation — it is an exercise in reading a disclosure, which is what point 7 will ask them to write. The construction rule is this day's discharge of the obligation [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) placed on it under instrumentation rule 6, and it is a branch in what the phase asks students to do rather than in what it shows them.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **A benchmark is a criterion constructed by named people, from evidence, for a purpose.** [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) without modification. The only difference from an ordinary criterion is that nobody using it can name them, which does not make it less constructed.

2. **What a benchmark fixes: a corpus, a query population, and a definition of relevance.** A score on it licenses a claim about that triple. [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md)'s licensing sentence in retrieval form, and the three parts are the three terms [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md) point 7 said dominate the method.

3. **Zero-shot is a claim about the relationship between two corpora, not a property of a model.** It is falsifiable by measuring overlap between the evaluation corpus and the training corpus, that measurement is cheap, and it is rarely reported. **A zero-shot claim with no overlap measurement is an unbounded claim.**

4. **The lexical baseline's role in a comparison is diagnostic.** Where a learned retriever loses to term matching on an unfamiliar corpus, the size of the gap is a measure of how far that corpus sits from its training distribution. This is the most informative single number in most published retrieval comparisons and it is the one most often omitted.

5. **A leaderboard is a selection process.** [S042](/wiki/seminars/S042.md)'s protocol A at community scale: one test set, thousands of evaluations against it, nobody logging the search, and every published figure a maximum over that search. The mechanism is identical to the one students corrected in their own model selection two quarters ago.

6. **The judgement ceiling.** Your own relevance judgements disagree at a rate you measured at [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md). No difference smaller than that rate is observable on judgements of that kind, by you or by anyone. **A reported difference below the ceiling is not a small result; it is no result**, and this is [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md)'s dependency argument with the annotator as the cluster.

7. **What you owe the reader of a retrieval comparison — seven things.** [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) point 8's five, unchanged: the point estimate, the interval, the dependency structure, the unit of analysis, and the smallest detectable effect. Plus two specific to this material: **the query population's provenance**, and **the relevance criterion's author.** A comparison missing the last two is a comparison whose central claim cannot be located.

8. **The platform instance, and the reason it is the best one available. Marked perishable.** The Foundry model leaderboard is a working, current, first-party benchmark surface, and it discloses more about its own construction than most published comparisons do. It states that it is a preview feature. It states that not all models are benchmarked and that its selection is curated. It states that some figures were produced by the vendor and some were reported by providers. It states that its scores are normalised indexes rather than metric values, and that its cost figure assumes a particular ratio of input to output tokens. And in its troubleshooting table, under the heading a person reaches only after being confused, it states: **"Public benchmarks use standardized datasets and might not reflect performance on your specific data."**

    **That sentence is point 2, written by the vendor, shipped with the product, and routinely ignored.** Learn the lesson in the right direction: the disclosure being present is a credit to whoever wrote it and is not a defence for whoever cites the number without it. **A disclosure that exists and is not carried with the claim is functionally a silence**, and the professional obligation this day installs is that you carry it.

    The same page also gives students a second, harder object. Elsewhere in the same product's documentation, hybrid retrieval combined with semantic ranking is recommended on the grounds that **"in benchmark testing, this combination consistently produced the most relevant results."** No benchmark is named. No metric is named. No interval is given. It is a well-founded recommendation stated in a form that licenses nothing, made by an organisation that elsewhere publishes a leaderboard with its construction disclosed — which tells you that the difference between the two is a choice about which page you are writing rather than about what is known. **The recommendation is probably right. It is still unciteable, and a student who repeats it as evidence has done what point 5 describes.**

9. **Defaults are opinions and thresholds are policy — and three of them are inside the ranking you would be comparing.**

    - **When two rankings are fused, they are fused by a formula with a constant in it**, and the constant's published justification is that experiments show a small value works well. Which experiments is not stated. That constant sets how much a top-ranked result from one method outweighs a mid-ranked result from the other, and it is the single largest undisclosed decision in any hybrid comparison.
    - **The semantic reranker sees a fixed and small number of candidates**, and cannot see past them. A comparison between a hybrid system and a reranked hybrid system is partly a comparison of two window sizes, one of which nobody chose.
    - **The reranker's own score has a documented range and a documented instability**: the vendor states that its distribution can vary with infrastructure conditions and with model updates, and advises against fine-grained thresholds. **That is a vendor telling you their instrument is uncalibrated and drifts.** It is more than most instruments in this stack disclose, and it means any threshold you set on it is a policy with an expiry date.

10. **Scope, in the strong form.** Points 3 and 6 are stated for evaluations whose relevance is judged by people. That is **safe** for retrieval, ranking, recommendation, summarisation and any evaluation where a human decides whether the output was right — which is the majority of what these students will be asked to read. It is **unsafe** where the criterion is mechanical: exact-answer question sets, execution-based code evaluation, formally checked outputs. There the judgement ceiling argument does not apply at all, the failure modes are different ones, and **a student who applies point 6 to an execution-based benchmark will make a confident wrong criticism in a room where somebody knows better.** The mechanical case is [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md)'s and the release-gate form is [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md)'s. Points 8 and 9 carry the same boundary in the platform direction: they are **safe** as an account of what this leaderboard and this ranking stack disclose today, and **unsafe** as a claim about vendor benchmarks in general or about this one next quarter. The durable content is the seven things at point 7; everything at points 8 and 9 is an instance of one of them.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Points 2 and 6 are the retained pair. Point 3 is the day's title claim and the one with the shortest path to use. **Point 8 is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s perishable platform point and is chosen because the vendor states the day's argument better than the day does**; its second half supplies the contrast that stops the point becoming a compliment. Point 9 names the defaults. Point 10 is written in the strong form because the day's argument is corrosive and generalises easily, and a student who leaves believing all benchmarks are unreadable has been made less useful rather than more.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 4. The comparison, the claim and the ceiling are the student's. This phase produces a build.**

1. **Choose a published comparison** whose conclusion you have relied on, or would.
2. **State what it fixed** — corpus, query population, relevance criterion — **and who authored each.** Where you cannot find out, say so; that is a finding about the report. **State also which kind of comparison it is: human-judged or mechanically judged.**
3. **State the claim it licenses about your corpus, and the nearest claim it does not**, in [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) step 7's form.
4. **Predict** which of its conclusions survives on the substrate. Commit in writing.
5. **Build the re-run and run it.** Configure the comparison on your own index — the systems, the query set, your [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md) relevance judgements — and report per query class. **The re-run is a runnable artefact, not a table: it must execute end to end from a stated configuration, so that somebody else can run it and get your numbers.**
6. **State the agreement ceiling your own judgements imply, and compare it to the size of the difference you are claiming. If the difference is smaller than the ceiling, say so and report no result.**
7. **Apply the [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) clustering checklist to your own re-run, and report which of its items your design fails.**

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** What you asked the agent for, in order, including what you abandoned. **On this day the record is diagnostic in its own right**: a student who asked the agent which system is best has asked a question the day exists to reject.
- **Intervention record.** Every correction and what it was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that its own design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a re-run that refuses to report a difference below the stated ceiling is the obvious example and is accepted, and is the strongest artefact this day can produce.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch.

**Fading:** step 2 is worked with the room, since locating a benchmark's authors and construction is unfamiliar and is often genuinely hard. The re-run's configuration is produced by the agent and read by the student. **Step 4 is unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 4 in which the student is the reader point 7 describes and then the author. **Step 5 is a build because a re-run that cannot be re-run is a table**, and the day's own argument about reproducible comparison collapses if the students' version is not one. **Step 6 is the artefact element [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md) named under instrumentation rule 6**: the disagreement measured there becomes the floor beneath which this day forbids a claim, and reporting no result is the assessed outcome rather than a failure. Step 7 discharges [Q6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md)'s reporting commitment on the S049 checklist and is deliberately self-incriminating — the checklist is applied to the student's own work, and the expected number of failed items is not zero.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: two internal comparisons of the same pair of substrate retrievers, six weeks apart, run by different people. The first reported that A beat B. The second reported that B beat A. Both were correctly run on correctly sampled query sets. **Both differences were smaller than the relevance-judgement disagreement rate measured on the same annotator pool**, which was known and was in a document nobody read. Attention is directed at what happened next: the second result was treated as contradicting the first, a meeting was held about which was right, and the answer — that neither was a result — was available to everyone in the room.

Then the agent case, and it is the day's own material inverted. A student asked Copilot which retrieval configuration to adopt. The agent produced a recommendation with four citations. **All four were real, current, correctly summarised and correctly linked.** One was a first-party page whose own troubleshooting section carries the standardised-datasets caveat; the agent did not mention it, because it was not in the part of the page that answered the question. The recommendation went into a design note and was accepted. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the citations were checkable and were checked, and what was missing was not in any of them — it was the sentence saying what they licensed.** A reviewer verifying sources verifies that the sources exist and say what is claimed. Nobody's review procedure includes *and does this evidence bear on our corpus*, which is point 2 and is the only question that mattered.

Then students, in threes: a time you explained a difference that was not there, or **a citation you passed on today whose licensing sentence you did not write.**

**Warrant.** Error management targeting point 6. The instructor case contains two correct measurements and no incorrect statement, which is what makes it a lesson about what a number can carry rather than about competence. The meeting is the part students recognise. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is deliberately not a hallucination case** — the programme has one of those at [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md), and the more dangerous agent failure in this material is impeccable sourcing with the scope statement absent, because it survives every review a careful team performs.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **their step 3 licensed claim and their step 6 ceiling comparison.**

The partner's task, both parts required:

- **Restate the licensed claim as one sentence a product owner would act on.**
- **Then identify which of [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s two symmetric failures the restatement commits** — a claim standing stronger than the evidence supports, or a limitation phrased so that the audience cannot act on it.

The student then adjudicates: was the restatement unfair, or did their own wording invite it?

Per rule 3, both the restatement and the adjudication are recorded in the artefact.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with bilateral production — the partner writes, the author judges — and the first application of [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s symmetric failures to a written claim rather than to a spoken encounter. The adjudication is the phase's point: most students will find their careful step 3 wording is unusable the moment somebody has to act on it, which is the failure S050 predicted this cohort would produce.

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- Sealed items opened. **The item 1 to item 4 conclusion-change rate on published results, placed beside [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md)'s step 7 rate on the students' own ablations.** The gap between the two is the day's most quoted result.
- Item 3's stated change conditions against what the breakdowns actually showed.
- **Item 5 opened.** What the agent cited, and what it did not say about what it cited, against each student's sealed prediction. The tally separates students who predicted fabrication from students who predicted a missing licensing sentence, **and the second is the correct answer.**
- Step 4's predictions against step 5's re-run.
- **The cohort's step 6 outcomes: how many reported no result.** Reported plainly and without commentary.
- **The [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) checklist failures, tallied by item**, which is [Q6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md)'s reporting commitment discharged.
- Each student names **one published claim they will stop citing**, and **one claim they will keep citing with a licensing sentence attached.**
- Retrieval schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 7.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in ME202 with IS203. Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the chosen comparison; what it fixed with each part's author or an explicit could-not-determine; **its kind, human-judged or mechanical**; the licensed claim and the nearest unlicensed one; the pre-committed prediction; **the runnable per-class re-run with its configuration**; **the ceiling comparison with its no-result finding where applicable**; the [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) checklist applied with failed items named; the phase 7 restatement and adjudication; **the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

**A claimed difference smaller than the stated ceiling, reported as a result, fails.** This is the day's single hard requirement.

**A judgement ceiling applied to a mechanically-judged comparison fails.** Point 10's scope boundary is made checkable in the artefact rather than only stated in the instruction, and [WP-059](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-059.md) §8 explains why that repair does not settle the general question.

**A re-run reported without the [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) checklist applied fails.** The checklist is applied to the student's own work and failed items are reported, not repaired.

**A re-run that cannot be executed from its stated configuration fails.** Step 5 is a build.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that its own design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

No entrustment judgement. The quarter's is at [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md).

**Rule-6 discharges.** Three obligations were placed on this day and all three are built.

- [S051](/wiki/seminars/S051.md) named this day's **phase 3** as the response occasion for its phase 7 defeat rate. **The element exists: phase 3 runs one of two constructions selected against that rate, and the branch determines whether the defeating evaluation is supplied for explanation or must be constructed by the group.** The obligation is discharged.
- [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md) named this day's **phase 1** as the response occasion for its step 7 aggregate-only counterfactual rate. **The element exists: phase 1 collects an adoption conclusion from aggregate scores, supplies the per-dataset breakdowns, and collects it again, so the same measurement is retaken on published results rather than on the student's own work.** The obligation is discharged.
- [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md) named this day's **phase 5** as the response occasion for its relevance-disagreement distribution. **The element exists, is assessed and is a fail condition: step 6 requires the student to state the ceiling their own judgements imply and to report no result where their claimed difference falls below it.** The obligation is discharged.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions:

- **The step 6 no-result rate** measures whether a cohort will decline to report a difference they have measured. **Response occasion: [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md), phase 5, where each student's offline difference is compared against the online outcome and the ceiling is tested as a predictor of which offline differences survived contact with users.**
- **The phase 1 conclusion-change rate on published results, against [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md)'s rate on the students' own work** measures whether scepticism transfers outward. **Response occasion: [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md), phase 4, where every student is asked which published claim their ship decision relies on and what its per-dataset breakdown showed.**

**Frontier instrumentation.** Item 5's sealed prediction accuracy, coded for whether the student anticipated fabrication or a missing licensing sentence, is carried to the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) per instrumentation rule 9. Per rule 3 the sample is stated: one cohort, one agent version, on a question chosen because a well-sourced answer with no scope statement was the likely output.
