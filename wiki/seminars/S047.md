# S047 · Plan Before Implementation

**Strands:** PE · ME

**Whitepaper:** [WP-047](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-047.md)

**Entry state:** either

## The situation

The corpus behind this stage states a prohibition as a headline takeaway, and it is the best single rule in approximately ninety documents.

**Use the tooling to make each step better, not to skip steps.**

The same collection's customer-facing material then tells its proudest story, in which a prototype is generated on the first day from a template with no discovery activity described at all. **Read against the collection's own method, the flagship story is step-skipping presented as acceleration, and neither document notices.**

Both of those things are in the same body of work, written by the same organisation, for different audiences. This session puts them on the same page.

The pinned repository above supplies the mechanism the prohibition needs to be more than a slogan. It separates the work into gated phases — research, plan, implement, review, follow-up — with explicit paths **back** to earlier phases rather than only forward, and it holds the record of each phase on disk under stable identifiers, declared authoritative over whatever the conversation remembers. The argument is provenance and restartability: an interrupted run resumes from files, and a decision made while planning can be found by identifier while implementing.

**That establishes that the record exists and is addressable. It establishes nothing about whether the decisions in it were correct**, and the repository says so about itself.

Answer these before instruction.

1. Where is your current plan, and what happens to it when this window closes?
2. You changed your approach yesterday. Where is that recorded, and by what name?
3. What is the difference between a phase that completed and a phase that was accepted?
4. What would you need to know before implementing that you do not know now?
5. Which planning step did the tooling let you skip this week, and what did skipping it cost?

**Item 5 is the session's subject.** The honest answer is often *none, because it did not cost anything* — and that answer needs the same scrutiny as its opposite, because a cost you did not observe is not a cost you did not pay.

## What you must produce

A **plan for one increment of your slice**, durable on disk, with:

- **Stable identifiers** for each phase and each task within it, used consistently across the research record, the plan, the implementation evidence and the review.
- A **research record** stating what was established before planning and where it came from.
- **Blockers** stated as conditions rather than as feelings, each with what would clear it.
- **A return path**: the named circumstance under which implementation stops and goes back to planning, and who may declare it.
- **A completion-versus-acceptance line**: what will make each phase complete, and separately, what will make it accepted.
- **A skipped-step register**: every step of your own process the tooling let you omit, with what you did instead and what evidence would show the omission was safe.

## What good looks like

- **A decision made during planning can be located from the implementation record by its identifier**, and this is demonstrated rather than claimed.
- **The plan survives the end of the session.** Close everything, reopen, and continue from files.
- **Completion and acceptance are separate lines with different signatories**, and at least one acceptance is a human decision the executor may not record.
- **The return path names a circumstance, not a mood.** *If it is not working* is not a return path.
- **The skipped-step register is non-empty and specific**, and at least one entry names something the learner would defend skipping.
- **The research record distinguishes what was verified from what was assumed**, per [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md)'s discipline about findings of fact.

## How it goes wrong

- **The conversation plan** — it exists in the exchange and nowhere else. *Warning sign:* you would have to scroll to answer item 2.
- **The plan written afterwards** — produced to accompany work already done. *Warning sign:* every phase completed as planned.
- **Completion read as acceptance** — a phase ran to the end and is therefore treated as approved. *Warning sign:* nobody said anything between the last task and the next phase.
- **The skipped plan** — generation is cheap, so implementation started before planning finished, and the plan was reconstructed to match. *Warning sign:* your first artefact of the day is code.
- **The unused identifier** — identifiers exist, nothing references them. *Warning sign:* you cannot trace a single decision forward.
- **The plan as ritual** — long, complete, and disconnected from what was built. *Warning sign:* nobody returned to it after the first hour.

## Do it

**First, run one increment through the phases and preserve everything.** Research, plan, implement, review. Keep every artefact and every identifier, including the ones for work you abandoned. **Then ask the question the repository asks of its own record: what does this prove, and what does it not?** It proves what was decided and when. It proves nothing about whether the decision was right, and a plan whose main effect is to make the work feel accountable is worse than no plan, because it is accountability theatre with a filing system.

**Second, put the prohibition and the anecdote side by side.** Write down which one your own week resembled. **A learner who finds their week resembled the anecdote has produced the session's most useful finding**, provided they can say which step was skipped and what evidence would show whether the skip mattered.

**Third, take the corpus's other rule seriously**: given a fixed time budget, run more iterations rather than fewer and more refined ones. That rule and the prohibition are in tension in a way the collection never resolves — more iterations of a step is *making the step better*, and fewer, longer steps is what a careful engineer does when told not to skip anything. **Decide which one your plan implements, and say so in it.**

**Fourth, exercise the return path deliberately.** Take a task in the implementation phase, declare the return condition met, and go back to planning. Record what changed. **A return path nobody has used is a diagram.**

## Show it

Hand somebody your identifiers and one question from your implementation record — *why is this like this?* — and let them find the answer without you.

If they cannot, the record is a filing cabinet rather than a chain of custody. **The test is not whether the record exists; it is whether it answers a question asked from the other end.**

## What this does not establish

Nothing here shows that phase separation produces better software, and the pinned repository is unusually clear that it makes no such demonstration.

It contains distinct evaluation lanes, a schema validator, link and metadata checks, and behavioural graders — and **no committed result corpus.** A grader exists; results do not. Its own documentation records that its behaviour checks test output shape rather than correctness, that some checks are advisory by default and lack established calibration, and that human oversight remains necessary. **The revision this session pins is not all-green**, and that is part of the evidence rather than a blemish on it.

The internal corpus's prohibition against skipping steps is **a practitioner judgement with no measurement attached**, stated as a takeaway and contradicted by its own author's flagship story. This session teaches the contradiction because the contradiction is the durable content; the prohibition on its own is advice.

**The strongest claim available is about provenance:** a record held under stable identifiers can answer a question asked later, and a conversation cannot. That is a property of files. It is not a claim about correctness and this session does not make one.

## Perishable content in this session

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| A plan you can re-enter is a different artefact from one you remember | The pinned repository's phase names, tracking directory and identifier formats, at commit `cf29fb45`, package version 3.3.101 |
| Completion and acceptance are different results | The current workflow's representation of each, and whether the host enforces the distinction |
