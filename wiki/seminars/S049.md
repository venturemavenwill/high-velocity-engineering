# S049 · Clustering, Power and the Interval You Owe the Reader

**Module:** [M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) · **Quarter:** [Q5](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** ME · MS
**Whitepaper:** [WP-049](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-049.md)
**Depends on:** S028
**Re-tests:** S022, S043
**Platform anchor:** Foundry evaluation run comparison and its t-test output; the `evaluation_level` parameter and conversation split strategies · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server (preview) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

[S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md) taught intervals and said, explicitly, that clustering was being set aside and would return with the machinery to handle it. This is that day.

The machinery matters because the assumption S028 made — that observations are independent — is false in almost every measurement a working engineer takes. Four hundred requests from twelve users are not four hundred independent observations. Four hundred outputs scored by four judges are not four hundred independent judgements. Four hundred sessions from one afternoon on one deployment are not four hundred independent samples of anything except that afternoon.

The target: **an interval is a statement about a procedure applied to a dependency structure, the same data yield different honest intervals under different structures, and the structure is something you argue rather than assume.**

The day's second half is the question that comes before the interval: what is the smallest effect this design could have detected? A design that could not have detected the effect you care about has not failed to find it — it never looked.

**Both halves are now something a service will do for you, quickly, with colours.** Foundry compares two evaluation runs with a t-test, reports a p-value, a delta estimate, a sample count and a standard deviation, and labels each cell improved, degraded, changed or **inconclusive**. Every one of those numbers is correct arithmetic. The sample count is a count of rows, the rows are usually not independent, and *inconclusive* is the label the platform applies both when there was no effect and when there were too few examples to have found one. The reasoning this day teaches is not made unnecessary by that tooling. It is the only thing standing between the tooling and a wrong decision.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

A result is supplied, in the form students will meet it — an evaluation run comparison as the platform returns it:

> The new retrieval component improved answer relevance by 0.08 on our criterion. 95% interval [0.03, 0.13], n = 400. Baseline mean 3.41, treatment mean 3.49, p = 0.004, **ImprovedWeak**.

Five items.

1. Do you believe it? What would you want to know?
2. Here are the same 400 observations with their user identifiers attached. **There are twelve users.** Now?
3. What is n? And what did the service count?
4. Predict: recomputed with the user as the unit, how much wider is the interval?
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to *tell us whether the new component is better.* Write down what it will produce, and **name the one thing it will get wrong**. Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 4's predictions are collected and kept for phase 8. Most are substantial underestimates.

Item 5's expectation is that the agent will run a correct comparison, read the p-value correctly, quote the label the service returned, and answer the question asked — without ever establishing what population the claim is about or what the sample count counted.

**Warrant.** Pretesting with a structural reveal. Nothing about the data changes at item 2 — only what the reader is told about how they were collected — and the interval nonetheless becomes indefensible. That the reveal requires no statistics to feel is the reason it opens the day. Item 3's second clause is deliberate: the number in the report was produced by a service, and asking what the service counted is a different question from asking what n is. Item 5 is administered here and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative retrieval set (20 min)

Free recall, no notes.

- The S028 sampling frame, and the four things a number does not license.
- The S027 four error sources.
- The S043 variance account: what raises variance, and what a high-variance estimate looks like.
- The S022 expectation-over-randomisation argument.
- **Bridge:** two observations from the same user are more alike than two from different users. Why does that reduce what four hundred observations tell you?

The bridge is answered by the room, not by the instructor. The answer wanted is that the second observation from a user carries less new information than the first, so the count of observations overstates the count of independent facts.

**Warrant.** Expanding-interval retrieval discharging S028's explicit deferral twenty-one days later. The S022 item is retrieved because expectation over a randomisation the engineer controls and expectation over a sampling structure the engineer inherited are the same object approached from opposite ends.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups receive **one point estimate and four dependency structures**, as four real evaluation runs over their own criterion's outputs. The measured difference is 0.08 in every case; only the collection story differs.

- **Structure A** — 400 observations, 400 distinct users, one each, sampled across four weeks.
- **Structure B** — 400 observations from 12 users.
- **Structure C** — 400 observations from 400 distinct users, all collected in one afternoon on one deployment version.
- **Structure D** — 400 observations from 100 users, each scored by 4 judges — the judges of [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md), whose systematic components the students have already characterised.

Groups rank the four by expected interval width, commit to the ranking and to a rough factor, then compute or estimate.

The intervals are then revealed. The ranking surprises most groups in at least one place, and **structure C's interval is not wide at all — its problem is not width but that it is an interval about the wrong population.**

Task after: **state, for each structure, the claim the interval supports.**

**Then two things are run that nobody asked for.**

First, **the same conversations are scored twice** — once with the evaluation level set to turn and once set to conversation — and the group records how many rows each produces. Nothing about the traffic changed. The service manufactured a different n from the same interactions, by a parameter that defaults to turn when it is omitted. The same choice appears one layer up as the conversation split strategy, whose default is the last turn, and whose documentation states the consequence in a single sentence: **how you split determines what you are evaluating.** A fifth dependency structure has thereby been produced by configuration rather than by collection, and nobody in the room collected it.

Second, groups run the platform's own comparison over structures A and B and read the label. **Structure B returns `Inconclusive`, and the published legend defines `Inconclusive` as too few examples, or p ≥ 0.05.** Two entirely different states — *there was no effect* and *this design could not have found one* — share a colour and a word. Groups are asked which of the two their result is, and how they could tell from what the service returned. They cannot, because nothing in the output reports a minimum detectable effect.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases holding the point estimate fixed and varying only the dependency structure. Structure C remains the phase's payload: it separates two failures that students merge — an interval that is too narrow, and an interval that is correctly computed about a population nobody cares about. The first is a statistics error; the second is S028's sampling-frame error wearing a statistics costume. **The two added runs are [WP-049](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-049.md) §8's objection-2 repair.** That objection conceded that the day's most-needed correction — point 7, on power and the emptiness of its post-hoc form — was the one load-bearing claim the day asserted rather than staged. The `Inconclusive` label stages it, because the confusion the correction exists to prevent is printed in the vendor's own legend, and the room meets it before phase 4 names it.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **An interval is a property of a procedure applied to a structure,** not a property of the data. Change what you assume about how the data arose and the interval changes though no observation has moved.

2. **Clustering, and the design effect.** Observations within a cluster are more alike than observations across clusters. Under the standard exchangeable model, the effective sample size is roughly $n_{\text{eff}} = \dfrac{n}{1 + (m-1)\rho}$ — where $m$ is the cluster size and $\rho$ the intra-cluster correlation. **This is an identity under a stated model, not a measured effect: it is exactly as good as the model, and the exchangeable model is often wrong about time.**

3. **The unit of analysis is the unit you want to generalise to.** A claim about users has users as its n. A claim about requests has requests. Most disputes about "what is n" are disputes about what the claim is, conducted in the wrong vocabulary.

4. **Ignoring positive clustering narrows the interval and never widens it.** Where the intra-cluster correlation is positive — which is the ordinary case for users, sessions, documents and machines — the independence assumption errs in exactly one direction: overconfidence. **This is why the specification fails an independence-assuming interval unless independence is argued.** The error is not symmetric, so the assumption is not neutral.

5. **Judges are a clustering factor.** Structure D. Every output scored by the same judge shares that judge's systematic component, which S048 established is not zero. A crossed design has two clustering factors and neither can be ignored.

6. **Power is a question you ask before.** State the smallest effect worth acting on, then ask what sample the design needs to detect it. A design that cannot detect the effect you care about has not produced a negative result; it has produced no result.

7. **Post-hoc power computed from the observed effect tells you nothing you did not already have.** It is a transformation of the same quantity the interval reports, and it cannot rescue a study that has already run. This is the day's most-needed correction, and it is common in exactly the reports students will be asked to review.

8. **What you owe the reader — five things.** The point estimate. The interval. **The dependency structure that produced the interval.** The unit of analysis. And the smallest effect the design could have detected. A report missing the third and fifth is not a shorter report; it is a report whose central claim cannot be checked.

9. **What the platform supplies against that list, checked item by item.** It returns an average, a standard deviation, a sample count, a delta estimate, a p-value and the name of the statistical method. **That is the point estimate and the raw material for an interval.** It does not report the dependency structure, because it does not know one. It does not report the unit of analysis, because it counts rows. It does not report a minimum detectable effect anywhere. **Two of the five things you owe a reader are absent from the output by construction, and they are the third and the fifth** — which is the report shape point 8 describes as uncheckable, produced by default, by a well-built service, at no effort. The CI extensions state that their results include confidence intervals and significance tests; nothing in any of them supplies a structure or a unit, and nothing can, because those are properties of how you collected the data and the service was not there.

10. **Defaults are opinions, and two of them decide your n.** The evaluation level defaults to **turn**, so a multi-turn agent is scored one reply at a time unless somebody says otherwise, and turn-only evaluators cannot be used at conversation level at all. The conversation split defaults to **the last turn**, so what precedes it becomes context rather than evidence. Both are reasonable defaults. Both change what the number is about, and neither announces itself in the result. **An engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system.**

11. **What is perishable here and what is not.** The legend's labels, the thresholds at 0.001 and 0.05, the parameter names, the preview markings and the fields in the comparison payload are all perishable. **Nothing else in this day is.** The dependency-structure argument, the unit-of-analysis rule, the direction of the independence error, power as a prior question and the five things owed to a reader predate every technology this programme teaches and will outlast them. This is the most durable day in the module, and its platform layer is the thinnest for that reason: the service is here to show that a correct arithmetic pipeline can produce an indefensible claim, which is a fact about claims and not about the service.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Point 2 carries the strong-form scope statement, and is stated as a modelled identity rather than a finding because the day's whole argument is against numbers whose provenance is unstated. Point 4 supplies the specification's justification, which the students therefore hold before they meet it. Point 7 is the correction, now staged at phase 3 rather than asserted here; point 8 is the retained list; point 9 audits the list against a real output, which is the form in which students will actually fail to supply it. Points 10 and 11 are the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required platform content.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 4. The claim, the structure and the unit are the student's, and this phase produces a build.**

Students use Copilot in agent mode, through the Foundry MCP Server, to run the comparison and then to **ship the report the comparison does not write**. They take their own criterion — built at [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md), validated at [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md), judged by the judge they characterised at [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) — and their deployed model from [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md), and:

1. **State the claim** they want to make, and the population it is about.
2. **Enumerate every clustering factor** in the data they have, including the judge.
3. **Choose the unit of analysis** and justify it against step 1.
4. **Predict the effective sample size** before computing it.
5. **Run the platform's comparison, and compute the interval under their chosen structure, and under independence, and report all three** — including what the service's sample count counted.
6. **State the smallest effect** their design could have detected.
7. **State the claim the interval supports — and the nearest claim it does not.**

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a report generator that refuses to emit a claim until a unit of analysis has been named is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch. Both specific, both from this build.

**Fading:** step 2 is supported by a candidate-factor checklist worked with the room — user, session, time window, deployment version, document source, judge, annotator, machine. Step 5's platform mechanics are supplied just in time. **Step 4 is unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 4 and the module's load peak, which is why steps 1 to 3 are heavily scaffolded and only the prediction is bare. Step 5's requirement to report all three numbers is deliberate: the gap between the two computed intervals is the cost of the assumption, expressed in the only unit that persuades anyone, and the service's own figure alongside them is what the student's colleagues will actually be shown. Step 7's "nearest claim it does not support" is harder than a general disclaimer and is the day's discriminator.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate measurement that drove a real decision, whose interval assumed independence, whose observations came from nine pilot teams, and which reversed when the analysis was redone by team. Attention is directed at one detail — **the original report contained the team identifiers.** Everything needed to catch it was on the page and nobody looked.

Then the agent case. A student asked Copilot to *compare the two runs and tell me if the change helped*. The agent created the comparison, polled it, read the result, and reported that the treatment improved relevance with high statistical significance, quoting the delta, the p-value and the label the service returned. **Every figure was the service's own and every sentence was accurate.** The rows came from eleven users. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: the reviewer checked the numbers against the platform's output, they matched, and **checking a claim against the system that produced it is the review most people perform** — it verifies transcription and nothing else.

Then students, in threes: a number you acted on without asking how it was collected, or a place today where you accepted agent output you had not checked.

**Warrant.** Error management targeting point 4. The instructor disclosure is chosen because the failure was not a hidden fact but an unasked question, which is the form the students' own version will take. The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is the same failure with a service in the loop, which makes it harder rather than easier to see.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **their step 2 enumeration and step 7 claim**, withholding step 5's numbers.

The partner's task: **name a clustering factor the student did not, and say what direction it moves the interval.**

Then: does the step 7 claim survive it?

Per rule 3, the outcome is recorded in the artefact — including a partner who found nothing, which is a result about the enumeration and not an absence of one. Per rule 5, this reuses [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md)'s adversarial-audit structure, including its withholding of the numbers, which prevents the partner reasoning backwards from the answer.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with an adversarial completeness test. Enumerations are checked by other people or not at all: the factor you did not name is, by construction, the one you cannot see.

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- Item 4's predicted widening against the actual.
- Step 4's predicted effective sample size against the computed, **split by direction** — over-estimation and under-estimation are different errors and the room's distribution between them is discussed.
- **Item 5 opened.** The sealed Copilot predictions are read against what the agent actually did at phase 5, and the cohort's hit rate is recorded in two bands: predicted *some* error, and named *the unit-of-analysis* error.
- The cohort's step 2 enumerations pooled into a candidate-factor checklist that is added to the substrate's measurement guidance and carried to S067.
- Each student names one number they currently believe whose dependency structure they have never asked about.
- Retrieval schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 6.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in ME201, contributing to M10's portfolio. Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: a stated claim with its population; an enumeration of clustering factors including the judge; a justified unit of analysis; a pre-committed effective-sample-size prediction; **all three numbers from step 5, with a statement of what the service's sample count counted**; the smallest detectable effect; the claim supported; the nearest claim not supported; the phase 7 outcome; **the prompt record**; **the intervention record**; **the frontier statement**. **Nothing else is assessed.**

**An interval computed as though observations were independent fails, unless independence is argued.** Arguing it is permitted and is not rare — a genuinely one-observation-per-user sample is independent in the relevant respect — but the argument must be present.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

No entrustment judgement. The module's is tomorrow.

**Programme instrumentation.** The step 4 prediction against the computed value, split by direction, is the day's headline measure. The phase 7 found-factor rate is carried to S067 as a measure of whether enumeration completeness improved.

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| An interval is a property of a procedure applied to a structure | A t-test over rows, with `sampleCount` and `standardDeviation` |
