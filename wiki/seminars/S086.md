# S086 · Reusable Starting Points

**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-086](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-086.md)

**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

A starter repository was assembled during the engagement and is now proposed for a different customer, in a different sector, whose scoping has already begun.

Inside it are the two review-routing thresholds set by an engineer late in a working week with no recorded provenance, and the evaluation-set selection rule that was never written down.

Answer these in writing before the session opens.

1. For each item you propose to carry: what does it rest on, and how would the next team find that out without you?
2. Which items are about the problem, and which are about the customer you built them for? Those are different, and only one may travel.
3. What in this repository would let somebody skip a step rather than do it better?
4. How will the next team know this is out of date? Name the signal, not the intention.
5. What are you retiring, and what does retiring it cost the next engagement?

**Item 3 is the session's subject.** A reusable starting point is exactly the instrument that makes step-skipping look like acceleration. The rule that holds this whole practice together — use the tooling to make each step better, not to skip steps — is stated as a headline in one part of the corpus and contradicted by the proudest story in another, in which a prototype is produced on the first day from a template with no discovery activity described at all. **Neither document notices.** You are about to build the thing that produced that contradiction.

## What you must produce

A **reuse ledger**. One row per item, with four fields and no others.

- **What it is**, at a granularity somebody could act on. Not *the repository*.
- **What it rests on** — the decision record, the observation, the constraint, the customer fact. **A name is not a provenance.** *Written by a senior engineer* records who to blame, not what to check.
- **What must be re-established before use**, by whom, on the receiving engagement.
- **Its staleness signal** — the observable thing that will tell somebody this is no longer current, and who would see it.

Plus a **retirement list**, with at least one entry, and the cost of each retirement stated.

## What good looks like

- Provenance is a fact about the item, not about its author. A threshold's provenance is the observation it was tuned against; if there is none, the row says so and the item is not carried without a re-establishment step.
- **Customer-specific content is separated from problem-specific content, and the separation is defended item by item.** A field structure that somebody at the operator believes was set by a regulator is not a reusable schema; it is one organisation's inheritance.
- The staleness signal is observable by the receiving team. *Review annually* is an intention. *This breaks when the platform's default changes, and the pipeline will fail on the day it does* is a signal.
- **At least one item is retired**, and the retirement costs something. A ledger with no retirements has not been read.
- Anything that was deliberately disposable stays disposable. Spike code was discarded at [S031](/wiki/seminars/S031.md) for a reason that has not changed because the code is now convenient.
- The ledger says what it does not contain: the parts of this engagement that must not travel, named.

## How it goes wrong

Five failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **Carrying by default** — everything goes in, because nothing in it is obviously wrong. *Warning sign:* your ledger and your repository have the same contents.
- **Residue** — one customer's data, identifiers, thresholds or assumptions travel inside something described as generic. *Warning sign:* you would not show the item to the receiving customer in the state it is in.
- **The unprovenanced constant** — a number that works, whose origin nobody can state, now reused as though it were a default. *Warning sign:* somebody defends a value by saying it has always been that.
- **The starting point that skips the step** — the next engagement generates something on day one and treats the discovery it did not do as time saved. *Warning sign:* the item's value is described in terms of elapsed time.
- **The invisible catalogue** — the reused asset carries no version, no age and no coverage statement, so a stale one produces confident, well-formed, out-of-date output with no signal that this happened. *Warning sign:* nothing in the item records when it was last true.

## Do it

Work from the supplied situation in [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md). Build the ledger against the artefacts the engagement actually produced, including the evaluation set from [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) and the decision records from [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md).

Take the two thresholds first. **They cannot be carried as values.** Decide what may be carried instead — the fact that a threshold is required at that point, the method for setting one, the observation that would justify a value — and write that row.

Then take the evaluation set. Its selection rule was never written down, which means the set encodes a rule nobody can state. Decide whether it travels, and if it does, what re-establishment the receiving team owes before a single result from it is believed.

Then retire something. **Pick the item whose retirement you will most resent**, and say what the next engagement loses.

## Show it

One row, read aloud in full, including its staleness signal. Then your retirement and its cost.

The room has one job: **to ask what happens if the receiving team uses the item without doing the re-establishment step.** If your answer is that they would notice, say how. If your answer is that they would not, your row is not finished.

## What this does not establish

**That carrying anything forward helps.** Reusable starting points are named in the practice corpus as one of the things that must hold together for accelerated delivery to produce value rather than activity. **That is the whole of it.** Nothing anywhere describes how one is built, what provenance it must carry, how it is versioned, who owns it, when it is retired, or whether reuse ever produced a better outcome than starting again. It is a pillar with nothing underneath it, and this session is written into that gap.

**The nearest thing to evidence points the other way.** The one honest note in the corpus about working on a mature codebase records that the assistant could not be asked to add a feature end to end, because the generated code reused outdated patterns and ignored the architecture's constraints — the workaround being to make failing tests the specification. That is a report about reuse producing the wrong thing, and it sits in speaker notes while the headline claims sit on slides.

**And the risk this session is most worried about is documented as a pattern, not a finding.** Where a single knowledge source drives a generated process, that source's blind spots become the process's blind spots, and no step records the source's version, age or coverage anywhere in any output. **A stale input produces a confident, well-formatted, out-of-date result containing no signal that this happened.** That is a description of one design. Whether it generalises to a starter repository is this session's argument and nobody's evidence.
