# S086 · What Actually Transfers

**Module:** [M18](/wiki/modules/M18.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** PE · CF · SY
**Whitepaper:** [WP-086](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-086.md)
**Depends on:** S015, S038, S084
**Platform anchor:** the substrate's own infrastructure repository — Bicep and `azure.yaml` — and the Microsoft 365 admin centre agent registry as the tenant's inventory of what exists and who owns it · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

In eleven weeks the students leave, and the substrate does not.

Every one of them has a component they are the reason for. They know which log line matters and which is noise, which test is load-bearing and which was written to make a coverage number move, which of the system's four oddities is a defect and which is the only thing standing between the substrate and a failure class nobody has written down. **None of that is in the repository.** It is in them.

The reflex is to write it down, and the reflex is not wrong. It is insufficient in a specific and measurable way, and the measurement is the day.

**Transfer is a property of the receiver, not of the sender.** A handover succeeded if a team that has never met the author can operate the system, diagnose it when it misbehaves, and change it without breaking something they could not have known about. It failed otherwise. The volume of what was written is close to uncorrelated with which of those two happened, and the departing engineer is the worst-placed person in the organisation to judge it, because they cannot read their own document without the context that makes it work.

And the receiver's verdict is now partly mechanical. **A receiving team either can provision the substrate from its repository or cannot.** That is not a matter of opinion, it takes twelve minutes to find out, and a student whose artefact produces a fluent description and a failed provision has learned something about their own work that no document review would have told them.

The target: **what a receiving team retains is measured on them, at a delay, by asking them to do something — and the gap between that measurement and the author's prediction of it is the day's finding.**

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Seven items on material not yet taught. Confidence attached to each. Never recorded.

1. You are leaving a component you own. **Name the three things a receiving team must be able to do with it.**
2. **Which of the three is hardest to transfer, and why?**
3. A departing engineer writes a forty-page handover document; a second spends the same time pairing and writes nothing. **Which receiving team is better off at three months, and what would you need to know to answer?**
4. **What proportion of what you know about your component do you believe is written down anywhere?** Give a fraction and a one-line justification.
5. Name one thing about your component that you have **never** said out loud to anyone.
6. Predict: **when a receiver who has not met you reads your best current documentation and tries to describe your component's job, how many things will they get wrong?** Give a number.
7. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to produce the handover material for your component from the repository alone. Write down what it will produce, and **name the one thing it will state confidently and get wrong.** Sealed with items 1 to 6 and opened at phase 8.

Items 4 and 6 are the day's honest items. Item 4 is a self-assessment that phase 5 tests directly; item 6 is a numeric prediction checked against an observation four hours later. Item 7 is the day's contribution to the running frontier map, and the design's expectation is that most students will predict the agent will invent a runbook step and a minority will name **which** step.

**Warrant.** Pretesting the transfer question before any framework is supplied, with two items constructed to be over-confident. Item 3 admits a plausible-but-wrong answer in both directions and is the item phase 3 defeats. Item 5 exists because it is answerable — every student has one — and because naming it makes the tacit layer concrete before the day argues about it. Item 7 is sealed at phase 1 and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8: an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.

The bridge is answered by the room, and the answer is the condition every subsequent day of the module operates under.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups of four receive **four completed handovers from this substrate's own history**, each with two records: what the departing engineer produced and believed they had transferred, and what the receiving team could actually do at three months, evidenced by the incident log, the change log and a structured interview conducted at the time.

**Each group also gets the repository as it stood on the day of each handover, and a disposable subscription.** Before they read the outcome records they attempt, from each of the four handovers and nothing else, to bring the component up: `azd provision` or `az deployment group what-if` against the archived template, whichever the handover supplied. Twenty minutes, four attempts, and the result of each attempt is written down before the outcome records appear.

**The sampling is disclosed, per instrumentation rule 3.** These four are the substrate's only handovers with both records available. They were not selected to make a point, they are not a sample of anything, and the receiving-team interviews were conducted by staff who knew which handover they were asking about. Groups are told this before they read. **The provision attempts are a further selection and are disclosed as one**: the archived templates are the templates that survived, which is not the same population as the templates that existed.

- **H1 · The comprehensive document.** Forty pages, indexed, accurate, reviewed. The receiving team could operate the component and could not change it: every modification they attempted broke something the document described but had not connected to anything. **Provisions on the second attempt, after the reader works out that two of the parameter values are given in the appendix and not in the procedure.**
- **H2 · The paired fortnight.** Two weeks of shadowing, almost nothing written. The receiving team could change the component confidently and could not explain to a third party why any of it was the way it was. When two of them left, the component became unmaintainable a second time. **Does not provision. The template is correct and the two environment values the pair agreed verbally are nowhere.**
- **H3 · The runbook and the recorded walkthroughs.** Excellent for the cases it covered. The receiving team followed it into an incident it did not cover and made the incident worse, because the runbook's confident tone had told them they were inside its scope. **Provisions first time, and the runbook's confident tone is the reason nobody checked what it had provisioned.**
- **H4 · The one that wrote nothing.** The departing engineer produced **no new document at all.** They spent the fortnight changing the system: renaming four things so the names carried their reasons, converting three pieces of undocumented knowledge into failing tests that state the constraint in their assertion messages, adding the *why* to eleven log lines that already existed, deleting two components nobody could explain, and putting the two values H2 lost into the template with a comment saying what breaks if they change. The receiving team retained more than any of the other three, on all three measures. **Provisions first time, and the reader can say afterwards what it provisioned.**

Groups rank the four on *what the receiving team could do at three months* and **commit before the records are supplied.** Then the records are supplied.

The payload is **H4**, and the room's expectation it defeats is that transfer happens through a representation of the system. H4 transferred by changing the system so that the system says it. That is [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md)'s "a name is a model" turned into an operation rather than a caution, and it is [S011](/wiki/seminars/S011.md)'s question — what does this representation make cheap — asked about the codebase rather than about a data structure.

Task after: **for each of the four, state which of the three receiver capabilities it delivered and which it did not, and name the mechanism.** Then: **which of the four provision outcomes did you predict from reading the handover, and which surprised you?** Then: **what did H4 lose?**

The last question matters and has an answer. H4 transferred nothing about the organisation — who to call, which team's roadmap this component is on, why the constraint that produced the failing test was agreed to in the first place. It is not a general solution and the day does not present it as one.

The middle question is the day's cheapest surprise and its most important boundary. **H3 provisions and H2 does not, and H2's receiving team was better off.** A provision result is a hard measurement of one capability and it is close to silent about the other two, which is exactly the failure the day is teaching about, performed by the day's own new instrument. The room is asked to say so, and it is the reason the specification does not assess whether the provision succeeded.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across the transfer medium with a real outcome record as adjudicator, matched on component, on organisation and on the fortnight of effort, and varying only what the departing engineer produced. The commit-before-records structure is what makes the ranking a generation act rather than a reading comprehension exercise. **The provision attempts convert one of the three capabilities from a claim into an observation and demonstrate their own insufficiency in the same forty-five minutes**, which is a better introduction to the day's measurement problem than any statement of it. The final question exists because a payload case that is not bounded becomes the next reflex, and this cohort has three days left in which to over-apply it.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **The three capabilities.** A receiving team must be able to **operate** the system, **diagnose** it when it misbehaves, and **change** it without breaking something they could not have known about. These are different capabilities with different evidence and different transfer media, and the room's phase 3 table shows that no single medium delivered all three. **The list is not exhaustive: it omits the capability to *retire* the system, which is real, is rarely transferred at all, and is [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md)'s.**

2. **Transfer is measured on the receiver.** The sender's belief about what they transferred is a prediction, and it is a prediction made by the person least able to evaluate it. The room's item 4 fractions are shown against nothing yet; phase 5 supplies the comparison.

3. **Four media, and what each carries.** A document carries facts and carries connections badly. Pairing carries connections and does not survive the pair leaving. A test carries a constraint and carries it into the future, because it fails when the constraint is violated. A name carries a model, cheaply, to every reader who ever touches the code — [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md) point 1, now load-bearing. **This is four of the media available and there are others; it omits the organisational media — a rota, an escalation path, a named contact — which carry more than any of the four and are [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md)'s material. Treating the four as the space is safe when the receiving team already exists inside the same organisation and is unsafe when the receiver is external, where the organisational transfer is the whole problem and the technical transfer is the easy part.**

4. **Prefer the medium that fails loudly.** A document that has gone stale looks exactly like a document that has not. A test that has gone stale fails. This is the operative reason H4 worked and is the day's most portable heuristic: **where you can move knowledge from a description of the system into the system, do it, because the system is the only artefact that is checked continuously.**

5. **The platform keeps an inventory, and it counts the thing this module is about.** The Microsoft 365 admin centre's agent registry lists every agent available to the tenant, including agents built outside Microsoft, and its summary carries three counters: total agents, **agents without owners** — the number that no longer have an owner at the organisation — and unmanaged agents, meaning those created or managed outside Agent 365 and therefore without its risk protection and observability. A named administrator action, *assign a new owner*, exists precisely because the first counter goes up. **The tenant's own dashboard has a number on it for the failure [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) spends a day resolving.** What the documentation does not give is any bound on how long an entry stays wrong, any statement of how an owner's departure is detected, or any rate at all. It counts the state and is silent about the transition into it. **Perishable: the counters, the portal path and the product name will all move. Durable: an inventory that can tell you an owner is missing and cannot tell you when they went missing is the ordinary condition of every inventory, and a handover that relies on one is relying on a lagging indicator.**

6. **Defaults are opinions, and one of today's is about whether your infrastructure exists as an artefact at all.** `azd ai agent init` is **Bicep-less by default**: it writes no `infra/` directory, and the infrastructure is synthesised from `azure.yaml` at provision time. You get infrastructure-as-code files only if you eject them, with `--infra` for Bicep or `--infra=terraform`. Nothing warns you. The project provisions, deploys and runs, and **the repository you hand over contains no reviewable declaration of what it built.** Two more were chosen for you and neither was announced: `azure.yaml` carries two substitution syntaxes, `${VAR_NAME}` resolved on your machine from `.azure/<env>/.env` and `${{ ... }}` resolved by the service at runtime, and only one of those two travels with the repository; and each deploy creates a new agent version, previous versions are preserved, and **the latest is active by default**, so a receiving team inherits a version history and an implicit rollback story nobody wrote down. **An engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system**, and this generalises far beyond this vendor.

7. **The illusion of transfer is a recency effect and the programme has taught it already.** A receiving team that has just been walked through a component can do everything; the same team at four weeks cannot. Immediate performance after instruction is the fluency signal [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) named and phase 8 has confronted every week for three years. **A handover validated at the moment it is delivered has been validated at the one moment when the measurement is worthless.** The programme's own design has been telling the cohort this since week one, applied to themselves; it applies identically to the people they are handing to.

8. **What you cannot transfer.** Judgement about which of the system's anomalies matter. It is the thing every departing engineer holds and nobody has ever written down, and the honest position is that it does not transfer as knowledge — it transfers, partially and slowly, as exposure. **What you can transfer is the list of anomalies you know about and your position on each, which is not the judgement but is what a receiver needs to build it. Safe as a handover element wherever the receiving team will operate the system long enough to accumulate their own; unsafe as preparation for a receiver who will operate it briefly and then hand it on again, where the list is all they will ever have and its omissions are permanent.**

9. **The handover's own failure modes, from phase 3.** Facts without connections (H1). Connections without a carrier (H2). Confident scope that does not announce its edge (H3). And the one none of the four exhibits: **transfer to a receiver who cannot act on it** — the right artefact given to a team without the authority, the access or the time. **The list omits the case where the receiving team is competent, empowered and does not want the system, which is [S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md)'s material and is the commonest failure in practice.**

10. **Adoption is not transfer.** [S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md)'s finding, applied. A receiving team that can operate, diagnose and change the system and has decided not to own it has received a complete handover and the handover has failed. The remedy is not a better document, and pretending otherwise is how engineers spend six weeks improving an artefact that was never the obstacle. **A successful provision does not touch this**, which is the boundary phase 3's middle question already established and which the specification enforces by not assessing whether anything ran.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to the phase 3 table, with the room's own commitments shown against the outcome records. Points 1, 3, 8 and 9 carry the scope statements required by the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), three of them in strong form. Point 5 is the day's perishable platform-specific content and point 6 is its named default, both required by the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md); point 5 also carries the day's first documentation silence, which is that the registry counts a state and reports nothing about the transition into it. Point 7 is the day's recursion onto the programme's own design and is stated as such: the cohort has been the subject of this effect for three years and is now on the other side of it. Point 4 is the day's retained heuristic and is what [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) builds the warranty on.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 6. The student decides what to hand over and to whom, and the measurement is taken on somebody else.**

Each student takes **the substrate component they will hand over**, chosen today and not chosen again for the remainder of the module.

1. **Name the receiving party** — a specific team or named individuals from the next cohort or the operating staff — and state, for each of the three capabilities, what they must be able to do and by when.
2. **Write the retention prediction.** For each of the three capabilities, predict what this receiver will be able to do at four weeks, specifically enough to be wrong. Sealed.
3. **Build the transfer inventory.** Three required parts.
   - **(a)** **Enumerate what you know that is not written down anywhere**, and classify each item by the medium that could carry it: document, pairing, test, name, or none. The [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) item 8 handover recommendations for this component are inputs here, not separate items.
   - **(b)** **One moved-work item**, per [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md) step 5: name a piece of work that moved between people or teams because of this component, and where it moved to — **or state explicitly that the evidence you named at [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md) was sought and not found.** Either answer satisfies the element; silence does not.
   - **(c)** **For each item you intend to transfer, the substrate's weekly usage series to date, with your [S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md) two-week predicted point marked on it.** The series is returned by the substrate's telemetry and costs the student nothing to obtain; the predictions were made two weeks ago and the measurement exists. **The series rather than the single point is the element, per [WP-084](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-084.md) §8**, because a rate that rose for a fortnight and a rate that rose and is already falling are the same two-week number and are different findings. Where an item was not in the [S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md) set, say so.
4. **Build and ship one transfer artefact,** in whichever medium point 4 argues for, using Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server in read configuration. **At least one item from step 3(a) must move into the system rather than into a description of it** — a rename, a test whose assertion message states the constraint, a log line that says why, a parameter lifted out of somebody's head and into the template with a comment saying what breaks if it changes. **It is merged, not drafted.** The substrate's repository is where it goes and the substrate's own checks are what it has to pass.
5. **Run the reconstruction test.** A receiver who has not met you and has not seen your component is given your artefact and the repository and nothing else, for twelve minutes, and must then:
   - **state what this component is for, what would make you call them at three in the morning, and one change they would be afraid to make**; and
   - **attempt one operation** you nominate in advance — a provision into a disposable resource group, a what-if against the current one, or the running of your test suite — and say what they think happened.
   **You say nothing until they have finished.** Record both the answers and the outcome of the attempt. **Whether the attempt succeeded is not assessed**; what is recorded is what they did, what they expected, and what they could not find.
6. **Record the gap** between step 2's prediction and step 5's reconstruction, item by item, and the item 6 pretest number against the count of things the receiver got wrong.
7. **State what you have decided not to transfer**, with the cost of each decision and who bears it. Per [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 8, an entry with no owner is an unmade decision, not an accepted one. **The workaround counts** — [S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md)'s finding that adoption produces a parallel practice the design did not intend applies here, and a workaround the receiving team will inherit is a transfer item whether or not you approve of it.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned. Not a transcript. **The instruction you gave it about what the receiving team already knows is the one the assessors will ask about**, because it is the only place in the record where the author's model of the receiver becomes visible.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a test whose assertion message states the constraint is the obvious example here and is accepted, because it is the detection the day is arguing for.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch. Both specific, both from this build.

**Receivers are drawn from the next cohort and from the operating staff rota, and each student receives one who has not worked on their component.** Twelve minutes each, run in two waves.

**Fading:** step 3(a)'s medium classification is worked with the room on one volunteered example; step 5's three reconstruction questions and the nominate-an-operation form are supplied verbatim; step 7 reuses [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s owner-or-explicit-none form. **Steps 2 and 4 are unsupported** — the prediction and the choice of medium are the day's whole judgement.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 6 with the measurement taken on a third party rather than self-reported, which is instrumentation rule 1 applied at the point where the day's central claim lives: a receiver capable of supplying the observation is in the room, and the design does not ask the student to estimate what the receiver understood. Step 2's sealed prediction against step 5's observed reconstruction is the day's instrument and is the only measurement in the programme of a student's calibration about their own artefact's legibility to a stranger. Step 4's requirement that at least one item move into the system is point 4 made into an obligation rather than a recommendation, because a heuristic that is merely endorsed is not exercised, and **the requirement that it be merged rather than drafted is what makes the medium argument checkable**: a rename that is not in the repository has not been made. **Step 5's operation attempt is the day's only mechanical measurement and is deliberately excluded from assessment**, because a specification that rewarded a successful provision would reward the one capability of three that a template can carry. **Step 3 parts (b) and (c) are the artefact elements [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md) and [S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md) named under instrumentation rule 6**, and they are placed inside the inventory rather than appended to it because both concern what has already moved — work in (b), usage in (c) — and a transfer inventory that counts only what the author intends to hand over has missed the transfers that happened without them.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: **a substrate handover this cohort received.** A component they inherited in year two, with the departing party's handover artefact read out as written, and the incident from month three in which the receiving students — the people in the room — did the thing the artefact had not anticipated. Attention is directed at one fact: **the artefact was not wrong.** Everything in it was true, and the receiving team read it, and the failure happened in the space between two true sentences that nobody had connected.

Then the agent case: **a Copilot-generated provisioning procedure that was confidently wrong and passed review.** The student asked the agent to write the receiving team's setup steps from the repository. It produced a clean, ordered, correct procedure: every command was right, every flag was right, and it named two configuration values as `${VAR_NAME}` substitutions exactly as `azure.yaml` does. **Those substitutions are resolved on the author's machine, from `.azure/<env>/.env`, which the repository does not carry.** The reviewer checked the commands, the commands were correct, and the procedure was unrunnable by anybody except its author. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: the reviewer had the file, so the values resolved when they read it, and **the artefact was legible to every reader who did not need it.**

Then students, in threes: **something you learned about your component by breaking it, that you have never written down.**

The firewall applies and is stated: nothing surfaced here reaches any assessment judgement about the student who surfaced it.

**Warrant.** Error management on the day's specific risk, which is not producing a bad artefact but producing a true one that does not carry. The instructor case is chosen because the cohort is the receiving team in it — they hold the receiver's experience of an artefact that satisfied every documentary standard — and because there is no villain, which is the condition the clinic has required since [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md). **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen because it is the day's whole thesis in one artefact**: the failure is invisible to the author, invisible to a reviewer who holds the context, and immediately visible to the receiver — which is the argument for measuring on the receiver, arriving as an error rather than as a claim. The student prompt targets step 3 directly and is timed after step 3 so that the omissions surfaced here are ones the artefact has already been built without.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Triads, with a fixed rotation so that every participant produces.

Round one: **A** hands **B** their step 4 artefact and says nothing. **B** states one change they would make to the component and what they expect to break. **C** records where B's expectation and A's private knowledge diverge, without saying which.

Round two: rotate. Round three: rotate again.

The triad then reconciles: **for each of the three divergences, was it a gap in the artefact or a gap in the receiver's context?** The distinction must be argued, not agreed, and where the triad cannot separate the two they record that as the outcome.

Per specification rule 3, the divergence record and the triad's classification are elements of the artefact, not merely activities that occurred.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation in which every member holds a different role in each round, which is what prevents the degradation into one explainer and two listeners. The artefact-gap versus context-gap distinction is the phase's product and it is the one judgement the reconstruction test at step 5 cannot make, because a single receiver cannot tell you whether the second receiver would have failed the same way. The refusal to accept "we agreed it was both" is the reconciliation discipline the pattern requires.

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- **Item 4's fractions returned** — what proportion of what you know is written down — against step 6's measured reconstruction gap, as a distribution without names. The design's expectation is that the two are unrelated, and the room is shown whether they were.
- **Item 6's predicted error count against the observed count**, per student, privately, and as a cohort distribution.
- **Item 7 opened.** The sealed Copilot predictions are read against what the agent actually produced at step 4, and the cohort's hit rate is recorded. **Both halves count**: predicting that the agent would state something confidently and wrongly is easy, and naming what it would be is the measure. ## Assessment

**One specification item**, in PE390 with CAP390. Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the named receiving party with a stated requirement for each of the three capabilities; the sealed four-week retention prediction; the transfer inventory's three parts — the not-written-down enumeration with a medium classified for each item, the [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md) moved-work item or the explicit statement that the named evidence was sought and not found, and the substrate's weekly usage series with the [S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md) two-week predicted point marked for each intended transfer item; the transfer artefact **as merged**, including at least one item moved into the system rather than into a description of it; the reconstruction test's three answers **and the record of the nominated operation attempt** as recorded by the receiver; the measured prediction-to-reconstruction gap item by item; the not-transferred list with an owner or an explicit none for each entry and the cost of each; the phase 7 divergence record with the triad's artefact-gap or context-gap classification; **the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

**The artefact's length, format and completeness are not assessed**, and the specification says so in writing, because a specification that rewards coverage would reproduce H1 across the entire cohort. **Whether the nominated operation succeeded is not assessed either**, and for the same class of reason: a provision result measures one of the three capabilities and is silent about the other two, which is the boundary phase 3's middle question establishes and phase 4 point 10 states.

**Four hard requirements.** A transfer artefact with **no item moved into the system** fails. A transfer artefact **that was not merged** fails, and a draft is not a transfer. A reconstruction test **conducted by a receiver who has worked on the component** fails, and is re-run. A transfer inventory **missing part (b) in both of its permitted forms** fails — the [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md) element is satisfied by a moved-work item or by an explicit not-found statement, and by nothing else. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**The reconstruction gap is not assessed for size.** A large gap is a finding about the artefact and is what the day exists to produce; assessing it would make step 2's prediction strategic, and a student who predicts a receiver will retain nothing cannot be wrong. This is [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §7's argument applied to a within-day instrument, and the specification carries it in writing.

**Error-clinic firewall.** Phase 6 is unobserved by anyone contributing to a specification judgement, and nothing from it is recorded.

**Rule-6 discharge.** Two obligations land on this day and **both elements exist.**

- **[S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md)** named this day as the response occasion for its step 5 moved-work do-not-know rate, specifying that the transfer inventory carry one moved-work item per student **or an explicit statement that the evidence named there was sought and not found.** **That is phase 5 step 3(b), it is a checked element, and it is one of the day's four hard requirements.** Both permitted answers satisfy it; the failure condition is silence, which is the state [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md)'s instrument was measuring.
- **[S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md)** named this day as the response occasion for the two-week leg of its adoption predictions, specifying that the transfer inventory state, for each transferred item, the measured usage at two weeks against the student's prediction. **That is phase 5 step 3(c) and it is a checked element.** The six-week leg is [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md)'s.

**Programme-level obligation discharged.** The [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) states that what the programme owes students in place of currency is the ability to re-acquire the perishable layer quickly, and names S011, S015 and this day as where that is taught explicitly. **That is phase 4 point 4 and phase 5 step 4**: the operative technique is to move knowledge out of descriptions, which decay silently, and into the system, which is checked continuously — which is the same argument the register makes about perishable content, applied to a codebase rather than to a curriculum. It is a checked element and two of the day's four hard requirements. **Phase 4 point 6 supplies the register's own counter-example on the day**: an infrastructure declaration that is synthesised rather than written is knowledge that was never moved into the system at all, and a receiving team cannot re-acquire what the repository does not contain.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions:

- **The prediction-to-reconstruction gap and its direction** — whether students over- or under-estimate what a stranger retains from their artefact. **Response occasion: [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md) phase 5, whose warranty must state the conditions under which the guarantee holds, and where students whose gap ran in the over-confident direction are required to write the receiver-obligation clause first, before the guarantee clause.** The cohort's direction is reported to the room at S089 phase 4.
- **The step 3 medium classification, in particular the count of items classified "none"** — knowledge the student believes no medium can carry. **Response occasion: [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) phase 3, where the handover is performed for real and the receiving party is asked, after it, which of the author's "none" items they now hold.** The cohort total is reported at S090 phase 8.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **Transfer is a property of the receiver, not of the sender.** A handover succeeded if a team that never met you can operate the system, diagnose it and change it without breaking something they could not have known about. Prefer the medium that fails loudly: move knowledge into the system, which is checked continuously, rather than into a description of it.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** Transfer as a property of the receiver, and the limits of documentation as a medium, are grounded in [research/05-fde-craft/collected-materials.md](/research/05-fde-craft/collected-materials.md), particularly [research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/evans-domain-driven-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/evans-domain-driven-design.md). Commit-before-reveal staging, silent reconstruction as a comprehension test and the fluency-versus-retention argument are cited for **direction and mechanism only, and no claim is made about the magnitude of the sender–receiver estimation gap.**

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Move at least one thing into the system and merge it** — a rename, a test whose assertion message states the constraint, a parameter out of somebody's head. A draft transfers nothing.
- **Validate on the receiver, at a delay.** A handover checked as it is delivered was checked when the measurement is worthless.
- **Hand over the anomaly list and your position on each.** That is what a receiver needs to build the judgement you cannot transfer.
- **Pin the revision, host and environment your artefact was produced against.** Per the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md), a value that resolves only on your machine has not been transferred.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Reading a successful provision as a successful handover. It measures one capability of three, and the artefact that would not provision belonged to the better-off team.
