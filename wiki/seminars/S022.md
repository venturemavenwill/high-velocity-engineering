# S022 · Amortisation, Randomisation and Expected Cost

**Module:** [M05](wiki/modules/M05.md) · **Quarter:** [Q3](wiki/quarters/Q3.md) · **Complexity class:** 2
**Strands:** CF · MS
**Whitepaper:** [WP-022](wiki/whitepapers/WP-022.md)
**Depends on:** S013
**Re-tests:** S019, S021
**Platform anchor:** Azure AI Search vector filter modes · Foundry `document_retrieval` · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

S013 established that worst-case and amortised claims are different claims for different callers. This day adds two more forms — expected cost under randomisation, and high-probability bounds — and then asks the question S013 could not: **which guarantee are you actually able to offer, and what does the caller do when it does not hold?**

The day also supplies the tool S013 deferred: the **potential function**, which turns an amortised claim from an observation about a measured sequence into an argument that holds for every sequence.

The professional target: a guarantee that holds *in expectation* is a promise about a distribution, and a caller who receives one instance is not receiving the expectation.

**Today the taxonomy is not a teaching device.** Yesterday's index accepts a filter alongside a vector query, and the service offers three ways to apply it. One of them carries a written guarantee: apply the filter during the graph traversal, and you are guaranteed the requested number of results *if they exist in the index*. The others do not, and the documentation says so plainly — one can produce false negatives, and one can return nothing at all. **Three guarantee forms, one parameter, one line of configuration, and a real caller downstream who does not know which one they are getting.** By the end of the day the room has read a vendor's guarantee sentence the way this module reads guarantee sentences, and has found the promise it does not make.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items.

1. A randomised algorithm has expected linear running time. You run it once. What have you been promised?
2. Ten thousand callers each run it once. What has the population been promised?
3. Which of these two situations describes an interactive service, and which a batch pipeline?
4. Predict: will your item 1 and item 2 answers differ?
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to *make this filtered vector query faster.* Write down what it will change, and **name the guarantee it will silently give up.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 4's answer should be yes and most students initially say no. Item 5's expectation is that most students predict the agent will change something about speed and few will name the guarantee, because they do not yet know one exists.

**Warrant.** Pretesting on the individual-versus-population distinction, which is the day's core and is also the foundation of every evaluation claim from S041 onward. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups have the module's index and one filtered query set: the same vector query, run against a filter that selects a narrow slice of the corpus. Four configurations, differing only in how and when the filter is applied.

| Case | Configuration | Guarantee form |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Exhaustive scan with the filter | Worst case, exact, and the poorest latency bound in the room |
| **B** | Graph traversal, filter applied **during** traversal | A written guarantee: the requested number of results is returned **if they exist in the index** |
| **C** | Graph traversal, filter applied **after**, per shard | No guarantee. Documented as able to produce false negatives, and able to return fewer than requested |
| **D** | Graph traversal, filter applied **after the global result set is chosen** | Documented as the lowest recall and the highest false-negative risk, and able to return zero |

Groups receive **four callers** — the S013 set, extended — and must match, predicting for each configuration how many results come back and how many of them case A also returned. They commit.

Then they run all four across the query set, with the filter's selectivity varied from broad to narrow, and record the result counts and the overlap with case A.

**Case D is preview and is demonstrated rather than operated.** The instructor runs it once, on the projector, on the preview API version. It is not in the specification and it is not assessed. It is present because the taxonomy has a fourth position and the service has a fourth mode, and omitting it would teach a tidier world than the one that exists.

Task after: **for the caller who is served by none of the four, state what guarantee they would need and whether it exists.**

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across guarantee forms with a measured adjudicator, now run against a metered service rather than supplied as distributions. Case A is the adjudicator and is available on the same field, which is what makes the overlap measurable rather than asserted. The unserved caller is the phase's payload and is sharper than the constructed version it replaces: **the caller who needs to know which documents were missed, or how often, is served by nothing here — the documentation grades the false-negative risk as moderate and highest and publishes no rate anywhere.** A group that reaches "the guarantee they need is not on offer and nobody has measured it" has reached S024 a day early, which is the intent.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **The guarantee taxonomy, completed.** Worst case, amortised, expected, high probability — and what each quantifies over. **Phase 3 did not instantiate this list and the day says so.** The four configurations you ran vary a guarantee's *strength* — exact, conditional, absent, worse than absent — and not its *form*. Amortised and high-probability guarantees have no instance on today's service, you will meet both in documentation and in library specifications, and the fact that a service happens not to offer a form is not evidence that the form is rare.
2. **The potential function,** worked completely, on the table-doubling example from S013. This turns the amortised claim into a proof.
3. **Randomisation** as a way of removing an adversary's advantage rather than as a way of being fast on average.
4. **Expectation is not a promise to an individual.** Phase 1's items 1 and 2, resolved. The population receives the expectation; the caller receives a draw. **Every relevance claim you will meet this quarter is of this shape.** An approximate search described as high-recall is making a claim about a distribution of queries; the user in front of you is issuing one.
5. **High-probability bounds and what the residual probability means** operationally — how often, over what horizon, and what happens then.
6. **Which caller needs which,** indexed to phase 3.
7. **The guarantee you cannot give,** from phase 3's unserved caller. Naming it is the professional act, and S024 is about doing so.
8. **Reading a vendor's guarantee sentence the way this module reads guarantee sentences.** Case B's promise is that the requested number of results is returned if they exist in the index. Read it again. **It guarantees a count and says nothing about which documents those are.** Case A returns the correct set; case B returns a full set. Both sentences are true, one is a quality claim and the other is not, and they are one word apart in ordinary reading. This is the day's most transferable five minutes and it costs nothing but attention.
9. **Defaults are opinions and thresholds are policy.** Three were chosen for you. The filter mode defaults to the slowest of the three, and **the vendor states its own reason in its own documentation** — prefiltering is almost always slower and is the default anyway because the bias favours recall and precision over speed. That is a value judgement, written down, shipped as a default, and almost never read. Second: **which default you get depends on when your index was created.** Indexes created before approximately October 2023 default to the postfiltering mode instead, so two indexes in one service can answer the same query under different guarantees with no visible difference in the request. Third: the reranking score you will meet at S025 has a distribution that **varies with infrastructure conditions and moves when the ranking model is updated**, and the documentation's own advice is not to set granular thresholds against it. A threshold on a quantity whose distribution moves is a policy nobody has reviewed.
10. **What is perishable here and what is not.** The mode names, the defaults, the date of the default change and every measured overlap are perishable. **The durable claim is that a guarantee has a form, that the form determines who is served, and that a count guarantee is not a quality guarantee.** The correspondence is safe for reasoning about what a filtered retrieval promises and unsafe for reasoning about *how often* the weaker modes fail — no rate is published, none may be inferred from the day's own measurements, and a student who converts today's observed overlap into a general failure rate has done exactly what S028 will teach them not to do.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Point 2 discharges S013's deferral. Point 4 is the day's central distinction and is the direct foundation of S041 and S049. Point 8 is the day's principal gain from the platform anchor and is the point the design would keep if it had to keep one; point 9 satisfies the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s default-naming requirement, and point 10 carries the strong-form scope statement.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 2. The guarantee form is not given. This phase produces a build.**

Students use Copilot in agent mode, through the Azure MCP Server in read configuration and the Learn MCP Server for grounding, to ship a **filtered retrieval path for one real substrate caller**, and:

1. **State the strongest guarantee you can actually prove.** Not the one you would like.
2. **Prove it** — potential function if amortised, expectation argument if randomised, direct if worst case. Where the guarantee is the platform's rather than yours, **state whose claim it is and what it is conditional on**, and do not present a vendor sentence as a proof.
3. **Measure the distribution** over a thousand queries drawn from the substrate's real query log, with the exhaustive configuration as the adjudicator.
4. **State the gap** between the proved guarantee and the observed distribution, in both directions.
5. **Identify the caller** this guarantee serves and one it does not.
6. **Write the guarantee as a sentence** the unserved caller could act on — including what they should do instead.
7. **State the failure rate** they would experience, with the horizon over which it is expressed, and **state what your thousand queries do and do not license you to say about that rate.**

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned.
- **Intervention record.** Every correction, and what it was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that your own design would have caught an intervention-worthy error — a result-count assertion against the exhaustive adjudicator is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch.

**Fading:** step 2's potential-function template is supplied; step 1 is worked with the room.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 2, now shipping a path rather than characterising an algorithm. Step 1's *strongest you can prove* is the day's honesty demand. Step 2's added clause is new with the platform anchor and closes the day's most likely dishonesty, which is quoting a vendor's guarantee as though the student had established it. Step 7's horizon requirement is the difference between a probability and an operational fact, and its second clause is the sampling disclosure instrumentation rule 3 requires of the day and now requires of the student.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate component whose expected-case guarantee was quoted to a caller who needed a worst case, and the incident that followed — with attention to whether anyone at the time knew the difference.

Then the agent case: a Copilot change that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. Asked to reduce the latency of a filtered query, the agent switched the filter mode from the guaranteeing one to the faster one, cited the documented performance difference accurately in its explanation, and left the tests passing. The change was correct, well-justified and merged. **The test suite's filters each matched a large fraction of the corpus, which is precisely the regime in which the two modes behave alike.** Production's filters were narrow. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: the reviewer checked the justification, the justification was true, and the fact that made it inapplicable was a property of the test data rather than of the code.

Then students: a probability you quoted without a horizon, or a place today where you accepted a performance justification without checking the regime it holds in.

**Warrant.** Error management. The instructor disclosure targets the communication failure rather than the analytical one, because the analysis is usually right and the sentence is usually wrong. The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen because the agent's reasoning was accurate and its conclusion was not — the failure is in the unstated condition, which is the day's subject exactly.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student reads out **step 6's sentence**, and the partner — playing the unserved caller — must say what they would do on hearing it.

If the answer is "nothing different," the sentence has failed.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with an actionability test. A guarantee statement that does not change the recipient's behaviour has communicated nothing, and this is the cheapest available test of that.

## Assessment

Specification item in CF103 and MS103 jointly. Checked elements: the strongest provable guarantee with its proof and its attribution; the measured distribution with its adjudicator; the two-directional gap; served and unserved callers; the actionable sentence; the failure rate with its horizon and its sampling statement; the prompt record; the intervention record; the frontier statement.

**A probability stated without a horizon fails the specification.** **A vendor's guarantee presented as the student's proof fails the specification.** **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

Nothing else is assessed. **The preview mode demonstrated at phase 3 is not assessed.**

## Perishable content in this day

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| A guarantee has a form and the form decides who is served | Three filter modes with three different promises |
| A count guarantee is not a quality guarantee | "The requested number of results if they exist in the index" |
| The population receives the expectation; the caller receives a draw | A high-recall configuration answering one user's one query |
| The guarantee a caller needs may not exist | No published false-negative rate for the non-guaranteeing modes |
| A threshold is a policy someone must own | A reranker score whose distribution moves with the infrastructure |

The mode names, the defaults, the index-creation date at which the default changed, the preview boundary and every measured overlap are **perishable** and are marked at point 10 explicitly. The guarantee taxonomy, the potential function, randomisation's purpose, the individual-versus-population distinction, the count-versus-quality distinction and the horizon requirement are durable and are what the specification assesses.
