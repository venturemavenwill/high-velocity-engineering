# S022 · Making a Requirement Falsifiable

**Module:** [M05](/wiki/modules/M05.md) · **Quarter:** [Q3](/wiki/quarters/Q3.md) · **Complexity class:** 2
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** GitHub Copilot agent mode
**Whitepaper:** [WP-022](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-022.md)
**Retrieves from:** S016, S020, S021
**Entry state:** either

---

## The situation

You have a requirement set from [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md). It is written in the customer's language, it names no technology, and everybody who has read it has agreed with it.

**That last fact is the problem.** Universal agreement is what a set of unfalsifiable sentences produces.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught. Item 5 is submitted and kept.

1. Take your first requirement. Write the sentence that would show it had **not** been met.
2. Who would run that check, on what, and when?
3. If nobody can run it, is it a requirement or a hope?
4. What would you accept as evidence that it had been met — and would the customer accept the same evidence?
5. Name a requirement in your set that you cannot verify at all. **Do not delete it.**

**Item 1 is the session.** [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) established that a question inviting a flattering answer will receive one. This is the same claim about a sentence: a requirement that cannot be failed will not be.

## What you must produce

Every requirement in your set, rewritten into the capture form the open engineering playbook publishes — a name, a description, a priority, a measurement, a verification method, constraints, an owner, and dependencies. Page read 2026-07-30.

**Only two of those fields are assessed here, and the other six are administrative.** The measurement, and the verification method. Everything this session is for lives in those two.

Plus one more artefact, which is the session's second point: **the unverifiable list.** Requirements you could not put a measurement or a verification method against are moved there, kept, and carried forward. They are not deleted.

## What good looks like

- **The measurement names a quantity and an instrument that exists.** Not a quality. "Timely" is a quality. A named latency, observed at a named point, by something that is already running or that somebody has agreed to build, is a measurement.
- **The verification method names who runs the check, on what data, and when.** A verification method that is a meeting is a decision, not a verification.
- **A stranger could use the two fields to fail the requirement.** This is the test, and it is mechanical: hand the two fields to somebody who was not in the room and ask them to produce a system that satisfies the words and would be rejected. If they can, you are not finished.
- **Every threshold has a stated origin**, and *borrowed from an example bank that carries no source* is an acceptable origin when it is written down as one. [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md) is where that admission gets paid for.
- **The unverifiable list is present and is not empty**, or its emptiness is defended. A set in which everything is verifiable on the first pass has usually had its hard requirements quietly rewritten into easy ones.

## How it goes wrong

- **The agreeable requirement** — nobody objects, because there is nothing to object to. *Warning sign:* the review meeting was short and pleasant.
- **The borrowed threshold** — a number lifted from a catalogue of example values that carries no citation anywhere. It then becomes the number everybody defends. *Warning sign:* you cannot say where the figure came from, and neither can the person who signed it off.
- **The verification that is a meeting** — the method reads *reviewed and approved by the steering group*. That establishes that somebody approved it. *Warning sign:* your verification method contains a job title and no instrument.
- **The instrument that does not exist** — the measurement is fine and nothing can currently produce it. This is legitimate, but only if the instrument becomes work with an owner. *Warning sign:* the measurement is precise and nobody has asked who builds the thing that measures it.
- **The deleted unverifiable requirement** — you could not verify it, so you dropped it. It was a real constraint, and it is now invisible rather than absent. *Warning sign:* your set got shorter during this session and nothing was added anywhere else.

## Do it

Fill the two fields for every requirement. Expect to be unable to.

Then use the agent as an adversary, once per requirement, with one instruction: **satisfy this requirement in a way I would reject.**

This is the cheapest falsification test available and it works because the instrument is literal. It will not infer the constraint you left unstated, it will not fill the gap out of professional courtesy, and it will not tell you the requirement was clear when it was not. If it produces something that satisfies your words and appals you, the appalling part is the requirement you failed to write. **Write it, and run the test again.**

Record every prompt, what the agent produced, and what you changed as a result. **An agent that could not satisfy your requirement badly was given the requirement plus your intentions**, which is not the test.

## Show it

Swap sets with one other person. Their only job is to satisfy your requirements badly, by hand, without using an instrument.

Then compare: the requirements a person broke and the agent did not, and the ones the agent broke and the person did not. **The second list is the more interesting one.** It contains the places where your requirement relied on a reader who wanted it to work.

## What this does not establish

Nothing here shows that requirements written in this form produce better systems. The playbook that supplies the form offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices and does not claim to.

**Two limits of that source are carried forward rather than assumed away.** Its pages were written over several years and it has no single currency, so this session cites one page and the date it was read, never the document as a whole. And the same page that supplies the form also supplies a large catalogue of example thresholds **which carries no citation of any kind** — those numbers are illustrations and this session forbids their use as benchmarks. [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md) makes that prohibition into an exercise.

**The decision to mark only two of the eight fields is this design's, not the source's**, and it has not been tested. It is made because the other six can be filled correctly by somebody who has understood nothing, and these two cannot.

## Perishable content in this session

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| A requirement that cannot be failed will not be | The agent's willingness to satisfy a specification adversarially, in the version installed this term |
