## The situation

This is an assessment occasion.

**The test is not whether your specification is good.** It is whether somebody who was not in the room, and who cannot ask you anything, can build from it and produce something you would accept.

Answer these in writing before you begin, and submit them.

1. Which requirement in your set are you least willing to have misread?
2. What in this specification is not written down and exists only in your head?
3. Name the reader you are writing for. What do they already know?
4. What in this specification would you concede to the customer, and what would you refuse?
5. What did you leave out on purpose?

**Item 2 is the assessment's real question.** Everyone has an answer to it and almost nobody writes the answer into the specification, because the thing in your head feels obvious. It is obvious to you because you were there.

## What you must produce

A **specification packet**, assembled from the module and handed to two readers who have nothing else.

- **The requirement set** from [S021](/wiki/seminars/S021.md), stated as business outcomes in the customer's sharpened vocabulary.
- **The capture form for each**, with the measurement and the verification method filled.
- **The non-functional set** from [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md), with every threshold's origin and an owner named inside the customer's organisation.
- **The decision records and the log** from [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md), including the superseded one.
- **The unverifiable list**, intact.
- **The glossary**, including the words you and the customer meant differently and which meaning was adopted.
- **The build record** — what a stranger produced from the packet alone, what they got wrong, and what you changed as a result.
- **The build-path statement** — which construction path the specification assumes, one consequence of that path that you accept, and the person or role who agreed to accept it. If the path is deliberately left open, say so and say what that costs.

## The material

### A specification is a claim about a reader, and the claim is testable in exactly one way

A specification is written by somebody who knows what it means. That is the defect, it is unavoidable, and no amount of care removes it.

**The author's knowledge is not in the document and cannot be inspected from inside it.** Reading your own specification, you supply the missing constraint automatically, exactly as you supply the missing word in a familiar sentence. Reading it with a colleague, they supply it too, because they were in the same meetings. The gap is invisible to everybody who could conveniently be asked.

The conventional remedy is review, and review does not work for this. **A reviewer's job is to find fault with what is written, and the defect here is what is not.** A reviewer who cannot see the absence has no procedure for detecting it, and a reviewer who can see it usually knows the answer and mentally inserts it before noticing they have done so.

So the assessment replaces review with use. Not a discussion of the specification — **an attempt to act on it, by somebody with no access to the author**, and a record of what they did.

### Anchoring is why the readers are given nothing

There is a warrant for the *give them nothing* rule and it is not stylistic.

Studies of assessors judging complex written evidence, thinking aloud as they worked, reconstruct a cyclical process of acquiring, organising and integrating evidence, followed by a return to the remaining material seeking confirmation or disconfirmation. The finding that bears on today: **"assessors were inclined to stick to their initial judgments even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence"**, and their final judgements were more elaborate than their preliminary ones but not substantially different ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)).

**One sentence of context from you fixes your reader's interpretation before they have read the specification**, and everything they read afterwards will elaborate that interpretation rather than test the document. A single clarifying answer therefore does not merely help them; it destroys the instrument. That is why a question is recorded and not answered.

The same literature explains why the same packet goes to two readers rather than one: assessors reaching the same conclusion frequently reach it by different reasoning, and **the divergence is invisible in the outcome and visible only in what each of them says.** Two readers who both build something acceptable, for incompatible reasons, have told you more about your document than one reader who succeeded.

**Its limits.** Clinical-education research using think-aloud protocols on mock portfolios, in a single institution and one portfolio type; think-aloud cannot capture fast or automatic processing and risks participants confabulating explanations. **No magnitude from it is reproduced here**, and its application to specification readers is this repository's inference rather than the source's claim.

### The two readers fail differently, and both failures are wanted

**The instrument is literal.** It will not fill a gap out of courtesy, will not decide you must have meant something sensible, and will build exactly what your words permit. That property is what the assessment is buying, and it is the same property [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) used adversarially.

**The person is slower and supplies the thing the instrument cannot.** They will tell you which parts felt ambiguous rather than silently choosing an interpretation. An instrument's silent choice is a finding you have to reconstruct; a person's stated hesitation is a finding delivered.

**Neither is a proof.** A build that went well establishes that the specification was clear enough for one reader on one occasion, which is a much weaker statement than it feels like. **A build that went badly and produced a specification revision is the successful case**, and the assessment marks the revision rather than the build for exactly that reason.

### The build path is part of the specification, and it binds at the end

Here is the addition that makes this a Stage 3 assessment rather than a document exercise.

A specification that names no construction path is not neutral; it silently assumes one, and the assumption is discovered late. The published guidance for this platform makes the fork explicit and it is a hosting distinction rather than a capability one ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). **Prompt agents** use "declarative config in portal or code", are "managed by Foundry with no agent compute", and suit "agents that don't include custom code or require custom hosting". **Hosted agents** run "your code (containerized)", are "managed by Foundry (container compute)", and suit "agents with custom code running in a managed container".

**The line between them is whether you are running code, and therefore whether you are paying for and operating compute.** That is a requirements fact, not an implementation detail: it decides who owns the runtime, what the operating cost is, what can be changed without a deployment, and who is on call.

Three further published facts turn that fork into consequences a specification must accept.

**A build choice made at the start binds distribution at the end.** The documentation states that only agents built with certain toolchains can be published to the commercial store with the toolkit. **A specification that is silent on the build path has therefore made a distribution decision without noticing**, and the noticing happens when somebody asks how the thing will be shipped.

**Prototyping topology and production topology are different things.** The basic setup is described as being for rapid prototyping when speed and ease of access are the priorities, and it **"lacks network isolation"**; the standard setup provides fine-grained control over data, security and networking. The transition between them is a deliberate act, not a configuration discovered at the end.

**The options are not mutually exclusive.** The guidance says so directly, and gives the example of one runtime exposing tools that agents built elsewhere consume. **The choice is not a platform for a system; it is a platform per component, composed through protocols** — which means the build-path statement is a statement about *this* component and should say which one.

**Its limits.** First-party product documentation with a selection-guidance purpose: its authority is definitional. **It offers no adoption data, no failure-rate data and no cost comparison for any path**, so its recommendations are a reasoned framework and not validated findings. It was read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches, so page structure is reconstructed. It is highly perishable; product names and feature boundaries are actively changing. And it publishes a scale claim for only one of the tools it compares — **the absence of a stated scale envelope for the others is not evidence that they have none.**

### What can be assessed here, and what cannot

The seven checked elements below assess properties of a document and of a change. They do not assess whether the design implied by the requirements is any good, and they cannot.

That boundary follows the entrustment literature this programme's assessment architecture rests on ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). The unit of assessment is a bounded activity with a beginning and an end that a supervisor could hand over whole. *Producing a specification a stranger can build from, and revising it on what they produced* is such an activity. *Producing a good system* is not — it is an outcome, and its correctness depends on facts the assessment cannot hold constant.

Two further things from that source shape today. **A title carries no adjectives**, because an adjective smuggles a quality standard into the name of the activity; quality standards belong in the specification and its limits. And the oral component is an **entrustment-based discussion**, which "should probe the student's deep understanding of what was done, but should include 'what-if' questions". Retrospective explanation shows you understood the case you met. **Counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases you did not**, which is why the defence asks what would have to be true rather than what happened.

**Its limits.** A clinical model, from a primer by the concept's originator; it is guidance rather than evaluation and reports adoption rather than effectiveness. Medicine has patients, legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint; **a course has to construct the stakes artificially, and the construction is the hardest part of the transfer.**

## The workflow

**Hand the packet to two readers who get nothing else, record what they do, and change the document.**

**Step 1 — write the build-path statement before either reader sees the packet.** Name the path, name one consequence you accept, and name who agreed. Writing it afterwards would let the readers' behaviour decide it, which is the opposite of a specification.

```markdown
## Build path assumed by this specification

Component: claim-triage assistant (this statement covers this component only;
other components may take other paths and compose through protocols).

Path: hosted agent — our code, containerised, on managed container compute.

Why this rather than a prompt agent: requirements R03 and R07 need orchestration
we control, and R11 requires the routing rule to be testable in continuous
integration without a portal round trip.

Consequence accepted: we own and pay for agent compute, and a change to the
routing rule is a deployment rather than a configuration edit. Agreed by:
<role inside the customer's organisation>, <date>.

Topology: prototyping topology is not production topology. The rapid-setup
option is documented as lacking network isolation. The move to the controlled
setup is a scheduled act with an owner, not a late discovery.

Downstream binding noticed: publication route is constrained by this choice.
Documentation read <date>; verify before relying on it.
```

**Step 2 — give the packet, and nothing else, to two readers separately: an agent, and a person who is not on your team.** Give them the same instruction and give them nothing more. No context, no conversation, no clarification.

**Step 3 — record every question and answer none of them.** The questions are the finding. Each one marks a place where your specification required you to be present. **A single clarifying answer does not merely help the reader; it destroys the instrument**, for the anchoring reason above.

**Step 4 — record what the instrument built and where it diverged from what you intended.** Note especially the places where it chose an interpretation silently. Those are the expensive ones, because in production nobody would have asked either.

**Step 5 — record what the person found ambiguous.** This is the complementary reading and it arrives as words rather than as behaviour.

**Step 6 — attack your own specification with the agent, once.** One instruction: *build the thing this packet permits that its author would refuse to accept.* Then a second question: *which single sentence of this packet, if deleted, would change what you built?* **The answer to the second question is your specification's load-bearing sentence, and most authors guess wrong about which one it is.**

**Step 7 — change the specification, and record what changed and why.** The build record is assessed on the change, not on the build.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** That the thing in your head from opening item 2 is real, is specific, and has a location in the document. Everyone believes they have written down what matters. **A reader with no access to you converts that belief into a list of sentences you did not write**, in an afternoon, at almost no cost — and the build-path statement makes visible the one decision most specifications leave to whoever implements first.

## What good looks like

These are the assessed elements. Each is checked as present and adequate, or not.

1. **Every requirement names a measurement and a verification method, and both can be executed by somebody who is not the author.** An instrument that does not exist yet is acceptable when it is named as work with an owner.
2. **No requirement names a technology unless the technology is a constraint imposed on you**, and where it is, the constraint's owner is named.
3. **Every threshold has a stated origin**, and *borrowed from a catalogue that carries no source* is an acceptable origin when it is declared as one.
4. **The unverifiable list is present and not empty**, or its emptiness is defended in a sentence.
5. **At least one decision record is present whose context does not reveal which way the decision went**, checked by handing the section to the assessor.
6. **The glossary records at least one word the customer and the team meant differently**, says which meaning was adopted, and names who agreed.
7. **The build record is present**, and it names something the builder got wrong and something the author changed because of it.
8. **The build-path statement is present**, names the path, names one accepted consequence, and names a role inside the customer's organisation who agreed to it — or states that the path is deliberately open and says what that costs.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Not the number of requirements, not whether the design implied by them is any good, not whether the customer agreed, not the presentation of the packet, not whether the build worked, not which build path was chosen, and not whether the assessor would have written the same requirements.

## How it goes wrong

- **The complete-looking specification** — it has every section, reads well, and cannot be built from. *Warning sign:* it took less time to write than it took to discover.
- **The private constraint** — the thing in your head from item 2 is still in your head, and the builder violated it immediately. *Warning sign:* you found yourself explaining the build result rather than recording it.
- **The unfalsifiable set** — every measurement is a quality and every verification method is a review. *Warning sign:* nobody could fail any of it.
- **The undisputed glossary** — the glossary defines terms nobody ever disagreed about. *Warning sign:* it contains no date and no name.
- **The builder blamed** — the build went wrong and the record explains why the builder was inadequate. **The builder is the instrument. An instrument that produced an unwelcome reading has been used correctly.** *Warning sign:* your build record contains the word *misunderstood* and no change to the specification.
- **The reader nobody named** — item 3 was answered with *an engineer*, so the specification assumes everything and states nothing. *Warning sign:* the packet has no statement of what the reader is expected to already know.
- **The unstated build path** — the packet never names one, so the first person to implement decides it, and the decision binds the deployment topology and the distribution route. *Warning sign:* you cannot say whether your requirements need compute you own, and nobody has asked who pays for it.

## Exercises

**Elements 1 to 8 above are assessed. These exercises are not**, and they are the preparation the assessment assumes.

**1 · Predict the questions.** Before either reader sees the packet, write the three questions you expect them to ask. Seal them. Compare afterwards.

> **What you should find.** You will predict one of them. The other two will be about things so obvious to you that it did not occur to you they could be asked — which is the definition of the knowledge that is in your head rather than in the document. **The two you did not predict are worth more than the one you did**, and they are the ones to write into the specification first.

**2 · Name the load-bearing sentence.** Before running the workflow's step 6, write down which single sentence of your packet you believe is doing the most work. Then ask the instrument the same question and compare.

> **What to notice.** Authors usually nominate the sentence they found hardest to write. Readers usually nominate the one that constrained the most decisions, which is often a short, unremarkable clause about scope or ordering. **The gap between the two is a reliable measure of how well you know your own document.**

**3 · Choose the build path, then price the other one.** Write the build-path statement, then write the version of it that would have followed from the opposite choice, with its own accepted consequence.

> **Criterion.** A passing pair shows the choice was genuinely open, names a different accepted consequence in each case, and names who would have had to agree to each. **The most common failure is a pair whose two halves differ only in a product name**, which shows that the path was inherited rather than decided. Note also which fork you were actually on: the published distinction is about whether you are running code and therefore paying for and operating compute, not about capability.

**4 · Go back to your own item 2, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 2 — what in this specification is not written down and exists only in your head. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the build record and write one sentence naming the difference between what you predicted and what the readers actually exposed.

> **Criterion.** A passing comparison names at least one thing the readers found that you did not list, and says why you did not list it. "I forgot" is not a reason; "I have used that term daily for six months and it did not occur to me that it names two things in this system" is. **The point is not that your list was incomplete. It is that you could not have completed it, which is the whole argument for the build.**
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the build record is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**5 · Rehearse the defence.** Prepare two requirements: one you would concede to the customer, and one you would refuse. For each, prepare the answer to *what would have to be true for you to change that?*

> **Scoring criterion.** The concession is assessed for the same property as the refusal: whether you can say what would have to be true, and whether the person on the other side could take your answer back to people who were not in the room and justify it. **Say what interest sits behind the requirement you would refuse, rather than restating the requirement more firmly** — a stated requirement is a position, and the reason it exists is what you are actually defending.
>
> **Expect what-if questions rather than a walkthrough.** The assessment's oral component is an entrustment-based discussion, and its stated purpose is to probe understanding of cases you did not meet as well as the one you did. Retrospective explanation shows you understood what happened. **Counterfactuals are the only available evidence about what you would do next time.**

**6 · Read your own packet as a stranger, once, out loud.** Read it end to end without stopping to explain anything, aloud, to nobody.

> **What to notice.** You will hear yourself want to add a clause. Every place you wanted to add one is a place the document does not stand alone. Write them down as you go and do not add them until after the build — **the packet the readers get must be the packet you thought was finished**, because a packet improved in anticipation of the test is a packet that has already been given context.

## What this does not establish

This assessment shows that a stranger could build something from your specification and that you changed it in response. **It does not show that the resulting system would be worth having.**

**Nothing in the material behind this module establishes that better-specified requirements produce better systems.** The publication that supplies the form and the field lists offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices, states no measured compliance, and in its own worked examples does not always follow its own specifications. Pages read 2026-07-30. It also reaches for a magnitude in several places and warrants it in none; **none of those figures is reproduced anywhere in this wiki.**

**The corpus behind the wider programme reports no engagement in which a specification was found defective at build time** — because it reports no engagement that went badly. Its case studies all succeed and its only failure narrative is a synthetic composite, fictional by its own admission. **So this session's central move, deliberately generating a bad build in order to learn from it, has no precedent in the practice it is drawn from.** It is design reasoning.

**The scheme cannot distinguish a complete specification from one whose reader happened to guess well.** A build that succeeded establishes that one reader, on one occasion, filled the gaps in a way you accepted. It does not establish that the gaps are gone.

**The generative reader's behaviour is neither stable nor characterised in any published measure.** It changes between releases, hosts and models. A cohort running this assessment in one term and a cohort running it in the next are not using the same instrument, and neither can say what the instrument measures. **What it finds is real; what it fails to find establishes nothing.**

**The build-path guidance is definitional and unvalidated.** It is first-party product documentation with a selection purpose: it tells you what the paths are, not that choosing between them this way produces better outcomes. **It offers no adoption data, no failure-rate data and no cost comparison**, it was read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, and it is highly perishable — product names and feature boundaries are actively changing.

**The assessment frame is borrowed from a clinical model.** Medicine has patients, legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint. A course constructs its stakes artificially, and the construction is the weakest part of the transfer. The primer behind it reports adoption rather than effectiveness.
