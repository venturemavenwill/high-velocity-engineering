# S026 · What Cannot Move, and Who Decides

**Module:** [M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md) · **Quarter:** [Q3](/wiki/quarters/Q3.md) · **Complexity class:** 3
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-026](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-026.md)
**Retrieves from:** S006, S007, S021
**Entry state:** either

---

## The situation

You have a specification. Somebody has now handed you the constraints.

It is a list. None of the items on it has a name attached, several of them are wrong, and at least one is something you assumed in the second week and have been repeating back to people ever since.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught. This is the first session in the stage where nobody supplies the criterion.

1. List everything in this engagement you have been told cannot change.
2. For each item, name the person who could change it. **A role is acceptable. A department is not.**
3. Which items did you assume rather than hear?
4. Which single constraint, if it moved, would change the design most?
5. What does the person holding that constraint get out of holding it?

**Item 3 is usually the longest list and nobody expects it to be.** Constraints propagate by repetition: an engineer says something is not possible, a second engineer hears it as a fact, a slide records it, and within a month it is in the register with the authority of a policy and the provenance of a guess.

## What you must produce

A **constraint register**. Every element of the problem space that bears on your specification, classified on an axis running from frozen to fluid, and carrying four things for each entry:

- **The authority** — the role that could move it, or an explicit statement that no such role is known.
- **The provenance** — stated by somebody, or assumed by you.
- **The evidence** — what you actually have that it is frozen.
- **The interest** — what the holder of the constraint gets from it, insofar as you can tell.

**The axis has more than two values.** Something that is movable at a price is not frozen, and the register must name the price.

## What good looks like

- **Every frozen item names an authority, or is marked as having none.** The second condition is worse than the first and must not be allowed to look the same. A constraint nobody owns cannot be moved by anybody, cannot be defended when it becomes expensive, and cannot be checked.
- **Assumed constraints are separated from stated ones** and the separation is visible at a glance.
- **At least one item you were told was frozen has been reclassified** after you asked somebody. If nothing moved, either you asked nobody or you asked the wrong people, and the register should say which.
- **The register records the interest behind the position, not only the position.** A constraint as stated is an answer somebody gave. The need behind it is a different thing and can frequently be met another way.
- **The price is named where an item is movable at one.** "The platform team could take this dependency, at the cost of a quarter of their roadmap and a support obligation they have not agreed to" is a usable register entry. "Not possible" is not.
- **The register is written so that somebody else could use it**, because at [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) somebody will.

## How it goes wrong

- **The anonymous constraint** — the item is on the list, everybody respects it, and no one person is behind it. *Warning sign:* when you ask who decided, three people each name a fourth.
- **The inherited freeze** — it is frozen because it always has been. The original reason has expired and nobody has noticed because nobody has asked. *Warning sign:* the constraint predates everyone currently on the engagement.
- **The position taken for a constraint** — what you were given is an answer, not a need. *Warning sign:* the constraint is stated as a solution — a named product, a specific arrangement — rather than as a property that must hold.
- **The constraint you invented** — you assumed something, said it out loud, and it entered the register with your own authority behind it. *Warning sign:* you cannot remember who told you, and you are confident anyway.
- **The all-frozen register** — everything is fixed, so nothing is attempted. This is the failure the module exists against and [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md) is where it is confronted. *Warning sign:* your register has no entry marked movable at a price.
- **The register that is a complaint** — every entry is written as an obstacle placed in your way rather than as somebody's reasonable decision. *Warning sign:* nobody from the customer's side could be shown this document.

## Do it

Build the register. Then take the three constraints that matter most and do the work that only this session does.

**For each, find out why the constraint exists and why the obvious alternative was not taken.** The second question is the informative one, and it requires you to reconstruct the choice as the person holding the constraint sees it, including consequences for people who are not in the room and who they will have to answer to. **A constraint that looks arbitrary from outside is almost never arbitrary from inside.**

Then, for the single constraint from item 4, write two things down before you have the conversation:

- **What you will do if it does not move.** Not a threat and not a fallback you have not thought about — the actual course of action available to you without agreement. Knowing it is what stops you accepting a worse arrangement than the one you already have, and not knowing it is what makes people accept less than their alternative.
- **The threshold at which you stop pressing and re-plan.** Set it now, while you are calm, and set it above the point at which you would be forced to stop. It exists to make you pause and reconsider rather than to bind you, and its whole function is to prevent drift into a commitment nobody chose.

**Both are named devices from the negotiation literature and both are carried here with that source's own limit attached: it establishes no effect, and its cases illustrate a mechanism rather than isolate a cause.**

## Show it

Read the register to one other person whose only instruction is to ask, for each frozen item, **who says?**

Items with no answer move to a list headed *assumed*. In most first registers that list is longer than the author expects, and its length is the session's result.

## What this does not establish

**The frozen-to-fluid classification is entirely unevidenced.** It appears in the practice material behind this module as a method and is never used there. There is no worked instance of a problem space classified, no engagement in which the classification changed a decision, no account of what it costs to produce, and no record of it being wrong. **That material contains no reference, footnote, study or citation of any kind anywhere in it**, so there is nothing behind the classification except the assertion that it is useful.

It is taught here because it names a failure mode nothing else in this programme names, and because it is cheap to test — an afternoon's work produces a register you can check. **That is a reason to try a method. It is not a reason to believe it.**

The negotiation devices in the *Do it* movement come from a case-based practitioner framework whose authors state plainly that it is not a controlled evaluation and that its examples establish possibility and mechanism rather than effect. **No claim is made here about how often a constraint conversation succeeds**, because the source licenses none.
