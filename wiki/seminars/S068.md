## The situation

You have been handed a model. It has a name, a structure, a set of cells to fill in and, alongside it, a workbook that runs a simulation. Whatever comes out of it will be quoted with the framework's name attached.

**The framework asserts nothing.**

That is not a criticism you were expecting and it is the whole session. This module's source contains no claimed multiples, no claimed savings and no efficiency figures. Its metric catalogue is a taxonomy in which not one entry is populated with an observed value, a benchmark or a typical range. Its case-studies section is reserved and empty. **It contains no unwarranted magnitudes because it contains no magnitudes at all.**

And it ships models that produce them.

**Figures produced downstream carry the authority of a named framework while resting entirely on elicited inputs — and because the framework asserts nothing, those figures cannot be traced back to any claim in it.** There is no proposition to check them against. Asked where the number came from, the honest answer is *the model*; asked what the model says, the honest answer is *nothing*.

**That is a worse failure than an unwarranted magnitude, which is at least locatable and refutable.**

The same shape has a purer form, and it is this session's platform anchor. An openly published agent configuration corpus, read at a pinned commit, contains **evaluation machinery**: graded behavioural checks, conformance lanes, baseline-equivalence comparisons, validators, test suites, eval drivers. It is real engineering, done carefully, by people who know how. **It contains no committed result corpus** — the storage location is an empty placeholder. And its own prose uses the vocabulary of demonstrated benefit.

**The machinery is real and it is not a result.** That distinction is the module's central skill, and the reason it needs teaching is that the mistake is not made by careless readers. **It is made by everybody, including the authors**, because an apparatus that could produce evidence feels like evidence, and nothing in a repository tree announces the difference.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. Where did every input to your model come from, and who supplied it?
2. Which claim in the framework could your output contradict?
3. If the output is wrong, who is wrong?
4. What in the model was measured, and what was elicited?
5. Your model runs a simulation over a distribution. Where did the distribution come from?

**Item 2 is the instrument.** It has no answer, and finding that out is the session.

## What you must produce

**A provenance sheet for every number in the model, a machinery-versus-results audit of the pinned corpus, and a traceability check that fails.**

1. **Every input classified** as measured, elicited, assumed or inherited — with the source, the supplier and the date, and **the classification visible beside the number rather than in an appendix.**
2. **The sensitivity statement**: which input the output moves with most.
3. **The machinery inventory**: what evaluation apparatus the pinned corpus contains, enumerated.
4. **The results inventory**: what committed results it contains, enumerated. **This list may be empty and the document must say so in the same words as the first.**
5. **The vocabulary audit**: every benefit word in the corpus's own prose, with what in the corpus would have to exist for it to be warranted.
6. **The framework's own assertions, listed.** The list may be empty. **If it is empty, the document says so and states that the output cannot be checked against the framework it came from.**
7. **The distribution's provenance**, for any simulated quantity — or the simulation is not run.
8. **The inherited figures marked as illustrations**, never as benchmarks.

## The material

### Two ways to have no evidence, and only one of them is recognisable

Every reader of technical material has been trained to detect the first. A document asserts a magnitude — a multiple, a saving, a percentage improvement — and supplies no baseline, no dataset, no method. **You know how to handle that. You locate the claim, note the missing method, and discount it.** The claim is refutable because it exists.

The second failure has no such handle. **A document asserts nothing at all, ships an apparatus that produces numbers, and writes its prose in the vocabulary of demonstrated benefit.** There is no claim to locate. The numbers that circulate were produced downstream, by a model, from inputs somebody supplied, and they carry the framework's name because the model did. Ask what the framework says and the honest answer is *nothing*. **A figure produced by an apparatus that asserts nothing has no parent claim**, and a reader who wants to check it has nowhere to go.

**A learner trained to spot an unwarranted number is unprepared for a document that contains none and generates them anyway.** That sentence is the module's thesis and this session is its demonstration.

### The pinned corpus: excellent machinery, no committed results, benefit prose

The cleanest available specimen is an openly published agent configuration corpus, read at a pinned commit and recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md). It is a good teaching object precisely because **nothing about it is careless.**

**The machinery is substantial and real.** Distinct evaluation lanes: regex-graded behavioural smoke tests, behaviour-conformance contract checks against declared artefact shapes, and baseline-equivalence comparisons against documented divergences — alongside two test frameworks and validators for schema, links, metadata, dependency pinning, moderation and security. There is a generated plugin distribution, a review subsystem decomposed into orientation, fixed lenses, a normalised severity taxonomy and risk-scaled depth, and a durable on-disk record under stable identifiers so that a decision made in one phase can be found by identifier in another.

**And the honesty is unusual.** The corpus's own documentation states that its behavioural checks are regex-graded and **test output shape and not correctness**; that behaviour conformance is **advisory by default** and lacks established calibration; that baseline equivalence covers a narrow contract with unresolved work; and that **a check can be advisory at one tier while the nightly run treats the same check as authoritative.** Its transparency statement records that the project ships no model and no safety classifier, that host behaviour varies, and that human oversight remains necessary.

**Then the finding.** At the pinned revision there is **no committed controlled study, no calibrated baseline, no time-to-delivery dataset, no defect-rate comparison, no human-rated outcome dataset and no learner study.** The evals produce artefacts at run time; the repository stores none of them as evidence. **The storage location for results is an empty placeholder.**

**And the prose uses causal and outcome vocabulary anyway** — the note records words of the kind *ship faster*, *accelerate*, *prevents*, *measurably different*, *proven*. **Those readings are not established by anything in the repository, and the gap between the mechanism claims it warrants and the benefit claims it does not is the single most useful thing about it.**

One further detail belongs in the teaching. **The revision this programme pins is not all-green**: of the checks reported, most were skipped, many succeeded, and one failed. **That is part of the evidence rather than a blemish on it**, and finding it takes deliberate looking.

**Its limits are extreme and travel with every use.** Perishability is total on paths, counts, plugin names and package versions, all of which moved while the note was being written. **Host and model dependence is absolute** — every artefact is inert without a host that loads it, and a result observed on one client and one model is not a result about the corpus. Scale will overwhelm a small team. **And a single vendor's opinion about agent configuration is not the field.**

### The platform documentation has the same shape one layer up

The evaluation framework's own documentation is definitional and accurate about what the tooling computes: three core types, two evaluator tiers, three split strategies, repetitions for non-determinism, a four-category evaluator catalogue, and pass-or-fail output with a reason and a threshold ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)).

**And it publishes no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold.** Most of these evaluators are model-judged.

Read that as an instance of this session's pattern rather than as a complaint. **The apparatus is complete, documented, buildable and free of results about itself.** A team can stand up a full evaluation harness, produce a dashboard of scores, and have no basis whatever for believing those scores correspond to what a competent human would say. **Assert no agreement figure for any of them, here or anywhere**, and note that a cohort finding one published should treat this session's opening as due for revision.

**The documentation's own limits:** read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, with structure reconstructed; most agent evaluators are marked preview; one consulted page is an explicitly superseded variant.

### Three metrics named, none published — the cleanest instance in the repository

The previous subsection is about an absence you have to notice. **This one is about an assertion you can check in a minute, and it is the strongest specimen this session has**, because the vendor names the metrics itself.

The documentation for the work-data access layer states that its servers **undergo continuous evaluation measuring accuracy, latency and reliability** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-iq-intelligence-layer.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-iq-intelligence-layer.md)). Three metrics, named, in the vendor's own voice, presented as an ongoing programme rather than as an aspiration.

**Now go and look for a value. There is none.** Not for accuracy, not for latency, not for reliability. No figure, no range, no target, no historical series, no methodology, no date. **The claim is that measurement happens. The result of the measurement is nowhere.**

**Read the sentence again and notice how much work it does without asserting anything.** It is not false — the evaluation may well be running, thoroughly, with good instruments and competent people. It is not a magnitude claim, so nothing in it can be checked or refuted. And it reads, to anybody skimming, as reassurance about quality. **A reader who has met this session's material can say precisely what it licenses: that an apparatus exists. Nothing about what the apparatus found.** A reader who has not will file it under *evaluated* and move on.

**The parallel with the pinned corpus is exact and worth stating explicitly**, because seeing the same shape twice in unrelated material is what converts it from an anecdote into a pattern. The corpus has a complete harness and an empty results placeholder. This has a named evaluation programme and no published value for any of the three metrics it names. **Neither is dishonest. Neither publishes a number. Both are read as evidence of quality by people who have not looked.**

**A second, smaller instance sits on the same pages and is worth collecting.** The same layer **asserts rate limiting and quantifies no limit.** So a team designing against it knows that a ceiling exists, cannot discover where, and will find out empirically in production. **That is the same pattern in a load-bearing operational field rather than a quality one**, and it is the version that will actually hurt somebody.

**And the pattern recurs one layer out.** The governance surface documents continuous evaluation of agent responses in some detail — the sampling, the metrics surfaced, the dashboard, the connection to traces — and **publishes no evidence that it catches anything a manual review would not.** No study, no comparison, no false-negative rate, no worked case ([research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-control-plane-and-governance.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-control-plane-and-governance.md)). The stated benefit — identify and troubleshoot issues early — **is a claim about what the feature is for, not a finding about what it does.** Three instances, three products, one shape.

**What this establishes, stated narrowly.** That a first-party page names three metrics and publishes none of their values, on a dated reading, verifiable by anybody with a browser. **It does not establish that the evaluation is not happening**, that it is poorly done, or that the layer performs badly — all three are unknown, which is the entire point. **And it establishes nothing about how common this is**; no survey exists and none is asserted here.

**The source's limits are heavy and it must not be pooled.** The family contains definitional product documentation, which establishes what a system is and requires and contains no measurement of anything, and one evidential-but-weak engineering evaluation. **Definitional authority is not evidential authority.** It is **the most perishable material in this repository and the only entry whose *names* are more perishable than its capabilities**; the layer's own pages contradict each other on licensing, on layer membership, on storage and on topology, so **no single page can be trusted alone**; substantial parts were not read; and one member of the family is a name on a hub page with no documentation behind it at all. **Every number in it is forbidden downstream and none appears here.** **No retrieval quality measure, no coverage statement and no freshness bound is published for any layer in it**, which means the three unpublished metrics are not an outlier but the norm across the whole surface.

### The opposite failure, and why it is easier to live with

The openly published engineering handbook this programme uses elsewhere fails in the first way rather than the second. **It asserts four unsupported magnitude claims** — figures with no method, no baseline and no citation — and it contradicts itself in at least a dozen places without noticing ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md), pages read 2026-07-30).

**This repository records those four as specimens and reproduces none of them, here or anywhere.** Naming the pattern is the teaching; repeating the figures would install the hazard the session exists to prevent.

The comparison is the point. **An unsupported figure is locatable, dateable, sourceable and refutable.** You can find it, note that it has no method, and discount it — and a reader who does not notice has at least been given something that could have been noticed. **A number produced by an apparatus that asserts nothing offers no such purchase.** The handbook's failure is worse manners and a better epistemic position.

The handbook also supplies the mechanism by which its own thresholds become other people's benchmarks: it carries a catalogue of non-functional threshold values with **no citation of any kind**, which a reader who does not notice will absorb as a normal range. **Specimen values become benchmarks whether or not anyone intends it.** **Cite it by page and by the date that page was read, never as a whole**; its own footers span years, and several tools it recommends are archived or deprecated.

### Who validates the validators, and why the rubric will not hold still

If the machinery is not the result, the obvious repair is to validate the machinery. That is harder than it sounds, and the human-computer-interaction literature on this is exact about why ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md)).

The starting observation is that using a model to grade a model **relocates the trust problem rather than solving it**: generated evaluators "simply inherit all the problems of the LLMs they evaluate, requiring further human validation." The proposed repair is alignment — have a person grade a subset directly, then prefer the evaluator implementation whose verdicts best reproduce the person's own grades. **That converts an unvalidated generated evaluator into one with an evidentiary basis, and it is the only route in this repository from machinery to result.**

The finding that makes the session's job harder is named and defined: **criteria drift** — "users need criteria to grade outputs, but grading outputs helps users define criteria." That is a genuine circularity rather than a workflow inefficiency. A person cannot fully specify what counts as a good output before seeing outputs, because seeing outputs is how they discover which dimensions of quality vary and matter. The sharper form is that some criteria appear to be **dependent on the particular outputs observed**, which the authors say "raises serious questions for approaches that assume the independence of evaluation from observation of model outputs."

**So an apparatus built before any output was seen is measuring criteria its authors could not yet have known were the right ones** — which is a second, independent reason machinery is not evidence, and a reason that would hold even if every check were perfectly calibrated.

**Its limits:** the criteria-drift evidence is qualitative, establishing that the phenomenon is real and consequential rather than how often or how much; the user study is small by construction, as studies of novel interfaces usually are; and the tool is a research prototype. **Take the drift concept and the alignment pattern, not the interface.** It addresses criteria alignment and says nothing about statistical validity.

### An apparatus that produces a number without an interval overclaims

The statistical treatment this module rests on opens by attacking the convention this session's specimens exemplify: results are reported with a highest-number-is-best mentality, in which a leading figure is highlighted "but not necessarily to test that result for any kind of statistical significance" ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)).

Two of its conclusions bear directly. **A comparison of two systems on a small evaluation set frequently cannot distinguish them at all**, and the correct response is to determine in advance what difference the instrument could have detected rather than to report the difference it happened to show. And **where the items in an evaluation arrive in related groups rather than independently, an uncorrected interval is too narrow**, which makes a measurement look more precise than it is.

**The transfer to this session is one sentence: an apparatus that emits a number and no interval has not told you whether it found anything.** A dashboard of scores with no dispersion is machinery in the most exact sense — it is running, it is producing output, and it is silent on the only question that matters.

**Its limits:** an analytical paper rather than an empirical one, with most illustrative magnitudes resting on stylised assumptions; its machinery is valid only where the items genuinely sample the population you care about; and **statistical validity says nothing about construct validity — a tight interval around a meaningless metric is still meaningless.** **No figure from it is reproduced here.** The full treatment is [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s.

### What a benchmark can and cannot do for you

The evaluation book supplies the conclusion a learner needs before they reach for a published comparison to fill the gap their own apparatus left: **"Public benchmarks will help you filter out bad models, but they won't help you find the best models for your application"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md)). The same source states that benchmarks saturate quickly and are replaced; that contamination is assumed rather than suspected; and that leaderboards omit expensive benchmarks for cost reasons, so **"a small set of benchmarks is not nearly enough to represent the vast capabilities and different failure modes of foundation models."**

It also records the state of practice with unusual bluntness — that when asked how they evaluate their applications, many people said they just eyeballed the results, which is adequate to get a project off the ground and not adequate for iteration.

And the governance point completes it. An audit of the leaderboard that had become the de facto public ranking of frontier models found its scores distorted by undisclosed platform policy rather than by any technical flaw: private testing with the ability to retract unfavourable results produced **"biased Arena scores due to selective disclosure of performance results"**, and the conclusion was **"overfitting to Arena-specific dynamics rather than general model quality"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md)). **A best-of-many reported as a single draw is a bias with a computable shape.** So the external number you were going to borrow has its own provenance problem, one layer further away and harder to inspect.

**Limits on both.** The book is a practitioner text rather than a study, heavily citational, with much of its most useful material resting on unattributed interviews; **its illustrative mappings are shapes rather than measurements and no magnitude from it appears here.** The audit's allocation figures are external estimates by its own account, its findings are specific to one platform at one time, and **nothing in it establishes that models are not improving** — reading it as leaderboard nihilism misses its reform argument. **No figure from it is reproduced here.**

### This repository is a specimen of the same pattern, and says so

The session would be dishonest if it exempted the material teaching it.

**Every whitepaper in this programme closes with falsifiable predictions, each naming an instrument. There are hundreds of them. Not one has been measured.** The apparatus is complete: the predictions are written, the instruments are named, the coding schemes are specified, and the artefacts they would be computed from are defined. **That is machinery. It is not a result**, and a reader who mistook the existence of this repository's prediction register for evidence about this repository's effectiveness would be making exactly the error this session exists to prevent.

**The closing paper of the programme concedes it explicitly**, predicting that fewer than a small fraction of those predictions will ever be measured. **Hold that beside the pinned corpus's empty results placeholder and notice that they are the same object.**

## The workflow

**Audit one real apparatus for the gap between what it can compute and what it has established.**

**Step 1 — pin it.** Commit or version, package version, host, model, enabled configuration, date. **Never audit a moving target**; every count you take is a count at a revision, and a later reader who cannot reproduce your revision cannot check your audit.

**Step 2 — enumerate the machinery, generously.** Every eval lane, grader, validator, test suite and driver. **Be as impressed as the evidence warrants.** The point of this step is that the inventory should be genuinely long, because a short one would make the session's finding trivial.

**Step 3 — go looking for the results, in exactly the same way.** Search the tree for a committed study, a stored result corpus, a calibrated baseline, an outcome dataset, a human-rated sample. **Record where you looked and what the search returned**, because an absence is only evidence if the search was competent.

**Step 4 — find the failing job.** The pinned revision is not all-green. Find it in the record rather than on this page, and notice how much looking it took. **The lesson is not that a build failed; it is that a mostly-green summary conceals a specific fact**, and that concealment is the normal condition of every dashboard a supplier will ever show you.

**Step 5 — audit the vocabulary.** List every benefit word in the corpus's own prose and write, beside each, what would have to exist in the tree for it to be warranted. **Do not editorialise. Put the word in one column and its missing evidence in the next.**

**Step 6 — classify every conclusion you have drawn into five buckets**, and write it down where a reader can see it.

```yaml
# .evidence/apparatus-audit.yaml   <- SHAPE, NOT SCHEMA. Every path and count is
#                                     a fact about ONE revision. Re-derive, never copy.
pin:
  commit: "<full sha>"
  package_version: "<version>"
  host: "<client+version>"          # artefacts are inert without a host that loads them
  model: "<model+version>"          # a result on one model is not a result about the corpus
  read_on: "<YYYY-MM-DD>"

machinery_present:                  # be generous. A short list makes the finding trivial.
  - "behavioural checks, regex-graded - documented as testing output SHAPE, not correctness"
  - "behaviour-conformance contract checks - documented as ADVISORY by default, uncalibrated"
  - "baseline-equivalence comparisons - documented as a narrow contract, work unresolved"
  - "schema, link, metadata, dependency, moderation and security validators"

results_committed:                  # searched with the SAME effort as the list above
  - null                            # storage location present and empty
search_performed:
  looked_for: ["controlled study", "stored result corpus", "calibrated baseline",
               "outcome dataset", "human-rated sample"]
  outcome: "none found at this revision"
  caveat: "Absence at one commit is not proof of absence in the project."

vocabulary_audit:                   # the word, then what would have to exist for it to hold
  - word: "<benefit verb from the corpus's own prose>"
    warrant_required: "a committed comparison against a stated alternative, with an interval"
    warrant_found: "none"

conclusion_classification:          # EVERY conclusion goes in exactly one bucket
  configured_control: []            # a file declares it
  executable_check:   []            # something runs and can fail
  observed_result:    []            # I ran it and recorded what happened, under this pin
  outcome_evidence:   []            # it changed something in the world, measured
  unsupported_inference: []         # <- the bucket where the learning is

self_audit:                         # the same instrument, turned on this programme
  predictions_written: "hundreds, each naming an instrument"
  predictions_measured: 0
  therefore: "This repository's prediction register is machinery. It is not a result."
```

**Step 7 — now do your own model.** Classify every cell as measured, elicited, assumed or inherited, with the source, the supplier and the date. **Every cell is one of the four**, and the uncomfortable finding is how few are the first. Run the sensitivity: move each input across its plausible range and see which one the output follows, then look at that input's provenance line. **In most models the output is most sensitive to an elicited input**, and that sentence goes at the top of the document. Where a simulated quantity's distribution came from a default, **do not run the simulation** — a distribution nobody chose does not become evidence by being sampled many times.

**Step 8 — attempt the traceability check and report where it stopped.** Take one output figure and trace it back to a claim the framework makes. For this framework it stops immediately. **Report that as the result. The failed check is the work.**

**Step 9 — do the same audit on one vendor page you were about to trust.** Find a sentence asserting that something is measured, evaluated, monitored or continuously assessed. Write down the metrics it names. Then search for a published value for each, and record what you searched and what came back. **This takes minutes, needs no tenant, and is the version of the audit you will actually perform on an engagement.**

```yaml
# .evidence/apparatus-audit.yaml  <- appended to the block in step 6.

asserted_measurement_claims:        # sentences that say measuring happens
  - claim: "<verbatim sentence from the vendor page>"
    page_and_date: "<where, and when you read it>"
    metrics_named: [accuracy, latency, reliability]   # THREE. named by them, not by you.
    published_value_found:
      accuracy: none
      latency: none
      reliability: none
    methodology_published: false
    cadence_published: false
    what_it_licenses: "that an apparatus exists"
    what_it_does_not_license: "anything whatever about what the apparatus found"
    bucket: unsupported_inference   # if a reader treats it as quality evidence

  - claim: "<a rate limit is applied>"
    quantified: false               # <- the operational version, and the one that hurts
    consequence: "a ceiling exists, its position is undiscoverable, and you will
                  find it in production"

  - claim: "<continuous evaluation identifies issues early>"
    is_a_claim_about: "what the feature is FOR"
    is_not_a_claim_about: "what the feature DOES"
    comparison_against_manual_review: "NOT PUBLISHED"

note: >
  none of the above is evidence that the evaluation is not happening, or that it is
  poorly done, or that the product is bad. all three are UNKNOWN. that is the finding.
```

**Step 10 — use the agent to attack your own audit, not to extend it.** One prompt: *for each item in my `unsupported_inference` bucket, argue that it belongs in `observed_result` instead — then find the item I have wrongly placed in `observed_result`.* A second, for the block above: *for each vendor sentence I classified as an assertion of measurement, argue that a published value exists somewhere I did not look, and name where.* Read it for the misplacement you made. Keep what is better than your own reasoning and record what you rejected. **Record separately that this instrument shares a model family with the tooling being audited and is therefore not independent of it**, and that an agent which endorsed your audit was not used as an instrument.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** The physical asymmetry between the two lists. **The machinery inventory takes half an hour to write and is genuinely impressive; the results inventory takes the same half hour and is empty**, and the two sit on one screen. No amount of arguing produces that.

**You will also observe the second-order finding, which is the one worth carrying.** The corpus is not dishonest and its authors are not careless. **They built the apparatus, documented its limits with unusual candour, and wrote benefit vocabulary anyway** — which is the strongest available evidence that this error is structural rather than moral, and that you will make it too.

## What good looks like

- **The two inventories are the same length of effort and are presented together.** Machinery and results, searched with equal diligence, on one page. **A report that lists only the first is a brochure.**
- **The search for results is described, not just its outcome.** An absence is evidence only if the search was competent, and a reader must be able to judge that.
- **The classification is beside the number.** A reader who sees the output sees, in the same place, that this input was elicited in a workshop and that one came from telemetry. **Uncertainty relocated is uncertainty preserved; uncertainty stripped is a guess wearing a fact's clothes** — this is [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md)'s inference laundering, moved from a security attribute table into a financial model.
- **Every conclusion sits in exactly one of the five buckets**, and the unsupported-inference bucket is not empty. **An audit with nothing in that bucket has not been performed.**
- **The traceability check was attempted and reported honestly**, including when it could not be completed. **The failed check is the work**, not an obstacle to it.
- **The sensitivity statement names one input**, and that input's provenance line is the first thing a reader is pointed at.
- **A simulation over an invented distribution is not run.** Precision computed from a guess is precision manufactured.
- **Inherited figures are marked.** Any threshold, range or specimen value taken from an example bank is labelled as an illustration with no source, and is never used to characterise a real figure as high or low.
- **The audit is dated and pinned**, and says that absence at one revision is not proof of absence in the project.

## How it goes wrong

- **Machinery counted as evidence** — the apparatus is inventoried, the results are not searched for, and the report reads as a validation. *Warning sign:* your document has a list of what the system can check and no list of what it has found.
- **The asymmetric search** — you looked hard for the machinery and glanced for the results. *Warning sign:* you can name where the graders live and cannot say where results would be stored if they existed.
- **The borrowed authority** — the figure circulates with the framework's name attached and rests entirely on what somebody said in a workshop. *Warning sign:* the output is being forwarded and the input sheet is not.
- **The unfalsifiable output** — no proposition anywhere in the framework could contradict it. *Warning sign:* you cannot say what would make the model wrong, and you had not tried.
- **The benefit word absorbed** — the corpus's own prose says *proven* and you have started saying it too. *Warning sign:* a verb in your summary is doing work no artefact in your inventory supports.
- **The example bank as benchmark** — illustrative thresholds treated as normal ranges. *Warning sign:* you described a real customer's figure as high or low, and cannot say against what.
- **The precise simulation over an invented distribution** — spurious precision. *Warning sign:* your output is stated more finely than any input's provenance would support.
- **The asymmetric ratio** — an instrumented denominator and an elicited numerator, presented as one measurement. *Warning sign:* one side of the ratio has telemetry and the other has a menu.
- **Specimen answers become norms** — figures embedded in illustrative response menus internalised as the plausible range. *Warning sign:* you hold an expectation about what a customer's number should be and cannot say where it came from. **These are the items to quarantine most aggressively.**
- **Provenance in an appendix** — the classification exists and nobody who reads the output sees it. *Warning sign:* the summary page has numbers and no marks.

## Exercises

**None of these is assessed.** The provenance sheet, the apparatus audit and the failed traceability check are carried to [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md), where the provenance sheet is one of the assessed artefacts.

**1 · Sort seven statements about a corpus into machinery, observation and outcome.** For each, say what it licenses.

- *The repository contains conformance checks for every artefact kind.* · *I ran the conformance suite and three artefacts failed.* · *Teams using this corpus ship faster.* · *A check can be advisory at one tier and authoritative at another.* · *The behavioural graders test output shape rather than correctness.* · *The results directory is an empty placeholder.* · *This approach is proven.*

> **Model answers.** The first, fourth and fifth are **machinery** statements — facts about what exists and what it is documented to do; the fifth is unusually valuable because the corpus says it about itself. The second and sixth are **observations** — things somebody looked at and recorded, both bound to a pin. The third and seventh are **outcome claims**, and nothing in the corpus supports either. **The general rule: a statement is machinery if it would still be true with the system switched off, an observation if it required somebody to run something, and an outcome claim if it asserts a change in the world.** Almost every disagreement about evidence in this industry is two people using sentences from different rows as though they were the same row.

**2 · Run the two inventories and put them on one page.** Using the workflow, produce the machinery list and the results list with equal effort. Record how long each took.

> **What you should find.** A long first list, a short or empty second one, and — more usefully — that the second search was harder to do well because you had to decide in advance what a result would have looked like. **That decision is the exercise.** A learner who searched for the word *results* and reported nothing has performed a keyword search; a learner who wrote down five specific things that would count as evidence and then looked for each has performed an audit. **If you find committed results, that is a real finding and this session's opening needs revising** — record the revision, with the pin.

**3 · Go back to your own item 2, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 2 — which claim in the framework your output could contradict. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then write the honest version.

> **Criterion.** A traceability answer passes if it either names a specific proposition in the framework, with its location, that your output could contradict — or states plainly that the framework contains no such proposition, that the check therefore cannot be completed, and that the output cannot be validated against the framework it came from. **A pass is available by either route and only by those two routes.** *"The framework's assumptions"* fails. *"It says the approach is effective"* fails, because that is prose rather than a checkable proposition, and it is the exact confusion this session exists to remove.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**4 · Audit the vocabulary.** Take the pinned corpus's own prose and list its benefit words. Beside each, write what would have to exist in the tree for it to be warranted.

> **What to notice.** That the required warrant is usually the same in every row — a committed comparison against a stated alternative, with an interval — and that its absence is therefore a single missing artefact rather than a long list of missing ones. **Notice also how reasonable the prose sounds.** Nobody wrote those words cynically. **An organisation that has built real machinery believes, correctly, that it has done serious work, and describes serious work in the vocabulary available for describing serious work.** The vocabulary is the problem and it is nobody's fault, which is why a mechanical audit beats an accusation.

**5 · Turn the instrument on this programme.** Open any whitepaper in this wiki, find its §9, and classify the prediction register using the same five buckets you used on the corpus.

> **Scoring criterion.** A pass places the register in **machinery** and states that no prediction in this repository has been measured. **A fail places it in observed result or outcome evidence**, which is the error the session is about, committed on the material teaching it. **The purpose of this exercise is not humility.** It is to establish that the error is structural: this repository was written by people who knew the distinction, stated it in every paper, and still built an apparatus and no results. **If it can happen here it will happen to you.**

**6 · Find one number you already believe and cannot source.** Write down an expectation you hold about what a normal value looks like — an acceptable latency, a reasonable adoption rate, a plausible saving — and try to trace it.

> **What you should find.** Frequently that it came from a specimen answer, an example bank, an illustrative threshold catalogue or a slide. **Specimen values become benchmarks whether or not anyone intended it**, and an openly published handbook this programme uses carries a catalogue of thresholds with no citation of any kind that does exactly this to its readers. **Mark it as an illustration and stop using it to characterise a real figure as high or low.** Note that this repository records four unsupported figures from that handbook as specimens and reproduces none of them, for the same reason you are being asked to quarantine yours.

**7 · Find the three metrics and then fail to find their values.** Open the documentation for the work-data access layer described in the material. Find the sentence asserting continuous evaluation. Write down the metrics it names. Then look for a published value for each — a figure, a range, a target, a series, a methodology, a date. Record what you searched, where, and what came back.

> **What you should find.** **Three metrics named — accuracy, latency and reliability — and no value for any of them.** No methodology, no cadence, no date. Then write the two sentences that matter and keep them apart: **what the assertion licenses** is that an apparatus exists; **what it does not license** is anything whatever about what the apparatus found. **A learner who concludes the layer is unreliable has failed this exercise as badly as one who concludes it is reliable** — both are claims, and the evidence supports neither. The finding is *unknown*, and stating an unknown as an unknown is the professional act this whole module is about.
>
> **Then collect the second and third instances, because the pattern matters more than the case.** The same layer **asserts rate limiting and quantifies no limit**, which is the same shape in an operational field rather than a quality one — you know a ceiling exists, cannot find it, and will meet it in production. And the governance surface documents continuous evaluation of agent responses in detail and **publishes no evidence that it catches anything a manual review would not**; its stated benefit is a claim about what the feature is for, not a finding about what it does. **Three products, one shape.** Compare all three against the pinned corpus's complete harness and empty results placeholder, and note that you now have four independent instances and still not one number. **That is the session.**

## What this does not establish

**Nothing here establishes that a provenance-marked model produces better decisions than an unmarked one.** No evidence for that exists in any source available to this programme, and the classification scheme itself — measured, elicited, assumed, inherited, and the five conclusion buckets — **is this design's invention.** It is derived from a verified structural observation, that the cost side of the source material is engineered and the return side elicited, but the schemes and the requirement to place them beside the number are not in any source.

**The pinned corpus's absence is an absence at one revision.** The note records the finding as the result of a deliberate search of the working tree for a committed study, baseline, dataset or result corpus, and records that **absence at one commit is not proof of absence in the project.** A later revision may hold results, and a cohort that finds them should revise this session rather than defend it.

**Everything observed through that corpus is bound to one host, one model and one revision.** Perishability is total on paths, counts, plugin names and package versions. **A result seen on one client with one model is not a result about the corpus**, and the corpus's own transparency statement says so.

**The platform documentation's silence is a silence and not a defect claim.** No agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data and no threshold rationale — which is evidence about what you may claim, and no evidence at all that the evaluators are poor. **They may be excellent.** You have no way to know, and neither does anyone quoting their output at you.

**This session is where the corpus is weakest and therefore where the teaching is least warranted.** The value framework has never exhibited a baseline being established, never reported a metric being measured, and reserves an empty case-studies section. **There is no worked instance of anything this session asks a learner to do.**

**The traceability check is guaranteed to fail against this particular framework, and that is a designed outcome rather than a discovered one.** A learner should notice that the exercise cannot come out any other way, and should ask what a framework would have to look like for it to come out differently. **The session has no example of one.**

**No figure from the internal source material is reproduced here or anywhere in this repository**, including the specimen answers this session warns about, and **no figure from the openly published handbook's four unsupported claims is reproduced either.** Both omissions are deliberate: reproducing them to demonstrate the hazard would install the hazard.

**The three-metrics specimen establishes an absence on a dated reading and nothing else.** It establishes that a first-party page names accuracy, latency and reliability as things it continuously evaluates and publishes no value for any of them. **It does not establish that the evaluation is not happening, that it is poorly done, or that the layer performs badly.** All three are unknown, and a learner who converts the absence into a criticism of the product has committed the session's error in the opposite direction. **It establishes nothing about frequency either** — no survey exists of how often vendor documentation asserts measurement without publishing results, and none is asserted here.

**The source is the most perishable in this repository and must not be pooled.** It contains definitional product documentation, which contains no measurement of anything, alongside one evidential-but-weak engineering evaluation. **Its own pages contradict each other on licensing, layer membership, storage and topology, so no single page can be trusted alone.** Substantial parts were not read; one named member of the family has no documentation behind it at all; **no retrieval quality measure, coverage statement or freshness bound is published for any layer in it.** Every number in it is forbidden downstream and none appears here. **The governance surface beside it is vendor documentation about vendor governance, definitional and not evidential, with two interpretive thresholds asserted on no stated basis and reproduced nowhere.**
