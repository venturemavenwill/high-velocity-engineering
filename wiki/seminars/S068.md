# S068 · A Number-Generating Apparatus With No Numbers

**Module:** [M14](/wiki/modules/M14.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-068](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-068.md)
**Retrieves from:** S029, S054, S067
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

---

## The situation

You have been handed a model. It has a name, a structure, a set of cells to fill in and, alongside it, a workbook that runs a simulation. Whatever comes out of it will be quoted with the framework's name attached.

**The framework asserts nothing.**

That is not a criticism you were expecting and it is the whole session. This module's source contains no claimed multiples, no claimed savings and no efficiency figures. Its metric catalogue is a taxonomy in which not one entry is populated with an observed value, a benchmark or a typical range. Its case-studies section is reserved and empty. **It contains no unwarranted magnitudes because it contains no magnitudes at all.**

And it ships models that produce them.

**Figures produced downstream carry the authority of a named framework while resting entirely on elicited inputs — and because the framework asserts nothing, those figures cannot be traced back to any claim in it.** There is no proposition to check them against. Asked where the number came from, the honest answer is *the model*; asked what the model says, the honest answer is *nothing*.

**That is a worse failure than an unwarranted magnitude, which is at least locatable and refutable.**

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. Where did every input to your model come from, and who supplied it?
2. Which claim in the framework could your output contradict?
3. If the output is wrong, who is wrong?
4. What in the model was measured, and what was elicited?
5. Your model runs a simulation over a distribution. Where did the distribution come from?

**Item 2 is the instrument.** It has no answer, and finding that out is the session.

## What you must produce

**A provenance sheet for every number in the model, and a traceability check that fails.**

- **Every input classified** as measured, elicited, assumed or inherited — with the source, the supplier and the date, and **the classification visible beside the number rather than in an appendix.**
- **The sensitivity statement**: which input the output moves with most.
- **The framework's own assertions, listed.** The list may be empty. **If it is empty, the document says so and states that the output cannot be checked against the framework it came from.**
- **The distribution's provenance**, for any simulated quantity — or the simulation is not run.
- **The inherited figures marked as illustrations**, never as benchmarks.

## What good looks like

- **The classification is beside the number.** A reader who sees the output sees, in the same place, that this input was elicited in a workshop and that one came from telemetry. **Uncertainty relocated is uncertainty preserved; uncertainty stripped is a guess wearing a fact's clothes** — this is [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md)'s inference laundering, moved from a security attribute table into a financial model.
- **The traceability check was attempted and reported honestly**, including when it could not be completed. **The failed check is the work**, not an obstacle to it.
- **The sensitivity statement names one input**, and that input's provenance line is the first thing a reader is pointed at.
- **A simulation over an invented distribution is not run.** Precision computed from a guess is precision manufactured.
- **Inherited figures are marked.** Any threshold, range or specimen value taken from an example bank is labelled as an illustration with no source, and is never used to characterise a real figure as high or low.
- **The output states what would make it wrong.** Where nothing would, the document says that too.

## How it goes wrong

- **The borrowed authority** — the figure circulates with the framework's name attached and rests entirely on what somebody said in a workshop. *Warning sign:* the output is being forwarded and the input sheet is not.
- **The unfalsifiable output** — no proposition anywhere in the framework could contradict it. *Warning sign:* you cannot say what would make the model wrong, and you had not tried.
- **The example bank as benchmark** — illustrative thresholds treated as normal ranges. *Warning sign:* you described a real customer's figure as high or low, and cannot say against what.
- **The precise simulation over an invented distribution** — spurious precision. *Warning sign:* your output is stated more finely than any input's provenance would support.
- **The asymmetric ratio** — an instrumented denominator and an elicited numerator, presented as one measurement. *Warning sign:* one side of the ratio has telemetry and the other has a menu.
- **Specimen answers become norms** — figures embedded in illustrative response menus internalised as the plausible range. *Warning sign:* you hold an expectation about what a customer's number should be and cannot say where it came from. **These are the items to quarantine most aggressively.**
- **Provenance in an appendix** — the classification exists and nobody who reads the output sees it. *Warning sign:* the summary page has numbers and no marks.

## Do it

**First, take the model apart and classify every cell.** Measured means an instrument produced it and you can name the instrument. Elicited means a person supplied it and you can name the person. Assumed means you chose it. Inherited means it came from an example, a template or a specimen answer. **Every cell is one of the four**, and the uncomfortable finding is how few are the first.

**Second, attempt the traceability check.** Take one output figure and try to trace it back to a claim the framework makes. **Follow it as far as it goes and then write down where it stopped.** For this framework it will stop immediately, because the framework asserts nothing. Report that as the result. It is the finding, not a failure to obtain one.

**Third, run the sensitivity.** Move each input across its plausible range and see which one the output follows. Then look at that input's provenance line. **In most models the output is most sensitive to an elicited input**, and that sentence is the one to put at the top of the document.

**Fourth, deal with the simulation.** If the distribution came from data, name the data. If it came from judgement, say whose and record the elicitation. **If it came from a default, do not run the simulation** — a distribution nobody chose does not become evidence by being sampled many times.

**Fifth, hunt the inherited figures.** Go through everything you have absorbed about what a normal value looks like for the measures in your model and ask where each expectation came from. **Specimen answers in a question bank become benchmarks whether or not anyone intended them to**, and the openly published playbook contains a catalogue of non-functional thresholds with no citation of any kind that will do the same to a reader who does not notice. Mark every inherited figure as an illustration.

**Sixth, move the marks next to the numbers.** Not an appendix. Not a methodology note. **Beside the figure, on the page somebody will forward.**

## Show it

Hand your output figure and your provenance sheet to two different people, separately.

Give the first the figure alone and ask what they would do with it. Give the second the provenance sheet alone and ask what they would do with it. **The gap between the two answers is what the sheet is for**, and it is the argument for putting the marks on the page that travels.

Then ask anybody: **what would make this number wrong?** Whatever they say, check whether the framework you took the model from contains a proposition that could be contradicted. Record the result.

## What this does not establish

**Nothing here establishes that a provenance-marked model produces better decisions than an unmarked one.** No evidence for that exists in any source available to this programme, and the classification scheme itself — measured, elicited, assumed, inherited — **is this design's invention.** It is derived from a verified structural observation, that the cost side of the source material is engineered and the return side elicited, but the four-way scheme and the requirement to place it beside the number are not in the source.

**This session is where the corpus is weakest and therefore where the teaching is least warranted.** The framework has never exhibited a baseline being established, never reported a metric being measured, and reserves an empty case-studies section. **There is no worked instance of anything this session asks a learner to do.**

**The traceability check is guaranteed to fail against this particular framework, and that is a designed outcome rather than a discovered one.** A learner should notice that the exercise cannot come out any other way, and should ask what a framework would have to look like for it to come out differently. **The session has no example of one.**

**No figure from the source material is reproduced here or anywhere in this repository**, including the specimen answers this session warns about, and that omission is deliberate. Reproducing them to demonstrate the hazard would install the hazard.
