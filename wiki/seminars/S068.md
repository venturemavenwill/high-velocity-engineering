**Platform anchor:** Foundry observability — tracing, the Agent Monitoring Dashboard and continuous evaluation, the last two preview · Azure Monitor alerts · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server (preview) · Azure MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## What this day is for

Yesterday ended with eleven weeks, four locales, correct dashboards, and a user who wrote in.

[S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) established that a break without a stated criterion produces an event and not evidence, and that blast radius is who is affected, how many, for how long, and what they experience. **Three of those four are fixed at the moment the failure starts. The fourth — how long — is the only one you can still act on, and it is entirely determined by decisions made before release.**

[S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md) established the third outcome: success, failure, and unknown. **A monitoring system that cannot distinguish *working* from *not reporting* has one state fewer than it needs**, and it is the state it spends most of its time in during an incident.

[S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md) point 2 named three omissions from its four error sources and assigned one of them here: **error introduced by the measurement itself.** [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md) point 1 named its own omission and assigned it here too: **drift caused by a change in the measurement pipeline rather than in the criterion.** Both arrive at the same place, and it is this day's payload.

The target: **detectability is a design property decided before release, not an operational activity performed after it. A detection design states what condition, within what time, observed by whom, at what false-alarm rate — and a failure you have not made detectable is a failure you have decided to accept, whether or not anybody made that decision.**

---

## The third outcome, in a vendor's own troubleshooting table

[S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md)'s third outcome has been taught for three quarters as a claim about callers. Today it is on a documentation page the students will read for themselves.

Foundry's Agent Monitoring Dashboard, which Microsoft labels preview, carries a troubleshooting table. Two of its rows are this day's whole subject.

**Row one.** Symptom: *dashboard charts are empty.* Documented causes: **no recent traffic, a time range that excludes the data, or an ingestion delay.** Three causes, one picture. The first means the system is fine and quiet, the second means you are looking in the wrong place, and the third means the telemetry path is behind. **The dashboard renders all three identically**, and the one a human on call will assume is the first.

**Row two.** Symptom: *evaluation runs are skipped.* Documented cause: **the hourly run limit was reached.** Documented resolution: raise the limit, or wait for the next hour. The documented default is a hundred runs an hour. So the online half of the release gate — continuous evaluation, scoring a sample of production responses — **stops scoring under exactly the condition that makes scoring most valuable, which is a burst of traffic**; and **nothing in the documentation says the skip emits anything.** It is recorded as a troubleshooting entry, which means the way you find out is that you noticed the results were not there.

That is not a defect and it is not a criticism. A rate limit is a sensible thing to have and writing it down is better than not writing it down. **It is the cleanest instance in the whole programme of a monitor whose silence is indistinguishable from a clean bill of health**, and the students can reproduce it in twenty minutes.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

A substrate component is supplied with its current monitoring: every signal, every alert, every dashboard, and the incident log for the past two years. Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. **For each incident in the log, say which signal detected it.** Where none did, say what did.
2. **Estimate the time from start to detection** for each, before reading it.
3. **How would you know if a signal had stopped reporting?** Answer for each of the three signals.
4. Predict: **which of your component's failures would take longest to notice?**
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to set up monitoring for your component so that you find out when it degrades. Write down what it will produce, and **name the one thing it will get wrong.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 1 is the day's opening finding and requires no framework: for a substantial share of the log, the answer to *which signal detected it* is *a person*.

Item 5's expected shape is the day's own subject turned on its own tooling: the agent will produce signals, dashboards and alerts competently and quickly, and **it will not give any of them a liveness property**, because nothing in the request asked how you would know the monitor had stopped and it is not the kind of thing that gets asked.

**Warrant.** Pretesting against a real two-year record with the answer available on the page. Item 2 is a calibration item and the errors run one way, which is the finding. Item 3 is the day's payload administered before the day names it, so that the phase 8 comparison measures a shift rather than a recall. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8, and its pairing with item 3 is deliberate — they are the same question asked about a human design and an agent design.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups receive **four monitoring designs for the same substrate component**, each with the incident record of the period it ran in, and each stood up against a live agent for the phase. **The four are selected against the difference the cohort could not anticipate at [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md)** — whichever of the five differences most students failed to predict is the one the four designs are tested against.

| Design | Construction | Run today? |
|---|---|---|
| **M1** | A threshold alert on the aggregate success rate. Detects total failure in seconds; detects partial degradation never. | **Executed** — an Azure Monitor alert on the run success rate |
| **M2** | A dashboard of thirty panels, reviewed by a human every Monday. Detects almost everything, in up to seven days. | **Demonstrated rather than operated** — the review is a week long and the phase is forty-five minutes |
| **M3** | A synthetic probe running the primary path every minute from outside the system. Detects exactly what it probes, fast, and is blind to everything else. | **Executed** — a scheduled request from outside the agent's own host |
| **M4** | A rich derived signal computed by the same pipeline that produces the metric being monitored. The most sensitive of the four to real degradation. | **Executed** — a continuous evaluation rule scoring a sample of the agent's own emitted traces |

Groups rank the four by *how long until you know*, commit, then the incident records are supplied — **and then they break something.**

Two induced conditions, both operable in the phase and both documented. **Both are induced on the group's own agent and its own telemetry resource, never on the substrate's shared observability path**, for the reason [WP-068](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-068.md) §8 gives: a day that teaches blast radius may not enlarge one to make a point.

- **The telemetry path stops.** Span emission is disabled for the group's own agent. M4's charts go empty. So do the charts for a quiet period and for an ingestion delay, and the platform's own troubleshooting table lists all three under the same symptom. **Groups are asked which of the three they are looking at, using only what is on the screen.** They cannot tell.
- **The cap is reached.** The group's own evaluation rule has its hourly run limit set low, and traffic is driven above it. Runs are skipped. **The dashboard does not go red; it goes sparse.** Nothing announces the skip, and the documentation's remedy is to raise the limit or wait.

The payload is **M4**. It wins on every incident in the record except one, and on that one it reported healthy for nine days, because the fault was in the pipeline and the signal moved with the metric. **A monitor computed from the thing it monitors cannot detect a fault in the path they share.** This is [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md)'s error-introduced-by-measurement and [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md)'s pipeline drift, arriving together — and the induced conditions are the same failure in the room, on the students' own telemetry, in under ten minutes.

Task after: **for each design, state the failure it cannot detect, and how long it would take you to notice that the monitor itself had stopped.** Then: **write the smallest signal that distinguishes a quiet system from a stopped pipeline.**

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across detection designs with a real incident record as adjudicator, with the case selection instrumented to the previous day's measured blind spot, and now with three of the four operated. M4 defeats the room's expectation that sensitivity is the property to optimise, and it defeats it on the one incident the record contains that the other three caught — so the lesson is not that M4 is bad but that a detection design has a dimension the ranking did not have. **The two induced conditions are what make point 8 a demonstration rather than an assertion**, and they were chosen because both are documented behaviours of a supported product rather than contrived faults: a room that has watched a vendor's own dashboard render *healthy*, *stopped* and *delayed* as the same empty chart does not need to be told that *nothing* is read as *fine*. **M2 is demonstrated rather than operated and nothing that depends on running it is assessed.**

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **Detectability is a design property, not an operational one.** It is decided before release, it has a cost in engineering time and in system complexity, and it is traded against other things. A monitoring system assembled after the incidents that motivated each part is a record of the past, not a design.

2. **A detection target has four parts:** what condition, within what time, observed by whom, at what false-alarm rate. **A signal without all four is not a detection design.** This formalises what [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md) steps 3 and 4 did informally, what [S018](/wiki/seminars/S018.md) step 7 asked for without a form, and what [S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md) point 8 named as the unobservable event.

   **A second vocabulary names two of the four properties from a different direction and is worth holding beside them.** A production-machine-learning survey imports observability from control theory with its governing asymmetry stated plainly — **you can only control a system to the extent that you can observe it** — and then decomposes it into two design obligations. **Alertability** is metrics and thresholds constructed so that a failure is unambiguous. **Actionability** is enough signal, at the moment the alert arrives, to identify a cause and choose a remedy. The four-part target already carries the first; **it does not carry the second, and the second is the one that decides whether the person at step 6 can do anything at three in the morning.** A signal that fires correctly and supports no diagnosis has satisfied the design and wasted the response, and it is the alert everybody in this room has been woken by. Step 6's narration is where you find out which one you built. See [research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md); **vocabulary and mechanism only, and the survey's stack is one vendor's own.**

3. **Three detection failures.** The condition produces no signal. The signal exists and nobody is watching. **The signal exists, someone is watching, and it is indistinguishable from normal.** The third is the common one and it is the one no amount of instrumentation fixes, because it is a discrimination problem rather than a coverage problem.

   **And its commonest single form is an average.** The survey cited at point 2 states it as a property of the metric rather than of the monitor: **a system with good performance on average may still exhibit failure modes that are not apparent by looking at an aggregate metric.** The remedy it proposes is to decide in advance which slices of the traffic are watched separately — and it insists on two things about that decision that matter here. **Which slices matter is a domain question and not a statistical one**, because slicing on every available attribute produces more slices than anyone can read; and **slicing on metadata the system does not consume is often more informative than slicing on the attributes it does**, because the population that is being failed is frequently not a population the system knows about. M1 is the aggregate case standing in the room this morning: it detects total failure in seconds and partial degradation never, and no threshold placed on it repairs that, because the information was destroyed by the averaging and not by the threshold.

4. **A monitor derived from the monitored pipeline cannot detect a fault in that pipeline.** Independence of the detection path from the thing detected is a structural requirement, not a refinement. This is [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md)'s second omitted error source and [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md)'s omitted drift mechanism, and they are the same failure: **the instrument and the observation moved together, so the difference between them carried no information.**

   **Two further sensitivities make step 1's enumeration a judgement rather than a checklist, and both are named in the same survey.** The first is **entanglement**: in a system whose behaviour is learned from data rather than written, *changing one thing changes everything*, so there is no local change whose consequences a reviewer can bound by reading the diff. The second is **configuration**: hyperparameters, versions, model deployments and feature lists live in configuration rather than in code, and a small error there produces markedly different behaviour that no ordinary functional test fails on. Together they mean that **the set of conditions worth detecting cannot be derived from the change that motivated the release**, which is why step 1 is an act of enumeration under uncertainty and why step 3's not-detected list is the day's honest output rather than its embarrassing one. **The same survey also separates the two monitoring jobs and refuses to rank them**: functional monitoring watches predictive quality and the distribution of what is arriving, non-functional monitoring watches failures, latency and resource use, and the second matters independently of the first, because **if the system is down it does not matter how good it is.** **M4 is a functional monitor with no non-functional companion**, and the split is worth naming here because a non-functional check on the telemetry path is one of the few independent detectors available in a stack where everything else shares that path.

5. **Detection latency multiplies blast radius.** [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md)'s quantity is a rate times a duration, and after release the duration is the only term you control. Halving detection time halves the harm, and this is the only lever available once the failure has begun.

6. **False alarms cost credibility, not time.** The operator-hours are trivial. What a high false-alarm rate consumes is the response to the next alarm, and the withdrawal is informal: an individual mutes it, nobody records the decision, and the signal continues to appear in the design documents. **A muted alert is a detection design that no longer exists and still looks like one.**

7. **Some failures you decide not to detect**, and naming them with their expected cost is the honest form. This is [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md) point 6 applied to observation rather than to handling. **This day's repertoire is safe for failures that produce some change in a signal your system can emit; it is unsafe for failures visible only to a user — a wrong-but-plausible answer is the canonical case, produces no anomaly anywhere, and is undetectable by every technique on this day.** That class requires a channel from the affected party, which the substrate has and which this day does not design; the artefact requires the student to name which of their own component's failures fall in it.

8. **The third outcome, in monitoring.** [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md) point 1. A monitoring system reports healthy, unhealthy, or nothing, and *nothing* is read as healthy by every human being who has ever looked at a dashboard. Every signal needs a liveness property of its own, and [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md) point 8 applies here unchanged: **a monitor that has never fired is evidence about the monitor.**

9. **Defaults chose your detection design before you did.** Four, from this morning. **The evaluation rule's hourly run limit** — a documented default of a hundred runs an hour, above which runs are skipped, with no documented signal. **The sample rate** on continuous evaluation, which decides what fraction of production you are looking at and is set once and forgotten. **The dashboard's own guidance thresholds**, which describe latency above ten seconds as possibly indicating throttling and a run success rate below ninety-five per cent as warranting investigation — sensible numbers, somebody else's, and a room that adopts them has adopted a false-alarm rate it did not compute. And **the alerting surface itself is preview**, which is a statement about how long its behaviour is guaranteed to stay the same. Point 6's credibility argument applies to all four: **an alert whose threshold you inherited is an alert you will mute**, because you have no account of why it fires.

10. **Independence has a concrete shape on this platform, and it is not obvious.** Your agent emits OpenTelemetry spans to Application Insights. Your traces are read from there. Your continuous evaluation scores those traces. Your dashboard renders the scores. **That is one path, four times.** The documentation is explicit about what happens at one break in it: where the span message attributes are empty or missing, the quality evaluators return no score, and the safety evaluators still return scores that the documentation says might not be meaningful — **so a partial instrumentation failure produces a partial verdict that reads like a whole one.** Ingestion is documented as taking on the order of a minute, so a check run immediately after traffic sees nothing and is not entitled to conclude anything. **An independent detector is one that does not traverse that path**: a synthetic probe from outside, a business-side count, a person. This is point 4 with an address.

11. **What is perishable here and what is not.** The hundred-runs-an-hour default, the ten-second latency guidance, the ninety-five per cent success guidance, the ingestion delay, the preview labels and the parameter names will all change. **None of it touches the durable claim**, which is that a monitor has three states, that the third is silent, and that a detection path sharing infrastructure with the thing it watches carries no information about that infrastructure. **This is safe for reasoning about any monitoring design you meet — what does its silence mean, and what does it share with what it watches. It is unsafe for reasoning about what these features will do next term, and it is unsafe as evidence for the durable claim itself**: the vendor's troubleshooting table is a vivid illustration of the third outcome, not a proof of it, and the proof is [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md)'s.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Point 2 is the retained form and the assessed object. Point 4 is the payload and discharges two obligations placed on this day by earlier scope statements. Point 7 carries the strong-form scope statement and is bound in the artefact rather than left in this paper. **Points 9 and 10 are the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s two required platform points**, and point 10 is deliberately placed after point 4 rather than inside it, because the durable claim must be available to a student whose next employer runs none of this. Point 11 carries the perishability boundary to the students.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 5. This phase produces a build. The failure enumeration, the detection targets and the accepted blindness are the student's.**

Students use **the component and change they have carried since [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md)**, and yesterday's measured gap.

1. **Enumerate the failure conditions** you would need to know about if you released this change.
2. **For each, state the detection target's four parts:** condition, time, observer, false-alarm rate.
3. **Identify the conditions for which you have no signal, and decide explicitly which of those you will not detect** — with the expected cost of not detecting each, **and, in the same field as the cost, where the figure came from and how confident you are in it.** This list is an output, not an omission.
4. **Build one signal** for the condition you judge most consequential, using Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server and the Foundry MCP Server, **and argue its independence from the pipeline it monitors.** **Then build a second, minimal signal for the [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) failure class you did not anticipate** — the queries that failed in production and that your predicted lost-document set missed. The second signal need not be sophisticated and its four-part target may be weak; it must exist, and you must say what made that class invisible to you in advance.
5. **Give the first signal a liveness property and prove it.** Stop the signal and show that something says so, within the time you committed to. **A monitor whose stopped state and healthy state look the same fails this step**, and this is the one step of the day with a demonstration rather than a description — the room has already watched an empty chart mean three different things.
6. **State its false-alarm rate and the person it goes to.** They are on the substrate rota and are present for the last thirty minutes of the phase. **You do not ask whether the signal is acceptable. You ask them to narrate what they would do when it arrives at three in the morning, and to say where they would stop, and you record the narration.**
7. **Cover one class the substrate's taxonomy does not contain** — the class you constructed from residuals at [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md), or a new one — and say how your design sees it.
8. **State the detection latency you are committing to**, and compute the blast radius at that latency.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md): the **prompt record**, the **intervention record** — **no entries fails**, unless the design demonstrates it could have detected an intervention-worthy error, of which step 5's liveness proof is the obvious example and is accepted — and the **frontier statement**.

**Fading:** step 2 is worked with the room, since the four-part target is a new object and is the day's assessed form. Step 4's telemetry wiring and credentials are supplied. **Steps 3 and 5 are unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 5 producing an input to [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s decision, now building the signal rather than specifying it. **Step 6 uses an external observer per instrumentation rule 1**: the person who would receive the alert is in the room for other reasons, and whether they will act on it is theirs to say and not the student's to assume. **Step 6's narrate-rather-than-approve form is [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md)'s expert-politeness countermeasure, applied here because rule 5 requires it**: a rota member asked whether an alert is acceptable will say yes, since agreeing costs nothing until it fires; a rota member asked to narrate a three-a.m. response will say where they would stop. **Step 3's provenance clause is a correction under [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) specification rule 2** — an earlier draft required the cost without its confidence, which is the detached-qualification failure that rule exists to prevent. **Step 4's second signal is the artefact element [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) named under instrumentation rule 6**, and it is deliberately permitted to be weak: the demand is that the class the student could not anticipate acquires a detector, not that the detector be good. **Step 5 is new with the build and is the day's answer to instrumentation rule 7**: point 8 is a claim the room's folk model resists, the phase 3 induced conditions stage it, and step 5 is where the student has to satisfy it in their own work rather than agree with it. **Step 7 is the artefact element [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) named under instrumentation rule 6.** Step 3 is the day's discriminator — an unenumerated blind spot is not a decision — and step 8 makes the commitment quantitative rather than dispositional.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate alert that fired weekly for four months, was spurious every time, was muted by one person on a Thursday afternoon with no record, and then fired correctly. Attention is directed at one fact — **nobody decided to stop monitoring that condition.** The decision was made by attrition, by a person who was not choosing a monitoring policy and would have been alarmed to be told they were.

Then the agent case: a Copilot-built monitoring setup that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. Asked to make a component's degradation visible, it produced a dashboard, three charts, a threshold alert and a continuous evaluation rule, all correctly wired, all rendering, all green. Attention goes to two things. **It set the evaluation rule's hourly limit at the default and said nothing about it**, so under load the scoring stops and the charts thin out; and **it gave nothing a liveness property**, so a stopped emitter and a healthy service produce the same screen. The review that would have caught either is not the review a student knows to perform: the reviewer checked that each signal was correct, and every signal was correct. **The question nobody asked was what this looks like when it is broken**, and the answer — which the vendor's own troubleshooting table gives — is that it looks like this.

Then students, in threes: an alert, a check or a notification you have silenced — **or a monitor you accepted today whose stopped state you did not look at.**

**Warrant.** Error management targeting points 6, 8 and 9. The instructor case contains no incorrect judgement at any step: each individual dismissal was right, the mute was reasonable, and the aggregate was a policy change nobody made. It is chosen because every student in the room has done it, which is what makes the disclosure cheap to make and expensive to have ignored. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition**, and it is the same failure as the instructor's from the other end — there the monitoring stopped because a person quietly decided; here it stops because nobody decided anything and a default did.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **their step 4 signal with its independence argument, their step 5 liveness proof, and their step 8 latency commitment.**

The partner has two tasks and both are required:

- **Construct a failure the signal will not see.**
- **State how long it would take the student to notice that the signal itself had stopped** — and, where the liveness proof at step 5 claims a bound, say whether the construction defeats it.

A construction that the signal misses but that the student's liveness property would surface is not a defeat.

Per rule 3, the outcome is recorded in the artefact. **The second task's answer is recorded in minutes and is the cohort's detection-timeliness measure**, extending the series [WP-044](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-044.md) §7 began and superseding the informal signal-comparison baselines carried from [S018](/wiki/seminars/S018.md) and [S019](/wiki/seminars/S019.md).

Per rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8, this reuses [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md)'s dual-defeat structure; no day has run, so the reuse is **contingent** on S029's reported outcome.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with a dual demand in which the second half attacks the monitor rather than the system. Pairing the signal with its own liveness is what makes point 8 an assessed property instead of a remark.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in SY301 with ME301. Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the failure enumeration; a four-part detection target for each condition; the explicit not-detected list with a cost for each entry **carrying its provenance and confidence in the same field**, per specification rule 2; one built signal with its independence argument; **its liveness property with the demonstration that stopping the signal produced a report within the committed time**; **a second signal for the [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) class the student did not anticipate, with a statement of what made it invisible**; its false-alarm rate and the recorded three-a.m. narration from the named recipient; the coverage of one class outside the taxonomy; the committed latency with the blast radius computed at it; the named failures that are visible only to a user; **the prompt record**; **the intervention record**; **the frontier statement**; and the phase 7 outcome. **Nothing else is assessed.**

**A signal whose independence from the monitored pipeline is not argued fails.** This is the day's single hard requirement.

**A detection target missing any of its four parts fails.** All four, every time.

**An empty not-detected list fails.** A student who claims to detect everything has either not enumerated or not decided, and both are the same defect.

**A signal whose stopped state is indistinguishable from its healthy state fails step 5.** The demonstration is required; an argument that liveness is present is not a demonstration that it works.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the design demonstrates it could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**Nothing that depends on operating M2 is assessed.** The weekly dashboard review is demonstrated rather than operated, and no element turns on a result the students did not produce.

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md).

**Rule-6 discharge.** [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) named this day as the response occasion for two instruments, and [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) named it for a third. **All three elements exist.** The step 3-against-step 6 attribution matrix responds at phase 3, whose four monitoring designs are selected against whichever of the five differences the cohort could not anticipate, so that the detection work targets the measured blind spot rather than the familiar case. ## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **Detectability is a design property decided before release, not an operational activity performed after it.** A detection target has four parts — what condition, within what time, observed by whom, at what false-alarm rate — and a signal without all four is not a detection design. **A monitor has three states, the third is silence, and everyone who has looked at a dashboard reads silence as health.**

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** The blameless construction the clinic depends on follows [research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md), cited for **direction and mechanism only**: disclosure is a condition of learning from error, not how much. The production-monitoring framing is grounded in [research/01-course-structure/cmu-17-645-ml-in-production-ai-engineering.md](/research/01-course-structure/cmu-17-645-ml-in-production-ai-engineering.md). **No magnitude is asserted from either**, and every latency figure, guidance threshold and default named today belongs to one product at one version.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Give every signal a liveness property and prove it** by stopping the signal and watching something say so.
- **Argue independence.** A monitor computed by the pipeline it monitors carries no information about that pipeline.
- **Keep an explicit not-detected list with a cost against each entry.** Claiming to detect everything means you have not enumerated or have not decided.
- **A configured control is not evidence**, per the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). That an alert exists establishes only that somebody configured it; whether it fired is an observed result, and that the system is well is neither.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Reading an empty chart. It means a quiet system, a stopped pipeline or a delayed ingestion, and the one your on-call will assume is the first.
