## The situation

You are about to claim that the system improved something. To do that you need the state before, and the state before is gone.

Nobody destroyed it. It simply was never recorded, because at the moment it could have been recorded nobody was thinking about measurement — they were thinking about whether the thing could be built. **By the time anybody wants a before, the before has become a memory held by people with an interest in the after.**

This is the module's simplest claim and its most violated one. Every other measurement in this programme can be taken late. A latency distribution can be captured next week. A cost attribution can be reconstructed from a bill. A quality score can be recomputed over stored traces. **The baseline is the only measurement that cannot be taken later at any price**, because the thing it measures stopped existing the moment you shipped.

The internal practice framework this module is built on instructs its reader to establish a baseline. **It never exhibits one being established.** It catalogues metric definitions and never reports one measured. Its case-studies section is reserved and empty. **There is no worked example available to this programme, and this session says so in its own text rather than leaving it to the paper.**

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. What was the value of your headline measure the week before you arrived, and how do you know?
2. Who measured it, with what instrument, and does that instrument still exist?
3. What result would make the customer conclude this had not worked?
4. What else changed in the same period?
5. If you cannot establish the baseline, what will you say instead?

**Item 3 is missing from the corpus's entire discovery apparatus, and it is the item this session is really about.** Its question bank asks in every block what good would look like and how improvement would be measured. It asks nowhere what result would mean it had failed. That absence is the reason the item is here, and the distribution of your answers to items 1 and 3 is the session's opening measurement: most people can produce something for item 1 and nothing concrete for item 3.

## What you must produce

**A baseline record, or a written statement that there is not one.**

1. **The measure**, defined precisely enough that two people would compute it the same way.
2. **The value before**, with the period it covers stated as a period rather than as "before".
3. **The instrument**, named, together with whether it still exists and who owns it.
4. **The unit of analysis** — what one observation is, and how a multi-step interaction was cut into query and response before anything was scored.
5. **Who took the measurement**, and when.
6. **The disconfirming result**: the value that would mean this did not work, written before any output was examined.
7. **The confounders present in the period** — listed, not analysed. Analysis is [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md).
8. **Where no baseline exists**: a statement saying so, what the claim has been downgraded to, and what the downgrade gives up.

## The material

### The asymmetry that makes this the module's first session

Measurements differ in whether they can be deferred, and almost nobody sorts them that way.

A deferrable measurement is one whose subject persists. The system is still running, so its latency, its cost, its failure rate and the quality of its outputs are all available whenever you decide to look. A non-deferrable measurement is one whose subject is destroyed by the act you want to measure. **The state of the world before your change is the canonical example, and shipping is the act that destroys it.**

The practical consequence is that baseline capture has to happen at the moment of least motivation. At that moment the system does not work yet, nobody is asking for numbers, and the engineering question in the room is feasibility rather than value. Every incentive points at building. **The record you are being asked to create is one whose entire value is realised by somebody else, later, in a conversation you may not attend.**

### What the practice corpus does with this, and what it does not

The internal corpus behind this module is a value framework from a customer-embedded engineering organisation within a large technology vendor, recorded as an original paraphrase at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md).

Its failure is not the one you have been trained to expect. **It contains no magnitudes at all** — no claimed multiples, no claimed savings, no efficiency figures. Its metric catalogue is a taxonomy of definitions in which not one entry carries an observed value, a benchmark or a typical range.

**Its weakness is non-closure rather than exaggeration.** It instructs the reader to establish a baseline and never exhibits one being established. It catalogues metrics and never reports one being measured. It recommends a stochastic simulation method for quantities it never quantifies. **A reader trained to spot an unwarranted number is unprepared for a document that contains none and generates them anyway.**

The structural diagnosis is worth carrying: **the cost side is engineered and the return side is elicited.** Consumption is forecast by engineers, instrumented by engineers, and refined against telemetry from their own system. Return is chosen by the customer from a menu, measured by the customer with instrumentation the customer may not possess, over a period nobody specifies. And several metric definitions define themselves as the change *attributable to* the system — **which presupposes exactly the thing that needed establishing.**

**The corpus's limits are heavy and travel with every use.** It is access-controlled, so no public reader can check it; it is paraphrased here and never quoted; it publishes no outcome, evaluation, comparison or measurement of any kind; and no figure from it is reproduced anywhere in this repository.

### Define the criteria before you build, and then hold the instrument still

The one book in this repository that treats evaluation as an engineering problem inside a running system names the discipline and attributes the name honestly: "Before investing time, money, and resources into building an application, it's important to understand how this application will be evaluated. I call this approach *evaluation-driven development*. The name is inspired by *test-driven development* in software engineering" ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md)). The same author concedes the objection in her own voice, which is why the idea is teachable rather than merely fashionable: focusing only on applications whose outcomes can be measured "is similar to looking for the lost key under the lamppost (at night). It's easier to do, but it doesn't mean we'll find the key."

The sharper claim from the same source is the one most teams get backwards. **"An application evolves over time, but the way it's evaluated ideally should be fixed. This way, evaluation metrics can be used to monitor the application's changes."** The failure mode is given concretely: if the application and the judge are managed by different teams, "the application team might mistakenly attribute the changes in the evaluation results to changes in the application, rather than the changes in the judges." A baseline and a follow-up produced by different instruments are two measurements of two things.

**That source is a practitioner book, not a study.** It is heavily citational and honest about it, but a large number of its most useful sentences begin *several people have told me*, and its interview base is unattributed and unquotable. Where it reports a figure, the figure almost always originates with somebody else. **No magnitude from it appears here.**

### A baseline is a claim about a population, and yours may not be your population

The retrieval benchmark literature supplies the correction that stops a baseline from being over-read. Neural retrieval systems had been reporting improvements measured overwhelmingly on the collection they were trained on, and that design cannot distinguish a model that has learned retrieval from a model that has learned a particular corpus, a particular query style and a particular notion of relevance ([research/02-technical-foundations/thakur-beir.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/thakur-beir.md)). The benchmark's response was to make heterogeneity the design principle: eighteen datasets whose query lengths, document lengths and notions of relevance genuinely differ, evaluated zero-shot.

Two findings transfer directly. **A lexical baseline predating the neural literature did not win and did not collapse anywhere**, and that stability is itself the finding — a method whose worst case is acceptable is operationally different from one whose average is higher and whose worst case is unknown. And **the families that were computationally cheapest were the ones that transferred worst.** For your baseline the lesson is one sentence: **a baseline established on a domain, a query style or a document shape unlike yours is not a baseline for your domain**, and importing one is a category error dressed as diligence.

**Its limits:** the results are a snapshot of one year's practice, the eighteen datasets are a sample of retrieval tasks rather than a map of them, the constituent relevance judgements differ in density and construction, and the benchmark has since become a target of optimisation, which weakens its own zero-shot premise. **Take the methodology and the trade-off structure, not the rankings.**

### Your own perception is not a baseline, and experience does not repair it

The most important thing you carry into the baseline conversation is a belief about whether the work helped, and that belief is unreliable in a known direction.

A randomised trial of experienced open-source maintainers working on their own repositories found them **slower with AI assistance while believing they had been sped up** ([research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md](/research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md)). Forecasts before the study, from the developers and from independent expert forecasters, all pointed the other way. **The datum that matters here is the post-hoc one: after doing the tasks, the developers still believed the assistance had made them faster.** Direct personal experience did not correct the misperception.

**The study's limits are large and stated by its authors, who published an explicit list of things their result does not show.** The developer sample is very small; the population — experts in codebases they know intimately — is close to a worst case for assistance and does not generalise to novices or greenfield work; the outcome is self-reported time. The research group itself now labels the result out of date and has redesigned the study, because participants increasingly decline to work without assistance at all, which compromises the control arm. **That redesign is part of what makes the source worth citing: a group discovering in public that its instrument has been degraded by changes in the world.** No magnitude from it is reproduced here.

### What the platform gives you, and the one thing it cannot give back

The evaluation surface on this platform makes three things possible that a spreadsheet does not, and each maps onto a part of the baseline record ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)).

**The unit of analysis is a choice you must make explicitly.** A multi-turn conversation has to be cut into a query half and a response half before anything can be scored, and the documentation states the consequence in its own words: **"How you split determines *what you're evaluating*."** Three strategies are supplied — splitting at the last user message, treating the first message as the query and everything after as the response, and scoring each exchange independently. An agent that recovers well from a bad first attempt scores well under one and badly under another. **Neither score is wrong; they answer different questions, and a team that never chose is reporting an answer to a question it did not ask.**

**One sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement.** The framework supplies repetitions "to detect non-deterministic behavior", which is the documentation conceding the point directly. A baseline taken once is an anecdote with a timestamp.

**Stored responses can be evaluated "from logs or previous runs… without re-running the agent."** This is the capability that makes a baseline durable: the artefact you preserve is the interaction, and the scoring can be redone later under a definition you had not thought of yet. It is the closest thing available to insurance against the criteria drift you will meet at [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md).

**And the silence.** The documentation is definitional and accurate about what the tooling computes, and it publishes **no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold.** Most of these evaluators are model-judged. **Assert no agreement figure for any of them, here or anywhere.** The pages were read through search excerpts rather than full fetches; most agent evaluators are marked preview; one consulted page is an explicitly superseded variant. **The tooling will let you produce a baseline. It will not tell you whether the thing it scored is the thing you care about.**

### The labelling budget, and the third option nobody uses

You will not be able to hand-label everything, and the two obvious moves are both wrong. Treating a model's judgements as though they were labels propagates its systematic error straight into your estimate and produces an interval that is invalid because it ignores prediction error. Discarding the model's judgements and using only the small labelled sample is valid and wasteful.

There is a third construction. A small gold-standard sample is used to estimate and remove the systematic discrepancy between what the model predicts and what the trustworthy labels say, and that correction is then applied to the estimate computed over the large model-labelled set — with the correction's own uncertainty propagated into the final interval ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/angelopoulos-prediction-powered-inference.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/angelopoulos-prediction-powered-inference.md)). The load-bearing property is that validity holds **without any assumptions about the machine-learning algorithm supplying the predictions**. A poor model gives you a wider interval, not a wrong answer; a better model buys precision rather than permission.

**Its conditions are strict and are where teams break it.** The gold-standard labels must actually be gold standard, because the method corrects the model's error against the labels and cannot correct error in the labels. The labelled subset must be a valid sample of the same population as the unlabelled data — convenience sampling breaks the guarantee silently. And it says nothing about whether the quantity you are estimating is the right one. **This note was written from the abstract and the published summary; no coverage rate, interval width or per-domain result is reproduced here.** The full construction is used properly at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md); today it exists to tell you that a labelling budget is not a reason to abandon the baseline.

### A baseline that was run, and then renamed — a live, dated, first-party specimen

Everything above concerns baselines nobody took. **This subsection is about the other failure, which is rarer and much more instructive: a baseline that was taken carefully, reported honestly, and then described as something else by the documentation that cites it.**

The repository carries a standing prohibition on one particular retrieval-improvement claim ([09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md)). **The reason for that prohibition has changed, and the change is the whole lesson.** It used to rest on the absence of any published methodology. **A methodology now exists**: an engineering evaluation published by the vendor, with named authors, named datasets, a named metric and per-dataset results reported rather than only an average ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-iq-intelligence-layer.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-iq-intelligence-layer.md)). **The prohibition stands. Its justification is now the opposite of what it was**, and a repository that had quietly updated the conclusion without updating the reason would have been carrying a stale claim of exactly the kind it exists to prevent.

**Here is what the experiment actually compared.** The retrieval product has a dial — a reasoning-effort setting with several positions. The evaluation ran the product at a higher setting and compared it against **the same product at its lowest setting.** That is a real and useful comparison. It answers the question *is turning the dial up worth the latency and the tokens*, which is a question a practitioner genuinely has, and it is the question the blog's own careful sentence answers.

**Here is what the documentation that cites it says.** The product's own frequently-asked-questions page restates the same result as an improvement over traditional single-shot retrieval-augmented generation. **That comparison was never run.** No single-shot system appears in the experiment. Nobody lied; a sentence was compressed by somebody who was not the person who ran the experiment, and the compression changed the baseline. **The baseline was the product turned down. The restatement makes it a different architecture entirely.**

**Read that as this session's thesis appearing in the wild, and read the direction carefully.** A baseline *was* run — which puts this experiment ahead of most of the delivery work any of you will inherit. The failure is one layer up, in the transmission, and it is the more dangerous failure precisely because the underlying work was good. **A missing baseline announces itself. A renamed one does not**, and the renamed version is the one that travels, because it is the one on the documentation page a customer will read.

**Two further properties of that experiment belong in your notes, because they are the ones you will have to imitate.** The judge is a language model, and **no agreement rate between it and human raters is published**; and **no interval, variance or run count accompanies any figure**, so nothing in the report states whether the difference could be distinguished from noise. Both are the same absences you will be asked to fill in your own record at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md), which is why they are worth naming here rather than treating as somebody else's problem. **A substantial part of the evaluation corpus was constructed by the authors**, and the scoring method is deferred by reference to an earlier post that was **not read for this repository.**

**And the most useful findings in the whole experiment are the unheadlined ones**, which is normal and worth expecting. The gains concentrate on questions that need several sources rather than one. Federating public web content fills gaps in a private corpus. And how a knowledge source is *named* moves how often the system selects the right one — a cosmetic-looking property that turns out to be operational. **None of those made the sentence anybody quotes**, and none of their magnitudes is reproduced here.

**You may not state the figure.** Not in a deliverable, not in a slide, not in this programme. **The rule is not that the number is wrong** — it is a real measurement of a real comparison. The rule is that **a number detached from its baseline is a claim about whatever the reader assumes the baseline was**, and this specimen exists to show that the detaching happens on the vendor's own site, between two of its own pages, in the space of one click. **Teach the mechanism and the fact that a number exists. Never the number.**

**The limits on this source are heavy and it must not be pooled.** Two different kinds of material sit inside it. The product documentation is **definitional** — it establishes what the system is and requires, and contains no measurement of anything. The engineering evaluation is **evidential but weak** — the baseline is the same system at a lower setting, a substantial part of the corpus was author-constructed, the judge is a model with no reported human agreement, and no figure carries an interval. **Definitional authority is not evidential authority**, and pooling them is precisely the error the frequently-asked-questions page commits. **The whole family is the most perishable material in this repository and the only entry whose *names* are more perishable than its capabilities**; substantial parts were not read, including the earlier post the scoring method defers to; the charts were not retrievable, so anything visible only in a chart is unread; and **every number in it is forbidden downstream.**

## The workflow

**Capture a baseline you could defend in six months, on the evaluation surface, before you change anything.**

**Step 1 — write the disconfirming result first, in the file, before you look at any output.** The value that would mean this did not work. It goes at the top of the record because a threshold written after the numbers arrive is a description of the numbers.

**Step 2 — choose the split strategy and write down why.** This is the unit of analysis and it silently determines what every later number means. If your claim is about task completion, splitting at the last user message will not measure it. State the choice and the question it answers in the same line.

**Step 3 — freeze the dataset.** Write the evaluation set as JSONL and commit it. Simple string fields where the interaction is straightforward; conversation arrays where tools are involved, because the trajectory evaluators need the structured output and not only the text.

**Step 4 — run deterministic checks before judged ones.** Local checks run without API calls and cost nothing but time; judged evaluators cost money and carry biases nobody has published a calibration for. Establish what you can establish cheaply, then add the judged layer knowingly.

**Step 5 — set repetitions above one.** One sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement, and the documentation says so.

**Step 6 — commit the record with the pin attached.** Commit or version, host, model, evaluator set, thresholds with a written reason for each, dataset hash, date.

```yaml
# .evidence/baseline.yaml   <- SHAPE, NOT SCHEMA. Verify every product key separately.
measure: "Proportion of submitted claims routed to the correct queue on first attempt."
period: "2026-04-01/2026-06-30"           # a PERIOD, never the word 'before'
instrument: "Queue routing table export, owned by operations. Access ends with the engagement."
instrument_survives_engagement: false     # if false, say what replaces it

disconfirming_result: >                   # WRITTEN BEFORE ANY OUTPUT WAS EXAMINED
  A first-attempt routing proportion at or below the recorded period value, or any
  rise in manual re-routing volume, means this did not work.

unit_of_analysis:
  split_strategy: "full"                  # last-turn | full | per-turn
  because: "The claim is about completing a routing task, not about one reply."

run:
  repetitions: 5                          # one sample is not a measurement
  local_checks: ["queue_id_present", "no_raw_identifier_leak"]
  judged_evaluators: ["task_adherence", "tool_call_accuracy"]
  thresholds:
    tool_call_accuracy: { justification: "INHERITED DEFAULT - NOT JUSTIFIED" }

pin: { host: "<client+version>", model: "<model+version>", dataset_sha: "<sha>", date: "<YYYY-MM-DD>" }

confounders_in_period:                    # listed, not analysed - analysis is S067
  - "Two experienced routing staff left in May."
  - "A queue was merged on 12 May."
  - "Seasonal volume peak overlaps the second half of the period."

no_baseline_available: null               # if there is none, replace the record with this
```

**Step 7 — read one vendor claim back to its own experiment before you cite it.** Any published figure you are about to put in a deliverable gets this treatment: open the citation, find what the comparison was actually made against, and write the single sentence the experiment supports. If that sentence differs from the one on the page you were reading, **the difference is the finding and it goes in the record.** This takes minutes and needs no tenant.

```yaml
# .evidence/claims-read-back.yaml   <- SHAPE, NOT SCHEMA.
# One entry per external figure you were tempted to repeat.

- claim_as_printed: "<the sentence on the page you were reading, verbatim>"
  printed_where: "<page + date you read it>"
  cites: "<the linked study, post or report>"
  did_i_open_it: true                 # if false, STOP. you may not use the claim.

  baseline_the_experiment_actually_used: >
    <what the new thing was compared AGAINST. this is the whole exercise.>
    # for the worked example in the material: THE SAME PRODUCT AT A LOWER SETTING.

  baseline_the_printed_sentence_implies: >
    <what a reader would assume it was compared against>
    # for the worked example: A DIFFERENT ARCHITECTURE ENTIRELY. never run.

  sentence_the_experiment_supports: >
    <one sentence. yours. no adjectives.>

  what_would_have_to_be_measured_to_support_the_printed_sentence: >
    <name the missing arm. it is usually a whole system nobody built.>

  precision_reported:
    interval_or_variance: false        # <- if false, the difference may be noise
    run_count: unstated
    judge: "model-judged"
    judge_agreement_with_humans: "NOT PUBLISHED"

  corpus_provenance: "<who built the evaluation set. often the authors.>"

  verdict: cite_the_experiment | cite_neither | cite_the_disagreement
  figure_reproduced_in_our_deliverable: false   # <- this stays false.
  note: >
    a number detached from its baseline is a claim about whatever baseline
    the reader assumes. teach the mechanism and that a number exists.
```

**Step 8 — attack your own baseline.** One prompt to the agent: *argue that this baseline cannot support any later improvement claim, and name the specific observation that would show it.* A second, for the step-7 record: *argue that the sentence I wrote is still broader than the experiment supports, and name the word that carries the excess.* Then read what came back for the objection you had not thought of. Keep what is better than your own reasoning; record what you rejected and why. **Record separately that the threshold marked `NOT JUSTIFIED` is still not justified**, because an inherited default is a policy choice somebody else made about your system.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** That the same stored interaction produces different scores under different split strategies, with nothing in either number announcing which question it answered. Run one trace under two strategies and read both. **You will also observe the thing this session is named after: that the record you have just built took under an hour and could not have been built at all a fortnight later.**

## What good looks like

- **The measure was defined before the system was built**, or the record says plainly that it was not, and when it was defined instead.
- **The instrument is named and belongs to somebody who will still have it later.** A measurement depending on access you will lose is a measurement you cannot repeat.
- **The period is stated as a period.** Not "before the project", not "historically".
- **The split strategy is stated with the question it answers.** A score whose unit of analysis is unrecorded is not reproducible even by you.
- **The disconfirming result is written down, and it was written first.** This is [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s disqualifying condition in a measurement's clothing, and the same test applies: **a result that could never occur is a decoration.**
- **The before and after use the same definition and, wherever possible, the same instrument.** Where they do not, the difference is stated rather than absorbed.
- **Where no baseline is available, the record says so and the claim is downgraded** rather than the baseline being reconstructed from the memory of people with an interest in the answer.
- **The confounders are listed exhaustively enough to be uncomfortable.**

## How it goes wrong

- **The retrospective baseline** — the before state estimated after the fact by the people who built the after state. *Warning sign:* your before figure and your after figure were produced in the same meeting.
- **The instrument you do not own** — the measurement depends on a system the customer does not run or will not share. *Warning sign:* your measurement plan begins with "we would need access to".
- **The unfalsifiable improvement** — no result would have counted against it. *Warning sign:* item 3 is blank, and you did not notice it was blank.
- **The moved measure** — the definition changed between before and after, usually improving. *Warning sign:* the after measure is better instrumented than the before one.
- **The moved instrument** — the definition held and the judge changed underneath it. *Warning sign:* you cannot say which model version scored the baseline.
- **The borrowed baseline** — a published figure from another domain is used as the before value. *Warning sign:* your baseline has a citation and your after value has a dataset.
- **The measure nobody had** — you are the first person ever to measure this, and you are presenting it as an improvement. *Warning sign:* your before column is empty and your after column is full, and the document reads as a success.
- **The reconstructed memory** — the baseline comes from an interview conducted after the result. *Warning sign:* your source for the before state is a person rather than a record.

## Exercises

**None of these is assessed.** The baseline record is carried into [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) and defended at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md), where it is one of the two artefacts the occasion is built on.

**1 · Sort five measurements by deferability.** For each, say whether it can still be taken after the change ships, and what is destroyed if it cannot.

- *Median response latency.* · *Monthly token spend by team.* · *Proportion of tickets resolved without escalation, before the assistant existed.* · *User-reported satisfaction.* · *The correctness of the routing table on the day you arrived.*

> **Model answers.** Latency and token spend are deferrable — their subject persists and both can be captured whenever you decide to look. Satisfaction is deferrable in form and not in substance: you can survey next month, but you cannot survey the population as it was before it had the tool, so the *pre-change* value is not deferrable even though the *instrument* is. The third and fifth are not deferrable at all; their subject is destroyed by the change. **The general rule: ask what the measurement's subject is, then ask whether shipping destroys it. If it does, this is your only chance, and the cost of missing it is a claim you can never make.**

**2 · Run one trace under two split strategies.** Using the workflow, evaluate a single stored interaction twice — once split at the last user message, once treating the whole exchange as the response. Record both scores and reconcile them.

> **What you should find.** Two different numbers from one unchanged interaction. **This is not an error and neither score is wrong.** One asks how good the final reply was; the other asks whether the task was completed. **If your two scores are identical, your interaction was probably single-turn, and the exercise has told you something else useful: your split choice was not load-bearing this time, and you should say so rather than leaving it unstated.**

**3 · Go back to your own item 3, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 3 — the result that would make the customer conclude this had not worked. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then write the disconfirming result properly, and write the scenario in which it fires.

> **Criterion.** A disconfirming result passes if a reader can name an observation that would trigger it, on an instrument that exists, within a period you have stated. *"If it doesn't deliver value"* fails on all three. *"If first-attempt routing accuracy over any full month in the engagement is at or below the recorded April-to-June value, on the operations export"* passes. **If you cannot write the scenario, the condition is decoration.**
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**4 · Cost the missing baseline.** Take one claim your engagement will want to make. Write the sentence you would have to say at the closing meeting if the baseline does not exist, and the sentence you could say if it does. Show both to somebody who was not in the session.

> **What to notice.** The honest no-baseline sentence is almost always *we cannot say whether this improved anything; here is what we can say instead.* Most learners find they cannot write it without flinching, and the flinch is the finding. **The downgrade is the professional act. The reconstruction is the failure**, and the reconstruction is easier, faster and more welcome in the room.

**5 · Find the silence.** Using the platform documentation, look for the published agreement between any built-in evaluator and human judgement. Record what you searched and what you found.

> **What you should find.** Nothing. **No agreement rate, no calibration data and no threshold rationale is published for any evaluator**, and most of them are model-judged. **The correct output of this exercise is a recorded absence with the date you looked**, not an inference that the evaluators are bad. They may be excellent. You have no way to know, and neither does anyone quoting their output at you.

**6 · Interrogate one inherited threshold.** Take a default threshold in your run configuration and write the justification. If you cannot, mark it `INHERITED DEFAULT - NOT JUSTIFIED` and leave it in.

> **Scoring criterion.** A pass either names why that cut point is right for this decision, or admits that it does not. **A threshold is a policy choice separable from the measurement: moving it changes the pass rate without changing the system.** A fail is a justification reverse-engineered from the fact that the current system passes — which is the same defect as the retrospective baseline, one layer up.

**7 · Read a vendor claim back to its own experiment, and write the one sentence it supports.** Take the retrieval-improvement claim described in the material. Open the page that prints it. Open the study it links to. Identify the baseline the experiment actually used. Then write **one sentence** — no adjectives, no hedging — that the experiment supports. Then write what would have to be measured to support the sentence the page printed. **You may not state the figure in either sentence.**

> **What you should find.** That the comparison was made against **the same product at a lower setting of its own dial**, and that the page restates it as an improvement over a different architecture entirely — **a comparison nobody ran.** The correct sentence is about the dial. The printed sentence is about an architecture. **That distance is one click wide, on the vendor's own site, between two of its own pages.** Then notice the two absences you will be held to yourself: the judge is a model with **no published agreement with human raters**, and **no interval, variance or run count accompanies any figure**, so nothing states whether the difference is distinguishable from noise. **The output of this exercise is the sentence and the missing arm, never the number.** A submission containing the figure fails, and it fails for the reason the exercise exists: **a number detached from its baseline is a claim about whatever baseline the reader assumes.**
>
> **And notice which way the failure runs.** A baseline *was* taken here, carefully, by people who reported it honestly and published their per-dataset results rather than only an average. **The failure is in the transmission**, which makes it worse rather than better: **a missing baseline announces itself, and a renamed one does not.** The renamed version is the one that travels, because it is the one printed on the page a customer reads.

## What this does not establish

**Nothing here shows that having a baseline improves any outcome.** No source behind this module measures it. The practice corpus instructs its reader to establish one and never exhibits one being established; it reports no engagement outcome, no evaluation, no comparison and no measurement of any kind.

**The claim that the baseline is non-deferrable is definitional, not empirical.** It follows from what a baseline is. It is not a finding, nobody has tested whether teams that capture baselines produce better engagements, and this session would be dishonest to imply otherwise.

**The platform documentation is definitional and silent where it matters most.** It describes accurately what the tooling computes and publishes no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data and no threshold rationale — so it is no evidence that what it computes is right. It was read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, most agent evaluators are preview, and one consulted page is an explicitly superseded variant.

**The productivity study is small, narrow and declared out of date by its own authors.** Its population is expert maintainers on codebases they know intimately, which is close to a worst case for assistance. Its outcome is self-reported time. **The transferable finding is the direction of the perception error and the fact that direct experience did not correct it. No magnitude from it appears here.**

**The retrieval benchmark is a snapshot and has since become an optimisation target**, which weakens its own zero-shot premise. It licenses the claim that a baseline from an unlike domain is not your baseline. It licenses nothing about which methods are currently best.

**The correction method for a small labelled sample was read from an abstract and a published summary.** Its guarantee is real and its conditions are strict, and no coverage rate, interval width or sample size from it is reproduced here.

**The record's structure — the disconfirming result written first, the instrument's survival recorded, the confounders listed before they are analysed — is this design's invention.** No source proposes it. The argument that a baseline written under those constraints is more defensible than one written without them is an argument about professional accountability, and nobody has tested whether it changes what engineers do.

**The renamed-baseline specimen establishes a discrepancy between two pages, and nothing more.** It shows that a first-party evaluation compared a product against itself at a lower setting and that a first-party page restates the result as a comparison against a different architecture. **It does not establish that the product is not better than that architecture** — it may well be, and the experiment simply does not say. **It does not establish that anybody intended the restatement**, and the likeliest explanation is compression by somebody who did not run the experiment. **And it establishes nothing about how often this happens**, in this vendor's documentation or anybody's; no survey exists and none is asserted. **The specimen's whole value is that it is checkable in minutes by a reader with no tenant and no privileged access**, which is a property of this instance rather than a general claim about vendor material.

**The source it rests on must not be pooled.** Its product documentation is definitional and contains no measurement; its engineering evaluation is evidential but weak — the baseline is the same system turned down, a substantial part of the corpus was author-constructed, the judge is a model with **no published agreement with human raters**, and **no figure carries an interval, a variance or a run count.** Substantial parts of the family were not read, including the earlier post the scoring method defers to; the charts were not retrievable; the layer names are actively churning. **Every number in it is forbidden downstream and none appears in this session.**
