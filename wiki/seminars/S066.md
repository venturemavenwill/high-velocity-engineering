# S066 · The Baseline Nobody Measured

**Module:** [M14](/wiki/modules/M14.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-066](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-066.md)
**Retrieves from:** S003, S022, S033
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

---

## The situation

You are about to claim that the system improved something. To do that you need the state before, and the state before is gone.

Nobody destroyed it. It simply was never recorded, because at the moment it could have been recorded nobody was thinking about measurement — they were thinking about whether the thing could be built. **By the time anybody wants a before, the before has become a memory held by people with an interest in the after.**

The framework this module teaches from instructs its reader to establish a baseline. **It never exhibits one being established.** It catalogues metric definitions and never reports one measured. Its case-studies section is reserved and empty. **There is no worked example available to this programme, and this session says so in its own text rather than leaving it to the paper.**

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. What was the value of your headline measure the week before you arrived, and how do you know?
2. Who measured it, with what instrument, and does that instrument still exist?
3. What result would make the customer conclude this had not worked?
4. What else changed in the same period?
5. If you cannot establish the baseline, what will you say instead?

**Item 3 is missing from the corpus's entire discovery apparatus.** Its question bank asks in every block what good would look like and how improvement would be measured. It asks nowhere what result would mean it had failed. That absence is the reason this item is here.

## What you must produce

**A baseline record, or a written statement that there is not one.**

- **The measure**, defined precisely enough that two people would compute it the same way.
- **The value before**, with the period it covers stated as a period rather than as "before".
- **The instrument**, named, together with whether it still exists and who owns it.
- **Who took the measurement**, and when.
- **The disconfirming result**: the value that would mean this did not work.
- **The confounders present in the period** — listed, not analysed. Analysis is [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md).
- **Where no baseline exists**: a statement saying so, what the claim has been downgraded to, and what the downgrade gives up.

## What good looks like

- **The measure was defined before the system was built**, or the record says plainly that it was not and when it was defined instead.
- **The instrument is named and belongs to somebody who will still have it later.** A measurement that depends on access you will lose is a measurement you cannot repeat.
- **The period is stated.** Not "before the project", not "historically".
- **The disconfirming result is written down.** This is [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s disqualifying condition in a measurement's clothing, and the same test applies: **a result that could never occur is a decoration.**
- **The before and after use the same definition and, wherever possible, the same instrument.** Where they do not, the difference is stated.
- **Where no baseline is available, the record says so and the claim is downgraded** rather than the baseline being reconstructed from the memory of people with an interest in the answer.
- **The confounders are listed exhaustively enough to be uncomfortable.**

## How it goes wrong

- **The retrospective baseline** — the before state estimated after the fact by the people who built the after state. *Warning sign:* your before figure and your after figure were produced in the same meeting.
- **The instrument you do not own** — the measurement depends on a system the customer does not run or will not share. *Warning sign:* your measurement plan begins with "we would need access to".
- **The unfalsifiable improvement** — no result would have counted against it. *Warning sign:* item 3 is blank, and you did not notice it was blank.
- **The moved measure** — the definition changed between before and after, usually improving. *Warning sign:* the after measure is better instrumented than the before one.
- **The baseline that is a target** — the before value is what somebody said it should have been. *Warning sign:* the figure is round.
- **The measure nobody had** — you are the first person ever to measure this, and you are presenting it as an improvement. *Warning sign:* your before column is empty and your after column is full, and the document reads as a success.
- **The reconstructed memory** — the baseline comes from an interview conducted after the result. *Warning sign:* your source for the before state is a person rather than a record.

## Do it

**First, write the measure down and hand it to somebody to compute.** If they compute it differently from you, the definition is not finished and the baseline you take with it will not be comparable to anything.

**Second, go looking for a record rather than for a person.** Tickets, logs, invoices, rotas, exports, anything generated by the work rather than about it. **A record made for another purpose is better evidence than a recollection made for this one**, because nobody was trying to make it come out any particular way.

**Third, write the disconfirming result before you look at any data.** What value would mean this had not worked. Take it to the customer and ask whether they agree — **and if they will not name one, that is the finding**, and it belongs in your record.

**Fourth, list what else changed.** A reorganisation, a policy change, a seasonal pattern, a new hire, a price change, training you delivered. **List it even where you believe it is irrelevant**, because you are not the person who gets to decide that and [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) will need the list.

**Fifth, if there is no baseline, say so and downgrade.** The honest forms are available and they are weaker than a causal claim: a description of the current state, a comparison against a stated expectation, an observation with the confounders named. **Choose one, say what it gives up, and do not reconstruct.**

## Show it

Give your baseline record to somebody who was not there and ask them one question: **could you have taken this measurement yourself, from what is written here?**

Then ask the customer the disconfirming question directly. **What would have told you this had not worked?**

Their answer, or their refusal to give one, goes into the record either way. **A customer who will not name a disconfirming result has told you something about how the result will be read**, and that is worth more than the figure you were going to collect.

## What this does not establish

**Nothing here shows that a baseline improves a decision, or that engagements with baselines produce better outcomes than those without.** No source available to this programme supplies evidence of either.

**More sharply: this session teaches a procedure for which its own source contains no instance.** The framework it draws on instructs the reader to establish a baseline, never exhibits one being established, catalogues metrics and never reports one measured, and reserves an empty case-studies section. **The procedure taught here is therefore assembled rather than observed**, and it has not been run against a real engagement by anybody in this programme.

**The disconfirming-result requirement is written past the source.** The corpus's question bank contains no question asking what result would mean the work had failed. Adding one is this design's decision, and the argument for it is the same argument [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) made about disqualifying conditions — **an argument, not a finding.**

**And the downgrade this session requires is professionally costly in a way it cannot compensate for.** A described state is a weaker deliverable than a claimed improvement, and this programme has no evidence that anybody is rewarded for producing one.

## Perishable content in this session

| Durable claim | Perishable instance |
|---|---|
| A before state reconstructed after the after state is not a baseline | Whichever records your customer's current systems happen to retain |
| A measure whose definition moved between before and after has measured the definition | Present metric definitions in whatever framework you were handed |
| An instrument you will lose access to is an instrument you cannot repeat | This year's data-sharing arrangements and their expiry |
| A claim with no disconfirming value is a decoration | Current commercial expectations about what a result document says |
