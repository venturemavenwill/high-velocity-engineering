# S006 · Constraints You Cannot See Yet

**Module:** [M02](/wiki/modules/M02.md) · **Quarter:** [Q1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md) · **Complexity class:** 2
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-006](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-006.md)
**Retrieves from:** S003, S005
**Entry state:** either

## The situation

Every engagement arrives with a list of things that cannot change. Almost none of them has been tested.

Some are genuinely immovable — a regulator's requirement, a contract already signed, a system nobody has the source for. Some were immovable three years ago. Some were never immovable at all and are simply the shape of an argument nobody wanted to have.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. Name three things about this engagement that cannot change. For each, write who told you.
2. Which of the three has anybody actually tested this year?
3. What would you have to ask, and of whom, to find out that one of them has moved?
4. Name one constraint you have been treating as immovable because moving it would be somebody else's argument to have.
5. If a problem space were entirely frozen, what would be left?

**Item 5 has an answer, and refusing to find it is the failure this session exists to name.** Constraint is not a licence to stop.

## What you must produce

A **frozen/fluid register**: every constraint you can name, classified by how much it can actually move, with the source of the belief and the specific test that would settle it.

## What good looks like

- Every entry names a person as the source, not a document. **A constraint whose only source is a document is a constraint nobody currently holds.**
- At least one item is reclassified from frozen to fluid, with the reasoning shown.
- At least one item is genuinely frozen, and the consequence for scope is stated rather than lamented.
- Untested entries are marked untested and carry a named next action, not a hope.

## How it goes wrong

Four failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **Inherited freeze** — a constraint is immovable because it was immovable on the last engagement. *Warning sign:* the source of the belief is "everyone knows" or a person who has left.
- **Learned helplessness** — the space is declared entirely frozen and the work quietly stops. *Warning sign:* your fluid column is empty and your register reads as an explanation.
- **The convenient freeze** — the items you filed as immovable are precisely those whose testing would require an uncomfortable conversation. *Warning sign:* the frozen list and the list of people you have not contacted are the same list.
- **The untested thaw** — a constraint is declared fluid because that would be convenient. *Warning sign:* the test you wrote for it is "ask and see".

## Do it

Work from [Brief A](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md), where the record's field structure is described by everyone as set by a regulator and by one person as inherited from a system that no longer exists. Classify it, name your source, and write the test that would settle it.

Method is yours. Nobody will tell you what counts as sufficient evidence that a constraint has moved, and **the choice of test is the judgement being exercised** — a constraint settled by asking the person who told you is not settled.

## Show it

Read out your frozen list to somebody who then reads back only the sources. Anywhere the source is a document, a departed colleague or a consensus, mark the entry untested.

Count what is left. That, and not the original list, is what you actually know cannot move.

## What this does not establish

The frozen/fluid classification comes from a body of professional practice that offers no evidence for it whatever — no engagement in which it was applied, no comparison against working without it, and no record of a constraint it correctly reclassified. It is the most transferable original idea in that corpus and it is entirely unwarranted.

**The accompanying rule — that even in an almost entirely frozen space there is something you can change — is an exhortation, not a finding.** It is included because the failure it addresses is real and observable, and because a practice with no answer to a hard constraint produces engineers who treat difficulty as a reason.
