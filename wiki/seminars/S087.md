# S087 · Engineering Feedback That Goes Somewhere

## The situation

Three pieces of friction with the tooling were found during this engagement. Two were written up and filed. Nobody can say what happened to either. The third was never written, because the engineer who hit it left that week.

There is a published standard for how to write one of these. It specifies a title, a summary, a categorisation block naming the product area, the kind of problem, the stage of work it was hit at, its impact and its time frame, an ordinal priority, then steps to reproduce it and any notes. It is a good standard and the fields are the right fields.

**It describes no routing, no triage, no ownership, no service-level expectation and no closure loop, anywhere.** It does not claim to. It teaches an engineer how to write a report a product team could act on, and stops.

Answer these in writing before the session opens.

1. Write the third report — the one that was never written. Whose knowledge is now unavailable, and what did it cost?
2. For the two that were filed: what would tell you the report was never read? Name the observable.
3. Who receives a report of this kind in your organisation, and what is their obligation on receiving it?
4. What is the difference between a report that a product team can act on and one that a product team will act on?
5. If nobody ever acts on any of them, what should an engineer do differently — and is *stop writing them* on your list of answers?

**Item 5 is the honest one.** A writing standard with nothing behind it is a request for unpaid work from the person who hit the problem, and the person who hits the problem knows that. Any loop you design has to survive that reading.

## What you must produce

Two things, and the second is the session's work.

- **One feedback item**, written to the published standard, about something this engagement actually hit. Reproducible, specific, and about a defect or a friction rather than about a preference.
- **The routing the standard omits**: who receives it, what they are obliged to do and by when, who owns it after that, what states it can be in, what closes it, and what tells the original reporter that any of this happened.

## What good looks like

- The report is reproducible by somebody who was not there. **A report that cannot be reproduced is a complaint with fields.**
- It is specific enough to be assessed, implemented or refuted. A generic report is worse than an absent one, for exactly the reason a generic mitigation is: nothing can be done with it and nobody can say it was wrong.
- **The routing names a person or a role, not a queue.** A queue is where a report goes to be counted.
- There is a state the report can be in that means *seen and declined*, with a reason. A loop with no legitimate way to say no is a loop that will be quietly ignored, in exactly the way a compliance regime with no approvable exception is.
- The reporter learns the outcome. **If the only observable difference between a report that was acted on and one that was never opened is on the product team's side, the loop does not exist.**
- The design says what happens to the report when its author leaves, because in this engagement that already happened once.

## How it goes wrong

Five failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **The report as the loop** — a well-written item is filed and the mechanism is considered to exist. *Warning sign:* your account of the process ends with the word *submitted*.
- **Volume as evidence** — the number of reports filed becomes the measure, and their fate is not measured at all. *Warning sign:* somebody can tell you how many were raised this quarter.
- **The unreproducible report** — real friction, badly captured, closed as not reproducible, and the reporter concludes the loop is theatre. *Warning sign:* your steps assume the reader has your environment.
- **The report nobody may decline** — every item must be actioned, so the receiving team stops reading them. *Warning sign:* your design has no state for a considered no.
- **The knowledge that left with the person** — the friction was real, the engineer moved on, and nothing captured it. *Warning sign:* the best story about your tooling this engagement is being told verbally.

## Do it

Work from the supplied situation in [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md), and from whatever your own engagement actually hit — the thing at [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) you could not explain is a good candidate, and so is anything from [S063](/wiki/seminars/S063.md).

Write the item first, to the standard, without improving on the standard. **Doing it exactly as specified is how you find out what the specification leaves out**, and it is a faster route to that than reading criticism of it.

Then design the loop. Six questions, answered in order: who receives, what is owed on receipt, who owns it afterwards, what states exist, what closes it, and what the reporter sees. Where you cannot answer one for your own organisation, write *unknown* rather than inventing an answer — **an honest blank is the finding, and inventing a process you have never seen is the failure mode this session is about.**

## Show it

Read your report's reproduction steps to somebody who was not on your team, and have them tell you whether they could follow them.

Then state your loop's weakest link in one sentence, and who would have to agree to it. **Almost every loop of this kind fails at the same point, and it is not the writing.**

## What this does not establish

**That any of this reaches a product.** The field set is verified and public. The loop is not described anywhere, by anybody, in any of the material behind this programme — and the source that specifies the fields does not claim to describe one. **This session is written into that absence**, and inferring a practice from an omission is a weak move that this programme has now made in three separate stages.

**The source's own currency is uneven and visible.** Its pages are individually dated and the dates run across several years without any signal in its navigation that distinguishes a maintained page from an abandoned one. **The feedback guidance page carries the oldest date on the whole site.** That is not a criticism of the standard; it is the single most relevant fact about it, and a reader who cites the site rather than the page will not have noticed.

**And no outcome is reported for any of it.** Nothing establishes that structured feedback of this kind changes a product, that a routed report is acted on more often than an unrouted one, or that an engineer who receives an outcome writes more reports afterwards. Every one of those is plausible and none is evidenced.
