# S087 · The Safety Case

**Module:** [M18](wiki/modules/M18.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](wiki/quarters/Q9.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** SG · ME · SY
**Whitepaper:** [WP-087](wiki/whitepapers/WP-087.md)
**Depends on:** S024, S068, S076, S077, S078, S079
**Re-tests:** S044
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Foundry observability — agent tracing into the one Application Insights resource connected to the project — as the store every evidence item in the case is produced from · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read) · Foundry MCP Server (preview) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

Yesterday's question was what a receiving team can do. Today's is what they are entitled to believe.

A receiving organisation will ask, in one form or another, *is this safe.* The engineering answer to that question is not a test report, a dashboard, a certification or a green build. It is an **argument**: a claim about a specific use in a specific context, the reasoning that connects evidence to the claim, the evidence itself with its own error structure, and — the part that carries the weight and is always the part that is missing — **the assumptions under which the argument holds.**

The students already own every piece of this. [S004](wiki/seminars/S004.md) established that a proof is a claim addressed to a reader entitled to disbelieve you. [S009](wiki/seminars/S009.md) established that passing is weak evidence and failing is strong. [S024](wiki/seminars/S024.md) established the duty to say so when the answer is that it cannot be done. [S044](wiki/seminars/S044.md) built the threat model of a learned component. [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) produced a detection design and, more importantly, an explicit list of what the student decided not to detect. [S076](wiki/seminars/S076.md)–[S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md) supplied the adversary who is one of the case's assumptions and the drift that erodes it.

What they do not own is the assembly, and they hold a belief that actively obstructs it: **that a safety argument is made stronger by being made broader.** It is made weaker. A wider claim requires more evidence and gets less, and the resulting case is one whose assumptions nobody wrote down because nobody could enumerate the conditions of an unbounded claim.

There is a second thing they do not own, and today is where they find out. **Every evidence item in a case has a command that produces it, and a case whose evidence cannot be produced on demand is a case about the past.** The substrate's traces live in one Application Insights resource, the portal shows ninety days of them, and the retention behind that is a setting somebody chose. A student who writes *twelve months of clean traces* into a safety case is going to be asked to show them.

The target: **a safety case is a bounded claim, and its strength is in the boundary. State what use, in what context, for which population, until when — and state what would defeat it.**

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Eight items on material not yet taught. Confidence attached. Never recorded.

1. **Write, in one sentence, the safety claim you would make about your own substrate component**, as you would state it to a receiving organisation.
2. **What evidence supports it?** List up to five items.
3. For each item in your list, two columns: **what is its error rate in the direction that would hurt you**, and **what command would produce it in front of a sceptical reader?** Where there is no command, write *none*.
4. **Name one thing that must remain true about the world for your claim to hold**, and say who is responsible for it remaining true.
5. A release gate has passed your change for eleven months. **Is that evidence? Of what?**
6. **What would have to be observed for you to withdraw the claim in item 1?**
7. Predict: **how many of your item 2 evidence items will still be admissible after phase 4?**
8. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to assemble the evidence for your case from the substrate's trace store. Write down what it will produce, and **name the one query it will write that runs, returns rows, and does not show what you asked for.** Sealed with items 1 to 7 and opened at phase 8.

Item 3 is the day's honest item and the one most students will not be able to answer for most of their list. Its second column is expected to collapse the list further: **an evidence item nobody can produce is an evidence item nobody has checked.** Item 5 is the payload's pretest.

**Warrant.** Pretesting the whole artefact in miniature before any framework exists, so that phase 4 corrects a claim the student has already written rather than an abstraction. Items 2 and 3 are deliberately adjacent: the evidence list is easy and neither of item 3's columns is, and the three lengths together are the day's opening finding. Item 7 is a numeric self-prediction resolved four hours later. Item 8 is the day's contribution to the running frontier map, is sealed at phase 1, and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8: an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups of four receive **four safety cases written for the same substrate change**, by four different authors, at the same time, before the change was released. Each is one to three pages. **What happened afterwards is withheld.**

**Each group then tries to produce the evidence.** Every case cites its evidence; the group has read access to the substrate's Foundry project, its connected Application Insights resource and its release history, and twenty minutes to establish, for each cited item in each case, whether it can be produced now. Three outcomes are recorded per item: **produced**, **produced and does not say what the case says it says**, and **not available**. The results are written down before the outcome records appear.

**The sampling is disclosed, per instrumentation rule 3.** These four were written as a deliberate exercise during a prior cohort's operation of the substrate, not found in the wild; the authors knew they were being compared; and the change was chosen by staff because its outcome was informative. Groups are told this before they read, and are told that a genuine corpus of four independent safety cases for one change does not exist anywhere the design could obtain it. **The evidence-production exercise carries its own disclosure**: the substrate's Foundry portal shows traces from the last ninety days and the retention behind that is whatever the connected Application Insights resource is configured for, so a case written more than a quarter ago is being tested partly against a retention policy and not only against its own quality.

- **C1 · The evidence case.** Eleven pages of appendix in three pages of body. Every test, every metric, every review. **No sentence connects any of it to a claim**, and the claim itself appears once, in the title, unbounded: *the component is safe for production use.*
- **C2 · The structured case.** A clean claim-argument-evidence decomposition, properly nested, each leaf supported. Its principal evidence is the release gate: eleven months of passes. **The gate's error structure is nowhere in the document, and it was never characterised.**
- **C3 · The assumption case.** Every assumption stated, numbered, and traceable. All of them are about the environment — input rates, upstream availability, data distribution. **None is about the operator and none is about an adversary.**
- **C4 · The short one.** One page. The claim is narrower than any of the other three: *safe for the internal reporting path, for the current user population, under the current authorisation model, until the next authentication change or six months, whichever is first.* Five pieces of evidence, each with its error direction named. Three assumptions, one of them about the on-call rota. One paragraph headed *what would defeat this*.

Groups rank the four on *which claim they would be willing to sign* and **commit before the outcome is supplied.** Then the outcome is supplied.

The payload is **C4**, and what defeats the room's expectation is not that it is short. It is that **it is the only one that is still true.** The change is still running. C1's claim was falsified within a fortnight by a use nobody had excluded. C2's gate was found, at [S066](wiki/seminars/S066.md)'s validation, to have an uncharacterised false-pass rate, which retrospectively removed its case's principal evidence. C3 survived until an operator did something its assumption set had no vocabulary for. C4's boundary excluded all three failures in advance, and it excluded them by claiming less.

**The evidence-production results are the phase's second finding and they arrive before the outcome.** C1's eleven pages of appendix reproduce almost entirely, because they are test artefacts and tests can be re-run. C2's gate record reproduces perfectly and says nothing about false passes, because a gate that wrongly passes leaves no row. C3's environment assumptions cannot be produced at all — nothing in the trace store records what the upstream service was doing. **C4's five items all produce, and each one produces the thing the case says it produces.** That is not an accident of C4's brevity; it is what selecting a claim against the evidence you can actually generate looks like from the outside.

Task after: **for each case, name the claim's four boundaries — use, context, population, time — or state which are absent.** Then: **for each case, how much of your ranking survives the evidence-production result?** Then: **what is C4 unable to be used for?**

The last question has an answer and the day needs it. C4 cannot be used to argue that the component is safe for a new use, and its author would have to write a new case rather than extend this one. That is a real cost, it falls on whoever wants the new use, and a receiving organisation that does not understand it will read the narrow case as an obstruction.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases matched on change, on organisation and on the moment of writing, varying only the argument's construction, with a real outcome as adjudicator and the corpus's artificiality disclosed. Commit-before-outcome makes the ranking a generation act. **The evidence-production exercise is the day's technical payload arriving as an observation rather than as point 3**: a group that has just watched a gate record reproduce perfectly and say nothing about false passes does not need to be told that evidence inherits its own error structure. The payload defeats the specific belief the day exists to defeat — that a safety argument is strengthened by breadth — and the final question bounds the payload before the cohort spends three days over-applying it, which is the same discipline [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) phase 3 used on H4.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **What a safety case is.** A structured argument that a system is **acceptably safe for a stated use in a stated context**, comprising a claim, an argument connecting evidence to the claim, the evidence, and the assumptions under which the connection holds. Five parts, and the two that get dropped are the context and the assumptions. **The four-boundary form — use, context, population, time — is the minimum this design requires and is not the only structure in use; formal argument notations exist and carry more. Safe as the form of a case for a component you operate and are handing over; unsafe as a substitute for a regulated safety argument in a domain with a statutory form, where the notation is prescribed and this one will not be accepted.**

2. **"Acceptably" has an owner, and it is not you.** [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 2 applied to a safety claim: the person who decides what is acceptable is the person who bears the cost of being wrong. An engineer who writes *acceptably safe* without naming whose acceptance it is has made a decision that was not theirs — [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 3's first symmetric error, in written form and with the author absent when it is read.

3. **Evidence inherits its own error structure, and the case inherits it too.** This is C2's defect and it is the day's most consequential technical point. A release gate cited as evidence is an evaluation artefact with a construct, a criterion, a judge and two error rates, one of which — the false pass — leaves no record ([S066](wiki/seminars/S066.md)). **A case that cites a gate whose error structure was never characterised has cited eleven months of an unmeasured quantity.** Every evidence item in a case must carry the direction and, where available, the magnitude of the error that would hurt the claim, in the same field as the item, per specification rule 2.

4. **A detection commitment is a claim.** [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s four-part target — what you will know, within what time, observed by whom, at what false-alarm rate — is a promise about the future that the reader cannot check. A case whose argument contains *and we would notice* has made that promise load-bearing, and it must appear as a claim with its own evidence, not as a reassurance in a sentence. **The promise's own failure case — what happens if the signal dies — is part of it**, which is [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s finding and [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s artefact item 4.

5. **Assumptions come in three kinds and one of them is always missing.** Environment: the world outside the system behaves as it did. Operator: the people running it do what they are expected to do and not what they are able to do. Adversary: nobody is trying. C3 had a complete environment set and nothing else. **The adversary assumption is not a security appendix; it is a premise of the argument, and [S076](wiki/seminars/S076.md)–[S078](wiki/seminars/S078.md) supply its content. This list of three is the decomposition that catches the errors students make; it omits assumptions about the organisation itself — funding, staffing, whether the receiving team still exists — which are real, are frequently the first to fail, and are [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md)'s material.**

6. **The duty to say so applies to the case.** [S024](wiki/seminars/S024.md), generalised again. Where the evidence does not support a claim the receiving organisation needs, the professional act is to say that the case cannot be made, and to say what would let it be made. **The duty does not apply where making the statement is itself the harm** — a case that cannot be made because of a live, exploitable weakness is disclosed to a party who can fix it, on a timetable, not published to the receiving organisation's general readership. That exception is real, is narrow, is the most abused clause in professional practice, and is [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md) point 8's, where the conditions on it are stated.

7. **Defeaters.** A case is defeated in three ways, and naming them is what makes a case reviewable rather than merely readable. **Rebutting**: evidence that the claim is false. **Undermining**: evidence that a piece of the case's evidence is unsound — C2's gate. **Undercutting**: evidence that the inference from evidence to claim does not hold, even though both are fine — the eleven months of passes were real and the population changed. **The three are the classes this design uses; they are not a complete logic of defeat, and the boundary between undermining and undercutting is arguable in specific cases. Safe as a checklist for reviewing your own case before somebody else does; unsafe as a formal taxonomy to argue from.**

8. **A case expires.** C4's boundary includes *until the next authentication change or six months, whichever is first*, and that clause is not caution — it is the expiry condition [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md) point 5 named and [WP-012](wiki/whitepapers/WP-012.md) identified as a competence this programme assesses. **A safety case with no expiry is a claim about all future states of a system you are about to stop watching.** The expiry must name an event as well as a date, because the event is what actually invalidates it and the date is what makes somebody look.

9. **Your evidence lives somewhere, and the somewhere has terms.** On this substrate every agent trace goes to **one** Application Insights resource, connected to the Foundry project; the project decides where telemetry is collected and the Application Insights resource is where it lives and where it is queried. Four properties of that arrangement are load-bearing for a case and none of them is about your component. **The Foundry portal shows the last ninety days**, and the actual retention is whatever the connected Application Insights and Log Analytics configuration says, which is a different number set by a different person. **Reading it requires a role** — Log Analytics Reader on the resource, and Privileged Monitoring Data Reader as well where the tables are protected — so a receiving organisation that holds your case and not that role holds an argument they cannot check. **The traces can contain user inputs, model outputs and tool arguments**, which the documentation tells you to treat as production telemetry under the same access controls and retention policy as any other log, so the evidence for your safety claim is itself a data-protection object. And **the whole of it is one resource**: delete the resource group and the evidence goes with the system it was evidence about. **This is perishable in every particular and durable in one: evidence has a custodian, and a case that does not name the custodian has cited something it does not control.**

10. **Defaults are opinions, and three of today's decide what your evidence contains.** Tracing turns on for a project the moment an Application Insights resource is connected, with no code change — which is convenient and means **nobody decided to start collecting**. Spans arrive **without input and output content** unless `OTEL_INSTRUMENTATION_GENAI_CAPTURE_MESSAGE_CONTENT` is set to true, so a case that says *we can see what the model was asked* is making a claim about a setting rather than about a system, and the students will find out which they have during phase 5. And registering an agent that runs outside Foundry, so that its spans join the same store, is a **preview** capability that requires every span to carry an agent-identifying attribute — which means the completeness of your evidence is bounded by a preview feature and by whether somebody set an attribute. **An engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system**, and a safety case is the document where that failure becomes somebody else's problem.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to the four cases, with the room's ranking shown against the outcome. Points 1, 5 and 7 carry the scope statements required by the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), two in strong form; point 6 carries the normative-claim exception and names where its conditions are stated. Point 3 is the day's technical payload and is the moment [WP-066](wiki/whitepapers/WP-066.md) §6 named for this day: the gate the cohort built at S066 becomes, here, a defect they can name in somebody else's argument and then find in their own. Point 4 is [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s detection design arriving as an argumentative commitment rather than an operational one. **Points 9 and 10 are the day's perishable platform content and its named defaults, required by the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)**, and they are placed after the expiry point deliberately: a student who has just been told that a case expires is in the right position to be told that its evidence expires on a schedule somebody else set. **Point 9 carries the day's documentation silence** — the ninety-day portal window and the configurable retention are both stated, and nothing anywhere states a sampling rate, so no case may claim that its trace evidence is complete.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 6. The claim, its boundary and its assumptions are the student's, and the boundary is the judgement.**

Each student takes **the substrate component they chose at [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)** and constructs its safety case.

1. **State the claim, bounded on all four axes** — use, context, population, time. The time boundary must name an event and a date. **The claim must be selected against [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md)'s pooled record-gap list** — the cohort's own inventory of what this substrate does not record — so that the case rests on quantities the substrate can actually evidence, and any claim that requires a quantity on that list is either rewritten or carried with the gap named.
2. **List the evidence, in two passes.** First **enumerate everything you considered** — including the operator account, the design review, the incident you remember and the thing somebody told you — and keep that list. Then, for each item you carry forward: what it is, what it establishes, **the direction and magnitude of the error that would hurt the claim, in the same field**, and **the command that produces it.** Where the magnitude is unknown, write *uncharacterised*. Where there is no command, write *not reproducible*. **Both are admissible; neither is admissible silently; and an item that leaves the case between the two passes is recorded as having left, with why.** The enumeration is working material and is not the deliverable.
3. **Build the evidence bundle and ship it.** Using Copilot in agent mode through the Foundry and Azure MCP servers, produce **one prompt file in the substrate's repository that regenerates every reproducible item in step 2**, and run it. It is merged, not drafted. **An item that the bundle cannot produce is marked *not reproducible* in step 2 and stays in the case**; the bundle's job is to make the case checkable by a reader who is not you, not to make the case look complete.
4. **Write the argument** connecting evidence to claim. One paragraph per inference step, in the receiving organisation's language rather than yours.
5. **State the assumptions in three sets** — environment, operator, adversary — and for each, **name who is responsible for it remaining true.** An assumption with no responsible party is recorded as having none, per [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 8.
6. **State the detection commitments the case relies on**, in [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s four-part form, including what happens if the signal itself fails. **No detection commitment may rest on an alert whose named recipient stated, at [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md) step 7, a condition under which they would mute it — unless that condition appears in the case as a stated limit on the commitment.** A promise to notice, made on a signal its recipient has already told you they would silence, is a claim about somebody else's behaviour that they have contradicted in writing.
7. **Write the defeaters:** name one rebutting, one undermining and one undercutting observation that would defeat this case, and state which of them you would currently notice.
8. **Carry the claim you lost.** From [S082](wiki/seminars/S082.md) step 6: **one claim you raised and were overruled on**, stated here as a claim the case does not make, with the record of the raising and the overruling party's grounds as they gave them. Then **run the reduction** — take the narrowest claim in step 1 you are confident in, and write the sentence you would say to the receiving organisation if they asked for a broader one, per [S024](wiki/seminars/S024.md) point 5's reply form.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent to build the bundle, in order, including the ones you abandoned. **The instruction in which you told it what the evidence was supposed to show is the one the assessors will ask about**, because that is where a student's belief about their own evidence becomes a written artefact and can be compared against what the query returned.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a bundle that asserts the row count it expects and fails when it does not get it is the obvious example here and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch. Both specific, both from this build.

**The staff member accountable for the substrate is present for the last twenty-five minutes** as the party whose acceptance point 2 requires, and is not obliged to accept any case. Six minutes each.

**Fading:** step 4's inference-step form is supplied as headings; step 6 reuses [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s four-part target verbatim; step 8's reduction reuses [S024](wiki/seminars/S024.md)'s reply form; step 3's bundle has a supplied skeleton naming where prompt files live in the repository. **Steps 1 and 7 are unsupported** — the boundary and the defeaters are the day's whole judgement, and a supplied template for either would produce the boundary the template implies.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 6 on the student's own component, with the acceptance-owner present so that point 2 is exercised rather than asserted. Step 2's same-field error requirement is specification rule 2 applied to a safety argument, and the explicit permission to write *uncharacterised* is what prevents the requirement producing invented numbers — which is the failure mode a mandatory-magnitude rule would create. **Step 2's command column and step 3's bundle are the practical turn's contribution and they are one idea in two places**: an evidence item is a claim about what a query would return, and the cheapest way to find out whether a student holds that claim or has inherited it is to make them run it. The permission to write *not reproducible* is the exact counterpart of *uncharacterised* and exists for the same reason — a mandatory-reproducibility rule would produce cases that cite only what is easy to regenerate, which is a worse case. Step 7 is unsupported because a supplied defeater list would be answered rather than generated, and generating a defeater against your own case is the same act [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s artefact item 8 required and is its class-6 written form. **Three inbound rule-6 elements sit in this task: step 1's selection against the [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md) record-gap list, step 6's [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md) mute-condition limit, and step 8's [S082](wiki/seminars/S082.md) overruled claim.** All three are placed inside the artefact rather than beside it, because each is a constraint on what the case may say and a constraint recorded outside the case does not constrain it.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: **a safety argument on this substrate that was correct and was read as saying more than it said.** The case bounded its claim to one user population; the receiving reader took the bounded claim, quoted the claim sentence without its boundary in a decision record, and the decision was made for a different population. Attention is directed at two facts: **the boundary was in the document, one line below the sentence that was quoted**, and the person who quoted it was not careless — they were reading for the answer to their question, which is what readers do.

Then the agent case: **a Copilot-written evidence query that was confidently wrong, ran, returned rows, and passed review.** A student asked the agent for the agent-inventory evidence their case needed. It produced a well-formed Advanced Hunting query against the `AIAgentsInfo` table — the table the documentation states is being replaced by `AgentsInfo` and deprecated as part of the 1 July 2026 transition to Agent 365. **The query ran. It returned rows. Every row was true.** It was reviewed by a second student who checked the syntax, checked the join, checked the filter, and had no reason to check whether the table was the one that would still exist when the case's reader read it. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the reviewer verified everything about the query except the only thing that was going to change**, and the case's expiry clause did not name it because the author did not know it was a dependency.

Then students, in threes: **a claim you have made this year whose boundary you could not now state.**

The firewall applies and is stated: nothing surfaced here reaches any assessment judgement about the student who surfaced it.

**Warrant.** Error management targeting the interface between this day and [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md). The instructor case is chosen because nobody in it is dishonest and nobody is incompetent, which is the condition the clinic has required since [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md), and because the failure is a placement failure rather than a content failure — the same structure as [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md) phase 7's case, deliberately, at class 6 and with the author permanently absent. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen because it is an undermining defeater arriving from outside the system entirely**: the evidence was sound, the query was sound, and the platform moved. It is also the day's bridge into [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md), where that same transition is the worked example of a guarantee changing hands. The student prompt targets step 1 and is timed after it so that what surfaces is a boundary they have just failed to state on something else.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student's partner receives **the safety case alone** — no evidence appendix, no conversation — and must produce three things in writing before the author speaks:

- **one use they believe this claim covers** that the author did not intend;
- **the assumption they judge most likely to fail first**, and why;
- **one defeater the author did not list.**

The author then states which of the three are correct. Where the partner's first answer identifies a real gap in the boundary, **the claim is revised on the spot** and the revision is recorded.

Per specification rule 3, the three partner answers, the author's adjudication and any revision are elements of the artefact.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with a document-only test, structurally identical to [S034](wiki/seminars/S034.md) phase 7's and [S028](wiki/seminars/S028.md)'s. Per instrumentation rule 2 this is the sixth document-only elicitation in the programme and **its rate is not compared against the earlier five** — it is an intervention here, not a measurement. The first question is the load-bearing one and it is the only construction in the day that tests the boundary from outside: an author cannot find the use they did not exclude, because the reason they did not exclude it is that it did not occur to them.

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- **Item 1's pretest claim returned** against step 1's bounded claim, side by side, by the student. **How many of the room's opening claims had any boundary at all**, reported as a cohort figure.
- **Item 3 resolved, in both columns:** the proportion of the room's phase 1 evidence items that could be given an error direction at the time, against the proportion that carry one at step 2; and the proportion for which a command was named at the time, against the proportion the step 3 bundle actually produces.
- Item 7's predicted admissible-evidence count against the actual, as a distribution without names.
- **Item 8 opened.** The sealed Copilot predictions are read against what the agent actually produced at step 3, and the cohort's hit rate is recorded. **Both halves count**: predicting that a query would run and mislead is easy, and naming how is the measure. This is the day's contribution to the running frontier map.
- The room's phase 3 rankings against the outcome: **how many groups ranked C4 last**, and the most common criterion given; and **how many rankings changed after the evidence-production results and before the outcome records.**
- **The unowned-assumption total:** across the cohort, how many step 5 assumptions have no responsible party. This is the number [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) consumes.
- The step 8 outcomes: **how many cases the accountable staff member declined to accept, and on what grounds.**
- Each student names **one claim they are currently relying on in someone else's system whose boundary they have never read.**

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in SG390 with ME390. Per [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the claim with all four boundaries and a time boundary naming both an event and a date, selected against the [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md) record-gap list with any residual gap named; the evidence list with each item's error direction and magnitude or an explicit *uncharacterised*, and the command that produces it or an explicit *not reproducible*, both in the same field as the item; **the evidence bundle as merged, and its run output**; the argument in inference steps in the receiving organisation's language; the three assumption sets with a responsible party or an explicit none for each; the detection commitments in four-part form including the signal-failure case, with any [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md) mute condition stated as a limit; one rebutting, one undermining and one undercutting defeater with a statement of which would be noticed; the [S082](wiki/seminars/S082.md) overruled claim with the record of the raising and the overruling party's grounds; the step 8 reduction sentence; the phase 7 partner answers with the author's adjudication and any revision; **the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed** — not the case's length, not whether the accountable staff member accepted it, and **not how many of the evidence items turned out to be reproducible.**

**Five hard requirements.** A claim **unbounded on any of the four axes fails.** An evidence item carrying **a magnitude that is not derivable from the substrate's own records fails**, which is the counterpart to the *uncharacterised* permission and exists so that the same-field rule cannot be satisfied by invention. **An evidence item claiming a command that the step 3 bundle does not run fails**, which is the counterpart to the *not reproducible* permission and exists for the same reason. A case whose argument contains **an unstated detection commitment** — any form of *we would notice* not written as a step 6 claim — fails. **A detection commitment resting on an alert whose [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md) recipient named a mute condition, where that condition does not appear in the case, fails.** **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**A low reproducibility count is a pass.** A case whose evidence is mostly marked *not reproducible* is a finding about the substrate's records, which is what [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md)'s pooled gap list already established, and penalising it would produce cases built out of whatever happens to be queryable. **What fails is a claim of reproducibility that does not survive being run.**

**A case the accountable staff member declines to accept is a pass.** A declined case recorded with the grounds is the expected outcome for a substantial share of the cohort. A case recorded as accepted when it was not is a specification failure and is treated as [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) treated the same act.

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md).

**Error-clinic firewall.** Phase 6 is unobserved by anyone contributing to a specification judgement, and nothing from it is recorded.

**Rule-6 discharge.** Three obligations land on this day and **all three elements exist.**

- **[S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md)** named this day as the response occasion for its step 7 mute answers, specifying that the safety case must not rest on a detection claim whose named recipient stated a condition under which they would mute it, unless that condition appears in the case. **That is phase 5 step 6's final clause, and it is one of the day's five hard requirements.**
- **[S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md)** named this day as the response occasion for its pooled phase 1 item 3 list — what the cohort wanted to know and the substrate does not record — specifying that the safety case's claims be selected against it so that the case rests on quantities the substrate can evidence. **That is phase 5 step 1's selection clause, and it is a checked element with a named-gap requirement for any claim that survives selection on a listed quantity.** **Step 3's bundle is the same instrument turned into an operation**: an item the bundle cannot produce is a record gap the student has found for themselves rather than inherited from the pooled list.
- **[S082](wiki/seminars/S082.md)** named this day as the response occasion for its step 6 outcomes, specifying that the safety case include one claim the student raised and was overruled on, with the record of the raising and the overruling party's grounds. **That is phase 5 step 8's first half, and it is a checked element.** It is carried as a claim the case does not make, which is the only honest place for it: the overruling stands, and the record of it is what a receiving reader needs in order to know that the boundary was contested.

**Discharge of three inbound retention obligations.** Three prior whitepapers named this day in their §6 retention architecture as the occasion on which their material becomes something else, and **all three elements exist.**

- **[WP-066](wiki/whitepapers/WP-066.md) §6** named this day as where citing a release gate whose error structure was never characterised becomes a defect the student can name. **That is phase 3 case C2 and its evidence-production result, phase 4 point 3, and phase 5 step 2's same-field error requirement, which is a hard specification requirement.**
- **[WP-068](wiki/whitepapers/WP-068.md) §6** named this day as where a detection commitment becomes a claim the case rests on. **That is phase 4 point 4 and phase 5 step 6, and an unstated detection commitment is a fail condition.**
- **[WP-069](wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §6** named this day as where a case's claims require owners. **That is phase 4 point 2 for the acceptance owner, phase 5 step 5's responsible-party requirement for every assumption in all three sets, and the accountable staff member's presence at step 8.**

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions:

- **The unowned-assumption total** — how many step 5 assumptions across the cohort have no responsible party. **Response occasion: [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) phase 5, where every unowned assumption must be either assigned an owner in the handover artefact or listed in the warranty's exclusion set, and where an assumption appearing in neither is a fail condition.** The cohort total is reported at S089 phase 4.
- **The phase 7 first-answer hit rate** — how often a partner, reading the case alone, identifies a use the author did not intend to cover. **Response occasion: [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md) phase 3, where the receiving party reads the case cold as part of the handover and the same question is asked of them, with the two rates compared and reported at S090 phase 8.** The comparison is between a peer reader and a real receiving reader on the same instrument, which is the comparison [WP-086](wiki/whitepapers/WP-086.md) §8 objection 2 says the design cannot otherwise make.
