# S070 · A Measurement You Will Defend ⊘

**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none

## The situation

This is the stage's second assessment occasion and the last in the module. It marks one thing.

**Does your claim survive somebody checking it?**

Not whether the result is good. Not whether the customer is pleased. **Whether a person with no stake in the answer, given what you have written, could establish where every part of it came from** — and whether you said so where they could not.

Answer these in writing before you begin, and submit them.

1. State your claim in one sentence, and say what class of claim it is: causal, associative or descriptive.
2. Which part of it rests on something nobody measured?
3. Who is responsible for the attribution, and have they accepted?
4. What result would have meant this did not work, and did that get written down before you looked?
5. Which of your figures would you remove if you had to defend the claim to somebody hostile?

**Item 1 is where most candidates fail before they start**, by writing a causal sentence and classifying it as descriptive, or the reverse.

## What you must produce

**A claim about value, and everything required to check it.**

- **The baseline record** from [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md), or the written statement that there is none and what the claim was downgraded to.
- **The disconfirming result**, dated to before the measurement.
- **The attribution statement** from [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md), with a named person and their acceptance or their recorded refusal.
- **The competing explanations**, and what was done about each.
- **The rewritten metric definitions**, with the originals retained.
- **The provenance sheet** from [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md), with classifications beside the numbers.
- **The traceability check**, including its failure.
- **At least one unfavourable result**, reported per [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md), or a defended statement that none was found.

## What good looks like

These are the assessed elements. Each is checked as present and adequate, or not.

- **The claim is stated in one sentence and correctly classified** as causal, associative or descriptive. **A causal sentence classified as descriptive fails this element, and so does the reverse.**
- **A baseline record exists naming the measure, the value, the period, the instrument and who took it — or a written statement says there is none**, names what the claim was downgraded to, and says what the downgrade gives up.
- **The disconfirming result is present and dated before the measurement.** A disconfirming value written afterwards is not one.
- **Attribution is assigned to a named person**, and their acceptance or refusal is recorded. **A recorded refusal satisfies this element; an unowned claim does not.**
- **The competing explanations are listed, and each is either addressed or explicitly not addressed** — with the claim downgraded to association wherever separation was not achieved.
- **No metric definition presupposes attribution.** Every definition that did has been rewritten, and the original is retained beside it.
- **Every number carries a provenance classification beside it** — measured, elicited, assumed or inherited — with a source and a date.
- **The traceability check is reported, including where it could not be completed**, and any inherited figure is marked as an illustration rather than a benchmark.
- **At least one unfavourable result is reported with the unfavourable reading leading**, or the absence is defended by naming what was looked for and where.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Whether the measurement is favourable is not marked, and **an unfavourable measurement properly defended passes ahead of a favourable one that is not.** Whether any number is correct is not marked, because **nothing available to this programme could check one.** The size of any change is not marked. The number of metrics is not marked and more is not better. Whether attribution was successfully established is not marked — only whether somebody is responsible for it and whether the claim was downgraded when it was not. Whether the customer accepted the result is not marked. Presentation is not marked. Whether your classification of the claim matches the assessor's is not marked, provided you defended it.

## How it goes wrong

- **The misclassified sentence** — a causal claim wearing descriptive language, or a descriptive one dressed as causal. *Warning sign:* your sentence contains a verb like *drove* and your classification says observation.
- **The reconstructed baseline** — a before state assembled after the result by people who wanted the result. *Warning sign:* both figures were produced in the same week.
- **The retrofitted disconfirmation** — the failure condition written once the result was known, and it conveniently did not occur. *Warning sign:* it has no date, or its date is the date of the report.
- **The unowned attribution** — nobody was asked, so nobody refused. *Warning sign:* you have no refusal and no acceptance.
- **The undowngraded claim** — separation was not achieved and the sentence still says the system caused it. *Warning sign:* your competing-explanation list is longer than your analysis.
- **The appendix provenance** — the classifications exist and nobody reading the number sees them. *Warning sign:* the page that will be forwarded has figures and no marks.
- **The absent bad news** — no unfavourable result and no account of looking for one. *Warning sign:* everything you measured supported your position.

## Do it

Assemble the artefacts, then work in this order.

**First, write the sentence and classify it.** Then hand it to somebody and ask them which class they would have put it in. **The disagreement is the useful part**, and it usually reveals a verb doing causal work in a sentence you intended as an observation.

**Second, check the dates.** Baseline before build where possible. Disconfirming result before measurement. Provenance dates on every elicited input. **An artefact whose dates are all the same date was produced at the end**, and an assessor will notice.

**Third, go and get the attribution accepted or refused.** Do this before the defence, not during it. **A refusal recorded in advance is an artefact; a refusal discovered under questioning is a gap.**

**Fourth, downgrade wherever you have to.** Association rather than causation. Description rather than association. **Each downgrade costs you something commercially and buys you a claim that will still be true next year**, and the specification pays for the second.

**Fifth, move the provenance marks onto the page people will forward.** Not the appendix. Not the methodology note.

**Sixth, find your unfavourable result and lead with it.** If you genuinely have none, say what you looked for, where you looked and why you are confident. **An engagement with no unfavourable finding is possible and it is uncommon**, and the defended absence is a stronger artefact than a manufactured negative.

## Show it

Defend the claim, not the result.

The assessor has your one-sentence claim and its classification, and nothing else. They will ask three things: **what rests on something nobody measured, who owns the attribution, and what you would remove if the room were hostile.**

They will also do one thing.

**They will offer you a better number.** They will point at a defensible alternative definition of your measure — one that is genuinely defensible, that they can argue for, and under which your result improves — and offer it as a helpful suggestion.

**Accepting is the failure this occasion exists to catch.** The definition is not wrong; the timing is. **A definition changed after seeing the result is a definition chosen by the result**, and every claim built on it inherits that choice invisibly.

The passing answer is to decline, and to say what changing the definition now would cost the claim — **and, if the alternative really is better, to say what it would take to adopt it properly: restating the baseline under the new definition, redating it, and reporting both.**

## What this does not establish

**This occasion shows that a claim is checkable. It does not show that it is true, that the value was realised, or that anybody will act on it.**

**No source available to this programme reports an outcome for any measurement of this kind.** The framework this module teaches from has never exhibited a baseline being established, never reported a metric being measured, and reserves an empty case-studies section. **The specification therefore marks a discipline whose product has never been observed**, and it says so rather than implying otherwise.

**The exclusion list carries a real hazard.** Marking an unfavourable result as passing ahead of a favourable one creates an incentive to produce a negative finding, and nothing in the nine elements distinguishes a real one from a manufactured one. **The counterweight is indirect** — the provenance, dating and attribution elements make a fabricated negative as expensive as a fabricated positive — and it is not a solution. The gap is named in [WP-070](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §8 and the design accepts it rather than filling it with a judgement nothing supports.

**And the occasion cannot mark honesty.** A candidate who omitted their weakest figure before the provenance pass passes every element. **That is the same limit [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) has about its register**, it is the limit of any specification that marks artefacts rather than intentions, and a graduate should hold it as one.
