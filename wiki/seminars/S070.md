## The situation

This is the stage's second assessment occasion and the last in the module. It marks one thing.

**Does your claim survive somebody checking it?**

Not whether the result is good. Not whether the customer is pleased. **Whether a person with no stake in the answer, given what you have written, could establish where every part of it came from** — and whether you said so where they could not.

The module has been building to one sentence and this is where it is tested. **A defensible measurement states what it does not license as clearly as what it does.** Everything below is machinery for producing a claim with that property: an interval rather than a point, a correction rather than a hope, an independent record rather than one instrument's word for itself, and a written statement of the questions the measurement cannot answer.

Answer these in writing before you begin, and submit them.

1. State your claim in one sentence, and say what class of claim it is: causal, associative or descriptive.
2. Which part of it rests on something nobody measured?
3. Who is responsible for the attribution, and have they accepted?
4. What result would have meant this did not work, and did that get written down before you looked?
5. What does your measurement **not** license — which questions would a reader be wrong to answer from it?

**Item 1 is where most candidates fail before they start**, by writing a causal sentence and classifying it as descriptive, or the reverse. **Item 5 is the one the whole module was for**, and it is the item most candidates leave shortest.

## What you must produce

**A claim about value, stated with its uncertainty, and everything required to check it.**

1. **The claim in one sentence**, with its class, and its **does-not-license** statement in the same paragraph.
2. **The estimate with its interval**, and the frozen configuration it was computed under.
3. **The gold sample**: the small set you labelled by hand, the labelling rule written before you labelled, and how the set was drawn.
4. **The correction**: how the large judged set was reconciled against the small trusted one, and what the correction's own uncertainty contributes.
5. **The independent record**: the gateway telemetry your result was checked against, and what it agreed and disagreed with.
6. **The baseline record** from [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md), or the written statement that there is none and what the claim was downgraded to.
7. **The disconfirming result**, dated to before the measurement.
8. **The attribution statement** from [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md), with a named person and their acceptance or their recorded refusal.
9. **The competing explanations**, and what was done about each.
10. **The rewritten metric definitions**, with the originals retained.
11. **The provenance sheet** from [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md), with classifications beside the numbers.
12. **The traceability check**, including its failure.
13. **At least one unfavourable result**, reported per [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md), or a defended statement that none was found.

## The material

### An evaluation without uncertainty overclaims, and usually by more than anyone expects

The module's backbone is a short statistics treatment written for practitioners, and it opens by attacking exactly the practice this occasion assesses. Evaluations are run and reported with a highest-number-is-best mentality, in which a leading result is highlighted **"but not necessarily to test that result for any kind of statistical significance"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). Its ambition, stated at the close, is that practitioners **"will think of their model evaluations as informative experiments rather than a series of contests to produce the largest number."**

The analytic move that makes inference possible is a single conceptual step: treat the evaluation's items as drawn from a hypothetical larger population, which **"lets us jump 'through the looking glass' of the specific questions that appear in an eval in order to study the underlying skill that the eval is attempting to measure."** Without that step you can describe your evaluation set and nothing else.

Four of its conclusions are what this occasion is built on.

**A comparison of two systems on a small evaluation set frequently cannot distinguish them at all.** The paper carries a worked comparison through its entire length, and the correct analysis reverses the apparent winner — **ordinary practice produced the wrong answer, not merely an imprecise one.**

**Where items arrive in related groups rather than independently, the independence assumption is violated and an uncorrected interval is too narrow.** Several widely used evaluations draw multiple items from one passage, or translate one item into many languages. The paper's own conclusion is blunt: failure to adjust for clustered sampling **"may lead an unsuspecting analyst to suppose that the measurement of the overall eval score is much more precise than it actually is."** **If your evaluation set has several items per document, per customer or per scenario, your interval is too narrow and your claim is stronger than your data.**

**Comparing two systems should be done on paired item-level differences.** Models tend to agree about which items are easy, so the covariance between them is generally positive, and pairing exploits it — the paper describes it as a free reduction in estimator variance. **A before-and-after comparison that throws away the pairing has discarded information it already had.**

**And there is a prohibition worth memorising by its title: "Don't touch the thermostat!"** Lowering the sampling temperature to make scores look more stable is a trap, because it can shift variance from the part you could have reduced into the part you cannot, or bias the estimate outright. **A stability you bought by changing the system's operating conditions is not a stability of the measurement.**

**Its limits are recorded and matter here.** It is an analytical paper rather than an empirical one, and most of its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions. **Its whole apparatus is valid only where the items genuinely sample the population you care about** — if they were hand-selected, curated for difficulty or leaked into training, the machinery is valid about the wrong target. **And statistical validity says nothing about construct validity: a tight interval around a meaningless metric is still meaningless.** **No figure from it is reproduced here.**

### The small trusted sample and the large untrusted one

You have thousands of outputs and a budget to label a few. **The two obvious responses are both wrong and this is the most technically apt result in the repository for fixing it.**

Treating a model's judgements as though they were labels is fast, uses all the data, and is invalid: any systematic error in the judge propagates directly into the estimate, and the resulting interval does not account for prediction error at all. Discarding the judge and using only the labelled sample is valid and wasteful, producing intervals far wider than necessary.

The third construction resolves the bind ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/angelopoulos-prediction-powered-inference.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/angelopoulos-prediction-powered-inference.md)). **The small gold-standard sample is used to estimate the systematic discrepancy between what the model predicts and what the trustworthy labels say. That correction is then applied to the estimate computed over the large model-labelled set**, and because the correction is itself estimated from data, its own uncertainty is propagated into the final interval. **That propagation is what makes the interval honest rather than merely narrower.**

The load-bearing property is stated in the source's own abstract: the method yields **"provably valid confidence intervals for quantities such as means, quantiles, and linear and logistic regression coefficients, without making any assumptions on the machine-learning algorithm that supplies the predictions."**

**Read that twice, because it inverts what most engineers expect.** The validity of the interval does not depend on the judge being accurate, unbiased, calibrated or correctly specified. **A bad judge does not invalidate the inference; it widens the interval.** What model quality buys is efficiency — **"more accurate predictions translate to smaller confidence intervals"** — which is the correct incentive structure, because improving the judge tightens your interval rather than risking your answer. In the limit of a useless judge the method degrades gracefully towards the interval you would have had from the labels alone; in the limit of a perfect one it approaches the interval you would have had from labelling everything.

**This is the answer to the silence that has haunted the whole module.** The platform publishes no agreement rate between any evaluator and human judgement. **You do not need it published. You can establish it yourself, on your own population, for your own criterion, and carry the residual uncertainty into your interval rather than assuming it away.**

**Its conditions are strict and are where teams break it.** The gold labels must genuinely be gold, because the method corrects the model's error against the labels and cannot correct error in the labels — **weak or inconsistent human annotation defeats it entirely.** The labelled subset must be a valid sample of the same population as the unlabelled data; **convenience sampling breaks the guarantee silently.** Sample-size requirements do not disappear: the method makes a labelling budget go further and does not make a tiny budget sufficient for a fine-grained claim. **And it says nothing about whether the quantity you are estimating is the right one — it cannot rescue a badly designed benchmark or a criterion that does not measure what matters.**

**This repository's note was written from the abstract and the published summary rather than from the full construction.** No coverage rate, interval width, sample size or per-domain result is reproduced here, and a candidate implementing it should go to the paper.

### The independent record, and why two instruments beat one

The anchor pairs your evaluation results with **the gateway's own telemetry**, and the reason is not redundancy. **The two instruments have different failure modes, which is the only property that makes a cross-check worth performing.**

Your evaluation result is produced by a scoring apparatus: a dataset you chose, a split strategy you chose, a threshold somebody chose, and frequently a model judge. Its failure modes are the ones this module has been cataloguing — a moved threshold, a drifted rubric, a judge whose agreement with humans nobody has established.

The gateway's telemetry is produced by the request path. Tokens consumed, keyed on a counter you named, dimensioned by subscription, product and API ([research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md)). **Its failure modes are unattributed traffic, a counter key that cannot answer your question, and a dimension somebody forgot to emit.** Notice that **none of those overlaps with the scoring failures.** A judge that has drifted will not change your token counts; an unattributed bucket will not change your quality scores.

**So the cross-check is genuinely informative.** If your evaluation claims a workload was exercised heavily and the gateway shows little consumption on that subscription, one of the two is wrong and you now know to find out which. **If they agree, you have a claim resting on two instruments that could not have failed the same way** — which is a materially stronger position than one instrument with a tight interval.

The documentation's own reasoning supports the placement. Logging supports **"billing and auditing"**, which is the point at which observability becomes a governance record rather than an operational convenience; and metric dimensions are what make a figure answerable rather than merely large.

**Its limits are the ones [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) recorded and they travel here.** First-party product documentation with a commercial frame, definitional rather than empirical, with **no benchmarks, no latency figures, no cost measurements and no comparison against any alternative.** Capability varies by service tier and several named capabilities are preview. Policy names and attributes drift between revisions. **And it does not discuss the privacy surface created by logging prompts and completions**, which is a live question the moment your independent record is fine-grained enough to be useful.

### Why the defence is oral, and why it contains a question about a case you never met

This occasion's format is not a presentation. It descends from the entrustment literature this programme's assessment architecture is built on, where the corresponding instrument is the **entrustment-based discussion**: a short, focused conversation, usually after the activity, that **"should probe the student's deep understanding of what was done, but should include 'what-if' questions, to grasp whether the learner would know what to do what unexpected or familiar situations would arise"** ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)).

**The what-if requirement is what distinguishes it from a walkthrough**, and the reason is exact. Retrospective explanation shows that a learner understood the case they met. **Counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases they did not meet** — and the source is explicit about why this matters: an entrustment decision implies the acceptance of risk, because it can never be fully predicted what the learner will encounter.

The same source supplies two further design facts this occasion uses. **Entrustment is expressed on an ordinal supervision scale rather than as a score**, running from observing only, through executing with a supervisor in the room, to executing with a supervisor reachable but absent, to unsupervised practice, to supervising others — with sub-levels in the middle where most of a course's actual progression happens. **And a summative decision "should include an agreement between multiple observers… and multiple observations"**, which is why the module's elements are checked across five sessions' artefacts rather than in one sitting.

It also names three professionalism characteristics in behavioural terms that transfer to engineering almost unchanged: **integrity**, as truthfulness in reports to a supervisor; **reliability**, as stable and conscientious working; and **humility**, as the ability to observe one's own limitations and willingness to ask for help. **The first of those is what [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) was about and what item 5 of this session tests.**

**Its limits:** it is a primer by the concept's originator, synthesising a decade of medical-education literature. **It is guidance rather than evaluation** — its empirical items are reported from prior studies rather than generated. Transfer from clinical supervision to engineering assessment is an argument this programme makes and has not tested.

### Why the record is narrative and the decision is not one person's

The assessment literature this programme rests on found something that shapes how this occasion must be run. Assessors reading real evidence **"mainly relied on narrative feedback to inform their judgment… Grades and qualifications were merely used to confirm impressions based on narratives"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)).

Two further findings bind the design. **Assessors reached similar final judgements through different reasoning** — convergence on the outcome concealed divergence in the rationale, driven by differing beliefs about which evidence is trustworthy, differing theories of what competence is, and differing inferences from the same evidence. And **rater idiosyncrasy is substantial and unrelated to rater expertise**, so a more experienced assessor is not a more consistent one.

**The consequences are the ones this occasion adopts.** High-stakes decisions **should not be made individually but should result from group discussion**, because deliberation surfaces reasoning that identical verdicts conceal. Assessors should **thoroughly substantiate their judgements**, because substantiation is what makes divergence discussable. And assessor training should target awareness of one's own beliefs and inferences rather than calibration to an instrument, which the same literature found to have had mixed success at best.

**And a warning that applies to you as much as to your assessor.** The same work found **anchoring**: assessors were inclined to stick to their initial judgements even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence, with further reading producing a more elaborate version of the same conclusion rather than a revised one. **That is the mechanism behind this occasion's central trap, described below.**

**Its limits:** single-institution medical-education research using mock portfolios and think-aloud methods, which cannot capture fast or automatic processing and risk participants constructing plausible explanations. **Narratives are not self-validating and can carry bias more easily than a number does.** Transfer is an argument.

### The obligation, stated once

The psychological-safety definition [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) worked from contains one word that belongs here rather than there. Psychological safety is present when colleagues trust and respect each other and **"feel able — even obligated — to be candid"** ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)).

**This occasion assumes the obligation and cannot supply the environment.** A defence room constructed to make candour safe is not the room a graduate will work in, and this session's limits say so. **What it can do is establish that the candid version of the claim is the one that passes**, so that a learner has at least once been rewarded rather than punished for it.

**The source's limits:** a trade-press book by the construct's originator, read in full, with case studies drawn almost entirely from secondary sources and an internal tension between a tight definition and a looser gloss; **the foundational 1999 paper is recorded in this repository as unread.**

### The rate you set, the ceiling you did not, and the denominator nobody can state

Everything above concerns the honesty of an interval. **This concerns the honesty of the denominator underneath it**, and it is the failure most likely to survive every other check in this module.

Production scoring on this platform does not evaluate everything. **Continuous evaluation runs evaluators on *sampled* agent responses at a configurable sample rate**, and surfaces the results on a dashboard ([research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-agent-build-and-runtime.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-agent-build-and-runtime.md)). That much is visible, deliberate and fine — a rate you chose is a decision you can defend.

**Underneath it sits a second limit you did not choose. Evaluation runs are skipped once an hourly run limit is reached, and that limit has a default.** The value is not reproduced here and does not matter; **the mechanism is the whole lesson.** Skipping is not an error. It does not fail, it does not warn, and it does not appear on the dashboard the score is read from. **On a quiet hour your configured rate is the rate. On a busy hour it is not, and the number on the screen looks identical either way.**

**Read what that does to a reported score.** You believe you sampled at your configured rate. **You may have sampled at whatever fraction of it the busiest hours allowed**, and the shortfall is systematically concentrated in periods of high traffic — which is to say in exactly the conditions a quality claim most needs to cover. **This is not noise. It is a bias with a direction**, and it removes precisely the population you would most want represented.

Two further mechanisms compound it and each is worth a line in your statement. **The default sampling strategy is uniform, and an alternative is offered which is described as a service-managed heuristic biased toward interesting traces — conversations with potential problems, edge cases or anomalies.** What it optimises, how it decides and what it systematically misses are **not documented.** **A sample drawn by an undocumented rule has no reportable coverage at all**, and a score computed over it cannot be described as representative of anything. Choosing it may still be right — for finding problems it is plainly better than uniform — but **you must then say that your number is a measurement of interesting cases and not of traffic.** And **conversation-level evaluation carries a lookback ceiling**, so anything older than the window is unreachable except through explicit time bounds inside your own telemetry retention; **the value is not reproduced here.**

**The consequence for this occasion is precise, and it is a required part of the claim rather than a caveat about it.** Your interval quantifies uncertainty *given* the items you scored. **It says nothing about whether those items are the items you meant.** So the statement has to carry both: the interval, and the coverage — configured rate, whether the ceiling was reached, which sampling strategy, and the population that is therefore represented. **A score without its coverage is not a rate**, and a candidate who reports one has produced a well-quantified statement about an unknown denominator.

**And the honest position on the ceiling is usually *I do not know*, which is a passing answer.** Nothing in the material read for this repository says the skipping is surfaced anywhere a reader would see it. **So the defensible form is: I set this rate, this limit exists and I could not establish whether it was reached, therefore my coverage is at most the configured rate and may be less in the busiest periods.** **That sentence is worth more than a confident number**, and writing it is the skill this element exists to install.

**What none of this establishes.** **It does not establish that continuous evaluation misses anything a manual review would catch** — no study, no comparison and no false-negative rate is published for that, anywhere ([research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-control-plane-and-governance.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-control-plane-and-governance.md)). It does not establish how often any team hits the ceiling. And it does not establish that the undocumented heuristic is poor — **only that its coverage cannot be stated, which is a different and narrower claim.**

**The sources' limits travel with all of it.** The first is **the most perishable material in this repository and it perished visibly while being read**: four pages read in full, roughly thirty reached only through search excerpts with tables frequently truncated, several consulted pages explicitly superseded and still rendering, **and the continuous-evaluation, scheduled-evaluation and conversation-level surfaces are all preview.** **Definitional and not evidential.** The second is **vendor documentation about vendor governance**, definitional, renaming its own roles mid-documentation, with **two interpretive thresholds asserted on no stated basis and reproduced nowhere in this wiki.** **Every number in either is a dated platform parameter and none appears here — including the default limit, the lookback window and the rate.**

## The workflow

**Produce a claim you would defend to somebody hostile: an interval, a correction, an independent record, and a written statement of what it does not license.**

**Step 1 — write the labelling rule before you label anything.** What counts as a correct output, in words another person could apply. **A rule written after labelling has begun is a description of what you labelled**, and it is the same defect as a threshold written after the score.

**Step 2 — draw the gold sample properly and say how.** It must be a valid sample of the same population as the outputs you will judge at scale. **Convenience sampling breaks the correction silently and produces an interval that is wrong rather than wide.**

**Step 3 — label it by hand, then compute agreement with the judge on that sample.** This is the measurement the vendor does not publish, and you have just made it for your own population and your own criterion. **Report it as agreement on your set, never as a property of the evaluator.**

**Step 4 — score the full set with the judge, then correct and interval.** The correction is the systematic discrepancy you measured in step 3, applied to the large estimate, **with the correction's own uncertainty carried into the final interval.** If your items arrive in related groups — several per document, per customer, per scenario — **say so, because an uncorrected interval on clustered items is too narrow and will make your claim stronger than your data.**

**Step 5 — cross-check against the gateway telemetry.** Pull consumption for the subscription and product your workload ran under and ask whether the two records are consistent. **Look specifically for the unattributed share**, because a large one means your independent record is not as independent as it looks.

**Step 6 — write the does-not-license statement**, in the same paragraph as the claim. **And write the coverage beside it**, because an interval quantifies uncertainty given the items you scored and says nothing about whether those items are the items you meant.

```yaml
# .evidence/claim.yaml   <- SHAPE, NOT SCHEMA. Verify every product key separately.
claim: >
  Under the frozen evaluation set, first-attempt routing accuracy is higher after
  the change than in the recorded baseline period.
class: "associative"        # causal | associative | descriptive - and DEFEND the choice

estimate: "<point>"
interval: "<interval>"      # in the SAME paragraph as the claim, always
clustered_items: true       # several items per source document
cluster_adjustment: "applied - an unadjusted interval here would be too narrow"
paired_comparison: true     # before/after on the SAME items; pairing is free precision

coverage:                   # THE DENOMINATOR. an interval does not supply this.
  sample_rate_configured: "<the rate you set>"
  hourly_run_ceiling_exists: true
  ceiling_reached: unknown  # <- 'unknown' is the DEFENSIBLE answer, not a gap
  why_unknown: >
    runs are SKIPPED at the ceiling. skipping does not error, does not warn and
    does not appear on the dashboard the score is read from. a busy hour and a
    quiet hour produce an identical-looking number.
  therefore: >
    coverage is AT MOST the configured rate, and may be less in the busiest
    periods - which is exactly where a quality claim most needs to hold.
    the shortfall is not noise. it has a direction.
  sampling_strategy: uniform        # uniform | heuristic
  if_heuristic: >
    the heuristic is service-managed and UNDOCUMENTED - what it optimises, how it
    decides and what it misses are unstated. a sample drawn by an undocumented
    rule has no reportable coverage. if used, the number measures INTERESTING
    CASES and not traffic, and must say so.
  lookback_ceiling: "a window applies; older material is unreachable except through
                     explicit bounds inside our own telemetry retention"
  population_actually_represented: "<state it. it is narrower than 'production'.>"

gold_sample:
  labelling_rule_written: "<date, BEFORE any labelling>"
  drawn_how: "random from the same population as the judged set - NOT convenience"
  size: "<n>"
  labelled_by: "<who, and whether independently duplicated>"

correction:
  judge_agreement_on_gold_sample: "<measured HERE, on THIS population>"
  # The vendor publishes no agreement rate for any evaluator. This is not that.
  # It is a property of this criterion, this population and this judge version.
  correction_uncertainty_propagated: true   # if false, the interval is not honest

independent_record:
  source: "gateway token telemetry, counter-key = subscription, dimensioned by product"
  agrees_with: "<what matched>"
  disagrees_with: "<what did not - and which instrument you now distrust>"
  unattributed_share: "<reported, always>"
  why_independent: "different failure modes: scoring drift cannot move token counts"

does_not_license:            # THE ITEM THE MODULE WAS FOR
  - "Any statement about a population the gold sample did not represent."
  - "Any statement about traffic the sampler skipped, whose volume we cannot state."
  - "Any causal statement: the confounders at S067 were listed and not separated."
  - "Any comparison against another organisation's published figure."
  - "Any claim about the period after the judge model version changes."

frozen_config_ref: "<path to the S069 frozen configuration, committed before the run>"
```

**Step 7 — use the agent to attack your own interval, not to widen it.** One prompt: *argue that my interval is too narrow, using only what is in this file, and name the specific structure in my evaluation set that would make it so.* **Then check the answer against your own clustering.** Keep what is better than your own reasoning, record what you rejected, and **record separately that this instrument may share a model family with the judge whose agreement you measured**, so it is not independent of the thing it is auditing.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** That the agreement between a judge and a human, on your own population, is a number you can obtain in an afternoon — **and that the whole module's central silence was therefore never a reason to stop, only a reason not to assume.** You will also observe the cross-check doing real work: **the commonest outcome is partial disagreement, and locating which instrument to distrust is a more valuable skill than either instrument's output.**

## What good looks like

These are the assessed elements. Each is checked as present and adequate, or not.

- **The claim is stated in one sentence and correctly classified** as causal, associative or descriptive. **A causal sentence classified as descriptive fails this element, and so does the reverse.**
- **The estimate carries an interval, in the same paragraph as the claim**, computed under the frozen configuration — and where the items are clustered, the adjustment is stated or its absence is declared.
- **The does-not-license statement is present and specific.** **A statement that names no question a reader would be wrong to answer from the measurement is not one.**
- **A gold sample exists, drawn from the same population as the judged set**, with the labelling rule dated before labelling began, and the judge's agreement with it reported as a property of this set rather than of the evaluator.
- **The correction's own uncertainty is carried into the interval**, or the report says plainly that it is not and what that costs.
- **The independent record is present, with the unattributed share reported**, and any disagreement between it and the evaluation result is named rather than reconciled silently.
- **A baseline record exists naming the measure, the value, the period, the instrument and who took it — or a written statement says there is none**, names what the claim was downgraded to, and says what the downgrade gives up.
- **The disconfirming result is present and dated before the measurement.** A disconfirming value written afterwards is not one.
- **Attribution is assigned to a named person**, and their acceptance or refusal is recorded. **A recorded refusal satisfies this element; an unowned claim does not.**
- **The competing explanations are listed, and each is either addressed or explicitly not addressed** — with the claim downgraded to association wherever separation was not achieved.
- **No metric definition presupposes attribution.** Every definition that did has been rewritten, and the original is retained beside it.
- **Every number carries a provenance classification beside it** — measured, elicited, assumed or inherited — with a source and a date.
- **The traceability check is reported, including where it could not be completed**, and any inherited figure is marked as an illustration rather than a benchmark.
- **At least one unfavourable result is reported with the unfavourable reading leading**, or the absence is defended by naming what was looked for and where.
- **The coverage of any sampled measurement is stated beside its interval** — the configured rate, whether the ceiling that skips runs was reached or could not be established, which sampling strategy was used, and the population therefore represented. **An interval reported without its coverage fails this element**, and ***could not be established* is a passing answer where the reason is given; a blank is not.**

**Nothing else is assessed.** Whether the measurement is favourable is not marked, and **an unfavourable measurement properly defended passes ahead of a favourable one that is not.** Whether any number is correct is not marked, because **nothing available to this programme could check one.** The size of any change is not marked. The width of the interval is not marked — only whether it is present, honestly computed and honestly described. The number of metrics is not marked and more is not better. Whether attribution was successfully established is not marked — only whether somebody is responsible for it and whether the claim was downgraded when it was not. Whether the customer accepted the result is not marked. Presentation is not marked. Whether your classification of the claim matches the assessor's is not marked, provided you defended it.

## How it goes wrong

- **The misclassified sentence** — a causal claim wearing descriptive language, or a descriptive one dressed as causal. *Warning sign:* your sentence contains a verb like *drove* and your classification says observation.
- **The point estimate** — a number reported with no interval, so the reader supplies their own precision. *Warning sign:* your headline figure has more digits than your gold sample could support.
- **The narrow interval** — clustered items treated as independent, making the measurement look more precise than it is. *Warning sign:* your evaluation set has several items per document and your interval assumed one.
- **The convenience gold sample** — the labelled set was whatever was easy to label. *Warning sign:* your gold items are all from the same period, team or scenario.
- **The rule written afterwards** — the labelling criterion was settled once labelling was under way. *Warning sign:* your labelling rule has no date, or its date is the date of the report.
- **The borrowed agreement** — the judge's agreement is asserted from a vendor page rather than measured. *Warning sign:* you have an agreement figure and no gold sample. **No such figure is published; if you have one, find out where you got it.**
- **The redundant cross-check** — the second record shares a failure mode with the first, so agreement establishes nothing. *Warning sign:* both of your records come out of the same scoring run.
- **The reconstructed baseline** — a before state assembled after the result by people who wanted the result. *Warning sign:* both figures were produced in the same week.
- **The retrofitted disconfirmation** — the failure condition written once the result was known, and it conveniently did not occur. *Warning sign:* it has no date, or its date is the date of the report.
- **The unowned attribution** — nobody was asked, so nobody refused. *Warning sign:* you have no refusal and no acceptance.
- **The undowngraded claim** — separation was not achieved and the sentence still says the system caused it. *Warning sign:* your competing-explanation list is longer than your analysis.
- **The appendix provenance** — the classifications exist and nobody reading the number sees them. *Warning sign:* the page that will be forwarded has figures and no marks.
- **The absent bad news** — no unfavourable result and no account of looking for one. *Warning sign:* everything you measured supported your position.

## Exercises

**These are preparation for an assessed occasion; the exercises themselves are not marked, and the fourteen elements above are.** Do them before the defence rather than after it.

**1 · Classify six sentences and defend each.** For each, say whether it is causal, associative or descriptive, and name the one word that decides it.

- *Routing accuracy improved after the change.* · *The assistant drove a reduction in handling time.* · *Teams using the assistant resolved more tickets.* · *We observed higher first-attempt accuracy in the post-change period.* · *The savings attributable to the system exceeded its cost.* · *We could not distinguish the assisted workflow from the baseline.*

> **Model answers.** The second and fifth are causal — *drove* and *attributable to* do it, and the fifth also embeds the attribution in a noun so the measure cannot come out at zero. The third is associative and reads as causal to almost every reader, which is why it is the dangerous one. The first, fourth and sixth are descriptive; the sixth is the strongest sentence in the list because **it names what it cannot establish inside the claim itself.** **The general test: delete every causal verb and see whether the sentence still says anything checkable.** If it empties, you have made a claim and it needs an owner, an interval and a confounder list.

**2 · Measure the agreement nobody published.** Using workflow steps 1 to 3, hand-label your gold sample and compute the judge's agreement with you on it. Report it correctly.

> **What you should find.** A number, obtained in an afternoon, that no vendor page contains. **Report it as agreement between this judge version and this labeller on this population for this criterion — never as a property of the evaluator**, which is precisely the over-generalisation the module has spent five sessions preventing. **Notice what has changed epistemically:** the silence that opened the module was never a reason to stop measuring, only a reason not to assume. **If your agreement is poor, that is a usable result and not a failure** — a poor judge widens your interval rather than invalidating it, which is the property that makes the correction worth using.

**3 · Go back to your own item 5, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 5 — what your measurement does not license. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then write it properly and put it in the same paragraph as your claim.

> **Criterion.** A does-not-license statement passes if each entry names a **specific question** a reader would be wrong to answer from the measurement, and says why. *"This is only an early result"* fails — it is a hedge, not a boundary. *"This licenses no statement about the night shift, because the gold sample was drawn only from daytime traffic"* passes. **It fails if it could be pasted unchanged onto somebody else's claim**, which is the test that catches the generic version.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this occasion's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**4 · Look for the clustering you assumed away.** Examine your evaluation set and ask whether the items are genuinely independent draws. Count how many come from the same source document, the same customer, the same scenario or the same translation.

> **What you should find.** Almost always some structure, because realistic evaluation sets are built by sampling documents and then extracting items from them. **Where that structure exists and was ignored, your interval is too narrow and your claim is stronger than your data** — which is the same defect as a moved threshold and is harder to see, because nothing about it looks like a choice. **The correct response is to state the structure and adjust, or to state the structure and declare that you did not adjust.** Both pass. Silence does not.

**5 · Run the cross-check and find a disagreement.** Compare your evaluation result against the gateway telemetry and report where the two records do not line up.

> **What to notice.** That partial disagreement is the normal outcome and is the point of the exercise. **The value is not agreement; it is that the two instruments cannot fail the same way**, so a discrepancy tells you where to look. **Report the unattributed share as well** — a large one means the independent record is less independent than it appears, because the traffic it failed to classify may be exactly the traffic your evaluation covered.

**6 · Rehearse the trap.** Ask a colleague to offer you a better metric definition after you have shown them your result, and practise declining.

> **Scoring criterion.** A pass declines, states what changing the definition now would cost the claim, and — if the alternative genuinely is better — names what adopting it properly would require: restating the baseline under the new definition, redating it, and reporting both. **A fail accepts, because the definition is defensible.** **It is defensible. The timing is the problem.** A definition changed after seeing the result is a definition chosen by the result, and every claim built on it inherits that choice invisibly. **The assessment literature explains why you will accept it if you are not ready:** assessors and authors alike anchor on an initial judgement and elaborate rather than revise, so a change offered late is absorbed as an improvement rather than examined as a change.

**7 · Defend your sample rate, then discover you cannot.** Take a sampled measurement. Write down the rate you configured. Then establish whether the ceiling that skips runs was reached during your measurement window — look for it on the dashboard, in the run history, anywhere a reader of the score would look. Then write the coverage sentence that goes beside your interval.

> **Scoring criterion.** A pass names the configured rate, names that a ceiling exists past which **runs are skipped rather than failed**, and states honestly whether it was reached. **In almost every case the honest answer is that it could not be established**, because skipping does not error, does not warn and does not appear beside the score — **a busy hour and a quiet hour produce an identical-looking number.** That answer passes when the reason is given. **A blank does not, and neither does a confident claim of full coverage.**
>
> **The strongest answer names the direction of the bias.** The shortfall is not spread evenly; it concentrates in the busiest periods, **which are precisely the conditions a quality claim most needs to cover.** So the coverage is at most the configured rate and is systematically worse where it matters. **Then check your sampling strategy.** If you used the uniform default, say so. If you used the heuristic alternative, note that it is **service-managed and undocumented** — what it optimises and what it misses are unstated — so **your number measures interesting cases rather than traffic and must say so.** It may still be the right choice; it is not a choice you can report as representative. **Finally, put the two claims side by side and see that they are different objects:** the interval quantifies uncertainty given the items you scored, and the coverage says whether those items are the items you meant. **Neither substitutes for the other, and a score without its coverage is not a rate.**

## What this does not establish

**This occasion shows that a claim is checkable. It does not show that it is true, that the value was realised, or that anybody will act on it.**

**No source available to this programme reports an outcome for any measurement of this kind.** The framework this module teaches from has never exhibited a baseline being established, never reported a metric being measured, and reserves an empty case-studies section. **The specification therefore marks a discipline whose product has never been observed**, and it says so rather than implying otherwise.

**The correction technique was read from an abstract and a published summary, not from its full construction.** Its guarantee is real and its conditions are strict: gold labels must genuinely be gold, the labelled subset must sample the same population, and the method makes a budget go further rather than making a small budget sufficient. **No coverage rate, interval width or sample size from it is reproduced here**, and a candidate implementing it should go to the paper rather than to this page.

**The statistical machinery is valid about the population your items sample and no other.** If your evaluation set was hand-picked, curated for difficulty, or drawn from what happened to be convenient, the interval is a correct answer to a question nobody asked. **And a tight interval around a badly chosen metric is still meaningless** — statistical validity is not construct validity, and nothing in this occasion marks the second.

**The agreement you measure is not the agreement the vendor did not publish.** It is a property of one judge version, one criterion, one labeller and one population, and it will move when any of the four moves. **Assert no evaluator agreement figure as a general property, here or anywhere.**

**The gateway telemetry is an independent record and not a true one.** It records what the request path saw. Traffic that bypassed the gateway, arrived without the counter key, or was emitted without the dimension is invisible to it, and its own documentation offers no accuracy, coverage or reliability data of any kind.

**The entrustment framework is medical-education guidance rather than evaluation**, synthesising a decade of literature by the concept's originator. Its empirical items are reported from prior studies rather than generated, and **transfer to engineering assessment is an argument this programme makes and has not tested.**

**The assessment findings behind the format are single-institution research using mock portfolios**, with think-aloud methods that cannot capture fast processing. **Rater idiosyncrasy is substantial and unrelated to expertise**, which is a warning about this occasion's own assessors as much as about anybody else's.

**The exclusion list carries a real hazard.** Marking an unfavourable result as passing ahead of a favourable one creates an incentive to produce a negative finding, and nothing in the elements distinguishes a real one from a manufactured one. **The counterweight is indirect** — the provenance, dating, gold-sample and attribution elements make a fabricated negative as expensive as a fabricated positive — and it is not a solution. The gap is named in [WP-070](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §8 and the design accepts it rather than filling it with a judgement nothing supports.

**And the occasion cannot mark honesty.** A candidate who omitted their weakest figure before the provenance pass passes every element. **That is the same limit [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md) has about its register**, it is the limit of any specification that marks artefacts rather than intentions, and a graduate should hold it as one.

**The coverage material establishes a mechanism and gives you no way to measure your own exposure to it.** That production scoring samples at a configurable rate, that **runs are skipped once an hourly limit is reached**, that the default sampling strategy is uniform and the alternative is an **undocumented service-managed heuristic**, and that conversation-level evaluation carries a lookback ceiling — all are documented. **What is not available anywhere is any way to establish, from the score's own surface, whether the ceiling was reached.** So the element requires an honest *could not be established* rather than a number, and **that is a limit of the instrument that this occasion converts into a required disclosure rather than repairs.**

**Nor does any of it establish that sampled scoring misses anything.** **No study, no comparison and no false-negative rate is published for continuous evaluation against manual review**, and none is asserted. **No data exists on how often any team reaches the ceiling.** And nothing establishes that the undocumented heuristic is a poor sampler — **only that its coverage cannot be stated**, which is narrower and is the only claim this occasion makes.

**Both platform sources are definitional and among the most perishable material here.** One perished visibly while being read — four pages read in full, roughly thirty through search excerpts with tables truncated, several consulted pages explicitly superseded and still rendering — and **its continuous-evaluation, scheduled-evaluation and conversation-level surfaces are all preview.** The other is **vendor documentation about vendor governance**, renaming its own roles mid-documentation, with **two interpretive thresholds asserted on no stated basis and reproduced nowhere.** **Every number in either is a dated platform parameter and none appears here — not the default limit, not the lookback window, not the rate.**
