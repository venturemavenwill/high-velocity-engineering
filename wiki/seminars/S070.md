# S070 · Ship or Do Not Ship: First Rehearsal ⊘

**Module:** [M14](wiki/modules/M14.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](wiki/quarters/Q7.md) · **Complexity class:** 5
**Strands:** ME · SY · SG · PE
**Whitepaper:** [WP-070](wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md)
**Retrieves from:** S030, S050, S065, S066, S067, S068, S069
**Platform anchor:** the student's own four-day chain — a Foundry evaluation gate in CI, a trace or response evaluation of live traffic, a signal with a liveness path, and an arrangement with one enforced element · **MCP servers:** none in use during the assessed phases; the Foundry, Azure and Learn MCP Servers are available for questions of fact only

⊘ **Assessment-bearing day, the quarter's second entrustment judgement, and the first rehearsal of EA-1 on a live system with real users.**

---

## What this day is for

The gate is built and validated. The gap is measured and attributed. The detection design is written and its recipient has said what they would actually do at three in the morning. The arrangement has an owner, an authority, a record and a reversal path — or it was refused.

Today the change goes to the substrate's users, or it does not, and the student decides which.

**The evidence is insufficient. It was always going to be.** The offline number was about a different population, the online measurement was taken over one period through one collection path, the gate has an error rate nobody has measured on one side, and the detection design has an explicit list of things it will not see. All of that is correct work, done well, and it does not add up to a sufficient case, because no amount of it would.

The question is not whether the student has enough evidence. It is: **what are you willing to be wrong about, in front of the people it will happen to, and how will you know?**

**And one part of that question is new.** Four days of evidence were produced with an agent, against a platform, at speed. The gate's judge has never been shown to agree with a human about anything. The threshold was a default until somebody decided it. The online measurement was a sample drawn by a rule. The signal has a liveness property because a specification demanded one. **None of that makes the evidence bad, and all of it is part of what the evidence licenses** — so the decision artefact carries an item for it, and the defence asks about it.

## Departures from the pattern, named

This is an assessment-bearing day and the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) permits the substitution: phases 5 to 7 of the standard shape are replaced by an entrustment occasion, and this day reallocates the whole sequence around it. Three consequences are stated rather than left to be noticed.

- **There is no contrasting-case phase.** Phase 3 is the decision. The [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s requirement that four cases be executed is discharged across [S066](wiki/seminars/S066.md) to [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md), whose case sets this day consumes.
- **This day produces no build**, for the reason [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §3.1 gives and this design accepts unchanged: a judgement about a decision under pressure is invalid if the student is simultaneously constructing the evidence. **The four builds' prompt records, intervention records and frontier statements are carried into the defence** as part of the material the assessors hold, and artefact item 9 is where the student uses them.
- **Consolidating instruction is phase 6, after the assessed work.** The two platform points the standard requires of every day are therefore delivered there, with assessors absent, so that nothing taught on the day can be performed back at the people judging it.

## The entrustment judgement, stated before phase 1

> Today's judgement is on: **make and defend a ship / do-not-ship decision on a live system with real users.** It is the first rehearsal of the first entrusted activity, EA-1, and it is not that activity — EA-1 is assessed at ME390 across at least four occasions with at least three distinct assessors, and this is one occasion with two.
>
> **This is a rehearsal, and it is named as one.** The purpose of saying so is not to lower the stakes. The users are real and the change is real. It is to make clear that the level awarded today is a reading of where you are now, on the way to a standard you are not expected to have reached.
>
> Two assessors judge independently before conferring, and **form their level from your decision artefact before the defence begins**, per rater discipline rule 7. The defence tests the level; it does not create it. The expected level is **2b to 3a**.
>
> **The S030, S050 and S065 judgements are not carried forward.** Each occasion is judged on its own.
>
> **A defended decision not to ship is a pass, at the same levels as a decision to ship.** A ship with no stated failure modes is not a pass, whatever else is in the artefact.
>
> **Nothing surfaced in phase 7 reaches any assessor.** Assessors are absent for phases 6 and 7 and receive no account of them.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.** Nothing here is recorded and nothing contributes to the judgement.

1. **Will you ship?** Yes, no, or under stated conditions. Written before you re-read anything.
2. **What is the strongest argument against your answer?**
3. **What will you find out afterwards that you do not know now?**
4. Predict your supervision level.
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** If you handed the agent your four days of evidence and asked it whether to ship, **what would it say, and what would it be unable to say?** Write both. Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 2 is the day's opening demand and is the [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) A4 mechanism administered to the student against their own position, before the artefact is written.

Item 5 is the programme's last frontier item before [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md) and it is deliberately asymmetric: the second half is the one that matters. An agent handed this evidence will produce a defensible recommendation, and **what it cannot produce is a statement of what the person making the decision is willing to be wrong about**, because that requires knowing who will be harmed and being answerable to them. A student who names that gap has stated the programme's central claim about its own primary instrument, unprompted, at the last rehearsal before the final.

**Warrant.** Pretesting the decision before the evidence is re-read, so that the movement between item 1 and phase 3 is measurable rather than remembered. Item 3 is the day's honest item: it is answered again at phase 5 with each student's own five-month-old version of the same question in front of them. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 and may not migrate, per instrumentation rule 8; it is placed on an assessment-bearing day because the frontier map's most valuable datum is the one taken when the stakes are real, and it is safe there because phase 1 is never recorded.

- **[S067](wiki/seminars/S067.md):** the five differences, and what an online measurement licenses.
- **[S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md):** the four-part detection target, and the three detection failures.

- **[S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md):** the two symmetric failures, and the sentence that will survive the conversation.
- **[S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md):** what the cost argument established, and to whom.

- **Name a way this could go wrong that none of the four days would have surfaced.**

The answer wanted: **a consequence that arrives after you have stopped looking.** S030's measurement was defended in the room and settled there; S050's finding was communicated and the encounter ended. Today's decision produces users who are affected next week, by something the student will or will not detect, and the student is answerable for that either way.

**Warrant.** Cumulative retrieval across the module and three earlier entrustment occasions, closing with the boundary item this design has used at S030, S035, S040 and S045, and a bridge that names what makes a class-5 occasion different from a class-4 one. The bridge is answered by the room.

## Phase 3 · The decision (60 min)

**The entrusted activity. Assessors observe and do not intervene. Staff answer questions of fact and refuse questions of judgement.**

Students work individually with everything they have produced across S066 to S069, and with the substrate's current state.

Required output before phase 4:

1. **The decision** — ship, do not ship, or ship under stated conditions. **A condition with no named verifier is not a condition, and an artefact containing one is assessed as an unconditional ship.**
2. **The evidence position:** what the gate said, what the offline number was, what the online measurement was, what the gap was, and what it was attributed to — with the distinguishing evidence. **And the cost position:** the [S064](wiki/seminars/S064.md) commitment shape with its break-even and its demand forecast, set against **the realised spend of the billing period that closes this week** — and **which [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md) category-3 term you now account for and which you still do not.**
3. **Three failure modes you are accepting**, each with who experiences it, how often, and **whether you will detect it.**
4. **The detection commitment:** what you will know, within what time, observed by whom, at what false-alarm rate — **and what happens if the signal itself fails.**
5. **Who owns each failure you cannot detect**, or the statement that nobody does.
6. **The reversal path and its cost**, in time, in user experience, and in anything that cannot be undone.
7. **The user-facing sentence:** what a substrate user should know, in their words, in one sentence.
8. **The strongest case against your own decision**, stated as its proponent would state it — and what you say to it.
9. **The tooling position.** Of the evidence in items 2 and 4, **what was produced by an agent, what did you verify yourself, and what are you relying on without having checked?** Three lists, however short. Then one sentence: **which of your gate's settings you decided and which you accepted**, and **what your gate's judge has never been shown to do.**

Item 8 is the [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) A4 mechanism, which produced the only recorded refusals to ship in that day's records, applied here to the student's own position.

Item 9 is new and is not a tooling audit. **It is a statement about what the evidence licenses**, which is [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s subject and this programme's oldest demand, arriving at the point where the evidence was produced faster than any cohort before this one could have produced it. Its second sentence has a correct answer available in one line — the judge is an uncalibrated model, because no agreement with human judgement is published for it — and a student who cannot supply that line is reporting a score whose provenance they have not established.

**Item 9's boundary, stated with the item.** Three clean lists are producible here because your evidence is four days old, you made it, and the platform documents its own settings. **They are not producible for an evidence chain assembled from several tools, several people and several years**, which is the ordinary condition of any system older than the person operating it. In that case the honest output is the boundary itself: *here is where I stopped being able to establish provenance.* **An empty third list on a four-day chain is not credible**, and the assessors will say so.

**Warrant.** Individual decision-making with factual support only, which is the assessment's validity requirement and matches the construction at [S020](wiki/seminars/S020.md) and [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md). **Items 4 and 5 are the artefact elements [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) and [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) named under instrumentation rule 6, and item 2's cost clause carries both the element [S064](wiki/seminars/S064.md) named and the category-3 element [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md) named** — a commitment made in week 64 against a forecast, defended in week 70 against the realised spend, alongside a statement of which of week 65's uncosted terms is now accounted for, which together are the mechanism by which a class-5 decision acquires a consequence the student cannot argue with. Item 1's condition clause exists because a conditional ship is the reflex of a student who does not want to decide, and a condition nobody will check is a ship with a sentence in front of it. **Item 9 is where the four days' prompt, intervention and frontier records are used**; the records themselves are in front of the assessors and are not separately assessed, because the judgement is on the decision and not on the tooling, and item 9 is the point at which the tooling becomes a property of the decision.

## Phase 4 · The defence (60 min)

Groups of four. Fifteen minutes each, to **two assessors, one substrate user who would be affected by the change, and the staff member accountable for the service.**

Assessors have formed a provisional level from the artefact before this phase begins, per rater discipline rule 7, and record whether the defence confirmed, raised or lowered it.

Questions published in advance:

- What does your evidence license, and what does it not?
- What will this do badly, to whom, and will you know?
- What would make you reverse it, and how long would that take?
- *(From the user)* **What should I know before this affects me?**
- *(From the accountable owner)* **What are you asking me to accept that you cannot detect?**
- If you are not shipping: what would have to be true for you to?
- **What if you are wrong?** — asked as perturbation, in EA-1's form: *what if the interval were twice as wide; what if the affected population were the one that cannot complain; what if the gap you attributed to population was the criterion.*
- **Which part of the evidence in front of me did you check yourself, and which part are you taking on trust?** — asked from item 9, and followed by *what would have to be true for the part you took on trust to be wrong?*
- **Your [S064](wiki/seminars/S064.md) commitment assumed a demand forecast. The billing period closed this week. What did it actually cost, and does your recommendation still hold?**
- **If your [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) arrangement was refused: what did you change?**

**Warrant.** Defence after the level is formed, per rule 7. The user's question cannot be answered in technical terms and is [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md)'s demand at class 5. The accountable owner's question is new to this occasion and is the one most students will not have prepared for, because the artefact's item 5 is where they will have written *nobody*. The perturbation set is EA-1's and is the day's only element taken directly from the assessed activity rather than from a rehearsal of it. **The trust question is the practical turn's contribution to the defence** and is deliberately not a question about tools: it asks what the student's evidence rests on, which is the same question [S030](wiki/seminars/S030.md) asked about a measurement and [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md) asked about a finding, in the register where this cohort's evidence is now actually produced. **The final question is the [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) rule-6 response occasion.**

## Phase 5 · The carry-forward check (35 min)

**Assessors' levels are recorded and sealed before this phase begins.** Nothing in it enters today's judgement.

**Part one — the [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md) comparison.** [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §8 conceded, without qualification, that the programme had no evidence that behaviour under constructed pressure predicts behaviour under real pressure, that this day was where the comparison could be made, and that it required one field in the S070 record. **That field exists.**

Each student's S050 entrustment level — awarded on four constructed audiences played by trained role-players — is retrieved and compared by the two assessors against their conduct today, in front of a real user, a real decision-owner and a real consequence. The comparison is written as a **separate field, after today's level is sealed**, and it is not an input to it. Individual comparisons are returned privately. The cohort sees the aggregate: how many students' real-pressure conduct was above, at, or below their constructed-pressure level, and in which direction the divergence ran.

The field has **two parts, and the second is the informative one.** The first is the whole-occasion comparison [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §8 asked for. The second compares S050's activity against the part of today that is the same activity: **the answer to the user's question at phase 4, and the item 7 user-facing sentence.** A divergence in the first part is ambiguous, because a communication occasion and a decision occasion are not the same thing; a divergence in the second is about the same capability under two pressures, which is what the concession wanted tested. Both are recorded. [WP-070](wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §8 argues that the second part is under-dosed and says what would fix it.

**Part two — the category-3 statement.** [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §8 also conceded that S050's category-3 result — failures that occurred on the student's deployed model and were not stated in advance — was measured, reported and dropped. It proposed carrying it forward with a requirement that the student say what they have changed. **That was built at [S066](wiki/seminars/S066.md) step 6 and is confirmed here.**

Each student receives their S050 category-3 list and their S066 step 6 answers, and writes one paragraph: **what they have changed in how they enumerate failure modes, and where in today's artefact the change is visible.** They point at a specific item. The cohort sees the S066 recatch rate — how many previously unstated failures the gates the cohort designed would now catch — which is the response occasion [S066](wiki/seminars/S066.md) named under instrumentation rule 6.

**No assessment attaches to either part, and that is stated before S050 as well as here.** The requirement is to have used the information, not to have scored well on it.

**Part three — the [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md) decision comparison.** [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md) named this day as the response occasion for its phase 3 decision split, asking whether declining to ship there predicts anything about conduct here. Each student's S060 decision — ship, conditional or decline — and the level awarded for it are retrieved and set against today's decision and level, **in the same sealed-after-level field as parts one and two.** It is written by the assessors and costs the student no phase time. The cohort sees the transition table; individual results are returned privately. Like the other two parts it is not assessed, and like them it is a measurement of the programme rather than of the student.

**Warrant.** The programme's fourth long-horizon prediction check, after S020, S040 and S050, and the first that compares a student against their own earlier assessed conduct rather than against their own earlier claim. Both of parts one and two discharge concessions [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §8 made explicitly and named this day for; part three discharges the obligation [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md) placed here under instrumentation rule 6. The sealing of today's level before the phase runs is what makes all three tests of the programme's assumptions rather than contaminants of the judgement they are testing.

## Phase 6 · Consolidating instruction (35 min) — assessors absent

1. **What the module was for.** A release gate is an evaluation artefact with a construct, a criterion, a judge, a threshold and an error structure, and nobody validates it. Every other day in the module is a consequence of that sentence.

2. **The cohort's decisions**, shown unattributed: the ship, do-not-ship and conditional split, and **what distinguished them** — which is rarely the quality of the evidence and is usually the detection design.

3. **What the users said** at phase 4, reported by the module lead. Including what they asked that nobody had prepared for.

4. **What the accountable owner said** they were being asked to accept, and how often the answer to item 5 was *nobody*.

5. **Why not shipping is a result.** The programme's position, restated at class 5 where the cost of not shipping is real, is visible, and falls on people who are not in the room — which is [S066](wiki/seminars/S066.md) point 2 and [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) phase 6 arriving in the student's own decision.

6. **What happens next.** The shipped changes run for the remainder of the quarter. Their behaviour is reported at [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md), against the detection commitments made today, and the decision records are the material [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md) negotiates from.

7. **What was chosen for you across the whole module, in one list.** The module's required platform point, delivered here because this is the day's consolidating phase. **A pass threshold of three. A conversation split at the last turn. An evaluator set that grows when tool definitions appear. A sampling strategy that defaults to uniform random. A lookback window that defaults to seven days and cannot exceed seven days. An hourly evaluation cap of a hundred runs, above which runs are skipped and nothing says so. A permission map assembled by whoever provisioned the project.** Every one of those was a decision, none of them was announced, and **the decision you defended this morning rests on all seven.** That is not an argument against the platform. It is the argument for the sentence this module opened with — a gate is an evaluation artefact and nobody validates it — and it is now a list the room can check rather than a claim they were asked to accept.

8. **What is perishable and what you keep.** Every number in point 7 will change and several are labelled preview by their own documentation. **What you keep is the question**: what did somebody decide for me, and what does this evidence license. **That is safe to carry into any system, on any platform, for the rest of your career. What is unsafe is carrying the numbers**, or carrying the belief that because this vendor documents its defaults, the next one will. The list at point 7 was reconstructible today because it was written down. Most are not.

**Warrant.** Consolidation after the assessed work, with assessors absent, so that every point indexes something the student has just done. Point 2's split is the day's instruction and is the cohort's own evidence that a release decision is not a quality decision. Point 6 names the two forward occasions and their instruments rather than promising a review in general. **Points 7 and 8 are the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s two required platform points**, placed after the assessed phases because a list of defaults delivered before the decision would be performed back at the assessors, and delivered as a single module-wide list because that is the form in which it is usable — seven decisions, none announced, all underneath a judgement the student has just defended in front of a real user.

## Phase 7 · Error clinic (40 min) — assessors absent

Opened by **a staff member who shipped a change on this substrate and reversed it**, with the failure modes and the detection commitment they had stated beforehand, read out as written.

Then the agent case, and it is the module's last: a decision brief that was **confidently wrong and passed review.** A colleague asked an agent to summarise whether a change was safe to release, gave it the evaluation results, the online numbers and the monitoring configuration, and got back a clear, well-organised, correctly-cited recommendation to ship. Every figure in it was accurate. **It reported the gate as passed, which was true; it reported the online measurement as stable, which was true of the sample; and it did not say that the sample was drawn by a default, that the judge was uncalibrated, or that one of the accepted failures had no owner** — because none of those was in the material it was given and none of them is a thing a brief is usually asked for. The reviewer read it, found nothing wrong, and was right. **What was missing was not an error. It was the entire content of item 9.**

Then students, in threes: **what you are least confident about in what you just decided.**

**Firewall applies. Nothing said here reaches an assessor, and this is the sixth demonstration of that under assessment conditions.**

**Warrant.** Error management immediately after the highest-consequence decision the cohort has made, opened by someone who made the same decision and was wrong. The staff member's prior statement is read as written rather than summarised, because the gap between what a competent engineer predicted and what happened is the phase's content and paraphrase closes it. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition** and is placed last in the module deliberately: it is the only one of the five whose failure is an omission rather than a defect, it is the omission the module's whole apparatus exists to prevent, and every student in the room has just written the artefact that would have caught it.

## Assessment

**Assessment-bearing. The quarter's second entrustment judgement, and the first rehearsal of EA-1 on a live system.**

Three outputs:

1. **The module artefact** — the S066 to S069 specification items — pass/fail, tokens available.
2. **The release decision and its defence** — a specification decision.
3. **The entrustment judgement** on *make and defend a ship / do-not-ship decision on a live system with real users*, formed independently by two assessors, **from the artefact first**, per rule 7.

**Ordinal level** from the scale in [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md): 1, 2a, 2b, 3a, 3b, 3c, 4a, 4b. **Expected 2b to 3a. 3b is available and will be rare.**

**Narrative substantiation is mandatory** and must cite specific moments from the artefact and the defence, not impressions of them.

**Named failure conditions.**

- **A ship with no stated failure modes is below 2b**, whatever else the artefact contains.
- **A decision whose accepted failures have neither a detection commitment nor a named owner is below 2b.** Both may legitimately be absent for a given failure; both absent with no statement that they are is not a decision.
- **A conditional ship whose condition has no named verifier is assessed as an unconditional ship.**
- **A decision that reports a judged score as evidence of quality, with no statement of what its judge has been shown to do, is below 2b.** This is [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s standard applied to the evidence the student actually has: reporting a score whose provenance you have not established is reporting a result as licensing more than it does. **Naming the uncalibration and shipping anyway is a complete answer** and is expected to be the common one; the condition bites only on silence.
- **A defended decision not to ship, with its reasons and with the conditions under which the student would ship, is a pass at the same levels as a decision to ship.**
- **An artefact whose item 8 counter-argument is weaker than the one the student wrote at item 1** indicates the case against was constructed to be beaten, and the assessors say so in the narrative.

Per rule 1, the checked elements are: the decision with its conditions and their verifiers; the evidence position with the gap's attribution and distinguishing evidence; **the cost position with the [S064](wiki/seminars/S064.md) commitment, its break-even and the realised spend of the closing billing period, the forecast and its confidence in the same field per specification rule 2, and the [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md) category-3 term now accounted for with the one still not**; three failure modes each with an affected party, a frequency and a detection statement; the four-part detection commitment including its own failure case; an owner or an explicit none for each undetectable failure; the reversal path with its cost; the user-facing sentence; the strongest case against with the student's answer; **the tooling position — the three lists, the decided-against-accepted settings statement, and the statement of what the gate's judge has been shown to do**; and the phase 5 carry-forward paragraph. **Nothing else is assessed** — not whether the change shipped, not the quality of the underlying change, and **not the four days' prompt, intervention or frontier records**, which are in front of the assessors as context and are the material item 9 is written from.

**Phase 5's results are not assessed**, and the specification says so in writing, for the reason [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §7 gave and this design accepts unchanged: an assessed prediction check is a strategy.

**Error-clinic firewall.** Assessors are absent for phases 6 and 7 and receive no account of them.

**Token economy.** This day is within the quarter's individual allocation; a deferred attempt costs one token and is re-run on a different change, not the same one.

**Rule-6 discharge.** Seven obligations land on this day and **all seven elements exist.**

- **[WP-066](wiki/whitepapers/WP-066.md) §7** named this day as the response occasion for the step 6 category-3 recatch rate. **That is phase 5 part two, and the cohort rate is reported at phase 8.**
