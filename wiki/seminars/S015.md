# S015 · Running a Workshop End to End ⊘

**Module:** [M03](/wiki/modules/M03.md) · **Quarter:** [Q2](/wiki/quarters/Q2.md) · **Complexity class:** 2
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Foundry prompt agents — capturing a workshop's output as a declarative agent specification, then using the agent to attack the specification
**Whitepaper:** [WP-015](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-015.md)
**Retrieves from:** S011, S012, S013, S014
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

This is an assessment occasion. You run a discovery session end to end, under time, with a room you do not control, and you defend what came out of it.

The room will not cooperate. That is arranged.

And at the end you will do something the module has been building towards without saying so: you will write the session's finding as a **specification an agent could be built from**, and then use that agent to attack the specification. If the finding cannot survive being made executable, it was a sentence rather than a finding.

Answer these in writing before you begin, and hand them in with everything else.

1. Which of your four artefacts — charter, gate, facilitation plan, rehearsal record — do you expect to abandon first when the session goes wrong?
2. Your gate has an unmet condition on the morning. Do you run? Who decides, and what gets written?
3. At the end, a participant says they are excited and will "definitely look at getting some time booked". Is that a commitment?
4. What will you record if nobody commits to anything?
5. What would make this session's finding wrong?

**Item 5 is the one the assessment is really about.** Items 1 to 4 you can answer from the module. Item 5 requires you to state a finding in a form that has a falsifier attached, and most candidates discover while writing it that what they were going to record has no falsifier because it is not a claim about the customer's world.

## What you must produce

Six artefacts, submitted together.

1. **The charter**, from [S011](/wiki/seminars/S011.md), amended if you have changed your mind, with the amendments visible rather than absorbed.
2. **The readiness gate**, from [S012](/wiki/seminars/S012.md), with its conditions marked met, unmet or waived, every waiver carrying the name of the person who waived it, and every platform premise carrying its check and its date.
3. **The facilitation plan**, from [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md), including the forbidden list you took into the room.
4. **The session**, run in front of assessors, with an assigned difficulty introduced without warning.
5. **The closing record** — what the customer committed to, or the explicit statement that they committed to nothing, together with what was asked and what was declined.
6. **The finding as a specification** — your session's central finding written as a declarative agent definition, with what would show it wrong stated inside the artefact rather than beside it.

## The material

### What this assessment refuses to look at, and why

The scheme is unusual in what it will not examine. It does not care whether the customer committed. It does not care whether the room enjoyed it, whether you recovered from the difficulty, or whether your finding turns out to be correct.

**Every one of those is either the customer's behaviour or luck, and assessing you on either is assessing the wrong person.** A scheme whose result varies with the sponsor's mood, the assigned difficulty's severity and the calendar is not measuring you.

What it checks instead is whether the artefacts held: that the charter's failure condition could fire and was observed against, that the gate's inward condition was actually checked, that every waiver names somebody, that the prohibitions survived pressure or the breach was self-reported, that a commitment was distinguished from a warm sentence by what somebody gave up, and that an absence was recorded rather than smoothed.

### A commitment is identified by its cost

The distinction you will be defending is not subtle and it is not intuitive. **Commitment** means a stakeholder gives up something they value; **advancement** means the relationship moves to a defined next stage; and the currencies are meaningful time, reputational risk and money ([research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md)). A friendly contact who repeatedly meets and praises without committing or rejecting is a **zombie lead**, and the process failure is leaving with neither advancement nor rejection.

Two cautions from the same source apply directly to this assessment. A trial is not automatically a commitment — opening a free account costs almost nothing, while supplying data or changing a workflow may cost a great deal. And **a person without authority may be unable to commit despite a genuine need**, which is why the specification checks that you can tell the difference rather than that you obtained one.

**Its limits.** A founder-oriented practitioner handbook: no controlled comparison, no sampling framework, no measured effect, and conversation counts that are the author's anecdote. The local copy establishes no publisher, year or ISBN.

### Why the closing record is written before the debrief

A group that has discussed a session has agreed a story about it, and the record written afterwards is a record of the story.

The assessment literature makes the underlying mechanism concrete, and it is worse than it sounds. Assessors reading a portfolio reached judgements through a cyclical process of acquiring, organising and integrating evidence — and then **"were inclined to stick to their initial judgments even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence"**, producing final judgements more elaborate than their first ones but not substantially different ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). Additional reading improved the articulation of a conclusion already reached.

If that is what a trained assessor does with a written record in front of them, a facilitator reconstructing a session from memory after their colleagues have told them what happened is not recording anything. **They are elaborating a conclusion somebody else reached in the corridor.**

The same literature explains why your defence is a conversation rather than a form. In that study, **"assessors mainly relied on narrative feedback to inform their judgment… Grades and qualifications were merely used to confirm impressions based on narratives."** The prose was the evidence; the numbers checked a conclusion already formed from it. It also found that assessors reaching the same pass-or-fail outcome differed in their reasoning, driven by their beliefs about which evidence is trustworthy, their private theories of what competence is, and the inferences they drew about the person — and that those private theories **deviated from the formal criteria.**

The remedy it recommends is the one this assessment uses: multiple assessors, substantiated judgements, and group discussion for anything high-stakes, because deliberation surfaces the divergent reasoning that identical outcomes conceal.

**Its limits.** Two studies from medical education at a single institution, with a specific portfolio type and competency framework; transfer to engineering assessment is an argument rather than a given. The portfolio study used mock portfolios and assessors who did not know the students. **Think-aloud methods cannot capture fast or automatic processing, and participants may construct plausible explanations for a researcher rather than report actual reasoning** — the authors state this themselves. And the findings describe how assessors reason; they **do not establish that narrative-based judgement is more valid than score-based judgement.**

### A specification check is not an entrustment decision

This matters for what your result means afterwards.

An **ad-hoc** entrustment judgement is the continuous informal one a supervisor makes when the learner's skills appear to match the situation's risk. A **summative** entrustment decision "has the nature of certification", is taken about future responsibilities, and requires **agreement between multiple observers and multiple observations** to avoid subjective bias ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)).

This assessment is neither. It is a closed-list specification check, run once, and **it generates no entrustment evidence at all.** Saying so is not modesty; it is the difference between a claim this design can support and one it cannot.

Two further pieces of that framework shape the defence. The **entrustment-based discussion** is a short conversation after the activity that probes deep understanding and **must include what-if questions**, because retrospective explanation shows only that the learner understood the case they met, and counterfactual questioning is the only evidence available about the cases they did not. That is exactly why one of your three defence questions is *what would show this finding is wrong*. And the framework names three professionalism characteristics in behavioural terms — **integrity**, meaning truthfulness in reports to a supervisor; **reliability**, meaning stable and conscientious working; and **humility**, meaning the ability to observe one's own limitations and willingness to ask for help. The self-recorded breach is a direct test of the first and third.

**Its limits.** A primer by the concept's originator: guidance, not evaluation; it reports adoption rather than effectiveness. It is a clinical model with legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint, and **a course must construct its stakes artificially.** Its full evidence standard is expensive, and this assessment is an explicitly reduced version of it.

### Why an absence scores

The corpus behind this stage reports **no failed workshop, no declined engagement, and no delivered system reported to have survived** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). Every case study succeeds; its single failure narrative is a synthetic composite and fictional by its own admission.

A programme that only rewards positive findings reproduces that shape in its graduates. **Rewarding a recorded absence is the cheapest available counterweight and it is an argument rather than a finding.**

### The finding as a specification

Now the last movement, and it is the one that makes the whole module's argument executable.

A finding stated in prose can absorb any amount of subsequent contradiction, because prose has no interface. A **prompt agent** — a declarative configuration with no compute of its own, defined by instructions, tools and knowledge — forces the finding to say who it serves, what it may use, what it may do and where it stops ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). The documented split is a hosting distinction: prompt agents use "declarative config in portal or code", are "managed by Foundry with no agent compute", and suit "agents that don't include custom code or require custom hosting"; hosted agents run your containerised code on managed container compute.

That is the right instrument here precisely **because it cannot do very much.** You are not building the system. You are writing down what the session established, in a form where the gaps are structural rather than rhetorical.

Two further documented facts bear on the artefact. The rapid setup path "lacks network isolation" while the controlled one gives "fine-grained control over data, security, and networking" — so a specification that assumes production topology must say so. And the whole family states that the options "aren't mutually exclusive" and compose through MCP, which means your specification's boundary is a claim about components rather than about products.

**Its limits.** First-party vendor selection guidance, read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, comparing Microsoft options only with each other, with several tables truncated. **No adoption, cost, failure-rate or outcome data of any kind**, and product names in active flux.

## The workflow

**Write your finding as a specification, then use the agent built from it to attack it.**

This runs after the session and before the debrief. Steps 1 to 3 need only a text editor; step 5 needs a Foundry project with a model deployment.

**Step 1 — write the closing record first, on paper, before you speak to anybody.** This is not a formality. It is the only version of the record that is yours.

**Step 2 — state the finding as a claim about the customer's world**, not about the session. "They are enthusiastic about agents" is about the room. "Their claims-triage backlog is caused by a handoff between two teams that neither owns" is about them, and can be wrong.

**Step 3 — write the specification, and put the falsifier inside it.**

```yaml
# finding-as-agent.yaml — illustrative shape. Foundry's declarative agent schema
# changes; verify field names against current documentation before reuse.
name: claims-triage-handoff-finding
kind: prompt_agent            # declarative config, no agent compute of its own
model: <deployment name>       # a deployment, not a model family — record which

instructions: |
  You act for a claims-triage supervisor at the customer, not for us.
  The finding this agent encodes: the triage backlog is produced by a handoff
  between intake and adjudication that neither team owns, not by adjudication
  throughput.
  You may only use the two knowledge sources below. If a question needs anything
  else, say which source is missing and stop.

knowledge:
  - name: intake_queue_export        # WHAT WE WERE GIVEN
    obtained: "the session, from the named intake lead"
  - name: adjudication_sla_definition
    obtained: "the session, from the operations manager"

tools:
  - name: summarise_queue_ages
    # No write tools. This agent asserts a finding; it does not act on one.

stops_at:
  - "Any question about individual claimants. Out of scope and out of consent."
  - "Any recommendation to change staffing. Nobody in the room owned that."

# THE FALSIFIER LIVES IN THE ARTEFACT, NOT BESIDE IT.
finding_is_wrong_if:
  - "Queue age is flat across the handoff and rises inside adjudication."
  - "The intake lead can name an owner for the handoff whom we did not meet."
  - "The backlog persists in a period when intake volume was near zero."

# WHAT THE SESSION DID NOT ESTABLISH, recorded rather than omitted.
not_established:
  - "Whether the handoff is a policy or a habit."
  - "Who, if anyone, is accountable for end-to-end cycle time."
```

**Step 4 — read the `not_established` list back against your charter.** If it is empty, you have not been honest. If it contains the thing your charter existed to decide, say so in the closing record.

**Step 5 — build it and interrogate it as the customer.** Deploy the prompt agent and ask it the questions the sponsor would ask. Watch where it stops. **A stop is a finding about your session, not a defect in the agent.**

**Step 6 — attack your own finding with the agent.** Give it your specification and one instruction: build the strongest case that this finding is an artefact of who was in the room rather than a property of the customer's world. Then ask it which of your three falsifiers could not be observed by anybody you actually have access to. Record what you kept and what changed — **and if the finding did not survive, the record of it not surviving is the submission.**

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** That a finding has an interface. Written as a specification, its knowledge sources are things somebody gave you or things you assumed, its stop conditions are questions you cannot answer, and its falsifiers are either observable or wishful. **You will find at least one falsifier that nobody you can reach could ever check**, and that discovery is worth more than the agent.

## What good looks like

- **The charter's failure condition is capable of firing**, and you can say what you observed against it.
- **The readiness gate contains at least one condition about your own organisation**, and that condition was checked rather than assumed.
- **Every waiver in the gate carries a named person.** A waiver recorded without a name is recorded as unmet.
- **The prohibitions in your forbidden list were honoured during the assigned difficulty**, or the breach is recorded by you rather than found by the assessor.
- **The closing record distinguishes a commitment from an expression of interest**, using what was given up rather than what was said.
- **An absence of commitment is recorded as an outcome**, not smoothed into a description of a positive session.
- **The session's finding is stated so that it could be wrong**, and the falsifier is inside the specification rather than beside it.
- The specification's `not_established` list is non-empty and at least one entry is something you wish you knew.

## How it goes wrong

- **The abandoned charter** — the session drifts, the charter stays in the folder, and the closing record describes what happened rather than what was decided. *Warning sign:* you have not looked at the charter since the first hour.
- **The retrospective waiver** — a condition was unmet, the session ran, and the waiver is written afterwards in the passive voice. *Warning sign:* the record says the condition was waived and cannot say by whom.
- **Interest banked as commitment** — a warm sentence about future intent is written into the closing record as a next step. *Warning sign:* the recorded commitment has no person, no date and no cost to anybody.
- **The rescued difficulty** — you handled the assigned difficulty by breaking a prohibition, it worked, and you are pleased. *Warning sign:* your account of the moment is about the outcome.
- **The smoothed absence** — nobody committed, and the record says the session built strong foundations. *Warning sign:* the closing record's most concrete noun is "alignment".
- **The unfalsifiable finding** — the specification's falsifiers are conditions nobody could observe, or are restatements of the finding with a negative in front. *Warning sign:* you cannot name who would see the falsifier fire.
- **The debriefed record** — the closing record was written after the room agreed what had happened. *Warning sign:* your record uses a phrase somebody else used first.

## Assessment

Seven checked elements. Each is checked present and adequate, or not.

1. The charter's failure condition is capable of firing, and the candidate can say what was observed against it.
2. The readiness gate contains at least one condition about the candidate's own organisation, and that condition was checked rather than assumed.
3. Every waiver carries a named person. A waiver recorded without a name is recorded as an unmet condition.
4. The prohibitions were honoured during the assigned difficulty, or the breach is self-recorded rather than found by the assessor.
5. The closing record distinguishes a commitment from an expression of interest by what was given up.
6. An absence of commitment is recorded as an outcome.
7. The finding is stated so that it could be wrong, the falsifier sits inside the specification, and what the session did not establish is recorded rather than omitted.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Whether the room enjoyed the session is not marked, whether the customer committed is not marked, whether you recovered from the assigned difficulty is not marked, whether you finished on time is not marked, whether the finding is correct is not marked, and the quality of any material you presented — including the specification's structure — is not marked.

**This produces no entrustment evidence.** It is a specification check run once by assessors who deliberate; a summative entrustment decision requires multiple observers and multiple observations, and this is not one.

## Exercises

**Elements 1 to 7 above are assessed. These are not**, and they are the preparation the specification assumes.

**1 · Run it.** You will receive an assigned difficulty during the session, unannounced, drawn from the [S014](/wiki/seminars/S014.md) catalogue. You may not choose it and you will not be told when it starts.

> **What to notice.** The difficulty is unannounced because a rehearsed one tests preparation and an unannounced one tests whether the prohibition survives surprise, which is the property [S014](/wiki/seminars/S014.md) actually asserts. **If you notice yourself deciding whether to break a prohibition, write down the moment before you write down the decision.**

**2 · Write the closing record before you discuss the session with anybody.**

> **Why this is a rule rather than a suggestion.** A group that has discussed an event has agreed a story about it. The assessment literature finds that judges anchor on an initial conclusion and that further evidence produces a more elaborate version of the same judgement rather than a different one ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). **Its limits: medical education, single institution, mock portfolios, and think-aloud methods that cannot capture fast processing and may elicit plausible reconstructions rather than actual reasoning.**

**3 · Attach the customer paragraph.** One paragraph: what you learned about the customer that you did not know before, stated as something about their situation rather than about their reaction to you.

> **Scoring criterion.** A passing paragraph could be checked by somebody who was not there, by asking somebody at the customer. "They're more ready than we thought" fails. If you cannot write the paragraph, say so — **a session that produced no such paragraph is a defensible submission and an honest one**, and it is worth more than an invented one.

**4 · Grade three falsifiers.** For your specification's three `finding_is_wrong_if` entries, name who would observe each one, with what, and when.

> **Model answers.** A passing falsifier names an observation available to a person you can reach. "Queue age is flat across the handoff" passes if somebody will give you queue ages. "The real cause is cultural" fails on all three counts — no observer, no instrument, no occasion. **Expect at least one of your three to fail**, and record which; the failure is the finding about your session.

**5 · Defend the closing record** to an assessor who has read only your charter. Their questions are the same three every time: *what did they give up?*, *what would show this finding is wrong?*, and *which of your own prohibitions did you break?*

> **Why the questions are published in advance.** They cannot be answered from preparation alone — *what did they give up* has no answer unless somebody gave something up, and *which prohibition did you break* has no safe answer. **Preparing for them is doing the work**, and a specification a candidate could not have read is a trap rather than a standard.
>
> **What the third question is testing.** Truthfulness in a report to a supervisor, and the willingness to name your own limits, are behavioural professionalism criteria in the entrustment literature ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). A self-recorded breach scores. A breach the assessor finds does not. **Its limits: a primer reporting adoption rather than effectiveness, from a clinical setting whose stakes a course must construct artificially.**

**6 · Go back to your own item 5.** Find what you wrote before the session about what would make the finding wrong. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside what you actually wrote into the specification and write one sentence naming the difference.

> **Criterion.** A passing comparison identifies whether your pre-session answer named an observation or a mood. Most pre-session answers are of the form *if the customer disagrees* — which makes the customer's opinion the falsifier and is therefore not a claim about their world at all. **The difference between those two answers is the whole assessment.**
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this module's structure rests on tested exactly this: presenting a correct answer beside a learner's own produced no benefit, and only the group **instructed to compare them** learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). **Its limits: school, university and laboratory evidence, with no adult-professional study in its reference list.**

## What this does not establish

This assessment shows that you can run the instrument and record its output honestly. **It does not show that the instrument works.**

No workshop in the corpus behind this stage is reported to have failed, and no delivered system is reported to have survived. The assessed properties are all properties of your artefacts — that a condition can fire, that a waiver has a name, that a commitment cost somebody something. **Every one of them is checkable and none of them is evidence about outcomes.**

That is deliberate and it is a limit. A programme that assessed you on whether the customer committed would be assessing the customer.

**It generates no entrustment evidence.** A summative entrustment decision requires agreement between multiple observers across multiple observations, and this is a specification check run once. The framework supplying that standard is a primer by the concept's originator which **reports adoption rather than effectiveness**, comes from a clinical setting with legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint, and describes an evidence standard this assessment deliberately reduces.

**The claim that a pre-debrief record differs from a post-debrief one is design reasoning.** The assessment literature supporting the mechanism is two studies from medical education at a single institution, using mock portfolios and assessors who did not know the students; **think-aloud methods cannot capture fast or automatic processing and participants may construct plausible explanations rather than report actual reasoning**, which the authors state themselves. Those findings describe how assessors reason and **do not establish that narrative-based judgement is more valid than score-based judgement.** No comparison of pre- and post-debrief records has ever been run here.

**The commitment distinction rests on a practitioner handbook** with no controlled comparison, no sampling framework and no measured effect, whose local copy establishes no publisher, year or ISBN. It tells you how to identify a costly act; it does not tell you that a costly act predicts anything.

**The platform documentation describes a hosting distinction and evidences no outcome.** It was read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, several tables were truncated, it compares Microsoft options only with each other, and product names are in active flux. **Nothing in it says that writing a finding as a declarative specification improves the finding** — that is this module's argument, and it is untested.

**No effect size is asserted anywhere in this session.** Direction and mechanism only.
