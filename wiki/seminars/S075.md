# S075 · Evaluating a Trajectory, Not an Answer

**Module:** [M15](/wiki/modules/M15.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md) · **Complexity class:** 5
**Strands:** ME · IS · SG
**Whitepaper:** [WP-075](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-075.md)
**Depends on:** S046, S048, S067, S074
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Agent Framework — conversation split strategies and cloud evaluation · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

[S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) point 3 established that an attention map is **a routing record, not an explanation**: a weight says how much of a value entered a sum, and it does not say the output depended on it. Point 4 established the only attribution this programme accepts, which is intervention. [WP-052](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) §6 then wrote down what its own day had not done, and named this day:

> **S075 at roughly six months is the most valuable target and the least likely to fire.** An agent trace is a routing record that reads as an explanation, and it is the same error in a domain where the artefact is even more convincing. Nothing in this day names the trajectory case, which is a gap.

Today closes it.

A trajectory is a record of which components ran and what they returned. It is a routing record in exactly [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md)'s sense, and it is **more convincing than an attention map for a reason that has nothing to do with the computation.** An attention map is a heatmap. It looks like data, and a student has to be talked into reading a story in it. A trajectory is prose. It is written in the first person, it gives reasons, and it reads as an explanation **because it is written in the form of one.** No property of the underlying system changed to make that so.

And [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) point 8 handed this day its hardest problem by name: **the trajectory record is produced by the same loop that produced the failure.** For a selection or an output-utilisation failure the record shows a clean run — the tool was called, it succeeded, the value came back. [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 4, verbatim, at the record rather than at the monitor: the instrument and the observation moved together, so the difference between them carried no information.

So the day has to build an evaluation over an artefact that is untrustworthy in a specific, statable way, for a system whose two silent failure shapes the artefact is structurally unable to exhibit.

The target: **a trajectory is a routing record that reads as an explanation; evaluating it requires choosing a unit of judgement — outcome, step or episode — and the three answer different questions, need different judges, and have different dependency structures; the evaluation that agrees best with human raters is blind to the two failure shapes that report success; and causal influence over a run is established by intervention and by nothing in the record.**

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. An agent trajectory is supplied that reached the right answer. **Was it a good run?** State your criterion in one sentence — **and name one thing your criterion could not detect about this run.**
2. **The same trajectory with every tool result removed**, leaving only the system's own stated reasoning. **Same question, same two parts.**
3. A second trajectory reached the **wrong** answer by a path that is defensible at every step. A third reached the **right** answer by a path that is wrong at every step. **Which is the better system, and what would you need to decide?**
4. Predict: **when a model judge scores these trajectories, which will it prefer** — the longer or the shorter, and the one that explains itself or the one that does not?
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to build an evaluation of your capability's task completion over last week's runs. **Write down what it will produce, and name the one thing it will get wrong.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 2 is the day's central finding administered before it is named, and it is the [WP-052](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) closure in its most direct form: **the stated reasoning is a produced output, not a record of the computation**, and a student whose judgement of the run does not move when the tool results are removed has been reading the narration. **Item 1's second clause is the day's headline instrument administered before phase 4 supplies its answer**; phase 5 step 7 is the same question about the student's own evaluation, and the measure is the movement.

**Item 5 is the day's contribution to the running frontier map**, and it is deliberately the last of the module's five, because by now the room has a two-day-old habit of predicting that the agent will get a *limit* wrong. The design's expectation is that most students predict a coding error and that few predict the failure that actually matters — **that the evaluation will be correct, will run, will produce a number, and will answer a different question from the one asked**, because nothing in the request determines the unit and something has to.

**Warrant.** Pretesting on an artefact the room will read as an explanation, with the removal at item 2 supplying the counter-evidence inside the same phase. Item 3 has no answer available from the trajectories alone and is the day's honest item: what a student needs is an intervention, which is point 3, and most of the room asks for more trajectories instead. Item 1's second clause is the pending elevation argued at [WP-074](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-074.md) §8 applied voluntarily on the day after it was argued, and [WP-075](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-075.md) §7 records it as a contingent reuse. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8.

- [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md): the four shapes, the observational-position criterion, and point 8.
- **Bridge:** [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) said an attention map is a routing record and not an explanation, and that causal influence is established only by intervention. **A trajectory is a record of which components ran and what they returned. What is it evidence of, and what would establish that a step caused the outcome?**

The bridge is the day's highest-value minute; the design's claim, tested at §9, is that a room which can produce it in phase 2 will still fall for E3 in phase 3, and that the gap between the two is the day's actual content.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups receive **four evaluations of the same set of agent runs on the substrate**, configured and runnable, each with its scores, its cost to run, its agreement with a held-back panel of human raters, and its record over six months.

**The runs are selected against the failure shape this cohort most often mis-attributed at [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md)** — the shape for which students named an observing party who would not in fact have seen it. **The selection rule and the cohort's own [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) shift are shown to students at the start of the phase**, per instrumentation rule 3.

- **E1 — outcome only.** Did the run reach the requested end state? Binary, cheap, computed locally from the system's effects rather than from its record, with no judge and no API call.
- **E2 — step level.** Every step judged for whether it was the right next action, scoring each exchange independently with cumulative context. Rich, expensive, and requiring a criterion for *right next action* that nobody has written down.
- **E3 — episode level.** A rubric applied to the whole trajectory, including the system's stated reasoning, scored by a model judge.
- **E4 — outcome plus replay.** The run is re-executed from a recorded state with one step's tool result substituted, and the change in outcome is measured.

Groups rank the four on **what each licenses you to say**, and commit before the records arrive.

Then all four are run over the same stored runs. **This is affordable inside a phase for one documented reason and the reason is taught**: the evaluation framework supports scoring **pre-existing responses from logs without re-running the agent**, so four evaluations over one run set is four readings rather than four executions. E4 is the exception and is the expensive one, because a replay is an execution by definition — which is most of why organisations do not do it.

The payload is **E3.** It has the highest agreement with the human panel, the highest reliability between runs, and the lowest cost per run of the three non-trivial options. It is the evaluation any organisation would adopt. And its six-month record shows it ranking a system that narrates its reasoning **above** one that does not, on pairs of runs whose outcomes are identical and whose effects on the substrate are identical.

**The judge is scoring the explanation the system produced about itself, not the run it performed.** That is [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md)'s length and self-style effects landing on an artefact the system authored, and it is the seduction [WP-052](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) §6 predicted — worse than an attention map's, because the artefact is prose.

Task after: **for each evaluation, state the question it answers and the question a reader will believe it answers.** Then: **for each, name which of [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md)'s four failure shapes it can see.** E3, the best-agreeing of the four, sees neither of the silent two.

**Demonstrated rather than operated, and not assessed:** continuous evaluation and the agent monitoring dashboard are in preview, and a preview capability is not a foundation for an assessed artefact. Both are shown; neither appears in the specification.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across units of judgement with a real six-month record and a held-back human panel as adjudicators, holding the run set fixed so that variation is attributable to the evaluation alone. **The four are run rather than described**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), and the running is what makes the cost argument concrete: E1 returns immediately and free, E3 returns quickly and cheaply, E4 is slow and expensive, and a room that has felt that gradient understands why the evaluation an organisation adopts is the one that is blind. **The run selection is instrumented to [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md)'s measured observation-position error**, so the unit-of-judgement work is done on the shape this cohort reasons about worst, and the selection is disclosed under rule 3. E3 is constructed to defeat the room by winning on every criterion the room would use to choose, which is the only construction under which a student who has just recited the routing-record argument in phase 2 will still adopt it.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **A trajectory is a routing record.** [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) point 3, in a new domain and at last named. A step's presence says it ran; it does not say the outcome depended on it. The step may have been redundant, its result may have been discarded, or the same outcome may have arrived by four other routes. **And the stated reasoning is not part of the record at all** — it is an output the system produced, subject to everything [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) established about what optimisation selects for, including the preference for confident, well-formed, agreeable narration.

2. **The seduction is stronger here than at [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md), and the reason is the register.** An attention map is a heatmap; a student must be talked into reading a story in it. A trajectory is prose, in the first person, giving reasons. **It reads as an explanation because it is written in the form of one**, and nothing about the underlying computation changed to make that true. This is the whole of what [WP-052](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) §6 said this day was for.

3. **Causal influence is established by intervention, unchanged.** [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) point 4. Here the interventions are four: remove the step, substitute the tool result, replay from a recorded state, perturb the request. **This is the only attribution this programme accepts**, it is why E4 exists, and it is what item 3 needed and nobody asked for.

4. **Three units of judgement, and they answer three questions.** **Outcome** — did the requested end state arrive. **Step** — was each action the right next one. **Episode** — was this run, as a whole, the kind of run we want. *Scope, strong form.* The three are **safe** for choosing what to measure on a capability you operate and can replay. They are **unsafe** as a complete account, in three named ways: they omit **cost per episode** as a unit, which is [S065](/wiki/seminars/S065.md)'s and is frequently the unit an operator actually optimises; they omit the unit that applies when **several runs interact on shared state**, where no single run is the object and the question is about the population; and they omit the **user-session** unit that spans episodes, which is where a system that is individually acceptable and cumulatively intolerable becomes visible. They are additionally **unsafe wherever the system cannot be replayed**, because then the outcome unit is the only one available and the other two are unavailable rather than rejected — a distinction that matters, because a report saying *we evaluate at the episode level* on an unreplayable system is describing a constraint as a choice.

5. **A step criterion is [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md)'s problem at scale.** *The right next action* is a criterion: constructed, by named people, from evidence, for a purpose, with a trade. **Nobody has written one.** A step-level evaluation whose criterion is not traceable is the engineer's own preference, applied thousands of times, at a rate no engineer could apply it by hand — which is [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) point 6's failure with a multiplier.

6. **The judge's degrees of freedom multiply, and [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md)'s method does not fully extend.** A judge scoring an output sees the output. A judge scoring a trajectory sees length, step count, ordering, formatting, tool-call count, and **the system's own account of itself.** Agreement is easier to obtain and means less. [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §6 said this day's retrieval would be the harder one and that its characterisation method does not straightforwardly extend; **it is right, and the specific break is the reorder test** — you cannot score the same trajectory twice reordered, because the order **is** the content. What replaces it must be stated by whoever uses the judge, and step 4 requires it.

   **And the judge is three parameters rather than one, which matters more here than it did at [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md).** A practitioner account of evaluating foundation-model systems states that *an AI judge is not just a model — it's a system that includes both a model and a prompt*, and that *altering the model, the prompt, or the model's sampling parameters results in a different judge.* On an output the prompt says what good means. **On a trajectory the prompt has to say what a trajectory is before it can say whether this one is good** — which turns and steps count, whether the stated reasoning is read, where the split falls — and two teams will answer that differently while calling the result the same thing. E3's rubric is therefore not one artefact but three, and a score is comparable only with scores taken under all three unchanged.

   **The same source requires the evaluation to be the stable thing, and names the trap this day is standing in.** *An application evolves over time, but the way it's evaluated ideally should be fixed*, so that a movement in the score can be read as a movement in the system — and **a model judge is itself an application, so it changes too.** Your capability will change every week this quarter. If the judge, its rubric or its split change with it, the six-month series in front of you at phase 3 is a sequence of unrelated readings under one heading, and the only honest report is the current one. **That is [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md)'s T4 arriving at the judge rather than at the criterion**, and it is why step 4's uncalibrated statement should name the judge, its prompt and its split alongside the score. See [research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/huyen-ai-engineering.md); direction, mechanism and vocabulary only, and **no magnitude is carried from it.**

7. **The unit of judgement is the unit of analysis.** [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) and [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) point 4. Steps within an episode are not independent; episodes within a session are not independent; **an interval computed over steps and reported over episodes is wrong in the direction [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) point 4 named** — ignoring positive clustering narrows, never widens. A step-level evaluation reporting thousands of judgements has a sample size closer to the number of episodes than to the number of steps.

8. **The four shapes need four evaluations, and the best-agreeing one sees neither silent shape.** [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) point 7 arriving as a measurement requirement. **Selection** failure is visible only at the step unit and only against an intent recorded before the call. **Output utilisation** failure is visible only at the outcome unit or by replay. **Input** and **execution** are visible in the record, because the tool was positioned to report them. **The episode rubric that scores highest and agrees best sees the two the tool already reported and misses the two nobody reported** — which means the evaluation an organisation will choose on evidence is the one blind to the failures that produced the evidence's absence. This is the day's most important sentence and it is the module's.

9. **The three units are shipped, and the platform says the same thing this day does.** *Perishable, and marked.* The evaluation framework requires a multi-turn conversation to be split into a query half and a response half before anything can be scored, and it supplies three strategies. **Last turn** splits at the last user message and is documented as suiting response quality at a specific point. **Full** treats the first user message as the query and the entire remainder as the response, and is documented as suiting task completion and overall trajectory. **Per-turn** scores each user-to-assistant exchange independently with cumulative context, for fine-grained analysis. A custom splitter is accepted: any callable that takes a conversation and returns a query and a response.

   **Those are this day's three units, with an escape hatch**, and the documentation states the consequence in one sentence that could stand as the day's epigraph: **how you split determines what you're evaluating.** A vendor wrote that down. It is the strongest available external corroboration that the unit of analysis is a choice, and it is corroboration of the *claim* and not of any number.

   Two further documented facts belong here because both are units of analysis wearing other clothes. **Repetitions exist to detect non-deterministic behaviour** — one sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement, which is [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) at the run level. And **workflow evaluation extracts each sub-agent's interactions and evaluates them individually alongside the workflow's overall output**, which is point 7's dependency problem at the multi-agent scale: the sub-agent scores are not independent of each other and the overall output is one observation, not many.

   **The silence, recorded and taught.** No agreement with human judgement is published for any evaluator in this stack. **Every judged number in phase 3, including E3's panel agreement, is a property of one judge, one panel and one run set**, and the day's own figures are marked perishable at the point of use.

10. **The default is a unit, and it is not the one most teams think they are reporting.** *Last turn is the default split.* It scores response quality at a point. **The number most commonly reported as an agent's quality is therefore, by default, an answer to a question about the last exchange** — and the question the reporter usually believes they asked is task completion, which is the full split. Nothing errors. No configuration is missing. The score is correct and it is about something else, which is phase 3's task-after made into a platform default. **Defaults are opinions**, and this one is the single cleanest instance in the programme of an instrument choosing the unit of analysis on your behalf, silently, in the direction of the cheaper measurement. A team that never chose is reporting an answer to a question it did not ask. **An engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system.**

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Point 2 is the [WP-052](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) closure stated, and it is placed second so it lands while the E3 record is still in front of the room. Point 4 carries the strong-form scope statement with three named omissions, because the three units are the day's assessed choice and a three-item list is the one students are most likely to treat as exhaustive. Point 6 discharges the difficulty [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §6 flagged rather than restating it. Point 8 joins the module and is stated last **of the argument**. **Points 9 and 10 are the platform layer**, required by the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), and they are placed after point 8 for a reason the day states aloud: point 9 is the only place in this module where the platform's documentation agrees with the programme's claim in the programme's own words, and a design that led with it would be teaching students to accept a claim because a vendor made it. **The claim is derived at point 4 and corroborated at point 9, in that order, and the order is the lesson.**

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 5. This phase produces a build. The criterion, the unit, the judge, the intervention and the statement are the student's.**

Students use **the capability, the drawing, the crossings and the shapes they have carried since [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md)**, the substrate's existing run log for it, and Copilot in agent mode through the Foundry MCP Server.

1. **State the criterion** in [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md)'s form: who decided, from what evidence, for what purpose, what it trades, and who would recognise it. **Name at least one party who would not.**
2. **Choose the unit of judgement.** State the question it answers, **the question a reader will believe it answers**, and **which of [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md)'s four shapes it cannot see.** **Name the split your unit corresponds to and say whether it is the default**, per point 10; a unit that happens to be the default is a permitted and common answer, and an unstated one is not. **Record also the unit the agent proposed before you chose, or record that it proposed none.**
3. **Build the evaluation** at that unit and **run it over the substrate's existing run log.**
4. **Characterise your judge**, per [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) step 2's method — score twice, correlate against artefact properties, hold back a reference — **and state which part of that method does not extend to a trajectory, and what you used instead.** **Report every judged score as uncalibrated**, per point 9's silence.
5. **Run one intervention** on a run your evaluation scored well: remove a step, substitute a tool result, or replay from a recorded state. Report whether the outcome moved. **A step the trajectory presented as load-bearing whose removal changes nothing is a required finding and must be reported if you find one.**
6. **Construct one class of runs after the results, from the residuals**, per [S067](/wiki/seminars/S067.md) point 7, and report your evaluation's behaviour on it. State whether that class exists in the substrate's taxonomy.
7. **State what your evaluation does not license**, in [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s form, **and name the shape it is blind to together with the evidence a reader would need in order to see that shape.**

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent to build the evaluation, in order, including the ones you abandoned. **Include the instruction in which you specified the unit, or record that you did not** — the second is the more interesting entry and it is not a deficiency.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — and today the qualifying demonstration is step 7's licensing statement naming a blind shape and the evidence a reader would need for it, which is a detection design in the [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) register rather than the [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) one.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch. Both specific, both from this build. **This is the module's last frontier statement and it is the fifth**, so it also carries one sentence saying whether the five, read together, describe a stable frontier or five unrelated tasks. **Either answer is correct and the second is the one the design expects.**

**Fading:** step 5's intervention procedure is worked with the room, since replay from a recorded state is new and the substrate's mechanism is unforgiving. **Step 2 is unsupported** — the unit choice is the day's discriminator and the class-5 boundary, because it is the decision that determines what the rest of the artefact can possibly say.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 5 in which the student builds the evaluation the module's four preceding days have been accumulating requirements for. Step 2's third clause is where [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) becomes a constraint on a measurement rather than a vocabulary, and its fourth clause is point 10 as an assessed decision: a student who cannot say which split their unit is has not chosen a unit, they have accepted one. Step 4's second clause is [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §6's named difficulty made into an assessed element rather than a caveat, and its uncalibrated-reporting clause is the platform map's standing prohibition applied by the student rather than by the page. Step 5 is the day's only source of causal evidence and its required finding is the [WP-052](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) closure in the student's own hands: **a step that read as load-bearing and was not is the thing a trajectory cannot tell you and an intervention can.** Step 7 is [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s discipline and closes the loop opened at item 1's second clause. **The frontier statement's fifth-instance clause is the module's recursion closed honestly**: five samples from five staff-chosen tasks is a sample, per instrumentation rule 3, and the student is asked to say so about their own evidence rather than being told.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate incident review, eighteen months ago, conducted competently. The trajectory record was read carefully by three people, three causal claims were drawn from it, and three changes were made on the strength of those claims.

A replay run eleven months later — for an unrelated reason — showed that one of the three steps had **no effect on the outcome at all.** Removing it changed nothing. The change made on the strength of it had been carried in the system for a year, had cost maintenance, and had prevented nothing.

Attention is directed at one fact: **nobody misread the record.** The reading was careful, the three claims were the reasonable claims, and the record does not contain the information the reading required. There was no way to have been right from the record alone.

Then the agent case: a student asked Copilot in agent mode to **evaluate the agent's task completion over last week's runs.** It produced a working evaluation in one pass — correct code, sensible configuration, a clean report with a single number. The review passed, and the review was right about everything it checked: the code runs, the evaluator is the one named in the request, and the number is computed correctly. **The split was the default.** The number is response quality at the last exchange, reported under the heading *task completion*.

Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the request named an evaluator and the answer used that evaluator**, so the name matched at every point a reviewer would look; the unit is not visible in the output, only in the configuration; and no error was available to be raised, because nothing was wrong. **This is point 10 committed in one prompt**, and it is the same shape as [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md)'s agent case one layer up — the label was accurate and the mechanism underneath it answered a different question.

Then students, in threes: **a log you have read as an explanation.**

**Warrant.** Error management targeting points 1 and 3, constructed like the module's other clinics so that the case contains no error of care. The eleven months and the unrelated reason are the phase's content: the correction arrived by accident, which is the only way it could have arrived, because nothing in the practice generated an intervention. It is chosen because every student in the room has read a log as an account of what happened, which is what makes the disclosure cheap to make and the omission expensive to have carried. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition**, and it is placed on the last day of the module deliberately: it is the only one of the module's five agent cases in which the agent's output contains no defect at all, and a room that can say what is wrong with it has acquired the module rather than a checklist.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **their step 2 unit choice and their step 5 intervention result.**

The partner has two obligations and both are required.

- **Construct a pair of runs your evaluation ranks wrongly** — one genuinely better, scored lower — and say which property of the judge or the unit produced the inversion.
- **Name one causal claim in the writer's own report that the trajectory alone does not support**, and state the intervention that would.

Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 3, both outcomes are recorded in the artefact. **The first obligation's success rate is the measure [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §9 carried to this day**, and it is reported at phase 8 against the [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) rate.

Per instrumentation rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8, the inversion-construction structure reuses [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) phase 7's and **its use here is contingent** on S048's reported outcome; the dual-defeat construction reuses [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) phase 7's and is contingent on the same discipline. A reported failure at either retracts the corresponding reuse here along with every other contingent reuse of it.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with a dual demand attacking the measurement and the reasoning separately. The second obligation is the day's argument turned on the student's own report, and it is the only place in the programme where a student's *account of their own evidence* is attacked rather than the evidence — which is the correct target, because the failure this day teaches is a failure of reading rather than of collection.

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- **Sealed items opened. Item 1 against item 2**: how many students' judgement of the run changed when the tool results were removed and only the stated reasoning remained. **This is the day's most quoted result and it is the direct measure of the [WP-052](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) closure.** **It is reported to the room together with its demand characteristic, stated plainly**: item 2 is visibly the same trajectory with something taken out, a student who infers that they are meant to change their answer will change it, and the figure is therefore an upper bound on how much the removal actually moved anyone. The design's reason for keeping a transparent instrument is given at [WP-075](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-075.md) §8, which concedes it.
- **Item 1's second clause against step 7**, per student, as a movement: what students could name their criterion failing to detect before the day, and what they can name after. Reported as a shift and as directional only, for the reason [WP-075](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-075.md) §7 gives.
- Item 3's answers sorted by whether the student asked for an intervention, more runs, or a better judge.
- Item 4's judge-preference predictions against E3's measured preferences.
- **Item 5 opened, and it is the module's last frontier datum.** The sealed predictions against what the agent actually produced at step 3. **The cohort is asked one question about the result: did anyone predict that the failure would be a correct evaluation of the wrong thing?** The design's expectation is that almost nobody did, and that the room's predictions across [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md) to [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md) will show a pattern worth naming aloud — **the cohort learned to predict that the agent would get a limit, a bound or a permission wrong, and did not learn to predict that it would produce something correct and irrelevant.** That is the harder prediction and it is the one the rest of their careers will require. Reported as directional only.
- **The cohort's step 5 intervention results: the proportion of steps that read as load-bearing and were not.** Second-most-quoted result and the one the design most wants on record.
- **The phase 7 inversion-construction success rate**, reported beside the same cohort's [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) rate — the instrument [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §9 carried here.
- The cohort's step 2 unit choices, cross-tabulated against **the shapes each cannot see**, set beside [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md)'s pooled undiscriminated-shape list. Where a student chose a unit blind to a shape they had also declined to discriminate, that is shown without names and named aloud as the module's compound blind spot.
- **The units the agent proposed, as a distribution, set against the units the students chose.** This is the module's recursion reported as a number rather than as an observation: if the instrument proposes the episode unit and the cohort mostly chose otherwise, the day's instruction survived contact with the tool's path of least resistance, and if it does not, the design has taught a lesson and supplied an instrument that undoes it. **Neither the agent's proposal nor the student's departure from it is assessed**; the distribution is reported to the room and carried to the following year's cohort under instrumentation rule 9.
- The classes constructed at step 6, pooled, with how many exist in the substrate's taxonomy.
- Each student names **one artefact they have treated as an explanation.**
- Retrieval schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 4.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in ME302 with IS302. Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the criterion in [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md)'s form with at least one party named who would not recognise it; the unit of judgement with the question it answers, the question a reader will believe it answers, the [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) shapes it cannot see, **and the split it corresponds to with a statement of whether that split is the default, together with the unit the agent proposed or an explicit none**; **the built evaluation run over the run log**; the judge characterisation stating which part of [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md)'s method does not extend and what replaced it, **with every judged score reported as uncalibrated**; one intervention result, including any load-bearing-looking step whose removal changed nothing; one class constructed from residuals with its taxonomy status; the statement of what the evaluation does not license with its blind shape and the evidence a reader would need; both phase 7 outcomes; **the prompt record**; **the intervention record**; and **the frontier statement with its fifth-instance sentence**. **Nothing else is assessed.**

**A causal claim in the artefact that is supported only by the trajectory fails.** This is the day's single hard requirement, it is the [WP-052](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-052.md) closure as a specification condition, and it applies to the student's own report as strictly as to the material.

**A unit of judgement chosen without naming the shapes it cannot see fails.** [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) as a constraint rather than a vocabulary. **A unit reported without its split fails**, per point 10: an unnamed unit is an accepted default, and the artefact must say which.

**A judge characterisation that claims [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md)'s method applied unchanged fails.** The reorder test does not run on an artefact whose order is its content, and a student who reports having run it has reported something that did not happen.

**A judged score reported without stating that it is uncalibrated fails.** No agreement with human judgement is published for any evaluator in this stack, and the artefact must say so where it reports one.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error; step 7's licensing statement, naming a blind shape and the evidence a reader would need for it, qualifies.

No entrustment judgement. The quarter's is at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), and [M15](/wiki/modules/M15.md) carries none for the reason published on [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md).

**Dispositions observed:** **humility**, evidenced by step 7's statement and by whether the student reported an intervention that dissolved their own account; **integrity**, evidenced at step 5, where a required finding is one that undermines the artefact the student has just built. Both recorded in narrative within the activity, never scored as activities.

**Rule-6 discharge.** One obligation lands on this day and **the element exists.**

- **[S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) named this day's phase 3** as the response occasion for its step 4 observation-position error rate, read as a shift against item 3, specifying that the four trajectory evaluations be selected against the shape this cohort most often mis-attributed. **The element exists: phase 3's run set is drawn from runs exhibiting that shape, the selection rule and the cohort's own S074 shift are disclosed to students at the start of the phase per instrumentation rule 3, and the phase's task-after requires each evaluation to be assessed for which shapes it can see** — so the cohort's weakest observational reasoning is the material the unit-of-judgement work is done on. The obligation is discharged.

## Perishable content in this day

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| A trajectory is a routing record | The stored run log and what it does and does not contain |
| Intervention is the only attribution | Replay from a recorded state, and what it costs |
| Three units, answering three questions | Last turn, full and per-turn, with a custom splitter as the fourth |
| The unit of analysis is a choice | Last turn as the default, chosen for you |
| The step criterion is [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md)'s problem at scale | A per-turn rubric with no traceable criterion behind it |
| The unit of judgement is the unit of analysis | Per-sub-agent workflow scoring, and the dependence between the scores |
| The best-agreeing evaluation is blind to the two silent shapes | E3's panel agreement, and every other agreement figure in the day |
| A trajectory judge is a model, a prompt and a sampling configuration, and the prompt has to define the unit before it can score it | E3's rubric text, its judge deployment and the split in force when each score was taken |
| An evaluation must change more slowly than the capability it evaluates | Six months of E3's record, across however many rubric edits nobody logged |
| A durable record makes a decision findable and does not make it correct | A tracking directory holding research, plan, implementation and review records under stable identifiers |
| A record's schema decides which questions can be asked of it later | Phase-scoped files, and the identifier scheme that joins them |

- **Perishable in full:** the four evaluations of phase 3 and every figure attached to them, including all agreement rates; the substrate's current judge; every named evaluation framework, evaluator, split strategy and harness; and the replay mechanism. **Agreement figures are the fastest-moving content in the day and are marked at the point of use**, because a judge's agreement with a human panel is a property of one judge, one panel and one run set — and because **no agreement with human judgement is published for any evaluator in this stack**, which is the silence point 9 teaches and which every reported score in the artefact must carry.
- **Local:** the substrate's run log, its held-back human panel, its taxonomy, the phase 6 incident review, the phase 6 agent case, and the cohort's [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md) results used for case selection.
- **Assessed:** the left-hand column only, **with the two rows added on 2026-07-30 excepted: the three-parameter account of a trajectory judge and the requirement that an evaluation change more slowly than the capability are durable and are not assessed.** They sharpen point 6 and step 4's uncalibrated statement; **they add no checked element and the specification's list is unaltered.** **Point 9's corroboration is corroboration of the claim and not of any number**, and a student who cites the documentation as evidence that the three units are the right three has made the error the day's own ordering was designed to prevent.

**The best-instrumented trajectory in the room is the one nobody evaluated.** A published configuration corpus declares files on disk authoritative over session memory and writes research, plan, implementation, critique and review records under stable identifiers, so that any decision in a run can be found from any later phase. **That is a strictly better trajectory record than the substrate's run log**, and it settles nothing this day is asking. It makes the routing record durable, addressable and complete; it does not make any step in it correct, does not supply a unit of judgement, and does not tell you which of the three units the record should be read at. **Provenance is a lookup and correctness is a claim**, and a record that is excellent at the first is silent on the second — which is [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md)'s argument surviving the best-case instrumentation. Pinned, perishable, not assessed; see the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md).

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **A trajectory is a routing record that reads as an explanation**, and it reads that way because it is prose in the first person giving reasons — not because anything about the computation changed. **Causal influence is established by intervention and by nothing in the record.** Evaluating one requires choosing a unit — outcome, step or episode — and the three answer different questions.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** The judge-as-instrument material, and the effects that make a model rater prefer longer and more self-explaining output, are grounded in [research/03-measurement-evaluation/zheng-mt-bench-chatbot-arena.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/zheng-mt-bench-chatbot-arena.md); the unit-of-analysis discipline follows [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md). Both are cited for **direction and mechanism only** and **no magnitude is asserted from either.** Nothing is published about how any evaluator in this stack compares with a human rater, so every judged score you report is uncharacterised and must say so.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Name the split your unit corresponds to, and whether it is the default.** An unstated unit is an accepted one.
- **Support every causal claim with an intervention**, including the claims in your own write-up.
- **State which failure shapes your evaluation cannot see**, and what evidence a reader would need to see them.
- **Hold provenance and correctness apart**, per the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). A record that makes every decision findable under a stable identifier is a better routing record and settles nothing about whether any step was right.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Adopting the evaluation that wins. The episode rubric agrees best, costs least, and is scoring the explanation the system produced about itself.
