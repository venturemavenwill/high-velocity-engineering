# S075 · A Handover with a Named Receiving Owner ⊘

**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-075](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-075.md)

**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

You have a manifest, a runbook that somebody else has executed, a harness record with an obligation attached, and a withdrawal trigger bound to a register.

Today you put a name against every line and somebody argues with you about the names.

Answer these in writing before the session opens. This is the last set you answer cold in [M15](/wiki/modules/M15.md).

1. Which line on your manifest will the receiving side dispute, and what will their argument be?
2. Which line will they accept without reading? Why is that worse?
3. What is your support taper, and on what date does it end?
4. What happens on the day after it ends, if something breaks?
5. Which of your acceptance conditions could the recipient evaluate without you? Count them.

**Item 2 is the one to think about.** A line accepted without argument is usually a line the recipient has not understood they now hold.

## What you must produce

A **handover**, assembled from the four artefacts you already have. Do not start again.

It carries: the manifest with a named receiving owner and acceptance condition per line; at least one line recorded as unaccepted with its consequence; the executed runbook and its execution record; the harness and cost-model handover record with threshold provenance and the obligation a bad result creates; the withdrawal trigger specification with evidence per step; and a **support taper with an end date and a named state on the far side of it.**

Then you defend it.

## What good looks like

- Ownership is stated as a **role and a person**, and where the two conflict — the person who cares has no authority, the person with authority has no interest — the conflict is on the page rather than resolved by optimism.
- The unaccepted line is real and its consequence is stated as something that will stop working, not as a risk score.
- The taper has an end. **A taper that fades out is not a taper; it is an assumption that you will still answer.**
- The withdrawal trigger is a register state change, and each of its steps leaves an artefact somebody outside the delivery team could find.
- Under challenge, you can distinguish the lines you are confident about from the lines you are hoping about, without being pushed into either defending everything or conceding everything.

## How it goes wrong

Five failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **The immaculate teardown** — the hygiene half is excellent, the transfer half is a folder of documents, and the whole thing reads as competence. *Warning sign:* your specification is longer than your manifest.
- **The signature farm** — every line has an owner because every line has been assigned to somebody who was not asked. *Warning sign:* you have not spoken to one of your named owners.
- **The taper without a floor** — support reduces gradually and never reaches zero, so the engagement continues informally and unbudgeted. *Warning sign:* you cannot name the date.
- **The conceded manifest** — challenged on one line, you begin conceding lines you had good reasons for, because the challenge is uncomfortable. *Warning sign:* your third concession has no reason attached.
- **The defended fantasy** — challenged on the under-authorised owner, you defend the assignment instead of recording the problem. *Warning sign:* your answer is about what the owner should be able to do.

## Do it

Work from the supplied situation in [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md). Assemble; do not rebuild.

You have one challenger, and they play the technology function: capable, unwilling, and correct that this was done to them rather than with them. Their instructions are to accept nothing on goodwill, to ask of every line what it will cost them, and to make one reasonable offer that leaves the hardest line unowned.

## Show it

The defence is the assessment. It is short, it is live, and it is not a presentation.

**You are not being marked on whether the handover would have been accepted.** A handover that records an unowned critical line and states the consequence passes; a handover that has an owner for everything because nobody was asked does not.

## Assessment

Checked elements:

1. Every manifest line names a receiving owner as a role and a person, or is explicitly recorded as unaccepted.
2. At least one line is recorded as unaccepted, with the consequence stated as a named capability that will stop working.
3. Every acceptance condition is stated in a form the named owner could evaluate with the learner absent.
4. The infrastructure definitions, the pipeline, the runbooks, the evaluation harness, the cost model and the monitoring each appear on the manifest, or their absence is stated with a reason.
5. Each threshold transferred carries a provenance line, and the harness record names the obligation a failing result creates and the authority the obliged party holds.
6. At least one runbook has an execution record produced by a non-author, with at least one recorded stop.
7. The withdrawal trigger is a state change in a register, with the permitted actor named, and reuse after close stated as prohibited.
8. Every step of the withdrawal specification names the artefact it leaves and where that artefact is written.
9. The support taper states an end date and the state that obtains after it.
10. Under challenge, the learner distinguishes at least one line held with confidence from one line held on hope, and gives the reason for each.

**Nothing else is assessed.** The quality of the underlying system is not assessed. The recipient's satisfaction is not assessed. Whether the transfer would have succeeded is not assessed, and could not be.

## What this does not establish

That this handover works, or that handovers of this shape produce systems that survive. **No source behind this programme reports sustainment of any kind** — not whether a delivered system was still running later, not whether a customer maintained one, not a single post-deployment record.

The specification is checkable and the thing it checks is form. **A learner can satisfy all ten items and still have designed a transfer that fails**, because the failure would occur months after the last observation this programme can make. The paper for this session states that limit as a concession rather than as a caveat.
