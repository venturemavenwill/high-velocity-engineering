## The situation

You have a manifest, a runbook that somebody else has executed, a harness record with an obligation attached, and a withdrawal trigger bound to a register.

Today you put a name against every line, a named individual runs the stack in front of you, and somebody argues with you about the names.

**The intervention this whole module has been building towards is one word: a name.** An artefact handed to a team is handed to nobody. Teams do not run harnesses, do not read runbooks at three in the morning and do not pause releases; people do, and only people who know they are the person. Everything else in the module — the manifest, the executed runbook, the provenance line, the register trigger — exists so that there is something specific for a named person to accept.

Answer these in writing before the session opens. This is the last set you answer cold in [M15](/wiki/modules/M15.md).

1. Which line on your manifest will the receiving side dispute, and what will their argument be?
2. Which line will they accept without reading? Why is that worse?
3. What is your support taper, and on what date does it end?
4. What happens on the day after it ends, if something breaks?
5. Which of your acceptance conditions could the recipient evaluate without you? Count them.

**Item 2 is the one to think about.** A line accepted without argument is usually a line the recipient has not understood they now hold.

## What you must produce

A **handover**, assembled from the four artefacts you already have. Do not start again.

It carries: the manifest with a named receiving owner and acceptance condition per line; at least one line recorded as unaccepted with its consequence; the executed runbook and its execution record; the harness and cost-model handover record with threshold provenance and the obligation a bad result creates; the withdrawal trigger specification with evidence per step; a **support taper with an end date and a named state on the far side of it**; and **a demonstration record** — the named owner operating the stack while you watch.

Then you defend it.

### The specification, in full

Every checked element, stated in advance. **This list is closed.**

1. Every manifest line names a receiving owner as a role and a person, or is explicitly recorded as unaccepted.
2. At least one line is recorded as unaccepted, with the consequence stated as a named capability that will stop working.
3. Every acceptance condition is stated in a form the named owner could evaluate with the learner absent.
4. The infrastructure definitions, the pipeline, the runbooks, the evaluation harness, the cost model and the monitoring each appear on the manifest, or their absence is stated with a reason.
5. Each threshold transferred carries a provenance line, and the harness record names the obligation a failing result creates and the authority the obliged party holds.
6. At least one runbook has an execution record produced by a non-author, with at least one recorded stop.
7. The withdrawal trigger is a state change in a register, with the permitted actor named, and reuse after close stated as prohibited.
8. Every step of the withdrawal specification names the artefact it leaves and where that artefact is written.
9. The support taper states an end date and the state that obtains after it.
10. A demonstration record exists in which the named owner performed the operate acts unaided, with the supervision level recorded for each act.
11. Under challenge, the learner distinguishes at least one line held with confidence from one line held on hope, and gives the reason for each.

## The material

### Entrustment is literally the subject, so use the framework properly

A handover claims that somebody can now do something unsupervised. That is not an analogy for entrustment. **It is the definition of it**, and the assessment literature has spent two decades working out what such a claim requires.

An entrustable professional activity is a unit of professional practice, made of "activities with a beginning and an end", which "are only entrusted to trained personnel" ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). Entrustment is expressed as an **ordinal supervision level**, not a score — from observing only, through executing with a supervisor in the room, through executing with a supervisor reachable, to working unsupervised, to supervising others. The middle of that range is where the useful detail is: level three splits into all findings double-checked, key findings double-checked, and a distant supervisor with prompt review afterwards. **The distance between "I check everything you did" and "I check the important parts" is where a handover actually lives**, and a five-point scale cannot see it.

Two of the framework's provisions are load-bearing here and are usually dropped from summaries.

**The entrustment-based discussion.** A short, focused conversation after the activity that "should probe the student's deep understanding of what was done, but should include 'what-if' questions." **The what-if requirement is the whole point.** Retrospective explanation shows the recipient understood the case they met. Counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases they did not meet — and the framework is explicit about why that matters: "an entrustment decision implies the acceptance of risk, as it can never be fully predicted what the student will encounter."

**The distinction between ad-hoc and summative entrustment.** Ad-hoc entrustment happens continuously and informally, whenever a supervisor feels the person's skills match the situation's complexity and the risk is acceptable. Summative entrustment "has the nature of certification" and requires "an agreement between multiple observers", multiple observations, and multiple evidence types. **Almost every handover in industry is an ad-hoc entrustment being reported as a summative one**, and naming that is more useful than any additional documentation.

The framework also supplies three professionalism characteristics stated behaviourally enough to observe: **integrity**, "truthfulness in reports to a supervising clinician and benevolence"; **reliability**, "stable, predictable behavior and a conscientious way of working"; and **humility**, "the ability to observe own limitations and willingness ask for help when needed". The third is the one this session assesses on your side rather than the recipient's.

And the provision nobody's handover document has: **entrustment expires** through disuse. If the recipient "has not practiced the EPA for a long time, the entrustment decision may expire." **Your taper's end date is not the end of the entrustment. Disuse is.**

**Its limits:** a clinical model from its originator, reporting adoption rather than effectiveness. Medicine has patients, legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint; an engagement constructs its stakes and the construction is the hardest part of the transfer. The full summative standard is expensive, and **the version this programme runs is reduced and says so.** Even in mature programmes learners complete few activities, so a handover claiming many entrusted units is claiming more than the model supports.

### The recipient who does not believe they can run it will not run it

This is the module's best-warranted psychological claim and it is under-used everywhere else in this programme.

Perceived self-efficacy is "beliefs in one's capabilities to organize and execute the courses of action required to manage prospective situations", and those beliefs determine four things: the goals people set, how much effort they expend, how long they persevere, and their resilience to failure ([research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md](/research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md)).

Three findings from that source change what a handover meeting should be.

**Mastery is the most effective source, and it is the only one explicitly ranked.** Not explanation. Not encouragement. Doing the thing. **A demonstration is not a test you administer to the recipient; it is the intervention.**

**Efficacy beliefs run ahead of cost–benefit reasoning.** "Such wholesale exclusions of large classes of options are made rapidly on perceived efficacy grounds" — people exclude whole classes of action before weighing them. The consequence for you is sharp: **a recipient who does not believe they can operate the harness will not weigh whether to; they will simply never open it, and will not experience that as a decision.** An absence of objection in your handover meeting is therefore not evidence of agreement.

**Persuasion is asymmetric, and the efficacy builder's job is task design rather than encouragement.** Efficacy is far easier to destroy with words than to build with them, and "Successful efficacy builders do more than convey positive appraisals… they structure situations for them in ways that bring success and avoid placing people in situations prematurely where they are likely to fail often." **Sequencing the demonstration is the intervention; the reassurance is incidental to it.** Start the recipient on the act they are most likely to complete, and do not start them on the one you find hardest.

One correction that must travel: **only easy successes build a brittle sense of efficacy.** "If people experience only easy successes they come to expect quick results and are easily discouraged by failure," and "A resilient sense of efficacy requires experience in overcoming obstacles through perseverant effort." **Do not stage a demonstration the recipient cannot fail.** Bandura's constraint is on frequency and timing, not on difficulty.

And the line to hold against your own handover meeting: "Saying something should not be confused with believing it to be so."

**Its limits are real.** The volume read is a conference proceedings edited by the field's founder: no search strategy, no inclusion criteria, and across the framing chapter no study whose result went the wrong way. Several load-bearing results are cited as unpublished manuscripts. Its empirical settings are schools, families, clinics and migration — **it contains no study of adult professionals learning a technical craft**, so applying it here is an inference. Efficacy is almost always measured by self-report immediately before a performance that is often self-rated. Directionality is not settled in field settings, and the volume concedes it twice. **This repository asserts no magnitude from it, and reading it in full does not change that.**

### The recipient must be able to say "I cannot run this"

The demonstration only produces information if the recipient can fail in front of you and say so.

Psychological safety is "the belief that the work environment is safe for interpersonal risk taking" ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)), where the interpersonal risk is appearing ignorant, incompetent or disruptive by asking a question, admitting a mistake or criticising a plan. It is explicitly **not** immunity from consequences.

The finding that governs your handover meeting is about silence. Most people, asked, report at least one occasion when they felt unable to raise a concern with a superior even though they believed the issue was important — **and the asymmetry that produces it is structural rather than personal: voice pays the organisation, later and uncertainly; silence pays the self, now and certainly.** In a handover, the recipient's incentives are exactly that shape. Saying *I cannot do this* costs them standing in front of their own colleagues, immediately, to protect an outcome months away that may never be traced back to this room.

Two practical items from that source are worth having in the room. **Situational humility** — "Humility is the simple recognition that you don't have all the answers, and you certainly don't have a crystal ball", with the note that "Confidence and humility are not opposites". And the four phrases available to anyone regardless of rank: **"I don't know. / I need help. / I made a mistake. / I'm sorry."** The accompanying observation is the one that applies to you rather than to them: **"Sometimes, you have to take an interpersonal risk to lower interpersonal risk."** If you want a recipient to say they cannot run something, say first what you could not make work.

The change-management literature converges from its own direction and adds the operational form: a supervisor must know "How to create a safe environment that allows employees to practice and to make mistakes without retribution", and must make people aware "that implementing change takes time and practice, and that mistakes or missteps are part of the learning process" ([research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md)). **Its limits are the ones this module has repeated: a trademarked practitioner model published by the firm that sells its training, with no independent evaluation anywhere in it.**

**The safety source's limits.** The trade book is a narrative synthesis by the construct's originator, citing densely and selectively; several of its results are reported from studies not read here; and the foundational 1999 paper is **paywalled and has not been read** by this repository, so the narrower team-level definition and the mediation finding rest on an abstract. **No magnitude from it is reproduced here.**

### What the demonstration must actually contain

The operate stack is four acts, and each maps onto an artefact you already hold.

**Start the evaluation harness and read a result.** The framework's aggregated result carries per-item detail and a `reason` string alongside pass or fail, and the `reason` is what makes a judgement auditable rather than merely numeric ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). **The recipient must read a `reason`, not a score**, because a score they cannot interrogate is a number they will eventually stop believing.

**Work one runbook to termination**, from the entry condition to the stated end state, including the escalation.

**Read the cost position** and state what would make it move.

**Show where the withdrawal trigger's evidence lands**, and find one artefact from a step already performed.

Record a supervision level per act, using the sub-levels. **Do not record a single overall level**, because a recipient who can run the harness unaided and cannot work the runbook is two different entrustment decisions, and one number hides that.

**The documentation's limits:** read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches; most agent evaluators are preview; one consulted page is superseded. **It publishes no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data and no threshold rationale — assert none.**

### Why the defence is written down rather than scored

The assessment literature on how assessors actually judge is unambiguous and uncomfortable.

Assessors reading real portfolios **used the narratives to form judgements and the numbers only to confirm them** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). A substantial share of rating variance is attributable to the rater rather than to the performance, **and it does not improve with experience** — assessor variability is structural, not a training deficiency. Assessors anchor early and then seek confirmation; additional evidence elaborates the initial judgement rather than overturning it. And agreement on the outcome does not imply agreement on the reasoning: two assessors can pass the same person for incompatible reasons, which is invisible in a score.

**The remedy the literature proposes is group deliberation with substantiated judgements, not better rating scales.** That is why item 11 of the specification asks the learner to distinguish confidence from hope with reasons attached, and why the assessor records a written reason rather than a mark.

**Its limits:** single-institution medical-education studies using mock portfolios and assessors who did not know the students; think-aloud methods cannot capture fast or automatic processing and participants may construct explanations for the researcher, which the authors state themselves. **They establish how assessors reason, not that narrative judgement is more valid than scored judgement.** No magnitude from them is reproduced here.

### The one handover obligation a platform actually enforces

This module has spent four sessions arguing for two propositions. **A handover needs a named person accountable for whether the thing should continue to exist. That person is not the same as the person who can change it.** Both have been argued from an absence — the practice corpus names no receiving owner anywhere — and an argument from an absence is the weakest kind this programme makes.

**Here is a platform that enforces both, as a constraint on object creation.** It is the only example in the whole of this programme's platform research of a handover obligation implemented as mechanism rather than as documentation, and it is worth studying whether or not anyone ever uses the product.

An enterprise directory has added an identity type for agents, and separates three administrative roles around it ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-365-and-agent-identity.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-365-and-agent-identity.md)). **Owners** are technical administrators: they manage credentials, edit configuration, add other owners, and hold the destructive powers — restore, re-enable, permanently delete. **Sponsors** hold business accountability: the documentation says they "understand the business purpose of the agent" and "can determine whether an agent is still needed", and their powers are deliberately confined to non-destructive lifecycle acts — enable, disable, soft-delete, change the sponsor. **They cannot change application settings.** **Managers** sit in the organisational hierarchy for the agent's paired user account.

The load-bearing rule is one sentence and it is the reason this section exists. **Owners are optional. Sponsors are not.** A sponsor is required at the moment an agent identity is created; the directory will not produce one without a name against it. And it is reinforced once more, in the place a governance mechanism usually fails: **if the sponsor leaves the organisation, sponsorship transfers automatically to their manager.** The obligation does not lapse when the person does.

Read that as the answer to the question this module opened with — *who decides whether this should still exist, and is that the same person who can change it?* **The answer is no, and it is not a recommendation.** A governance rule enforced by the directory beats the same rule written into a policy document, for the same reason a state-triggered withdrawal beats somebody remembering in [S074](/wiki/seminars/S074.md).

Two design details are worth noticing because they show somebody thought about how the mechanism would be defeated. **A group may be named as sponsor, but not any group** — certain group types are excluded, and the excluded ones are precisely those that would let accountability be assigned to a permissions container rather than to identifiable people. And **administrators are deliberately not made sponsors automatically** when they create an agent, with the stated reason being to avoid accountability collecting on the administrator by default. Somebody designed against the failure where everything ends up owned by whoever happened to hold the access.

**Now the limits, and the last of them is the one that keeps this session honest.**

**Enforcement covers one population, not the estate.** Agents created before this identity type exists on ordinary application service principals. They sit alongside the new objects in the same directory, look adjacent, behave differently, and **have none of the agent-specific behaviours — including enforced sponsorship.** The documentation states this plainly. **So "every agent in this tenant has an accountable sponsor" is false**, and telling the two populations apart requires filtering. Any handover claim you make here must name which population it covers.

**No evaluation exists of whether enforced sponsorship survives contact with an organisation.** Nothing published measures whether sponsors act on their accountability, or whether the requirement is quietly satisfied by pointing every agent at one long-suffering group. **The mechanism is documented. Its efficacy is measured nowhere**, which is the standing condition of every source in this strand.

**And the limit that matters most for today. A sponsor is a directory field.** A directory field is not a person who knows they are accountable, has agreed to be, and could act. **The platform can enforce that a name exists. It cannot enforce that the name was asked** — and `asked_and_agreed` in your own record is exactly the property no platform can check. This is the strongest possible version of the mechanism this module has been arguing for, and it still does not produce the conversation. **The conversation is the part that is yours**, and this session is where it happens.

**Its further limits.** First-party documentation, definitional throughout and evidential nowhere. The most perishable material in this repository: general availability is recent, preview status differs feature by feature, a dated migration of adjacent surfaces is already scheduled, and two registry experiences from sibling products are mid-convergence. Several supporting pages behind the note were read as search excerpts rather than full fetches, so their structure is reconstructed rather than confirmed. **Assess the reasoning. Never assess the portal path, the preview status or the role name.**

## The workflow

**Hand the operate stack to a named individual and have them run it in front of you.**

**Step 1 — name the person, not the team.** For each of the four operate acts, a role and a person. **If you cannot name a person for an act, that act is unaccepted** and goes on the manifest as such with its consequence. Do not soften this by naming a team; a team is what you write when you do not want to find out.

**Step 2 — sequence the demonstration deliberately.** Order the four acts so the recipient starts on the one they are most likely to complete. This is efficacy design and it is not politeness: a recipient who fails first will exclude the whole class of action, and you will not see them decide to.

**Step 3 — lower the interpersonal risk before you ask them to take it.** Say, out loud and first, one thing about this system you could not make work. **You are demonstrating the behaviour you need from them**, and the four phrases are available to you as well as to them.

**Step 4 — they run it. You are silent.** Same rule as [S072](/wiki/seminars/S072.md), for the same reason: anything you say is not in the artefact. Record what they did, where they stopped, what they asked.

**Step 5 — record a supervision level per act, with sub-levels.**

```yaml
# handover-entrustment.yaml
# The unit is an ACT, not a system. One level per act. Never one overall level.
receiving_owner:
  role: "<role>"
  person: "<named individual>"          # a team is what you write to avoid finding out
  asked_and_agreed: true                 # false is a legitimate and more useful answer

demonstration:
  observed_on: "<YYYY-MM-DD>"
  observer_silent: true
  acts:
    - act: "start the evaluation harness and read one result reason"
      level: "3b"        # executed without me in the room; I double-check key findings
      stopped_at: "could not locate the credential; found it after searching"
      what_if_asked: "what would you do if the run failed to start at 02:00?"
      what_if_answer: "<recorded verbatim>"
    - act: "work runbook R-04 to its stated end state"
      level: "2b"        # I was in the room, ready to step in
      stopped_at: "escalation route named a person who has left"
      what_if_asked: "what if the escalation does not answer?"
      what_if_answer: "<recorded verbatim>"
    - act: "read the cost position and state what moves it"
      level: "4a"        # unsupervised, remotely monitored
    - act: "locate one withdrawal-step evidence artefact"
      level: "1"         # observed only. NOT TRANSFERRED.

entrustment_class: "ad-hoc"   # summative requires multiple observers, multiple observations,
                              # multiple evidence types. One morning is ad-hoc. Say so.
expires_by: "disuse"          # not the taper end date. Entrustment lapses through non-practice.

taper:
  ends_on: "<YYYY-MM-DD>"     # a taper with no end is an unbudgeted commitment
  state_after: "<what obtains on the following day>"

unaccepted_after_demonstration:
  - act: "locate one withdrawal-step evidence artefact"
    consequence: "nobody can establish that a teardown step occurred"
```

**Step 6 — hold the entrustment-based discussion, with what-if questions.** Not a walkthrough of what they just did. Ask what they would do in the cases they did not meet: the run that fails at two in the morning, the escalation that does not answer, the result that is worse but not badly worse. **Record the answers verbatim.** Retrospective explanation is evidence about the case they met and nothing else.

**Step 7 — attack your own handover with the agent, and do not let it congratulate you.** One prompt: *here is my handover, my demonstration record and my supervision levels. For each act I marked at level 3 or above, argue from the recorded stops that the level is too generous. Then name the act I did not include in the demonstration at all, and say why its absence is convenient for me.*

Read it for the over-generous level and the omitted act. **Record what you rejected and why.** Note that the instrument sees only your record and not the room, so it can find inconsistencies in your account and cannot find what your account left out — except where the record's own shape implies it.

**Step 8 — defend it.** One challenger, playing the technology function: capable, unwilling, and correct that this was done to them rather than with them. Their instructions are to accept nothing on goodwill, to ask of every line what it will cost them, and to make one reasonable offer that leaves the hardest line unowned.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** The supervision level for each act, recorded separately. **A single overall judgement of a handover is unfalsifiable and always positive; four levels against four acts is a shape**, and the shape usually shows one act at level one that everyone in the room had been counting as transferred. That act is the finding, and no document review produces it.

### Second pass — the field the platform enforces, and the conversation it cannot

**Nothing in this pass is assessed. The eleven-item specification above is closed and unchanged**, and this pass exists because the sponsorship mechanism gives you a cheap external check on work you have already done, not because it adds an obligation.

**Step 9 — read the field, then find the person.** For every agent identity in scope, read the sponsor. Then do the thing the directory cannot: **establish whether that person knows.** Ask them. Record the answer, including the answer *no*, which is the useful one.

**Step 10 — say which population your claim covers.** Filter the directory for the older agent population, the objects that predate the identity type and carry none of its behaviours. **Any sentence you are about to write beginning "every agent" is false until you have done this**, and it will still be false afterwards unless you qualify it.

**Step 11 — defend the group, or replace it with a person.** Where a group is named as sponsor, state what that group does when the mail arrives. If the honest answer is that nobody in particular reads it, **you have found the signature farm implemented in a directory**, which is the same defect as naming a team on your manifest and is harder to see because a mechanism enforced it.

**Step 12 — test the departure path once, on paper.** Follow the transfer rule: if this sponsor left today, who becomes accountable? Now ask whether that person could act. **A transfer that moves accountability to somebody with no context is better than accountability evaporating, and it is not the same as a handover.**

**Step 13 — record it beside the demonstration.**

```yaml
# sponsorship-check.yaml — sits BESIDE handover-entrustment.yaml. Not assessed.
# Every role name and object type below is dated. Re-verify before repeating any of it.
population_covered:
  new_identity_type: "<count or list>"     # sponsorship enforced at creation
  older_service_principal_agents: "<count or list>"   # NO enforced sponsorship. Say so.
  therefore: >
    Claims of the form "every agent has an accountable sponsor" apply to the first
    group only. State the second group's size, or do not make the claim.

agents:
  - agent: "<name>"
    sponsor_field: "<person or group, as the directory holds it>"
    sponsor_is_a_group: false
    if_group_who_reads_the_mail: "<name, or NOBODY IN PARTICULAR>"
    asked_and_agreed: false        # <- the platform CANNOT check this. You can. Do.
    could_act: "<what they are actually able to do about it>"
    on_departure_transfers_to: "<their manager>"
    would_that_person_have_context: false

    # the technical owner is a DIFFERENT question and a different person
    owner_field: "<person>"
    owner_is_also_sponsor: false   # if true, the separation the directory offers was declined
```

**Step 14 — attack the sponsorship record, on a question the step-7 prompt cannot reach.** One prompt: *here is a list of agents with sponsors recorded in a directory. For each, describe the sequence of events by which the sponsor field stays correct and the accountability becomes fictional, without anybody doing anything wrong. Do not propose controls.*

The forbidden move is again the constraint that makes the exercise work. **An agent asked to propose controls proposes controls, and a control on top of an enforced mechanism is exactly the wrong response** to a mechanism that is already doing its job. An agent asked to narrate decay has to model an organisation, and its commonest output — a reorganisation, a manager who inherits sponsorship for a system they have never seen, a group that quietly becomes a distribution list — is testable against the customer in front of you. Record what you rejected. Record that the instrument sees a list and not a company, so every path it describes is a hypothesis about people it has never met.

**What the second pass makes observable.** That the platform's field and your `asked_and_agreed` line diverge, and by how much. **A directory can enforce that accountability has a name. Only a conversation establishes that the name is a person who knows** — and the size of that gap, measured on your own estate this morning, is the most concise argument for this session that anyone will ever hand you.

## What good looks like

- Ownership is stated as a **role and a person**, and where the two conflict — the person who cares has no authority, the person with authority has no interest — the conflict is on the page rather than resolved by optimism.
- **A supervision level is recorded per act, using sub-levels**, and no single overall level is claimed.
- The entrustment is labelled **ad-hoc**, honestly, unless multiple observers on multiple occasions genuinely produced it.
- The what-if answers are recorded verbatim, and at least one reveals a gap.
- The unaccepted line is real and its consequence is stated as something that will stop working, not as a risk score.
- The taper has an end. **A taper that fades out is not a taper; it is an assumption that you will still answer** — and the record states separately that entrustment expires through disuse rather than on the taper date.
- Under challenge, you can distinguish the lines you are confident about from the lines you are hoping about, without being pushed into either defending everything or conceding everything.

## How it goes wrong

- **The handover to a team** — every line is owned by a function and no line is owned by a person. *Warning sign:* you have not had a conversation with anyone you named.
- **The immaculate teardown** — the hygiene half is excellent, the transfer half is a folder of documents, and the whole thing reads as competence. *Warning sign:* your specification is longer than your manifest.
- **The signature farm** — every line has an owner because every line was assigned to somebody who was not asked. *Warning sign:* `asked_and_agreed` is missing from your record and you did not notice.
- **The demonstration nobody could fail** — you sequenced the acts so the recipient succeeded at everything, and learned nothing. *Warning sign:* every level is 3 or above and there are no recorded stops.
- **The rescue** — the recipient stalls, you explain, everybody feels the morning went well. *Warning sign:* you spoke.
- **The taper without a floor** — support reduces gradually and never reaches zero, so the engagement continues informally and unbudgeted. *Warning sign:* you cannot name the date.
- **The conceded manifest** — challenged on one line, you begin conceding lines you had good reasons for, because the challenge is uncomfortable. *Warning sign:* your third concession has no reason attached.
- **The defended fantasy** — challenged on the under-authorised owner, you defend the assignment instead of recording the problem. *Warning sign:* your answer is about what the owner *should* be able to do.

## Exercises

**These are the assessment.** The defence in step 8 and the demonstration record from steps 4 to 6 are the assessed artefacts, checked against the eleven-item specification above. Exercises 1, 2, 3 and 7 are preparation and are not themselves marked; exercises 4, 5 and 6 are performed live and are. **The second pass at steps 9 to 14 is not assessed and adds nothing to the specification, which is closed.**

**1 · Convert five manifest lines into entrustment units.** Take five lines and rewrite each as bounded work with a beginning and an end, delegable whole.

> **Criterion.** Following the framework: an activity a supervisor could hand over entire, whose completion the recipient would recognise. *"Own the monitoring"* fails on both counts. *"Respond to the weekly cost alert and record the disposition"* passes. **Then assign each an intended supervision level using sub-levels**, and notice that the honest levels are lower than the ones you would have written in a handover document.

**2 · Write three what-if questions for each demonstrated act.** Not questions about what happened. Questions about what did not.

> **What to notice.** That the good ones are uncomfortable to ask, because a plausible answer is *I do not know* and you will have to write it down. **That is the evidence.** The framework is explicit that an entrustment decision accepts risk precisely because what the person will encounter cannot be predicted; the what-if answers are the only purchase you have on the unencountered cases.

**3 · Go back to your own item 2, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 2 — which line the receiving side will accept without reading, and why that is worse. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside your demonstration record and write one sentence naming the difference between the line you predicted and the act that actually came out at the lowest supervision level.

> **Criterion.** A pass names a specific mismatch and says what produced it. The commonest honest answer is that the unread line and the low-level act are the same item, and that you knew it was fragile and expected the recipient's silence to be agreement rather than avoidance. **Bandura's finding is the one to hold against your prediction: options excluded on efficacy grounds are excluded before they are weighed, so an absence of objection is not evidence of agreement.**
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading your record is not the exercise. Naming the difference between your prediction and the observed level is.

**4 · Run the demonstration on the platform. Assessed.** The named owner starts the harness, reads one result's `reason`, works one runbook to its end state, reads the cost position and locates one evidence artefact — on their access, with you silent.

> **Scoring criterion.** Checked: that a level is recorded per act with sub-levels; that at least one stop is recorded; that the entrustment is labelled ad-hoc unless the summative standard was genuinely met. **A record with no stops and uniform high levels is scored as a failed observation, not a successful handover.** The recipient's competence is never scored. Yours is not either — what is checked is whether the observation happened and left a usable record.

**5 · Take the offer that leaves the hardest line unowned. Assessed.** The challenger will make one reasonable offer whose acceptance leaves your hardest line without an owner.

> **Scoring criterion.** A pass **records the line as unaccepted with its consequence** and does not accept the offer as though it solved anything, or accepts it and states plainly what remains unowned. A fail either defends an assignment nobody agreed to, or concedes lines it had good reasons for. **You are not marked on whether the handover would have been accepted.** A handover that records an unowned critical line and states the consequence passes; a handover with an owner for everything because nobody was asked does not.

**6 · Distinguish confidence from hope, with reasons. Assessed.** Under challenge, name one line you hold with confidence and one you hold on hope, and give the reason for each.

> **Scoring criterion.** Both must have reasons, and the reasons must differ in kind — a confident line rests on something observed, a hopeful one on something assumed. **This is item 11 and it is the one item that assesses a disposition rather than an artefact.** The framework's third professionalism characteristic is humility, "the ability to observe own limitations and willingness ask for help when needed", and this is where it is visible. The assessor records a written reason rather than a mark, because the useful information is why they judged as they did.

**7 · Sort six statements about an enforced sponsor field. Not assessed.** For each, say what it licenses and what it does not.

- *Every agent identity in this tenant has a sponsor.* · *Every agent in this tenant has a sponsor.* · *Every sponsor knows they are accountable.* · *If the sponsor leaves, accountability does not evaporate.* · *If the sponsor leaves, somebody with context becomes accountable.* · *The sponsor can decide the agent should be shut down, and cannot change what it does.*

> **Model answers.** True and checkable from the directory: the first, the fourth and the sixth. **The first and the second differ by one word and by a whole population** — sponsorship is enforced for the newer identity type, and agents predating it run on ordinary service principals with none of those behaviours, so the second is false in most real tenants and the documentation says so. The third is the one this session exists for: **the platform enforces that a name is present and cannot enforce that the name was asked**, which is the difference between your handover and a field. The fifth overreaches the fourth in exactly the way a summary would: transfer to a manager preserves accountability and supplies no context, and *somebody is now accountable* and *somebody now understands* are two claims, not one.
>
> **What to carry.** The sixth statement is the transferable idea and the reason this material is here at all: **the person who decides whether a thing should still exist is separated, by mechanism, from the person who can change what it does.** Every other statement on the list decays with the product. That one is a design principle you can apply to a system that has never heard of this directory — and applying it is what this module has been asking for since [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md).

## What this does not establish

**That this handover works, or that handovers of this shape produce systems that survive.** **No source behind this programme reports sustainment of any kind** — not whether a delivered system was still running later, not whether a customer maintained one, not a single post-deployment record.

**The specification is checkable and the thing it checks is form.** A learner can satisfy all eleven items and still have designed a transfer that fails, because the failure would occur months after the last observation this programme can make. The paper for this session states that limit as a concession rather than as a caveat.

**One morning is an ad-hoc entrustment.** The summative standard requires multiple observers in agreement, multiple observations and multiple evidence types. **This session runs a reduced version and the record must say so**, and a learner reporting an ad-hoc observation as a certification has committed the exact error the framework exists to prevent.

**The efficacy literature has never been tested on adult professionals learning a technical craft.** Its settings are schools, families, clinics and migration; its measure is self-report; its directionality is unsettled in field conditions; and the volume read is a conference proceedings edited by the construct's originator with no disconfirming study in its framing chapter. **The mechanism is used. No magnitude from it is asserted, and reading it in full does not change that.**

**The psychological-safety claim rests partly on an unread paper.** The foundational study is paywalled and has not been read by this repository; the trade book is a narrative synthesis by the construct's originator. Direction and mechanism only.

**The assessor-variability findings are single-institution medical-education studies using mock portfolios**, whose authors state that think-aloud methods cannot capture fast processing and that participants may construct explanations for the researcher. They establish how assessors reason, not that narrative judgement is more valid than scored judgement.

**No evaluator's agreement with human judgement is published, and none is asserted.** The recipient reading a `reason` is reading a model's stated rationale, not a validated one.

**And the honest limit of the whole module: naming a receiving owner is an intervention with no evidence behind it.** It is derived from an absence — the source corpus names no receiving owner anywhere — and from an argument that a team is not a thing that can run a harness. That argument is not a finding.

**A platform enforcing sponsorship does not weaken that concession, and it is important not to let it look as though it does.** The directory establishes that somebody built this mechanism and believes in it. **It establishes nothing about whether it works.** No evaluation exists of whether enforced sponsorship is maintained in practice, whether sponsors act, or whether the requirement is satisfied by naming a group nobody reads. **Enforcement is evidence of intent, not of outcome**, and treating a vendor's design decision as a validation of this module's design decision would be the same error this programme spends [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) teaching people to avoid.

**And the enforcement covers one population.** Agents predating the identity type run on ordinary service principals with no enforced sponsorship at all, in the same directory, looking adjacent. **Any claim about "every agent" is false in most real estates**, and the documentation states this rather than concealing it.
