# S082 · Adoption You Cannot Mandate

## The situation

The usage report says the system is used. It counts accounts provisioned and drafts generated, and both numbers are healthy.

At the second site, the reviewers stopped some weeks ago and went back to writing the records by hand. Their accounts are still provisioned. Drafts are still generated for them, and then discarded.

Answer these in writing before the session opens.

1. What act, performed by a named person, would you accept as evidence that this system is in use at a site?
2. What does your evidence do when somebody generates a draft and then ignores it?
3. Take one group that has stopped. Are they unaware, unwilling, untaught, unable, or unsupported — and how did you tell the difference?
4. Whose job got worse because this system exists? Answer for a named role, not for the organisation.
5. If the answer to item 4 is *nobody*, what were you delivering?

**Item 3 is the session's instrument and item 5 is its trap.** A system that automates part of somebody's work and made nobody's work worse has either been badly examined or was not worth building. Both are findings.

## What you must produce

An **adoption position**, per receiving group: what you claim about their use of the system, the observed act it rests on, the date of that observation, and — the field everybody omits — **the evidence that would change the position.**

Plus a diagnosis for at least one group that has stopped, locating them on the readiness sequence and naming the intervention that follows from that location. **If your diagnosis is a willingness problem and your intervention is a training session, you have not made a diagnosis.**

## What good looks like

- Every position rests on an observed act with a date. **A provisioned account is a capability, not a use.**
- The groups you cannot see are listed as groups you cannot see, not omitted. The managed-service supplier is on that list unless you can say otherwise.
- The diagnosis distinguishes being told from being willing, and knowing how from being able. Those are different states with different remedies and they are routinely collapsed into one.
- **Reversion is treated as information rather than as failure.** A group that used the system and stopped has told you something a group that never started has not.
- Where the honest answer is that somebody's job got worse, it is written down in those words, with the role named.
- The position names who is responsible for establishing that any observed change was caused by the system. **A claim whose attribution is assigned to nobody is not a claim.**

## How it goes wrong

Five failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **Provisioning read as adoption** — the account exists, therefore the person uses it. *Warning sign:* your strongest evidence came out of an administration console.
- **The enthusiast as the population** — one capable, interested person is using it well, and the group is recorded as adopting. *Warning sign:* you can name the user.
- **Training as the universal remedy** — every gap acquires a session, whatever the gap was. *Warning sign:* your intervention is the same for two groups with different diagnoses.
- **The unwelcome system explained as resistance** — somebody has a rational objection to a system that removes part of their work, and it is recorded as a communication problem. *Warning sign:* your plan involves explaining the benefits again.
- **The closed channel** — the group is failing and the system is at fault, and nobody can tell, because there is nowhere for them to say so. *Warning sign:* your evidence of a problem came from watching, and there is no route by which it could have come from them.

## Do it

Work from the supplied situation in [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md). Build the position for each group named there, including the ones you cannot observe.

For the site that reverted: you may not ask the delivery team what happened. **Design the observation instead** — what would you look at, who would you ask, and what would you ask them that does not invite a flattering answer? You have done this before, at [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) and [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md), and the constraint is the same one: what somebody says about their own use of a tool is weak evidence about their use of the tool.

Then write the diagnosis. One group, one location on the sequence, one intervention, and one statement of what you would expect to observe if the intervention worked.

## Show it

Report the group that stopped, your diagnosis, and the evidence that would overturn it.

Then answer one question from the room: **what would this group say about your diagnosis if they were here?** A diagnosis that could not be said out loud to the people it describes is a diagnosis about their deficiency, and it will be wrong for that reason before it is wrong for any other.

## What this does not establish

**Where adoption most often fails.** The readiness sequence this session uses comes from a practitioner model published by the consultancy that owns its trademark and sells training and certification in it. Its supporting evidence is that firm's own survey work; no independent evaluation of the model is cited anywhere in the book, and its ordering claim is argued from the meanings of its own terms rather than from any observation.

**That warrant licenses direction and mechanism only.** It licenses no ordering strength, no rate, no proportion and no claim that using the model improves anything. Two of its distinctions are worth having because they are real and routinely conflated — being told is not being aware, and knowing how is not being able — and one of its observations is directly reusable: a closed feedback channel makes a broken system look like a failing operator. **None of that is evidence.**

**One correction travels with the model.** A widely repeated summary holds that effort spent past the blocking element is wasted. **The book does not say that**, and this repository withdrew the claim on reading it. Its own formulations are conditional — that a given effort will not by itself produce the change, or will not be productive until the blocking element moves — with a single absolute restricted to knowledge in the absence of willingness. Use the conditional form.

**And the practice corpus behind this programme observes nobody.** Its discovery method is entirely self-report about an engineering system; nobody is asked to demonstrate anything or set up an environment while being watched, and no case in it reports whether a delivered system was still being used later. This session takes the opposite position on an argument rather than on evidence.
