# S082 · Adoption You Cannot Mandate

**Module:** [M17](/wiki/modules/M17.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** Copilot Studio at its published department-level scale, with gateway telemetry as the record of whether anyone actually used it
**Whitepaper:** [WP-082](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-082.md)
**Retrieves from:** S019, S066, S076, S078
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

---

## The situation

The usage report says the system is used. It counts accounts provisioned and drafts generated, and both numbers are healthy.

At the second site, the reviewers stopped some weeks ago and went back to writing the records by hand. **Their accounts are still provisioned. Drafts are still generated for them, and then discarded.**

Nobody lied. The report counts what the platform emits, and the platform emits provisioning and generation because those are the events the platform causes. It does not emit *somebody read this and kept it*, because that event happens in a person.

Answer these in writing before the session opens.

1. What act, performed by a named person, would you accept as evidence that this system is in use at a site?
2. What does your evidence do when somebody generates a draft and then ignores it?
3. Take one group that has stopped. Are they unaware, unwilling, untaught, unable, or unsupported — and how did you tell the difference?
4. Whose job got worse because this system exists? Answer for a named role, not for the organisation.
5. If the answer to item 4 is *nobody*, what were you delivering?

**Item 3 is the session's instrument and item 5 is its trap.** A system that automates part of somebody's work and made nobody's work worse has either been badly examined or was not worth building. Both are findings, and most learners write *nobody* without noticing they have written it.

## What you must produce

**An adoption position per receiving group, and a diagnosis for one group that stopped.**

1. **The claim**, per group: what you assert about their use of the system.
2. **The observed act** it rests on — performed by a person, with a date.
3. **The evidence that would change the position.** The field everybody omits.
4. **The groups you cannot see**, listed as such rather than left out. The managed-service supplier is on that list unless you can say otherwise.
5. **The instrumentation record**: what the telemetry emits, what it cannot emit, and what you added to make one receiving group distinguishable from another.
6. **The diagnosis** for one group that stopped: where they are on the readiness sequence, and the intervention that follows from that location. **If your diagnosis is a willingness problem and your intervention is a training session, you have not made a diagnosis.**
7. **The role whose job got worse**, named, in those words.
8. **The scale position**: whether the tool you shipped is being used inside or outside its published deployment envelope, with the page and the date you checked.

## The material

### Adoption cannot be mandated, because use is a choice made repeatedly by people with alternatives

This is the durable claim and it deserves its strongest form before anything softens it.

A person with a keyboard and a deadline decides, several times a day, whether to open the thing you built. **They decide again tomorrow.** Nothing about the previous decision binds the next one, and every decision is made against an alternative that already works and that they already know how to operate. A mandate changes the cost of the alternative; it does not remove the alternative, and it does not survive the first week in which the mandated path is slower.

The change-management literature this programme uses reaches the same place from a different direction and is unusually blunt about the limits of the practitioner's control. Willingness is "ultimately about personal choice", and "remains elusive and, by definition, not under our direct control" ([research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md)). The named executive error is precise: **"A common mistake made by many business leaders is to assume that by building awareness of the need for change they have also created desire. Resistance to change from employees takes them by surprise".** Telling somebody why is not making them willing, and the surprise is the tell.

The same source supplies the two distinctions learners collapse most often. Being told is not being aware — **"Awareness is not achieved based on the messages sent, but rather how the messages are received and internalized by each person"** — and knowing how is not being able: the presence of knowledge "is often insufficient by itself", and, unusually for a book of this kind, "Some employees, depending on the change, may never develop the required abilities."

One observation from it transfers directly into engineering and is the single most useful sentence in the source for this session: **"If the feedback channel is not open, then the employee will appear to be failing when the system is actually at fault."** Hold that against the second site. Nobody there filed a defect. The platform emitted healthy numbers. **The absence of a complaint was read as the absence of a problem, and it was the absence of a channel.**

**The warrant is thin and travels with every use.** This is a practitioner model published by the consultancy that owns its trademark and sells the training and certification in it; every quantitative claim in the book comes from that firm's own survey work; **no independent evaluation of the model is cited anywhere in it**; and its ordering claim is argued from the meanings of its own terms rather than from any observation. It licenses direction and mechanism and nothing else — no ordering strength, no rate, no proportion, no timescale.

**And one correction travels with it.** A widely repeated summary holds that effort spent past the blocking element is wasted. **The book does not say that**, and this repository withdrew the claim on reading it. Its own formulations are conditional — a given effort will not *by itself* produce the change, or will not be productive *until* the blocking element moves — with a single absolute restricted to knowledge in the absence of willingness. **Use the conditional form.**

### Adoption is an efficacy question before it is a preference question

There is a step before willingness that the readiness sequence blurs, and it explains a group that says all the right things and does nothing.

A person's judgement that they can successfully execute a behaviour is distinct from their judgement that the behaviour produces a good outcome ([research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md](/research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md)). The two come apart constantly in adoption. A reviewer can believe the assistant produces good drafts *and* believe that they personally cannot get a good draft out of it — and **the second belief governs whether they open it.**

The practical consequence is that *they do not see the value* is usually the wrong diagnosis and always the easiest one to reach, because it is the answer people offer when asked. The efficacy question is not asked directly, because the honest answer is embarrassing.

**The limits here are severe and must be carried.** The paper that introduced the construct is **paywalled and unread by this repository**; its account rests on the official abstract and on later authors' characterisations. The volume that was read in full is an edited conference collection in which the originator wrote one chapter, and a claim from a contributed chapter is that contributor's claim rather than his. **Direction and mechanism only.**

### Telemetry is the only honest record, and its absence is itself a finding

Here the platform stops illustrating and starts working.

The AI gateway material argues that the reverse proxy in front of model endpoints is the right place to enforce cost control, quota fairness, authentication, resilience and observability, and is explicit that this is "not a separate offering" — the AI gateway "extends API Management's existing API gateway" ([research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md)). The claim inside that framing is the useful part: **AI traffic is API traffic with different resource semantics, not a new category of thing.**

The observability capability is where this session lives. The `llm-emit-token-metric` policy attaches custom dimensions to emitted metrics — client address, API identity, a user identifier taken from a header. **Dimensions are what make a metric answerable.** Total consumption tells you the bill. Consumption per API per user tells you who to talk to.

Read that as an adoption instrument rather than a cost instrument and the session's method appears. **A metric with no dimension identifying the receiving group cannot distinguish a site that stopped from a site that never started**, and it cannot distinguish either from a site whose traffic is one enthusiast. The undimensioned metric is not neutral — it actively conceals the thing you need.

The token limit policy supplies the other half. It enforces a limit keyed on "any counter key, such as subscription key, originating IP address, or an arbitrary key defined through a policy expression", which means **the key that makes contention fair also makes consumption attributable.** The fairness mechanism and the adoption instrument are the same mechanism, and a team that configured the first has already built the second without noticing.

**The absence is the finding, and it is cheap.** A group with provisioned accounts and no dimensioned traffic in the window is a group that is not using the system, and that statement rests on an observation rather than on a conversation. It is also falsifiable: they may be reaching the model by a path that bypasses the gateway, and a competent position says so.

**The documentation's limits.** Policy names, tier availability and preview status change frequently; the Foundry integration was in preview at the reading; the individual policy reference pages were not fetched. And the page **does not discuss the privacy implications of logging prompts and completions**, in a section that recommends logging them for "billing and auditing". **A learner instrumenting adoption is instrumenting people, and the documentation will not raise that with you.**

### The scale envelope is published, and a tool used outside it fails as governance rather than as function

Microsoft's own comparison guidance positions **Copilot Studio as "designed for small-scale deployments, such as department-level solutions"** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). That is a scale claim published by a vendor about its own product, and it is unusually candid.

It matters here for a reason that is not obvious. **A tool used outside its stated envelope does not stop working — it stops being governable**, and the symptoms present as lifecycle and ownership problems rather than as failures. Who owns this agent. Who reviews its instructions when the model behind it changes. Who notices when the department that built it reorganises. **Those are adoption questions wearing governance clothes**, and they are why a healthy usage report can sit on top of a system nobody is maintaining.

The move is small and belongs in the adoption position: state whether what you shipped is inside the envelope the vendor published, on the date you checked. If it is outside, **the group most likely to stop is the one furthest from whoever built it** — because they are the group with no route to the person who could fix it.

**Its limits:** read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches; product names actively in flux; **no adoption, cost, failure-rate or outcome data supports any of its recommendations**; and a capability boundary recorded today may be closed tomorrow, so no limitation here may be taught as permanent.

### A win nobody can see is not a win

The other change literature this programme holds is specific about what counts as visible progress, and its definition is operational rather than motivational. A good short-term win is **visible** — "large numbers of people can see for themselves whether the result is real or just hype" — **unambiguous**, with "little argument over the call", and **clearly related to the change effort** ([research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md](/research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md)). What does not qualify is spelled out: having a good meeting does not, nor does producing a design the engineering manager thinks is terrific, nor does circulating a vision statement.

The distinction to carry is the next one: **"Creating short-term wins is different from hoping for short-term wins. The latter is passive, the former active."** An adoption position built on hoped-for wins is a forecast. One built on visible, unambiguous, attributable acts is a record.

The same source names the mechanism by which a stated commitment is quietly undone, and it is behavioural rather than communicative: **"Nothing undermines the communication of a change vision more than behavior on the part of key players that seems inconsistent with the vision."** A group told the assistant is now the way of working, whose own manager still asks for the hand-written version, has received a clear instruction and it was not the spoken one.

**Its limits are stated by the author himself** and are unusually easy to honour: the book contains no footnotes and cites no published source except his own writing; every extended case is pseudonymous or unnamed, so **no case in it can be traced or checked by a reader**; and its most-quoted figures are derived by inspecting his own diagrams. **Direction and mechanism only, and no figure from it appears here.**

### The practice corpus observes nobody, and this session takes the opposite position

The internal material this programme is built on collects its discovery data entirely by self-report about an engineering system. **Nobody is asked to demonstrate anything or to set up an environment while being watched.** And **no case in it reports whether a delivered system was still being used later** — sustainment is not reported once, anywhere ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)).

**So this session is written past its source rather than from it.** The requirement that adoption rest on an observed act is this design's own argument, and it is class 3 — design reasoning with no external warrant. **Say so when you use it.**

**Its limits:** access-controlled and unverifiable by any public reader; paraphrased and never quoted; it contradicts itself in at least a dozen places; no failure, no cost side and no quality evidence appears anywhere in it.

## The workflow

**Make one receiving group's use — or non-use — visible in telemetry that a stranger could read.**

You will need an API Management instance in front of the model endpoint your assistant calls, and permission to edit its policy. Where you have neither, run the procedure against a sandbox instance and record that you did.

**Step 1 — write the claim before you look.** One sentence per receiving group: what you believe about their use. **Date it.** You are about to acquire evidence, and a belief recorded afterwards is not a prediction.

**Step 2 — check the envelope.** Open the vendor comparison guidance for the tool you shipped and find its stated deployment scale. Record the page and the date. **If your deployment is outside the published envelope, put that in the adoption position rather than in a risk register**, because it predicts which group stops first.

**Step 3 — route the traffic through the gateway.** Import the model API into API Management and authenticate to the backend with a managed identity, so that no long-lived key exists. **This is not incidental:** traffic that bypasses the gateway is traffic you cannot attribute, and an adoption claim with an unmeasured path has a hole in it.

**Step 4 — add the dimension that makes the metric answerable.** Edit the inbound policy. The dimension that matters is the one identifying the *receiving group*, not the application.

```xml
<!-- APIM inbound policy fragment. SHAPE, NOT SCHEMA.
     Policy names, attributes and tier availability change frequently.
     Verify every element against current documentation on the day you use it. -->
<policies>
  <inbound>
    <base />

    <!-- Attribution. A metric with no group dimension cannot tell a site that
         STOPPED from a site that never started. That is the whole exercise.
         Note the &quot; entities: a policy expression's own string literals
         must be escaped, and forgetting it is the commonest authoring error. -->
    <llm-emit-token-metric namespace="adoption">
      <dimension name="ReceivingGroup"
                 value="@(context.Request.Headers.GetValueOrDefault(&quot;x-receiving-group&quot;,&quot;unattributed&quot;))" />
      <dimension name="ApiId" value="@(context.Api.Id)" />
      <dimension name="UserId"
                 value="@(context.Request.Headers.GetValueOrDefault(&quot;x-user-id&quot;,&quot;N/A&quot;))" />
    </llm-emit-token-metric>

    <!-- Fairness and attribution are the SAME mechanism. The counter key that
         stops one group starving another also makes each group countable. -->
    <llm-token-limit counter-key="@(context.Request.Headers.GetValueOrDefault(&quot;x-receiving-group&quot;,&quot;unattributed&quot;))"
                     tokens-per-minute="<derive from your own capacity plan>"
                     estimate-prompt-tokens="true"
                     remaining-tokens-variable-name="remainingTokens" />
  </inbound>
</policies>
```

**Step 5 — watch the `unattributed` bucket.** It will not be empty. Every request arriving without the group header is a path you did not know about, and **the size of that bucket is a fact about your understanding of the estate rather than about the estate.** Resolve it before you report anything.

**Step 6 — query per group, per week, and read the shape rather than the total.** Three shapes matter and each means something different. **Flat and low** is a group that never started. **High then zero** is a group that started and stopped — the most informative result available and the one your report is least likely to surface. **Concentrated in one identity** is an enthusiast, not a group.

**Step 7 — go and find out why, without inviting a flattering answer.** For the group that stopped, ask about the last time rather than about the general case: *walk me through the last record you wrote by hand — what happened when you tried it the other way?* You may not ask the delivery team what happened. **Design the observation instead**, as at [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md) and [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md).

**Step 8 — write the diagnosis and its falsifier together.** One group, one location on the readiness sequence, one intervention, and one statement of what you would expect to see in the telemetry if the intervention worked. **If you cannot state the observation, the intervention is a gesture.**

**Step 9 — use the agent to attack your own position, not to extend it.** One prompt: *for each group, argue that my adoption claim is contradicted by the telemetry I collected — then find the group where my evidence is weakest and say what I should have measured instead.* Read it for the second half. Record what you kept and what you rejected, and record that the instrument shares a model family with the system under assessment and is therefore not independent of it.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** The `unattributed` bucket, and the *high then zero* shape.

Neither can be argued into existence. The first is a measured hole in your own map of the estate and it appears within minutes of the first request. The second is a group that made a decision repeatedly and then made a different one — **and it is invisible in every total, in every dashboard, and in the usage report that opened this session.** A learner who has once watched a group's line fall to zero while its provisioned-account count stayed flat does not afterwards accept a provisioning number as evidence of anything.

## What good looks like

- Every position rests on an observed act with a date. **A provisioned account is a capability, not a use.**
- The groups you cannot see are listed as groups you cannot see, not omitted. The managed-service supplier is on that list unless you can say otherwise.
- **The instrumentation record says what the telemetry cannot emit**, and the `unattributed` share is reported rather than quietly excluded.
- The diagnosis distinguishes being told from being willing, and knowing how from being able. Those are different states with different remedies and they are routinely collapsed into one.
- **Reversion is treated as information rather than as failure.** A group that used the system and stopped has told you something a group that never started has not.
- Where the honest answer is that somebody's job got worse, it is written down in those words, with the role named.
- The tool's published deployment envelope is stated, with the page and the date, and the position says whether the deployment sits inside it.
- The position names who is responsible for establishing that any observed change was caused by the system. **A claim whose attribution is assigned to nobody is not a claim.**

## How it goes wrong

- **Provisioning read as adoption** — the account exists, therefore the person uses it. *Warning sign:* your strongest evidence came out of an administration console.
- **The undimensioned metric** — consumption is measured in total and cannot be attributed to a group. *Warning sign:* you can state the bill and cannot state who incurred it.
- **The unattributed bucket ignored** — traffic arrives without a group header and is silently dropped from the analysis. *Warning sign:* your per-group figures do not sum to your total and you had not checked.
- **The enthusiast as the population** — one capable, interested person is using it well, and the group is recorded as adopting. *Warning sign:* you can name the user.
- **Training as the universal remedy** — every gap acquires a session, whatever the gap was. *Warning sign:* your intervention is the same for two groups with different diagnoses.
- **Value substituted for efficacy** — the group is recorded as unconvinced, when the truth is that they cannot get a usable result out of it and will not say so. *Warning sign:* you asked whether they found it useful and accepted the answer.
- **The unwelcome system explained as resistance** — somebody has a rational objection to a system that removes part of their work, and it is recorded as a communication problem. *Warning sign:* your plan involves explaining the benefits again.
- **The closed channel** — the group is failing, the system is at fault, and nobody can tell because there is nowhere for them to say so. *Warning sign:* your evidence of a problem came from watching, and there is no route by which it could have come from them.
- **The envelope ignored** — a department-scale tool is running estate-wide and the symptoms are read as adoption problems. *Warning sign:* nobody can say who owns the agent's instructions.

## Exercises

**None of these is assessed.** The adoption position is carried to [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md), where it becomes a question about time, and to [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md), where it either supports the maturity claim or does not.

**1 · Sort seven statements into capability, act and inference.** For each, say what it licenses about adoption.

- *Every reviewer at the site has an account.* · *Drafts were generated for the site every working day last month.* · *Two named reviewers submitted records containing generated text last week.* · *The site is bought in.* · *Gateway traffic for the site fell to zero four weeks ago and has not returned.* · *The team says the tool is working well.* · *One identity accounts for the site's entire traffic.*

> **Model answers.** The first is a **capability** — provisioning, which licenses nothing about use. The second, third, fifth and seventh are **acts**: something happened, at a time, and each is checkable. The fourth and sixth are **inferences** from self-report and license nothing. **The general rule: a statement is an act if you can name what happened, when, and to whom; a capability if it describes what somebody could do; and an inference if it summarises what somebody said.** The second is the trap in this list — a draft generated and discarded is an act performed by the *system*, not by a person, and it is exactly the event the opening report was counting.

**2 · Instrument one group and read the shape.** Using the workflow, add a receiving-group dimension and query the metric per group per week for as long a window as you have.

> **What you should find.** Three shapes and one bucket: flat-and-low, high-then-zero, one-identity, and `unattributed`. **Report the `unattributed` share as a headline rather than a footnote.** A learner who reports per-group figures without saying what fraction of traffic could not be attributed has produced the same artefact as the usage report that opened the session, with more steps. If your `unattributed` share is zero, check whether the header is being set by a component you control, in which case you have measured your own configuration.

**3 · Go back to your own item 3, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 3 — how you told the difference between the readiness states for a group that stopped. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then write the honest version.

> **Criterion.** A pass names, for the state you assigned, **an observation that would have come out differently had the group been in the adjacent state.** *Unaware* passes if you can say what they would have known. *Unable* passes if you can say what they attempted and could not complete. **A pass is available only by naming a discriminating observation.** *"They were not engaged"* fails, because no adjacent state would look different. *"They said they did not have time"* fails, because it is what people say in at least three of the five states.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**4 · Find the published envelope.** For the agent-building tool your project uses, find where the vendor states its intended deployment scale. Record the page and the date, then write one sentence saying whether your deployment is inside it.

> **What you should find.** The comparison guidance states that Copilot Studio is designed for small-scale deployments such as department-level solutions. **If you cannot find a scale statement for the tool you chose, that absence is the finding** — record it as an open question rather than inferring a limit, and note that an absent envelope is not a permissive one.

**5 · Answer for the room.** Take your diagnosis of the group that stopped and answer one question in writing: **what would this group say about your diagnosis if they were in the room?**

> **Scoring criterion.** A pass produces a sentence the group could hear without being insulted, and which they would recognise as accurate. A fail produces a diagnosis about their deficiency. **A diagnosis that could not be said out loud to the people it describes is a diagnosis about their deficiency, and it will be wrong for that reason before it is wrong for any other.** The test is not politeness; it is that a deficiency diagnosis stops you looking at the system.

**6 · Name the role whose job got worse.** From your own delivery, name one role that lost something. Write what they lost, in their words rather than yours.

> **What to notice.** How hard this is, and how often the first honest answer is a role nobody consulted. **A system that automates part of somebody's work and made nobody's work worse has either been badly examined or was not worth building** — and if your answer to opening item 4 was *nobody*, this exercise is where you find out which of the two it was.

## What this does not establish

**Where adoption most often fails.** The readiness sequence this session uses comes from a practitioner model published by the consultancy that owns its trademark and sells training and certification in it. Its supporting evidence is that firm's own survey work; no independent evaluation of the model is cited anywhere in the book; and its ordering claim is argued from the meanings of its own terms rather than from any observation.

**That warrant licenses direction and mechanism only.** No ordering strength, no rate, no proportion, no timescale, and no claim that using the model improves anything. Two of its distinctions are worth having because they are real and routinely conflated — being told is not being aware, and knowing how is not being able — and one of its observations is directly reusable: a closed feedback channel makes a broken system look like a failing operator. **None of that is evidence.**

**One correction travels with the model.** A widely repeated summary holds that effort spent past the blocking element is wasted. **The book does not say that**, and this repository withdrew the claim on reading it. Its own formulations are conditional — that a given effort will not by itself produce the change, or will not be productive until the blocking element moves — with a single absolute restricted to knowledge in the absence of willingness. Use the conditional form.

**The other change source is weaker still, by its author's own statement.** It contains no footnotes, cites no published source except his own writing, and every extended case in it is pseudonymous or unnamed — **no case can be traced or checked by any reader.** Its short-term-win criteria are a definition worth using and are not a finding.

**The efficacy material rests on an unread paper.** The originating work is paywalled and was not read by this repository; the volume that was read in full is an edited collection in which the originator wrote one chapter. **Direction and mechanism only, and the distinction between believing a thing works and believing you can make it work is the only part used here.**

**Telemetry establishes traffic, not use.** A dimensioned metric shows that a request was made under an identity. It does not show that a human read the output, kept it, or was helped by it. **The gateway is a better instrument than the usage report and it is not a good instrument**, and a position that treats a token count as evidence of value has repeated the opening error one layer down.

**The vendor documentation publishes no adoption data.** No adoption, cost, failure-rate or outcome data supports any of its recommendations, and its scale statements are design intent rather than measured limits. **No detection, accuracy or effectiveness figure appears in any of it, and none is asserted here.**

**And the practice corpus behind this programme observes nobody.** Its discovery method is entirely self-report about an engineering system; nobody is asked to demonstrate anything or set up an environment while being watched; and no case in it reports whether a delivered system was still being used later. **This session takes the opposite position on an argument rather than on evidence, and is class 3 throughout.**
