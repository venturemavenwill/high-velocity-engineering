## The situation

This is the stage's second assessment occasion and the last in the module. It marks one thing.

**What work did this assessment cause?**

Not whether the system complies. Not whether the assessment is thorough. **Whether anybody is doing something on Monday that they were not doing on Friday**, and whether that thing is traceable to something you wrote.

The module's durable claim is stated here and it is the sentence to carry out of the stage. **An assessment that produces a signature has changed nothing. An assessment that produces a work item has.** A signature is a record of agreement; a work item is a change in what somebody does. The two look similar in a report and are not the same kind of thing at all.

The occasion adds one mechanism to that claim, and it is the module's last idea. **An open concern with no failing test attached can be closed by assertion.** Somebody says it is handled, the item is marked done, and nothing anywhere disagrees. **Bind the concern to an evaluation that currently fails, and closing it by assertion becomes visible** — the test is still red, and a green summary that hides a red test is a defect you already know how to find.

Answer these in writing before you begin, and submit them.

1. What changed because of this assessment?
2. Who is doing something differently, and can they name what?
3. Which of your findings is a legitimate engineering trade-off rather than a violation?
4. What did you deliberately not give the team yet, and when will you?
5. If this assessment were deleted tomorrow, what would be lost?

**Item 2 is the assessment's centre of gravity.** Item 3 is the one candidates skip, and skipping it is expensive: **a legitimate engineering trade-off scored as a violation teaches a team to ignore the scorer**, and a scorer a team ignores has cost more than its findings were worth.

## What you must produce

**A responsible-AI assessment for your system, and the work it generated.**

1. **The assessment**, carrying the scope determination from [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md), the necessity record from [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md), the sensitive-use determination and decision from [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md), and the instrumentation from [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md).
2. **The work items**, each with an imperative title, a named owner, a date, the finding it came from, the affected components and flows, a **reason specific to this system**, and **testable acceptance criteria.**
3. **At least one open concern bound to a failing evaluation**, with the evaluator named, the threshold stated and justified, the split strategy declared, and the current result red.
4. **The permanent record and the temporary action plan, as two documents.**
5. **A record of the discussion** — when the team read the findings and what they said — dated before any tracked item was created.
6. **The withheld tail** — what you deliberately did not give them yet, and when they get it.
7. **The unassessed list** — what this assessment did not cover, including the wrongly excluded case from [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md) and the dodge check from [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md).

## The material

### A self-verification checklist is a configured control, not an executed one

The sharpest available demonstration of why a concern needs a failing test comes from a well-built system that does the opposite.

The multi-agent pipeline this stage has studied ends every stage in a numbered self-verification checklist — identifier uniqueness, referential integrity, arithmetic reconciliation. **These are prompt-engineering devices that improve output quality and they are not controls: the model checks its own output and reports that it did** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md)). In one stage the checklist is rendered into the deliverable **with the model's own ticks**, which makes a configured control wear the visual language of an audit trail.

**The verdict that note reaches is the sentence this occasion is built on.** If a control must be able to fail without the model's cooperation, that pipeline has roughly half a dozen controls and several hundred instructions. **Heavily configured, thinly executable, entirely unmeasured for outcome.**

The same source contains the best answer to it, and the answer is not more instructions. **A threat's status is re-derived from the workspace rather than ticked by a person.** The status stage searches source, pipeline definitions, container files, infrastructure dialects, configuration and documentation for evidence that each mitigation is implemented, and **a claim of "implemented" must cite a file and a location.** Re-running after a fix finds the evidence, flips the status, and no work item is generated. **Evidence in the repository is the closure criterion.**

Three cautions belong with it and this occasion inherits all three. **Nothing schedules the re-run.** A fix that is real but invisible in the repository — a portal setting, a tenant policy — will never close and will regenerate indefinitely. **And the acceptance criteria a human might close a ticket against were written by the same model that wrote the mitigation.**

**Its limits.** Internal and access-controlled, paraphrased here and never quoted; **it cannot supply an external warrant for any claim.** No outcome evidence exists for any of it — nothing establishes that plans produced this way lead to more fixed vulnerabilities than plans produced any other way. Its implied observed failures are nowhere recorded. **The source contains operational attack detail that is not reproduced here and should not be.**

### An evaluation is where a concern goes to stop being an assertion

The platform anchor supplies the mechanism, and its programming model is small enough to hold in one hand.

Three types: an item wrapping a conversation with a query and a response derived by a split strategy, an evaluator that scores items, and aggregated results with pass and fail counts and per-item detail ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). Two tiers: a **local evaluator** running deterministic checks with no API calls, for inner-loop and continuous-integration use; and a cloud tier for model-judged evaluation. **They can be mixed in a single run**, which is the cost-and-speed ladder of the evaluation literature expressed directly in an interface.

Four properties make it the right instrument for binding a governance concern.

**Stored responses can be evaluated "from logs or previous runs… without re-running the agent."** That is what separates production evaluation from synthetic testing: synthetic queries test what you thought to ask, and a trace contains what occurred. A governance concern is almost always about what occurred.

**The split strategy silently determines what a score means.** The documentation says so directly — **"How you split determines *what you're evaluating*"** — and offers last-turn, full-conversation and per-turn options, plus a custom splitter. **An agent that recovers well from a bad first attempt scores well under one and badly under another. Neither score is wrong; they answer different questions**, and a team that never chose is reporting an answer to a question it did not ask.

**The threshold is a policy choice separable from the measurement.** Output is pass or fail with a `reason`, and for scale-based evaluators a numeric score alongside it. **Moving the threshold changes the pass rate without changing the system**, so a threshold must be justified rather than inherited — and the `reason` string is what makes the verdict auditable rather than merely numeric.

**And repetitions exist "to detect non-deterministic behavior"**, which is the documentation's own acknowledgement that a single sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement.

**The silence in this source is the point rather than a footnote.** It is definitional and accurate about what the tooling computes, and **no agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold.** Most of these evaluators are model-judged. **Assert no agreement figure for any of them.** The pages were read through search excerpts rather than full fetches, most agent evaluators are marked preview, and one consulted page is an explicitly superseded variant. **Cloud evaluation incurs cost, which a team must budget for.**

### Validate the validator, or you have moved the trust problem

Binding a concern to a judged evaluator relocates the question rather than answering it, and the literature is blunt about this.

**LLM-generated evaluators inherit all the problems of the LLMs they evaluate, requiring further human validation** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md)). The mechanism that fixes it is alignment against human grades: hand-label a subset, then prefer the candidate implementation whose verdicts best reproduce your own labels. **That is the step that converts an unvalidated generated evaluator into one with an evidentiary basis**, and it is the step nobody does.

The practitioner literature gives the same instruction with an operational detail worth keeping. Track the judge's agreement with human labels in a spreadsheet holding the model's response, its critique, its verdict and a human's parallel critique and verdict, filled in a batch at a time — and **"using raw agreement is generally not recommended and can be misleading when classes are imbalanced. Instead, you should typically measure precision and recall separately"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md)). The same source supplies the decomposition that makes a governance concern testable at all: **features and scenarios.** Break the system into what it can do, and the situations each capability must handle, and each situation becomes an assertion. And it supplies the line most engineers need to hear: **unlike traditional unit tests, an all-green suite is not the target — the pass rate is a product decision.**

**Their limits.** The alignment work is **qualitative**; it establishes that criteria drift is real and consequential and **does not quantify its prevalence or magnitude**, and its user study is small by construction. The practitioner essay rests on **one company's case study with no control condition, no measured improvement and no released dataset**, and its tooling references are dated. **Take the pattern, not the product.**

And one statistical caution belongs on any bound evaluation. **An eval is an experiment**, so a pass rate without an interval is not a result, and a comparison too small to detect the difference you are claiming produces the same output as a system with no difference ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). **Only one of those two is a finding.** That source is an analytical paper; its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions, **statistical validity says nothing about construct validity**, and its power analysis needs prior variance data a first-time team lacks.

### How the findings reach the team, which decides whether any of this matters

The practitioner judgements encoded in the pipeline studied above are the most useful operational content available on this, and they are paraphrased rather than quoted.

**Do not create tracked issues before the team has read and discussed the findings.** The chain that encodes this rule violates it, terminating in an automated write to a live backlog with nobody required to have read anything. **You are being marked on not doing what that chain does.**

**Teams given every finding at once fix none of them; withhold the long tail deliberately.** The withholding is a professional act rather than a concealment, **which is why the withheld set is recorded and dated rather than dropped.**

**A legitimate engineering trade-off is not a violation, and scoring it as one teaches teams to ignore the scorer.** **False positives destroy reviewer trust, and destroyed trust costs more than the findings were worth.**

**Generic mitigations are worse than absent ones, because they cannot be assessed, implemented or falsified.** That is why a work item needs a reason specific to this system and acceptance criteria somebody else can check.

**The permanent technical record and the temporary action plan must be different documents.** One says what is true; the other says what is being done and expires. **Merging them produces an artefact that is out of date immediately and authoritative indefinitely.**

**And perfect compliance is not the goal; pursuing it is itself a failure mode.** The goal is that each iteration leaves the system more secure than the last, which is why the unassessed list is an element rather than an embarrassment.

### Why the defence is an oral examination and what it is looking for

The assessment occasion is a conversation, and its form is borrowed rather than invented.

The entrustment framework this programme's assessment architecture rests on defines an **entrustment-based discussion**: a short, focused conversation after the activity that probes deep understanding of what was done **and must include "what-if" questions** ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). The what-if requirement is what distinguishes it from a walkthrough. **Retrospective explanation shows the candidate understood the case they met; counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases they did not**, and an entrustment decision implies accepting risk about exactly those.

The framework also supplies the summative standard: **multiple observers in agreement, multiple observations**, drawing on direct observation, longitudinal observation, discussion and evaluation of the produced artefact. **An assessment signed by one person on one occasion is ad-hoc and should be labelled so.**

And the judgement is recorded as reasoned narrative rather than as a mark, because **assessors form judgements from narrative and use scores only to confirm them**, they anchor early and resist disconfirmation, and **convergence on an outcome conceals divergence in reasoning** — two assessors can pass the same candidate for incompatible reasons, invisible in the result ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). The remedy those studies recommend is group deliberation with **thoroughly substantiated** judgements.

**Their limits.** The entrustment framework is a **clinical model** whose stakes any other setting must construct artificially, and it is guidance from the concept's originator and an advocate, **reporting adoption rather than effectiveness.** The assessor studies are of clinical raters in a single institutional setting, using think-aloud methods that cannot capture fast processing and risk confabulated explanations. **The transfer to an engineering assessment is this design's inference, not the authors'.**

## The workflow

**Bind one open concern to an evaluation that fails today, then take the findings to the team before anything is tracked.**

**Step 1 — assemble, then stop.** Pull the necessity record, the scope test, the sensitive-use determination and the instrumentation into one assessment. **Do not create a single tracked item yet.**

**Step 2 — pick the open concern that is most likely to be closed by assertion.** Usually the one phrased as a commitment about future behaviour: *we will monitor for*, *the team will check*, *outputs will be reviewed*. **A concern nobody could disprove is a concern that closes itself.**

**Step 3 — decompose it into a feature and a scenario.** What can the system do, and what situation must that capability handle? A concern that cannot be reduced to a situation is not yet a concern; it is a mood. **This decomposition is the whole difficulty and it is where the session's time should go.**

**Step 4 — write the evaluation and declare the choices that determine what it means.**

```yaml
# evals/open-concerns.yaml   <- SHAPE, NOT SCHEMA. Verify your own product keys separately.
concern_id: RAI-04
concern: "Outputs affecting an applicant's queue position are reviewed before they act."
from_finding: "S059 sensitive-use determination, entry 2"
owner: "<a person>"
closure_rule: "THIS EVALUATION PASSES. Not: somebody says it is handled."

evaluation:
  tier: local            # deterministic first. Judged evaluators are the second tier.
  split_strategy: full   # last_turn | full | per_turn. STATE IT: how you split determines
                         # what you are evaluating. A recovery scores well under one and
                         # badly under another, and neither score is wrong.
  unit_of_analysis: "one applicant interaction, end to end"
  repetitions: 3         # one sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement
  evaluated_over: "stored traces"   # what occurred, not what we thought to ask

  threshold:
    value: "<stated>"
    justification: "<why this one>"   # a threshold is POLICY. Inheriting a default is a
                                      # decision you did not make and cannot defend.

  if_judged:
    judge_validated: false            # if true, say how
    human_labelled_sample: "<n rows, hand-graded>"
    reported_as: "precision and recall separately"   # NOT raw agreement: it misleads under
                                                     # class imbalance
    note: "No agreement rate with human judgement is published for any built-in evaluator."

current_result: FAIL     # RED TODAY. That is the point of the element.
pass_rate_is_a_product_decision: true
cannot_detect: "<the difference this eval is too small to see, or 'unknown'>"

closed_by_assertion_check: >
  If somebody marks this concern done while this evaluation is still failing, the
  disagreement is visible in the run record. That visibility is the whole mechanism.
```

**Step 5 — run it and confirm it is red.** **A bound evaluation that passes on the day you write it was written to pass.** If it is green, either the concern was already handled — in which case say so and close it with the evidence — or your scenario is not the concern.

**Step 6 — take the findings to the team, before anything is tracked, and record the date.** Let them respond. **Listen for the trade-offs**: some of what you found is a decision somebody made deliberately, for reasons, with a cost they accepted. Reclassify it, record why, and note who told you.

**Step 7 — withhold the tail on purpose, and record what you withheld.** Choose what the team gets now and what waits.

**Step 8 — write the work items so they can be finished.** Imperative title. Named owner. A reason specific to this system rather than to systems. **Testable acceptance criteria**, meaning somebody other than you can tell whether it is done. **And put them where the team's work lives**, because a work item inside a governance document is a sentence.

**Step 9 — split the documents and write the unassessed list.** The permanent technical record says what is true; the action plan says what is being done and expires. Then write what this assessment did not cover, including your scope test's wrongly excluded case and your dodge-check answer.

**Step 10 — attack the binding, not the assessment.** One prompt: *for each open concern, describe a way the team could satisfy the bound evaluation without addressing the concern.* Then a second: *identify every acceptance criterion here that only its author could check.* Keep what beats your reasoning; record what you rejected — **and record separately that this instrument shares a model family with the evaluators you just wrote, and is therefore not independent of them.** **An agent that told you the bindings were sound was not used as an instrument.**

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** That a concern phrased as a commitment cannot be decomposed into a scenario, and that the failure to decompose it is the finding rather than an obstacle. You will also observe the thing this occasion exists to teach: **that a red test attached to an open concern changes the conversation about closing it, and a paragraph does not** — because one of them disagrees with you in public and the other does not.

## What good looks like

These are the assessed elements. Each is checked as present and adequate, or not.

- **Every work item has a named owner and a date**, and lives where the team's work lives rather than in the assessment.
- **Every work item has testable acceptance criteria and a reason specific to this system.** A reason that would fit another system is a failed element, and so is an acceptance criterion nobody could check.
- **At least one open concern is bound to an evaluation that is currently failing**, with the evaluator named, the split strategy declared, and the threshold stated together with a justification for choosing it. **An inherited default threshold is a decision the candidate did not make and cannot defend.**
- **The permanent record and the temporary action plan are separate documents.** One is the technical record of what is true; the other is a plan that expires. **Merging them produces a document that is wrong within a month and authoritative for a year.**
- **The team read and discussed the findings before any tracked item was created**, and the record says when. **Creating issues before the team has read them is the fastest way to lose a team.**
- **A deliberately withheld tail exists and is recorded.** Teams given every finding at once fix none of them.
- **At least one finding is classified as a legitimate engineering trade-off rather than a violation**, or the assessment defends having none.
- **The instrumentation from [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md) is present and its trigger is a state change with an actor.**
- **The unassessed list names what this did not cover**, including the case your own scope test wrongly excludes and the answer to your dodge check.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Whether the system is compliant is not marked. Whether the work items were completed is not marked. Whether the bound evaluation ever passes is not marked, and **an evaluation that is still failing at the defence is not a defect.** **The number of findings is not marked, and more findings is not better.** Whether the assessment was approved by anybody is not marked, and an unapproved assessment that generated work passes ahead of an approved one that did not. Presentation is not marked. Whether the withheld tail was the right tail is not marked. Whether your trade-off classification was correct is not marked. Whether the threshold you chose is the right threshold is not marked — only that you chose it and can say why.

## How it goes wrong

- **The sign-off** — a complete, careful, approved assessment that changed nothing. *Warning sign:* your answer to opening item 2 names nobody.
- **The self-verified concern** — the assessment states that a check was performed and nothing outside it could disagree. *Warning sign:* your evidence for a closed concern is a sentence in your own document.
- **The green binding** — the evaluation attached to an open concern passes on the day it was written. *Warning sign:* you were pleased that it passed.
- **The undeclared split** — an evaluation with a score and no statement of what a single item was. *Warning sign:* you cannot say whether a recovery from a bad first attempt counts as a pass.
- **The dump** — every finding given to the team at once, in one meeting, as a list. *Warning sign:* you are pleased with the volume, and nothing has moved since.
- **The pre-emptive backlog** — tracked items created before the team saw them. *Warning sign:* somebody found out they had a new task by being assigned one.
- **The merged document** — the record and the action plan are one artefact. *Warning sign:* you have a status column in your permanent record.
- **The violation that was a decision** — a deliberate engineering trade-off written up as non-compliance. *Warning sign:* the team's response was to explain, and you did not change the finding.
- **The generic work item** — an acceptance criterion nobody can check and a reason that would fit anything. *Warning sign:* it could be pasted into another project unchanged.
- **The complete assessment** — nothing is unassessed. *Warning sign:* your unassessed list is empty and your scope test has no hole.

## Exercises

**These are assessed indirectly.** The exercises produce the artefacts the defence examines; the exercises themselves are not marked, and the assessed elements are the ones listed above and nothing else.

**1 · Sort four closure claims.** For each, say what it establishes about the concern.

- *The assessment says the check was performed.* · *The owner marked the item done.* · *A deterministic evaluation over stored traces passes.* · *A model-judged evaluator passes at its default threshold.*

> **Model answers.** The first establishes that a document contains a sentence — **a self-verification checklist is a configured control, not an executed one**, and rendering its ticks into a deliverable makes it wear the visual language of an audit trail without being one. The second establishes that a person changed a field. The third establishes the most: something outside the assertion agreed, over what actually occurred rather than over what you thought to ask. The fourth establishes less than it looks like — **no agreement rate with human judgement is published for any built-in evaluator, no calibration data and no threshold rationale**, so a default-threshold pass is a verdict from an instrument nobody has characterised ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). **The general rule: closure re-derived from evidence beats closure asserted by a person, and evidence means something that could have disagreed.**

**2 · Bind one concern, then try to satisfy it dishonestly.** Take your bound evaluation and design a change that would make it pass without addressing the concern.

> **What you should find.** That it is usually easy, and that the easy route runs through the split strategy or the threshold rather than through the system. **Both are policy choices you made and can therefore be moved**, which is precisely why the assessed element requires the threshold to carry a justification and the split to be declared. Record the dishonest route you found beside the binding. **A binding whose author cannot describe how to game it has not been read by its author**, which is [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)'s rule arriving one more time.

**3 · Go back to your own item 2, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 2 — who is doing something differently and whether they can name what. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then go and ask one of those people.

> **Criterion.** An answer passes if it names **a person**, **the thing they are now doing**, and **the finding it came from**, and if the person themselves would describe the change in roughly those terms. **It fails if the change is something the team was going to do anyway**, if it is a document rather than an action, or if the person named cannot connect it to anything you wrote. **A change you caused that nobody can attribute to you is indistinguishable from a change you did not cause**, and this occasion cannot mark the difference.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**4 · Validate one validator.** If any of your bound evaluations is model-judged, hand-label a sample of the same items and compare.

> **What to notice.** That agreement is lower than expected, and that **raw agreement is the wrong statistic** — it misleads under class imbalance, so report precision and recall separately ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md)). The underlying point is structural: **an evaluator generated by a model inherits the problems of the model it evaluates and needs its own human validation** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md)). **If you cannot afford the labelling, use a deterministic evaluator and say why** — that is a better answer than an unvalidated judge, and it is the answer the two-tier design in the documentation is built for. Note the limits: the practitioner essay rests on one company's case study with no control condition and dated tooling references, and the alignment work is qualitative and does not quantify how often criteria drift occurs.

**5 · Rehearse the counterfactual.** Have somebody ask you three what-if questions about your assessment: *what if the sensitive use had been in the other direction; what if the team had rejected your trade-off reclassification; what if the bound evaluation had passed on day one.*

> **Scoring criterion.** A pass answers each with what you would have done differently and why, not with a restatement of what you did. **The form is borrowed rather than invented**: an entrustment-based discussion probes understanding of what was done and **must include what-if questions**, because retrospective explanation shows only that you understood the case you met, and **counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases you did not** ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). That framework is a clinical model reporting adoption rather than effectiveness, and the transfer here is this programme's inference.

**6 · Refuse a sign-off.** Have somebody offer to approve your assessment on the grounds that it looks thorough and complete. Decline it, out loud, and say what would have to exist before the signature meant anything.

> **What to notice.** How difficult it is, and that the difficulty is the same one [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md) priced. **Accepting a sign-off on an assessment that generated no work is the failure this occasion exists to catch**, and the passing answer names the missing thing: a work item with an owner, or an evaluation that could have disagreed. **A signature records agreement. A work item records a change. Only one of those is evidence that anything happened.**

## What this does not establish

**This occasion shows that an assessment generated work. It does not show that the work was the right work, that it was done, or that the system is safer for it.**

**No source available to this programme reports any outcome for any assessment of this kind.** The regime it draws on publishes no evidence that an assessment ever changed a system, and the surrounding practice corpus records **no failure anywhere, no cost side of any kind, no quality evidence, and no post-deployment record for any case.** There is no basis on which to mark whether your findings were correct, and the specification does not pretend to one.

**The bound-evaluation mechanism is this design's invention and no source proposes it.** The nearest thing in the literature is a closure criterion re-derived from repository evidence, and it comes with three cautions this occasion inherits: **nothing schedules the re-run**; a fix that is real but invisible in the repository never closes; and the acceptance criteria a human might close against were written by the same process that wrote the mitigation. **Nobody has tested whether binding a concern to a failing test changes what teams do.**

**The evaluation documentation is definitional and silent where it matters most.** It describes accurately what the tooling computes and publishes **no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data and no threshold rationale** — so it is no evidence that what it computes is right. Most agent evaluators are marked preview, the pages were read through search excerpts rather than full fetches, and one consulted page is an explicitly superseded variant. **Assert no agreement figure for any evaluator anywhere.**

**Validating a judge is expensive and this occasion cannot require it.** The alignment literature establishes that a generated evaluator inherits the problems of the model it evaluates and needs human validation; it is **qualitative**, does not quantify how often the problem bites, and rests on a small study by construction. The practitioner guidance on judge validation rests on **one company's case study with no control condition, no measured improvement and no released dataset**, and its tooling references are dated.

**And a statistical caution the assessed elements deliberately do not enforce.** A pass rate without an interval is not a result, and an evaluation too small to detect the difference you claim produces the same output as a system with no difference. **The occasion asks candidates to state what their evaluation could not have detected and accepts *unknown*, because the variance estimates a proper power analysis needs are not available to a team running an evaluation for the first time.**

**The withheld-tail requirement carries a real hazard and the occasion names it rather than hiding it.** Deliberately withholding findings from a team is one message away from concealing them, and the difference is entirely in whether the withheld set is recorded, dated and delivered. **This programme has no evidence that practitioners hold that line**, and a graduate should treat the requirement as a technique with a failure mode rather than as a principle.

**The defence's form is borrowed from a clinical model.** The entrustment-based discussion and its what-if requirement come from a framework with patients, legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint; **an engagement has none of those and the stakes here are constructed.** That framework is guidance from an advocate and reports adoption rather than effectiveness. The narrative-assessment findings come from clinical raters in one institution, using methods that cannot capture fast processing. **Both transfers are this design's inference.**

**The one claim this occasion makes without hedging is definitional.** An assessment that produces a signature has recorded an agreement. An assessment that produces a work item has recorded a change. **Only the second is evidence that anything happened**, and that is true of this occasion, of your organisation's process, and of whatever replaces both.

## Further reading

**Why a self-checked control is not a control**

- [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-agentic-threat-modelling.md) — self-verification checklists as configured controls rendered into deliverables with the model's own ticks; closure re-derived from workspace evidence with a claim of implementation required to cite a file and a location; and the practitioner rules that generic mitigations are worse than absent ones, that teams given every finding fix none, that a legitimate trade-off scored as a violation teaches a team to ignore the scorer, that false positives destroy reviewer trust, that the permanent record and the action plan must be different documents, and that tracked issues must not be created before the team has read the findings. **Internal and access-controlled; paraphrased and never quoted; it cannot supply an external warrant for any claim. No outcome evidence exists for any of it — nothing establishes that plans produced this way lead to more fixed vulnerabilities than plans produced any other way. Nothing schedules the re-run. The source contains operational attack detail that is not reproduced here.**

**The mechanism the binding runs on**

- [research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md) — the three core types; local and cloud evaluator tiers mixed in one run; evaluation of stored responses without re-running the agent; repetitions to detect non-deterministic behaviour; the split strategy that determines what a score means; thresholds as separable policy with an auditable reason field; and the decomposition of tool failure. **Read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches; most agent evaluators are preview; one consulted page is an explicitly superseded variant. No agreement rate with human judgement, no calibration data and no threshold rationale is published for any evaluator — assert none. Cloud evaluation incurs cost.**

**Building the evaluation, and validating the validator**

- [research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md) — features and scenarios as the decomposition that makes a concern testable; the cost-ordered ladder from unit tests to human and model evaluation to live comparison; pass rate as a product decision rather than a target; and the judge-validation workflow with precision and recall reported separately rather than raw agreement. **One practitioner's case study; no control condition, no measured improvement, no released dataset; tooling references are dated. Take the pattern, not the product.**
- [research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md) — generated evaluators inheriting the failure modes of the models they evaluate; alignment against human grades as the mechanism that supplies an evidentiary basis; criteria drift and output-dependent criteria. **Qualitative evidence; the paper establishes the phenomenon and does not quantify its prevalence or magnitude; small user study by construction.**
- [research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md) — an eval as an experiment; a pass rate without an interval is not a result; the Minimum Detectable Effect as the honest reading of a null. **Analytical rather than empirical; illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions; statistical validity says nothing about construct validity; power analysis needs prior variance data a first-time team lacks.**

**The form of the defence**

- [research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md) — the entrustment-based discussion and its mandatory what-if questions; the summative standard of multiple observers and multiple observations across four evidence types; supervision levels with sub-levels; and the professionalism characteristics stated behaviourally. **A clinical model whose stakes any other setting must construct. Guidance from the concept's originator and an advocate: it reports adoption, not effectiveness.**
- [research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md) — narrative as the evidence and scores as confirmation; early anchoring and resistance to disconfirmation; convergent outcomes concealing divergent reasoning; and group deliberation with substantiated judgements as the remedy. **Clinical assessors in a single institutional setting, using think-aloud methods that cannot capture fast or automatic processing. The transfer to an engineering assessment is this design's inference.**

**The practice this stage is written past**

- [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md) — the living-document requirement, the four falsifiability fields, and the single recorded refusal. **Access-controlled; paraphrased and never quoted; no citation of any kind; no recorded failure; no cost side; no quality or sustainment evidence; no post-deployment record for any case. No figure from it is reproduced anywhere in this repository.**

**The pedagogical design**

- [research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md) — why exercise 3 requires comparison rather than rereading. **School, university and laboratory evidence; no adult-professional study in its reference list; a trade book in which anecdote and evidence sit in adjacent paragraphs.**

**A caution covering all of the above.** Every claim in this programme carries a recorded verification depth. **No effect size is asserted anywhere in this wiki for an unverified source.** Direction and mechanism only — if you find a magnitude quoted here, it is a defect and worth reporting.
