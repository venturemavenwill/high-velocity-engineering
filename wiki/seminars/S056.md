# S056 · Can This Be Solved Without AI At All

**Module:** [M12](/wiki/modules/M12.md) · **Quarter:** [Q6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-056](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-056.md)
**Retrieves from:** S003, S008, S033
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

---

## The situation

The governance regime this module is built on opens with a question, and it is the right one.

**Can this be solved without AI at all?**

You are five stages past the point where that was easy to ask. There is a running increment. There is a threat model. People have been paid. **The question is now expensive, and in most engagements it is never asked at any price**, because everyone arrived selling the answer.

Answer these before instruction.

1. State your problem in one sentence containing no technology.
2. Design the non-AI solution. Not the current process — the one you would build from scratch, given everything except a model.
3. What does each option cost, including the things nobody counts?
4. What evidence would have made you choose the alternative?
5. Who benefits from the AI answer, and were they in the room when the problem was described?

**Item 4 is the session.** A rejection that no evidence could have reversed is not a rejection, and this programme has already said that once, in week one, about engagements. **Item 2 is where most people fail**, because they offer the status quo and call it an alternative.

## What you must produce

**A designed non-AI alternative**, at the same fidelity as the thing you are building, and a **rejection recorded where a later reader will find it.**

- **The problem, stated without a technology in it.**
- **The alternative, designed from scratch** — what it is made of, who operates it, what it cannot do.
- **A comparison on cost**, carrying the categories the practice material behind this programme never carries: staffing, compute, the cost of the discovery activity itself, opportunity cost, and the cost of being wrong.
- **The disconfirming evidence** — what you would have had to see to choose the alternative.
- **The rejection**, dated, in a place somebody will read it in a year.

## What good looks like

- **The problem statement survives having every product name removed from it.** If it does not, you have written a solution.
- **The alternative is a design, not a description of the present.** It has components, an operator and a failure mode.
- **Both options are priced in the same units**, and the AI option's price includes what it consumes rather than only what it saves.
- **The disconfirming evidence is something that could actually have appeared** during this engagement, at a moment when the decision was still open.
- **The rejection is findable.** Filed where a decision record lives, not in a slide.
- **Somebody's interest is named.** If the AI answer benefits your organisation commercially, the record says so.

## How it goes wrong

- **The straw alternative** — a non-AI option designed to lose. *Warning sign:* you enjoyed writing it.
- **The status quo as alternative** — you offered *carry on as now* and treated it as a design. *Warning sign:* your alternative has no components.
- **The unpriced comparison** — the alternative is costed and the AI option is described. *Warning sign:* one column has numbers and the other has adjectives.
- **The retrofitted question** — asked after the model was chosen, to be answered rather than to decide anything. *Warning sign:* nothing you could have written would have changed the plan.
- **The capability answer** — you justified the choice by what the technology can do rather than by what the problem requires. *Warning sign:* your justification would fit a different problem unchanged.
- **The unowned interest** — nobody in the record has anything to gain. *Warning sign:* your organisation is selling the thing you chose and the record does not mention it.

## Do it

**First, strip the technology out of the problem.** Write it as something a person experiences, then delete every product, model and capability from the sentence. **If nothing survives, you have been describing a solution since Stage 1** and this session is the last cheap moment to find that out.

**Second, design the alternative properly, from scratch.** The practice material behind this programme has a rule worth borrowing here and inverting: **do not automate the existing workflow — ask how you would do this from scratch given the tooling.** The same discipline applies to the alternative. A non-AI option that is just the current process with better forms is not a design, and comparing against it is comparing against a straw figure you built yourself.

**Third, price both, and carry the categories nobody carries.** The corpus this programme rests on contains **no cost side anywhere** — every claim in it is a gross benefit, and staffing, compute, the cost of the discovery activity itself and opportunity cost are all absent. **Yours will contain them.** Add one more: the cost of being wrong, which is different for the two options and is usually where the argument actually lives.

**Fourth, state what would have changed your mind, and check that it could have appeared.** Not *if the model had not worked* — that is available only after the money is spent. Something you could have observed during discovery or proof.

**Fifth, file the rejection where a decision record lives.** A rejection nobody can find is a rejection nobody can revisit, and this decision will be revisited: the alternative you rejected is the first thing anyone reaches for when the system disappoints.

Then use an agent to argue for the alternative as hard as it can, and read what it says with the assumption that it is right. **An agent that agreed with your choice was not used as an instrument**, which is [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s claim arriving in a stage that needs it.

## Show it

Give both designs to somebody who does not know which one you prefer, and remove any signal of which is yours.

**Ask them which they would fund.** Then ask the harder question: **what would change their mind?** If they cannot answer, your comparison has not given them anything to reason with, and the document is advocacy in the shape of an analysis.

## What this does not establish

**Nothing here shows that asking the question improves any outcome**, and no source available to this programme measures it.

**The regime that asks it publishes no evidence that any assessment ever changed a system.** The practice corpus around it is worse placed still: it arrives at every engagement selling an AI-accelerated delivery model, its readiness criteria are composed almost entirely of customer dispositions, and its prepared answer to the objection *your tooling improved and our delivery did not* reattributes the shortfall to the customer's own organisational constraints. **A model whose failure explanation is always the customer's readiness has arranged its evidence so that a disappointing result confirms rather than challenges the thesis**, and it will rarely conclude that the tooling was the wrong instrument.

**That is the environment this session is asked to work in, and the session does not fix it.** It requires one designed alternative and one findable rejection. Whether either changes a decision is untested, and the honest expectation is that most will not.

## Perishable content in this session

| Durable claim | Perishable instance |
|---|---|
| A rejection no evidence could reverse is not a rejection | The specific capabilities that make the AI option attractive this term |
| The status quo is not an alternative design | Current expectations of what a non-AI system costs to build |
| A justification that fits a different problem unchanged is not a justification | Today's model capabilities, which move faster than this page |
| The cost of being wrong differs between options | Present compute and staffing prices |
