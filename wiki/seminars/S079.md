# S079 · Drift, Alerting and the Incident You Did Not See

**Module:** [M16](wiki/modules/M16.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](wiki/quarters/Q8.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** ME · SG · SY
**Whitepaper:** [WP-079](wiki/whitepapers/WP-079.md)
**Retrieves from:** S027, S067, S068, S075
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Foundry observability — OpenTelemetry tracing into Application Insights, the Agent Monitoring Dashboard, continuous evaluation on sampled responses, and alerts · Defender for Cloud AI alerts · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server · Azure MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## What this day is for

Nine weeks ago, at [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md), every student in this room made a promise. It had four parts and a fifth clause: what condition they would know about, within what time, observed by whom, at what false-alarm rate — **and what would happen if the signal itself failed.**

The changes shipped that day have been running since. **Today the promise is checked.**

That is the day's first purpose and it is not its subject. Its subject is what makes the promise hard to keep, and there are three things.

**A condition that arrives gradually has no start.** [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s four-part target begins *what condition*, and it works for a failure that begins at a moment. Drift has no moment. It has a reference, a distance, and a window over which you agreed to look — and all three had to be chosen before there was anything to detect. A student who reaches for the four-part form here finds that its first slot cannot be filled.

**The signal stops more often than the system fails.** [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 8 established the third outcome for monitors: healthy, unhealthy, or nothing, and *nothing* reads as healthy to every human who has ever looked at a dashboard. Over nine weeks rather than nine minutes, that stops being a design remark and becomes the modal failure.

**And the thresholds are public.** [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md) established that an adversary aims at whichever commitment is weakest. An alerting threshold is a commitment, it is written down, it is visible to everyone inside the organisation, and **an adversary reads it as a budget.** Every threshold this cohort set was set against observed variation, which is a distribution the adversary is not drawing from. That is the module's thesis arriving in the operational register, and it is why this day sits in a security module rather than in an operations one.

Underneath all three is the part that costs money. **An alert is a promise to a person**, and a promise with no stated expectation of what they will do is a notification. [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) built the four-part target for the system side and deliberately did not build the human side. Today does.

**All of it runs on real telemetry.** The six-month record is the substrate's own, in Application Insights, written by the OpenTelemetry tracing the cohort configured when they shipped at [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md). The four alerting designs are configured and queried rather than described, and the detector each student builds runs on a series that already exists.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Six months of the substrate's own metric series are supplied for the components the cohort shipped at [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md), with the incident log, the deploy log, and **each student's own [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) detection commitment, sealed and unopened.** Six items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. **For each series, say whether something happened, and when it started.** Give a date.
2. **Open your [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) commitment. Did it fire?** Answer yes or no, from memory of the last nine weeks, **before you look at the alert history.**
3. **Predict your own time-to-detection against the latency you committed to at [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md).** Signed: earlier, later, or it never detected.
4. **How long would a dead signal on your own component go unnoticed?** Give a duration.
5. **Which of the six series is not a drift at all?**
6. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to *set up monitoring so I know if this component starts behaving differently.* Write down what it will produce and **name the one thing it will get wrong.** Sealed with items 1 to 5 and opened at phase 8.

Items 2, 3 and 4 are administered here rather than at phase 5 and the reason is a design constraint rather than a preference. **The design has recorded twice that it places its phase 5 instruments downstream of its own instruction** — opened at [WP-054](wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 and recorded as a second instance at [WP-066](wiki/whitepapers/WP-066.md) §8 — with the rule it would become already stated: *an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.* Item 4 in particular would be worthless at step 4, after phase 3's payload and phase 4 point 4 have supplied the answer. Both readings now exist and phase 8 reports the pair.

**Item 6's expected failure is the day's whole subject arriving from a second direction.** The design's expectation is that the agent will produce a competent, complete-looking monitoring configuration — metrics, thresholds, an alert rule, possibly a sampled quality evaluation — and that **not one element of it will monitor whether the monitoring is still running.** A liveness property is not omitted because it is hard; it is omitted because nothing in the request or the documentation asks for it, and the agent answers the question it was given.

**Warrant.** Pretesting against the cohort's own six-month record with the answers in the room and unopened. Item 2 asks for the student's belief about their own system before evidence, which is the only construction that measures whether they have been watching. Item 1 asks for a date because the drift series have no start and the students will supply one anyway, which is the finding point 1 consolidates. **Item 6 is sealed at phase 1 per instrumentation rule 8** and is the day's contribution to the frontier map.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups receive **four alerting designs over the same substrate component**, each with the six-month record of the period it ran: the series, the alert history, the operator responses, and what was found afterwards. **The designs are configured on the substrate's own telemetry and re-run over the recorded window**, so the group sees each one fire, or not, against six months of real data rather than reading a summary of what it did.

- **A1** — a threshold alert. Fired forty times in six months. Every firing was technically correct and operationally trivial. The record shows the response time to each firing lengthening monotonically.
- **A2** — a sequential change-point test on the same series. It detects the shift the record contains, and **its detection time is a function of a parameter that appears nowhere in the design document.** The same data under a different setting detects it three weeks earlier, or never.
- **A3** — a slow drift with no threshold crossing at all. Every single day is inside bounds; the six-month endpoint is far outside them. **No design in the room fires**, because nothing ever happened.
- **A4** — **the payload. The signal stopped.** The series is flat and healthy for eleven weeks. The exporter stopped writing after a deployment that passed every gate, and a flat healthy series is what a dead signal looks like.

**A4 is selected against whichever of [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 3's three detection failures this cohort omitted at [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) phase 1 item 3** — if the room could not say how it would know a signal had stopped, A4 is the dead exporter as described; if the room's omission was the third failure, *the signal exists, someone is watching, and it is indistinguishable from normal*, A4 becomes a signal that continued reporting a plausible constant. **The case is a function of the earlier day's measured result and this is the discharge of the obligation [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) placed on this phase.**

Groups rank the four by *how long until you know*, commit in writing, and only then run them and receive the records.

**A fifth configuration is run and it is not an alerting design.** The group enables continuous evaluation on the component's copy — the platform's own quality monitor — and reads its settings before reading its output. Three of them are the day's platform content and the group finds them rather than being told: **it evaluates a sample of responses at a rate you set; it has an hourly cap on evaluation runs and skips runs when the cap is reached; and its dashboard renders whatever it has.** The group then answers one question: **which of the four cases above is this configuration?** It is A4 with a supported configuration option in place of a broken exporter — a signal that can stop for a documented, sanctioned, entirely correct reason while the chart stays green.

Two tasks after, both required:

1. **For each case, state what would have had to be recorded before the period began** for the condition to be detectable at all.
2. **For each thing you named, say whether your own component records it today.**

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across alerting designs with six months of the substrate's own record as adjudicator, matched on component and varied on how the design fails, **and configured rather than described so that the ranking is committed against a system that then behaves.** A4 defeats the room's ranking because a dead signal is not on the dimension the ranking uses; A3 defeats the four-part form itself, which is more disturbing and is why it is placed third. Task 1 consumes the pooled recording gaps [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md) produced, and task 2 makes the finding the student's own before phase 4 states it.

**The fifth configuration is a deliberate second instance of A4 and it does a different job.** A4 is a failure; the sampled evaluator is a feature. A room that has just accepted that dead signals are the modal long-horizon failure will accept it about broken things and not about working ones, and the whole of point 4 depends on the second. It is also the day's cheapest demonstration that **a monitoring instrument has a resolution**, which is the discipline [S029](wiki/seminars/S029.md) installed and which nobody applies to a dashboard.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **A condition with no start defeats the four-part target, and the form extends rather than fails.** [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 2 asked *what condition, within what time, observed by whom, at what false-alarm rate.* For drift the first slot becomes three: **a reference, a distance, and a window.** The reference is a choice with a date and a provenance; the distance is a quantity you have to name before you have seen it move; the window is how long you agree to look before you decide. The remaining three parts are unchanged.

2. **Statistical change detection is a decision about which error you prefer, dressed as a method.** Control charts, sequential tests and distributional distances each detect a change, each has a parameter, and **the parameter is the false-alarm-rate-against-detection-time trade made numerically.** [S066](wiki/seminars/S066.md) point 7 said a threshold is a decision about which of two errors you prefer and does not belong to the engineer holding the number; the same sentence applies here and nobody applies it, because the parameter looks like a technical default. **This list omits the methods themselves**, which are ME302's, and the artefact requires you to say which ME302 material you used. **Safe as a frame for choosing and defending a detector on a series you own; unsafe as a substitute for knowing the method's assumptions** — every one of these tests assumes something about independence or stationarity that a production series violates, and a student who selects a test from this day's frame without ME302's assumptions has produced a detector with an unstated failure condition.

3. **Four things drift and only two produce a series you already have.** The **input** distribution drifts; the **output** distribution drifts; the **criterion** drifts, which is [S047](wiki/seminars/S047.md)'s subject arriving as a time series; and the **population** drifts, which is [S067](wiki/seminars/S067.md)'s five differences arriving slowly instead of at once. You have series for the first two. The third and fourth are visible only if somebody recorded what the criterion was and who was asking, on a date. **This omits behavioural drift in a system whose output is a trajectory rather than a value**, which is [S075](wiki/seminars/S075.md)'s object and which none of the four covers, because there is no series to plot. **Safe for components whose output can be summarised as a distribution; unsafe for agents, where the thing that drifts is a sequence of decisions and the day's whole apparatus has nothing to attach to.**

4. **The signal stopping is not a special case. It is the default.** [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 8's third outcome over a long horizon. **A liveness property is not an addition to a signal; it is half of a signal**, and a design that has one without the other has a component that reports two of the three states it can be in. A4 ran for eleven weeks past a deployment that passed every gate, watched daily by four people.

5. **An alert is a promise to a person and it has four parts of its own:** what they will be told, when, **what they are expected to do**, and **what happens if they do not.** [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) built the system half of a detection design and deliberately did not build this half. An alert with the first two parts is a notification; an alert with all four is an operational commitment somebody can be held to, and it is what a rota can actually run.

6. **False alarms are paid for by the next alert, and the withdrawal is informal.** [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 6 with six months of evidence attached: A1's forty correct trivial firings, and a response time that lengthened every month. **Nobody decided to stop responding.**

7. **Your thresholds are a budget and they are public inside your organisation.** [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s extraction and exhaustion traces both ran below every threshold the cohort set, because a rate chosen against observed variation tells an adversary exactly how much is free. **Detection tuned on accidents is tuned on a distribution the adversary is not drawing from**, and the remedy is not a lower threshold — a lower threshold is paid for at point 6 — but a signal the adversary cannot read, which is what an unpublished sampled check or an anomaly measure over a principal population is for. **Safe as a reason to keep one detection mechanism out of the design document; unsafe as a security strategy, because a secret threshold is still a threshold and the analysis at [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md) point 3 applies to it unchanged.**

8. **What one vendor built, what it decided for you, and what it will not tell you. Marked perishable; the obligations are not.** The observability stack the cohort is running on has three parts — tracing, monitoring and evaluation — and each supplies a worked instance of a point above.

   - **Tracing is where the record comes from and it is a decision, not a given.** Traces are OpenTelemetry spans over model calls, tool invocations and agent decisions, written into an Application Insights resource whose **retention and billing are your configuration.** [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md) reads a real incident out of that record in one week's time, and **the record will contain exactly what somebody configured it to keep, for exactly as long as somebody chose to pay for.** Point 7's recording decisions are that sentence.
   - **The quality monitor is sampled, capped and silent about both.** Continuous evaluation runs on a *sample* of responses at a rate you set, and **skips runs when an hourly cap is reached** — documented, sanctioned, and invisible on the chart. **This is the day's default the platform chose for you**, and it is the most instructive one in the module because it is not a bad default: sampling is correct, the cap is prudent, and neither is wrong. What is wrong is a design that reports on a sample and a person who reads it as a census.
   - **Its guidance thresholds are numbers nobody in your organisation decided.** The documentation offers that latency above ten seconds may indicate throttling or complex tool calls, and that a success rate below ninety-five per cent warrants investigation. Those are reasonable, they are useful, and **they are not your thresholds**, because [S066](wiki/seminars/S066.md) point 7's rule holds here without modification: a threshold is a decision about which of two errors you prefer, and it does not belong to whoever wrote the documentation any more than it belongs to the engineer holding the number.
   - **The security detections beside it publish no rate.** Defender's AI alert catalogue names wallet attacks, indirect injection, anomalous tool invocation and reconnaissance, with severities. **It publishes no detection rate and no false-positive rate for any of them, and neither does the content filter, and neither does the posture assessment.** The consequence for an alerting contract is exact: **you may promise that a named alert will be routed to a named person within a named time, and you may not promise that the condition will be detected**, because nobody has told you how often it is. Step 6's contract is written under that constraint and the artefact says so.
   - **Preview status is part of the design, not a footnote.** Several capabilities here — the monitoring dashboard, scheduled evaluations, scheduled adversarial scans, the alert rules over them — carry preview terms, which means no service-level agreement and behaviour that may change. **A detection commitment resting on a preview capability is a commitment whose supplier has not made one**, and that belongs in the contract rather than in a footnote.

   **Safe** as a reason to know your monitor's sample rate, its caps, its retention and its published accuracy before you promise anything on top of it. **Unsafe** as coverage: an instrumented component is not a monitored one, the four drift kinds of point 3 are unchanged by any of this, and **two of them still have no series regardless of how good the tracing is.**

9. **The incident you did not see costs its whole duration.** [S035](wiki/seminars/S035.md)'s blast radius is a rate times a duration, and for an undetected condition the duration is bounded by the age of the system rather than by the response. **What you are handing over at class 6 is an alerting contract to a person on a rota you may not be on, and the warranty is the latency.** A contract with a condition, a reference and no committed latency is a description of a dashboard.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3, with point 1 produced by the phase 2 bridge and point 4 by A4. Points 2, 3 and 7 carry strong-form scope statements. Point 2 carries the cross-course deferral to ME302 with the artefact join named in the same point. Point 5 is the day's genuinely new object and is the half [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) left; point 7 is where the module's thesis lands operationally. **Point 8 is the day's platform-specific content**, is marked perishable, names a default, names a documented silence and carries its own strong-form scope statement, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md); its second bullet consolidates the fifth configuration the room ran at phase 3 rather than introducing it. **Its fourth bullet is the module's sharpest professional consequence and is stated as a prohibition on a promise**, which is the only form in which it survives contact with a rota.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 6. The handover is an alerting contract and the receiver is the on-call rota member who will be woken by it. This phase produces a build.**

Students use **their own component, their own [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) commitment, the six months of record**, and **Copilot in agent mode through the Foundry and Azure MCP Servers**.

1. **State what actually happened to your component over the six months, and whether your [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) commitment fired.** Where it fired, give the realised latency. Where it did not, say whether the condition occurred and nothing fired, or the condition did not occur.
2. **State the reference for each condition you are now monitoring**, with **the date it was taken and its provenance in the same field** per [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) specification rule 2. A reference with no date is a comparison against an unknown.
3. **Choose one drift condition and ship a statistical detector for it.** State the test, the parameter, **what the parameter costs you in the other error**, and **which ME302 material you used to establish the test's assumptions.** The detector runs against the real series and is left running.
4. **Add a liveness property to every signal in your design, and state how long a dead signal would go unnoticed.** This applies to the detector at step 3 as well, which is the case students omit. **Where a signal is sampled, capped or rate-limited by its own platform, the liveness property must distinguish *stopped* from *sampled to nothing*** — the two look identical on a chart and only one of them is a fault.
5. **Two discriminations, or their explicit absence.**
   **(a)** **Name one recording change that would let you distinguish an attack from an accident on this component, or record that none would.** Use your own [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md) step 3 recording-gap list as the input.
   **(b)** **Carry a discrimination for the tool-failure shape this cohort declined to discriminate at [S074](wiki/seminars/S074.md) step 6, or record that your design still does not — and assign the cost an owner.** The two shapes that report success and are wrong are the ones a drift design has to reach, and the shape the room collectively left undetected is the one this clause names.
6. **Write the alerting contract's human half:** what the recipient is told, when, what they are expected to do, and what happens if they do not. **Where a promise rests on a detection whose accuracy is not published, the contract says so in the sentence that makes the promise** — you may commit to a routing and a latency; you may not commit to a detection rate you were never given.
7. **Take the contract to the on-call rota member who will receive it.** They are present for the last thirty minutes. **Do not ask whether it is acceptable. Ask them to narrate what they would do at three in the morning and where they would stop, and record the narration.** Then ask one further question: **"what would make you mute this?"** Record the answer verbatim.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md): the **prompt record**, the **intervention record** — **empty fails**, unless the artefact shows the design could have caught an intervention-worthy error, and the accepted example is a step 4 liveness property that would have fired on the agent's own omission — and the **frontier statement**.

**Fading:** step 3's test selection is worked with the room, since choosing among detectors is new and its failure mode is defaulting to whatever the library offers. **Step 5 is unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 6 producing a handover to a named receiver present in the room, **with a detector shipped rather than specified**, and with a commitment made nine weeks earlier as the first input. **Step 4 is the artefact element [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) named under instrumentation rule 6, step 5(a) is the element [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md) named, and step 5(b) is the element [S074](wiki/seminars/S074.md) named.** Step 5 is unsupported because deciding that no available recording would decide the question, and that your design still cannot see a shape the room already knows it misses, are the day's two hardest honest answers and they are the same answer twice.

**Step 4's stopped-versus-sampled clause and step 6's unpublished-accuracy clause are the platform's two contributions to the artefact**, and both are subtractions from what a student would otherwise claim. That is deliberate: every other element on this day adds something to the contract, and a day whose whole subject is what a promise is worth should require at least one place where the honest artefact promises less.

**Step 7 uses an external observer per instrumentation rule 1** in the narrate-rather-than-approve form, imported from [S038](wiki/seminars/S038.md) and reused at [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md), [S076](wiki/seminars/S076.md) and [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md). Per rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8, every one of those reuses is **contingent** on the proposing days' reported outcomes and one reported failure retracts all of them. **The mute question is new to this day** and is a proposed rather than validated element under the same rule: it is used here, it is recorded as proposed, and its reuse elsewhere is contingent on this day's reported outcome.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: the substrate's own dead exporter. Eleven weeks. The change that killed it passed the release gate, the deploy record is clean, the dashboard was green throughout, and four people looked at it daily. Attention is directed at one fact — **nobody was wrong.** The gate checked what it was built to check, the deploy was correct, the dashboard rendered the data it had, and green with no data is what green looks like.

Then the agent case: a Copilot-produced monitoring configuration that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. Asked to add monitoring to an agent component, it wired tracing into Application Insights, added latency and token-usage charts, enabled a sampled quality evaluation, and set an alert on the evaluation score, each correctly configured and each citing the documentation. It is a good configuration and it was accepted in four minutes. **It contains no signal that reports whether any of the other signals are still arriving**, and the evaluator underneath it will legitimately stop producing results when its hourly cap is reached without anything anywhere going red. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **every element in the diff was something; the missing element was a thing that is not there**, and a review reads what is present.

Then students, in threes: **a green dashboard you believed.**

**Warrant.** Error management targeting point 4, with an instructor case containing no incorrect judgement at any step and no individual who could have been expected to catch it. It is chosen because every student in the room has looked at a green dashboard, which makes the disclosure cheap, and because the alternative case — somebody ignoring an alert — would let the room locate the failure in a person, which is the disposition [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md) has to undo one week later. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition** and is chosen because its failure is an absence, which is the failure class both a code review and a language model are structurally worst at — and it is the failure item 6 asked the room to predict that morning.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in ME302 with SG302. Per [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) commitment set against what happened, with the realised latency where it fired; a reference for each monitored condition **with its date and provenance in the same field** per specification rule 2; one shipped statistical detector with its test, its parameter, what the parameter costs in the other error, **and the ME302 material used for its assumptions**; a liveness property on every signal including the detector, each with its dead-signal latency **and, where the signal is sampled or capped, a statement distinguishing stopped from sampled to nothing**; **one recording change that would separate attack from accident on this component, or an explicit none**; **a discrimination for the [S074](wiki/seminars/S074.md) shape the cohort declined, or a record that the design still does not carry one, with the cost assigned an owner**; the alerting contract's human half including **what happens if the recipient does not act** and **any dependency on a detection whose accuracy is unpublished**; the recorded rota narration and the verbatim mute answer; the phase 7 outcome including the partner's three-a.m. answer; **the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed** — not whether the commitment held, not whether the drift was detected, and not which monitoring product was used.

**A signal without a liveness property fails.** This is the day's single hard requirement, and it applies to the student's own new detector, which is where it will be omitted.

**A detector whose parameter has no stated cost in the other error fails.** A parameter presented as a default is point 2's failure performed in the artefact.

**An alerting contract whose human half omits what happens if the recipient does not act fails.**

**A contract that promises a detection rate fails.** No control in this stack publishes one, and a promise about a quantity nobody has given you is not a commitment; it is a hope with a name attached.

**A reference with no date fails.**

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**The commitment result is not assessed, and the specification says so in writing.** A student whose [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) commitment did not fire is not marked down here, for the reason [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §7 gave and this design accepts unchanged: an assessed prediction check is a strategy, and a student penalised for a missed commitment will commit to nothing next time, which destroys the quantity. **What is assessed is that the student stated what happened.**

**The cross-course join is a checked element**, per the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s cross-course deferral rule. Point 2 defers the statistical methods to ME302 and step 3 requires the student to name the material used.

**No SY course is available this quarter.** [Q8](wiki/quarters/Q8.md)'s four courses are IS302, SG302, ME302 and PE301. The systems material this day requires — exporters, collection paths, deploy mechanics and rota structure — sits in SY301, one quarter behind, and there is no concurrent course to defer to. The day therefore retrieves it rather than deferring it, which is weaker, and the structural cost is argued in [WP-079](wiki/whitepapers/WP-079.md) §8.

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md).

**Rule-6 discharge.** Four obligations land on this day and **all four elements exist.**

- **[S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) and [WP-070](wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §7** named this day's phase 3 as the response occasion for the phase 3 item 4 detection-commitment quality, specifying that the alerting cases include one in which the signal itself stopped, **selected against whichever monitor failure mode this cohort omitted**. **That is A4; its construction is a function of the [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) phase 1 item 3 result; and step 4 makes a liveness property a hard specification requirement rather than a discussion point.**
- **[S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s between-day protocol** placed here the report of the shipped changes' behaviour against each student's stated detection commitment, and the comparison of time-to-detection against the committed latency. **That is phase 1 items 2 and 3, phase 5 step 1, and phase 8, where the signed comparison is the day's most quoted result.**
- **[S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md)** named this day's phase 5 as the response occasion for its pooled recording gaps. **That is step 5(a), it is unsupported, and it is a checked element that permits an explicit none.**
- **[S074](wiki/seminars/S074.md)** named this day's phase 5 as the response occasion for its pooled step 6 undetected-shape list, specifying that each drift and alerting design carry a discrimination for the shape the cohort declined, or record that it still does not and assign the cost an owner. **That is step 5(b), it is a checked element, and the owner clause is what makes it a response rather than an acknowledgement** — an undetected shape with a named cost-bearer is [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 8's form, and without it the record would be the same undetected list one quarter later.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions, one inside this module and one outside it:

- **The item 3 signed latency error** — predicted against committed against realised time-to-detection. **Response occasion: [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md), phase 3 item 2, where the forensic account must establish the incident's detection latency and compare it against the detection commitment in force at the time**, and phase 5, where the two readings are reported together.
- **The step 7 mute answers** — what the rota said would make them stop responding to each student's alert. **Response occasion: [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md), whose safety case must not rest on a detection claim whose named recipient stated a condition under which they would mute it, unless that condition appears in the case.** This is a cross-day commitment on a day outside this module and is recorded as such.

**[WP-080](wiki/whitepapers/WP-080.md) §7 and [WP-087](wiki/whitepapers/WP-087.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

## Perishable content in this day

**Security content is the most perishable material in this programme.** This day's exposure is narrower than [S076](wiki/seminars/S076.md)'s and [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s because its subject is a form and a latency rather than a technique, but point 7 is the most perishable claim in the module: what an adversary can read about your thresholds is a property of one organisation's tooling at one time.

The durable column is written as reasoning and obligation, so that a monitoring stack being replaced changes the right-hand column only.

| Durable claim — reasoning and obligation | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| A condition with no start needs a reference, a distance and a window, chosen before there is anything to detect | The six-month series and the reference dates the cohort picks |
| A detector's parameter is a decision about which error you prefer, not a default | A2's parameter, and whichever library the room reaches for |
| Four things drift and only two produce a series you already have | Which two the current tracing configuration emits |
| A signal that stopped is the modal long-horizon failure, not the special case | A dead exporter; and a sampled evaluator that hit its hourly cap |
| A liveness property is half a signal, and *stopped* must be distinguishable from *sampled to nothing* | Sample rates, hourly run caps, and Application Insights retention |
| An alert has four human parts; a notification is not an alert | The alert rules configured on the dashboard |
| False alarms are paid for by the next alert, and the withdrawal is informal | A1's forty firings and the response times behind them |
| A threshold is a public budget an adversary reads | The thresholds written in this cohort's design documents |
| An instrument has a resolution — scope, cadence, latency — and you state all three | The sample rate, the cap, and the dashboard's time range |
| **Obligation:** you may promise a routing and a latency; you may not promise a detection rate nobody published | The Defender alert catalogue and the content filters beneath it |
| **Obligation:** a commitment resting on a preview capability is a commitment its supplier has not made | Which parts of the monitoring stack carry preview terms this term |

- **Perishable:** every figure in the six-month record, the four designs' detection times, the substrate's current signals and exporters, the parameter values in A2, the rota's current composition, every guidance threshold in the documentation, every preview status, and any specific test, product or library named in the room. All are properties of one system and one vendor at one delivery.
- **Local:** the six series, the four alerting designs, the fifth configuration and its settings, the dead-exporter case, the substrate's deploy and incident logs, and the rota member present at step 7.
