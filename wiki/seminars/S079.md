## The situation

Everything so far is intention. A manifest is intention. A capability statement is intention. A teaching plan is a very well-organised intention.

Today the recipient operates the system, and you watch, and you do not help.

Answer these in writing before the session opens.

1. Who will actually hold this at three in the morning? Name them. Is it the person you have been teaching?
2. What are you going to watch? Name the acts, from your capability statement.
3. What counts as an intervention? Does answering a direct question count?
4. Write the condition under which you will intervene. One condition, stated now, in observable terms.
5. What will you do when they are about to do something you know is wrong, and the condition has not fired?

**Item 5 has one correct answer and it is uncomfortable: you record it and you say nothing.** Every other answer converts the rehearsal into a demonstration.

## What you must produce

A **rehearsal record.** A supervised period in which the recipient operates the system against real work, followed by a period in which you are reachable and are not used.

The record states: what was attempted; what was observed, act by act; **what the recipient could not do**; every intervention that occurred and whether it was inside the written condition; and what the observed incapacity obliges — a changed disposal, a changed owner, a changed date, or a decision not to leave yet.

## What good looks like

- The rehearsal is run by **the person who will hold the responsibility**, not the person most interested in it. Those are frequently different, and choosing the interested one is the commonest way to produce a reassuring rehearsal.
- The work is real and consequential. A rehearsal on the quiet day, on a synthetic case, with the failure paths avoided, measures nothing.
- **It produces at least one observed incapacity.** A rehearsal in which everything went well was run at the wrong difficulty, and the record should say which difficulty was avoided.
- Interventions are counted, including the small ones — the clarifying sentence, the hand pointing at the screen, the reassuring noise.
- The unsupervised interval is genuine: you are reachable and you are not consulted, and the record says whether you were.
- What the incapacity obliges is stated as a change to something, not as a note.

## How it goes wrong

Five failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **The rescued rehearsal** — you help, because watching somebody struggle with something you could fix in a moment is genuinely hard, and afterwards everybody agrees it went well. *Warning sign:* the record has no stops in it.
- **The wrong operator** — the rehearsal is run by the enthusiast, and the person who will carry the pager was not free that week. *Warning sign:* the operator volunteered.
- **The easy day** — real work, quiet conditions, no incident, nothing contended. *Warning sign:* nothing in the rehearsal was time-critical.
- **Willingness recorded as ability** — the recipient tried hard, was positive throughout, and could not complete the act. The record captures the attitude. *Warning sign:* your observation notes contain adjectives about the person.
- **The finding with no consequence** — an incapacity is observed, recorded, and changes nothing, because the date is fixed and the plan is already written. *Warning sign:* your rehearsal happens after your handover is signed.

## Do it

Work from the supplied situation in [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md).

Design the rehearsal around the acts from your capability statement — running the harness and interpreting a result, working a runbook to termination, reviewing the rejection log and deciding whether something has degraded, and taking one change through the pipeline.

**Write the intervention condition before you begin, and make it about consequence rather than about discomfort.** Something like: you intervene if an act is about to affect a compliance record that the assessor may sample. Not: you intervene if it is going badly.

Then run it, in silence, and record. Then run the unsupervised interval, and record whether you were contacted and for what.

## Show it

Report the incapacity you observed and what it obliged.

The room is listening for the connection. An observed incapacity that produced a note is a rehearsal you ran for the sake of having run one. An observed incapacity that moved an owner, removed a dependency, changed a date or stopped a departure is a rehearsal that did something.

**If you intervened outside your written condition, that is your report.** It is the most useful finding available in this session and it is about you.

## What this does not establish

Almost nothing about the real case, and the limit is severe enough to state plainly.

**This is a simulation, over a short interval, with a recipient who cannot be genuinely harmed by a bad handover and who knows they are being observed.** Nobody is woken at three in the morning. Nobody carries the consequence into the following month. The unsupervised interval is short enough that its silence proves very little.

No source behind this programme reports whether any customer was ever observed operating a delivered system, or whether such an observation predicted anything. **The practice corpus contains no observation of any kind** — its discovery method is entirely self-report, with nobody asked to demonstrate a deployment or set up an environment while being watched. This session takes the opposite position from its source, and takes it on an argument rather than on evidence: that a claim about somebody's ability, made without watching them, is a claim about their willingness.
