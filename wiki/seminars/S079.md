## The situation

Everything so far is intention. A manifest is intention. A capability statement is intention. A teaching plan is a very well-organised intention. A disposal record is an intention with a table in it.

Today the recipient operates the system, on their own credentials, and you watch, and you do not help.

**Hands off the keyboard is not a nicety of this method. It is the method.** An author who touches the keyboard during the proof has not run a weaker version of the demonstration; they have run a different one, whose result is about the author. There is no partial credit available here and no version of this that survives a small helpful correction.

**Answer these in writing before reading further.** Item 5 has one correct answer and it is uncomfortable.

1. Who will actually hold this at three in the morning? Name them. Is it the person you have been teaching?
2. What are you going to watch? Name the acts, from your capability statement.
3. What counts as an intervention? Does answering a direct question count? Does logging in for them count?
4. Write the condition under which you will intervene. One condition, stated now, in observable terms.
5. What will you do when they are about to do something you know is wrong, and the condition has not fired?

**You record it and you say nothing.** Every other answer converts the rehearsal into a demonstration, and a demonstration measures you.

## What you must produce

**A rehearsal record.** A supervised period in which the recipient operates the system against real work on their own credentials, followed by a period in which you are reachable and are not used.

1. **What was attempted**, act by act, from the capability statement at [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md).
2. **What was observed**, in the recipient's actions rather than in adjectives about the recipient.
3. **What the recipient could not do.** This section is the artefact. A record with nothing in it was run at the wrong difficulty.
4. **Every intervention that occurred**, counted, with whether each was inside the written condition.
5. **What the observed incapacity obliges** — a changed disposal, a changed owner, a changed date, or a decision not to leave yet.
6. **The access findings**, separately: every permission, credential, quota or project the recipient discovered they did not have. **This list is usually longer than anybody expects and is not a distraction from the rehearsal; it is a result of it.**
7. **The unsupervised interval's record**: whether you were contacted, by whom, and for what.

## The material

### Why the keyboard rule is absolute

The method rests on a single mechanism, and the mechanism has one enemy.

Of the four sources from which a person forms a belief about their own capability, **only one is explicitly ranked**: "The most effective way of creating a strong sense of efficacy is through mastery experiences. **They provide the most authentic evidence of whether one can muster whatever it takes to succeed**" ([research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md](/research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md)).

Read the second sentence as a specification for this session. A mastery experience is *evidence*, and it is evidence for the person who had it. **The moment you take the keyboard, the experience becomes yours and the evidence becomes about you** — and it becomes vicarious experience for the recipient, which is the second source and a weaker one, conditional on perceived similarity, and symmetric in a direction that works against you here.

The same source states directly what a handover meeting achieves without this: **"The multiple benefits of a resilient sense of personal efficacy do not arise simply from the incantation of capability. Saying something should not be confused with believing it to be so."** And it specifies the change model's efficacy component as "repeated opportunities for guided practice in applying the skills successfully in situations simulating those they are likely to encounter in their everyday life" — which is what this session is, minus the word *guided*, because the guidance is the part that has to be withheld.

One further passage decides how hard the rehearsal should be. **"If people experience only easy successes they come to expect quick results and are easily discouraged by failure. A resilient sense of efficacy requires experience in overcoming obstacles through perseverant effort."** The easy day is not a kindness. It builds the brittle version.

**Its limits:** a conference collection edited by the field's founder, with no search strategy, no inclusion criteria and no disconfirming study in the editor's own chapter; empirical settings in schools, families, clinics, migration and addiction treatment; **no study of adult professionals learning a technical craft**; self-report measurement throughout; directionality conceded as unsettled in field settings; and the foundational 1977 paper unread in this repository. **Direction and mechanism only, and no magnitude from it appears anywhere in this wiki.**

### The training literature says the same thing and names the exact behaviour to suppress

A synthesis of error-management training across two dozen studies specifies the intervention in operational terms, and its most transferable sentence is about what the supervisor does not do ([research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md](/research/04-professional-formation/keith-frese-error-management-training.md)).

The method has two elements: **minimal guidance**, where participants receive only basic information and then work independently on difficult tasks, "a procedure that almost inevitably leads to many errors"; and **error-management instructions**, brief framing statements telling participants that errors are expected and informative. And then the operational detail: during the session the trainer repeats the framing and reminds participants to reflect on errors, **"but provides no further assistance when an error occurs"**.

**That clause is the rehearsal protocol.** Say once that errors are expected. Then stop helping.

The synthesis also names the condition under which withholding assistance is a bad idea, and it is the same one that governs task choice at [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md): because the method withholds guidance, **"the task itself needs to provide clearly interpretable and informative feedback"**, and on tasks whose feedback cannot be readily interpreted the approach may be worse than instruction that supplies external guidance. **The authors state that this hypothesis received only limited support**, so treat it as a caution about which acts to observe rather than as a rule.

**Two structural limits govern every use of this source.** Every study in the pool compared error-management training against *another training method*, never against no training, so all effects are relative. And **no study in the pool measured on-the-job performance** — every outcome is a post-training task in a laboratory, so the transfer established is transfer to a novel task rather than to a job. A third: the pooled variability was not statistically greater than sampling error would predict, and the authors say so while proceeding to the moderator analysis on stated grounds.

### The recipient must be able to fail in front of you and say so

A rehearsal is a situation engineered so that somebody's inadequacy becomes visible to a person they have a working relationship with, and possibly to their own management. **That is an interpersonal risk before it is a technical exercise.**

The organisational-behaviour construct for this is psychological safety, and its account of what people avoid is precise: "No one wakes up in the morning excited to go to work and look ignorant, incompetent, or disruptive. These are called interpersonal risks, and they are what nearly everyone seeks to avoid, not always consciously" ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)). The learned avoidance is automatic: "Don't want to look incompetent? Don't admit to mistakes or weaknesses."

**Applied here: a recipient who cannot afford to fail will not attempt anything they might fail at**, and your rehearsal will record a series of successes on acts they already knew they could perform. **The absence of failure is then read as capability, which is the exact inversion this session exists to prevent.**

The quantitative synthesis of that literature ranks the antecedents rather than merely listing them, and tests whether the construct explains anything beyond having a good manager or being engaged at work ([research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)). **That incremental-validity question is the right sceptical one and the reason this programme keeps the construct.**

**The limits on both are real.** The book read here is a trade press statement by the construct's originator, whose case material is drawn almost entirely from secondary sources — teaching cases, journalism, accident reports — and whose narrative review is not a systematic one; the 1999 paper that defined the construct empirically **is unread in this repository**, and the book does not verify it. The meta-analysis note **rests on the official abstract rather than the full text.** **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude from either appears anywhere in this wiki.**

The practical consequence for the session's design is narrow and worth stating: **you cannot manufacture safety in an afternoon**, and a session that claims to would be selling the environmental variable as a technique. What you can do is remove the cheapest threats — tell the recipient in advance what is being observed and what is not, keep their line manager out of the room unless the manager is the operator, and say plainly that an incapacity found today changes the plan rather than the person's record.

### What counts as evidence of capability, and what an observation licenses

The entrustment literature supplies the vocabulary for what you are producing today, and it is stricter than the session can meet ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)).

It names **four sources of information** that support an entrustment decision: direct observations, longitudinal observations, case-based discussions and product evaluations. **This session produces the first and gestures at the third.** It produces no longitudinal record, because there has not been time.

It separates two kinds of decision. **Ad-hoc entrustment** is continuous and informal, taken whenever a supervisor "feels the skills of the learner at that time match the complexity of the patient and the risks in doing this are acceptable". **Summative entrustment** "has the nature of certification", is a decision "taken for the future responsibilities of the learner from that moment on", and **"should include an agreement between multiple observers that this entrustment is justified, to avoid subjective bias, and multiple observations"**.

**One person watching once is ad-hoc, and a rehearsal record should say so on its face.** Calling it more is the commonest way an honest observation becomes an overclaim.

The supervision scale is the other reusable item, and the sub-levels are where the useful distinctions live: level 3 splits into all findings double-checked, key findings double-checked, and a distant supervisor with prompt review; level 4 splits into remote monitoring and none. **The distance between "I check everything you did" and "I check the important parts" is where a handover actually sits**, and a record that reports only *supervised* or *unsupervised* has thrown away the resolution.

The same source records that **entrustment expires** if the activity has not been practised for a long time — which means today's observation has a shelf life nobody writes down.

**Its limits:** a clinical model with patients, legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint, so a course must construct its stakes artificially and the construction is the hardest part of the transfer; guidance from the concept's originator reporting adoption rather than effectiveness; and a full evidence standard — multiple trained observers, documented and deliberated — expensive enough that **any course adopts a reduced version and should say honestly that it is reduced.** This one is reduced.

### Their credentials, not yours — and the access findings are a result

Here is where the session stops being a workshop exercise.

Running an evaluation against a deployed agent is not one act. It requires a project, a model deployment, permission to invoke it, permission to read the traces, and — for judged evaluation — a second model deployment that costs money to run ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). The documentation's two evaluator tiers make the split concrete: a **`LocalEvaluator`** "runs checks locally without API calls — ideal for inner-loop development, CI smoke tests, and fast iteration", while **`FoundryEvals`** connects to the cloud service "for cloud-based LLM-as-judge evaluation", requiring a project with a model deployment and incurring cost.

**Almost every rehearsal that fails, fails at the first tier and never reaches the second.** The recipient cannot authenticate, or authenticates and cannot see the project, or sees the project and cannot invoke the deployment. **That is not a distraction from the rehearsal. It is the single most common way a delivered system turns out to be unoperable**, and it is invisible from the delivery side because the delivery team's credentials worked.

Three properties of the harness itself decide whether the recipient's run means anything.

**The split strategy.** "Multi-turn conversations must be split into query and response halves for evaluation. **How you split determines *what you're evaluating*.**" Last turn, full, or per-turn — an agent that recovers from a bad first attempt scores well under one and badly under another. **Ask the recipient which one they used and why. If they cannot say, they ran a command rather than an evaluation.**

**The threshold.** Output is pass or fail with a `reason`, plus a numeric score for scale-based evaluators; the documented example shows a tool-call-accuracy score of 2 against a threshold of 3 producing a fail. **The threshold is policy and is separable from the measurement**, so moving it changes the pass rate without changing the system.

**Repetitions.** Supplied "to detect non-deterministic behavior", which is the acknowledgement that one sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement. A recipient who runs the suite once and reads the result has not measured anything.

The framework also permits evaluating **pre-existing responses** "from logs or previous runs… without re-running the agent", which is what makes production trace evaluation possible — and which is the act to observe if you want to know whether they can diagnose rather than merely execute.

**And the silence that must travel with all of it: no agreement rate with human judgement is published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold.** Most agent evaluators are model-judged. **Assert no agreement figure for any of them, here or anywhere.**

**Its limits:** read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, with structure reconstructed; most agent evaluators marked preview; one consulted page an explicitly superseded variant; and API surfaces differing across languages so examples are not interchangeable.

### The source this session contradicts, and the contradiction is the point

The practice corpus behind this programme contains a large, well-constructed customer discovery instrument — and **no observation of any kind** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). It is entirely self-report about the engineering system. **Nobody is asked to demonstrate a deployment, share a screen during a pipeline run, or set up an environment while being watched.**

That is not an oversight in a weak document; it is the discovery method of an experienced organisation, and it does not contain the move this session is built on.

**So this session takes the opposite position from its source, and takes it on an argument rather than on evidence:** a claim about somebody's ability, made without watching them, is a claim about their willingness. **No source behind this programme reports whether any customer was ever observed operating a delivered system, or whether such an observation predicted anything.**

**Its limits:** access-controlled internal material, paraphrased and never quoted, carrying no reference, footnote, study or dataset anywhere across roughly ninety documents. Its case studies all succeed and it reports no engagement where capability transfer failed.

### An act where the failure is visible, self-inflicted and reversible

The rehearsal has a casting problem the previous sections did not solve. **Most operate acts either succeed or fail on access**, which tests whether a permission was granted rather than whether understanding transferred. You need at least one act where the recipient can fail for a reason that is theirs.

Here is one, and it is unusually good because it looks cosmetic and is not.

A managed knowledge layer groups one or more **knowledge sources** under a single endpoint, and at query time **a model chooses which sources to consult, from their names and descriptions** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-iq-intelligence-layer.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-iq-intelligence-layer.md)). The name is not a label on a folder. **It is an input to a routing decision**, and the vendor's own evaluation reports that blurred or cross-domain descriptions degrade source selection, while a well-named source stays selectable even when its description is adversarial.

**Direction and mechanism only — this session states no magnitude from that evaluation and neither should you.** What matters here is the shape: a change that any competent administrator would classify as cosmetic, made in seconds, degrades retrieval in a way nobody will attribute to it.

**Which makes it the best rehearsal act available in this stage.** Ask the recipient to add a source, name it badly on purpose, observe selection change, and restore it. Four properties earn its place:

- **The failure is visible.** Selection either changes or it does not, and they can see which sources were consulted rather than inferring quality from an answer.
- **The failure is self-inflicted.** Nothing was refused them and no credential was missing, so what the act tests is understanding rather than entitlement — the one thing every other act in the rehearsal struggles to isolate.
- **The failure is reversible**, which is what lets you run it on a real system on a real day without the intervention condition firing.
- **It replicates a published direction**, so the recipient is not guessing at whether they saw an effect; they are checking whether their system behaves as documented.

**Watch for the diagnosis rather than the repair.** A recipient who restores the name has fixed it. A recipient who says *the selection changed, so the name is doing routing work* has understood it, and only the second one will diagnose the next instance, which will not be a name.

One standing property of that layer belongs in the same session and is worth an oral question rather than a demonstration. **Permission enforcement over these sources is opt-in and off unless the source's own documentation says otherwise and somebody configured it.** The consequence is that two users of different entitlement can receive the same answer and neither the system nor the demonstration will complain. **A recipient who cannot state that is inheriting a retrieval system whose authorisation story they believe is automatic**, and this session is the last cheap opportunity to find out.

**Its limits, and they are the most severe in this session.** The naming finding comes from a **first-party engineering blog**, not from documentation and not from independent work: named authors, named datasets, a named metric, and no external replication. **The headline comparison in the surrounding material measures the product against itself at a lower setting**, which is [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md)'s subject and a reason to treat the surrounding prose with suspicion even where the per-source finding is sound. The layer is **the most perishable material in this repository and the only entry whose names are more perishable than its capabilities** — first-party pages contradict each other on layer membership, on storage, on topology and on whether actions are supported at all, and several supporting pages were read at excerpt depth only. **Never assess recall of component names, source lists or membership. Assess the reasoning about routing, and re-verify every particular before an offering.**

### The rebuild, which fails on exactly what a handover document conceals

The second act closes the gap between *can operate* and *can restore*, and it fails in a place documents never look.

An agent development toolchain provisions and deploys per environment, with the target named as a flag rather than edited into a file ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agents-toolkit-and-sdk-family.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agents-toolkit-and-sdk-family.md)). **Have the recipient run it against their own environment, from a clean machine, with their own service principal, while you watch and say nothing.**

The act is unambiguous in a way most rehearsal acts are not: **it either provisions or it does not.** There is no partial credit, no adjective, and no room for a generous reading. And when it fails, it fails on the class of thing a handover document is structurally incapable of surfacing — **a credential that was never yours to give, a service principal nobody created, and values living in a deliberately uncommitted per-user file that exists on precisely one laptop.** A document cannot record the absence of a file it has never seen.

**Run it from a clean machine or do not run it.** A rebuild on a workstation that has been used for this project carries residue — cached sign-ins, tool versions, files below the repository root — and every piece of that residue is a thing the recipient will not have on the day they need it.

**Its limits.** First-party documentation for a fast-moving toolchain, definitional and evidential nowhere. **The family has renamed components repeatedly, and one component currently ships under two package names and two commands simultaneously, both documented as current**, so pin every command you put into a rehearsal script with the date you verified it. Publishing and distribution behaviour differ by tenant type. **No failure rate, no comparison against any other provisioning path, and no evidence that a successful rebuild predicts anything about a later one.**

## The workflow

**The recipient runs the evaluation, on their own credentials, from their own machine, while you keep both hands visible and empty.**

**Step 1 — write the intervention condition before anything starts, and make it about consequence.** One condition, in observable terms. *You intervene if an act is about to modify a compliance record the assessor may sample.* Not *you intervene if it is going badly.* **A condition written during the rehearsal is a rescue with a justification attached.**

**Step 2 — say the framing once, then stop.** Errors are expected. Errors are what the session is for. You will not be helping when one occurs, and an incapacity found today changes the plan rather than the record. **Do not repeat it.** Repeating encouragement while supplying assistance is the failure mode the training synthesis warns about, and it is what your hands will want to do.

**Step 3 — have them authenticate, and start the clock there.** Not at the evaluation. At the sign-in. Record every access failure with what was missing: a role assignment, a project membership, a deployment permission, a quota allocation. **This is where most rehearsals stop, and stopping here is a finding rather than a failure of the session.**

**Step 4 — have them read the evaluation configuration aloud before running it, and ask two questions.** *Which split strategy is this, and what does it mean the score is about?* *Where did this threshold come from?* The configuration is a small file and the questions are short:

```jsonl
{"query": "cancel the order placed yesterday and confirm the refund window", "expected_tool_calls": ["lookup_order", "cancel_order", "get_refund_policy"]}
{"query": "why was my claim rejected", "expected_tool_calls": ["lookup_claim", "get_rejection_reason"]}
```

```yaml
# eval/handover-suite.yml   <- SHAPE, NOT SCHEMA. Re-derive against current docs.
run_by: "<the RECIPIENT's name>"        # if this is your name, the rehearsal did not happen
credentials: "<recipient's own principal>"
run_on: "<YYYY-MM-DD>"

data: eval/handover-suite.jsonl

split_strategy: last_turn          # last_turn | full | per_turn
# WHY: we care about response quality at the point of handoff, not whole-trajectory
# recovery. 'full' would score this agent HIGHER and answer a different question.

repetitions: 5                     # one sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement

evaluators:
  local:                           # no API calls; runs in CI; costs nothing
    - schema_valid
    - no_pii_in_output
  foundry:                         # LLM-as-judge; needs a project + model deployment; costs money
    - intent_resolution
    - task_adherence
    - tool_call_accuracy

thresholds:
  tool_call_accuracy: 3            # POLICY, not measurement. Moving this changes the pass
                                   # rate without changing the system.
  justified_by: "<link to the decision, or 'INHERITED DEFAULT - not justified'>"

data_mapping:
  # trajectory evaluators need the agent's structured output, not just its words
  task_adherence:     "{{sample.output_items}}"
  tool_call_accuracy: "{{sample.output_items}}"
  coherence:          "{{sample.output_text}}"

judge_agreement_with_humans: null  # no agreement rate is published for any evaluator
```

**Step 5 — sit down, put your hands where you can see them, and count.** They run it. You count every intervention, including the ones you will not remember: the clarifying sentence, the finger raised toward the screen, the noise you make when they are about to go the wrong way. **The noise counts.**

**Step 6 — have them break something and diagnose it, not fix it.** Point them at a stored trace from a run that failed and ask what went wrong. Evaluating pre-existing responses without re-running the agent is a documented capability of the framework and it is the act that separates an operator from an executor. **Watch whether they reach for the `reason` field or for the score.**

**Step 7 — run the unsupervised interval and record its silence honestly.** You are reachable and you are not consulted. Afterwards, record whether you were contacted, by whom and for what — **and record that a short silence proves very little**, because the interval is not long enough for the system to have surprised anybody.

**Step 8 — debrief on the attribution, and write down what they say.** Ask them to account for what went wrong. Record whether they name effort, an acquirable skill, the situation, or their own ability. **That split is an observable, unlike a confidence rating**, and it is the one piece of efficacy evidence a supervisor can actually collect in a debrief.

**Step 9 — attack your own rehearsal rather than defending it.** One prompt: *here is my rehearsal record and my intervention condition. Name three acts from the capability statement this rehearsal did not exercise, and the one intervention in my log that was outside my written condition. Then argue that the rehearsal was run at too low a difficulty, using only what is in the record.* Read it for the act you avoided. Keep what is better than your own reasoning and record what you rejected. **Record separately that this instrument is not independent of the system it is assessing, and that an agent which called your rehearsal thorough was not used as an instrument.**

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** Two things that no document can produce. **The first is the access wall**, which arrives in the first two minutes and is invisible from the delivery side because your credentials always worked. **The second is your own intervention count**, which is always higher than you would have guessed — and the reason it matters is that every intervention you made is an act the recipient did not perform, subtracted from the evidence you came here to collect.

### Second pass — two acts where the failure is theirs

The first pass measures access and execution. **It struggles to measure understanding, because almost everything in it fails on a permission.** These two acts fail on the recipient, which is the only way to find out whether anything transferred.

**Step 10 — the self-inflicted failure.** Have the recipient add a knowledge source, name and describe it well, and record which sources are selected for a fixed set of questions. Then have them **rename it badly on purpose** — blurred, cross-domain, indistinguishable from its neighbour — and record selection again. Then restore it. **You say nothing throughout, including when they hesitate over whether this is really going to do anything.**

**Step 11 — ask for the diagnosis, not the fix.** Before they restore it: *what changed, and what does that tell you the name is doing?* **Record the answer verbatim.** *The name is used for routing* is understanding. *It broke and I put it back* is execution, and execution is what you already knew they had.

**Step 12 — the rebuild, from a clean machine.** Their environment, their service principal, their own uncommitted values, a workstation with no residue. **Start the clock at the clone.** Record every value they had to obtain, from whom, and how long it took to find out who.

**Step 13 — record both, separately from the access findings.**

```yaml
# rehearsal-understanding.yaml — sits beside the access findings. DIFFERENT evidence.
# Access findings show what they were refused. These show what they understood.
# Every product surface below is dated. Re-derive; the names here move faster than the capabilities.
self_inflicted_act:
  act: "rename a knowledge source badly, observe source selection, restore it"
  run_by: "<the RECIPIENT>"          # if this is your name, the act did not happen
  selection_before: ["<sources consulted>"]
  selection_after_bad_name: ["<sources consulted>"]
  changed: true
  diagnosis_offered_verbatim: "<their words, before they restored it>"
  diagnosis_class: "understanding | execution"   # 'it broke and I fixed it' is execution
  restored: true
  # NOTE: no magnitude is recorded here. The published finding is a DIRECTION.

  oral_check:
    question: "who sees what, when two users of different entitlement ask this?"
    answer_verbatim: "<their words>"
    correct: false     # enforcement over these sources is opt-in and off unless configured

rebuild_act:
  act: "provision to their environment from a clean machine"
  clean_machine: true                # residue on your workstation invalidates the result
  their_service_principal: true
  outcome: "provisioned | did not provision"    # no partial credit exists here
  values_they_had_to_obtain:
    - value: "<the uncommitted per-user value>"
      from: "<role>"
      minutes_to_find_out_who: "<n>"   # this number, not the total, is the finding
  commands_pinned_on: "<YYYY-MM-DD>"   # component names in this toolchain have moved repeatedly

observer:
  interventions_during_these_two_acts: 0    # any other number invalidates both
```

**Step 14 — attack the second pass on a question step 9 cannot reach.** One prompt: *here are two rehearsal acts and the recipient's verbatim diagnoses. Argue that the diagnosis shows pattern-matching rather than understanding, using only their words. Then name the operate act I chose not to rehearse because its failure would have been mine rather than theirs.*

The second clause is the one to read. **The acts you leave out of a rehearsal are chosen, and the selection is not random** — it runs away from the parts of the system whose fragility is yours. Record what you rejected. Record that the instrument has only the transcript, so its reading of a diagnosis is a reading of wording, and a terse expert and a confused novice can produce similar sentences.

**What the second pass makes observable.** The gap between execution and understanding, in the recipient's own words, recorded before they were allowed to fix anything. **An operator who can restore a system and cannot say why it broke will restore this failure and not the next one** — and that distinction is invisible in every artefact this programme has produced up to now, including the capability statement.

## What good looks like

- The rehearsal is run by **the person who will hold the responsibility**, not the person most interested in it. Those are frequently different, and choosing the interested one is the commonest way to produce a reassuring rehearsal.
- **It runs on the recipient's own credentials, from the recipient's own machine.** A rehearsal on your laptop with your sign-in has measured your access.
- The work is real and consequential. A rehearsal on the quiet day, on a synthetic case, with the failure paths avoided, measures nothing.
- **It produces at least one observed incapacity.** A rehearsal in which everything went well was run at the wrong difficulty, and the record should say which difficulty was avoided.
- Interventions are counted, including the small ones — the clarifying sentence, the hand pointing at the screen, the reassuring noise.
- **The access findings are recorded separately and are treated as results**, not as setup problems that delayed the real exercise.
- The recipient can say what the score is about — which split strategy produced it, and where the threshold came from — or the record states that they ran a command rather than an evaluation.
- The unsupervised interval is genuine: you are reachable and you are not consulted, and the record says whether you were, **and says that a short silence proves very little**.
- The record names its own standard honestly: one observer, one occasion, which is an ad-hoc judgement rather than a certification.
- What the incapacity obliges is stated as a change to something, not as a note.

## How it goes wrong

- **The rescued rehearsal** — you help, because watching somebody struggle with something you could fix in a moment is genuinely hard, and afterwards everybody agrees it went well. *Warning sign:* the record has no stops in it.
- **The borrowed credential** — the recipient runs everything from your session, or with a shared account created for the day. *Warning sign:* nobody was refused anything.
- **The wrong operator** — the rehearsal is run by the enthusiast, and the person who will carry the pager was not free that week. *Warning sign:* the operator volunteered.
- **The easy day** — real work, quiet conditions, no incident, nothing contended. *Warning sign:* nothing in the rehearsal was time-critical.
- **Willingness recorded as ability** — the recipient tried hard, was positive throughout, and could not complete the act. The record captures the attitude. *Warning sign:* your observation notes contain adjectives about the person.
- **The command mistaken for the evaluation** — the suite ran, something went green, and nobody can say what the number is about. *Warning sign:* the recipient could not name the split strategy and nobody asked.
- **The single sample** — the suite ran once against a stochastic system and the result was recorded as a measurement. *Warning sign:* your repetitions field says one, or is absent.
- **The finding with no consequence** — an incapacity is observed, recorded, and changes nothing, because the date is fixed and the plan is already written. *Warning sign:* your rehearsal happens after your handover is signed.

## Exercises

**None of these is assessed.** The rehearsal record is the principal evidence carried to [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), where it is the thing the entrustment decision rests on and the thing your challenger will use against you.

**1 · Classify eight moments as intervention or not.** For each, decide, and say what rule you used:

- You answer a direct factual question about where a file lives.
- You say "hm".
- You point at the screen.
- You sign in for them because their account is not provisioned.
- You say "have you checked the logs?"
- You wait twenty seconds after they stop typing, then wait twenty more.
- You correct a spelling error in a command they typed.
- You say nothing while they deploy the wrong configuration to a test environment.

> **Model answers.** The first is a judgement call and should be declared in advance either way; the second, third, fifth and seventh are all interventions and are the ones nobody logs, because they do not feel like helping. Signing in for them is the most consequential intervention on the list, because it removes the access finding — **record what was missing and stop the rehearsal rather than fixing it.** Waiting is not an intervention and is the hardest item to perform. The last is correct behaviour if the condition has not fired, and it is uncomfortable, and it is the session. **The usable rule: an intervention is anything that changes what they do next.** That covers noises.

**2 · Read a configuration and answer two questions.** Take the evaluation configuration from the workflow, or your own, and answer without looking anything up: which split strategy is in force, and what would the score be about if it were changed?

> **Model answer.** Under *last turn* the score is about response quality at the point where the conversation was handed off; under *full* it is about task completion and the whole trajectory; under *per-turn* it is about each exchange separately. **An agent that recovers well from a bad first attempt scores higher under *full* and lower under *per-turn*, and neither number is wrong.** They answer different questions, and a team that never chose deliberately is reporting an answer to a question it did not ask. **If your own configuration has no split strategy stated, the default is in force and somebody chose it for you.**

**3 · Go back to your own item 5, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 5 — what you would do when they are about to do something wrong and the condition has not fired. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference.

> **Criterion.** A pass says, in some form: **record it and say nothing.** Anything containing *just*, *gently*, *a small hint* or *only if* fails, and it fails for a reason worth holding — every one of those is a written permission for the intervention you were always going to make, drafted in advance so that it will not feel like one at the time. **The condition exists to make interventions countable. A condition with a discretionary clause counts nothing.**
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**4 · Run the access wall deliberately, on somebody else's principal.** Before any evaluation, have another learner attempt to authenticate and reach the project, the deployment and the traces using only permissions they hold. Record every refusal.

> **What you should find.** More refusals than you expected, at least one of which nobody knew about, and at least one that would have taken days to resolve through a real access-request process. **The finding to record is not the count but the discovery route:** you found it because somebody tried, and no document you wrote would have found it. **If everything worked first time, check whether the account being used is one your team created**, because a credential you provisioned tests your provisioning rather than their access.

**5 · Score the same run under two split strategies and reconcile the difference.** Take one stored trace, evaluate it under *last turn* and under *full*, and write one paragraph explaining why both numbers are correct.

> **Scoring criterion.** A pass explains the divergence in terms of *what question each strategy asks*, and names which question this system's owners actually care about. **A fail treats one number as right and the other as an artefact.** This is the highest-value single exercise in the platform half of the programme, and it is the one a recipient must be able to do before any dashboard they inherit means anything.

**6 · Record the attribution, not the mood.** After the rehearsal, ask the recipient to account for what went wrong. Write down which of four things they name: effort, an acquirable skill, the situation, or their own ability.

> **What to notice.** That it is easy to record and hard to resist paraphrasing. **A record containing adjectives about the person — *keen*, *struggling*, *confident* — has substituted your impression for their account**, and it will be quoted back at you at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) as evidence you were reading willingness as ability. Naming ability as the cause of failure is the signal that a further unsupported attempt will not help, and it is a fact about the situation the person is in as much as about the person.

**7 · Classify six rehearsal acts by what a failure would prove.** For each, say whether failing it demonstrates missing access, missing understanding, or neither.

- *Signing in to the project.* · *Reaching the model deployment.* · *Naming which split strategy produced a score.* · *Renaming a knowledge source badly and saying why selection changed.* · *Provisioning to their own environment from a clean machine.* · *Getting a good answer out of the agent on the first try.*

> **Model answers.** Missing access: the sign-in and the deployment — both real findings, both about entitlement rather than about the person, and both invisible from your side because your credentials worked. **Missing understanding: naming the split strategy, and diagnosing the renamed source** — nothing was refused, nothing was missing, and a failure is theirs. The rebuild is deliberately the awkward one: **it fails on access and on understanding at once**, and the two are separable only by recording which value they could not obtain and whether they knew who to ask. The sixth proves neither: a stochastic system produced an output, and a single good or bad answer is evidence about that sample.
>
> **Why this matters more than it looks.** A rehearsal made entirely of the first two acts measures your permission administration and calls it capability. **A rehearsal must contain at least one act whose failure would be the recipient's**, or its central claim is unsupported — and this is the property your record should be checked against before anybody reads the conclusions.

## What this does not establish

**Almost nothing about the real case, and the limit is severe enough to state plainly.**

**This is a simulation, over a short interval, with a recipient who cannot be genuinely harmed by a bad handover and who knows they are being observed.** Nobody is woken at three in the morning. Nobody carries the consequence into the following month. The unsupervised interval is short enough that its silence proves very little.

**One observer on one occasion is an ad-hoc judgement, not a certification.** The entrustment literature's summative standard requires agreement between multiple observers and multiple observations, drawing on four evidence types. **This session produces one of them.** It is a reduced version and says so.

**The efficacy literature that supplies the keyboard rule is about schoolchildren, families, clinics and treatment programmes**, contains no study of adult professionals learning a technical craft, is edited by the field's founder with no disconfirming result in his own chapter, and rests throughout on self-report.

**The training synthesis measured nobody doing a job.** Every study in it compared one training method against another, in a laboratory, on a post-training task. Its feedback-clarity condition — which decides whether withholding help is wise on a given act — received only limited support by its authors' own statement.

**The safety literature is read at two removes.** The book consulted is a trade press statement whose case material comes almost entirely from secondary sources; the empirical paper that defined the construct is unread in this repository; the meta-analysis rests on its abstract. **A session cannot manufacture psychological safety in an afternoon, and this one does not claim to.**

**The platform documentation publishes no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data and no threshold rationale.** Most agent evaluators are model-judged and marked preview. **Assert no agreement figure for any of them.**

**No source behind this programme reports whether any customer was ever observed operating a delivered system, or whether such an observation predicted anything.** The practice corpus contains no observation of any kind; its discovery method is entirely self-report. **This session takes the opposite position from its source, on an argument rather than on evidence: a claim about somebody's ability, made without watching them, is a claim about their willingness.**

**That a self-inflicted failure distinguishes understanding from execution.** The classification of a recipient's verbatim diagnosis as one or the other is this design's invention, resting on an argument about what a diagnosis has to contain, and no source establishes that a recipient who diagnoses a renamed source will diagnose anything else. **A terse expert and a confused novice can produce similar sentences**, and this session offers no method for telling them apart beyond asking a second question.

**The knowledge-source naming finding is first-party engineering evaluation, not documentation and not independent work.** Named authors, named datasets, a named metric, no external replication, and published alongside a headline comparison that measures the product against itself at a lower setting. **This session takes a direction from it and no magnitude**, and a learner who observes no change on their own system has learned something about their own system rather than falsified anything.

**Direction and mechanism only. No magnitude, no rate, no proportion.**
