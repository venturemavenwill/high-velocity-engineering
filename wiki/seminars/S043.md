## What this day is for

Capacity is the amount of structure a model can express. Too little and it cannot represent the pattern; too much and it represents the sample's noise as though it were pattern. Every regularisation technique is a way of buying one at the expense of the other.

Students learn this as a curve with a minimum and go looking for the minimum. The day's target is different: **a capacity choice is a structural hypothesis about the data, in S021's exact sense, and it can be checked.**

Choosing a strong prior asserts that the pattern is simple. Choosing a flexible model asserts that it is not, and that you have enough data to tell. Both are claims about the world, both are usually unexamined, and both fail silently.

And on this platform, both will be made for you if you do not make them. Foundry's fine-tuning service accepts `auto` for every hyperparameter and **replaces it with a default derived by heuristic from your own training data.** That is not a shortcut the vendor is hiding; it is documented, it is sensible, and it is the day's entire subject decided by a procedure nobody in the room can inspect. A student who submits a job with `auto` everywhere has made a structural hypothesis about their data. They have simply not made it themselves.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items on the module's task.

1. Two models are supplied, one simple and one flexible. Which will generalise better?
2. Here is the training set size. Does your answer change?
3. At what training set size would your answer flip?
4. Predict: is your flip point higher or lower than the measured one?
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to *fine-tune a model on this dataset.* Write down what it will produce, and **name the one thing it will get wrong**. Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 3 is the day's move and is a threshold demand, which the cohort last met at S012.

Item 5's prompt names a method and no parameters, which is what a competent engineer would write. The design's expectation is that a majority predict the agent will pick reasonable defaults, and that a minority predict the specific thing: **it will choose `supervised` without asking whether the problem has finite or many correct answers, and it will leave the hyperparameters on `auto`, so the capacity decision will be made twice by two parties and stated by neither.**

**Warrant.** Pretesting with a crossover demand. The answer depends on a quantity supplied at item 2, which most students do not ask for at item 1. Item 5 is administered here and does not migrate, per instrumentation rule 8.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups train their own component at four capacity settings and measure a learning curve at five training-set sizes for each. The component is small enough to train in minutes, deliberately, so that the curves are the group's own measurements rather than a handout.

| Case | Setting |
|---|---|
| A | Low capacity. Flat curve; more data does not help |
| B | High capacity, no regularisation. Steep curve; still improving at the largest size |
| C | High capacity with strong regularisation. Matches A at small sizes and B at large |
| D | High capacity with regularisation tuned on the *test* set. Best curve, and meaningless |

Groups state, for each, what the curve's shape tells them and commit.

Then a much larger training set is supplied and all four are re-run.

Task after: **state what setting A's flat curve was telling you** — which is that the model, not the data, is the binding constraint, and that collecting more data, which is what a team would otherwise do next at cost, will not help.

**Setting D is not announced.** It is S042's failure appearing one day later in a new costume, and whether groups recognise it is the day's transfer measurement.

### The fifth case, demonstrated rather than operated

A reinforcement fine-tuning job is shown running, not run. **The programme does not assess it**, because a job of that shape takes hours and its Developer-tier training is preemptible, and neither fits a forty-five-minute phase.

What is shown is one graph. Reinforcement fine-tuning reports a mean reward on training data and a mean reward on validation data, and the documentation states plainly what it means when they diverge: **the model may be reward hacking, and the grader requires more engineering.** The room is asked one question about it and then the case is closed: *whose capacity was wrong?* The answer is not the model's. It is the grader's — the thing that defines the objective was too easy to satisfy, so the model found the cheap satisfaction. **Capacity-as-hypothesis applies to the criterion as well as to the model**, which is M10's whole subject arriving five days early as a picture.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across capacity with a data-scaling adjudicator, executed on the group's own component. Setting D is the unannounced transfer test. The demonstrated fifth case is marked as demonstrated per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) and carries no assessment weight; it is included because a documented failure mode whose remedy is *engineer the grader better* is the strongest available evidence that the day's frame generalises beyond the model.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **Capacity** as expressible structure, and the two failure directions.
2. **The learning curve** as the diagnostic: what a flat curve, a steep curve and a converged curve each tell you about which constraint binds.
3. **Regularisation** as a prior, not a patch. Every form asserts something about the pattern's shape. Worked on three forms.
4. **The capacity choice as a structural hypothesis,** per S021, and how to check it: state what the choice assumes, then find the data on which the assumption fails.
5. **The bias-variance account:** what it explains, and where it is a description rather than a mechanism. **This treats the decomposition as a diagnostic vocabulary; it omits the regimes in which the classical curve does not describe observed behaviour, which are real, are current research, and are not settled. Safe as a way of naming which constraint binds on a given curve; unsafe as a prediction of how a model will behave at capacities far outside those you have measured.**
6. **Data size and capacity are one decision,** not two. The right capacity for ten thousand examples is wrong for ten million.
7. **Setting D,** named. S042's protocol violation is not a separate topic; it is available everywhere and this is where it recurs.
8. **Three fine-tuning methods are three structural hypotheses, and the documentation states them as such.** Supervised fine-tuning is recommended where **there are finite ways of solving a problem**; reinforcement fine-tuning where **there are many**; direct preference optimisation where what you hold is not correct answers but **binary preferences between responses**, and where fitting a reward model is not wanted. Those are not three tools of increasing sophistication. They are three claims about the shape of your problem, written down by the vendor in the same grammar this day has been using since phase 2, and **a student who picks a method without stating which of the three claims they are making has made one anyway.** The correspondence is exact and it is the day's best evidence that the S021 frame is not a course conceit.
9. **Defaults are opinions and thresholds are policy.** Three were chosen for you. **Every fine-tuning hyperparameter accepts `auto`, and the service replaces it with a default derived by heuristic from your training data** — which means the number of epochs, the batch size and the learning-rate multiplier, the three levers that most directly set effective capacity, are a function of your data computed by a procedure that is not published. It is probably a better choice than yours. **It is still not yours, and you cannot state what it assumed.** Reinforcement fine-tuning's reasoning effort defaults to `medium` on a three-value scale, which is a capacity decision wearing a different word. And the training tier is a decision too: Standard keeps the job in region, Global is cheaper and queues faster, and **Developer is preemptible** — so the cheapest way to explore capacity is the one that can be interrupted, and a student who does not know that will interpret a vanished job as a failure of their configuration.
10. **Why this instance is perishable and the reasoning is not.** Method names, hyperparameter names, default values and tier names will all change. That a capacity choice asserts something about the data, that the assertion is checkable, and that an unset parameter is a decision delegated rather than avoided — those do not.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Point 5 carries the strong-form scope statement, which is unusually important here because the classical account is taught confidently and its limits are current. Point 8 is the day's platform-specific content and is chosen because the vendor's own selection criteria are stated as claims about the problem rather than as feature comparisons, which is the correspondence the day needs and did not previously have. Point 9 names the defaults, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 3. The capacity is the student's, and this phase produces a build.**

Working within the S042 protocol, through Copilot in agent mode against the Foundry MCP Server, students:

1. **State the structural hypothesis** your intended model family asserts about this data.
2. **Choose a capacity** and state what it assumes. Where the platform offers a method choice, state which of point 8's three claims you are making.
3. **Measure the learning curve** at four training-set sizes.
4. **State which constraint binds** — model or data — and the evidence.
5. **Construct the data** on which your assumption fails, or state why the substrate cannot produce it.
6. **State the capacity you would choose** at ten times the data, and why.
7. **Report the figure** with its licensing sentence and, for any judged component, the uncalibrated clause, within the protocol's single consultation.
8. **Name every parameter you left on a default,** and for each, state what it was set to and what that setting assumes. Where the value was resolved by the service, say so and say that you cannot inspect the heuristic.

Step 8 is the day's platform bite and it is short. Most students will find they left more on defaults than they expected, and the honest entry — *resolved by the service; I do not know what it assumed* — is a passing entry. **What fails is an unnamed default**, because a parameter you did not notice is a hypothesis you did not know you were asserting.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the Copilot Practice Standard.

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a check that refuses to submit a job with unset capacity-relevant parameters is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch.

**Fading:** step 3's curve procedure is supplied; step 1 is worked with the room; steps 5, 6 and 8 are unsupported.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 3 inside the previous day's protocol, which is the module's first task constrained by a prior day's commitment. Step 5 is the day's discriminator and is S021's hypothesis check applied to capacity. Step 8 is the [WP-043](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-043.md) §8 objection-1 repair in a second register: the objection said the account's boundary was enforced by the specification and never communicated, and an element requiring the student to name what they did not choose is a boundary the artefact makes visible.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate model whose capacity was chosen correctly for the data available at the time and became wrong as the data grew, with nobody noticing for a year — with attention to **what signal would have shown it**.

Then the agent case: a Copilot session that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. Asked to fine-tune a model to improve a component's behaviour, the agent produced a complete, correct job submission: well-formed training data, sensible file handling, a submitted job, polling, and a deployment of the result. It chose `supervised`. The task was one where several different responses were acceptable and the data contained one arbitrary phrasing of each — which is the condition the documentation names for preference-based or reinforcement methods and not for supervised ones. The model learned the phrasings.

The review passed it because there was nothing to fail. **The method choice was never stated as a choice**; it appeared as the value of a field, and the field had to have a value. The reviewer saw `"type": "supervised"` and read it as configuration rather than as a claim that the problem had finite correct answers.

Attention goes to why: a hypothesis expressed as an enum value does not read like a hypothesis, and **the artefact that would have caught this is step 2's one sentence**, which the agent was not asked for and does not volunteer.

Then students: a model you made more complex because it was not working, or a parameter you left on a default today without noticing.

**Warrant.** Error management targeting point 6. The instructor disclosure's shape — a correct decision that became wrong — is the S007 seam disclosure's structure in a new domain and is the module's second such case. The agent case is chosen because it contains no error at any step and produces a wrong model, which is the module's standing claim about learned components applied to the tool that builds them.

## Assessment

Specification item in IS201. Per rule 1, the checked elements are: a stated structural hypothesis, a capacity choice with its assumption, a measured learning curve at four sizes, a binding-constraint statement with evidence, a constructed failing dataset or a substrate-reachability argument, a ten-times-data capacity statement with reasoning, a licensing sentence with the uncalibrated clause where applicable, **a named list of every parameter left on a default with what each assumes**, the phase 7 outcome, **the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement**. **Nothing else is assessed.**

**A capacity choice with no stated assumption fails the specification.**

**An unnamed default fails. A default named honestly — including "resolved by the service; I cannot inspect the heuristic" — passes.** The requirement is to know what you delegated, not to have delegated nothing.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**The demonstrated reinforcement fine-tuning case is not assessed**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s provision for capabilities the programme shows rather than operates.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** A capacity choice is a **structural hypothesis about your data**, and it is checkable: state what it assumes, then find the data on which the assumption fails. An unset parameter is a decision delegated, not avoided.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** Capacity, regularisation and learning curves are held at folder level here; the three technique families are grounded in [research/02-technical-foundations/ouyang-instructgpt.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/ouyang-instructgpt.md) and [research/02-technical-foundations/rafailov-dpo.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/rafailov-dpo.md), for **direction and mechanism only, with no magnitude asserted.** The vendor's method names, selection criteria and hyperparameters have no note behind them and are cited as documentation; the heuristics that resolve `auto` are not published; and the regimes in which the classical curve fails are unsettled, so this day takes no position.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Say what your capacity choice assumes, then construct the data that would break it.**
- **Read the learning curve before you buy more data.** A flat curve says the model is the binding constraint and more data will not help.
- **Name every parameter you left on a default.** *Resolved by the service; I cannot inspect the heuristic* is honest — an unnamed default is not.
- **Treat a method value as a claim, not as configuration.** On the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md) it is a *configured control*: it licenses that the hypothesis was stated, and nothing about whether it is true.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Reviewing a hypothesis that arrived as an enum value. There is no wrong line to find and nothing for the review to fail — the field had to have a value, so the claim about the problem's shape is made silently.
