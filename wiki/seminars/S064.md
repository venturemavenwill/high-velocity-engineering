# S064 · Capacity Is Not Quota

**Module:** [M13](wiki/modules/M13.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](wiki/quarters/Q7.md) · **Complexity class:** 5
**Strands:** SY · ME · IS
**Whitepaper:** [WP-064](wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md)
**Depends on:** S020, S039, S062, S063
**Platform anchor:** PTU quota against PTU capacity; hourly billing; Azure Reservations; the gateway's rate limit against its period quota · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (write) · Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

At [S020](wiki/seminars/S020.md), nine quarters ago, students made their first capacity argument: a model, a binding constraint, a margin, and the assumption that would break it. It was a good argument and it was about a system.

Since then the substrate has acquired a bill, a granted allocation, and a monthly ceiling with a degradation path. Three things now stand between the students and serving a request, and **only one of them is a property of the system.**

- **Capacity** is what the system can do. It is a measurement, it has a date, and — after [S062](wiki/seminars/S062.md) and [S063](wiki/seminars/S063.md) — it is not a scalar, because it depends on the mix of requests arriving.
- **Quota** is what you are permitted to consume. Somebody granted it, for a reason, and can revoke it. It is not a fact about the system and no measurement of the system will tell you anything about it.
- **Budget** is what you have agreed to pay. You committed it, its unit is money per unit time, and it changes at a period boundary rather than continuously.

All three present identically when they bind: requests are refused. Each is owned by a different party, moves for different reasons, and is argued about with different evidence.

**On this platform none of that is an argument. It is documentation, and it is unusually blunt about it.**

Quota for dedicated model capacity is defined as *a policy limit enforced by Azure, and it has no associated cost.* Capacity is defined as what is actually available to deploy right now. Between the two definitions sits a call-out that the design did not have to invent: **having quota does not guarantee that capacity is available, and if regional capacity is insufficient the deployment fails.** A separate page adds the second half, which is worse: **a reservation is a discount applied to a billing meter, and it does not guarantee capacity either.** The correct sequence is deploy, confirm, then commit — and the intuitive sequence is exactly backwards.

So the day's central distinction is not taught. **It is executed**, and the room is refused twice for two different reasons before anybody explains anything.

The target: **an engineer who cannot say which of the four quantities is binding will approach the wrong party with the wrong request, and will receive a correct answer to a question nobody was asking.**

This is the day at which the module's complexity class is actually earned. Step 7 permits *do not proceed*, and it means it.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed where the programme can execute it, and the boundary is stated to the room before they start.** Four situations, all presenting as *we ran out of capacity*, all with the same dashboard shape and the same first-hour incident notes.

| Case | What it actually is |
|---|---|
| **A** | Demand exceeded measured capacity. A capacity event. |
| **B** | Demand well within capacity; the granted allocation was exhausted. An allocation event, produced by somebody else's planning cycle. |
| **C** | Demand within capacity and within allocation; the money ran out before the period did. **Every dashboard is green and the service degraded on a finance calendar.** |
| **D** | Demand within all three, and nothing was changed by anyone. The request mix shifted toward longer outputs, the threshold moved, and effective capacity fell. |

Groups classify each, state **what evidence would distinguish their classification from the other three**, and commit. Then — and this is the change — **they produce three of the four themselves.**

- **B is produced in about ninety seconds.** The group takes the capacity figure their [S061](wiki/seminars/S061.md) sizing called for, asks for a dedicated deployment of that size through the Azure MCP Server, and **is refused.** The refusal is a policy limit, it cost nothing to hit, and no measurement of the substrate would have predicted it.
- **C is produced at the gateway.** A period token budget is set for one consumer and exhausted. The refusal that comes back is **a different status code from the one a rate limit produces** — the platform distinguishes *you are going too fast* from *you are finished for this period*, and the two are the difference between a capacity conversation and a money conversation, encoded in the response.
- **D is produced with arithmetic.** The group re-runs its S061 sizing twice: once on the substrate's traffic mix from two quarters ago, once on today's. **Nothing was touched and the required capacity moved.** That is the whole of situation D and it takes one spreadsheet.
- **A is the one the room reads about rather than causes**, and the reason is instructive: to be refused for capacity you must first have quota, and the programme does not. What the room can do is query the capacity read API across regions and watch the answer differ by region and by hour — the documentation states that availability changes through the day with other customers' demand — and then read the recorded session in which a deployment with sufficient quota is refused anyway.

Then the actual causes and outcomes are supplied, with what each cost and how long each took to diagnose.

The payload is **D**. The room classifies it as a capacity event, and it is not one: no hardware changed, no grant changed, no commitment changed. **The capacity figure was never a scalar, and the measurement that produced it was taken on a different mix** — which is [S063](wiki/seminars/S063.md)'s threshold and [S062](wiki/seminars/S062.md)'s policy arriving as a capacity claim that expired while nobody was touching it. It is also the one situation the sizing method itself predicts, since the mix is two of its three inputs.

**C** is the case that produces the professional shock, and it is deliberately second-hardest rather than the payload: a service that degraded with no incident, no error, and no engineering cause at all.

Tasks after: **for each situation, state the measurement whose absence made the diagnosis slow.** Then, and this is the second demand: **for each situation, name the party you would approach and the sentence you would say to them.**

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across four causes with the presenting symptom held identical, which is the only construction that makes the classification a discrimination rather than a description. Three of the four are now caused by the room rather than described to it, and **the one that is not is named as such before the phase begins**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s requirement that a demonstrated capability be declared and not assessed. D is the payload because it requires the previous two days to be composed; C is placed second because its lesson is affective rather than technical and would swamp the phase if it led. **The second closing task is new and is this day's repair of the defect [WP-064](wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 recorded**: point 8's routing claim is now generated here rather than asserted at phase 4, which is what instrumentation rule 7 requires of a claim the day's own pretest predicts the room will resist.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **Four quantities, four owners, four cadences.** *Demand* is owned by users and moves continuously. *Capacity* is a property of the system, is established by a measurement, and carries a date. *Quota* is owned by a grantor and moves when they decide. *Budget* is owned by you and whoever signs, and moves at a period boundary. **Every confusion between two of them is a confusion about who to talk to.**

2. **Capacity is not a scalar.** It is a function of the request mix, because [S062](wiki/seminars/S062.md)'s policy and [S063](wiki/seminars/S063.md)'s cliff both depend on the mix. A capacity figure therefore carries a date **and** a mix, and it expires when either moves. Situation D is a capacity figure that expired without anyone touching the system.

3. **Quota is [S039](wiki/seminars/S039.md)'s kind of constraint.** It is stated as immovable. It has a purpose — usually somebody else's capacity planning, or somebody's revenue. It moves when you find out what the purpose is and serve it another way. That is transcript D's engineer, with a commercial purpose in place of a technical one, and it is the only one of the four quantities that is negotiable in principle.

4. **Budget is a rate, not a stock.** Its unit is money per unit time. A capacity decision is a rate commitment, and a plan that fits the monthly figure while exceeding the weekly one has not fitted. Situation C is a stock-shaped plan meeting a flow-shaped constraint.

5. **Commitment shapes.** Pay-per-use, a reserved rate committed in advance, and mixtures of the two. The trade is unit price against the risk of being wrong about demand: a commitment is cheaper per unit and is paid whether it is used or not. **This list omits three shapes this day does not handle** — pre-emptible capacity, transfer or resale of unused commitment, and pricing that varies within the period. **Safe** as a frame for choosing between the two families on a service whose demand you are forecasting yourself. **Unsafe** as a menu, and specifically unsafe wherever commitment can be traded or price varies within the period, because in both cases point 6's break-even is not the calculation that decides it.

   **The platform's two shapes are worth reading exactly, because each carries a clause nobody expects.** Hourly billing charges for capacity deployed rather than tokens consumed, meters from creation to deletion, and **the deployment cannot be paused** — so an idle deployment is a full-price deployment. A reservation is a one-month or one-year discount **applied to that meter and not to any deployment**, so scaling a deployment down does not shrink the commitment: you keep paying for coverage you are no longer using, and anything above the reserved quantity is billed at the undiscounted hourly rate anyway. **A reservation is therefore not a purchase of capacity and not a cap on spend. It is a price on a meter you still have to run.**

6. **The break-even is arithmetic and it is the number that makes the argument.** The utilisation at which the commitment beats pay-per-use. Compute it, state it, and **state the demand forecast it depends on and that forecast's confidence in the same field**, per specification rule 2. A break-even quoted without its forecast is a number that will travel alone.

   **And every rate in it is perishable, so the break-even carries a third qualification: the date the rates were retrieved.** The rates are not supplied by this day and appear nowhere in this wiki. Each group fetches them through the Learn MCP Server on the morning, records where and when, and **a break-even computed from an undated rate fails the specification.** That is not bureaucracy; it is the only way a number computed in week 64 can be checked in week 68.

7. **Headroom is bought, and its price is per unit time.** [S019](wiki/seminars/S019.md) point 8's operating-point trade with currency attached. **Headroom expressed as a utilisation percentage is not something anyone can buy.** Headroom expressed as distance to the measured threshold, in the units of the demand change, is a quantity a decision-maker can price.

8. **Which number binds determines who you approach and what you ask them for.** *The system cannot do this* goes to whoever owns the system and is an engineering conversation. *I am not permitted to* goes to the grantor and is a negotiation about their purpose. *We have not agreed to pay for it* goes to whoever signs and is a commitment argument with a break-even in it. **An engineer who takes a commercial constraint to a technical party receives a technical answer, and it is a correct answer to a question nobody asked.** This is the sentence the room produced at phase 3's second closing task, and it is consolidated here rather than introduced.

9. **What the platform actually guarantees, and what it declines to.** The day's perishable platform-specific content, verified for this offering on **2026-07-26**, none of it assessed, all of it dated in the delivered material.

   - **Quota is a policy limit with no cost.** It is granted per subscription, per region, and per deployment type, and the three deployment types are separate pools. Quota in one region does not carry to another.
   - **Capacity is what is deployable right now**, it is allocated at deployment time and held for the deployment's lifetime, and **it changes through the day with other customers' demand.**
   - **Releasing capacity is a one-way door in practice.** Deleting or scaling down returns it to a regional pool with no guarantee that the same capacity is there when you want it back. That single sentence is why the documentation warns against using dedicated capacity as an autoscaling mechanism, and it gives a second reason: **continuous hourly billing at high utilisation typically exceeds reservation pricing.**
   - **Neither quota nor a reservation guarantees capacity.** Two separate call-outs, on two separate pages, saying the same thing about two different things you might have thought were the thing.
   - **The overflow mechanism is triggered by a failure response, not by a forecast.** When dedicated capacity is exhausted the request can be redirected to shared capacity, and the redirected request is then billed per token — so **a capacity event silently becomes a variable-cost event**, and the two components of the bill are the two halves of your traffic.
   - **Quota is requested through a form and approval "might take several days."** No bound is published. A plan whose critical path includes somebody else's approval queue has a dependency it cannot schedule.

10. **Defaults are opinions, and here they are commercial.** *The deployment type.* You got shared capacity with no latency guarantee because that is what a new deployment is, and nobody offered you the alternative. *The routing.* Whether your traffic is processed globally, within a data zone, or in one region is a residency-against-availability trade somebody made when the resource was created. *The unit of quota.* Capacity is bought in a unit that is independent of the model, so **the same purchase yields different amounts of service depending on what you run on it**, and each model has a floor below which you cannot deploy at all. *And the sequence.* Nothing stops you buying a reservation before confirming capacity; the documentation tells you not to, and the tooling will happily let you. **An engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system**, and on this day two of the four defaults are decisions about money that were made by whoever clicked first.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3's four situations. Points 1 to 4 are the classification the phase generated. Point 5's scope statement is required by the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md) in strong form. Point 6's date requirement is what makes a class-5 commitment checkable at the later occasion that checks it. **Point 8 is now consolidation rather than assertion**, which is this day's response to the defect [WP-064](wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 recorded under instrumentation rule 7. **Point 9 is the day's perishable platform-specific point and point 10 is the day's defaults point**, both required by the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 5, fully. The recommendation may be *do not proceed*, and that is a conforming answer. This phase produces a build — a commitment somebody could act on tomorrow, not a memorandum about one.**

Students work with Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server and the Foundry MCP Server. **The deliverable is a capacity commitment in deployable form**: the infrastructure definition that would create what is being asked for, the gateway policy that would enforce the budget it implies, and the argument that says whether to apply either. It is reviewed and it is not applied today; [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) is where something like it is.

1. **State the forthcoming demand change** on the substrate — its size, its shape, and its date.
2. **State the four quantities separately** — demand, capacity, quota, budget — each with its owner, its as-of date and its update cadence. **Two of the four are retrieved rather than measured**, and the artefact says where each came from.
3. **Carry your [S061](wiki/seminars/S061.md) cost model forward and state its measured divergence as an uncertainty band on the capacity claim.** The band travels in the same field as the number, per specification rule 2. A model that diverged badly is used with a wide band, not discarded.
4. **State headroom as distance to the measured [S063](wiki/seminars/S063.md) refusal threshold, in the units of the demand change** — requests, or tokens, or users. Not as a utilisation percentage.
5. **Identify which of the four binds**, and say what evidence establishes it rather than the other three. **Where the evidence is a refusal, name which refusal**, because the platform distinguishes them and so must you.
6. **Choose a commitment shape and compute its break-even**, with the demand forecast, the forecast's confidence, and **the retrieval date of every rate it uses**, all in the same field.
7. **Commit.** Recommend *proceed*, *do not proceed*, or *proceed under a named condition* — with **the date at which the recommendation becomes checkable**, **the number that will show you were wrong**, and, where the binding constraint is quota, **what you would ask the grantor for and which purpose of theirs your request serves.**

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a definition that will not deploy without a confirmed capacity check, or a break-even that will not compute without a dated rate, is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch.

**And one element specific to this day.** For each figure in the artefact that the agent produced, **state which of the four quantities it is a claim about.** An agent will hand you a capacity number, a permission number and a money number in one paragraph in the same typeface, and separating them is the day.

**Fading:** step 6's break-even arithmetic is worked with the room, and step 3's divergence computation is supported. **Step 7 is unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 5, and the first task in the programme whose conforming answer set includes declining. **Step 3 is the artefact element [S061](wiki/seminars/S061.md) named under instrumentation rule 6**, and the requirement that a divergent model be widened rather than discarded is what makes the response a use of the measurement rather than an acknowledgement of it. **Step 4 is the artefact element [S063](wiki/seminars/S063.md) named**, and the prohibition on utilisation percentages is what converts a number the student can compute into a number a payer can act on. Step 7's third clause is the only place in Q7 where a student states a negotiating position, and [WP-064](wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 argues it is not enough. The day-specific element is the module's countermeasure against its characteristic agent failure and is assessed.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate capacity request that was well argued, well evidenced, granted in full, and wrong. The binding constraint was budget rather than capacity. The granted capacity was correct about the system, was paid for, and stood unused for a quarter while the service continued to degrade for the original reason. Attention is directed at the request itself — **every sentence in it was true.**

Then the agent case, and it is the most expensive one in the module. Copilot in agent mode, asked to *"reserve capacity for the next year so we stop getting throttled,"* produced a correct and complete plan: it computed the requirement from the documented sizing method, selected a deployment type, drafted the infrastructure definition, and drafted the reservation purchase. **The plan was reviewed by two engineers, approved, and executed in the stated order.** The reservation was purchased. The deployment then failed, because the capacity was not available in the region — and the reservation, which is a discount on a meter rather than a claim on a machine, carried on billing against a meter nothing was running on.

Attention goes to three things held together: **the arithmetic was right, the order was the order a reasonable person would use, and the documentation says in one sentence to use the other order.** Neither reviewer had read that sentence, and neither had any reason to think a purchase would not secure the thing purchased.

Then students, in threes: **a time you asked the wrong person for something**, or a time you assumed that paying for something meant having it.

**Warrant.** Error management targeting points 8 and 9. The instructor case is chosen because the analysis it contains is correct and the routing is not, which isolates the failure the day cares about from every failure of rigour the cohort already knows how to avoid. The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen because **it is the one place in the programme where accepting a confident agent plan spends real money on nothing** — and because the error is not in the plan's content but in a property of the world that only the documentation states.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in SY301 with ME301. Per [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the demand change with its shape and date; the four quantities stated separately with owner, as-of date, cadence and source; the carried cost model with its divergence as an uncertainty band in the same field; headroom as distance to the measured refusal threshold in the units of the demand change; the binding quantity with the evidence distinguishing it from the other three, and the named refusal where the evidence is one; the commitment shape with its break-even and the forecast, confidence and rate retrieval dates in the same field; the recommendation with its check date and its falsifying number; the grantor request where quota binds; **the quantity-attribution of every agent-produced figure**; the phase 7 outcome; **the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

**Capacity, quota and budget presented as a single number fails.** This is the day's first hard requirement and it is published to students before S061.

**A recommendation without a check date and a falsifying number fails.** This is the second, and it is the class-5 requirement made operative.

**A break-even computed from an undated rate fails.** This is the third. Every commercial figure on this platform is perishable, and a break-even whose inputs cannot be re-derived on the day it is checked cannot be checked.

**A recommendation of *do not proceed*, properly evidenced, passes.** Stated explicitly because a specification that never says so will be read as requiring a yes.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**No quota mechanism, pricing figure, commitment product, provisioning mode, deployment category or routing variant is assessed.** All are listed as **perishable** in the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) and may appear only as instances.

**Demonstrated rather than operated, and therefore not assessed:** being refused for capacity after holding sufficient quota, and purchasing a reservation. The room produces the quota refusal, the budget refusal and the mix-driven expiry itself; **the capacity refusal is the one it reads about**, and [WP-064](wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 argues that this is the most consequential gap in the quarter.

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md).

**Rule-6 discharges.** Two obligations placed on this day are discharged here.

- [S061](wiki/seminars/S061.md) named this day as the response occasion for its step 4-against-step 5 divergence, requiring an artefact element in which the model is carried forward and its divergence stated as an uncertainty band rather than discarded. **The element exists as phase 5 step 3, it is assessed, and the requirement that a divergent model be widened rather than dropped is what makes it a response.** The obligation is discharged.
- [S063](wiki/seminars/S063.md) named this day as the response occasion for its item 3 threshold-position error, requiring headroom to be stated as distance to the measured refusal threshold in the units of the demand change. **The element exists as phase 5 step 4, it is assessed, and the prohibition on utilisation percentages is written into the checked elements.** The obligation is discharged.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions:

- **The phase 3 classification error rate, and specifically situation D's misclassification rate**, measures whether the cohort can separate a capacity event from an allocation, commitment or mix event. **Response occasion: [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md), phase 3, where the cost argument must present capacity, quota and budget as three numbers with three owners — a required element of the entrustment artefact and one of its published failure conditions.**
- **The step 6 commitment shape and break-even, with the demand forecast it depends on**, measures whether the cohort's commitments are arithmetic or preference. **Response occasion: [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md), where the cost position defended in the release decision must carry this commitment together with the realised spend of the billing period closing in week 68.** This is the module's only forward link outside its own five days and it is the mechanism by which a week-64 decision becomes a week-70 consequence.

## Perishable content in this day

**Quota mechanics, throughput unit definitions, provisioning modes, pricing and commitment discounts are all listed as perishable** in the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) — never assessed, usable only as instances, and every use marked. This day is built almost entirely on top of that material and the separation is therefore explicit.

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| Permission is not availability | Quota is a policy limit with no cost; capacity is what is deployable now |
| Paying for something is not having it | A reservation discounts a meter and does not hold capacity |
| Capacity is a function of the mix, not a scalar | Two of the three sizing inputs describe the traffic, not the machine |
| A released resource may not come back | Scaling down returns capacity to a regional pool with no reclaim guarantee |
| Budget is a rate, not a stock | An hourly meter that runs from creation to deletion and cannot be paused |
| Different refusals mean different conversations | A rate refusal and a period-budget refusal carry different status codes |
| A capacity event can become a variable-cost event | Overflow to shared capacity, billed per token |
| Your plan inherits somebody else's queue | Quota requests approved through a form, in an unbounded number of days |

- **Durable:** the four quantities with their owners and cadences; capacity as a function of the mix rather than a scalar; quota as a constraint with a purpose; budget as a rate; the break-even as the number that carries a commitment argument; headroom as a purchasable distance; the routing consequence at point 8; and the requirement that every commercial figure carry the date it was retrieved.
- **Perishable:** every commitment product named; every price, discount, ratio and unit definition; every deployment category, routing variant and status code; the substrate's own quota and its granting mechanism; and the specific commercial constraints in item 3. **No item in this list may appear in an assessment item, and each is marked inline in the delivered material.** All were verified for this offering on **2026-07-26**.
- **Local:** the four organisations' situations, the substrate's forthcoming demand change, its granted allocation, the phase 6 capacity request, and the phase 6 agent plan.
