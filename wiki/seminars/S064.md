# S064 · Cost as a Design Constraint

**Module:** [M13](/wiki/modules/M13.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md)
**Retrieves from:** S009, S029, S041
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

The system runs, and somebody pays for it every day. That somebody is not you. After [Stage 8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md) it is definitely not you, and it is not your organisation either.

**The cost side of this practice is the part it does well.** Consumption is forecast by engineers, instrumented by engineers, and refined against telemetry from the system itself. That is a real method and this session teaches it as content rather than as specimen.

Two things are missing from it, and both are assessed here because nothing else in the programme assesses them. **The first is that the corpus's own value claims have no cost side at all** — staffing, compute, the cost of the discovery activity itself and opportunity cost are absent, and every claim offered is a gross benefit. **The second is that the model is never handed over.** The one place the right instinct appears — that the customer should be left holding the cost-modelling capability, because they will need it after the team disengages — sits as a reflection inside a narrative rather than as a step in any procedure. Nobody is required to transfer it and nobody is required to check that it runs.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. What does one unit of your system's work cost, and what is the unit?
2. Which design decision in your system is the most expensive to run, and was it chosen for that reason?
3. What does your observability cost, and against which signals?
4. What happens to the bill if usage grows and nothing else changes?
5. Who will hold this model after you leave, and can they run it?

**Item 1 is the one that separates a cost model from a bill.** A figure per compute hour is a bill. A figure per unit of the customer's work is a model.

## What you must produce

**A cost model a customer engineer can run without you.**

- Cost expressed **per unit of the customer's work** — per claim processed, per document reviewed, per site surveyed — not per unit of infrastructure.
- **Instrumented against the system's own telemetry**, with the instruments named and pointing at signals that survived [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md).
- **Observability as a cost line**, not as overhead.
- **The cost-bearing design decisions named**, each with the alternative that was not taken and what that alternative would have cost.
- **The delivery cost in the same document as the running cost.**
- **A named person on the customer's side who has run it**, with the date they ran it.

## What good looks like

- **The unit is a unit of the customer's work.** They can read the model and tell what one of their own transactions costs them.
- **The model is refined against telemetry rather than assembled from estimates.** Every input that is still an estimate is marked as one and dated.
- **Observability appears as spend.** [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) deleted signals for a reason and this is where the reason becomes visible.
- **At least one design decision is identified as cost-bearing**, with its alternative and the alternative's cost. This is a trade study in a smaller container and the standard from [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md) applies.
- **The growth behaviour is stated.** What the bill does as usage rises, and which term dominates first.
- **Somebody on the customer's side has run the model and their name and date are in it.** Not "will be trained to" — has.
- **The delivery cost is in the document.** **A gross benefit is not a result**, and a document with one column is a proposal rather than a model.

## How it goes wrong

- **The gross benefit** — a return offered with no cost beside it. *Warning sign:* your value document has one column and it is the good one.
- **The infrastructure unit** — cost per node, per hour, per instance, per token. *Warning sign:* the customer cannot tell what one of their own transactions costs.
- **The forecast that never met telemetry** — a model built once from estimates, never refined against what actually happened. *Warning sign:* every input is an estimate and none is dated.
- **Free observability** — telemetry treated as overhead rather than as spend, so nothing in the model ever argues against collecting more. *Warning sign:* it is not a line.
- **The optimisation nobody measured** — a change adopted because a tool labelled it an efficiency, with no measurement of the thing it optimised. *Warning sign:* your justification is the name of a feature. **The openly published playbook demonstrates this failure against itself**, in a checklist recommending a set of efficiency measures that its own sibling page argues may each be net negative.
- **The model you take with you** — the capability leaves when the team does. *Warning sign:* the model is a spreadsheet on your machine, and the handover plan says "we will share it".
- **The dominated term nobody found** — the model is accurate today and wrong at scale, because nothing states which term grows fastest. *Warning sign:* item 4 was answered with "it scales".

## Do it

**First, pick the unit and defend it.** It has to be something the customer already counts. If they do not count it, you have chosen the wrong unit or found a discovery gap, and either way that is the finding.

**Second, forecast, then instrument, then refine.** The forecast is the cheap part and it is where most teams stop. **Refining it against telemetry from your own system is what turns a proposal into a model**, and it is the practice the source does genuinely well.

**Third, put observability in.** Take the signal list from [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) and attach a cost to it. Some of what survived deletion will not survive this, and that is the session working.

**Fourth, find the cost-bearing decision.** Walk your architecture and identify the choice whose running cost dominates. Then say whether cost was a criterion when it was made. **Often it was not, and saying so is more useful than pretending otherwise** — it is the same honesty [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md) demanded of a trade study that was never run.

**Fifth, add the delivery cost.** Your time, the discovery activity, the workshops, the assurance work in [Stage 6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md). Put it next to the benefit. It will make the document worse to read and better to trust.

**Sixth, hand it over and watch.** Sit with somebody on the customer's side while they run it. Do not touch anything. **Record where they got stuck**, because that is the runbook [S072](/wiki/seminars/S072.md) will need and you will not otherwise discover it.

## Show it

Give the model to a customer engineer and ask them to answer one question with it: **what would it cost to double the volume of one thing they care about?**

You may not help. You may watch.

Whatever they could not do is the model's real specification, and **the gap between a model you can run and a model they can run is the entire content of this session.**

## What this does not establish

**Nothing here shows that a cost model changes a design decision, or that a customer who holds one uses it.** No source available to this programme reports an outcome for either. The practice corpus records no cost side anywhere in its value claims, no failure, no post-deployment record and no evidence that any delivered system survived, was maintained by the customer, or was still running months later.

**The transfer requirement in this session is written past the source.** The corpus mechanises the teardown of its own assets to the level of a register-triggered state machine and leaves the transfer of capability as an unrecorded aspiration. **There is no procedure to teach**, so this session assembles one from a single reflective sentence and marks it. That is design reasoning, and [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md) inherits it as the same class of claim.

**The efficiency warning is verified and narrow.** The openly published playbook's sustainability checklist recommends a set of measures that its own disclaimers page immediately undercuts row by row, arguing that each may be net negative in context and that optimisation tooling does not guarantee the saving it names. **That is a specimen of a contradiction inside one document, not a finding about any particular technique**, and this session uses it as the former.

## Perishable content in this session

| Durable claim | Perishable instance |
|---|---|
| A cost expressed in units of infrastructure is a bill; in units of the customer's work it is a model | Current pricing units, meters and billing granularity |

| Observability is spend and belongs in the model as a line | The present price of retention at each tier |
