# S074 · Tool Failure Has Four Shapes

**Module:** [M15](wiki/modules/M15.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](wiki/quarters/Q8.md) · **Complexity class:** 5
**Strands:** IS · ME · SY
**Whitepaper:** [WP-074](wiki/whitepapers/WP-074.md)
**Retrieves from:** S027, S032, S068, S073
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Agent Framework — the five tool-call evaluators · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

A tool call returns success. The run completes. The trace is green. The answer is wrong.

[S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 3 named three detection failures and said the third is the common one: **the signal exists, someone is watching, and it is indistinguishable from normal.** It called that a discrimination problem rather than a coverage problem and said that no amount of instrumentation fixes it. [WP-068](wiki/whitepapers/WP-068.md) §6 then named this day as the one that must supply the bridge, and said plainly that S068 could not: *point 3's third detection failure is exactly the condition an agent's confidently-wrong output produces, and this day frames it as a discrimination problem in a signal rather than as a property of an answer. S074 must supply the bridge; this day cannot.*

Here is the bridge. **A tool can report on what happens inside it and on nothing else.** It validates its own arguments, so it can tell you they were malformed. It executes, so it can tell you it failed. **It cannot observe that it was the wrong tool to call, and it cannot observe what the system did with its answer** — because both of those happen outside it, and there is no other party in the loop whose job it is to report them.

That single observation generates the decomposition. Four shapes, defined by **position relative to the tool boundary** [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) established: **selection** before it, **input** at it, **execution** inside it, **output utilisation** after it. Two are inside and are reported. Two are outside and are not. The two that are outside are the two that produce runs that look clean, and that is not a failure of anybody's monitoring.

The four terms are IS302's and the [Course Catalogue](wiki/program/06-Course-Catalogue.md) names them in those words. **They are also, name for name, four of the five tool-usage evaluators the platform ships**, which is the day's most useful coincidence and its most dangerous one — useful because the decomposition arrives from two independent directions, dangerous because a student who runs five evaluators will believe they have applied it. Point 9 is where that is taken apart.

The [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) records this decomposition as **durable**, on the ground that it is a decomposition rather than an implementation. It earns that here: nothing in it depends on a framework, a protocol or a model, and it survives any of them being replaced, because it is a claim about who is positioned to see what.

The target: **tool failure decomposes four ways by position relative to the boundary; the criterion that makes them four classes rather than four examples is who is positioned to observe; two of the four have no such party; and a single tool-error rate reports on one shape of four.**

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. An agent run is supplied that produced a wrong result. **Every tool call in it returned success.** Where did it go wrong?
2. **Enumerate the outcomes a tool call can have.** Write the complete list.
3. Three more runs are supplied. All reported success, all were wrong, and all were wrong differently. **State what distinguishes them** — and, for each, **name who in that system would have been positioned to notice it, or state that nobody would.**
4. Predict: **of the four failures you will see today, how many would appear on a monitoring dashboard?**
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** For two years you have watched this agent choose tools, supply arguments, execute them and use what came back. **Write down which of those four it gets wrong most often in your experience, and predict which one today's cases will show it is worst at.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 2 is the re-probe [WP-033](wiki/whitepapers/WP-033.md) §9 carried here as the cohort's outcome-space baseline; the [S033](wiki/seminars/S033.md) item 1 count is the figure this one is read against, and both are reported together at phase 8. **Item 3's second clause is the day's headline instrument administered before the day supplies its answer**, and phase 5 step 4 is the same question on the student's own system; the measure is the shift between them rather than either alone. Item 4's answer is two, and the room's median is four.

**Item 5 is the day's contribution to the running frontier map, and it is the module's recursion made into a measurement.** The students' own instrument exhibits all four shapes; they have two years of experience of it; and the question is whether that experience has produced a usable model or an anecdote. The design's expectation is that the room names execution and input — the two it has seen reported — and that the two it cannot see are the two it has been living with.

**Warrant.** Pretesting on runs whose failure is invisible in the artefact the room would normally consult, so that item 1 establishes the day's problem before any vocabulary exists. Item 2 is a genuine re-measurement rather than a recall check: [S033](wiki/seminars/S033.md) asked the same question of a callee that was not a component producing plausible things, and whether four quarters and a module of agent material change the answer is the instrument's whole point. **Item 3's second clause was added after [WP-074](wiki/whitepapers/WP-074.md) §8 found the day's headline instrument sitting downstream of the instruction that supplies its answer** — the third recorded instance of that pattern in this design — and it is what converts the step 4 measure from a recall check into a movement. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8, and it is deliberately about the tool in the students' hands rather than about the substrate's, because the frontier map's whole subject is that instrument.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative retrieval set (20 min)

Free recall, no notes.

- [S027](wiki/seminars/S027.md): the four error sources, and the three the list omits — particularly **error introduced by the measurement itself.**
- [S032](wiki/seminars/S032.md): what makes an event unobservable, and why reproduction is the wrong first move.
- [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md): the four-part detection target, and **the three detection failures.**
- [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md): the four enforceable things, and the boundary of trust as a place.
- **Bridge:** S068 named three detection failures and said the third — the signal exists, someone is watching, and it is indistinguishable from normal — is a discrimination problem rather than a coverage problem. **A tool call that succeeded and was the wrong tool emits exactly the signal a correct call emits. Which of the three is that, and what would have to be different about the tool's signal for it to be one of the other two?**

The room answers: the third — and then, working it through, that **nothing about the tool's signal can be different, because the tool is not the party that failed.** That second half is the day's entire taxonomy in one sentence, and the room produces it.

**Warrant.** Expanding-interval retrieval closing with the bridge [WP-068](wiki/whitepapers/WP-068.md) §6 required this day to supply and stated S068 could not. The bridge is constructed so that its first half is a classification the room can do immediately and its second half is the thing S068 could not reach — and the second half is what makes the day's criterion structural rather than definitional. It is answered by the room in under three minutes and costs no exposition.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups receive **four failures of one agent capability**, each with its full trace, its tool logs and its outcome. **All four reported success.**

**The four are drawn from the paths this cohort did not bound at [S072](wiki/seminars/S072.md)** — the region between each student's stated path bound and the distinct paths realised on the shadow run. **The selection rule and the cohort's own S072 bound errors are shown to students at the start of the phase**, per instrumentation rule 3, because a day that supplies its own cases and conceals how it chose them is running the selection effect this programme teaches students to detect.

- **F1** — the system called a tool that succeeded and was not the tool the request needed. A neighbouring tool, plausibly named, with an overlapping surface.
- **F2** — the right tool, with arguments that encode a different question. The call was valid, the schema accepted it, the result is well-formed and is about something else.
- **F3** — the tool returned a stale value from a cache after its upstream dependency had been unavailable for two hours. The call reported success and the value was two hours old.
- **F4** — the tool returned the correct value and the system's next step used it wrongly: read a rate as a total.

Groups diagnose each and commit, then the traces are supplied in full.

**Then the platform is asked.** Each group runs **the five tool-usage evaluators** — `tool_call_accuracy`, `tool_selection`, `tool_input_accuracy`, `tool_output_utilization`, `tool_call_success` — over all four runs, and records what each returns beside the group's own diagnosis. The task is not to check whether the evaluators are right, which nothing in the room can establish; **it is to say, for each evaluator, which party it is.** The answer is the same for all five and it is the day's finding: **an evaluator is a judge reading the record, not a party positioned at the failure.** `tool_call_success` is documented as evaluating whether tool calls executed without technical failures, and by construction there is no technical failure in F1, F2 or F4. The other four read the trace — the artefact produced by the loop that failed.

The payload is **F4**, and it defeats the previous day rather than the previous minute. [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) spent six hours establishing that the boundary of trust is where content the system did not author becomes something it acts on, and every group arrives holding that frame. **F4's failure is entirely downstream of the boundary.** Nothing crossed. Nothing external was authored. The value was correct, the system's own arithmetic was the failure, and there is no crossing anywhere on the path to mark. The room has to accept that the boundary is a necessary object and not a sufficient one, one day after learning it.

Task after: **for each of the four, name the party positioned to notice it, and say what they would have seen.** For F2 and F3 the answer is the tool. For F1 and F4 there is no such party, and groups are required to write *none* rather than nominate a reviewer — **and, having just run five evaluators named after these shapes, to write *none* rather than nominate an evaluator.**

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases holding the reported outcome fixed — all four succeed — and varying only the position of the failure, which is the only construction that makes position visible when the observable is identical. **The evaluator run is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s executed-cases requirement doing real work rather than decoration**: the platform ships a decomposition with the same names as the day's, and the fastest way for a student to lose the criterion is to acquire the names from a tool that computes them. Running the five and being required to classify the evaluator itself by observational position is the countermeasure, and it is administered before phase 4 defines the criterion. **The case selection is instrumented to [S072](wiki/seminars/S072.md)'s measured bound error** so that the decomposition is exercised on the region this cohort's enumeration failed rather than on a convenient set, and the selection is disclosed under rule 3. F4 is placed as payload deliberately against the immediately preceding day, because a frame acquired yesterday and unqualified today becomes the frame students carry out of the module.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **Four shapes, by position relative to the boundary.** **Selection** — which tool, whether to call one at all, and when — happens before it. **Input** — the arguments — happens at it. **Execution** — what the tool did — happens inside it. **Output utilisation** — what the system did with what came back — happens after it.

2. **The criterion that makes them four classes rather than four examples is who is positioned to observe.** A tool validates its own arguments, so it can report an input failure. A tool executes, so it can report an execution failure. **It cannot observe that it was the wrong tool, because it does not see the request. It cannot observe what happened to its answer, because it has already returned.** Selection and output utilisation happen outside the tool and no party in the loop is positioned to report them. **This is the day's claim and everything else follows from it.**

3. **Therefore two of the four report success and are wrong, structurally.** Not because anybody built the monitoring badly. [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 3's third detection failure with a cause attached: the signal is indistinguishable from normal because **the party emitting the signal is not the party that failed.**

4. **The criterion has one boundary and the day names it.** *Scope, strong form.* An **input** failure whose arguments are *valid and wrong* — F2 — is not observable by the tool either: the schema accepted it, so the tool has nothing to report, and it will present exactly as a selection failure does. The correct statement is therefore that **silence is a property of observational position, not of the class label**, and that a valid-and-wrong argument moves input into the silent set. The decomposition is **safe** as a triage for attributing a failure you can replay against the tool's own logs, and as the design frame for deciding what to discriminate. It is **unsafe** as a claim that exactly two classes are silent — a student who quotes "two of four are silent" as a property of the taxonomy will under-instrument F2 systematically — and it is **unsafe** for a system you cannot replay, where the trace is the only record and the trace is produced by the loop that failed. **A student who classifies by label rather than by who could have seen it has learned the wrong thing**, and the artefact requires them to answer the observation question per shape rather than to assign labels.

5. **Execution failure inherits [S033](wiki/seminars/S033.md) and adds one.** Success, failure, unknown — **and returned-something-plausible-and-wrong.** [S033](wiki/seminars/S033.md) could not have the fourth, because its callee was a service and not a component that produces plausible things; [WP-033](wiki/whitepapers/WP-033.md) §6 said so four quarters ago and named this day. F3's stale cache is the ordinary case and it is not exotic: **every caching layer, every fallback, every default is a mechanism for returning something plausible when the true answer is unavailable**, and each was built for a good reason.

6. **Output utilisation is the shape with no literature and, in this design's judgement, the highest incidence.** *The judgement is a judgement and the day says so*: nobody counts it, so nobody can state a rate, and this design does not state one either. It is ordinary program error — a field read wrong, a null coerced, a unit assumed, a rate read as a total — occurring at a place where the value's provenance makes it look authoritative. **The system trusts its own tool's output more than it trusts its own input, and there is no reason for that ordering.** F4. The shape has no name in most organisations, which is why its rate is unknown everywhere.

7. **The four need four different discriminations, and they are not variations of one.** Selection needs the **intent recorded before the call**, compared against the tool actually called. Input needs the **argument compared against the request**. Execution needs the tool's own **liveness and freshness**, per [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 8. Output utilisation needs the **value's use compared against its type and its provenance**. Note what these have in common: **a discrimination is a comparison between two records that should agree**, which is a different object from [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s signal-for-a-condition, and it is the day's assessed form. **A single tool-error rate reports on one shape of four**, and it is usually execution.

8. **[S027](wiki/seminars/S027.md) point 2's second omitted source, arriving again.** Error introduced by the measurement itself. **The trajectory record is produced by the same loop that produced the failure**, so for a selection or output-utilisation failure the record shows a clean run: the tool was called, it succeeded, the value came back. [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 4 verbatim — the instrument and the observation moved together, so the difference between them carried no information — now at the record rather than at the monitor. **This is the problem [S075](wiki/seminars/S075.md) exists to solve and this day cannot.**

9. **The platform ships this decomposition, and shipping it is not observing it.** *Perishable, and marked.* The evaluator catalogue groups five evaluators under tool usage: **`tool_selection`**, **`tool_input_accuracy`**, **`tool_call_success`**, **`tool_output_utilization`**, and **`tool_call_accuracy`**, documented as covering the overall quality of tool calls including selection, parameter correctness and efficiency. **Four of those names are this day's four shapes.** That correspondence is worth having and it is worth being precise about what it is: it is an instance, arrived at independently, and it is not evidence, because a decomposition agreeing with another decomposition establishes nothing about either.

   **What the correspondence does not do is supply an observing party**, and phase 3 is where the room found that out. Four of the five are model-judged readings of the trace, and the trace is the artefact point 8 has just established is produced by the loop that failed. **An evaluator named after a silent shape does not make the shape observable; it makes a judgement about a record in which the shape left no mark.** Two of the four discriminations at point 7 — selection and output utilisation — require a second record that the trace does not contain, and no evaluator reading only the trace can supply it.

   **One evaluator in the catalogue is built the other way and it is worth the room's attention.** `task_navigation_efficiency` takes `actions` and `expected_actions` and is documented as requiring **no model parameter** — the one agent-behaviour check whose result does not depend on a judge. It works because it demands ground truth: a path stated before the run, compared against the path taken. **That is point 7's selection discrimination in shipped form**, and it is the proof that the discrimination is buildable rather than aspirational. It is also the reason the platform cannot supply it for you: **the expected path is a claim about intent, and only the person who wanted something can state it.**

   **A silence, recorded and taught.** No agreement with human judgement is published for any evaluator in this catalogue, so every judged score in it is uncalibrated and students are required to say so when they report one. A second, smaller silence is instructive about the genre: two Microsoft Learn pages describe `tool_output_utilization`'s output differently — one lists it as binary pass or fail, another groups it with the judged evaluators returning a one-to-five score — and neither says which governs. **The day does not resolve it. It records it, and the students see that reading two pages was enough to find it.**

10. **Defaults are opinions and thresholds are policy, and both were set for you.** *The evaluation framework's cloud evaluator set defaults to relevance, coherence and task adherence — and adds tool call accuracy automatically when the items contain tool definitions.* Nobody chooses that. It encodes a real position, and a defensible one: **an agent with tools has an obligation an agent without them does not.** It also means a team that never chose an evaluator is running a tool-quality check they did not select, on a definition they have not read. *And the threshold:* the documented worked example scores a tool call at 2 against a threshold of 3 and fails it. **A pass threshold of three on a one-to-five scale is a decision nobody made**, it is separable from the measurement, and moving it changes the pass rate without changing the system. A threshold must be justified rather than inherited, and [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s gate work is where the cohort learned why. **An engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system.**

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Point 2 is the day's whole content and is stated as a criterion rather than as a list, because the list is what students will otherwise retain. Point 4 carries the strong-form scope statement **on the design's own criterion**, which is unusual and is deliberate: the criterion is the durable claim, and a durable claim with an unnamed boundary is exactly what the register's durable classification would otherwise license. Point 6 is the shape students will find in their own systems most often and is the one with no external literature to lean on; its incidence claim is marked as a judgement at the point of use, because the day has no measurement of it and neither does anyone else. Point 7 is the assessed object. Point 8 hands the module's remaining problem to the next day by name. **Points 9 and 10 are the platform layer**, required by the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), and point 9 is the most load-bearing platform point in the module: the single most likely way for a student to leave this day with a vocabulary instead of a criterion is to acquire the vocabulary from a tool that computes it, and point 9 is where the difference between naming a shape and observing one is made explicit on the exact artefact that invites the confusion.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 5. This phase produces a build. The instances, the observation positions, the discrimination and the accepted blindness are the student's.**

Students use **the capability, the drawing and the crossings they have carried since [S071](wiki/seminars/S071.md)**, working in Copilot agent mode through the Foundry MCP Server.

1. **Take every crossing you failed to mark at [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md)** — from the boundary inventory — **and classify the failure each could produce by shape.**
2. **Produce one instance of each of the four shapes for your own capability**, drawn from the substrate's existing trace log rather than invented. Where you cannot find one, say which shape and say what that means.
3. **Take the shadow-run proposals you failed to anticipate at [S071](wiki/seminars/S071.md) step 6 and classify each by shape.**
4. **For each shape, name the party positioned to observe it in your system, or state that none is.** For every *none*, say what would have to be added for a party to exist — **and, where the platform ships an evaluator named after that shape, state what that evaluator read in order to produce its score.** **An evaluator is not a party**, per point 9; naming one here is the error the specification fails, and tracing its input is what makes the difference between asserting that and understanding it.
5. **Design one discrimination for a silent shape** — a comparison between two records that should agree and do not. **State its false-alarm rate and argue its independence from the pipeline it compares**, per [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) points 2 and 4.
6. **State the shape you are not going to discriminate**, with its expected cost, **and its provenance and your confidence in the same field**, per [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) specification rule 2.
7. **Build the discrimination, run it over the substrate's existing trace log**, and report what it found and what it missed, with the missed cases classified by shape.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent to build the discrimination, in order, including the ones you abandoned.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — and today the qualifying demonstration is the discrimination itself, provided step 7 shows it finding something over real data. **This is the module's cleanest instance of the escape clause working as intended**: a student who built the detector does not have to prove they were watching.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch. Both specific, both from this build. **The day's own subject makes this element unusually sharp**: if what it did badly was a selection, an input, an execution or an output-utilisation failure, name which, and say which party in your own workflow was positioned to notice it.

**Fading:** step 5's discrimination form is worked with the room, since a comparison between two records is a new object and it is the day's assessed form; step 7's harness is supplied. **Step 4 is unsupported** — naming the party positioned to observe is the day's discriminator, and a student who supplies a party who is not positioned has produced the exact error point 2 exists to prevent.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 5 whose inputs are three prior days' measured blind spots. **Step 1 is the receiving element [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) named under instrumentation rule 6 and step 3 is [S071](wiki/seminars/S071.md)'s**; both take the cohort's own failures as material rather than a supplied set, which is what makes the decomposition a method rather than a vocabulary. Step 2's *where you cannot find one* clause is deliberate: a shape the trace log cannot exhibit is either absent or invisible, and distinguishing those is point 2 applied by the student. Step 4's *an evaluator is not a party* clause is point 9 as an assessed decision, and it is placed in the unsupported step because that is where the day expects the error. Step 5's independence requirement is carried from [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 4 unchanged, because a discrimination computed from the record it is checking has the same defect a monitor computed from its own pipeline has. **Step 7 ships**, which is what makes the day's answer to its own fatalism risk a working instrument rather than a reassurance.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate agent capability whose tool-error rate ran under one percent for a full year. It was reported weekly, it was accurate, it was watched by people who understood it, and it was on the dashboard the whole time.

Every one of the capability's worst outcomes over that year is absent from it — because in every case the tools succeeded.

Attention is directed at one fact: **the metric was correct and it measured one shape of four.** Nobody computed it wrongly, nobody chose it lazily, and it was the metric the platform emitted by default.

Then the agent case: a student asked Copilot in agent mode to **classify a set of failed runs by shape**, having given it the four definitions. It produced a clean table with a reason for every row, and the reasons were good. **It put F2 — the right tool called with arguments encoding a different question — in selection**, and argued that the system had evidently misunderstood which capability was needed. The argument is plausible. The classification is wrong, and it is wrong in the one place point 4 says the criterion bends.

Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the answer is unfalsifiable without opening the trace**, the reason given was the reason a competent engineer would give, and stage-based reasoning produces the right answer on most cases — which is exactly what makes its failures rare, systematic and confidently defended. This is [WP-074](wiki/whitepapers/WP-074.md) §5's predicted failure of the most experienced students in the room, produced in thirty seconds by the instrument all of them are using.

Then students, in threes: **a number you have reported that was right and answered a different question.**

**Warrant.** Error management targeting point 7's final clause, constructed like the module's other clinics so that the case contains no error of care: the metric was correct, its owners were competent, and its coverage was a property of where it was emitted from rather than of anyone's judgement. The default-emission detail is the phase's content — the number was chosen by the platform, which is how most numbers are chosen — and the student disclosure is deliberately about correctness rather than about error, because this clinic's failure is not a mistake anybody made. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition** and is chosen so that the agent commits the day's own boundary error with a good argument attached, which is the form in which the students will meet it in their phase 5 step 1.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **their step 5 discrimination and their step 4 observation-position claims.**

The partner has two obligations and both are required.

- **Construct a failure of a silent shape that the discrimination will not catch**, and name the shape.
- **Challenge one observation-position claim.** Pick a party the writer said was positioned to observe a shape, and state **what that party would actually have seen** in the trace. A challenge that shows the party sees a signal but cannot distinguish it from normal is a successful challenge, and is [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 3's third failure produced by a peer.

Per [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 3, both outcomes are recorded in the artefact.

Per instrumentation rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8, the dual-defeat construction reuses [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) phase 7's and **its use here is contingent** on S068's reported outcome; a reported failure there retracts it here along with every other contingent reuse.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with a dual demand in which the second half attacks the day's own criterion applied to the student's system. The observation-position challenge is the phase's real content: naming a party is the step students find easy and get wrong, and the only correction that works is somebody reading the trace and saying what that party would have had in front of them.

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- **Sealed items opened. Item 2's outcome enumeration reported beside the same cohort's [S033](wiki/seminars/S033.md) item 1 count** — both figures together, with the fourth outcome counted separately. This is the re-probe [WP-033](wiki/whitepapers/WP-033.md) §9 carried here and it is the day's most quoted result.
- Item 4's predicted dashboard count against two, as a distribution.
- **Item 5 opened.** The cohort's predictions about their own agent's worst shape, against the day's cases and against their own step 2 instances. **Two figures are reported.** Which shape the room nominated, and which shape the room's own frontier statements across [S071](wiki/seminars/S071.md) to [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) actually record most often. **The design's expectation is that the two disagree and that the nominated shape is one of the two the tool reports.** If it holds, the room has a demonstration of point 2 taken from two years of its own experience: **you nominate what you were told about.** It is reported as a shift and as directional only, for the same reason item 3's is.
- Items 1 and 3's attributions sorted by shape by the room, with the disagreements read out rather than resolved.
- **Item 3's observation-position claims against step 4's**, per student, as a movement: how many parties named before the taxonomy survive contact with what the trace would actually have shown them, and how many *none* answers appear before against after. This is the day's headline instrument and it is reported as a shift, for the reason [WP-074](wiki/whitepapers/WP-074.md) §8 gives.
- **The cohort's [S071](wiki/seminars/S071.md) unanticipated shadow proposals, sorted by shape** — which shape the cohort's boundaries most often permitted. This is the response [S071](wiki/seminars/S071.md) named for its step 6-against-step 7 prediction accuracy, and it is the first time the cohort sees what kind of thing they failed to anticipate rather than how much.
- **The cohort's [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) unmarked crossings, sorted by shape**, on the same axis — with the lower-bound caveat repeated.
- Step 7's discrimination results: what each found, what each missed, and the missed cases by shape.
- The cohort's step 6 undetected shapes, pooled, **with the total expected cost summed** and stated as the room's collective decision.
- Each student names **one number they report that covers one shape of four.**
- Retrieval schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 5.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in IS302 with ME302. Per [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: every unmarked [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) crossing classified by shape; one instance of each of the four shapes drawn from the substrate's trace log, or a statement of which shape could not be found and what that means; every unanticipated [S071](wiki/seminars/S071.md) shadow proposal classified by shape; the observing party or an explicit none for each shape, with what would have to be added for each none **and, for each none with a same-named evaluator, what that evaluator read**; one discrimination for a silent shape with its false-alarm rate and its independence argued; the shape not being discriminated with its expected cost, provenance and confidence in the same field; **the built discrimination's results over the trace log with its misses classified**; both phase 7 outcomes; **the prompt record**; **the intervention record**; and **the frontier statement**. **Nothing else is assessed.**

**A named observing party who would not in fact have seen the failure fails**, and the test is what the trace would have shown them, not whether the role plausibly covers it. This is the day's single hard requirement and it is point 2 as a specification condition. **An evaluator named as an observing party fails under the same condition**, per point 9: an evaluator reads the record, and for a silent shape the record is the thing that carries no mark.

**A discrimination computed from the record it checks fails.** [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 4, unchanged, at the record rather than at the monitor.

**A classification defended by label rather than by observational position fails**, per point 4's scope statement.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error; a discrimination that step 7 shows finding something over real data qualifies.

**No evaluator's output is assessed and no evaluator's score is accepted as evidence.** Point 9's catalogue is perishable, and no agreement with human judgement is published for any of it; a student who reports a judged score must state that it is uncalibrated.

No entrustment judgement. The quarter's is at [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md), and [M15](wiki/modules/M15.md) carries none for the reason published on [Q8](wiki/quarters/Q8.md).

**Disposition observed:** integrity, evidenced by whether the observing parties named at step 4 are ones the trace would actually have informed, and by whether *none* was written where *none* is true. Recorded in narrative within the activity, never scored as one.

**Rule-6 discharge.** Three obligations land on this day and **all three elements exist.**

- **[S071](wiki/seminars/S071.md) named this day's phase 5 step 3** as the response occasion for its step 6-against-step 7 prediction accuracy, specifying that every shadow-run proposal the student failed to anticipate be classified by failure shape. **The element exists as step 3, it is a checked element, and the cohort's proposals sorted by shape are reported at phase 8** — which converts an accuracy figure into a statement about *which kind* of thing the cohort's boundaries permitted. The obligation is discharged.
- **[S072](wiki/seminars/S072.md) named this day's phase 3** as the response occasion for its step 2 path-bound error, specifying that the four tool-failure cases be drawn from the paths the cohort did not bound. **The element exists: phase 3's four cases are selected from the region between each student's stated bound and the realised distinct paths, and the selection rule together with the cohort's own bound errors is disclosed to students at the start of the phase**, per instrumentation rule 3. The obligation is discharged.
- **[S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) named this day's phase 5 step 1** as the response occasion for its crossing-count error, specifying that every crossing the student failed to mark be classified by the failure shape it could produce. **The element exists as step 1, it is a checked element, and the cohort's unmarked crossings sorted by shape are reported at phase 8 with the lower-bound caveat [WP-073](wiki/whitepapers/WP-073.md) §8 requires.** The obligation is discharged.

**Two older forward links discharged.** [WP-068](wiki/whitepapers/WP-068.md) §6 named this day as the one that must supply the bridge from a discrimination problem in a signal to a property of an answer, and stated that S068 could not; **that is phase 2's bridge, and its second half — that nothing about the tool's signal can be different because the tool is not the party that failed — is answered by the room rather than asserted.** [WP-033](wiki/whitepapers/WP-033.md) §6 named this day as its principal target on the claim that a tool call has S033's three outcomes plus a fourth, and [WP-033](wiki/whitepapers/WP-033.md) §9 carried its item 1 enumeration completeness here as the cohort's outcome-space baseline; **that is point 5 and phase 1 item 2, and phase 8 reports both counts together with the fourth outcome counted separately.**

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions:

- **The step 4 observation-position error rate, read as a shift against item 3** — how often a student named a party who is not in fact positioned, before the taxonomy and after it, as corrected at phase 7, split by shape. **Response occasion: [S075](wiki/seminars/S075.md), phase 3**, whose four trajectory evaluations are selected against the shape this cohort most often mis-attributed, so that the unit-of-judgement work is done where the cohort's observational reasoning was weakest. The selection rule is disclosed to students at the start of that phase.
- **The pooled step 6 undetected-shape list**, with its summed expected cost — which of the four shapes this room collectively declines to discriminate. **Response occasion: [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md), phase 5**, where each student's drift and alerting design must carry a discrimination for the shape the cohort declined here, or record that it still does not and assign the cost an owner.

**[WP-075](wiki/whitepapers/WP-075.md) §7 and [WP-079](wiki/whitepapers/WP-079.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist**, per the commitment [WP-051](wiki/whitepapers/WP-051.md) §8 established.

## Perishable content in this day

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| Four shapes, by position relative to the boundary | `tool_selection`, `tool_input_accuracy`, `tool_call_success`, `tool_output_utilization` |
| A tool reports on what happens inside it and nothing else | What each of the five evaluators reads in order to produce a score |
| Selection and output utilisation are structurally silent | An evaluator named after a silent shape, reading a record that carries no mark |
| Execution has a fourth outcome: plausible and wrong | The substrate's caching and fallback behaviour |
| A discrimination is a comparison between two records that should agree | `task_navigation_efficiency`, which takes `expected_actions` and needs no judge |
| A single tool-error rate covers one shape of four | `tool_call_success` and the dashboard it feeds |
| Thresholds are policy | A pass threshold of three on a one-to-five scale |

- **Perishable in full:** every evaluator name, required input, output format and threshold at points 9 and 10; the four cases of phase 3 and every figure in them; the substrate's current tool set, caching behaviour and default metrics; and every named framework or platform whose error taxonomy is mentioned in passing. The [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) records the decomposition as durable and point 4 is where that classification is bounded.
- **Local:** the substrate's trace log, its boundary inventory, the cohort's [S071](wiki/seminars/S071.md), [S072](wiki/seminars/S072.md) and [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) results used for case selection, the phase 6 tool-error-rate case, and the phase 6 agent case.
- **Assessed:** the left-hand column only. **The correspondence between the four shapes and four evaluator names is an instance and is not evidence**, and a student who cites the catalogue as warrant for the decomposition has made the error [WP-074](wiki/whitepapers/WP-074.md) §8 records.
