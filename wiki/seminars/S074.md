# S074 · Estate Hygiene, Triggered by a Register

## The situation

This session teaches the best single mechanism in the corpus behind this programme, then the thing it does not do, then the thing that would find what both of them miss — and then what *that* does not cover.

**The mechanism.** In a customer-embedded engineering organisation inside a large technology vendor, every environment is tagged to a project in the organisation's own project register. The register is declared the source of truth for project status. **Marking the project closed is what initiates decommissioning.** Reusing an environment after project close is named a policy violation rather than an inefficiency, explicitly because the asset is bound to a project identity.

Two further controls sit alongside it. **Access revocation is a two-party act**, performed by the project lead jointly with the departing person's own manager. And **a person's departure is treated as a security event with consequences across the organisational boundary**, triggering credential rotation inside the customer's estate, performed jointly with the customer.

That is genuinely good work, done by people who have handled incidents. It is also **a mechanism that can only fire on things somebody remembered to register**, and the whole session is what follows from that sentence.

Answer these in writing before the session opens.

1. In your engagement, what event would currently cause the environments to be removed? Name the event, not the intention.
2. Who would have to remember? What are they doing that week?
3. An environment survives its project and gets used for the next customer. Name the harm. Be specific about what crosses.
4. Somebody leaves your team on a Friday. List every act that ought to follow, and say who performs each.
5. When each of those acts has been performed, what artefact exists that says so?

**Item 5 has no answer in the source, and that is not an oversight you should be comfortable with.** The trigger is mechanised to the level of a state machine and there is no evidentiary requirement that the teardown was performed at all.

## What you must produce

A **withdrawal trigger specification** in five parts.

1. **The trigger.** The register, the field, the state change, and who is permitted to make it. It must be a state somebody other than you can observe.
2. **The consequences.** What follows automatically, and what requires a two-party act with both parties named by role.
3. **The boundary acts.** What must happen inside the customer's estate — which credentials rotate, who performs the rotation, and jointly with whom.
4. **The evidence.** For every step, the artefact it leaves: what it is, where it is written, and who could read it later to establish that the step happened.
5. **The discovery gap.** What your register cannot see, how you looked, and what the discovery tooling you used does not cover.

Parts 4 and 5 are this programme's additions and are marked as such wherever they appear.

## The material

### A register is a list of what somebody remembered

Start with the failure that makes this session necessary, because it is not the failure people expect.

A register-triggered withdrawal is excellent engineering. It converts *somebody should tear this down* into a state machine, and state machines fire whether or not anyone is having a difficult week. **What it cannot do is fire on an asset that is not in it.** An environment created before tagging was enforced, a subscription spun up during an incident, a service principal made on a Tuesday to unblock a demonstration — none of these is in the register, none will ever be triggered, and all of them will still be running in a year.

**The gap between a register and an estate is not a hygiene problem. It is an epistemic one**, and the only repair is an instrument that looks at what is actually there rather than at what was recorded.

### The organisational reason nobody triggers it

Before the tooling, the reason.

Kotter's diagnosis of failed change opens on complacency and is unhedged about it: "By far the biggest mistake people make when trying to change organizations is to plunge ahead without establishing a high enough sense of urgency in fellow managers and employees," and "This error is fatal because transformations always fail to achieve their objectives when complacency levels are high" ([research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md](/research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md)).

**Three of his named sources of complacency are exactly the mechanics of an untriggered register**, and they are measurement failures rather than character failures. Narrow functional goals, where each function meets its own targets and nobody owns the whole. Internal planning and control systems "rigged to make it easy for everyone to meet their functional goals". Performance feedback arriving only through those internal systems, so that the organisation's picture of itself is assembled from its own instruments.

**A register is an internal system that reports on itself.** It says every registered project is in a valid state, which is true, and which is silent about everything not registered. An organisation reading it will conclude that the estate is clean, and the conclusion will be sincere.

The fourth source is the sharpest and the one that predicts what will happen to a learner who runs this session's workflow: hostility to people who go and get external data — "when enterprising young employees went out of their way to collect external performance feedback, they were often treated like lepers." **The person who runs a discovery scan and produces a list of unclaimed assets is producing external feedback about an internal system, and they should expect the reception that carries.**

**Limits, and they are substantial.** *Leading Change* is practitioner counsel and says so: its own preface records that the author drew no examples or major ideas from any published source except his own writing and did not try to cite evidence to bolster his conclusions. Every case is anonymised and untraceable. Its numbers are stated with no method — including a leadership-to-management split derived by inspecting two of his own diagrams, and an urgency scale he invents in the sentence he uses it in. **This repository reproduces none of them, here or anywhere.** Take the taxonomy of complacency sources, which is observable, and take nothing quantitative.

**One correction travels with this source and must not be dropped.** An earlier version of this repository's note claimed Kotter insists on honest confrontation rather than manufactured crisis. **The book says the opposite**: it contains a section on the role of crises in which engineered crises are endorsed, with approving examples. That position is not this programme's and is not taught here — but a reader citing him for the complacency taxonomy should know what else is in the chapter.

### The instrument that looks at the estate instead of the register

Now the platform, and it is a genuinely different kind of object from a register.

**Purview's data security posture management for AI** governs what sensitive information flows into and out of AI tools; **Defender's AI security posture management** governs which AI applications exist in the estate at all ([research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md)). Read together they answer two different questions — *what is our data doing in AI systems* and *what AI systems do we even have* — **and the fact that these require separate products is itself the lesson.**

The oversharing argument is the strongest reasoning on the Purview page and the one to carry out of this session. Its statement is precise: "Because of the power and speed AI can proactively surface content that might be obsolete, over-permissioned, or lacking governance controls, generative AI amplifies the problem of oversharing data."

**Oversharing is not new. What is new is that something now reads everything.** Organisations have held over-permissioned document libraries for two decades and suffered little, because nobody read them. A retrieval system reads all of them, instantly, on behalf of anyone who asks. **Deploying a capable retrieval system converts every dormant permissions error in an organisation into a live one**, on the day it is switched on — which generalises well beyond this product and is the durable claim of this half of the session.

The response is a **data risk assessment**, whose detail view is organised as **Identify, Protect and Monitor**. That structure is worth borrowing regardless of tooling, because it separates discovery from remediation from ongoing observation. Its remediation options are concrete, and one of them carries the whole governance dilemma in a single sentence: removing a sharing link is flagged as "an action to be used sparingly because it could prevent people from accessing the item legitimately." **The safest action is the most disruptive, and somebody has to decide.**

Defender approaches the same estate from the infrastructure side, and its core artefact is a **generative AI bill of materials** — "application components, data, and AI artifacts from code to cloud" — discovered automatically and continuously across a named set of first-party and third-party model services. Its **attack path analysis** targets what the documentation calls "toxic combinations": individually acceptable configurations that compose into a breach, with the specific concern that "data might be exposed during the grounding of AI models to specific data and the fine-tuning of a pretrained model on a specific dataset." **Grounding and fine-tuning are the two moments a model touches proprietary data, and are therefore the two moments it can leak by construction rather than by attack.**

Its threat list also names an AI-native failure mode the rest of the platform documentation does not: a **wallet attack**, driving cost rather than downtime. That follows directly from usage-based billing and connects straight back to [S072](/wiki/seminars/S072.md)'s quota mechanics.

### What the discovery does not cover, which is this session's actual point

**A tool that looks at the estate is still an instrument with a resolution, and reading its output as coverage is the error this session exists to prevent.**

The documentation is unusually candid about its own operating envelope, and every element of it bounds what a scan establishes. **The default assessment runs on a schedule against a bounded number of the busiest locations rather than against all of them** — a sample, chosen by usage, not a census. **Item-level scanning is capped at a small number of locations and is not supported for one major storage surface at all.** There are **caps on items per location, above which counts are documented as possibly inaccurate.** Results arrive after a **latency measured in days rather than minutes**, custom results **expire**, and a newly configured policy produces nothing for a period after it is set.

**Read those as the resolution of the instrument.** A scan of a sample of the busiest locations, on a multi-day cadence, with caps and an unsupported surface, produces a **posture estimate and not a control**. It is far better than a register and it is not an inventory.

**This repository states those limits qualitatively and reproduces no figure from them.** The reason is the same one that governs every magnitude in this wiki: the specific caps and cadences are version-bound facts about one product at one moment, and a learner who memorises them has memorised something that will be wrong before they use it. **What survives is the shape: bounded sample, capped depth, delayed and expiring results, one surface not covered.**

Two structural facts complete the picture and both are perishability warnings. The Purview page this repository read is explicitly labelled **classic** and states it has been replaced by a newer version whose improvements will not be back-ported. And **agent-level discovery on the Defender side moves to a separate licence on a stated date**, after which the general posture product continues to discover accounts and projects but not agents. **A discovery capability that is available today may sit behind a different licence tomorrow, and a governance design that assumed it will fail silently.**

One trap is worth memorising because it is a default rather than a limit: **without an explicit policy, some first-party AI experiences audit that an interaction happened and do not capture the prompts and responses.** Knowing that an interaction occurred is not knowing what was said, and the default gives you only the former.

**The documentation's evidence base.** First-party, definitional, describing capabilities and stating operational limits — which is unusually useful. **It provides no detection rates, no false-positive rates, no measurement of how much oversharing exists in a typical estate, and no evaluation of any control's effectiveness. Assert none.** The Purview page read is superseded by a version not read, and the Defender material was read through search excerpts rather than a full page fetch, so its structure is reconstructed.

### What a teardown must leave behind, and why lineage is the same problem

Part 4 of your specification — the evidence column — is this programme's addition, and there is one adjacent literature that makes the case better than a design argument would.

**Lineage is what makes a model reproducible, comparable across runs, and deletable on request**, and recording artefacts, executions and contexts turns provenance questions into queries rather than investigations ([research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md)). The sharpest consequence is stated plainly: **a deletion request may force a retrain**, which makes lineage a precondition for meeting a data-protection obligation rather than an engineering nicety.

The same source gives the cheapest control available anywhere in this stage: **collect less.** Every field not collected is one that need not be protected, monitored, explained or deleted. And it names the end state of an estate that grew without a retrieval design — a **data swamp**, a store that has become unusable because identification was never designed.

**Read the teardown as a lineage problem.** A withdrawal that leaves no artefact per step cannot answer the only question anybody will ask afterwards, which is *did this happen?* — and reconstructing it later is the same impossible task as reconstructing lineage that was never recorded.

**Its limits:** a practitioner survey from a commercial publisher, not research; no comparative evidence for any recommended practice; three of four authors work at the vendor supplying its worked pipelines; its legal content is jurisdiction- and date-bound and is not legal advice; every number in it is a property of a particular system and none is reproduced here.

## The workflow

**Run a discovery over the estate, diff it against the register, and then write down what the discovery could not see.**

**Step 1 — write the register-derived list first, and commit to it.** From your engagement's own records: every environment, subscription, identity, endpoint and dataset you believe exists. **Write it before you scan.** A list written after a scan is a transcription and teaches nothing.

**Step 2 — run the discovery.** Use the posture tooling to enumerate what is actually present: the AI applications and services discovered, the dependencies scanned, the locations assessed. Record the scope it reports operating over, in its own terms.

**Step 3 — diff, and read the diff in both directions.** Things in the estate and not in your register are **unclaimed assets**, and each is a withdrawal trigger that will never fire. Things in your register and not in the estate are **stale entries**, and each is a reason somebody will trust the register less. Both directions matter; only the first is uncomfortable.

**Step 4 — write down what the discovery did not cover.** The scope it reported, the surfaces it does not support, the sampling rule it applied, the cadence and latency of its results, and the licence or version boundary that could remove a capability. **This step is the session.** A learner who produces a diff and stops has produced a list; a learner who produces a diff plus a coverage statement has produced a finding with a known resolution.

**Step 5 — write the specification, with evidence and coverage in it.**

```yaml
# withdrawal-trigger.yaml — the trigger is a STATE somebody else can observe,
# never a decision only your team can take.
trigger:
  register: "<the authoritative project register>"
  field: "project_status"
  state_change: "active -> closed"
  who_may_set: "<role>"          # not your team
  observable_by: "<role outside the delivery team>"

consequences:
  automatic:
    - "decommission every asset tagged to this project id"
  two_party:
    - act: "revoke delivery-team access"
      parties: ["project lead", "departing person's own manager"]
      # two roles that do not report to each other; otherwise the second signature is ceremonial
  cross_boundary:
    - act: "rotate credentials held inside the customer estate"
      performed_by: "<customer-side role>"
      jointly_with: "<our role>"
      complication: "customer-side party has attended none of the engagement"

evidence:            # ADDED BY THIS PROGRAMME. The source has no evidentiary requirement.
  - step: "decommission"
    artefact: "<what it is>"
    location: "<where a stranger could find it>"
    answers_question: "did this happen, and when"
    readable_by: "<role outside the delivery team>"

discovery_gap:       # ADDED BY THIS PROGRAMME. The register cannot see these.
  untagged_assets_found: []      # from the step-3 diff
  how_i_looked: "<the scan, its scope as it reported it, the date>"
  not_covered_by_the_scan:
    - "assessment runs against a bounded sample of the busiest locations, not a census"
    - "item-level scanning is capped and one major storage surface is unsupported"
    - "results are delayed by days and custom results expire"
    - "agent-level discovery sits behind a licence boundary that may move"
  therefore: >
    This is a posture estimate at a date, not an inventory. Absence from
    both the register and the scan is not evidence of absence from the estate.
```

**Step 6 — attack your own specification with the agent, and do not let it tidy it.** One prompt: *here is my withdrawal trigger specification and the scope statement from my discovery scan. Name every asset class that would be invisible to both. Then argue that my evidence artefacts would not, in fact, allow somebody outside my team to establish that a step happened.*

Read it for the invisible classes, not the wording. **Record what you rejected and why.** Note that the instrument has no access to your estate and is reasoning from your description of it, so it can only find gaps your own description already implies — which is a real limit and also the reason its output is worth reading, because your description is the artefact the recipient will inherit.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** The step-1 list beside the step-2 output. **You cannot argue somebody into believing their register is incomplete, and you do not have to**, because the diff is a fact about their estate that takes an afternoon to produce. The second observation is subtler and is the one worth carrying: after step 4, the learner is holding a list of unclaimed assets *and* a statement that the instrument which found them looked at a sample. **Both lists are shorter than the truth, and knowing by how much is impossible.**

## What good looks like

- The trigger is **a state, not a decision**. Somebody flipping a field is an observable event; somebody concluding that the project is finished is not.
- The specification says what happens when the state changes and nothing else has been done — because that is the case it exists for.
- **Reuse after close is stated as prohibited, with the reason given as identity rather than tidiness.** An environment carries the residue of one customer's data, configuration and access; binding it to a project identity is what stops that residue arriving at the next engagement.
- Two-party acts name two roles who do not report to each other in the way that would make the second signature ceremonial.
- The cross-boundary rotation names the customer-side party who performs it jointly, and accounts for the case where that party is a third-party supplier who has never met you.
- Every step's evidence artefact is something a person outside the delivery team could later find, and the specification says what question it answers.
- **The discovery gap is written down**, with the scan's own reported scope, the date, and what it does not cover.
- The specification states that absence from register and scan alike is not evidence of absence from the estate.

## How it goes wrong

- **The trigger that is an intention** — decommissioning begins when the team decides it is finished. *Warning sign:* your specification's first step contains a verb only your team can perform.
- **The register read as an inventory** — the list of registered assets is treated as the list of assets. *Warning sign:* you have never compared it against anything external, and it has never been wrong.
- **The scan read as coverage** — a discovery result is treated as a census. *Warning sign:* you can state what the scan found and not what it looked at.
- **The environment that outlives its project** — kept because the next piece of work starts soon and re-creating it is tedious. *Warning sign:* somebody says it would be a waste to tear it down.
- **The single-party revocation** — one person holds the whole responsibility, competing with delivery work on their busiest week. *Warning sign:* nobody would notice if it did not happen.
- **The boundary-blind departure** — your own credentials are revoked properly and the ones your engineer held inside the customer's estate are not, because they are somebody else's system. *Warning sign:* your leaver checklist stops at your own organisation's edge.
- **The unevidenced teardown** — every step is specified, none leaves a record, and a year later nobody can establish that any of it occurred. *Warning sign:* your specification is a list of instructions with no outputs.
- **The capability assumed permanent** — the governance design depends on a discovery feature that moves behind a different licence. *Warning sign:* your specification names a product capability and no fallback.

## Exercises

**None of these is assessed.** The withdrawal trigger specification is carried to [S075](/wiki/seminars/S075.md), where the trigger and the per-step evidence are assessed.

**1 · Sort six statements into register facts, estate facts and coverage claims.**

- *Every project in the register is in a valid state.* · *The scan found four AI services in this subscription.* · *We have four AI services in this subscription.* · *Item-level scanning is not supported for one of our storage surfaces.* · *No unclaimed environments exist.* · *No unclaimed environments appear in either the register or this scan, on this date.*

> **Model answers.** Register fact: the first, which is true and says nothing about anything unregistered. Estate facts, both bound to a scan and a date: the second and the sixth. Coverage claims: the fourth, which is a statement about the instrument, and the third and fifth, which are **assertions the available evidence cannot support** — the third promotes a scan result to a census, and the fifth promotes two bounded searches to a proof of absence. **The general rule: a register tells you what was recorded, a scan tells you what one bounded search found on one date, and neither tells you what exists.** Almost every governance dispute in this area is two people using sentences from different rows as though they were the same row.

**2 · Run steps 1 to 4 and produce the diff with its coverage statement.** Time step 1 and step 4 separately.

> **What you should find.** That step 1 is slower than you expected and step 4 is harder than either, because it requires reading the tooling's own operating limits rather than its output. **A learner who reports a diff with no coverage statement has performed a scan; a learner who reports both has performed an audit.** If your diff is empty in both directions, check whether you wrote step 1 after step 2 — and if you genuinely did not, record it as a real finding with the date, because an estate that matches its register is worth knowing about.

**3 · Go back to your own item 5, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 5 — what artefact exists once each act has been performed. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then write the honest version.

> **Criterion.** An evidence line passes if it names **an artefact, a location a stranger could reach, and the question it answers**, and if a person outside the delivery team could use it to establish that the step happened. *"The ticket is closed"* fails unless a reader outside the team can see the ticket and tell what closing it meant. *"A dated decommission record in the customer's own change system, showing the resource identifiers removed"* passes. **A specification whose evidence column repeats its instruction column has no evidence column.**
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**4 · Play the register.** Give your specification to a learner whose only instruction is to change the state and then do nothing else — no reminders, no chase, no goodwill.

> **Scoring criterion.** Whatever your specification says happens automatically is all that happens; everything else stalls until you name the person who performs it and show that the specification would reach them. **What stalls is the finding.** A pass names the first thing that stalled and rewrites that step so it either fires from the state change or names a reachable person. A specification where nothing stalls has either been written very well or has never been read literally, and the second is more common.

**5 · Find the three traps in the supplied situation.** [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md) contains three and you should find all three.

> **What you should find.** One environment tagged to the project in the operator's asset register and **a second created before tagging was enforced, tagged to nothing** — an untagged asset is invisible to a register-driven trigger, and **a mechanism that fires reliably on tagged assets and silently on untagged ones is worse than one nobody trusts**, because it produces confidence. A credential issued to a named delivery engineer holding write access inside the customer's estate, whose rotation is a joint act with a managed-service supplier who has attended none of the engagement — name how that act gets performed by somebody who does not know you. And the absence the whole session turns on: **no step anywhere leaves an artefact.**

**6 · State the harm out loud.** Take the environment that survives its project and gets reused for the next customer. Name what crosses, specifically.

> **What to notice.** That the honest answer is not *data* in the abstract. It is configuration, cached credentials, network paths, service principals, log content, and — in an AI system — grounding sources and any fine-tuning corpus, which the posture documentation names as the two moments a model touches proprietary data. **Binding an asset to a project identity is what stops that residue arriving at the next engagement**, and stating the harm concretely is what stops *it would be a waste to tear it down* from sounding reasonable.

## What this does not establish

**That register-triggered withdrawal works.** The corpus this mechanism comes from **reports no outcome for it at all** — no incident it prevented, no environment it caught, no measure of compliance, and no case study in which it was applied. It is documented practice from one organisation, taught here as a specification that organisation publishes internally, not as a finding.

**And it is the wrong half of the problem.** The mechanism answers *is our house clean?* It does not answer *can they run it without us?* Nothing in the corpus describes transferring the running system, its definitions, its pipelines, its runbooks, its harness, its cost model or its monitoring to the customer; there is no named receiving owner, no exit criteria, no acceptance and no support taper anywhere in it.

A team can execute everything in this session to a high standard and leave behind a system nobody can operate. **That is not a hypothetical failure mode. It is the default one, and it is what the rest of this stage exists to address.**

**No detection rate and no false-positive rate is published for any posture or discovery capability named here, and none is asserted.** The documentation supplies no measurement of how much oversharing exists in a typical estate and no evaluation of any control's effectiveness. That bounds what you may claim and is not evidence that the tooling is poor.

**The discovery covers a sample of the estate rather than all of it**, on a delayed cadence, with capped depth and at least one unsupported surface — and this repository states those bounds qualitatively and reproduces no figure from them, because each is a version-bound fact about one product at one moment. **A scan is a posture estimate at a date. Absence from it is not absence from the estate.**

**The organisational diagnosis is practitioner counsel with no evidence base.** Its author records that he drew no major ideas from any published source except his own writing and did not attempt to cite evidence; every case in it is anonymised and untraceable; and every figure in it is stated without method and is reproduced nowhere in this repository. **It also endorses engineering artificial crises, which this programme does not teach.**

**The evidence column and the discovery-gap section are this design's inventions.** They are built from what the source omits, and no source establishes that recording either improves anything.
