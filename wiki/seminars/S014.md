## The situation

Forty minutes in. One person has answered every question. One has not spoken. Two are having their own conversation at the end of the table. The person whose environment you needed for the second exercise cannot get it working, and somebody has just asked a question you cannot answer.

You will handle some of this badly. The session is about which parts you are allowed to handle badly.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. Somebody says: *we tried this three years ago and it did not work.* What are your next two sentences?
2. A participant has not spoken for an hour. Write down what you will do. Now write what would happen if the reason is that their manager is in the room.
3. You cannot answer a question. What are your options, and which of them costs you least later?
4. You are running behind. Name the thing you will cut, and say who decides.
5. Which of the situations above would you rather handle badly than handle by breaking a rule?

**Item 5 has an answer and most people do not have it yet.** By the end of the session you should.

## What you must produce

A **rehearsal record** covering three assigned situations. For each one:

- The situation as it appeared, in your own words.
- What you did.
- **Which prohibition you were closest to breaking**, and whether you broke it.
- What the situation was evidence of, if anything, about the customer rather than about the room.

The record is marked against the prohibitions. **It is not marked against whether the room recovered.**

## What good looks like

- The sceptic is **recruited, not converted.** You validated the history, asked what was different last time, asked what would need to be true now, and then asked them to be the person who explains the obstacles. You did not argue.
- Silence is treated as **information about the conditions**, not as a property of the silent person. You changed something about the room before you changed anything about them.
- The question you could not answer is parked visibly, with an owner and a date, and you did not guess.
- Running behind produced a **stated cut with a named decider**, rather than a compression that quietly removed the part where the customer talks.
- Your record contains at least one situation you handled badly while keeping the prohibition. **That is a pass.** The prohibitions exist for the moments when your judgement is degraded, which is exactly when you will most want to override them.

## How it goes wrong

- **Marking yourself on recovery** — you judge the handling by whether the room came back, so any intervention that worked is retrospectively correct. *Warning sign:* your account of the moment contains the word "eventually" and no account of what you were tempted to do.
- **Converting the sceptic** — you answer their objection, well, with evidence. They stop objecting. They also stop telling you what will actually block this. *Warning sign:* the room got easier and you learned nothing new.
- **Reading silence as agreement** — nobody objected, so the proposal is accepted. *Warning sign:* your notes record consensus and cannot name who expressed it.
- **Answering the unanswerable** — you produce a plausible answer to a question outside your knowledge, because not knowing feels like a failure of the competence you were selected for. *Warning sign:* you are speaking in the conditional and nobody has written anything down.
- **The silent cut** — you are behind, so you shorten the exercise where the customer works and keep the part where you present. *Warning sign:* the schedule slipped and your own speaking time did not.
- **The dominating participant as the customer** — one voice answers everything, and your notes become that person's view of their organisation. *Warning sign:* your consolidated finding at [S018](/wiki/seminars/S018.md) traces to one informant.

## Do it

The rehearsal is the session. Situations are assigned, not chosen, and are drawn from the ordinary catastrophes: the person who answers everything, the person who answers nothing, the sceptic with history, the private conversation, the failed environment, the disagreement between two participants who both report to somebody present, the drift, the overrun, the late arrival, the exercise that does not land, and the question you cannot answer.

Run each for a few minutes. **The observer marks against the prohibitions only.**

Then, on the silence: before you decide what to do about a person who is not speaking, list what it might cost them to speak. Withholding at work is usually not shyness. It is a rapid and not fully conscious calculation in which staying quiet has an immediate and certain benefit and speaking has a delayed and uncertain one. That asymmetry does not need a difficult person to produce it, and it does not announce itself — **a moment where somebody could have spoken and did not leaves no trace except in the person who held back.**

So the intervention that has any chance of working is a change to the conditions rather than an appeal to the individual. Ask a question that has a factual answer they own. Ask it of them by name and by expertise. Take the manager out of the segment. Split the room. **Then see whether the silence was about them.**

Finally, the corpus attaches a claim to the sceptic entry: that sceptics who feel heard often become the practice's strongest advocates. **That is a practitioner belief with no evidence behind it, and it is a comfortable one to hold**, because it converts a person who is telling you something inconvenient into a future asset. Write down what you would have to observe for it to be false.

## Show it

Read one rehearsal record aloud — the one where you handled it worst.

The room's only question is: **did you break a prohibition?** Not whether you recovered. If the answer is no, that record is the strongest thing in your file.

## What this does not establish

Nothing here shows that any prescribed handling produces a better session than any other.

The catalogue this session teaches is **codified experience with no evaluation attached.** No handling in the corpus is reported to have been tried and compared. The prohibitions are the most valuable part precisely because they are the part that cannot be justified by a good outcome, and they are also the part with the least support.

**The claim about silence is class 2** — direction and mechanism only, from a trade book read in full for this repository, whose underlying empirical paper has not been read here. No magnitude is asserted. The claim that the psychological conditions for speaking are a property of the group rather than of the organisation comes from that same book.

The sceptic-becomes-advocate claim is **unwarranted and is taught as a specimen of a convenient belief**, not as a technique.
