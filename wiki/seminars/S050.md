# S050 · Saying What the Measurement Does Not License ⊘

**Module:** [M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) · **Quarter:** [Q5](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** ME · PE
**Whitepaper:** [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md)
**Depends on:** S046, S048, S049
**Re-tests:** S024, S030, S047
**Platform anchor:** the student's own Foundry evaluation report, and the uncalibrated clause every judged score in it must carry · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server (preview) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

⊘ **Assessment-bearing day, the quarter's second entrustment judgement, and the closest precursor yet to EA-3.**

---

## What this day is for

The criterion is built, validated, judged and bounded. Four days of work have produced a finding and an honest interval around it.

Today somebody wants it to say more than it says.

Not out of malice. The engineer who built the system wants it to mean *it works*. The product owner with a date wants it to mean *ship*. The person who will use the thing wants it to mean *this is safe for me* — and that want is entirely legitimate and cannot be satisfied. The executive who will quote one sentence wants one sentence, and will have one whether you supply a good one or not.

**The target is not honesty. Every student here is honest.** The target is the harder thing: saying what a measurement does not license, in language the audience can act on, to a person with an interest in a different answer — and doing it four times, when the pressure is different each time and the finding is not.

**One sentence in the finding does more work than the rest of it, and every student will be arguing about that sentence by mid-morning.** The judged scores in their evaluation were produced by evaluators whose agreement with human judgement is not published — not badly, not partially, but not at all. So every one of those scores is uncalibrated, and the student has to say so, four times, to four people who each have a reason to prefer that they did not. Audience A will point out, correctly, that the score came back from a real service that a large company operates and documents. That is true. It is also not evidence about agreement with a human, and the difference between those two sentences is what the day is for.

---

## The entrustment judgement, stated before phase 1

> Today's judgement is on: **state what an evaluation does not license, to a party who wants it to license more.** It is the closest precursor the programme has to the third entrusted activity, EA-3, and it is not that activity — EA-3 is a risk communication to a non-technical decision-maker on a live decision, and today's audiences are constructed.
>
> Two assessors judge independently before conferring, and **form their level from your artefact and your four encounters before the defence**, per rater discipline rule 7. The expected level is **2b**.
>
> **The S030, S040 and S045 judgements are not carried forward.** Each occasion is judged on its own.
>
> **Two failures are named in advance, and they are symmetric.** Letting a claim stand stronger than your evidence supports is a failure. Stating a limitation in language the audience cannot act on is also a failure — and it is the one this cohort is at risk of, because it looks like rigour and is rewarded everywhere else.

---

## A stated departure from the design pattern

This is an assessment-bearing day, and per the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](/wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) phases 5 to 7 are replaced by an entrustment occasion. The [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s six additions are therefore carried differently, and the differences are named rather than left implicit, following the precedent set at [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md).

**There is no build, and the three records are carried into the defence instead.** The prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement from the four-day chain — S046's criterion, S047's check, S048's characterisation, S049's interval — are brought to the day complete and are read by the assessors alongside the artefact. They are not re-produced today, because today produces a statement rather than a system.

**Phase 3 is not four contrasting cases.** It is the entrusted activity. The requirement that phase 3's cases be executed rather than read applies where the day's subject permits, and this day's subject does not. **The requirement is discharged at phase 6 instead**, after the judgement is formed and the assessors have left, where the student's own finding is recomputed under four configurations they were free to change and did not.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items, sealed and opened at phase 8.

1. **Write your finding as one sentence,** in the form you would actually send it.
2. What in that sentence will your audience want to be stronger?
3. **Which part of it are you most likely to soften under pressure, and to whom?**
4. Predict your supervision level.
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** After the encounters you will ask the agent to *write the one sentence we should give the executive.* Write down now what it will produce, and **name the one thing it will get wrong**.

Item 3 is the day's honest item and the one phase 4 returns to.

Item 5 is the day's contribution to the running frontier map and is placed here because it must be written before the student knows how hard audience D is. The design's expectation is that the agent will produce a clean, confident, genuinely quotable sentence with the uncertainty removed — not falsified, removed — because a sentence built to survive quotation and a sentence built to carry its own limits are different objects, and only one of them was asked for.

**Warrant.** Pretesting the position before the pressure arrives, so movement under pressure is measurable rather than remembered. Item 3 asks for a self-prediction that is uncomfortable to write and is checked against observed behaviour four hours later, which is the only construction in the programme that catches softening at the moment it happens. Item 5 is administered here and may not migrate, per instrumentation rule 8.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative module retrieval (30 min)

Free recall, no notes, then paired reconstruction.

- The [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) traceability requirement, and what a criterion nobody recognises indicates.
- The [S047](/wiki/seminars/S047.md) three drift mechanisms.
- The [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md) judge error structure, and why agreement is not accuracy.
- **The uncalibrated clause:** state it from memory, in the form you will use today — and say what would have to be published for it to stop being required.
- The [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) five things owed to a reader.
- **The [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md) duty to say so:** what it required, and to whom.
- **The [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md) formulation:** what a measurement does and does not establish.
- **Bridge:** [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md)'s step 7 asked what a number does not license, and you answered it on paper. What does today add?

The answer wanted for the bridge is: **an audience with an interest.** S028's step 7 is this day's direct ancestor at class 2, and the difference is not the reasoning but the room.

**Warrant.** Cumulative retrieval across the module and across two earlier modules, closing with a bridge that names the day's own lineage. Students who see that they have done the epistemic half of this before are freed to spend the day on the part that is new. The uncalibrated-clause item is retrieved here rather than taught, because it is the sentence four audiences will contest and a student reconstructing it under time pressure is in the state they will be in at eleven o'clock.

## Phase 3 · The four audiences (60 min)

**The entrusted activity. Assessors observe and do not intervene.**

Each student delivers **their own finding** — the interval from S049, on the criterion from S046, judged by the judge from S048 — four times. Twelve minutes each, three minutes to reset.

- **Audience A · The engineer who built the evaluated system.** Wants the finding to mean *it works*. Argues technically, competently, and in good faith. **Scripted to press specifically on the uncalibrated clause:** the score came from a documented service, the evaluator has a published definition, the threshold is the vendor's own, and the student is the only person in the room describing the result as uncalibrated.
- **Audience B · The product owner with a date.** Wants it to mean *ship*. Will accept a conditional, and will treat the condition as satisfied.
- **Audience C · The person who will use the system.** Wants it to mean *this is safe for me*. **This want is legitimate, and no honest formulation satisfies it.**
- **Audience D · Someone who will quote one sentence.** Wants a sentence. One sentence will survive the conversation whatever the student does.

The order is fixed. **D is last** because it is the hardest and because the student must arrive at it having already discovered, at C, that some wants cannot be met.

Audiences are played by trained role-players working from scripts, not by peers.

**Warrant.** The task-class variability plan for M10 varies the audience: same finding, four parties, four different pressures. The variability is what makes the day a test of a transferable capability rather than of one rehearsed delivery. Audience C exists because the programme's other communication days all have a formulation that works, and this one does not — a student whose repertoire is *find the wording that satisfies them* meets a case where the honest answer is that nothing will. **Audience A's added pressure is the day's platform content and is deliberately the first encounter**: it is the only one where the student is contradicted by an artefact rather than by a want, and a student who concedes calibration to a vendor's authority in the first twelve minutes has nothing left to defend in the next thirty-six.

---

## Phase 4 · The defence (60 min)

Assessors have formed a level before this phase begins, **from the artefact, the three records carried in from S046 to S049, and the four encounters.** The defence tests it; it does not create it.

- Which audience did you soften for?
- What did you say to C that you did not say to A? Why the difference?
- If D quotes the weakest sentence you said, what happens?
- **Audience A said the score came from a real service. What is the difference between that and a calibrated score, in one sentence you would be willing to send?**
- **Your intervention record says you corrected the agent here. What would have happened if you had not?**
- What would it have taken to make you say something you do not believe?
- **Name the encounter in which you were least honest.**

The last is asked of every student. **Declining to name one is recorded as an answer**, not as a refusal, and is discussed rather than penalised — but it is a datum about calibration and the assessors treat it as one.

**Warrant.** Defence-after-artefact, per rater discipline rule 7. The questions are diagnostic rather than evaluative: each is designed to surface a discrimination the four encounters may have made invisible, particularly the A-versus-C difference, which is the difference between adjusting vocabulary and adjusting substance. The two added questions are the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s discharge on an assessment-bearing day: the platform question tests whether the student can distinguish an authoritative source from a calibrated one under contradiction, and the intervention question tests whether a recorded correction was understood or merely logged.

---

## Phase 5 · The prediction check (35 min)

**[S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md) committed that this day would report what the deployed models actually did.** That commitment is discharged here.

Each student's model has been running for five weeks. Their S045 failure-mode statements are retrieved and sorted into three categories:

1. **Failure modes stated, which occurred** — roughly as described.
2. **Failure modes stated, which did not occur.**
3. **Failures that occurred and were not stated.**

Individual results are given to each student privately. The cohort sees the aggregate.

**No assessment attaches to this result, and that is stated before S045 as well as here.** A student whose category 3 is large is not marked down. If the check were assessed, S045's failure-mode statements would become strategic — students would state every conceivable failure to avoid category 3 — and the statement the programme actually wants, which is a considered prediction, would stop being produced.

**Warrant.** The programme's third long-horizon prediction check, after S020 and S040, and the first whose subject is the student's own claim about their own artefact under real use. Category 3 is the payload: it is the only measurement in the programme of what a student did not think to say, which is a different and larger quantity than what they said wrongly. The explicit decoupling from assessment is a design commitment defended in the whitepaper.

## Phase 6 · Consolidating instruction (35 min) — assessors absent

**The four runs, first.** Each student's own finding is recomputed under four configurations, from a harness prepared against their S049 artefact. The system is unchanged, the responses are unchanged, and nobody collected new data.

| Run | What is changed | What moves |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The conversation split, from the last turn to the full conversation | What counts as the response |
| 2 | The evaluation level, from turn to conversation | The number of rows, and therefore n |
| 3 | The pass threshold, from 3 to 4 | The pass rate, with no score changed |
| 4 | The judge model, to another the documentation also recommends | The scores themselves |

Every one of those four was a decision the student was free to make and did not, because each has a default. **The student has just spent an hour defending one of the numbers this table contains against four people, without having established, to any of them, which of the four it was.** Nothing here invalidates the finding. It relocates it: the finding is a claim about a system under a configuration, and the configuration was chosen partly by the student and partly by a vendor.

Then the seven points.

1. **A limitation stated in language the audience cannot act on has not been stated.** The audience's vocabulary is part of the claim, not packaging around it.
2. **The two failures are symmetric,** and only one of them is punished socially. Overclaiming is recognised as a fault. Unusable hedging is recognised as rigour, and it fails the same duty.
3. **Selective true reporting.** Every true statement is selected from a set of true statements, and the set you did not say is part of what you communicated. This is the epistemic half of the question [S013](/wiki/seminars/S013.md) phase 7 left open. **The ethical half — when selection becomes deception — is not answered today and returns at S088.**
4. **You cannot control the surviving sentence; you can control the set.** If one sentence will be quoted, ensure the most quotable sentence available is one you are willing to have quoted.
5. **Some wants cannot be met.** Audience C wants an assurance the evidence cannot support. Saying so, plainly, is the answer — and it is an answer, not a failure to find one.
6. **[S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md)'s duty generalises.** It was stated for intractability: when the answer is that it cannot be done, you say so. It applies wherever the honest answer is worse than the expected one, which is most of the cases that matter.
7. **The five things owed to a reader (S049 point 8) are owed to every audience,** and none of the four had them unless the student supplied them. **The four runs add a sixth for a judged finding: the configuration the number was produced under, including the parts of it nobody chose.** That sixth item is perishable in its content and not in its existence — the parameter names will change and the obligation will not.

**Warrant.** Consolidation placed after the performance rather than before, so that every point indexes an encounter the student has just had. Point 2 is the day's most-needed correction and is the one this cohort is least prepared to hear. Point 3 discharges half of a question left open thirty-seven days ago and names where the other half goes, per the deferral register in the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md). **The four runs discharge the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s executed-cases requirement on a day whose phase 3 is an assessment**, and they are placed after the judgement and with assessors absent so that a student whose finding moves is not penalised for having discovered it.

## Phase 7 · Error clinic (40 min) — assessors absent

Instructor first: a substrate case in which a limitation *was* stated, in writing, in the report, and the decision was made as though it had not been. Attention is directed at where it was stated — a section the decision-maker did not read — and at the fact that the engineer who wrote it believed they had discharged the duty.

Then the agent case. A student asked Copilot to *write the evaluation summary for the stakeholders*. It produced a good one: the pass rate, the sample size, the evaluator by name, the threshold, and the interval. **Every sentence was true and the summary was read as a clean result.** It did not say that the threshold was the vendor's default, that the evaluator's agreement with human judgement is unpublished, or that the sample count counted rows. None of those is a fact the summary got wrong; each is a fact the summary did not contain, and the summary was assembled by a system optimising for a clear report rather than for a checkable one. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **there was nothing to catch.** The reviewer's method — read each sentence and ask whether it is true — cannot detect an omission, and point 3 is the only tool in the day that can.

Then students, in threes: **a time you told someone something true and they did not act on it.** What did you conclude about them, and what else could explain it?

**Warrant.** Error management targeting point 1. The instructor's case is chosen because it is not a failure of honesty at all — the statement existed and was correct — which is the only way to make the placement question feel like part of the duty rather than a presentational nicety. The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is deliberately an omission rather than an error, because selective true reporting is the one failure in this programme that an agent produces by default and a line-by-line review cannot see.

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- Sealed items opened. **Item 3 — what you predicted you would soften, and to whom — against phase 4's named encounter.** The room's agreement rate is reported.
- **Item 5 opened.** The agent is asked, now, for the one sentence to give the executive, and the sealed predictions are read against what it produced. The cohort's hit rate is recorded in two bands: predicted *some* error, and named *the removed uncertainty*. **The sentence the agent produced is then compared with the sentence each student actually gave audience D**, and the room is asked which of the two they would rather have quoted.
- Predicted supervision level against awarded. Distribution shown without names.
- Phase 5's three categories in aggregate, with category 3's cohort total stated plainly.
- **The [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) psychological-safety instrument, sixth administration.** The full series across five quarters is shown to the cohort, per the instrument's own adoption conditions.
- Each student writes **one sentence they will not say again**, and keeps it.
- Retrieval schedule: next morning, day 3, week 3, week 8.

## Assessment

**Entrustment judgement.** Activity: *state what an evaluation does not license, to a party who wants it to license more.* Ordinal level from the scale in [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md): 1, 2a, 2b, 3a, 3b, 3c, 4a, 4b. **Expected 2b; 3a is available and will be rare.**

**Narrative substantiation is mandatory** and must cite specific moments from the encounters, not impressions of them.

Per rule 7, the level is formed from the artefact and the four encounters, and the defence is used to test it. **The three records — prompt, intervention and frontier statement, carried in from S046 to S049 — are read as part of the artefact**, because this day has no build of its own and they are the only evidence available of how the finding was made.

**Named failure conditions.** A student who allows the finding to stand stronger than the interval supports, in any of the four encounters, is below 2b regardless of the other three. A student whose four encounters are the same words four times has not performed the activity. A student who satisfies audience C is either mistaken or has changed the finding, and the assessors check which. **A student who withdraws the uncalibrated clause under audience A's pressure has allowed the finding to stand stronger than the evidence supports, and is covered by the first condition rather than by a new one** — the clause is part of the finding, not a caveat attached to it.

**One specification item**, in ME201, jointly with PE201. Per rule 1, the checked elements are: the finding as delivered, **carrying the uncalibrated clause for every judged score in it**; for each of the four audiences, what they wanted it to say and what was said instead; the phase 4 self-identified least-honest encounter or an explicit declining; **the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement carried in from the four preceding days**; and one sentence the student will not say again. **Nothing else is assessed.**

**Phase 5's result is not assessed.** Stated in this specification so that the decoupling is a written commitment and not a practice.

**Phase 6's four runs are not assessed**, and the assessors do not receive them. A finding that moves under reconfiguration is a fact about evaluation and not a fault of the student who defended it.

**Error-clinic firewall.** Assessors are absent for phases 6 and 7 and receive no account of them.

**Token economy.** This day is within the quarter's individual allocation; a deferred attempt costs one token and is re-run with a different finding, not the same one.

## Perishable content in this day

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| A limitation stated in language the audience cannot act on has not been stated | The uncalibrated clause, and whether four people could act on it |
| Overclaiming and unusable hedging fail the same duty | — nothing platform-specific |
| A score's provenance is not its calibration | An authoritative service that publishes no agreement data |
| A finding is a claim about a system under a configuration | Split strategy, evaluation level, threshold, judge model |
| Selection is communication | An accurate summary that omits its own defaults |

- **Durable:** effectively the whole day. The symmetric failures, selective true reporting, the unmeetable want, the surviving sentence, the placement duty, and the generalised duty to say so.
- **Perishable:** the four reconfigurations at phase 6, the parameter names they use, and the fact that no evaluator agreement data exists. **If that data is published, phase 6's run 4 changes and the uncalibrated clause is retired for the evaluators it covers — and nothing else in the day moves.**
- **Local:** the four audience scripts, the substrate's own findings, and the psychological-safety series.
