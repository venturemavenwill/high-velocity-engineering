## The situation

This is the stage's second assessment occasion and it does not assess the same thing as [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md).

There, the boundary of the increment was marked. **Here the boundary of your understanding is marked**, and the standing prohibition from the source material is the spine of the whole occasion:

**Use the tooling to make each step better, not to skip steps.**

The assessment asks you to say which steps it made better and which ones it let you skip. **Both answers are expected. A candidate with no skipped steps has not audited, and a candidate with no improved steps has not delegated.**

And every claim you make will be conditioned on a pin — commit, package version, host, model, date. That is not bureaucracy. **Pinning does not slow the decay of anything you observed. It makes the decay visible**, which is the only property available to you, because the alternative is a claim about a moving object that will read as true long after it has stopped being true. A defence with no pin is not a weaker defence; it is a different kind of statement, and one nobody can check.

**Answer these in writing before you begin, and submit them.**

1. Which step of your process did the tooling genuinely make better, and what specifically got better?
2. Which step did it let you skip, and what would show whether the skip cost anything?
3. Which region of your increment can you not explain, and what is it doing in a shipped build?
4. What did you configure that changed what the executor produced, and how do you know it was the configuration?
5. What in your account would stop being true if the pin moved?

**Item 4 is the one candidates get wrong.** A configuration you wrote and an output you liked is not a causal chain, and the honest answer is often *I do not know, because I changed it and never observed the version without it.*

## What you must produce

A **build defence**, presented to an assessor who has read your [S045](/wiki/seminars/S045.md) increment pack and your [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) instruction set and nothing else.

1. **The increment**, and the pin under which it was produced: commit, package version, host, model and date.
2. **The instruction set**, with each constraint's conformance mode.
3. **The plan**, with identifiers that resolve into the implementation record.
4. **The comprehension audit** from [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md), with its opaque regions still classified opaque.
5. **The confirmation record**, including standing permissions.
6. **The inexplicability register** from [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md), with its stopping rules and its silence note.
7. **The check inventory**, with every entry labelled advisory or authoritative and each label's basis.
8. **The improved-and-skipped ledger**: which steps the tooling improved, which it let you skip, and what evidence bears on each.
9. **The cross-check record**: for each platform claim in the defence, the page and the date it was read, **and what a second first-party surface said about the same thing** — or the statement that you looked for one and found none.

## The material

### The pin, and what it is actually for

Every platform observation in this module has been recorded with five things attached: commit, package version, host, model, date. This occasion is where that discipline is defended rather than followed.

The instruction comes from the corpus this module is built on, and it is unusually explicit for a source describing itself: **pin the revision, never teach it live, record commit, package version, host, model, enabled collections and date, and treat any of those changing as a re-verification trigger** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)). The reason is not tidiness. **Perishability in that corpus is extreme** — file counts, paths, names and package version all changed during the period the reading describes — and only the mechanism layer is quotable without a date.

Two further statements from the same note decide how a defence must be worded. **Host and model dependence is total**: every artefact is inert without a host that loads it, and **a result observed on one client and model is not a result about the corpus.** And, on the question of what an absence proves: **absence at one commit is not proof of absence in the project.** Both are limits on what you may say, and both are the kind of limit candidates drop under questioning.

**So the pin is a scope declaration, not a citation format.** It says: this claim is about this thing at this moment on this equipment. **A claim without it is not more confident; it is unbounded, and an unbounded claim cannot be checked and therefore cannot be defended.** The name for what a pin buys is visibility of decay — you can see, from the pin alone, that a term has passed and a version has moved, and you can tell which of your conclusions that touches.

### A citation of a moving target requires a pin, and the openly published handbook shows what happens without one

The handbook this programme reads is a live, openly published, contributable site — and it has no single date. **Its pages carry individual footer dates spread across several years**, roughly a fifth of it is stubs, link lists or tool catalogues, and **it contradicts itself in at least a dozen places without noticing** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md), pages read 2026-07-30). Its branch-naming convention differs across three pages. Its trade-study template disagrees with the page that introduces it. Its testing comparison marks one test type manual while three other pages assume it automated.

**None of those are errors of fact. They are the normal residue of a document written by many hands over several years with no reconciliation pass** — and the practical consequence is the citation rule this repository uses: **cite that handbook by page and by the date the page was read, never as a whole.** A defence that cites it as a whole has cited something that does not exist.

The same source is also the only place in this repository's reading where an engineering organisation has written down what to do about generated code in a commit, and its answer is deliberately narrow. In the **agentic** case the coding agent is recorded as the commit author and the supervising human is recorded with a co-authorship trailer. In the **assistant** case it recommends a team convention using **a deliberately different trailer key**, on the stated grounds that the co-authorship field expects an address and that **authorship should not be confused with assistance.** It gives three triggers for applying the convention, the first being that more than half the lines in the commit were generated.

**That is bookkeeping and it is useful as bookkeeping.** It records who supervised. It records nothing about whether anybody understood, which is what this occasion marks — and the distinction between provenance and comprehension is the one your defence must hold.

**The handbook asserts four unsupported magnitude claims, one of which states a cost outcome with no evidence, no baseline and no source. This repository records all four as specimens and reproduces none of them, and neither may your defence.**

### Four surfaces, three positions, one day

Everything above is an argument for the pin. **This is the demonstration, and it is not hypothetical, not old, and not a third party's mistake.** On **2026-07-31**, four first-party surfaces described the same file — the declarative definition of an agent, the artefact [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) had you put under review — in three incompatible ways, all reachable in one sitting ([research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-agent-build-and-runtime.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-agent-build-and-runtime.md)).

**The schema reference for that file opens by declaring it deprecated.** Agent manifests and standalone agent definitions are superseded; as of a named tooling version all hosted-agent configuration lives in a single consolidated project file; and the schema model the page then documents at length is, in its own words, retained for reference and superseded. **The page documents in detail a thing its first paragraph says you should not use.**

**The command-line development page agrees, from the other side.** It states that there is **no standalone definition or manifest file** in the current hosted-agent project model, and describes the consolidated file instead.

**A third page disagrees, without noticing.** Documenting deployment to the platform's own endpoint, it still describes the definition file as the file the tooling deploys, states that the tooling validates the file's kind and rejects any other value, and notes that the published specification is broader than what deployment actually accepts — a fourth position embedded inside the third.

**And a locally installed first-party authoring skill, at a stated version, still lists the definition file in its own description as something it helps you write.**

**Nothing in that is a factual error, and that is the whole point.** The pages are at different points on a migration, which is the normal condition of a platform documenting itself while changing underneath its own documentation. **The consequence is not normal at all.** A practitioner who reads any one of those surfaces and generalises has learned something false — and **no amount of care in reading that page would have caught it.** Diligence within a source cannot detect a disagreement between sources. **Only pinning and cross-checking can**, which is exactly why this occasion assesses the pin rather than the platform knowledge.

**So the minimum defensible form of a platform citation is longer than most candidates expect.** Not *the documentation says X*. Not even *this page said X on this date*. **This page said X on this date, and I checked these other first-party surfaces, and here is what they said.** A citation that silently resolves a contradiction it did not disclose has made a judgement and hidden it, which is a worse failure than being wrong in public.

The module's existing rule has a twin, and this specimen is where it arrives. [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) carried the corpus's own limit that **absence at one commit is not proof of absence in the project.** Its mirror is the sentence this session adds: **presence on one page is not proof of currency in the product.** Both are limits on what a single reading entitles you to say, and candidates drop the second one faster than the first, because a rendering page feels like a fact in a way a missing file does not.

**The limits on this specimen are heavy and they matter more here than usual, because the specimen is about limits.** The four surfaces were read at **mixed depth**: the schema reference was fetched and read in full; two were reached only through search-returned excerpts with tables frequently truncated, **so their page structure is reconstructed rather than confirmed**; and one is a locally installed artefact with **no public address, no currency guarantee and no citation available except its version.** **The contradiction is unresolved in the research note and must not be resolved by guessing** — the instruction carried forward is to verify against the specific tooling version you are running before writing a line of that file. **That note is the most perishable material in this repository and it perished visibly while being read**; several of its consulted pages are explicitly superseded and still render; preview status is pervasive; it is **definitional and not evidential**, and every number in it is a dated platform parameter reproduced nowhere here.

### What an oral defence is for, and why the artefact stops being the evidence

Once a grade is earned by explaining the work, the question of how the work was produced stops being an enforcement problem.

The clearest published statement of this is a long-running course that permits its AI tooling almost without restriction — recommending it, in fact — and carries the weight in two clauses instead. **You are solely responsible for the correctness of the solution.** And: **using code generation tools without understanding the generated code will likely create challenges when answering questions** ([research/08-assessment-epas/cmu-oral-defense-and-token-economy-patterns.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/cmu-oral-defense-and-token-economy-patterns.md)). The same course permits collaboration on its practical work **including outright copying**, for exactly one reason: *you will have to present and explain your solution on your own.* **Once the assessment is oral, the artefact stops being the evidence, and policing its provenance stops being necessary.**

That is why this occasion does not mark how much of your increment was generated, and it is why it can afford not to.

**Its limits:** this is course policy documentation from an offering that has run for years, not a study of assessment. **No effectiveness data is published — no grade distributions, no comparison against a prior examination-based version, and no measurement of whether oral verification detects understanding that written work misses.** It is well-tested practice, which is a real form of evidence, and it is not an evaluated intervention.

### The what-if question, and why the assessor chooses the region

The assessment literature this programme uses supplies the shape of the conversation and it is more specific than *ask them about their work*.

An **entrustment-based discussion** is a short, focused conversation after the activity that should probe deep understanding of what was done, **but should include what-if questions**, to establish whether the candidate would know what to do in unexpected or unfamiliar situations ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). The reason the counterfactual half is mandatory rather than decorative is stated plainly: **an entrustment decision implies the acceptance of risk, as it can never be fully predicted what the candidate will encounter.** Retrospective explanation shows they understood the case they met. **Counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases they did not.**

The same source names three professional characteristics in behavioural terms, and all three transfer to engineering with almost no modification: **integrity** — truthfulness in reports to a supervisor; **reliability** — stable, predictable, conscientious working; and **humility** — the ability to observe one's own limitations and the willingness to ask for help. **The opaque class in your audit is the humility item made into an artefact**, which is why an empty opaque class is treated as a defect here rather than as an achievement.

**Its limits:** a clinical model, from a domain with patients, legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint. It is guidance from the concept's originator and reports adoption rather than effectiveness. **The full evidence standard — multiple trained observers producing documented, deliberated summative judgements — is expensive, and this occasion runs a reduced version and says so.**

### The assessor is an instrument too, and a poorly characterised one

This is the part of the occasion the design is least comfortable with and states most plainly.

Studies of how assessors actually judge accumulated evidence find something uncomfortable for anybody designing a rubric. **Assessors relied mainly on narrative feedback to form their judgement, and used grades and qualifications merely to confirm impressions already formed from the prose** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). They reached judgement through a cyclical process of acquiring, organising and integrating evidence — and then, having formed a view, **were inclined to stick to their initial judgements even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence.** Final judgements were more elaborate than initial ones and not substantially different.

Worse for a specification's authority: **assessors reaching similar outcomes differed in their reasoning**, driven by different beliefs about which evidence is trustworthy, different implicit theories of what competence is, and different inferences from the same piece of evidence. The rater literature behind it reports that **idiosyncratic rater effects account for a substantial share of variance in performance ratings and that this is unrelated to rater expertise**, and that attempts to fix it by standardising instruments and training raters have met with mixed success at best.

**The remedy that literature proposes is not a better scale. It is substantiated judgements and group deliberation**, so that divergent reasoning becomes visible and discussable. This occasion adopts what it can afford: a closed specification, so that what is marked is fixed in advance; a written substantiation from the assessor; and the requirement that the assessor choose the region rather than the candidate.

**Its limits:** medical education, a single institution, mock portfolios, and assessors who did not know the students. Think-aloud methods cannot capture fast or automatic processing and participants may construct plausible explanations for the researcher. **Transfer to an engineering assessment is an argument rather than a given, and this repository makes it explicitly rather than assuming it.**

## The workflow

Assemble it, then attack it in this order.

**Step 1 — record the pin before anything else.** Commit, package version, host, model, date. Everything else in the defence is a claim conditioned on those five things, and a defence that does not state them is a claim about a moving object.

```yaml
# defence/pin.yaml   <- the first file, written before any of the rest
pin:
  corpus_commit: "<full 40-character SHA>"     # not the short form; the short form ages badly
  package_version: "<x.y.z>"
  host: "<client name + version>"
  model: "<model name + version as reported by the host, not as remembered>"
  date_observed: "<YYYY-MM-DD>"
  collections_enabled: ["<...>"]

# EVERY claim below cites one of these ids. A claim with no pin id is unbounded.
claims:
  - id: C1
    text: "The instruction set's repository-interface constraint was delivered."
    basis: "Observed the instruction text in the request payload (S046 step 4)."
    pin: "as above"
    survives_pin_move: false        # a host change could stop delivering it silently

  - id: C2
    text: "A phase-2 decision is locatable from the implementation record by identifier."
    basis: "Demonstrated live."
    pin: "not host-dependent"
    survives_pin_move: true         # this is a property of files

  - id: C3
    text: "The conformance check on the money type is authoritative."
    basis: "Made it fail; the change was blocked."
    pin: "as above"
    survives_pin_move: false        # tier is a repository setting and moves with it

  - id: C4
    text: "My instruction set caused the executor to stop constructing clients directly."
    basis: "UNESTABLISHED. I never observed the increment produced without it."
    pin: n/a
    withdrawn: true                 # withdrawn WITH its cost stated - see step 4
    cost_to_establish: "Reproduce the increment without the constraint: ~1 day, and the
                        model has since moved, so the comparison would not be clean."
```

**Step 2 — write the improved-and-skipped ledger honestly and in that order.** Improvements first, because they are easier and they calibrate the standard for specificity. Then the skips. **A skip is not a confession** — it is the professional content of this occasion, and the prohibition it stands against is the source material's own best rule, contradicted by that same material's proudest story.

**Step 3 — rehearse item 3 out loud, on a region you did not choose.** You are going to be asked to defend a region chosen by somebody else, and it will not be the one you prepared. Practise on regions selected at random by another candidate. **Then rehearse the what-if half**, because that is the half candidates have never practised: *what would you do if this region had to handle twice the volume, or a partial upstream failure, or a different tenant's data?* Retrospective explanation is the easy part.

**Step 4 — establish one causal claim properly, or withdraw it.** Take the configuration change you believe altered the output. Produce the increment without it. If you cannot — because it is expensive, because the model has moved, because the increment is already shipped — **say that, and say what it would take.** An unestablished causal claim withdrawn with its cost stated scores better here than one asserted confidently.

**Step 5 — mark every claim for whether it survives the pin moving.** Go through your defence line by line. Which claims are about files, and therefore durable? Which are about a host, a model or a tier setting, and therefore not? **Item 5 has a real answer and most of your platform claims are in it.**

**Step 6 — have an agent argue that your defence is unfounded.** That you cannot distinguish the configuration's effect from the model's default behaviour; that your audit's *explained* class is optimistic; that your check inventory's labels were read rather than observed; and that at least one of your claims is stated as though it were unbounded. Keep what is better than your own reasoning; record what you rejected. **An agent that endorsed your defence was not used as an instrument**, which is [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s claim closing the stage that finally delegated to it. Record separately that this instrument shares a model family with your executor and is therefore not independent of it.

**Step 7 — cross-check every platform claim against a second first-party surface, and record the disagreement rather than resolving it.** A pin says when you looked. **It does not say whether the page you looked at was the only one, or the current one**, and a defence built on one rendering page is a defence built on something that felt like a fact.

```yaml
# defence/cross-check.yaml   <- one row per platform claim in the defence.
# A row with one surface is not a cross-check; it is a citation that has not been tested.

- claim_id: C5
  claim: "The agent's definition lives in a reviewable file of this shape."
  surfaces_checked:
    - page: "<the schema reference>"
      date: 2026-07-31
      says: "Documents this shape AND declares it deprecated and superseded in its
             own opening note."
    - page: "<the command-line development page>"
      date: 2026-07-31
      says: "No standalone file of this kind exists in the current project model."
    - page: "<the deployment page>"
      date: 2026-07-31
      says: "Still describes this file as the one the tooling deploys."
    - page: "<a locally installed authoring skill, version recorded>"
      date: 2026-07-31
      says: "Still lists this file as something it helps you author."
  agreement: "NO. Four surfaces, three positions, one formal deprecation."
  resolved_by_me: false          # <- and it must stay false
  what_i_will_say_instead: >
    The surfaces disagree as of this date. I verified against the tooling version we
    are actually running, which is <version>, and that is the only claim I will make.
  survives_pin_move: false

- claim_id: C6
  claim: "<a second platform claim from your defence>"
  surfaces_checked:
    - page: "<...>"
      date: <...>
      says: "<...>"
  agreement: "Only one surface found."
  looked_for_a_second: true      # <- saying you looked IS the assessed content
  where_i_looked: "<...>"
```

**The `resolved_by_me: false` line is the discipline and the temptation is real.** Faced with four surfaces and three positions, the reflex is to decide which one is right and cite that. **Do not.** You may state which one governs the version you are running — that is a fact about your engagement — and you may not state which one is correct in general, because none of them is wrong and the disagreement is the finding. **A defence that reports agreement it did not check is indistinguishable, on the page, from one that checked and found it.**

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** How much of what you believe about your own build is conditioned on a configuration you never varied. **Step 5 is where most candidates discover that their durable claims are the boring ones — files, identifiers, records — and that everything they were proud of is pinned**, which is the correct discovery and the one this module was arranged to produce.


## What good looks like

These are the assessed elements. Each is checked as present and adequate, or not.

- **Every region of the increment is classified in the comprehension audit**, and the unexplained regions are named rather than absent.
- **A step the tooling made better is named**, with what specifically improved rather than a claim that things went faster.
- **A step that was skipped is named**, with what evidence would show whether the skip cost anything.
- **Each constraint in the instruction set states its conformance mode** — by execution, by review, or not at all — and the *not at all* entries are present.
- **A decision recorded during planning can be located from the implementation record by its identifier**, demonstrated live.
- **The inexplicability register is present and non-empty**, or its emptiness is defended with what was looked for and where.
- **Every check cited in the defence is labelled advisory or authoritative**, and the basis of the label is stated.
- **The pin is recorded in full**, and the candidate names which of their own conclusions would not survive it moving.
- **Nothing in the defence asserts a magnitude that was not measured by a named instrument.**
- **Every platform claim carries the page and the date it was read, and states what a second first-party surface said about the same thing** — or records that a second was looked for and none was found. Where surfaces disagree, the disagreement is reported rather than resolved.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Whether the increment works is not marked. How much of it was generated is not marked. The ratio of generated to hand-written work is not marked. Speed, throughput and volume are not marked. The elegance of the code is not marked. Whether an agent was used at all is not marked. Whether the opaque regions were eventually explained is not marked. Whether the configuration in fact caused the output is not marked — only whether the candidate can say how they would find out. **Whether the platform surfaces in fact agree is not marked either — only whether the candidate looked, and what they did with the answer.**

## How it goes wrong

- **The delegated defence** — every difficult question is answered with *the agent wrote that*. *Warning sign:* your account of the increment has no author in it.
- **The unpinned claim** — a statement about the tooling is made as though it held generally. *Warning sign:* item 5 is blank, or every claim you make survives the pin moving.
- **The unaudited green build** — everything passes, so everything is understood. *Warning sign:* your comprehension audit has no opaque class.
- **The improvement with no content** — the tooling made everything better, in no specified way. *Warning sign:* item 1's answer would fit any increment.
- **The confident causal chain** — the configuration produced the output, established by having written the configuration. *Warning sign:* you never saw the increment produced without it.
- **The reattributed shortfall** — everything that did not work is explained by the host, the model or the customer's environment. *Warning sign:* your account contains no mistake of your own. **This is the failure the source corpus itself institutionalises**, in a prepared answer that reattributes disappointing delivery to the customer's own constraints, and it is the one this occasion marks hardest against.
- **The rehearsed region** — the candidate steers the defence toward the region they prepared. *Warning sign:* you have an answer ready before the assessor has finished choosing.

## Exercises

**This session is the assessment.** These are preparation exercises and none of them is marked; the specification above is what is marked, and nothing else is.

**1 · Sort six claims into pinned and durable.** For each, say whether it survives the commit, package version, host and model all moving.

- *The instruction file exists at this path with this glob.* · *A decision made in planning is locatable by its identifier.* · *The behaviour grader tests output shape rather than correctness.* · *This check blocks a merge.* · *Completion and acceptance are different results.* · *The executor refused an embedded instruction in a fetched document.*

> **Model answers.** Durable: the identifier resolution and the completion-versus-acceptance distinction, because both are properties of files and of how you decided to work. Pinned: the path and glob, the blocking behaviour of the check, and the refusal — the first is a product layout, the second is a tier setting, and the third is a property of a host and a model on a day. **The grader claim is the interesting one**: that behavioural checks test output shape rather than correctness is a statement the corpus makes about itself at a revision, so it is pinned as a fact and durable as a caution. **If you sorted more than two into durable, you are treating observations as principles**, which is the failure this occasion marks against item 5.

**2 · Write one commit trailer properly.** Take a commit in your increment that was substantially generated and record the supervision and the assistance separately.

> **What you should find.** That the published convention deliberately keeps them apart — the co-authorship field for the agentic case where the agent is the author and the human supervised, and a differently named key for the assistant case, on the stated grounds that authorship should not be confused with assistance. **And that having recorded both, you still have not recorded whether anybody understood the change**, which is the gap this occasion exists to mark. **Bookkeeping is provenance. It is not comprehension**, and a defence that offers the first when asked for the second has answered a different question.

**3 · Go back to your own item 4, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 4 — what you configured that changed what the executor produced, and how you know. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then either establish the claim or withdraw it with its cost.

> **Criterion.** A causal claim passes if it names a comparison that was actually made: the increment, or a comparable slice of it, produced with and without the configuration, under a stated pin, with what differed. **It fails if the evidence is that you wrote the configuration and liked the output**, because a system whose behaviour varies run to run will produce output you like without any help from you. **And a withdrawal passes.** *I do not know, because I never observed the version without it; establishing it would take about a day and the model has since moved, so the comparison would not be clean* is a complete and creditable answer, and it is the answer most honest candidates reach.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this occasion's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**4 · Answer a what-if on a region you did not choose.** Have another candidate select a region at random and ask you two questions: what does it do and why is it like that, then what would you do if a stated condition changed.

> **Scoring criterion.** The first question is the one you can prepare for. **The second is the one that carries the weight**, because retrospective explanation shows you understood the case you met and counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases you did not — and an entrustment decision is an acceptance of risk about cases nobody can predict. **A candidate who classifies the region as opaque, points to its entry in the audit, states the remediation price and says why it was not worth paying has answered completely.** The occasion does not require you to understand everything you shipped. It requires you to know what you did not understand, and to have decided about it on purpose.

**5 · Find a magnitude in your own defence and remove it.** Read your ledger and your register for any number, multiple, percentage or comparative that was not produced by a named instrument.

> **What to notice.** How many arrive without being noticed — *much faster*, *far fewer*, *about half*. The standing rule in this repository is that **no effect size is asserted anywhere for an unverified source**, and the reason is visible in the sources themselves: the openly published handbook asserts four unsupported magnitude claims, one of them a cost outcome with no evidence, no baseline and no source at all. **Those four are recorded here as specimens and reproduced nowhere, and your defence is held to the same rule.** Replace each with a direction, a mechanism, or a named instrument and a date.

**6 · Have the agent attack the defence, then read what it did not attack.** Run workflow step 6 and list the claims it left alone.

> **What to notice.** The unattacked claims are usually the vaguest ones, because a vague claim offers nothing to grip. **That is the opposite of reassurance** — a claim nobody can attack is frequently a claim that says nothing, and the assessor will find it faster than the instrument did. Note also that this instrument shares a model family with your executor and is therefore not independent of it, which is a reason to record its output rather than to trust it.


**7 · Find a disagreement between two first-party pages, and refuse to settle it.** Run workflow step 7 on the platform claim in your defence you are most confident about.

> **What you should find.** Frequently that a second surface exists, that you had not read it, and that it says something you would have to reconcile. **The specimen this occasion is built on is the extreme case and it is real and dated**: four first-party surfaces, three positions, one of them a formal deprecation of the very file another page still describes as current, all rendering on the same day. **The exercise is not to work out who is right.** It is to notice that a careful reading of any one of them produces a confident and false generalisation, and that **no amount of diligence inside a source detects a disagreement between sources.** A pass reports the disagreement, names the version actually in use, and states a claim scoped to that version and nothing wider. **A fail picks a winner** — which is a judgement, made privately, presented as a citation. Note that the surfaces behind the specimen were read at mixed depth, that one has no public address and is citable only by its version, and that the contradiction is deliberately left unresolved in this repository rather than guessed at.

**8 · Add the mirror rule to the one you already hold.** Write out both limits, then find a claim in your own defence that breaks the second.

> **The two rules.** *Absence at one commit is not proof of absence in the project*, which [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) gave you. And its mirror: **presence on one page is not proof of currency in the product.** **Most candidates hold the first and break the second**, because a page that renders feels like a fact in a way that a file which is missing does not — a rendered page has been written, formatted, published and served, and none of those is evidence that it is current. **A pass finds the claim and re-scopes it.** The re-scoping is usually one clause long: *at the version we are running, on the date I checked, this page governs* — and that clause is the difference between a claim somebody can act on next year and one they cannot.

## What this does not establish

This occasion shows that you can account for a build produced partly by delegation. **It does not show that the delegation helped**, and nothing in the material behind this stage could show that.

The agent-configuration corpus contains evaluation machinery and **no committed results** — a grader exists, a result corpus does not — and its own documentation states that its behaviour checks test output shape rather than correctness and that some of its checks are advisory. Every benefit word in its prose is unwarranted at the revision this stage pins. **The internal corpus offers approximately thirty quantitative assertions of which almost all measure activity rather than outcome, exactly one names a measurement instrument, and no figure from any of them is reproduced anywhere in this repository.** The openly published handbook offers no comparative evidence for any practice on its site, contradicts itself in at least a dozen places, and asserts four unsupported magnitude claims recorded here as specimens.

**No cohort running this occasion will settle any of that**, and the design says so rather than implying that the defence is a measurement.

**The pin does not preserve anything.** It records the conditions under which an observation was made, so that a reader can tell whether those conditions still hold. **Nothing about pinning slows the decay of the thing observed, and a candidate who leaves believing that a pinned claim is a durable claim has learned the opposite of the occasion's argument.**

**And the specimen establishes that a contradiction existed on one day, not that contradictions are common.** Four first-party surfaces disagreeing about one file on 2026-07-31 is a real, dated, checkable instance and it is a sample of one. **It shows that a single-page citation can be confidently wrong. It does not establish any rate at which that happens**, and no such rate is published or asserted here. The four surfaces were read at mixed depth — one fetched and read in full, two through search excerpts with tables truncated and their structure reconstructed, and one a locally installed artefact with no public address citable only by version. **The contradiction is deliberately left unresolved in this repository rather than guessed at**, and a candidate who resolves it in a defence has done the thing the specimen exists to warn against.

**The oral form is well-tested practice and not an evaluated intervention.** The course policy this occasion borrows from has run for years and publishes **no grade distributions, no comparison against a prior examination-based version and no measurement of whether oral verification detects understanding that written work misses.** The entrustment literature is guidance from the concept's originator and reports adoption rather than effectiveness; it comes from a clinical domain with patients, legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint, and the transfer is an argument made explicitly here rather than a given. **The full evidence standard it specifies — multiple trained observers producing documented, deliberated judgements — is expensive, and this occasion runs a reduced version.**

**The assessor is an instrument and a poorly characterised one.** The rater literature reports that idiosyncratic rater effects account for a substantial share of variance in performance ratings, that this is unrelated to rater expertise, and that assessors reaching the same outcome frequently reason to it differently and anchor early. **Its studies are from medical education, single-institution, using mock portfolios and think-aloud methods that cannot capture fast processing.** A closed specification and a written substantiation are what this occasion can afford against that; **group deliberation, which is what the literature actually recommends, is not run here and its absence is a known weakness.**

**The occasion cannot distinguish a candidate who understood their build from one who audited it well.** That is conceded at [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §8, and the design accepts it, because the alternative — marking comprehension directly — would be the assessor's opinion in a specification's clothing.
