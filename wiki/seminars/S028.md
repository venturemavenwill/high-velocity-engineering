# S028 · The Architecture You Are Not Allowed to Build

**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none

## The situation

Your register from [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) is nearly all frozen.

The design that would actually solve this is not available to you. The one that is available is worse, and you know exactly how much worse.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. Describe the system you would build if nothing were frozen. One paragraph.
2. Which single constraint, removed, gets you closest to it?
3. **What is the smallest thing in this space you can change without anyone's permission?**
4. What would you have to demonstrate before anybody would seriously consider unfreezing the constraint from item 2?
5. If nothing moves, what do you build?

**Item 3 is the session and most people answer it with "nothing".** That answer is almost always false, and this session exists to make it expensive to give.

## What you must produce

Two artefacts. They are produced together and neither is acceptable alone.

- **The unavailable design** — the system you would build, written down properly, with the single constraint that forecloses it named. Not a complaint. Written well enough that somebody could build it if the constraint moved.
- **The available change** — the smallest thing you can move now, without permission, chosen for **what it would demonstrate about the frozen constraint** rather than for being easy.

Plus the argument for unfreezing, addressed to the person your register names, written in the terms of that person's own interest.

## What good looks like

- **The unavailable design is buildable.** Somebody could pick it up. It has a shape, a boundary, and a reason. A paragraph of grievance is not a design.
- **The foreclosing constraint is named singly.** Not a fog of circumstances — one constraint, one holder, one thing that would have to change. If you cannot narrow it to one, you cannot make a case, and the fog is doing the work of an excuse.
- **The available change is genuinely available.** You could start it this week and you would not have to ask. If you would have to ask, it is not the available change and you have not found it yet.
- **The available change was chosen for its evidential value.** It demonstrates something about the frozen constraint — that the risk it guards against is smaller than assumed, that the alternative works, that the cost is bearable. **Choosing the easiest available change instead of the most informative one is the commonest way to satisfy this session and learn nothing.**
- **The unfreezing argument is written in the holder's interest, not yours.** What they are accountable for, what they would have to explain to somebody who was not in the room, and what would make that explanation easy.
- **The fallback design is not sulking.** It is designed to be good at what it is, with its compromises acknowledged rather than resented. **A design produced under protest is a design nobody will maintain.**

## How it goes wrong

- **Learned helplessness** — everything is frozen, therefore nothing is attempted, and the eventual disappointment is attributed to the constraints. It is convenient, unfalsifiable, and usually partly true. *Warning sign:* your answer to item 3 was "nothing", and you did not spend long on it.
- **The shadow build** — you build the unavailable design anyway, in a corner, without sanction. It works, people start depending on it, and it is now an unowned dependency inside somebody else's estate. *Warning sign:* you are pleased that nobody has noticed.
- **The protest design** — the fallback is deliberately unimpressive so that everyone can see what the constraint cost. *Warning sign:* you would not put your name on it.
- **The constraint fog** — item 2 has no answer because it is *the whole situation*. Nothing can be argued against a situation. *Warning sign:* your unfreezing argument has more than one addressee.
- **The demonstration nobody asked for** — the available change produces evidence about a constraint nobody was ever going to reconsider. *Warning sign:* you cannot name who would read the result.
- **The unfreezing argument written as a complaint** — it is correct, it is well reasoned, and it makes the recipient look negligent. It will not be read twice. *Warning sign:* it would embarrass its addressee if forwarded.

## Do it

Write the unavailable design first, while you are still annoyed. Then put it down.

Write the unfreezing argument second, and write it from the other side of the table. **A constraint is a position, and positions are held for reasons that are usually good ones from where the holder stands.** Say what those reasons are, in their words, before you say what you want. Then propose a standard that is independent of both of you — an existing policy, a precedent inside their own organisation, a published requirement, an obligation they already carry — because arguing from a standard gives them something to tell the people they answer to, and arguing from your preference does not.

Then start the available change. **Do not wait for a decision about the unavailable design**, because the available change is the evidence the decision needs and there is no order in which the evidence comes second.

## Show it

State the available change to one other person and have them ask one question: **do you need anybody's permission for this?**

If the honest answer is yes, it is not the available change and you go back. Most first attempts fail this, because the changes people reach for first are the visible ones and visible changes require sanction.

## What this does not establish

**The rule this session is built on is an assertion, and it has no evidence behind it whatsoever.** The practice material behind this module states that even in an almost entirely frozen space something can be changed, and that constraint is not an excuse for doing nothing. **No instance of a frozen space being opened is recorded anywhere in that material. No engagement is described in which the rule was applied. Nothing in it is cited, sourced or measured.**

There is worse, and it is taught rather than hidden. **The same body of material, elsewhere, explains disappointing delivery outcomes by pointing at the customer's organisational constraints** — the very constraints this session tells you to work inside instead of blaming. A practice whose method says constraint is not an excuse, and whose prepared answers use constraint as the explanation, has not read itself. **That contradiction is not resolved in the source and it is not resolved here.** Hold both and notice which one is being used when.

The negotiation material behind the unfreezing argument is case-based, establishes no effect by its authors' own statement, and licenses no claim about how often such an argument succeeds. **None is made.**
