# S028 · Sampling, Intervals and What a Number Licenses

**Module:** [M06](wiki/modules/M06.md) · **Quarter:** [Q3](wiki/quarters/Q3.md) · **Complexity class:** 2
**Strands:** ME · MS
**Whitepaper:** [WP-028](wiki/whitepapers/WP-028.md)
**Retrieves from:** S026, S027
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Foundry cloud evaluation — aggregate pass rate, conversation-level data, sampling · **MCP servers:** Microsoft Learn MCP Server · Azure MCP Server (read)

---

## What this day is for

You have a number. Someone is about to act on it. The question this day answers is: **what are they entitled to conclude?**

The answer is almost always less than the number appears to say, and the reasons are three. The sample is finite, so the number is an estimate of something else. The sample was drawn some way, and that way determines what population it estimates. And the claim being made is usually about a future or a different population, neither of which was sampled at all.

The number in front of the room today is a Foundry evaluation's **aggregate pass rate**: the proportion of scored items whose label came back `pass`. It is the number every team in this industry puts in a release note, and it is an average over whatever happened to be in the dataset, computed after each item's ordinal score was collapsed to a binary at a threshold nobody chose. Everything this day teaches applies to it directly.

The day is deliberately negative in its conclusions and constructive in its output: **every reported number carries an interval and a sentence, and the sentence says what the number does not license.**

**This day also closes S026's magnitude gap.** S026 taught students to identify a construct-instrument divergence and gave them no way to say whether it mattered. Step 6 requires them to return to their own S026 case and bound it.

---

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items.

1. A measurement of the substrate property gives 240ms from 30 observations. What can you tell a colleague?
2. Same number from 3,000 observations. What changes?
3. The 3,000 observations came from one week. A decision is being made about next quarter. What changes now?
4. Predict: which of the three items will the room disagree about most?
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to *tell us whether the assistant is good enough to ship*, with an evaluation result set in front of it. Write down what it will say, and **name the one thing it will get wrong**. Opened at phase 8.

Item 3 is the day's move: more data does not extend a claim to a population you did not sample.

Item 5's expectation is that the agent will report the aggregate pass rate, compare it to a round number, and answer the question — without stating which population the dataset represents, and without noticing that the answer concerns a population the dataset does not contain.

**Warrant.** Pretesting with escalating conditions. Item 2 rewards the intuition that more is better; item 3 defeats it in a direction sample size cannot address. Item 5 is sealed here per instrumentation rule 8.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative retrieval set (20 min)

Free recall, no notes.

- The four error sources from S027 and what each responds to.
- The construct-instrument distinction.
- The S022 individual-versus-population sentence.
- Which S027 source does an interval account for, and which three does it not?

The final item is the day's most important framing and is answered by few.

**Warrant.** Expanding-interval retrieval with a bridge that bounds the day's apparatus before it is taught, so that intervals are not received as a general solution.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups have one substrate corpus of assistant interactions and **one evaluator at fixed settings**. They run four evaluations that differ only in how the sample was drawn, and read the aggregate pass rate and its interval from each.

| Scheme | How the sample was drawn |
|---|---|
| **A** | A random sample of last week's turns |
| **B** | Every turn from last week |
| **C** | Every turn from the three highest-volume conversations |
| **D** | A random sample of last week's **conversations**, evaluated turn by turn, with every turn treated as an independent observation |

For each, groups state **which population the number estimates** and commit. Then the actual populations are revealed, with the true values where known.

Scheme D's interval is far too narrow and this is not visible from its output.

Task after: **state the claim each scheme supports, in one sentence, and rank the four by how useful that claim is** — which is not the same as ranking by interval width.

Then, second task: **take the scheme you ranked most useful and name what would have to be true for it to support a claim about next quarter.** Then go and find out from the corpus whether it is true. Did the assistant's model deployment change during the window? Did the mix of question types shift? Did the retrieval index get rebuilt? **You cannot sample the future, so this is the one population question the day cannot settle by running an evaluation** — and the closest available substitute is checking whether the period you did sample was internally stable. It usually was not.

**Scheme D is the payload.** Turns inside one conversation are not independent — they share a user, a topic, a retrieved context and an accumulated history — so the effective sample is closer to the number of conversations than to the number of turns. The platform reports an average pass rate over rows and neither knows nor claims otherwise; the arithmetic is correct and the interval is wrong. Scheme C is the day's second surprise, because it has the narrowest interval in the set and supports the least useful claim, which reliably inverts the ranking groups commit to.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across sampling schemes with a fixed quantity, now executed against a real evaluation service rather than supplied as five numbers. Scheme D is the payload: clustering makes an interval confidently wrong, nothing in the reported number reveals it, and the row-per-turn shape that causes it is the shape the platform's default evaluation level produces. **The second closing task is [WP-028](wiki/whitepapers/WP-028.md) §8's objection-3 repair**: extrapolation is the day's most important point and the only one no executable sampling scheme can stage, so the day stages the nearest reachable thing — that the period you did sample was not one population either.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **A number from a sample estimates a population,** and which population is determined by how the sample was drawn, not by how large it is.
2. **The interval,** introduced as the range of population values consistent with what you observed — stated operationally, computed for one case, and not derived.
3. **What the interval accounts for:** random variation only. Systematic error, drift and selection are all outside it, which is phase 2's answer. **This list of exclusions is not exhaustive; it omits model misspecification and the fact that an interval's own coverage depends on assumptions that may fail. Both are real and both are ME101's material. These three exclusions are the ones that produce the errors students make.**
4. **Clustering,** from scheme D: when observations are not independent, the effective sample is smaller than the count, and the interval computed from the count is too narrow.
5. **Extrapolation.** No sample supports a claim about a population it did not draw from — including the future. This is not a matter of confidence but of what was sampled.
6. **What a number licenses,** as a written sentence with three parts: the estimate, the interval, and the population it is about.
7. **The sentence you owe the reader:** what this number does not license. One sentence, and it is the day's assessed output.
8. **Magnitude.** An identified divergence, error source or limitation must be *bounded* before it is reported, or the report cannot be acted on. **This closes the gap identified in [WP-026](wiki/whitepapers/WP-026.md) §8.**
9. **Defaults are opinions and thresholds are policy.** Four chose your population for you today. **The evaluation level defaults to `turn`**, which produces exactly scheme D's row shape — the platform's default output is the one whose interval is wrong. **The conversation split defaults to the last turn**, so a multi-turn interaction is scored on its final exchange and the rest is treated as context; splitting at the full conversation instead measures task completion, and per-turn measures something else again, and *how you split determines what you are evaluating*. That mechanism belongs to ME201 and is named here so that the default is not invisible. **Continuous evaluation samples production at a rate somebody set**, and the dashboard's quality figures are computed over sampled outputs, not all of them. And **intelligent sampling, offered for trace evaluation, is explicitly diversity-preserving: it selects a subset that covers edge cases and error paths.** That is the right sample for finding faults and the wrong sample for estimating a rate, because a set constructed to over-represent unusual cases estimates no population that exists. Continuous evaluation and intelligent sampling are **demonstrated here rather than operated** — both are preview capabilities requiring production traffic and a connected Application Insights resource the programme does not hold for a student project — and neither is assessed.
10. **What is perishable here and what is not.** The default evaluation level, the default split, the preview status of intelligent sampling and the current sampling controls are perishable and will move. **The durable claim is that a sampling scheme optimised for fault discovery cannot also serve as a rate estimator**, and that any tool offering you a *representative* sample owes you a definition of the word. Diversity-representative and distribution-representative are different properties and only one of them supports an average.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Point 3 carries the scope statement. Point 8 is added at [WP-026](wiki/whitepapers/WP-026.md)'s prompting and is the module's self-correction. Points 9 and 10 are the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required platform default and perishability content, and point 9's first clause is reachable from scheme D's artefact rather than asserted, per instrumentation rule 7.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (85 min)

**Class 2. The claim is the student's. This phase produces a build.**

Students take their S027 measurement and error budget, and — working through Copilot in agent mode — **ship a reporting layer** that emits the licensing sentence alongside the number, so that the two cannot travel apart. They:

1. **State the population** your sample estimates, precisely.
2. **State the population** the question is about.
3. **Compute the interval,** and state what it excludes per point 3.
4. **Check for clustering** and adjust or state that you cannot. If your evaluation ran at the default level, state what your rows actually are.
5. **Write the licensing sentence** — estimate, interval, population.
6. **Return to your S026 divergence case and bound it.** How large is the construct-instrument gap, in the units of the measurement? If you cannot bound it, say what would.
7. **Write the sentence stating what this number does not license,** including the extrapolation you are not making.
8. **Ship it.** The harness prints the number and both sentences together, and prints the threshold that produced the labels. **A number that can be copied out of your output without its sentences is a defect in the harness**, not in the reader.

Step 8 is the day's build and it is small. It is also the direct repair for phase 6's failure mode: a limitation that travels by convention does not travel.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — step 8's coupling of number to sentence is such a demonstration and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch.

**Fading:** step 3's computation is worked with the room; step 6's bounding is modelled once.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 2 continuing from S027's artefact. Step 6 is the module's self-repair and converts S026's identification move into a bounded claim. Step 7 is the assessed output and the direct ancestor of S050. Step 8 makes the reporting obligation mechanical rather than cultural, which is the only form in which it survives contact with a second reader.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate figure reported with an interval that was computed correctly and was wrong, because the observations were clustered — with attention to the fact that the analysis was reviewed and passed.

Then the agent case. Asked to *summarise the evaluation results for the release note*, Copilot produced a clean paragraph: the aggregate pass rate, a comparison against the previous run, and a recommendation. It was accurate in every particular. **It also silently dropped the interval, because the interval was in a separate column and the paragraph was about the headline figure**, and it described a run over one week's data as evidence about the assistant. Two reviewers approved it. Attention goes to why: the number was right, the prose was good, and **the reviewers were checking whether the summary matched the results, which it did.** Nobody was checking whether the results matched the claim.

Then students: a number you extended to a population you had not sampled.

**Warrant.** Error management. The reviewed-and-passed detail is the phase's content: clustering failures survive review because the arithmetic is correct. The agent case is the required addition and is the strongest available argument for step 8 — the agent did not remove the limitation, it simply failed to carry a limitation that was not attached to the number.

---

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student hands their partner **only the step 5 and step 7 sentences** — not the analysis. The partner must state:

- a decision the number supports;
- a decision it does not;
- what they would need to make the second decision.

Where the partner's first answer exceeds what the analysis supports, the sentences are revised.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with a decision-elicitation test. Supplying only the sentences is deliberate: a reporting sentence must work without its analysis, because that is how it will travel.

---

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- Items 1 to 4 resolved, with the item 3 answers shown as the day's key distribution.
- **Item 5 opened.** The sealed Copilot predictions are read against what the agent actually said, in two bands: predicted *some* error, and named *the population substitution* specifically.
- The room's step 6 bounds collected; how many could bound their S026 gap is reported and the figure is compared against S026's phase 8 confidence.
- Each student names one number they have acted on whose population they did not check.
- Retrieval schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 6.

## Assessment

Specification item in ME101 and MS103 jointly. Checked elements: both populations stated; the interval with its exclusions; the clustering check with the row shape named; the licensing sentence; the bounded S026 divergence; the does-not-license sentence; **the shipped reporting layer that emits number and sentences together**; **the prompt record**; **the intervention record**; **the frontier statement**.

**A does-not-license sentence that names only sample size fails the specification.** Sample size is one of at least four limitations and is the one students reach for first.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

Nothing else is assessed.

## Perishable content in this day

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| Which population a number estimates is set by how the sample was drawn | Turn-level rows drawn from conversation-level traffic |
| Non-independent observations make an interval too narrow | The default evaluation level of `turn` |
| How you divide an interaction determines what you measured | The conversation split defaulting to the last turn |
| A sample optimised for fault discovery is not a rate estimator | Intelligent sampling's diversity-preserving selection |
| A limitation that travels by convention does not travel | The aggregate pass rate, copied out of a results table |

The default evaluation level, the default split, the preview status of trace-based sampling and the substrate's measured values are **perishable** and are marked at point 10. Population-not-size, the interval's exclusions, clustering, extrapolation, the three-part licensing sentence, the magnitude obligation and the diversity-versus-distribution distinction are durable and are what the specification assesses.
