# S029 · Trade Studies, and When Not to Run One

**Module:** [M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md) · **Quarter:** [Q3](/wiki/quarters/Q3.md) · **Complexity class:** 3
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** GitHub Copilot agent mode

**Retrieves from:** S024, S026, S027
**Entry state:** either

## The situation

Two options, a room that would like a decision by Thursday, and nobody willing to say which they prefer.

Or the other case, which is more common and less discussed: **one obvious option, and a stakeholder who would like to see it justified.**

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. State the decision in one sentence. **Is the answer already clear?**
2. If it is clear, what would a comparison be for?
3. For each option, what experiment would separate it from the others?
4. What evidence would you accept, and how long would it take to get?
5. Which criterion, if it were dropped, would change the answer?

**Item 2 has an honest answer that people rarely give: to make somebody comfortable.** That is a legitimate need and a comparison is a poor way to meet it, because a comparison run to ratify a decision produces a document that looks like evidence and is not.

## What you must produce

One of two things, and choosing correctly between them is the session.

- **A trade study**, following the published template: who conducted it, against which piece of work, the decision and the people who took it; then the desired outcomes, the criteria, the constraints, accessibility, the hypotheses, and — **per option, an experiment and the evidence it produced** — closing with results, decision and next steps. Page read 2026-07-30.
- **Or a recorded refusal.** A short statement that the choice was already clear, why, and what would have had to be true for a comparison to be worth running — filed as a decision record at [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md) rather than as a study.

**A comparison with fewer than two genuine options is not a comparison**, and the published specification says so in its own words.

## What good looks like

- **Each option carries an experiment and its evidence**, not a paragraph of assessment. This is the specification's most valuable requirement and the one most often skipped. An option you did not try is an opinion about an option.
- **The criteria were written before the options were scored.** Verifiable from the order of your own document and from the decision record that precedes it.
- **The criteria are few and each one is load-bearing.** Item 5 has an answer: name the criterion whose removal changes the result, because if no criterion has that property the criteria were decoration.
- **The customer's existing system is one of the options**, judged on the same criteria as the rest. [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md) established why it belongs there; a comparison that quietly excludes the incumbent has decided before it started.
- **The study reaches a decision and names the next steps.** A comparison that ends in a table has not finished.
- **Where the choice was clear, no study was run**, and the refusal is on file. This is assessed as favourably as a study.

## How it goes wrong

- **The justification study** — run to ratify a decision already taken. **The published guidance's own negative rule is that a comparison should be avoided where the choice is already clear**, which is the rule most often broken by the people who cite the template. *Warning sign:* you know the answer and you are choosing criteria.
- **The adjective table** — options down the side, criteria across the top, and weighted scores that are opinions with arithmetic performed on them. No experiment anywhere. *Warning sign:* your study could have been written without touching anything.
- **The specification its own author does not meet** — **the only worked comparison published alongside this template omits several of the sections the template requires and renames another.** A learner copying the example would fail the specification. Pages read 2026-07-30. *Warning sign:* you are following an example rather than the specification, and have not compared the two.
- **The single-option study** — one real candidate and one straw candidate, present to satisfy the floor. *Warning sign:* the second option has no experiment because nobody was ever going to run one.
- **The criterion added after the result** — the evidence came back inconveniently and a new consideration appeared. *Warning sign:* the criterion that decided it is not in the decision record that opened the study.
- **The unbounded study** — the comparison expands to include everything, and the decision it existed to inform is taken elsewhere while it runs. *Warning sign:* nobody is waiting for it any more.

## Do it

Fill the template properly, including the sections that feel like paperwork — the accessibility section in particular, which is the one everybody drops and the one that catches the option that cannot be used by some of the people who will have to use it.

Then, before you decide, use the agent twice.

**First: ask it for an option you did not consider.** Give it the criteria and the constraints from your [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) register, not your shortlist. **An agent that returned only the options you already had was pointed at your own list**, which is the same failure [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) named about an instrument that agrees with you.

**Second: ask it to argue for the option you are about to reject**, at its strongest. Keep whatever is better than your own reasoning and record what you rejected and why.

Neither use decides anything. The experiments decide, and the agent has not run any.

## Show it

Give the study to one other person **with the criteria section removed**, and ask them to reconstruct what the criteria must have been.

If they can, the criteria were doing the work. If they cannot — if the study reads as a narrative that arrives somewhere — then the decision was taken on something that is not written down, and finding out what that was is the rest of the session.

## What this does not establish

Nothing measures whether documented comparisons produce better choices. The publication supplying the template offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices and does not claim to.

**The specification is also internally inconsistent, and this is taught rather than smoothed over.** The template page and the page that introduces it disagree about how many options should be carried forward, and nothing on either page reconciles them. **A specification whose two halves disagree, whose worked example does not follow it, and whose register gives no indication of either, is the normal condition of an internal standard** — and recognising that is more useful to a learner than the template itself.

The negative rule — avoid the comparison where the choice is clear — is **stated as guidance and evidenced nowhere.** It is adopted here because the failure it names is legible and cheap to check, not because anybody has shown that following it improves decisions.
