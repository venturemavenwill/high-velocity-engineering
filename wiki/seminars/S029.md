# S029 · Trade Studies, and When Not to Run One

**Module:** [M06](/wiki/modules/M06.md) · **Quarter:** [Q3](/wiki/quarters/Q3.md) · **Complexity class:** 3
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** Foundry hosted agents, Copilot Studio and the serverless agents runtime compared across authoring model, hosting and cost for one stated requirement
**Whitepaper:** [WP-029](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-029.md)
**Retrieves from:** S024, S026, S027
**Entry state:** either

---

## The situation

Two options, a room that would like a decision by Thursday, and nobody willing to say which they prefer.

Or the other case, which is more common and less discussed: **one obvious option, and a stakeholder who would like to see it justified.**

**Answer these in writing before reading further.**

1. State the decision in one sentence. **Is the answer already clear?**
2. If it is clear, what would a comparison be for?
3. For each option, what experiment would separate it from the others?
4. What evidence would you accept, and how long would it take to get?
5. Which criterion, if it were dropped, would change the answer?

**Item 2 has an honest answer that people rarely give: to make somebody comfortable.** That is a legitimate need and a comparison is a poor way to meet it, because a comparison run to ratify a decision produces a document that looks like evidence and is not.

## What you must produce

One of two things, and choosing correctly between them is the session.

- **A trade study**, following the published template: who conducted it, against which piece of work, in which sprint, the decision and the people who took it; then an overview, the desired outcomes, the evaluation criteria, optional key metrics, optional constraints, accessibility, the solution hypotheses, and — **per option, an experiment and the evidence it produced** — closing with results, decision and next steps. Page read 2026-07-30.
- **Or a recorded refusal.** A short statement that the choice was already clear, why, and what would have had to be true for a comparison to be worth running — filed as a decision record at [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md) rather than as a study.

**A comparison with fewer than two genuine options is not a comparison**, and the published specification says so in its own words.

## The material

### The negative rule is the most valuable line in the specification and the one most often broken

The published guidance carries a rule that is easy to read past: **"Trade studies should be avoided if there is a clear solution choice."** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md), page read 2026-07-30.) The template page adds a floor from the other end: **"Additionally, there should be at least two options compared, otherwise you didn't need a trade study."**

Two failures sit either side of that rule and only one of them is discussed.

**The justification study** is run to ratify a decision already taken. It is expensive theatre: it consumes engineering time, produces a document that looks like evidence, and cannot be distinguished afterwards from a real comparison by anybody who was not in the room. **A trade study run on a decision that is already frozen is worse than one with a foregone conclusion**, because the second at least informs the person who ran it, while the first produces a record that will be cited later as though a choice had been made. If your register from [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) marks the option space frozen, the comparison is not a comparison; it is documentation of somebody else's decision, and it should be filed as that.

**The other failure is refusing to compare when the choice only looks clear.** The rule says avoid the study where the answer *is* clear. It does not say avoid it where you are confident, and confidence is not the same property. Item 5 is the test: name the criterion whose removal changes the result. **If no criterion has that property, the choice is genuinely clear and the study is theatre. If one does, the choice was never clear and your confidence was about something else.**

### The specification is internally inconsistent, and its own worked example fails it

This is taught rather than smoothed over, because recognising it is more useful than the template.

The template page and the page that introduces it **disagree on how many options should be carried forward**, and nothing on either page reconciles them. **The only worked comparison published alongside the template does not follow it** — it omits several required sections and renames another, so a learner copying the example would fail the specification. Neither page carries any indication that the other exists in a different form. **A specification whose two halves disagree, whose worked example does not follow it, and whose navigation gives no sign of either, is the normal condition of an internal standard.**

The same source supplies the wider warning. The playbook's pages carry footer dates spread across several years; roughly a fifth of the site is stubs, link lists or tool catalogues; **it contradicts itself in at least a dozen places and never notices**; and it contains four unsupported magnitude claims, one of which asserts a cost outcome with no evidence, no baseline and no source at all. **This repository records those four as specimens and reproduces none of them.** Cite the playbook by page and by the date that page was read, never as a whole.

### A real trade study needs axes that genuinely conflict, and the retrieval literature is where you learn what that looks like

The commonest failure in student comparisons is criteria that all point the same way, which produces a table with a winner and no information. **Criteria that conflict are what make a comparison a decision rather than a ranking.**

The vector-search literature is the cleanest available source of genuinely conflicting axes, and it is worth reading precisely because the conflicts are structural rather than incidental.

The compression literature makes the trade explicit: **code length, sub-space count, sub-codebook size and the number of probed lists are the parameters that define the memory, recall and latency operating point** ([research/02-technical-foundations/jegou-product-quantization.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/jegou-product-quantization.md)). Shorter codes mean more vectors held in memory and a coarser distance estimate; probing fewer lists means lower latency and a higher chance of missing a true neighbour. The conclusion generalises far beyond vectors: **an index is designed against a budget rather than selected from a catalogue.** That note is written without the paper's full text, records that fact explicitly, and asserts no experimental figure.

The disk-resident literature adds the axis that decides cost and is usually omitted from vendor claims: **density, meaning points indexed per node** ([research/02-technical-foundations/subramanya-diskann.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/subramanya-diskann.md)). Recall, latency and density can each be traded against the others, so **a claim stated on only one axis is uninformative** — which is exactly what a criteria table with one winner is. The same work makes a design point your study should imitate: changing the storage tier changed the algorithm, not the configuration. **A criterion that changes which design is even possible is load-bearing. One that changes a number is not.**

The serving literature supplies the vocabulary for stating a result honestly. Inference performance must be reported as a **Pareto frontier**, because latency and utilisation trade against each other and cannot be compressed into one figure of merit; and there is **no universally optimal configuration**, because the right one depends on the application's latency target ([research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/pope-scaling-transformer-inference.md)). It also names the asymmetry that ruins naive comparisons: **prefill and decode are two different workloads with different bottlenecks**, so a single throughput number describes neither. **Its concrete results are tied to one accelerator family, one model and one context length, and are not general benchmarks; none is reproduced here.**

The memory-management literature is the cautionary one. Its reported improvement is **conditional** — stated at matched latency against particular contemporary baselines, with gains growing for longer sequences, larger models and more complex decoding ([research/02-technical-foundations/kwon-pagedattention-vllm.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/kwon-pagedattention-vllm.md)). **A figure quoted without its baseline and its workload profile is not a result you can carry into a trade study**, and its own note records that later work partly supersedes the design. **No magnitude from it appears here.**

### A trade study is a measurement, and inherits every limit of one

This is the part learners skip, and it is why so many studies conclude nothing.

An evaluation is an experiment. A reported score needs a standard error and a question count, and where the questions come in related groups it needs a clustered one ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). The part that bears hardest on a trade study is **power**: doing the analysis before the run turns *we tested and found nothing* into a statement about the options rather than about the test. **A study whose experiment could not have detected the difference it was looking for has not compared anything**, and nothing in the finished document will reveal that.

Two further disciplines from the same source transfer directly. **Compare paired rather than separately** where the options are run against the same cases, because the options tend to agree about which cases are hard and the pairing removes that shared variation for free. And **never suppress variance by changing a setting whose purpose is something else** — the temptation to make a comparison look cleaner by reducing the randomness of one side is a way of shifting the thing you were measuring. **That paper is analytical rather than empirical, its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions, and no number from it appears here.**

There is one more limit and it applies to every published comparison you might use as a shortcut. Capability claims about these systems are systematically distorted by the conditions under which they are produced ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/singh-leaderboard-illusion.md)). **A trade study that rests on somebody else's standings has inherited their distortion**, which is why the specification requires an experiment per option rather than an assessment per option.

### The platform comparison you will actually run, and the sentence that decides it

The vendor's own comparison across five ways of building an agent is a usable worked example of a trade study's structure, and it is more instructive than the playbook's.

It compares options **by authoring model, by hosting and by cost** — a declarative runtime driven by files on scale-to-zero functions; prompt agents with **no agent compute**; hosted agents running **your code, containerised**, on managed container compute; a low-code service billed in credits; and an option running locally on a user device ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). **Those three axes conflict properly**: the cheapest hosting is the least controllable, the most controllable authoring model is the slowest to reach a working version, and the option with no agent compute cannot run your code at all.

Two published statements will decide most real studies before an experiment is run. **Copilot Studio is "designed for small-scale deployments, such as department-level solutions"** — a scale claim published by a vendor about its own product, and the most important line in the comparison, because a tool used outside its design envelope fails through governance and lifecycle rather than through function. And **"These options aren't mutually exclusive"**, with protocol composition given as the example. **The second is the one that most often collapses a study into a refusal**, because the question turns out not to be *which* but *which for this component* — and that is a smaller decision, frequently already clear, and therefore one the negative rule says not to study.

**The limits are heavy and must travel with every use.** First-party selection guidance whose authority is definitional; **no adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no evidence that its recommended mappings produce better outcomes**; it compares one vendor's options against each other and not against alternatives; read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, with several comparison tables truncated; and **highly perishable**, with product names and capability boundaries actively changing.

The internal platform synthesis behind this strand is worth knowing about for a different reason: it is the best available specimen of a well-constructed, internally coherent comparison containing a precise unverifiable performance number, a maturity model with no causal evidence, and analyst projections presented as fact ([research/06-microsoft-platform/03-accelerate-agentic-ai.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/03-accelerate-agentic-ai.md)). **It is internal, is not publicly accessible, cannot be cited externally, and is the most perishable artefact in this repository.** Its quantitative claims carry no methodology and **none is repeated anywhere in this wiki.** Read as a critical-reading exercise it teaches more than the template does: an engineer who cannot mark the unverifiable claims in a document like that will be persuaded by every architecture deck they are ever shown.

## The workflow

**Run the platform comparison properly for one stated requirement — or record, on the evidence, that it did not need running.**

**Step 1 — write the requirement in one sentence, and the criteria before you look at any option.** Criteria first is verifiable afterwards from the order of your own document and from the decision record at [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md) that precedes it.

**Step 2 — apply the negative rule and record the outcome either way.** Is the answer already clear? Check it against item 5: name the criterion whose removal changes the result. **If none does, stop here and write the refusal.** A refusal filed as a decision record is assessed as favourably as a study, and it costs a day less.

**Step 3 — fill the template's fields, including the ones that feel like paperwork.** The accessibility section in particular is the one everybody drops and the one that catches the option that cannot be used by some of the people who will have to use it.

**Step 4 — write the experiment per option before running any of them**, in a file, with the thing each experiment would have to show.

```yaml
# docs/trade-studies/agent-hosting.yaml
requirement: "Draft determinations for one department; adverse outcomes routed to a person."
criteria:                        # WRITTEN BEFORE ANY OPTION WAS SCORED
  - id: K1
    name: "Runs our own review-routing code"
    load_bearing: true           # drop it and the answer changes
  - id: K2
    name: "Operable by the department without our team"
    load_bearing: true
  - id: K3
    name: "Usable by staff relying on assistive technology"
    load_bearing: unknown        # accessibility: the section everybody drops
options:
  - id: A
    name: "Prompt agent (declarative, no agent compute)"
    experiment: "Attempt review routing with declarative configuration only."
    would_show: "Whether the requirement needs our code at all."
    evidence: "TO BE FILLED AFTER RUNNING — not before"
  - id: B
    name: "Hosted agent (our container, managed compute)"
    experiment: "Deploy the routing component; measure time to first working version."
    would_show: "The control-versus-speed cost, in days rather than adjectives."
    evidence: ""
  - id: C
    name: "Low-code service (credits)"
    experiment: "Build the same flow; record what cannot be expressed."
    would_show: "Whether the published department-scale envelope binds here."
    evidence: ""
  - id: D
    name: "Incumbent: the system already running"
    experiment: "Extend it by one operation via a protocol."
    would_show: "Whether any of A-C is needed."
    evidence: ""                 # S027: the incumbent is judged on the same criteria
power_note: "State, before running, what difference each experiment could detect. An experiment that could not have detected the difference has compared nothing."
```

**Step 5 — run the experiments and record what each showed, including the ones that showed nothing.** An option you did not try is an opinion about an option. **Where two options are exercised on the same cases, compare them case by case rather than by their separate totals**, because they will agree about which cases are hard and the pairing removes that shared variation at no cost.

**Step 6 — use the agent twice, and let it decide nothing.** First: give it the criteria and the constraints from your [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md) register — **not your shortlist** — and ask for an option you did not consider. An agent that returned only the options you already had was pointed at your own list, which is the failure [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) named about an instrument that agrees with you. Second: **ask it to argue for the option you are about to reject, at its strongest**, then to name the single observation that would settle the disagreement. Keep whatever is better than your own reasoning and record what you rejected and why.

**Step 7 — give the study to one other person with the criteria section removed**, and ask them to reconstruct what the criteria must have been. If they can, the criteria were doing the work. If they cannot — if the study reads as a narrative that arrives somewhere — the decision was taken on something that is not written down, and finding out what that was is the rest of the session.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** That three axes which sound compatible in a meeting — control, speed to a working version, and operating cost — are structurally opposed, and that you can watch the opposition appear the moment you actually deploy the same requirement two ways. **You will also discover, in most cases, that the honest output was a refusal**, because the vendor states that the options are not mutually exclusive and the real decision is per component rather than per system.

## What good looks like

- **Each option carries an experiment and its evidence**, not a paragraph of assessment. This is the specification's most valuable requirement and the one most often skipped. An option you did not try is an opinion about an option.
- **The criteria were written before the options were scored.** Verifiable from the order of your own document and from the decision record that precedes it.
- **The criteria are few and each one is load-bearing.** Item 5 has an answer: name the criterion whose removal changes the result, because if no criterion has that property the criteria were decoration.
- **The customer's existing system is one of the options**, judged on the same criteria as the rest. [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md) established why it belongs there; a comparison that quietly excludes the incumbent has decided before it started.
- **The study reaches a decision and names the next steps.** A comparison that ends in a table has not finished.
- **Each experiment states, before it ran, what difference it could have detected.** Otherwise *we found no difference* and *we could not have found one* are the same sentence.
- **Where the choice was clear, no study was run**, and the refusal is on file. This is assessed as favourably as a study.

## How it goes wrong

- **The justification study** — run to ratify a decision already taken. **The published guidance's own negative rule is that a comparison should be avoided where the choice is already clear**, which is the rule most often broken by the people who cite the template. *Warning sign:* you know the answer and you are choosing criteria.
- **The adjective table** — options down the side, criteria across the top, and weighted scores that are opinions with arithmetic performed on them. No experiment anywhere. *Warning sign:* your study could have been written without touching anything.
- **The specification its own author does not meet** — **the only worked comparison published alongside this template omits several of the sections the template requires and renames another.** A learner copying the example would fail the specification. Pages read 2026-07-30. *Warning sign:* you are following an example rather than the specification, and have not compared the two.
- **The single-option study** — one real candidate and one straw candidate, present to satisfy the floor. *Warning sign:* the second option has no experiment because nobody was ever going to run one.
- **The criterion added after the result** — the evidence came back inconveniently and a new consideration appeared. *Warning sign:* the criterion that decided it is not in the decision record that opened the study.
- **The unbounded study** — the comparison expands to include everything, and the decision it existed to inform is taken elsewhere while it runs. *Warning sign:* nobody is waiting for it any more.
- **The underpowered experiment** — each option was tried, briefly, and the differences were within the noise. *Warning sign:* you cannot say what difference your experiment could have detected.

## Exercises

**None of these is assessed.** The study or the refusal is carried into [S030](/wiki/seminars/S030.md), where only the chosen option's consequences appear in the design.

**1 · Sort six decisions into study and refusal.** For each, apply the negative rule and say what evidence would change your sorting.

- *Which of two hosting shapes for a component we already run.* · *Whether to use the customer's existing identity provider.* · *Which model family, where one is the only one approved in the region.* · *Whether to build the retrieval layer or extend the incumbent.* · *Which of two libraries, where the team knows one and neither is load-bearing.* · *Whether to deploy at all.*

> **Model answers.** Refuse the identity-provider question and the model-family question — both are frozen by somebody else's decision, and a study on a frozen option space documents another person's choice while looking like your own. **That is the worse of the two failures the negative rule guards against.** Refuse the library question too, but for the opposite reason: no criterion's removal changes the answer, so the study is theatre. Study the hosting shapes and the build-versus-extend question, because in both cases a load-bearing criterion exists and an experiment could move it. The deployment question is not a trade study at all; it is a decision record. **If your sorting did not name a load-bearing criterion for each study you kept, you sorted on how interesting the question felt.**

**2 · Run one axis against another until they conflict.** Take the platform comparison's three axes — authoring model, hosting and cost — and write the sentence in which improving one degrades another for your requirement.

> **What you should find.** That the option with no agent compute is the cheapest and cannot run your code; that the containerised option gives you control and costs you the fastest route to a working version; and that the low-code option is quickest and carries a published scale envelope. **The three do not have a joint optimum, which is why the comparison is a decision rather than a lookup.** Note the vendor's own statement that the options are not mutually exclusive: in many real cases the correct answer is a different option per component, and recognising that turns a study into a refusal.

**3 · Go back to your own item 2, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 2 — what a comparison would be for, if the answer is already clear. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference.

> **Criterion.** An honest answer to item 2 names a person and a need: *the sponsor has to defend this to a board and has nothing written down*; *the platform team will not accept a decision they were not shown*. **Those are legitimate needs and a trade study is a poor way to meet them**, because a comparison run to serve them will be shaped to reach the answer everybody already holds. The right response is a decision record naming the choice, the reasoning and the people, which meets the need in a day rather than a fortnight and does not pretend to be evidence. **If you wrote "to be rigorous" or "to have it documented", you named no person and no need**, and that is the answer this exercise exists to catch.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**4 · State what your experiment could have detected.** For one option in your study, write the difference your experiment would have shown, and the difference it would have missed.

> **Scoring criterion.** The answer passes if it distinguishes *we found no difference* from *we could not have found one*. **A study whose experiments could not detect the difference it was looking for has compared nothing**, and nothing in the finished document reveals that — which is why the analysis belongs before the run rather than after it. If you cannot answer at all, say so in the study's results section. **An admitted limit is a finding; an unadmitted one is a defect.**

**5 · Find the specification's disagreement with itself.** Read the trade-study template and the page that introduces it. Find the number they disagree on, and check the published worked example against the template's required sections.

> **What you should find.** The two pages disagree on how many options to carry forward, and the worked example omits several required sections and renames another. **A learner copying the example would fail the specification, and nothing on either page warns them.** The lesson is not that the playbook is bad — it is openly published, repairable by pull request, and better than most internal standards. The lesson is that **following an example rather than a specification, without comparing the two, is a failure mode that survives review**, because both artefacts look authoritative.

**6 · Point the agent at the criteria, not the shortlist.** Run workflow step 6 and record how many options the agent returned that were not already on your list.

> **What to notice.** If the answer is zero, check what you gave it. **An instrument pointed at your own shortlist returns your own shortlist**, which is the same failure [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) named about an instrument that agrees with you. Note also what the agent has not done: it has run no experiment, and the specification requires one per option. **The agent widens the option space and decides nothing.**

## What this does not establish

Nothing measures whether documented comparisons produce better choices. The publication supplying the template offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices and does not claim to.

**The specification is also internally inconsistent, and this is taught rather than smoothed over.** The template page and the page that introduces it disagree about how many options should be carried forward, and nothing on either page reconciles them. **A specification whose two halves disagree, whose worked example does not follow it, and whose register gives no indication of either, is the normal condition of an internal standard** — and recognising that is more useful to a learner than the template itself.

The negative rule — avoid the comparison where the choice is clear — is **stated as guidance and evidenced nowhere.** It is adopted here because the failure it names is legible and cheap to check, not because anybody has shown that following it improves decisions.

**The playbook supplying the template also asserts four unsupported figures**, one of them a cost claim offered with no evidence, no baseline, no comparison and no source. **This repository records all four as specimens of the failure it exists to prevent and reproduces none of them.** The site's pages carry footer dates spread across several years, roughly a fifth of it is stubs or link lists, and it contradicts itself in at least a dozen places without noticing. Cite it by page and by the date that page was read.

**The technical literature supplies conflicting axes and no transferable numbers.** The compression note was written without the paper's full text and records that explicitly, asserting no experimental figure. The disk-resident and serving results are tied to particular hardware, models and context lengths and are not general benchmarks. The memory-management result is conditional on matched latency against particular contemporary baselines, and its own note records that later work partly supersedes the design. **No magnitude from any of them appears in this session.**

**The measurement literature is analytical rather than empirical.** Its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions, and it addresses comparing scores rather than choosing what to score — so a tight interval around a meaningless criterion is still meaningless.

**The platform comparison is first-party selection guidance with no outcome data.** It offers no adoption figures, no failure rates and no cost comparisons; it compares one vendor's options against each other; it was read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, with several comparison tables truncated; and it is **highly perishable**. One consulted artefact is internal, is not publicly accessible and cannot be cited externally at all.
