# S046 · Where Do Criteria Come From?

**Module:** [M10](/wiki/modules/M10.md) · **Quarter:** [Q5](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** ME · PE · IS
**Whitepaper:** [WP-046](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-046.md)
**Depends on:** S026, S036, S037
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Foundry rubric evaluators (preview), the built-in evaluator catalogue, and custom evaluators (preview) · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server (preview) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server · Azure MCP Server (read)

---

## What this day is for

**This is the complexity class 4 boundary.** Every prior task supplied a success criterion. This one does not, and constructing it is the work.

The model deployed at S045 is being judged against something. Ask what, and the answer is a metric somebody chose. Ask who chose it and why, and the answer is usually unavailable — the metric predates everyone currently working on the system, or it was picked because it was computable, or it was inherited from a benchmark built for a different purpose.

The target: **a criterion is constructed, by named people, from evidence, for a purpose — and if you cannot name the people you have inherited someone else's judgement without knowing whose.**

This is S026's construct question with a criterion as the instrument, and S036's request question with a standard as the request.

**Today the abstraction has an address.** A Foundry evaluator is a criterion somebody constructed and shipped. It has a name, a scale, a threshold and, if it is a rubric evaluator, a list of weighted dimensions that a generation pipeline produced from inputs you can enumerate. By the end of the day every student will have run four criteria over one set of responses, watched them disagree, and found out which of their four stakeholder groups the catalogue has no evaluator for — and why the auto-generation path could never have produced one.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items on the deployed model.

1. What would a good output from this system look like?
2. Who would agree with your answer to item 1?
3. Who would not, and what would they say instead?
4. Predict: how many distinct positions exist among the room?
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent, in these words, to *build us an evaluator for this assistant*. Write down what it will produce, and **name the one thing it will get wrong**. Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 3 is the day's move. Most students cannot name a dissenting party.

Item 5 is the day's contribution to the running frontier map. The design's expectation is that the agent will produce a working, registered evaluator — real dimensions, sensible weights, a threshold, a judge model — built entirely from the assistant's own system prompt, and that no dimension in it will represent anybody who did not already have a voice in the system. The expectation is that most students will predict some error and few will predict that one, because it is not an error in the evaluator.

**Warrant.** Pretesting on the criterion's contestedness before it is asserted. Item 4's prediction is low and the room's actual spread is the day's opening finding. Item 5 is administered here and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8: an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative retrieval set (20 min)

Free recall, no notes.

- The S026 construct-instrument distinction and the four gap mechanisms.
- The S036 four shaping forces on a request.
- The S037 say-do divergence mechanisms.
- Which S036 shaping force applies to a metric you inherited?

The answer is all four, and the requester is absent.

**Warrant.** Expanding-interval retrieval importing M08's apparatus into evaluation, which is the day's central move.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Four real groups with a stake in the deployed model are represented** — by supplied interview records from S037-style sessions conducted by staff, one per group.

- **Group A** — the users the model serves directly.
- **Group B** — the people who handle the model's failures downstream.
- **Group C** — the person accountable for the system's cost.
- **Group D** — a group the model affects who did not ask for it and were not consulted.

Each has a defensible view of what good means. The views are incompatible.

**Executed, not read.** Each group's view has a candidate implementation in the Foundry evaluator catalogue, and the groups run all four over **the same fifty responses** from their own deployed model, reading `score`, `label`, `threshold`, `reason` and `passed` for each.

| Case | The group's view, as a runnable criterion |
|---|---|
| A | `builtin.customer_satisfaction` (preview) — six dimensions chosen by the vendor: helpfulness, completeness, clarity, tone, resolution, adaptability |
| B | `builtin.tool_call_success` and `builtin.tool_output_utilization` — binary pass or fail, the downstream handler's question |
| C | `builtin.task_navigation_efficiency` — whether the step sequence matches an expected path, the module's one deterministic agent check |
| D | **Nothing in the catalogue.** The group must construct a custom evaluator to represent this view at all |

Groups predict the four rankings and commit before running.

Then they construct a single criterion for the system and commit it. Then the four records are re-read with a specific question: **whose view did your criterion drop?**

**Case D is the payload and it does not fail the way the room expects.** Groups anticipate that the unconsulted party will be hard to measure. What actually happens is narrower and worse. There is no built-in evaluator for a party who never used the system, so the group writes a custom prompt-based one — and the service will not register it until they supply a **judge model deployment** and a **threshold**. They are required to make two decisions on behalf of a group they have never spoken to before the platform will accept the criterion at all. And when a group tries the documented shortcut and auto-generates a rubric instead, the generation inputs are the agent's own instructions, its description, reference files and its production traces. **Every one of those is authored by the system's builders or is a record of the people already using it.** There is no input channel for group D, so the pipeline cannot produce a dimension representing them, and it does not.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across stakeholders with incompatible legitimate positions, now executed against a metered service rather than argued from records alone. Group D remains the payload and its result is now produced rather than asserted, which is what converts *the unconsulted party is hard to include* into *the tooling has no place to put them.* Cases A to C are matched on the response set and vary only in whose question they encode. This is the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](/wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) rule 3 disclosure in action: students are told these four groups were selected by staff and that others exist.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **A criterion is a decision, not a discovery.** Somebody decided what to value, under constraints, and the decision is revisable — S039's requirement framing applied to a standard.
2. **Where criteria actually come from:** inherited from a predecessor system, borrowed from a benchmark, chosen because computable, or negotiated among stakeholders. **This list omits criteria imposed by regulation or contract, which are not negotiable by the engineer and require a different response; and criteria that exist to make a system look successful, which are S082's material. Safe for a system whose standard is genuinely open; unsafe where the criterion is externally mandated, where the correct move is to state what it fails to capture rather than to reconstruct it.**
3. **Whose view counts,** and the question that decides it: who bears the cost of a wrong output.
4. **Group D,** and why the unconsulted affected party is the hardest and most important case. Phase 3 has just supplied the mechanism: **an affected party with no channel into the system also has no channel into the tooling that judges it**, because the tooling's inputs are the system's own description and the system's own traffic.
5. **What a criterion trades.** Every criterion serves some views at others' expense, and naming the trade is the honest form.
6. **Traceability:** a criterion should be attributable to people who would recognise it. If none would, it is the engineer's.
7. **Constructing one:** evidence from each group, the trade made explicit, and the criterion stated in terms each group could check.
8. **Defaults are opinions and thresholds are policy.** Five were chosen for you in phase 3 and none was announced. An auto-generated rubric's **weights** come from a documented heuristic: the generation pipeline assigns **exactly one criterion a weight of 8 to 10** — the dimension it judges most outcome-decisive — and everything else 1 to 6. It adds a `general_quality` dimension marked `always_applicable`, so one criterion is scored on every response whether or not it applies, and the other dimensions' applicability is decided by the judge. A rubric passes at an **overall score of 0.5** on a weighted average normalised to zero-to-one. A quality evaluator passes at **3 on a 1-to-5 scale**. A content-safety evaluator passes at **3 or below on a 0-to-7 scale** — the same number, the opposite direction. A custom numeric evaluator is documented as *higher is better*, so a criterion whose desirable direction is downward has to be inverted by hand. And a code-based evaluator that never calls a model still requires you to name a model deployment, because the service schema demands one. **An engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system**, and this generalises far beyond this vendor.
9. **What is perishable here and what is not.** Every evaluator name, every scale, every threshold, the 8-to-10 weight heuristic and the preview status of half the catalogue will move, and some of it will move this year. **The durable claim is that a criterion is a decision made by identifiable people from identifiable evidence, and that the evidence a tool can reach determines which people it can represent.** A vendor that ships a stakeholder-consultation input for rubric generation next year changes the instance and not the claim. That is why the specification assesses the provenance and not the configuration.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Point 2 carries the strong-form scope statement. Point 3 supplies the adjudicating question when views conflict, which is the class-4 demand's only available discipline. Point 4's mechanism is reachable from the phase 3 artefact rather than asserted, per instrumentation rule 7. Points 8 and 9 are the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required platform content — a default the platform chose, and a perishable point marked as perishable.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 4. The criterion is the student's to construct, no success criterion is supplied for the construction, and this phase produces a build.**

Students use Copilot in agent mode, through the Foundry MCP Server, to **ship a working criterion** — a custom evaluator registered in their project's evaluator catalogue and run over their own deployed model's responses. They:

1. **Identify the groups** with a stake, including at least one who did not ask for it. **At least one group must be named who is not represented in the four supplied records**, with a statement of what evidence would be needed to include them.
2. **Gather what each wants** — from the supplied records, the substrate's data, and a thirty-minute window with one real stakeholder.
3. **State the incompatibilities** explicitly.
4. **Construct the criterion,** stating the trade it makes.
5. **Register and run it.** A custom evaluator, prompt-based or code-based, with its scale, its threshold and — if it uses a judge — its judge model named, run over a response set from the deployed model.
6. **Name the people** who would recognise it, and the people who would not.
7. **State what it fails to capture** about each group's position.
8. **Write it in terms each group could check** — one line per group.

Step 5 is where the day bites a second time. A criterion written in prose can leave the trade implicit. A criterion the service will accept cannot: it must have a scale, a direction and a number at which it stops passing, and every one of those is a further decision about the same people.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned. Not a transcript.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a dimension written so that the criterion can fail the system for something the system was never instructed to do is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch. Both specific, both from this build.

**Fading:** step 3 is worked with the room; step 5's registration mechanics are supplied just in time. **Step 4 is unsupported**, which is the class boundary.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 4 with a live stakeholder window, now shipping rather than describing. Step 1's out-of-sample group is [WP-046](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-046.md) §8's objection-1 repair: the rule 3 disclosure named a sampling limit it did not reduce, and requiring a fifth group converts the disclosure into a demand. Step 6 is the day's discriminator: a criterion nobody would recognise is the engineer's own, whatever evidence was gathered. The intervention record distinguishes a student who directed the construction from one who accepted a generated rubric, and its fail condition carries the sensitivity escape [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) established and [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 corrected.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a criterion used on the substrate for two years whose origin nobody could establish, and what was discovered when they tried — with attention to **what it had been optimising in the meantime**.

Then the agent case, and it is the one from phase 1 item 5. A student asked Copilot to *build us an evaluator for this assistant*. The agent produced a competent artefact: it read the assistant's system prompt, auto-generated a six-dimension rubric, assigned defensible weights, set a threshold, named a judge model, registered the evaluator and ran it. The rubric read well and the pass rate was plausible. **Every dimension was a restatement of an instruction the assistant had already been given.** The criterion could therefore fail the system for doing its job badly and could not fail it for doing the wrong job, and the two are not the same test. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: each dimension was individually reasonable, the reviewer checked them one at a time, and **the defect was in the set rather than in any member of it.**

Then students: a metric you have used without knowing who chose it, or a place today where you accepted agent output you had not checked.

**Warrant.** Error management. The unknown-origin case is the module's founding condition and is more common than any deliberate misjudgement. The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen so that the failure is invisible to the review the students would actually perform — it has no wrong line.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student's partner takes the role of **the group whose view was traded away** at step 4, reads the step 8 line addressed to them, and states whether they would accept it.

If they would not, the pair determines whether the trade is defensible anyway — and the answer is sometimes yes.

Per rule 3, the outcome is recorded in the artefact.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with a disadvantaged-party role. The sometimes-yes is the phase's most important feature: a defensible trade that the losing party rejects is still defensible, and students need that to be sayable.

---

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- Items 1 to 4 resolved: the room's actual spread against item 4's prediction.
- The cohort's step 6 recognition claims collected; how many criteria have no recognising party is named.
- **Item 5 opened.** The sealed Copilot predictions are read against what the agent actually produced at phase 5, and the cohort's hit rate is recorded in two bands: predicted *some* error, and named *the provenance* error. Both count, and the gap between them is the measure.
- **The stakeholder from step 2 is asked, privately and in writing, whether they recognise the criterion built from their input**, and the results are reported to the room in aggregate.
- Each student names one metric governing their own work whose origin they cannot state.
- Retrieval schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 6.

**The recognition check is taken privately rather than in the room.** [WP-046](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-046.md) §8 conceded that a stakeholder asked in public, in front of the cohort, will say yes; the private aggregate form costs nothing and removes the social pressure. This carries across the expert-briefing countermeasure found at [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md) and named as uncarried by [WP-046](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-046.md) §8, per the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](/wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md)'s rule 5.

## Assessment

Specification item in ME201 and PE201 jointly. Per rule 1, the checked elements are: identified groups including one unconsulted **and one not represented in the supplied records, with the evidence that would be needed to include them**; gathered positions with sources; stated incompatibilities; a constructed criterion with its trade named; **the criterion registered and run, with its scale, threshold and judge model named**; named recognising and non-recognising parties; a statement of what it fails to capture per group; one checkable line per group; the phase 7 outcome; **the prompt record**; **the intervention record**; **the frontier statement**. **Nothing else is assessed, including whether the criterion is good.**

**A criterion with no named party who would recognise it fails the specification.**

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

## Perishable content in this day

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| A criterion is a decision, not a discovery | A rubric evaluator's dimensions, weights and descriptions |
| The evidence a tool can reach determines which people it can represent | Rubric generation inputs: agent instructions, description, reference files, traces |
| Every criterion trades one view against another | `customer_satisfaction`'s six dimensions, and who was not asked about them |
| A threshold is policy, not measurement | 0.5 for rubrics, 3 on 1-to-5 for quality, 3 or below on 0-to-7 for safety |
| Weighting is a decision somebody made | One dimension at 8–10 and the rest at 1–6, by generation heuristic |
| A criterion nobody would recognise is the engineer's | Which built-in evaluator names a party who was consulted |

All evaluator names, scales, thresholds, weight heuristics, preview markings and catalogue contents are **perishable** and are marked at point 9 explicitly. Criterion-as-decision, the four origins, the who-bears-the-cost question, the unconsulted-party case and its tooling mechanism, the trade requirement and traceability are durable and are what the specification assesses.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** A criterion is a decision, not a discovery — made by identifiable people, from identifiable evidence, for a purpose. **A criterion nobody would recognise is the engineer's own**, whatever evidence was gathered, and the evidence a tool can reach determines which people it can represent.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** Criterion construction, construct validity and stakeholder-derived standards are grounded in [research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md) and [research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md); the inquiry apparatus in [research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md](/research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md). **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude is asserted.** No rationale is published for the generation pipeline's weighting heuristic — a design decision presented as a mechanism — and no agreement with human judgement is published for any evaluator you ran today.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Name who would recognise the criterion and who would not.** If nobody would, it is yours.
- **Name a group absent from the records you were handed**, and the evidence that would be needed to include them.
- **State the trade.** Every criterion serves some views at others' expense, and naming it is the honest form.
- **Do not read a clean generation path as a correct criterion.** Provenance is not correctness: on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md) a registered evaluator is a *configured control*, and whose standard it encodes is a separate question your artefact must answer.
- **Ask the stakeholder privately whether they recognise it.**

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Reviewing the dimensions one at a time. Each will be reasonable; the defect lives in the set — a criterion that can fail the system for doing its job badly and cannot fail it for doing the wrong job.
