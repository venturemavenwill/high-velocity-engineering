# S069 · Reporting a Result That Undermines You

**Module:** [M14](/wiki/modules/M14.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** An evaluation run whose result contradicts the delivery narrative, reported with its uncertainty rather than withheld — and a human-evaluation feature that ships beside judges whose agreement with it is published nowhere
**Whitepaper:** [WP-069](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md)

**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

You measured it. The result is not the one anybody wanted, and it may be the one that says your work did not help.

Everything about the situation is arranged to prevent you reporting it plainly. There is a commercial relationship. There are people who advocated for the work. There is a prepared answer waiting — **and in the corpus behind this programme, the objection its engineers are armed against most heavily is precisely this one: that the tooling improved and the delivery did not speed up. The prepared answer reattributes the shortfall to the customer's own organisational constraints.**

Paired with a readiness rubric composed almost entirely of customer dispositions, that arrangement has a property worth naming exactly. **A model whose failure explanation is always the customer's readiness has arranged its evidence so that a disappointing result confirms rather than challenges the thesis.** It is the single most teachable thing in the entire collection, and this is the session where a learner has to decide whether to use it.

**The professional act is reporting the result that costs you something. Everything else is reporting.** That sentence is the module's ethical core and it is deliberately unhedged, because the rest of this session is about how much easier it is to say than to do.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. What is the result, in one sentence, with no qualifying clause?
2. Who is worse off if you report it, and are you one of them?
3. What is the strongest honest reading in your favour, and is it honest?
4. Where in your organisation's prepared answers does this result already have a rebuttal waiting?
5. Where will this be recorded, and who will be able to find it in a year?

**Item 4 is the one nobody expects.** Most organisations have prepared an answer to your result before you obtained it, and you can usually find it in the material you were given during onboarding.

## What you must produce

**The result, reported, with the unfavourable reading first and its uncertainty attached — and the record placed where somebody will find it.**

1. **The result in a single sentence**, with no qualifying clause, at the top.
2. **Its uncertainty, in the same paragraph.** Not in a methods appendix. **A point estimate reported alone overclaims, and it overclaims in whichever direction the reader was already leaning.**
3. **What the run could have detected** — stated before the run, so that a null result means *we could not distinguish them* rather than *there was no difference*.
4. **The frozen configuration**: threshold, rubric version, split strategy, judge model version, dataset hash, all recorded before the run.
5. **What would have to be different** for the result to change, stated without asserting that it will.
6. **The prepared answer, named as a prepared answer** wherever you have used one, with your reason for using it.
7. **The placement**: where the record lives, and why a future reader will find it there.
8. **The instrument and the date.**

## The material

### What you are actually being asked to do has a name and a literature

Reporting a result that undermines you is not primarily an ethical puzzle or a writing problem. **It is speaking up when silence is the lower-risk option**, which is the construct the psychological-safety literature studies and which this programme has been building towards since [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md).

The opening case in that literature is a nurse who notices a missing prophylactic order, steps forward to say so, and stops — because she had recently seen a colleague publicly berated for questioning an order ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)). The reading offered is that she made **"a quick, not entirely conscious, risk calculation"** and in doing so engaged in **"discounting the future — underweighting the more important issue of the patients' health, which would take some time to play out, and overweighting the importance of the doctor's possible response, which would happen immediately."**

**That is the exact shape of your reporting decision.** The cost of reporting is immediate, personal and certain. The benefit is delayed, diffuse and uncertain, and accrues mostly to other people. Nothing about the arithmetic is irrational.

The generalisation is blunt: **"No one wakes up in the morning excited to go to work and look ignorant, incompetent, or disruptive. These are called interpersonal risks, and they are what nearly everyone seeks to avoid, not always consciously."** And the avoidance becomes automatic — do not ask questions, do not admit mistakes, do not make suggestions.

Two further findings from the same source bear directly on this session's artefact.

**Silence is invisible.** *"Silence — when voice was possible — rarely announces itself! The moment passes, and no one is the wiser except the person who held back."* **A result you did not report leaves no trace anywhere**, which is why the placement requirement is a requirement and not advice.

**And the asymmetry that produces the behaviour is structural.** Voice benefits the organisation, after a delay, with low certainty. Silence benefits oneself, immediately, with high certainty. Hence the line worth carrying out of the session: **"no one was ever fired for silence."** Alongside it sit **implicit theories of voice** — taken-for-granted rules derived from interviews with more than two hundred employees in one multinational technology firm: do not criticise something the boss may have helped create; do not speak unless you have solid data; do not say anything negative about the work in a group.

**Read that middle rule again.** *Do not speak unless you have solid data.* **This session is about a case where you have the data and it is the reason you would rather not speak**, which is the rule failing in the direction nobody anticipates.

**The source's limits.** The 2019 book is a trade press work by the construct's originator. Its case studies are drawn almost entirely from secondary sources — published teaching cases, journalism, accident reports, a documentary — rather than from original fieldwork. It contains an internal tension between a tight definition and a looser one in its own introduction. **And the foundational 1999 paper is recorded in this repository as unread**; where the book restates a finding from it, this session uses the book's wording and marks it as such.

### The construct survives the obvious sceptical test

A programme suspicious of soft-skills claims needs a way to tell which ones are load-bearing. This one passes, and the meta-analysis is why ([research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)).

It aggregates 136 independent samples covering over twenty-two thousand individuals and nearly five thousand groups, and it does four things beyond pooling. It **ranks antecedents by relative strength** rather than reporting that everything correlates. It tests **incremental validity** against named comparators — whether psychological safety predicts performance and citizenship behaviour over and above positive leader relations and work engagement, which is the sceptic's question asked properly. It tests whether **research design and national culture** moderate the findings. And it tests **homology** — whether the construct behaves equivalently at individual and group level — rather than assuming it.

**That last one matters for this session specifically.** Psychological safety is theorised at the group level; the reporting decision is made by an individual. **A design that assesses an individual's willingness to report is treating an environmental variable as a personal skill**, and the session's limits say so.

**Its limits:** the pooled evidence is overwhelmingly cross-sectional and self-report, so causal direction is not established by the meta-analysis; publication bias is live in a literature with a strongly positive prior; and construct heterogeneity across that many samples means the pooled estimate averages over measures that are not identical. **The abstract reports no numerical effect sizes and none is reproduced here** — anyone wanting a specific correlation must obtain it from the full text.

### The prepared answer, and reading an objection list as a map

The internal corpus recorded at [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md) arms its practitioners with prepared responses to anticipated objections. **The objection it is armed against most heavily is that the tooling improved and delivery did not speed up, and its prepared answer reattributes the shortfall to the customer's own organisational constraints.**

Set that beside a readiness rubric composed almost entirely of customer dispositions and the structure becomes visible. **When the model works, the model worked. When it does not, the customer was not ready.** **A thesis whose failure explanation is fixed in advance has arranged its evidence so that a disappointing result confirms it**, and no observation is available that could count against it.

**The transferable skill is the inversion.** A prepared-objection list is not a shield; **it is a map of where the people who wrote it expect the model to fail.** They know. They wrote the answer down. Reading that list as a diagnostic rather than as a script is one of the most useful analytical moves in this stage, and it is available in almost every organisation because almost every organisation has such a list.

Two further facts about the corpus bear on this session and are its most instructive content. **Every case study succeeds.** Its single failure narrative is a **synthetic composite, fictional in its particulars by its own admission.** And the two most trustworthy things in the entire collection are **refusals** — including a proposal to replace human reviewers in a legal-review process, judged irresponsible because those reviewers catch issues with legal consequences.

**The other refusal is the one this session is named for.** A finding that a coding assistant could not be asked to add a feature end to end on a mature production codebase — because the generated code reused outdated patterns and ignored architectural constraints — **directly contradicts the collection's own headline velocity claims and is buried in speaker notes.** Somebody found it, wrote it down, and put it where nobody would read it. **That is not dishonesty; it is the asymmetry of voice and silence operating on a person who did the harder half of the job.**

**The corpus's limits:** access-controlled and unverifiable by any public reader, paraphrased and never quoted, no outcome data of any kind, and no figure from it is reproduced anywhere in this repository.

### Reporting with uncertainty is not hedging; it is the result

The temptation in an unfavourable result is to attach a qualifier that softens it. **The discipline this session teaches is the opposite: attach the uncertainty that makes it precise**, which frequently makes it harder rather than softer to argue with.

The statistical treatment behind the module opens on exactly this practice failure: results reported with a highest-number-is-best mentality, in which a leading figure is highlighted "but not necessarily to test that result for any kind of statistical significance" ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). Three of its conclusions are what a professional report needs.

**A comparison of two systems on a small evaluation set frequently cannot distinguish them at all.** So the honest sentence for many unfavourable results is not *the system did not help* but **we could not distinguish the system from the baseline, on an evaluation of this size** — which is a different claim, a weaker one, and the true one.

**Power analysis belongs before the run.** Deciding in advance what difference the evaluation could reliably detect converts a null result from an embarrassment into a statement about the instrument. **A team that did not do this cannot tell a real null from an underpowered one, and will report whichever reading suits them without knowing they chose.**

**And where items arrive in related groups rather than independently, an uncorrected interval is too narrow.** The measurement looks more precise than it is, which cuts both ways — it will make a favourable result look solid and an unfavourable one look damning.

**Its limits:** an analytical paper rather than an empirical one, with most illustrative magnitudes resting on stylised assumptions; the machinery is valid only where the items genuinely sample the population you care about, which fails if they were hand-selected or curated; **and statistical validity says nothing about construct validity.** **No figure from it is reproduced here.**

### Three ways an unfavourable result can be made to disappear without anybody lying

**Move the threshold.** The evaluation surface returns pass or fail against a cut point applied after the score, and **a threshold is a policy choice separable from the measurement: moving it changes the pass rate without changing the system** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). Nothing in the tooling records that it moved. This is why the session requires the threshold to be frozen and recorded before the run.

**Move the criteria.** The rubric is not stable and there is a documented reason. **Criteria drift** — "users need criteria to grade outputs, but grading outputs helps users define criteria" — is a genuine circularity rather than a workflow failure, and some criteria appear to be **dependent on the particular outputs observed** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/shankar-evalgen.md)). **So a rubric revised after an unfavourable result is doing something the literature says is normal and legitimate**, and is indistinguishable from motivated revision unless the version is recorded. The paper's own limits note the cost: **if criteria are revised after seeing outputs, those outputs cannot cleanly serve as a held-out set.** **Its evidence is qualitative, its user study small, and its tool a research prototype.**

**Move the judge.** If the result came from a model-judged evaluator, re-running with a different judge is a re-measurement, not a correction — and judges carry **position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases**, with a judge sharing a family with the system under test not being independent of it ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/zheng-mt-bench-chatbot-arena.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/zheng-mt-bench-chatbot-arena.md)). **The platform publishes no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data and no threshold rationale**, so you cannot appeal to the tooling to settle which run was right. **That work predates current models; its taxonomy has held up better than its figures, none of which is reproduced here.**

**None of these three requires anybody to write a false sentence.** That is what makes them dangerous and what makes the frozen configuration a control rather than a formality.

### Your own reading of whether it helped is not admissible

A randomised trial of experienced maintainers found them **slower with AI assistance while believing they had been sped up**, with the belief surviving the experience ([research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md](/research/04-professional-formation/metr-ai-assisted-productivity-study.md)). Forecasts before the study, from participants and from independent experts, all pointed the other way.

**Two things from that source belong in this session and only one of them is the finding.**

The finding is that perceived and actual effect diverge, which is why *it felt faster* is not a counterweight to an unfavourable measurement.

**The other is the behaviour.** The authors published an explicit table of things their result does **not** show. They then labelled their own result out of date and redesigned the study in public, because participants had begun declining to work in the no-assistance arm at all, which compromised the randomisation. **A research group discovering that its instrument had been degraded by changes in the world and saying so is the clearest available model of this session's artefact**, and it is worth more to a learner than the result itself.

**Its limits:** a very small developer sample; a population close to a worst case for assistance; self-reported time; **and the authors' own statement that the result is out of date.** **No magnitude from it is reproduced here.**

### Why the report is prose and not a score

The assessment literature this programme's architecture rests on found that assessors reading real evidence **"mainly relied on narrative feedback to inform their judgment… Grades and qualifications were merely used to confirm impressions based on narratives"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)).

**A number without its account is not what a decision-maker actually uses**, whatever the reporting template says. The same work found assessors reaching similar conclusions through different reasoning, and **anchoring** — they were inclined to stick to initial judgements even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence, with later reading producing more elaborate versions of the same conclusion rather than a revised one.

**Both findings point the same way for this session.** The account has to lead, because the account is what is read. And it has to lead **early**, because a reader who has already formed a judgement will elaborate it rather than revise it — which is the mechanism behind the requirement that the unfavourable reading come first rather than being present somewhere.

**Its limits:** single-institution medical-education research using mock portfolios and think-aloud methods, which cannot capture fast or automatic processing and risk participants constructing plausible explanations for the researcher. **Transfer to engineering reporting is an argument rather than a given, and narratives are not self-validating** — unstructured comment carries bias more easily than a number does.

### The agreement nobody publishes, and the instrument that now exists to measure it

There is a question sitting underneath every unfavourable result you will report in this module, and it is the one that decides whether the result is worth defending: **does the judge that produced it agree with a competent human?**

**Nobody has published an answer.** No agreement rate, no correlation, no calibration study, no threshold rationale, for any built-in evaluator on this platform ([research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-agent-build-and-runtime.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-agent-build-and-runtime.md)). **That absence was established by a dedicated search rather than by not having encountered one** — the search returned the evaluator catalogue, the rubric documentation and a human-evaluation feature, and no agreement figure of any kind. **Assert none, here or anywhere.**

**What has changed is that the instrument for measuring it yourself now ships in the product.** A human-evaluation feature, in preview, lets you define question templates, have reviewers answer them per response in a browser-based application, and download the results. **So the platform now supplies the means of collecting human judgements about agent responses and publishes no comparison of its own judges against them.** Both halves of that sentence are true at once and they belong together, because separately each is unremarkable and together they are an instruction.

**The instruction is: measure it yourself, on your own responses, before you defend a number produced by a judge.** Take a stated number of stored responses. Have people label them against a template you wrote. Run the built-in evaluator over the same responses. Then report **how often the two disagree, and on which cases.** That is a small piece of work, it is now buildable end to end inside the product, and **it converts every subsequent claim you make from *the evaluator said so* into *the evaluator said so, and here is how closely it tracks us on this kind of case.***

**Read the disagreement cases rather than only the count.** A judge that disagrees uniformly is miscalibrated and can be corrected by moving a threshold. **A judge that disagrees on a *class* of case is measuring something else**, and no threshold repairs that — you have a construct problem rather than a calibration one, and it will surface exactly when the class matters. The commonest shape is a judge that agrees on obvious passes and obvious failures and diverges on the ambiguous middle, **which is the only region where the score was ever doing work.**

**One further published fact belongs here and is a genuine risk.** The documentation recommends making custom weighted rubrics your **primary** measure of agent quality, since a rubric expresses what good means for your case. **That advice is reasonable and it moves the problem rather than solving it**, because a rubric dimension is scored by a model judge too. **A rubric you wrote yourself, scored by a judge nobody calibrated, is a measurement of your own intentions filtered through an uncharacterised instrument.** Write the rubric. Then measure the agreement on the rubric.

**And the same shape recurs at the level of the reported score, which is where this session lives.** The governance surface describes continuous evaluation of sampled production responses, surfaced on a dashboard — and **publishes no evidence that it catches anything a manual review would not** ([research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-control-plane-and-governance.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/foundry-control-plane-and-governance.md)). So a rising dashboard score and a flat operational outcome can coexist indefinitely with nothing in the platform noticing. **When they do, you have the unfavourable result this session exists to teach you to report** — and the honest report says which of the two you trust and why, rather than presenting both and letting the reader choose the flattering one. **The measured agreement is what lets you answer that question with something other than a preference.**

**What none of this establishes.** **It does not establish that the built-in evaluators are poor.** They may be excellent; the point is that nobody has published the comparison, so neither you nor anybody quoting their output at you has grounds either way. **It does not establish that a locally measured agreement generalises** — yours is measured on your responses, your reviewers and your template, and it transfers to nothing. And **it establishes nothing about how often the human-evaluation feature is used or whether it improves anything**; no such data exists.

**The source's limits are the heaviest attached to anything in this session.** **The most perishable material in this repository, and it perished visibly while being read**: four pages read in full, roughly thirty reached only through search excerpts with tables frequently truncated, several consulted pages explicitly superseded and still rendering, and **the human-evaluation and rubric features are both preview**, which means no service level and an explicit recommendation against production reliance. **Definitional and not evidential** — an accurate account of what the platform is configured to do, and no evidence that doing so produces any outcome. **Every number in it is a dated platform parameter and none is reproduced here.** The governance note beside it is **vendor documentation about vendor governance**, definitional, renaming its own roles mid-documentation, with **two interpretive thresholds asserted on no stated basis and reproduced nowhere.**

## The workflow

**Run the evaluation, freeze what could be moved, and report the result with its uncertainty before anybody has seen it.**

**Step 1 — freeze the configuration and commit it before the run.** Threshold with its justification, rubric version, split strategy, judge model version, dataset hash, repetitions. **These are the three things that can make an unfavourable result disappear without anybody writing a false sentence**, and a frozen record is the only control available.

**Step 2 — state what the run could detect, before it runs.** Write down the smallest difference this evaluation could reliably distinguish. **A null result from an evaluation that could never have detected the effect is a statement about the instrument, and reporting it as a statement about the system is the commonest honest-looking error in this stage.**

**Step 3 — run with repetitions and record the dispersion, not just the mean.** One sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement, and the documentation supplies repetitions for exactly this reason.

**Step 4 — write the sentence.** One sentence, no clause, no comma doing rhetorical work. Read it aloud. **If it is uncomfortable to say, it is probably the result**; if it is comfortable, check whether a qualifier arrived while you were not looking. Then put the interval in the same paragraph.

```yaml
# .evidence/result-2026-07-31.yaml   <- SHAPE, NOT SCHEMA. Verify product keys separately.
frozen_before_run:                    # committed BEFORE any output was seen
  threshold: { value: "<cut point>", justification: "<why this one, for this decision>" }
  rubric_version: "v3"                # criteria drift is normal; UNRECORDED drift is not
  split_strategy: "full"
  judge_model: "<model+version>"       # changing this is a re-measurement, not a correction
  dataset_sha: "<sha>"
  repetitions: 5
  minimum_detectable_difference: "<stated before the run>"

one_sentence: >
  On the frozen evaluation set, the assisted workflow could not be distinguished
  from the baseline.
  # NOT 'showed no improvement'. NOT 'performed comparably'. NOT 'was broadly similar'.

uncertainty: "<interval, in the same paragraph as the sentence - never in an appendix>"
interpretation_limit: >
  This evaluation could not have detected a difference smaller than the value above,
  so a null result here does not establish that no difference exists.

prepared_answer_used:
  used: true
  which: "customer organisational readiness"
  labelled_as_prepared: true          # if used, it MUST be named as prepared
  evidence_in_this_case: "<what supports it HERE, or the word: none>"

what_would_have_to_differ: "<stated without asserting that it will>"
placement: "<repository path or system of record - NOT slides, NOT speaker notes>"
refusals_recorded: ["<anything declined during the engagement, and why>"]
instrument: "<named>"
date: "<YYYY-MM-DD>"
```

**Step 5 — find the prepared answer before you need it.** Go through your own organisation's material and locate the rebuttal to your result. It will be there. **Read the prepared-objection list as a map of where its authors expect the model to fail.** Then decide, deliberately, whether it applies to your case — and if you use it, say that it was prepared and say what supports it here.

**Step 6 — place the record.** Repository, decision log, system of record — somewhere a person looking in a year will find it without knowing to look. **Move it out of documents and slides**, which is a rule this practice already states for engineering artefacts and does not apply to its own findings.

**Step 7 — add your refusals.** Anything you declined, and why. Both of the genuinely trustworthy findings in the source corpus are refusals, and **a practice that records only what it did has recorded half of its judgement.**

**Step 8 — measure your judge against people, on your own responses, and attach the result to the report.** Pick a stated number of stored responses. Write a labelling template. Have people label them. Run the built-in evaluator over the same responses. **Report how often the two disagree and on which class of case**, and put that beside the result rather than in an appendix. **Do this before you defend the number, not after somebody challenges it.**

```yaml
# .evidence/judge-agreement-2026-07-31.yaml   <- SHAPE, NOT SCHEMA. Verify product keys separately.

why_this_exists: >
  NO agreement rate, correlation or calibration between any built-in evaluator and
  human judgement is published by the vendor. confirmed by a dedicated search, not
  merely not encountered. so the only agreement figure that can exist is one we made.

sample:
  responses: "<a stated number, chosen before you looked at any of them>"
  drawn_from: "<the stored set, and how you selected>"
  selection_was_random: true          # if false, say so; convenience sampling breaks the claim

human_side:
  template: "eval/templates/<name>.md"   # the questions reviewers actually answered
  reviewers: ["<person>", "<person>"]     # more than one, or say why not
  reviewers_saw_evaluator_output: false   # <- if true, you measured agreement with a hint
  inter_reviewer_disagreement: "<recorded, because it bounds everything below>"

machine_side:
  evaluator: "<name>"
  judge_model: "<model+version>"
  prompt_ref: "<path>"
  threshold: "<cut point, and whether it was inherited>"

agreement:
  overall: "<how often they agreed>"
  disagreement_cases: "<list them. the list is worth more than the count.>"
  shape:
    uniform: "<calibration problem. a threshold may fix it.>"
    by_class: "<CONSTRUCT problem. no threshold fixes it.>"
  where_it_diverged_most: "<usually the ambiguous middle - the only region the score was working in>"

rubric_note: >
  the documentation recommends custom weighted rubrics as the PRIMARY quality measure.
  a rubric dimension is scored by a model judge too. writing the rubric moves the
  problem; measuring agreement ON the rubric is what addresses it.

generalises_to: nothing
  # measured on OUR responses, OUR reviewers, OUR template. transfers to no other setting,
  # including our own next quarter. re-measure when any of the three changes.

attached_to_report: true              # <- beside the result. not an appendix.
```

**Step 9 — use the agent to attack your own report, in two passes.** First: *rewrite this so the result reads as a success, without adding a single false sentence.* **Read what it produced very carefully.** It will move the sentence, add a true qualifier, lead with the favourable reading, or reach for the prepared answer — and every one of those moves is available to you and none of them is a lie. **That output is a catalogue of the exact edits you are about to be asked to make in the room.** Second: *now argue that my original report is still burying the result, and name the observation that would show it.* A third, for the block above: *argue that my agreement measurement was set up to succeed, and name the reviewer instruction that biased it.* Keep what is better than your own reasoning, record what you rejected, and **record separately that this instrument shares a model family with the judge that produced the result and is not independent of it.**

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** The distance between your report and its spun version, measured in edits rather than argued about. **Most learners find the spun version requires three or four changes, none of which introduces a falsehood**, which is a far stronger demonstration that burial does not require dishonesty than any warning could be. **You will also observe that the frozen configuration is the only thing standing between the two versions** — without it, the spun report is not even detectable as a different measurement.

## What good looks like

- **The unfavourable reading leads.** **A result whose bad news is in the last paragraph has been buried whether or not you meant to bury it.**
- **The one-sentence result survives being read aloud.** No subordinate clause has been added to soften it.
- **The uncertainty is in the same paragraph as the result.** **A point estimate reported alone overclaims**, and the reader will supply their own precision, in whichever direction they were already leaning.
- **A null is reported as a null and not as an absence of effect.** *We could not distinguish them on an evaluation of this size* is the true sentence; *there was no difference* is not.
- **The configuration was frozen before the run and is shown.** Threshold, rubric version, judge model, dataset. **These are the three levers that make an unfavourable result disappear without a false sentence being written.**
- **A deflection to the customer's readiness is labelled as a deflection where it is used** — and is used only where you can say what evidence supports it in this specific case. **An explanation that was available before the result was is not an explanation of the result.**
- **The record is in the repository or the system of record, not in speaker notes.** **A finding in speaker notes is a finding that was not reported.**
- **The result is dated and attributed to an instrument**, so that a future reader can tell what it was a measurement of.
- **Any refusal you made during the engagement is recorded too.** Refusals are the most trustworthy content this practice produces and they are almost never written down.
- **The report does not wait for a better result.**

## How it goes wrong

- **The buried contradiction** — the finding that contradicts the headline claim is recorded somewhere nobody reads. *Warning sign:* the most interesting thing you learned this engagement is in a slide's notes field. **This is not hypothetical:** in the corpus behind this programme, a finding that a coding assistant could not be asked to add a feature end to end on a mature production codebase — because the generated code reused outdated patterns and ignored architectural constraints — **directly contradicts the collection's own headline velocity claims and is buried in speaker notes.**
- **The moved threshold** — the cut point was adjusted after the score arrived and nothing records that it moved. *Warning sign:* you can say what the threshold is and not when it was chosen.
- **The moved rubric** — the criteria were revised after an unfavourable run, which is legitimate and is indistinguishable from motivated revision without a version. *Warning sign:* your rubric has no version number.
- **The re-judged run** — an unwelcome model-judged result is re-run with a different judge and the second is reported. *Warning sign:* you have two runs and one number.
- **The underpowered null read as a finding** — the evaluation could never have detected the effect and the report says there was none. *Warning sign:* you cannot say what difference your run could have detected.
- **The readiness deflection** — the shortfall is reattributed to the customer's constraints. *Warning sign:* your explanation was written before your result existed.
- **The qualifying clause** — the result is true and the sentence containing it is not. *Warning sign:* your one-sentence result has three commas.
- **The synthetic failure** — a composed failure narrative offered in place of a real one. *Warning sign:* your failure case is illustrative and your successes are specific. **The corpus does exactly this**: every case study succeeds, and its single failure narrative is a synthetic composite, fictional by its own admission.
- **The delayed report** — the result waits for a better one. *Warning sign:* you are re-running the measurement before you have reported the first.
- **The unrecorded refusal** — you declined something, correctly, and nowhere says so. *Warning sign:* the only refusal in your engagement's record is in somebody's memory.
- **The bad-news sandwich** — the result is between two good ones. *Warning sign:* your document has a structure your result did not require.

## Exercises

**None of these is assessed.** The result record is carried to [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md), where it is defended against an assessor who will offer you a better metric definition and see what you do with it.

**1 · Rewrite five results as one sentence each, with no qualifying clause.**

- *Broadly speaking, while adoption was uneven across teams and the measurement window was shorter than ideal, we did not observe the improvement we had hoped for.* · *The system performed comparably to the existing process.* · *Results were mixed.* · *Early indications are encouraging, though it is too soon to say.* · *There was no statistically significant difference.*

> **Model answers.** The first: *We did not observe the improvement we expected.* Every clause removed was doing rhetorical work and none was load-bearing. The second and third are not results at all — *comparable* and *mixed* name a feeling about a number rather than the number. The fourth is a prediction wearing a result's clothes. **The fifth is the trap and it is the one most professionals get wrong:** *no statistically significant difference* is frequently reported as *no difference*, when the honest sentence is **we could not distinguish them on an evaluation of this size.** The difference between those two is the whole of exercise 4.

**2 · Find your organisation's prepared answer.** Search onboarding material, objection handling, sales enablement or internal training for the rebuttal to a result like yours. Record where you found it and what it says.

> **What you should find.** That it exists, that it is well written, and that it was authored before any specific engagement. **Read the list as a map: the objections an organisation has prepared for are the places its own people expect the model to fail.** That inversion is the transferable skill. **If you cannot find one, that is also a finding** — record it, because an organisation with no prepared answers has either not met the objection yet or does not write things down, and those call for very different next steps.

**3 · Go back to your own item 1, then break it.** Find the one-sentence result you wrote at the start. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then write it properly, with its uncertainty attached.

> **Criterion.** A result sentence passes if it is a single clause, contains no comma doing rhetorical work, could be read aloud to the person who commissioned the work without amendment, and carries its uncertainty in the same paragraph. **It fails if a reader could finish it and still not know whether the work succeeded.** *"The system performed as expected in most scenarios"* fails on three counts. *"We could not distinguish the assisted workflow from the baseline; the interval spans both a benefit and a cost"* passes.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**4 · State what your run could have detected, then re-read your null.** Using the workflow, write down the smallest difference your evaluation could reliably distinguish. Then re-read any null result you have reported this term.

> **What you should find.** Frequently that the evaluation could not have detected a difference you would have cared about, which means your null was a statement about the instrument. **That is not a failure and it is not nothing** — it is a precise, defensible, reportable finding, and it is stronger than the vague null it replaces because it tells the reader what to do next. **If you cannot compute this at all, record that too:** an evaluation whose detection floor is unknown cannot support a null in either direction.

**5 · Have the agent spin your report, then count the edits.** Run workflow step 8's first pass. Diff the two versions and list each change.

> **What to notice.** That the spun version is usually a small number of edits away, that none of them introduces a falsehood, and that **at least one of them is a change you would have made yourself and considered reasonable.** The moves are predictable: promote the favourable reading, demote the unfavourable one, add a true qualifier, reach for the prepared answer, replace a specific null with a vague one. **The purpose is not to be shocked at the model. It is to see the edit list before somebody in a meeting suggests them one at a time**, where each looks individually reasonable and the accumulation is a buried result.

**6 · Locate the silence in a record you already have.** Take any engagement record you have access to and look for what is not in it: a refusal, a contradicting finding, an abandoned approach.

> **Scoring criterion.** A pass names something specific that is known to have happened and is not recorded, and says where it would have gone. **A fail says the record is incomplete**, which is true of every record and is not a finding. **The point of the exercise is the property that silence is invisible** — nobody is worse off for it except the person who held back and everybody who reads the record later — which means it can only be found by somebody who was there. **You are the only person who can do this for your own engagement, and after you leave nobody can.**

**7 · Measure your judge against people, then read the disagreements rather than the count.** Take a stated number of stored responses. Write a labelling template. Have at least two people label them without showing them the evaluator's output. Run the built-in evaluator over the same responses. Report how often the two agree — and then list the cases where they did not.

> **What you should find.** First, that **no published agreement figure exists for any built-in evaluator**, so the number you just produced is the only one in the room; the vendor supplies the instrument for collecting human judgements and publishes no comparison of its own judges against them. **That is confirmed by a dedicated search, not merely not encountered, and you may assert no figure of your own from it either — yours is measured on your responses, your reviewers and your template, and generalises to nothing.**
>
> Second, and more useful: **the shape of the disagreement matters more than its size.** Uniform disagreement is a calibration problem and a threshold may address it. **Disagreement concentrated on a class of case is a construct problem, and no threshold repairs it** — the judge is measuring something else, and it will matter precisely when that class does. Expect the divergence in the ambiguous middle, **which is the only region where the score was ever doing work**; agreement on obvious passes and obvious failures is close to free and tells you almost nothing.
>
> Third, check your own setup before you trust the number. **If the reviewers saw the evaluator's output, you measured agreement with a hint.** If you recorded no disagreement between the reviewers themselves, you have no bound on how much agreement was available to be had. And if you are following the documentation's recommendation to make a custom weighted rubric your primary quality measure, note that **the rubric's dimensions are scored by a model judge too** — writing the rubric moves the problem, and measuring agreement on the rubric is what addresses it.

## What this does not establish

**Nothing here shows that reporting an unfavourable result improves anything for the person who reports it.** This programme has no evidence on that and would be misleading a learner to imply otherwise. **The professional cost is real, it falls on the individual, and the benefit — if any — falls on the organisation and on the next engagement.**

**The psychological-safety literature is about environments and this session teaches an individual.** The construct is theorised and largely measured at group level; the meta-analysis tests homology rather than assuming it, precisely because transporting findings across levels is not automatic. **A design that assesses whether a person reported honestly is treating an environmental variable as a personal skill**, and this session cannot fix a team where speaking up is punished. It can only make sure a learner knows what they are being asked to do.

**The meta-analytic evidence is overwhelmingly cross-sectional and self-report.** Causal direction is not established, publication bias is live in a literature with a strongly positive prior, and the pooled estimate averages over measures that are not identical. **No effect size from it is reproduced here** — the abstract reports none.

**The source corpus records no failure anywhere.** Every case study succeeds. The only failure narrative is synthetic and admits it. **There is therefore no example of this session's artefact being produced by anybody**, and the procedure taught here is assembled from two buried refusals and an absence.

**The statistical material is used at a fraction of its strength.** Reporting an interval is necessary and not sufficient; the source's machinery is valid only where the evaluation items genuinely sample the population you care about, and **a tight interval around a meaningless metric is still meaningless.** The full treatment is [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s.

**The judge-agreement material establishes an absence and supplies a method. It establishes nothing about the judges.** **No agreement rate, correlation or calibration between any built-in evaluator and human judgement is published**, confirmed by a dedicated search — which is evidence about what you may claim and **no evidence whatever that the evaluators are poor.** They may be excellent, and nobody, including the vendor, is in a position to say. **A locally measured agreement generalises to nothing**: it belongs to your responses, your reviewers and your template, and must be re-measured when any of the three changes. **No data exists on whether the human-evaluation feature is used, whether using it improves anything, or whether a measured agreement changes any decision**, and none is asserted.

**The platform source's limits are the heaviest attached to anything in this session.** **The most perishable material in this repository and it perished visibly while being read** — four pages read in full, roughly thirty reached only through search excerpts with tables frequently truncated, several consulted pages explicitly superseded and still rendering. **The human-evaluation and rubric features are both preview**, so they carry no service level and are documented as not recommended for production. **Definitional and not evidential**, and every number in it is a dated platform parameter reproduced nowhere here. The governance note beside it is **vendor documentation about vendor governance**, definitional, renaming its own roles mid-documentation, with **two interpretive thresholds asserted on no stated basis and reproduced nowhere.**

**The platform documentation is definitional and silent where it matters.** It records that thresholds are applied after scoring and that repetitions exist for non-determinism. **It publishes no agreement rate with human judgement for any evaluator, no calibration data and no threshold rationale**, so nothing in the tooling can adjudicate between two runs that disagree. It was read through search excerpts, most agent evaluators are preview, and one consulted page is superseded.

**The placement rule is stronger than its warrant.** Moving findings out of slides and into the repository is a stated practice in the source material, applied there to engineering artefacts and never to results. **Extending it to findings is this design's decision**, and the claim that a future reader will find them there is an assumption about how people search, not an observation.

**And the session cannot tell whether a deflection is legitimate.** Sometimes the customer's constraints genuinely are the explanation. The requirement here is only that the explanation be labelled and supported in this specific case — **not that it be abandoned**, which would be its own kind of dishonesty.
