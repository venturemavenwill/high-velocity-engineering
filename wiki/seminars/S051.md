# S051 · From Vectors to Meaning

**Module:** [M11](/wiki/modules/M11.md) · **Quarter:** [Q6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** IS · MS · ME
**Whitepaper:** [WP-051](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-051.md)
**Depends on:** S011
**Re-tests:** S016, S041
**Platform anchor:** Foundry model catalogue embedding deployments (`text-embedding-3`) and Azure AI Search vector fields · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server · Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

[S011](/wiki/seminars/S011.md) established that a representation is a decision about what is cheap. An embedding is a representation, and what it makes cheap is one operation: **find me things near this thing.**

The question almost nobody asks is *near in what sense*. Every embedding was trained against a similarity signal — co-occurrence, paraphrase pairs, clicks, labels, metadata — and **that signal is the definition of "similar" the system now holds.** It is not a property of the corpus. It is not discovered. It was chosen, usually by somebody who was solving a different problem than yours.

Today that abstraction acquires an invoice. The students build an Azure AI Search index over the substrate's own corpus, deploy embedding models from the Foundry catalogue into it, and discover that the choice they are being asked to make is one the search service's own documentation puts on them in writing: *all vectors uploaded to Azure AI Search must be generated externally from the service by using a model of your choice*, and **it is your responsibility to consider whether the embeddings it creates are optimized or even appropriate for your use case.** The same page states the consequence plainly — the vectorisation model *creates an embeddings space that defines the resulting end user search experience of an application.* The vendor is not hedging. It is telling you that the thing you are choosing today is what your users will live inside.

The target: **"similar" is a construction, it is recoverable by inspection without access to the training data, and choosing an embedding is choosing whose notion of similarity your users will live inside.**

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Twelve short documents from the substrate are supplied.

1. **Group them by similarity.** Write your grouping down before discussing.
2. Here are **four groupings of the same twelve documents**, each produced by a different deployed model. Which is right?
3. What is each one treating as similar?
4. Predict: which grouping will the substrate's users want?
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent, in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server, to build an index over the substrate corpus with a vector field and to embed the corpus into it as cheaply as it can. Write down what it will produce, and **name the one thing it will get wrong.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

The four groupings disagree substantially and **all four are defensible.** Item 2 has no correct answer and the room discovers this without being told.

**Warrant.** Pretesting on a task the student can do unaided, then showing four machine answers that disagree with each other and with them. The absence of a right answer at item 2 is the day's opening finding and it arrives before the word *embedding* is used. Item 5 is the day's contribution to the running frontier map; it is sealed at phase 1 and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8, because an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall. The design's expectation is that a majority will predict a wrong dimension count and a minority will predict the failure that actually occurs.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative retrieval set (20 min)

Free recall, no notes.

- [S011](/wiki/seminars/S011.md): what a representation makes cheap, and the price it charges elsewhere.
- [S016](/wiki/seminars/S016.md): why locality determines performance.
- [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md): what generalisation means and what it is measured against.
- **Bridge:** an embedding makes nearest-neighbour lookup cheap. What does S011 tell you it must therefore make expensive?

The bridge is answered by the room. The answer wanted is that everything not expressible as proximity in that geometry becomes expensive or impossible — negation, conjunction, "about the same system but disagreeing", and any relation that is not similarity.

**Warrant.** Expanding-interval retrieval that imports M03's apparatus wholesale rather than building a new one. A student who holds S011 already holds the day's central frame; the seminar's job is to make them notice.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** The substrate corpus is already loaded into one Azure AI Search index with four vector fields, one per deployment. Groups have query access and thirty query documents, and they run the neighbour lookups themselves.

**Four real deployments over one corpus.**

| Case | Deployment | *Similar* means | Billed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E1** | No embedding at all — the index's full-text BM25 arm over the same documents | Shares rare terms, as the index's analyser counts them | With the search service |
| **E2** | `text-embedding-3-large` at its default dimension count, serverless | Says roughly the same thing | Per token |
| **E3** | The substrate's own click-trained encoder, deployed to **managed compute** | Was clicked in the same session | Per virtual-machine core hour |
| **E4** | `text-embedding-3-large` again, with `dimensions` reduced | Whatever E2 means, in fewer coordinates | Per token |

Groups characterise what each treats as similar and commit. **Then the training constructions and the deployment provenance are revealed.**

The phase has two payloads and the second is the one that defeats the room.

**E3 is the attractive one.** It is the only case whose notion of similarity is the users' own behaviour, which makes it the most appealing — and it is also the one that cannot surface anything nobody has clicked. Its similarity is a photograph of the past. It is also the only one of the four billed by the hour rather than by the token, because managed compute deploys model weights to dedicated virtual machines and charges for their core hours whether or not anybody queries them. **A notion of similarity has a billing model, and the two are not independent decisions.**

**E4 is the surprise.** It is the same weights as E2, trained on the same signal, asked the same question — and its neighbour sets differ. `text-embedding-3` models are trained under Matryoshka Representation Learning, which produces usable vector representations at several levels of compression, so asking for fewer dimensions is not a lossy afterthought bolted onto E2; it is a different, sanctioned reading of the same model. Nothing about the training signal changed. The geometry did. **A group that has just concluded "the model defines what similar means" now has to account for one model meaning two things.**

Task after: **four jobs are stated, and each group assigns a case to each job with a reason.** The four jobs are constructed so that no single case wins more than two, and one of the four is a job for which metadata similarity — same author, same system, same origin — would beat all four, which the groups are expected to discover and are asked to name.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across similarity notions with a fixed corpus and a fixed index, adjudicated by the training constructions, now run against real deployments rather than supplied as neighbour tables. E1 is included as a case rather than as a foil because [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md) will require students to beat it and the day wants it already in the room as a peer. E4 replaces the metadata construction the earlier design used as a fourth case; metadata similarity survives as the fifth job, which is stronger, because a job no available case serves is the condition step 7 exists for and it now arrives inside phase 3 rather than as a hypothetical. The four-job assignment prevents the phase resolving into "E2 is the good one", which is what a phase without it would produce.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **An embedding is a representation,** and S011 applies without modification. It makes one operation cheap and no other operation cheaper.

2. **The geometry is a choice and must be checked, not assumed.** Cosine and Euclidean distance rank differently on unnormalised vectors; normalisation is a decision; and **the distribution of an embedding's vectors in its own space is an empirical property of that model which you measure rather than assume.** In particular, similarity scores are **not comparable across models**, and a threshold tuned on one is meaningless on another. The search service's own troubleshooting table says this in operational form: when text results dominate a hybrid query, one listed cause is a query vector generated by a different model from the document vectors — which produces no error, no warning and no empty result, only quietly wrong ranking.

3. **Similar-to-what.** Every embedding was trained against a similarity signal, and that signal is the operative definition. Recovering it — from behaviour, without the training data — is the day's core skill.

4. **What the training signal encodes, it also freezes.** Click-trained similarity cannot surface what nobody clicked. Paraphrase-trained similarity cannot separate two documents that say the same thing about different systems. Metadata-trained similarity cannot tell you that two documents from the same team disagree. **The failure is not noise; it is the definition working correctly.**

5. **Dimension buys capacity, not meaning.** [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md)'s account applies unchanged, and the bias-variance argument is the right one. E4 is the demonstration: the same trained model at a lower dimension count holds less, and what it drops is not chosen by you.

6. **Nearest-neighbour relations are not symmetric in usefulness.** In high-dimensional spaces some points are the nearest neighbour of very many others. Those points dominate results, they are usually uninformative, and a retrieval system that has not checked for them has a failure mode it cannot see. **The platform makes this worse in a documented way and the documentation says so:** nearest-neighbour search always returns the requested `k` neighbours, so a query with no good match returns `k` bad ones rather than nothing. There is a preview `threshold` parameter that filters low-scoring results before fusion, and it is off unless you set it.

7. **You can inspect a similarity notion without the training data.** Construct probe pairs that differ in exactly one respect — same content different system, same system different conclusion, same words different sentiment, same topic different recency — and observe what the deployment does. This is the method the day assesses, and it is the only content in the day the design expects to still be true when this cohort has been working for a decade.

8. **The corpus is part of the behaviour.** The same model over a narrow corpus and over a broad one produces different neighbourhood structure, so an embedding's characterisation does not transfer between deployments.

9. **Perishable, and marked as such.** The dimension ranges are model properties and they differ within one family: `text-embedding-ada-002` is fixed at 1,536, `text-embedding-3-small` accepts 1 to 1,536, `text-embedding-3-large` accepts 1 to 3,072. A single request accepts up to 2,048 inputs, each under a per-input token cap, **and additionally fails if the sum across all inputs exceeds 300,000 tokens** — a limit satisfied by every individual input and violated by the batch. The catalogue's embedding leaderboard exists and is preview; its quality index is an average of accuracy scores over benchmark datasets targeting information retrieval, document clustering and summarisation, with ranking reported as normalised discounted cumulative gain at ten results. **Every one of those numbers is this vendor's, at this date, and none of them is this programme's claim.** The documentation states the caveat itself and it is the one to carry: public benchmarks use standardised datasets and might not reflect performance on your specific data. It is silent on the thing you would most want — nothing anywhere states how a leaderboard position translates to retrieval quality on a corpus like yours.

10. **Defaults are opinions.** Five were chosen for you today and none was announced. `encoding_format` returns `float` unless you ask for `base64`. `dimensions` defaults to the model's maximum, so the most expensive option is the one you get by not deciding. **Cosine is the default vector similarity distance function**, which means point 2's geometry question was answered before you asked it. If you set neither `k` nor `top`, a response returns fifty results. And in a hybrid query the default reporting mode counts every document matching the text query **including documents that were never retrieved for ranking at all**, because they fell outside a recall window whose default is a thousand — so the count on your dashboard describes a set your users could not have seen. **An engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system**, and this generalises far beyond this vendor.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Point 2 carries the strong-form scope statement and is deliberately phrased as a measurement obligation rather than a fact, because the specific geometric findings about specific model families are the most perishable content in the module; its second sentence is new and is the vendor stating the same rule in its own troubleshooting register, which is worth more to a student than the principle alone. Points 3 and 4 are the retained pair. Point 6's second half converts a hubness argument into an operable check. Point 7 is the method. **Point 9 is the day's perishable platform content and point 10 is its required naming of defaults**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md); point 10's last clause is the day's first documented case of a metric that is not measuring what its name says, which [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md) point 6 and [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md) both retrieve.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 4. The job, the probe set and the judgement are the student's. This phase produces a build.**

Students use Copilot in agent mode, through the Azure MCP Server, to ship a working characterisation harness against a real Azure AI Search index over the substrate corpus. They:

1. **State the job.** What will this similarity be used for, and who decides whether it worked?
2. **Construct a probe set:** pairs differing in exactly one respect, covering **at least four respects**, drawn from the substrate's own documents.
3. **Predict**, for each deployment and each respect, what it will do.
4. **Run the probes** against the index, and record the scores and the ranks separately.
5. **Report** which deployment's notion of similarity matches the job.
6. **State one respect on which your chosen deployment is wrong for the job,** and what you would do about it.
7. **State what happens if none of them fit** — which is a real outcome, and per instrumentation rule 6 must name the occasion at which it is addressed, not merely be noted.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned. Not a transcript.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the harness's own checks would have caught an intervention-worthy error — an assertion that every probe's query vector came from the same deployment as the document vectors it is scored against is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch. Both specific, both from this build.

**Fading:** step 2 is worked with the room, with two respects supplied and two required from the student. **Step 3 is unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 4, now shipping a working harness rather than reading supplied neighbour tables. Step 2 is the day's technical content and step 6 is its discriminator — a deployment chosen with no named weakness has not been characterised. **Step 4's separation of score from rank is new and is load-bearing**: a student who records only the score has recorded the quantity point 2 forbids them to compare across deployments, and the rank is the quantity that survives. Step 7 is the first artefact element in the programme written under [instrumentation rule 6](/wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md), which requires a deferred response to name its occasion; here the occasion is [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md), where a student can train one. The intervention record's fail condition is written with the sensitivity escape [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) establishes tomorrow and [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 corrected.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate change in which one embedding was replaced by another with a better published benchmark score, and retrieval quality **fell for one group of users**. The replacement's notion of similarity was paraphrase; that group's need was same-system-different-conclusion, which paraphrase similarity actively collapses. Attention is directed at the fact that the aggregate metric improved.

Then the agent case, from this day's own build. A student asked Copilot to add a relevance cut-off so that weak matches stopped reaching users. **The agent produced a correct, well-structured, plausible-looking filter and took the threshold from a number the student had quoted from a different deployment's scores.** It ran. It returned results. Nothing errored, nothing warned, and the cut-off was meaningless — because a similarity score is a quantity in one model's geometry and the threshold was expressed in another's.

Attention goes to why review did not catch it: the code was clean, the constant had a comment explaining where it came from, and the comment was true. **The reviewer checked that the number had a provenance and not that the provenance was the same model.** This is point 2 failing in the one register where it is invisible, and it is the failure the vendor's own troubleshooting table lists.

Then students, in threes: a time you assumed two things were similar and acted on it, or a place today where you accepted agent output you had not checked.

**Warrant.** Error management targeting points 3 and 4. The instructor case is chosen because the change was justified by a real improvement on a real metric — it is not a story about carelessness, which is what makes it transferable. The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen so that the failure is invisible to the review the students would actually have performed: the artefact was correct in every respect a reader checks and wrong in the one respect the day exists to teach.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **their step 1 job and step 5 choice.**

The partner's task: **construct a query for which that choice returns something confidently wrong** — not merely unhelpful, but wrong in a way a user would act on.

Then: does the student's step 6 remedy handle it?

Per rule 3, the outcome is recorded in the artefact. Per rule 5, this reuses [S029](/wiki/seminars/S029.md)'s dual-defeat structure: the constructed case must defeat both the choice and the stated remedy.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with an adversarial construction. The demand for *confidently wrong* rather than *poor* is what makes the phase a test of the similarity notion rather than of the corpus.

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- Item 1's own groupings compared against the four deployments: how many students' unaided grouping matched E1, E2, E3, E4 or none. **The distribution is the day's most quoted result.**
- Step 3 predictions against step 4 results, **split by respect**, so that students see which respects they could predict and which they could not.
- The cohort's step 5 choices against the four jobs.
- **Item 5 opened.** The sealed Copilot predictions are read against what the agent actually built, and the cohort's hit rate is recorded. Both halves count: predicting that the agent would err is easy, and naming the error is the measure.
- Each student names one place they have assumed "similar" was objective.
- Retrieval schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 5.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in IS202 with IS203. Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: a stated job with its decider; a probe set covering at least four respects, at least two of them the student's own; pre-committed predictions; probe results with score and rank recorded separately; a chosen deployment with its reason; a named respect on which it is wrong for the job with a remedy; the none-fit response with its named occasion; the phase 7 outcome; **the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

**A probe set whose respects are all supplied fails.** The construction is the assessed skill.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

No entrustment judgement. The quarter's is at [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md).

**Instrumentation, and rule 6.** This is the first day designed under instrumentation rule 6, and its three instruments name their occasions explicitly:

- **Item 1 grouping distribution** — if a large majority match E3, the cohort's intuitive notion of similarity is behavioural rather than semantic, which changes how [S056](/wiki/seminars/S056.md) frames the lexical baseline. **Response occasion: S056, phase 1 construction.**
- **Step 3 prediction accuracy by respect** — respects the cohort cannot predict are carried into [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)'s training task as required probe dimensions. **Response occasion: S057, step 2.**
- **Phase 7 defeat rate** — a low rate indicates the partners could not construct confidently-wrong cases, which is a finding about the probe method rather than about the deployments, and is carried to [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md)'s benchmark work. **Response occasion: S059, phase 3.**

Item 5's sealed prediction accuracy is the module's first frontier-calibration datum and is read by the following year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), per instrumentation rule 9. It is a sample and this day says so, per instrumentation rule 3: one cohort, one substrate, one corpus, one index schema, on a task chosen by staff to have a known failure available.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** *Similar* is a construction. Every embedding was trained against a similarity signal — co-occurrence, paraphrase, clicks, labels, metadata — and **that signal is the operative definition your system now holds.** What the signal encodes it also freezes, and choosing an embedding is choosing whose notion of similarity your users will live inside.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** Embedding similarity as a training-signal artefact is verified at [research/02-technical-foundations/thakur-beir.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/thakur-beir.md). **Hubness and the non-comparability of scores across models are not verified here** and are carried for direction and mechanism only, which is why point 2 is a measurement obligation and no geometric claim is made about any named model family. The operational half is vendor documentation: a query vector from a different model is a listed cause of quietly wrong ranking.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Build probe pairs differing in exactly one respect** and observe what the deployment does.
- **Record rank separately from score.** The score is what you may not compare across deployments; the rank survives.
- **Name one respect on which your chosen deployment is wrong for the job.** A deployment with no named weakness has not been characterised.
- **Pin the revision, host and model.** A threshold is a per-model, per-corpus empirical quantity, so one quoted without its deployment is unusable — see the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md).

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Reading a clean cut-off, a real constant and an accurate comment as a checked decision. **The reviewer confirmed that the number had a provenance and not that the provenance was the same model.**
