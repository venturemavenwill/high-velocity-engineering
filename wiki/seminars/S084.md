# S084 · Change, Resistance and the Adoption You Cannot Mandate

**Module:** [M17](/wiki/modules/M17.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** PE · ME · all
**Whitepaper:** [WP-084](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-084.md)
**Depends on:** S037, S081, S082
**Platform anchor:** the AI gateway's per-consumer token metrics — `llm-emit-token-metric` with custom dimensions — against the substrate's own usage series · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (monitor) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

The service works. It has a boundary that includes the people who carry it, a party set with both axes populated, and a risk record that a decision-maker has acted on. Every day of this module so far has produced something that is true about the organisation.

**None of it makes anybody change what they do.**

That is the last of the module's three failure classes and it is the one engineers have the worst instincts about. The system is correct, evaluated and available; the people who were meant to use it have not; and the two interventions that come to mind are training and communication. Both are cheap, both are what everyone does, and both address one of the four things a person needs before they change how they work. **The other three are where the obstacle almost always is.**

The deeper error underneath is more expensive. **The organisation was working before you arrived.** The workaround your system replaces was somebody's solution to a problem you have not identified, and removing it without identifying it is the mechanism by which a correct system makes things worse. [S037](/wiki/seminars/S037.md) established, two years ago, that what people say they do and what they do diverge and that the divergence is not deception. **The workaround is the divergence with a purpose**, and the person maintaining it is the person whose adoption decision you are waiting on.

And adoption is not the goal. Adoption *by whom, for what* is the question, and the module's payload today is a deployment that was adopted, retained and used exactly as designed — by the wrong people, for a different purpose, successfully — so that every claim in its business case is now false and somebody has to be told.

The target: **adoption is a rate rather than a state, resistance is information before it is an obstacle, and the only things within your control are the conditions of use.**

---

## The delivery standard on this day

This day carries **principle 3 of the [FDE Delivery Standard](/wiki/program/14-FDE-Delivery-Standard.md): extend what the customer already runs before introducing anything new** — applied to a process rather than to a platform, which is the harder half. [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md) took the principle as a constraint on architecture. Here the thing the customer already owns is **the workaround**, and phase 4 point 8 is the principle stated as a warning: find the problem before you remove the solution.

**What the principle costs here is the assumption that your system is the improvement.** The workaround is usually visibly worse than the thing replacing it, which is exactly why it gets removed without inquiry, and the cost of removing it lands on whoever was carrying the problem it solved. The principle also costs you the intervention you came in wanting: it puts step 2's inquiry before step 7's remedy, and what step 2 most often establishes is that the remedy was aimed at the wrong condition.

**The playbook artefact that belongs here is the team agreement, and specifically the manifesto**, whose stated purpose is **to surface disagreement** and which is written by the team rather than issued to it. That is point 2 in a second register — disagreement recorded before the work is resistance collected while it is still information. It is also point 7's limit stated as a form: an agreement issued rather than written is a mandate, and use cannot be mandated. **Neither source reports any outcome for the practice**, and [WP-084](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-084.md)'s evidence status says so.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

A change this cohort shipped is named — one from the [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) release records, in service for several weeks.

1. **What proportion of the target population is using it today?**
2. **Name one person in that population who has not adopted it, and state what you believe they would say if asked why.**
3. You have shipped something correct that people are not using. **List every intervention available to you.**
4. **Predict: at six weeks from today, what proportion will be using it?**
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to measure adoption of this change from the substrate's own telemetry. **Write down what it will produce, and name the one thing it will get wrong.**

Item 1 is the day's opening finding and nobody in the room can answer it, including the students who shipped the change and wrote its detection commitment. Item 3 is the pretest for point 4 and the expected answers are training and communication, in that order.

**Item 5's expected result is that the agent will return a number quickly and that the number will not be about people.** The gateway meters requests and tokens; a person is not one of the things it meters unless somebody made it one. **Predicting that the agent will answer is not a prediction. Naming what it will count instead is.**

**Warrant.** Pretesting an adoption question the cohort has never asked about their own work, on a change they own. Item 1's unanswerability is the finding and is not a trick: nothing in the release record required an adoption measure, which is itself the point. Item 2 requires the reason in the other person's voice, which is the construction [WP-017](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-017.md) §6 identified as this programme's earliest stakeholder-representation measure and which is being asked here of a person the student can actually go and ask. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 and does not migrate, per instrumentation rule 8.

A party with no channel to object has a channel to not comply.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**The measurement first, executed (6 min).** Each group asks the agent, through the Azure MCP Server, for the current adoption rate of the change named at item 1. It returns a number in about ninety seconds: requests, tokens, calls per day, a chart. **It cannot resolve any of it to people**, because the gateway emits an aggregate and no dimension was configured when the change shipped. Groups record the number they were given and the question it actually answers. **That artefact stays on the table for the rest of the phase**, and it is the reason item 1 was unanswerable — not that the substrate is unmeasured, but that it is measured in a unit that is not a person.

Then groups receive **four changes, matched on technical quality and on the strength of their evaluation**, each with its adoption record over the following six months and the intervention its deploying team made. **These four are demonstrated rather than operated** — they are six-month histories from other deployments and there is nothing to run them against — **and they are not assessed.**

**Case selection is a function of the [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md) phase 3 result, per instrumentation rule 6, and the branch is disclosed to students.** The four cases are chosen so that their decisive differences fall on **whichever of the three carrying modes this cohort failed to produce unprompted at [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md)**. If the cohort omitted *licensing*, the decisive resistance in C2 and C4 is a manager declining to let correct output be acted on; if it omitted *routing*, the decisive resistance is in what reaches the system at all; if it omitted *absorbing*, in who does the correction work afterwards. **Sampling is disclosed** per instrumentation rule 3, including the fact that the selection is conditioned on the cohort's own prior performance.

- **C1** — not adopted; the target population was never told it existed. A communication failure, which is the one the room expects and is the rarest of the four in the substrate's record.
- **C2** — not adopted; the population understood it perfectly and it made their work worse, in a way the deploying team could not see because the cost fell on a step nobody was measuring. **The resistance was correct and was the most accurate information available about the change.**
- **C3** — adopted enthusiastically, then abandoned in the fifth week when the first case it handled badly arrived. The adoption curve rises and then falls, and every report written in weeks one to four is true.
- **C4** — **the payload.** Adopted, retained, and used exactly as designed — **by a different team, for a different purpose.** The team it was built for never used it. The deployment is reported as a success, the usage figures are real, and **every claim in the business case that justified it is false.**

Groups rank the four by how well the change was deployed and commit. **Then the adoption records and the interventions are supplied.**

The payload is **C4**, and it defeats the room's expectation that adoption is the success criterion. It is not; it is a proxy, and here the proxy is fully satisfied while the thing it stands for did not happen. **A deployment whose success has nothing to do with its justification is a governance finding**, not a win, and the person who has to be told is the one who approved it.

Task after: **for each, name the intervention that would have worked and the intervention the deploying team actually made.** Then: for C4, name who has to be told and what they have to be told. **Then, holding the number the agent gave you: would that number have distinguished C4 from C1?** It would not, and groups say why in one sentence.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases holding technical quality and evaluation strength constant and varying only the adoption outcome, with the six-month record as adjudicator. C4 is the payload because it separates adoption from benefit, which the room will not do unprompted, and because it makes the module's [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md) act necessary again on an uncomfortable subject. C2 is placed second because its lesson — that resistance carried information nobody collected — is the day's most portable and would otherwise be crowded out. **The branch on [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md)'s result is what makes this phase a response rather than a repetition, and the students are told that the cases they are holding are a consequence of what their cohort could not see three weeks ago.** **The executed opening is placed before the cases so that the task-after's third item can be asked of the room's own number rather than of a supplied one**: C4 is a story about somebody else until a student notices their own instrument would have reported it as a success.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **Adoption is a rate, not a state, and it decays.** C3. A single measurement at week two is a point on a curve whose shape you have not observed, and every adoption claim in this programme's own release records is exactly that. Measure it over time or you have not measured it.

2. **Resistance is information before it is an obstacle.** C2. The first question is what the resister knows that you do not, and it is a question with an answer surprisingly often. This is [S037](/wiki/seminars/S037.md)'s say-do divergence with a motive attached: the person is not describing their work incorrectly, they are declining to change it, and the reason is a fact about the work.

3. **Four things a person needs before they change what they do:** to know why, to want to, to be able to, and to be allowed to. Each fails differently, each has a different remedy, and **only the third is addressed by training. This enumeration omits two cases: the person who is right not to change, which is C2 and is common; and the change being imposed by somebody with no standing to impose it, which is [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md)'s and which no intervention repairs. Safe as a diagnostic for a change your organisation has decided to make and can justify; unsafe as a checklist for producing compliance, because applied that way it converts a legitimate refusal into a communications problem and the fourth condition into an instruction.**

   **Amended 2026-07-30, on a full reading of the individual-level source behind this point.** Three corrections follow and **none of them changes the four conditions**, which are this design's own construction and are what the specification checks. The original shorthand is left standing above so that the correction has something to correct.

   - **The first condition is not only *why*.** The source's own definition of the corresponding element covers the nature of the change, the reason for it, the risk of not changing, its internal and external drivers **and** what is in it for the person. Leading with future-state detail still fails, but that is a sequencing caution rather than a definition, and *to know why* was narrower than the source and narrower than the diagnostic needs to be.
   - **The ordering is argued by entailment and by nothing else.** The source reasons that desire cannot come before awareness, knowledge cannot come before desire, and so down the chain — an argument from what the words mean. **No evidence is offered for it, no hedge accompanies it, and no test of it is reported anywhere.** This day therefore uses the conditions as a diagnostic and asserts no ordering strength, which is the position it already held and which now has a stated reason rather than an implied one.
   - **Where changes most often fail is not settled, and this day must not imply it is.** The source's case narratives repeatedly land on *wanting to*; the single systematic survey it reports names *not knowing* as the leading cause of resistance. It does not reconcile them. The instruction that survives is step 3's: you find the failing condition by asking a named person, not by assuming which one it is.

   **A fourth correction concerns the instrument rather than the model.** The source's own method for finding the failing element is a threshold on an anonymous self-rating on a five-point scale, treating the bottom three points as weak. **No inter-rater agreement, calibration or validation is reported for it**, and two assessors can reach different answers about the same change. This day does not use that instrument — step 3 requires evidence from a named person instead — and the reason is now on the record.

4. **Training is the default intervention and it addresses one of the four.** It is the commonest professional error in this material, it is cheap, it is visible, it produces a record that the deploying team did something, and it is nearly always aimed at the wrong condition. Item 3's answers will show it before this point is made.

5. **The cost of the change falls somewhere other than the benefit, and usually on a different person.** [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md)'s moved work with a name attached, and the phase 2 bridge made explicit: **whoever bears the cost decides whether it is adopted**, regardless of who was consulted and regardless of who signed the business case.

6. **Adoption by the wrong party is a governance finding.** C4. The usage is real, the claim in the business case is now false, and somebody with authority is operating on the false version. Telling them is [S083](/wiki/seminars/S083.md)'s act performed on a subject where the student is the bearer of unwelcome news about their own success.

7. **You cannot mandate use and you can mandate the conditions of use.** What is actually within an engineer's control: the default, the cost of the alternative path, the visibility of the outcome, and who gets asked. That is a short list and it is a real one. Everything else belongs to somebody with authority, per [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 3, and an engineer who attempts it without the authority produces resentment and no change.

8. **The organisation was working before you arrived.** The workaround you are replacing is somebody's solution to a problem you have not identified. **Find the problem before you remove the solution**, or you will ship a correct system that makes the work worse and you will not know why for months. This is the day's most durable sentence and it is the one the cohort is least prepared to hear, because the workaround is usually visibly worse than the system replacing it.

**Points 9 and 10 are the platform instance and come after the argument, per this module's convention.**

9. **Adoption is measurable per consumer, and yours is not measured that way.** The AI gateway can emit token metrics **per consumer**, with custom dimensions attached; the documentation's own example emits client IP, API identifier and a user identifier read from a request header, and the metrics land in Application Insights where a built-in analytics dashboard reads them. Your change emits an aggregate, because **a dimension is a thing somebody configures and nobody did.** Two constraints travel with this and both matter: custom metrics with dimensions are an **Azure Monitor preview feature** with its own documented limitations, and the token-metric policy is **not available on the Consumption tier at all**, so the measurement you want may be unavailable at the tier your service runs on. And the consequence that makes point 1 a mechanism rather than a slogan: **you cannot backfill a dimension.** A series emitted without it is aggregate forever. C3's curve is unrecoverable at week six if nobody configured the dimension at week zero, which is why an adoption claim has to be designed before the change ships and not measured after it. **Safe** for establishing which consumers of a gateway-fronted model are using it, at the granularity of whatever identity your own header carries. **Unsafe** as a count of people: a header value is an identity your code put there, a shared service principal makes a hundred users look like one, and a dimension is not a person unless somebody arranged for it to be. **Perishable:** the policy name, the tier restrictions, the preview status. **Durable:** granularity is a design decision taken before the measurement exists, and it is not retrospectively available.

10. **A default the platform chose for you, and it is the dangerous kind.** With no dimension configured you do not get nothing. **You get a number**, and it is correct, and it is about requests. A default that yields silence gets investigated; a default that yields a plausible figure gets reported. That is why item 1 was unanswerable and the agent still answered it in ninety seconds at phase 3 — and it is [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md)'s construct-instrument gap arriving in the one register where the cohort had stopped looking for it, because a usage figure feels like a count rather than a measurement. Note also what the documentation does not say: it specifies the policy, the dimensions, the tiers and the preview status precisely and **states no figure anywhere for how well any of it corresponds to use by a human being.** There is no such figure, there is no way for the vendor to produce one, and the gap is yours.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3's four cases. Points 1, 2, 5 and 6 are each reached from a case rather than asserted; point 5 is additionally produced by the phase 2 bridge; point 8 is reached from C2 and is exercised at phase 5 step 2 before this phase names it, since step 2's demand for the workaround's purpose is what makes the sentence something the student has already done. Point 3 carries the strong-form scope statement and it is the one that matters most in this day, because the four conditions read as a compliance checklist unless the omission is stated. **Point 9 is reached from the phase 3 executed opening and the task-after's third item rather than asserted, and carries its own strong-form scope statement; point 10 is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required naming of a default the platform chose, and it is the day's sharpest instance because the default is not an absence — it is a plausible wrong answer, delivered fast.**

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 6. The adoption position is the student's, and one prediction is tested this afternoon.**

Students take one change the cohort owns — their own where possible, from the [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) records.

1. **Name the change and the population that must change what they do**, by name where the population is small enough and by role with a count where it is not.
2. **State what those people currently do**, from the [S037](/wiki/seminars/S037.md) record and from observation — **including the workaround — and state what problem the workaround solves.** Where you do not know, mark do-not-know.
3. **Classify the adoption risk against the four conditions**, with the evidence for each, and mark do-not-know where you have none.
4. **State where the cost of the change falls and where the benefit falls, by named party**, and say whether they are the same party.
5. **Predict the adoption rate at two weeks and at six weeks**, with **the number that would show you wrong and the date it becomes checkable**, per the class-5 expiry discipline from [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) step 7.
6. **Test one prediction now.** A real substrate user from the target population is present for thirty minutes. Ask about the thing you predicted. **Record what you hear, including anything that contradicts you, in three columns — said, done, inferred.**
7. **State the one intervention you would make, which of the four conditions it addresses, and why not one of the other three — and name the PE301 material you applied at steps 3 and 7.**
8. **Ship the measurement you did not have.** Thirty minutes, with Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server. Configure the gateway to emit a token metric carrying **a dimension that corresponds to the population you named at step 1**, and return **a weekly series rather than a point.** State in one line what a single value of that dimension actually is — a user, a service principal, a team, a shared mailbox — and whether it is a person. Where the tier or the preview status prevents it, **say so and ship the closest thing the platform will emit**, with the gap named.

**Step 8 is performed first in the room and is listed last because it is the position's evidence rather than the position.**

**The user is present for the last thirty minutes and is not there to be persuaded.** Six minutes each.

**The build is bounded and the encounter is not.** Step 8 is timeboxed to thirty of the ninety minutes. **The user's thirty minutes are timetabled first and the build is what compresses**, because a policy can be finished on Thursday and a person who came in on their own time cannot be re-booked. **A group that arrives at step 6 with an unconfigured dimension and a good question has made the correct trade**, and the specification says so.

**The weekly series is this day's repair of a conceded defect.** [WP-084](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-084.md) §8 objection 2 conceded that the day teaches adoption as a curve and measures two points chosen against a known shape, and named the fix: the substrate can return a weekly figure at the cost of a query. **Step 8 takes it.** The two-week and six-week predictions at step 5 are now marked on a series rather than checked against two points, and [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) receive the curve.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave, in order, including the ones you abandoned, each with why.
- **Intervention record.** Every correction and what it was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a policy that refuses to emit when the dimension expression resolves to a constant, or a query that flags a series in which every value shares one dimension key, is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have and one thing it did badly that you had to catch, both from this build.

**Fading:** step 3's four-condition grid is supplied; step 5's forecast form reuses [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) step 7's. **Steps 2 and 4 are unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 6 on a change the students shipped, with a member of the affected population present, which satisfies instrumentation rule 1 — the person whose adoption is in question is the one who supplies the observation, rather than the student estimating it or a peer role-playing it. Step 2 is unsupported because finding the workaround's purpose is point 8's whole content and a supplied prompt would collect a description of the workaround rather than its function. Step 4 is unsupported because naming the cost-bearer is the day's judgement. **Step 6's three-column record is [S037](/wiki/seminars/S037.md)'s discipline at a two-year gap and is the phase 7 object. Step 7's PE301 clause is the prior-course join first used at [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md) step 7, and per instrumentation rule 5 as amended it is a contingent reuse: a reported failure of that element at S081 retracts it here.** **Step 8's one-line demand — say what a value of the dimension actually is — is the whole build's discriminator**, and it is the sentence that separates a student who configured a measurement from one who configured a metric.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate change with thorough training, high satisfaction scores from the people trained, and near-zero sustained use. The reason was found eight months later and was a single approval step, held by somebody outside the target population, that made using the system slower than not using it. Attention is directed at the satisfaction survey: **it was a valid instrument, answered honestly, and it could not have detected this**, because it asked about the system and the obstacle was not in the system. That is [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md)'s boundary point with a measurement attached, and the survey's designer had drawn the boundary where every competent person draws it.

Then the agent case: a Copilot-generated adoption dashboard that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. Asked to measure per-user adoption, it wrote the token-metric policy correctly, took the dimension expressions from the documentation's own example, deployed cleanly, and produced a dashboard with a rising per-consumer curve. **The reviewer checked the policy against the documentation and it matched the documentation exactly.** Every request from the deployment arrives through one service principal, so the dimension resolved to a single value, and the rising curve was the same population using the service more — which is a different fact from more of the population using it, and the two are indistinguishable in the chart. Attention goes to the review: **it verified that the code was written correctly and could not have detected that the thing measured was not the thing asked about.** This is the day's point 10 in the machine register, and it is the failure step 8's one-line demand exists to prevent.

Then students, in threes: **something you were asked to adopt and did not.** What would have changed your mind, and did anyone ask you?

**Warrant.** Error management targeting points 3, 4 and 7 at once, with a case in which no individual made an error and the instrument was sound. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is deliberately the same failure as the instructor's** — a valid instrument answering a question nobody asked — which is what prevents the room reading the agent case as being about the agent. The student disclosure is the day's affective centre: a room that has spent ninety minutes analysing other people's non-adoption needs to be in the category, and the second question is asked because the answer is almost always *no* — which is what makes step 6's thirty minutes with a real user feel like a method rather than a courtesy.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs, exchanging **step 6 field records.**

Each student reads the partner's three-column record and must **identify one sentence recorded in the *done* or *said* column that is in fact an inference**, and point at the words. The partner then says whether they agree, and if they do, moves it.

Both students perform both roles. Where a pair cannot find one, that is recorded as the outcome, per specification rule 3, and the assessors read it as a claim about the record rather than as an absence.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with bilateral production, re-testing [S037](/wiki/seminars/S037.md)'s said/done/inferred discipline at a two-year gap on a record the student made ninety minutes earlier. **This is the direct test of the forward claim [WP-037](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-037.md) §6 made about this day** — that a student who has watched someone work has a category for the difference between an adoption failure that is visible in observation and one that is visible only in report. The structure is deliberately not an absent-party role-play and not a good-faith reconstruction, both of which have now run in this module.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in PE390 with CAP390. Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the change and its population; the current practice including the workaround and the problem it solves, or a do-not-know; the four-condition classification with evidence and do-not-know marks; the cost-bearer and benefit-holder by named party with a statement of whether they are the same; the two-week and six-week predictions with the falsifying number and the check dates in the same field as the predictions; the step 6 three-column record including anything that contradicted the student; the intervention with the condition it addresses and why not the other three; the named PE301 material; the phase 7 outcome; **the shipped per-consumer measurement with its weekly series and the one-line statement of what a value of its dimension is; the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

**A classification with no do-not-know marks at step 3 fails**, unless every one of the four conditions carries evidence from a named person. Four conditions confidently evidenced from the student's own judgement is the pattern of a student who has asked nobody, and it is [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md)'s do-not-know requirement applied to a population rather than to a constraint.

**A measurement whose dimension is not stated in the one-line form fails.** *This dimension is a service principal and is not a person* is a pass. *This dimension is the user* without evidence is a fail, and it is the same failure as a maintainer named as a function at [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md).

**A build that could not be completed does not fail**, provided the student states what the tier or the preview status prevented and ships the closest thing the platform will emit with the gap named. **A build that displaced the step 6 encounter fails**, and the user's own attendance record is the check.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**A step 6 record containing nothing that contradicted the student is examined.** It may be true; it is more often a record of a conversation the student steered, which is [S037](/wiki/seminars/S037.md) point 4's failure at a two-year gap. The assessors read the record's inferred column and the phase 7 outcome before concluding.

**An intervention that is training or communication is not a failure**, and the specification says so. Both are legitimate and both are sometimes right. What is checked is the third clause: **why not one of the other three conditions**, evidenced.

**Adoption rate is not assessed.** A student whose change is not being used has not failed the specification. This decoupling is stated at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) as well as here, because an assessed adoption rate would make step 5's prediction strategic and would make step 6 a persuasion exercise, which is the opposite of the day.

**The four historical changes are not assessed**, because they are demonstrated rather than operated.

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md).

**Rule-6 discharge.** One obligation lands on this day and **the element exists.**

- **[S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md)** named this day's phase 3 as the response occasion for its task-after category coverage, specifying that the four adoption cases be selected so that their decisive differences fall on whichever carrying mode the cohort failed to produce unprompted. **The element exists: phase 3's case set is a function of the S081 phase 3 reading, the substitution rule is stated for all three modes, the branch is disclosed to students at phase 3 and reported at phase 8, and the classification error rate is examined for concentration on the substituted mode.** The obligation is discharged.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions:

- **The step 5 adoption predictions against measured rates.** **Response occasion for the two-week prediction: [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md), whose transfer inventory must state, for each transferred item, the measured usage at two weeks against the student's prediction.** **Response occasion for the six-week prediction: [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md), where the measured six-week rate is set against the prediction in the student's own final defence material.** **Both are now delivered as the weekly series step 8 produces, with the student's two predicted points marked on it**, which is the fix [WP-084](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-084.md) §8 objection 2 named and handed to those days. [WP-086](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-086.md) §7 and [WP-090](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist and whether the series arrived.
- **The phase 3 branch outcome** — which carrying mode was substituted for, and whether the cohort's classification errors concentrated on it. **Response occasion: [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md), phase 3, whose scope statement must name which carrying mode the receiving organisation is required to supply, with a named maintainer, and state what happens to the scope if they do not supply it.**

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** Adoption is a rate rather than a state, and it decays. **Resistance is information before it is an obstacle.** The cost of a change usually falls on someone other than the beneficiary, and whoever bears it decides whether it survives. And the organisation was working before you arrived: find what the workaround solves before you remove it.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** Contextual inquiry and say–do divergence are verified at [research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md](/research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md). The individual-level conditions for behaviour change follow [research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md) **for direction and mechanism only, and no ordering strength or success rate is asserted for any named change framework.** **Deepened and bounded 2026-07-30**, when that source was read in full. Its element sequence is argued **purely by entailment, with no evidence and no hedge**, and its evidence base is the publishing firm's own benchmarking reports, its case narratives, the author's show-of-hands polling at conferences, and testimonial. **No independent evaluation of the model is cited anywhere in it**, the model is a registered trademark of the firm that sells training and certification in it, and the author writes of his own framework that he did not view it "to be some type of breakthrough". The organisational-change material carried alongside it at [research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md](/research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md) is bounded harder still: its author states in his own preface that he has drawn neither examples nor major ideas from any published source except his own writing and has not tried to cite evidence from other sources, so **the book cites nothing**; its stage sequence is explicitly usual rather than strict and **operating in several stages at once is his own position rather than a criticism of him**; and **no change-failure rate appears anywhere in it**, so the percentage commonly attributed to him is not his and is not repeated here. **No magnitude is asserted for adoption decay.** The metering documentation states policies, tiers and preview status precisely and **states nothing about how any of it corresponds to use by a person.**

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Design the granularity before the change ships.** You cannot backfill a dimension, and a series emitted without one is aggregate forever.
- **Say in one line what a value of your dimension actually is** — a user, a team, a service principal — and whether it is a person.
- **Name the cost-bearer and the benefit-holder separately**, and say whether they are the same party.
- **Classify what the chart licenses.** Per the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md), a rising per-consumer curve is an *observed result*; *more of the population is using it* is an *unsupported inference*.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Treating training as the intervention. It addresses one of the four conditions, it is cheap and visible, and the obstacle is nearly always one of the other three.
