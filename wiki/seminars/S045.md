## The situation

This is the stage's first assessment occasion and it marks something narrower than it appears to.

You shipped an increment. **Whether it worked is not the subject.** The subject is whether you can say where its boundary was, who moved it, and what you let past when the date stopped moving.

The internal corpus has a line about this moment that is true and dangerous in the same breath: **working software is hard to argue with, so political resistance dissolves in front of a running increment in a way it does not in front of a document.** That is an accurate observation about rooms. It is also the mechanism by which an increment ends an argument without settling the disagreement, and the disagreement returns later with interest.

Answer these in writing before you begin, and submit them.

1. What shipped, stated as something a named recipient can now do?
2. What did you decide not to ship, and on what day did you decide it?
3. What did the review not read?
4. Who could have refused this increment, and did they have the opportunity?
5. What did you learn that changes the next slice, and what would you have had to observe to learn it?

**Item 2's second clause is the assessment's centre of gravity.** Almost everybody can name an exclusion. Far fewer can say when it was excluded, and an exclusion dated to the moment of shipping was not a decision, it was an outcome.

## What you must produce

An **increment pack**, defended in front of an assessor who has read your [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md) proof pack and your [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md) slice definition and nothing else.

- **The increment**, and the named recipient who received it.
- **The slice definition** as written, with every departure from it marked and dated.
- **The exclusion list**, with each item's current status: still excluded, promoted to a new item, or built after all.
- **The scope ledger** from [S042](/wiki/seminars/S042.md), with its refusals intact.
- **The specification** from [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md), including the untestable residue and who held it.
- **The review record** from [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md), with what was not read still stated as not read.
- **The capacity statement**, unrevised.
- **The what-this-does-not-establish page** — everything a reader of the increment could reasonably infer that you did not demonstrate.

## What good looks like

These are the assessed elements. Each is checked as present and adequate, or not.

- **Every departure from the slice definition is marked and dated**, and a departure discovered at shipping is recorded as a departure rather than as a revision.
- **The increment's recipient is named and was in a position to refuse it.** An increment received only by the delivery team is submitted with that stated as its finding.
- **Tests arrived with the code**, in the same change, and the pack shows this rather than asserting it.
- **The review record still says what was not read.** A pack in which everything turned out to have been read is checked against the time budget it was conducted under.
- **Something was refused**, in the ledger or in the review, and the pack names it.
- **The capacity statement is unrevised** and is reconciled against what the increment actually produced.
- **The untestable residue has a holder**, and the pack says whether that person actually looked.
- **Nothing in the pack asserts a magnitude that was not measured by a named instrument.**
- **The what-this-does-not-establish page exists and is specific**, naming inferences rather than gesturing at limits.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Whether the increment works is not marked. Whether the recipient was pleased is not marked. The quality, elegance or size of the code is not marked. The number of items shipped, refused or recorded is not marked. Whether the slice was the right slice is not marked. Whether the exclusions should have been excluded is not marked. The pack's presentation is not marked.

## How it goes wrong

- **The tidy pack** — the departures have become revisions, the refusals have become deferrals, and the unread portion of the review has quietly closed. *Warning sign:* the pack reads better than the fortnight went.
- **The undated exclusion** — everything excluded was excluded *at the start*, on inspection of nothing. *Warning sign:* item 2's second clause is blank.
- **The internal recipient** — the increment was received by the team that built it. *Warning sign:* the demonstration needed somebody to narrate it.
- **The retrospective specification** — the tests are present and were written after the code. *Warning sign:* no check in the pack has ever been seen red.
- **The softened capacity statement** — the version in the pack is more comfortable than the version written at [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md). *Warning sign:* it no longer names anybody.
- **The increment that ended the argument** — everyone agreed once it ran, and nobody wrote down what they had disagreed about. *Warning sign:* your pack contains no unresolved objection.

## Do it

Assemble it, then attack it in this order.

**First, date every exclusion.** Go back through the ledger, the review threads and your own notes and find the day each exclusion became an exclusion. Anything you cannot date was not decided. **This is the exercise that distinguishes a delivery from a result**, and it takes longer than anybody expects because most of the dates are not written down anywhere.

**Second, reconcile the capacity statement against reality.** You predicted the review effort your rate of production would demand. Compare it to what happened. If the demand never exceeded the supply, establish whether that is because the supply was adequate or because the review was quietly narrowed to fit.

**Third, write the what-this-does-not-establish page last and specifically.** Not *this is an early increment*. Name the inferences: that it works at the volume the customer described; that it behaves on the data you did not see; that the recipient's colleagues would use it; that the exclusions are safe to exclude permanently. **This page is the pack's most valuable one and the first thing deleted before circulation**, which is why it is an assessed element here as it was at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md).

Then have an agent argue that the increment should not have shipped — that the boundary was drawn to make shipping possible rather than to make the increment valuable. Keep what is better than your own reasoning; record what you rejected. **An agent that congratulated you was not used as an instrument.**

## Show it

Defend the boundary, not the increment.

The assessor has your proof pack and your slice definition. They will ask three things: **what did you decide not to build, when did you decide it, and who was in a position to disagree?**

They will also do one thing the earlier occasions did not. **They will name something in your increment and ask why it was not an exclusion.** A candidate who can only answer *because it was needed* has not drawn a boundary; a candidate who concedes that it could have been excluded and says what that would have cost has answered the question properly, and that is a pass.

## What this does not establish

This occasion shows that you can state the boundary of what you shipped and account for how it moved. **It does not show that the boundary was well drawn**, and it cannot.

**No source behind this stage supplies a basis for marking that.** The playbook names the minimal valuable slice as a commitment and offers no comparative evidence for it or for any other practice on the site. The internal corpus reports no failed engagement, no cost side, no defect data and no post-deployment record, so it cannot supply a case in which a badly drawn slice caused harm — because it supplies no harm.

**The design's addition is that a dated exclusion is a decision and an undated one is an outcome.** That is a claim about evidence rather than about outcomes, and it has no external warrant. So does the requirement that the capacity statement travel unrevised into the pack, which exists because a statement that can be edited at assembly will be.
