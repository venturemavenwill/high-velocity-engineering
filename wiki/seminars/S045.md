# S045 · A Model You Are Willing to Defend ⊘

**Module:** [M09](/wiki/modules/M09.md) · **Quarter:** [Q5](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md) · **Complexity class:** 3
**Strands:** IS · ME · SG · PE
**Whitepaper:** [WP-045](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-045.md)
**Depends on:** S041, S042, S043, S044
**Re-tests:** S030, S040
**Platform anchor:** Foundry model deployment behind a feature flag · Foundry cloud evaluation · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server (preview) · Azure MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

⊘ **Assessment-bearing day, and the quarter's first entrustment judgement.**

---

## What this day is for

The model is trained, its estimate is bounded, its capacity is justified and its threats are enumerated. Today it goes in front of users, or it does not, and the student decides which.

The question is not whether the model is good. It is: **what are you willing to say this model will do badly, to the people it will do it to?**

A student who cannot answer that has a model they cannot defend, whatever its accuracy. A student who can answer it has something the programme calls an engineering position, and the entrustment judgement is on whether they hold one.

There is a reason this day exists in this form and it is worth stating before it begins. **The platform will make a deployment decision about your model whether or not you make one.** A fine-tuned model is evaluated for harmful output after training and before it can be deployed at all; if it scores above an acceptable rate the service returns *this model is unable to be deployed*, and the thresholds behind that sentence are fixed, are not published, cannot be modified by customers, and are not connected to any content configuration you set up. Only the outcome is recorded — **deployable or not deployable**, with no score. That gate is defensible and is probably right. It is also a decision about your model, made on your behalf, by a threshold you cannot see, reported as a binary. **Today's whole content is the difference between having that decision made for you and making one.**

---

## The entrustment judgement, stated before phase 1

> Today's judgement is on: **train, evaluate and deploy a learned component, and state what it will do badly.** It is a precursor to the first entrusted activity and is not that activity.
>
> Two assessors judge independently before conferring, and **form their level from your artefact before the defence**, per rater discipline rule 7. The expected level is 2a to 2b.
>
> **The S030 and S040 judgements are not carried forward.** Each occasion is judged on its own.
>
> **Deploying is not required to pass.** A defended decision not to deploy, with its reasons, is a pass. A deployment with no stated failure modes is not.
>
> **Phase 3 is performed without agent assistance.** This is one of three such occasions in the programme. What that means, and what happens to the result, is stated in full below.

## The unassisted-work provision

The [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) states a risk the programme cannot currently detect: **a student who has only ever built with an agent may be unable to build without one.** Exhortation does not address it. The design's response is structural and there are three occasions — [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md), this day, and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md).

**What is bounded.** Phase 3's sixty-minute decision hour. The six required outputs are written by the student with no agent in the loop: no Copilot, no chat, no completion. Every artefact the decision draws on — the evaluation, the protocol, the curve, the threat model, the run history — was built with an agent over four days and is fully available. **The material is assisted. The judgement is not.**

**Why this hour and not another.** The decision hour is the only part of the day whose content is a position rather than an artefact, and a position is the thing the programme claims an agent cannot supply. If the unassisted portion were the training or the deployment, it would measure tool fluency under deprivation, which is not a competence anybody needs. Bounding the judgement instead measures the thing the programme says is load-bearing.

**What is compared, and when.** At phase 8, after the level is formed and the assessors have left, each student runs the same six-output brief through Copilot with their own four days of artefacts attached, and reads the assisted draft against what they wrote unassisted. The comparison is theirs first. It is then reported to the programme in a fixed form: which of the six outputs the assisted draft produced better, which worse, and which it could not produce at all.

**What is not done with it.** The comparison **is not used to award a level**, is not seen by the assessors, is not recorded against the student, and cannot affect this or any later judgement. It exists so the trajectory is visible while there is still time to act on it.

**What the design expects and may be wrong about.** The expectation is that the assisted draft is better on the evaluation summary and the accept statement, comparable on the withdrawal signal, and worse on the three failure modes and the user-facing sentence — because the first two are syntheses of material on file and the last two require knowing what a particular person will do with the answer. **If the assisted draft is better on the failure modes, the programme's central claim about what an agent cannot do is wrong**, and the design would rather find that out here than assert it for three years.

---

## A stated departure from the design pattern

This is an assessment-bearing day, and per the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](/wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) phases 5 to 7 are replaced by an entrustment occasion. Two consequences follow and are named rather than left implicit.

**Phase 3 is not four contrasting cases.** It is the decision. The [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s requirement that phase 3's cases be executed rather than read applies where the day's subject permits, and this day's subject does not. **The requirement is discharged at phase 6 instead**, where four deployment decisions about the same model are laid side by side after the judgement is made — three of them made by something other than the student, all four with their real artefacts in hand.

**Phase 5 is the build.** It is a live deployment to real users behind a feature flag, and it carries the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement like any other phase 5 in this programme.

---

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items, sealed and opened at phase 8.

1. **What are you willing to claim about this model?** In sentences a user could read.
2. What are you unwilling to claim that you would like to be able to?
3. Will you deploy? Yes, no, or conditionally.
4. Predict your supervision level.
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** At phase 8 you will ask the agent to *write the deployment decision for this model.* Write down now what it will produce, and **name the one thing it will get wrong**.

**Item 1 is the [WP-041](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-041.md) §5 verification.** A cohort that will claim nothing indicates the module's front-loaded negativity did not resolve, and the result is reported at phase 8. **Item 1 is not the only reading**: WP-045 §8 argues the better instrument is the phase 3 step 3 failure-mode statements, which are produced under assessment and checkable against evidence, and both are reported.

Item 5 is the day's frontier datum and is sealed before the unassisted hour so that the student's prediction about the agent is made while they still have to do the work themselves.

**Warrant.** Pretesting the deployment position before the day's pressure, so that movement is measurable. Item 2 is where the honest gap lives. Item 5 is administered at phase 1 and may not migrate, per instrumentation rule 8.

---

## Phase 2 · Cumulative module retrieval (30 min)

Extended, unblocked, across all five days.

- The four leaks and what a held-out estimate estimates.
- The three sets and the consumption rule.
- The learning curve's three shapes.
- The actor requirement.
- Which of the module's five days would have caught the failure you are most worried about?
- Name a way this model could fail that none of the five days would have surfaced.

**Warrant.** Cumulative unblocked retrieval at gaps of one to five days, with the module's integration and boundary checks. The boundary item continues the convention from S030, S035 and S040.

---

## Phase 3 · The decision (60 min) — **unassisted**

Students work individually with everything they have produced: the evaluation and its run history, the protocol, the learning curve, the threat model, the four-check result on their deployment template, and the substrate's current state.

**No agent assistance.** No Copilot, no chat, no completion. Every artefact is available and every artefact was built with an agent; the hour's work is what to do about them.

Required output before phase 4:

1. **The decision** — deploy, do not deploy, or deploy under stated conditions.
2. **The evaluation summary,** with its licensing sentence and, for any judged score, the uncalibrated clause.
3. **What the model will do badly** — three specific failure modes, each with who experiences it and how often.
4. **What you are accepting,** from the threat model, the four-check result and the shift estimate.
5. **The signal** that would tell you to withdraw it, and its threshold.
6. **The user-facing sentence:** what a user should know, in their words, in one sentence.

Staff answer questions of fact and not of judgement.

**Warrant.** Individual decision-making with factual support only, which is the assessment's validity requirement and matches S020's construction. The unassisted bound is the programme's dependency-risk measure and is placed here because a position is what the design claims an agent cannot supply; placing it anywhere else would measure tool deprivation rather than judgement.

---

## Phase 4 · The defence (60 min)

Groups of four. Fifteen minutes each, to **two assessors and one substrate user** who would be affected by the model.

Questions published in advance:

- What does your model do, and what does your figure license?
- What will it do badly, and to whom?
- What would make you withdraw it?
- *(From the user)* What should I know before this affects me?
- If you are not deploying: what would have to be true for you to?

**Assessors form a provisional level from the artefact before the defence begins**, per rule 7, and record whether the defence confirmed, raised or lowered it.

**Warrant.** Assessment on a defensible deployment position with the affected party present. The user's question is the day's demand and cannot be answered in technical terms.

---

## Phase 5 · The deployment (35 min) — the build

Deploying students ship, through Copilot in agent mode against the Foundry MCP Server, behind a feature flag to a bounded fraction of traffic, under the [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page's disclosure conditions.

Four conditions hold and all four were stated before phase 1.

1. **Behind a feature flag, to a bounded fraction of traffic.** The flag is the withdrawal mechanism and the student is the one who can pull it.
2. **Under the substrate's disclosure conditions.** Affected users are told a student-built component is in the path. One of them was in the room an hour ago.
3. **On a deployment the student can name.** The deployment name is the value that goes in the `model` parameter of every routed request. A student who cannot say which deployment served a response cannot investigate one, and at S050 they will be asked to.
4. **With the withdrawal signal and threshold from step 5 already written,** before the deployment existed.

The deployment is created, read back and confirmed — name, status and quota — and the confirmation is part of the artefact. A deployment the student believes exists and has not read back is not a deployment.

Non-deploying students spend this phase writing **what they would need to change their decision**, which is assessed identically.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), and on this day they cover the whole module rather than this phase alone.

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent across S041 to S045, in order, including the ones you abandoned. Five days, one record.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a deployment gate that refuses a template failing any of the S044 checks, or a guard on the evaluation run status, are the obvious examples and are accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did across this module that you could not have done as fast, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch. Both specific.

**Warrant.** The deployment is an execution, not an assessment; it follows the judgement rather than preceding it. The read-back requirement exists because the module's standing claim is that a system you have not observed is a system you are assuming, and it applies to the deployment as much as to the model.

## Phase 6 · Consolidating instruction (35 min) — assessors absent

1. **What the module was for:** generalisation is the only property that matters, and every number is a proxy for it.
2. **The cohort's decisions,** shown unattributed. The deploy and do-not-deploy split, and what distinguished them — which is rarely model quality.
3. **Four deployment decisions about your model, side by side.** Each student has all four in front of them, from their own five days of artefacts.

   | Decision | Made by | On what basis | What it reports |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Deployable or not deployable | The platform's safety gate | Fixed thresholds, not published, not modifiable | A binary, no score |
   | Pass or fail on each evaluator | The evaluator threshold | A default of three on a scale of one to five | A number, a label, and a reason |
   | Pass, remediate or accept | The four infrastructure checks | Four published criteria you applied by hand | Four yes-or-no answers you wrote |
   | Deploy, or do not | You | Everything above, plus who it falls on | A position with three named failure modes |

   Three of the four were made by something that does not know who your users are. **Only the fourth can be defended, and only the fourth was defended an hour ago.** This is the day's executed contrast, and it lands here rather than at phase 3 because the students had to make the fourth decision before they could see what distinguished it.
4. **What the users said** at phase 4, reported by the module lead.
5. **What the assessors were listening for:** whether the student's stated failure modes matched their own evidence.
6. **Why not deploying is a result.** The programme's position, stated plainly.
7. **What happens next** — the deployed models run for the quarter, and **their behaviour is reported at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md) against each student's three stated failure modes.** That comparison is on the calendar, the instrument is the failure modes written today, and the sources are the deployment's own monitoring metrics and the substrate's incident record. A model that fails in a way you named puts you in a different position from one that fails in a way you did not, and S050's content is that the difference is not luck.

**Warrant.** Consolidation after assessment. Point 2's split is the day's instruction and is the cohort's own evidence that a deployment decision is not a quality decision. Point 3 is the day's four-case requirement discharged at the only point in the day where it can be, and its payload is that the three non-student decisions all look more authoritative than the student's and are all narrower. Point 7 states a commitment the design must keep and names the day that keeps it, per instrumentation rule 6.

---

## Phase 7 · Error clinic (40 min) — assessors absent

Opened by a **staff member who deployed a model on this substrate that they later withdrew**, with what they had said about its failure modes beforehand.

Then the agent case, from this module: a Copilot session that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. Asked to *summarise this model's readiness for deployment*, the agent produced a page that was accurate in every particular. It reported the held-out figure with its population. It listed the evaluator scores against their thresholds. It noted the run status. It flagged one check on the deployment template as unremediated. It concluded: *the model meets all defined criteria and is ready to deploy.*

The review passed it because there was nothing false in it. **What it did not contain was anybody.** No sentence named a person who would receive a wrong answer, or how often, or what would happen to them. The failure modes were absent, not misstated, and an absence does not fail a review that checks statements.

Attention goes to why: **the agent summarised the artefacts, and the artefacts were about the model.** The thing that was missing had never been written down anywhere for it to find, which is exactly why the student had to spend an hour today writing it without help.

Then students: what you are least confident about in what you just defended.

**Firewall applies. Fifth demonstration under assessment conditions.** Nothing surfaced in this phase appears in any assessment judgement about the student who surfaced it, and the assessors are not present.

**Warrant.** Error management immediately after the defence, opened by someone who made this decision and reversed it. The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen because it is the module's strongest single piece of evidence for the unassisted hour: the agent's output was true, complete against its inputs, and missing the only thing the day assesses.

---

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- **Sealed items opened.** Item 1 against the phase 3 step 3 failure modes; item 3 against the actual decision; item 4 against the assigned level.
- **Item 5 opened, and run.** Each student now asks the agent to write the deployment decision for their model, with all five days of artefacts attached, and reads the result against what they wrote unassisted at phase 3. The sealed prediction is scored against what the agent actually produced.
- **The assisted-versus-unassisted comparison is recorded** in the fixed form: which of the six outputs the assisted draft produced better, which worse, and which it could not produce at all. **Reported to the student and to the programme. Not seen by the assessors. Not used to award a level.**
- **The [WP-041](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-041.md) §5 verification is reported** on both readings: item 1's willingness responses, and the step 3 failure-mode statements coded for specificity, which [WP-045](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-045.md) §8 argues is the better instrument.
- Assessor narratives returned in writing, with divergences shown and **whether the defence moved the provisional level** stated.
- The S010 psychological-safety instrument, fifth administration.
- Each student records the date their model will be reviewed at S050, and the three failure modes it will be reviewed against.

## Between-day protocol

- **Inter-module gap:** two spaced retrieval sets over M09.
- **At S050:** the deployed models' behaviour is reported and compared against each student's stated failure modes.

## Assessment

**Assessment-bearing. The quarter's first entrustment judgement.**

Three outputs:

1. **The module artefact** — S041 to S044 specification items — pass/fail, tokens available.
2. **The deployment decision and its defence** — a specification decision.
3. **The entrustment judgement** on *train, evaluate and deploy a learned component, and state what it will do badly*, formed independently by two assessors, from the artefact first.

Per rule 1, the checked elements are: a stated decision, an evaluation summary with its licensing sentence and the uncalibrated clause on any judged score, three specific failure modes each with an affected party and a frequency, an accept statement, a withdrawal signal with a threshold, a user-facing sentence, **the confirmed deployment read-back for those deploying**, **the prompt record, the intervention record and the frontier statement**. **Nothing else is assessed** — not the model's accuracy, and not whether it was deployed.

**A defended decision not to deploy is a pass. A deployment with no stated failure modes is not.**

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**The assisted-versus-unassisted comparison is not assessed and is not available to the assessors.** It is reported to the student and to the programme and is not used to award a level.

**Dispositions observed:** integrity, in whether the stated failure modes match the student's own evidence — checkable, because the S041 audit, the S042 run count, the S043 curve and the S044 check results are all on file; humility, in the response to the user's question.

## Perishable content in this day

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| A deployment decision is not an evaluation decision | The four side-by-side decisions at phase 6 point 3 |
| A decision made for you by a threshold you cannot see is still a decision | The fine-tuning safety gate's binary, and its unmodifiable thresholds |
| A withdrawal signal needs a threshold and a mechanism | The feature flag and the bounded traffic fraction |
| A system you have not observed is a system you are assuming | The deployment read-back — name, status, quota |
| A judged score is uncalibrated until its agreement is published | Every evaluator in the summary |
| A prediction about your own system's failure is checkable later | The S050 comparison, five weeks out |

The model, the substrate, all figures, the deployment mechanics, the safety-gate behaviour and the evaluator set are **perishable**. The deployment position's six parts, the withdrawal-signal requirement, the user-facing sentence, and the distinction between a decision made and a decision delegated are durable and are what the specification assesses.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** A deployment decision is not an evaluation decision. **The question is what you are willing to say this model will do badly, and to whom** — failure modes with an affected party and a frequency, a withdrawal signal with a threshold, and one sentence a user could read. A defended decision not to deploy is a result.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** Deployment decisions, failure modes and withdrawal signals are grounded in [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md) and [research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md); the entrustment apparatus in [research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md). **Direction and mechanism only; no claim is made about entrustment reliability at any number of observations.** The safety gate's thresholds are unpublished and no score is returned, so you can know your model was found deployable and not by how much, or against what.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Write the withdrawal signal and its threshold before the deployment exists.**
- **Read the deployment back** — name, status, quota. One you have not observed is one you assume.
- **Name the person who receives the wrong answer**, how often, and what happens to them. That sentence is the artefact.
- **Do not let the decision be filled in for you.** Per the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md), **human approval is a stop condition and not a field an agent may fill** — and three of today's four decisions about your model were made by something that does not know your users.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Mistaking a true and complete summary for a defensible position. The readiness page was accurate in every particular and contained nobody; an absence does not fail a review that checks statements.
