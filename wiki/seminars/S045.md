# S045 · Shipping the First Increment ⊘

**Module:** [M09](/wiki/modules/M09.md) · **Quarter:** [Q5](/wiki/quarters/Q5.md) · **Complexity class:** 3
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** A Foundry hosted agent deployed behind an evaluation gate, where the gate's failure blocks the release
**Whitepaper:** [WP-045](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-045.md)

**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

This is the stage's first assessment occasion and it marks something narrower than it appears to.

You shipped an increment. **Whether it worked is not the subject.** The subject is whether you can say where its boundary was, who moved it, and what you let past when the date stopped moving.

The internal corpus has a line about this moment that is true and dangerous in the same breath: **working software is hard to argue with, so political resistance dissolves in front of a running increment in a way it does not in front of a document.** That is an accurate observation about rooms. It is also the mechanism by which an increment ends an argument without settling the disagreement, and the disagreement returns later with interest.

Answer these in writing before you begin, and submit them.

1. What shipped, stated as something a named recipient can now do?
2. What did you decide not to ship, and on what day did you decide it?
3. What did the review not read?
4. Who could have refused this increment, and did they have the opportunity?
5. What did you learn that changes the next slice, and what would you have had to observe to learn it?

**Item 2's second clause is the assessment's centre of gravity.** Almost everybody can name an exclusion. Far fewer can say when it was excluded, and an exclusion dated to the moment of shipping was not a decision, it was an outcome.

## What you must produce

An **increment pack**, defended in front of an assessor who has read your [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md) proof pack and your [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md) slice definition and nothing else.

- **The increment**, and the named recipient who received it.
- **The slice definition** as written, with every departure from it marked and dated.
- **The exclusion list**, with each item's current status: still excluded, promoted to a new item, or built after all.
- **The scope ledger** from [S042](/wiki/seminars/S042.md), with its refusals intact.
- **The specification** from [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md), including the untestable residue and who held it.
- **The review record** from [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md), with what was not read still stated as not read.
- **The capacity statement**, unrevised.
- **The what-this-does-not-establish page** — everything a reader of the increment could reasonably infer that you did not demonstrate.

## The material

### The gate that blocks the release, and the only two ways it can be honest

The increment ships behind a gate, and the gate has to be able to stop it. That is the whole point of the anchor, and it is the property most easily lost.

A gate that has never blocked anything is not a gate. It is a report that runs after the decision. **There are exactly two honest states for a release gate at the end of a first increment: it blocked something and you can say what, or it has never blocked anything and you can say why you believe it would.** The second is weaker and it is legitimate, and it must be stated as the weaker thing rather than presented as the first.

The platform makes both states cheap to produce. Evaluation results come back as **pass or fail with a `reason` string**, aggregated into counts and per-item detail, and for evaluators that use a scale before thresholding the output carries a numeric `score` alongside the pass or fail ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). Two consequences follow directly and both belong in your pack. **The threshold is a policy choice separable from the measurement** — moving it changes the pass rate without changing the system, so an inherited threshold is somebody else's release decision executing on your engagement. And **the `reason` field is what makes a judgement auditable rather than merely numeric**, which is what an assessor will ask for when they ask why the gate fired.

The deployment shape matters here for one reason only. A **hosted agent** runs "your code (containerized)" on managed container compute, which is what makes a gate possible in the first place: there is a build, an artefact and a promotion step for a gate to sit in front of ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). A declarative agent with **no agent compute** has fewer places to put one. **If your [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md) slice began as a prompt agent and is now hosted, that transition is a departure and it is dated**, and it is one of the departures assessors most often find unmarked.

The second thing to carry into the pack from the platform is what the vendor does not publish. **No agreement rates with human judgement are published for any evaluator, no calibration data, and no account of where the default thresholds came from.** Many evaluators are **preview**; one consulted page is a superseded **classic** variant; the documentation was read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches. **Assert no evaluator's agreement with human judgement anywhere in your pack.** None is published, and a pack that claims one has committed the exact failure this occasion exists to detect.

### A gate that fires on a difference smaller than its own noise is theatre

This is the hardest requirement in the occasion and the one that separates a pack from a report.

An evaluation is an experiment. **Power analysis before the run is what turns "we tested and found nothing" into a statement about the system rather than about the test** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). Applied to a release gate, the question becomes sharper and more uncomfortable: **what is the smallest regression this gate could have caught?** A gate whose case set could not have detected the difference it is meant to prevent will pass a broken increment and produce a green artefact saying so.

Two further disciplines from the same source belong in the pack. **Paired comparison** on the same cases removes the variation two versions share and costs nothing, because they agree about which cases are hard — so a gate comparing a candidate against the incumbent should compare case by case rather than by their separate totals. And **variance must not be suppressed by changing a setting whose purpose is something else**; lowering a sampling temperature to make a gate look stable shifts what is being measured rather than measuring it better. The platform's **repetitions** mechanism exists for exactly this and states its purpose plainly — running each query multiple times "to detect non-deterministic behavior" — which is the acknowledgement that one sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement.

**Limits:** that measurement source is analytical rather than empirical; its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions; and it addresses comparing scores rather than choosing what to score, so a tight interval around a badly chosen criterion is still meaningless. **No number from it appears here.**

### The model is the small part, and shipping is where that becomes visible

The production literature's organising claim is that **a model is a small fraction of the code required to put an application into production**, and the remainder is the inventory this occasion is really assessing: data validation, feature handling, serving, versioning, monitoring, privacy and orchestration ([research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md)).

Three of its distinctions belong in your what-this-does-not-establish page and almost never appear there.

**Training-and-serving skew.** Different preprocessing at build time and at serving time is described as a common production failure that is hard to detect **because the system continues to return plausible answers**. Your gate ran against your cases in your harness. Whether the same transformations run at serving is a separate claim, and one an increment demonstration does not establish.

**Drift and skew are different comparisons.** Drift compares successive iterations of one source over time; skew compares two different sources, characteristically build against serve. An increment that worked on the data you were given has established nothing about either.

**Monitoring divides into functional and non-functional, and the second matters regardless of the first** — "if the system is down, it really doesn't matter how good your model is". A pack that reports evaluation results and nothing about whether anybody would know the thing had stopped is reporting half of readiness.

The same source is honest about the threshold at which any of this is warranted: **not every project needs a pipeline**, and the test is whether the thing has users. Your increment does, by construction — it has a named recipient who could refuse it — which is why the question arrives now rather than later.

**Limits:** a practitioner survey by authors mostly from one large organisation, with a default toolchain from that organisation and both worked pipelines built in it; alternatives are named briefly. It is strong evidence about how one organisation operates production systems and **no evidence at all as a comparative evaluation of practices**. Its generative-AI chapter concedes its own perishability in its own words.

### What an assessor can and cannot certify from a pack

The assessment literature this programme uses is specific about what a single observer can conclude, and this occasion is designed against those limits rather than around them.

A distinction runs through it between **ad-hoc entrustment**, taken continuously and informally whenever the work in front of somebody matches what they can do, and **summative entrustment**, which "has the nature of certification" and is a decision about future responsibilities. The standard for the second is explicit: it **"should include an agreement between multiple observers that this entrustment is justified, to avoid subjective bias, and multiple observations"** ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). **This occasion has one assessor and one observation, so it certifies nothing about future responsibility and does not claim to.** It marks a specification, in front of somebody, once.

The mechanism it does borrow is the **entrustment-based discussion**: a short conversation after the activity that "should probe the student's deep understanding of what was done" and **must include what-if questions**, because retrospective explanation shows only that the candidate understood the case they met, while counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases they did not meet. **That is why the assessor names something in your increment and asks why it was not an exclusion.** It is not a trap. It is the only question in the format that reaches the cases you did not encounter.

The grading machinery is borrowed from a running course rather than invented. **Specification grading**: components marked pass or fail against a published specification, with no partial credit, and the specification published in advance so that a candidate can decide knowingly what to skip. **Oral verification**: the grade is earned by explaining the work to somebody, which is what makes the artefact's provenance stop being the evidence ([research/08-assessment-epas/cmu-oral-defense-and-token-economy-patterns.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/cmu-oral-defense-and-token-economy-patterns.md)). That course's own policy makes the consequence explicit and this occasion adopts it: tooling use is unrestricted in producing the artefact, because **the explanation must be your own** and cannot be delegated in the room.

**Limits:** the entrustment primer is guidance by the concept's originator rather than an evaluation, and its empirical items are reported from prior studies rather than generated; the course policy is one institution's published design with no outcome evaluation attached.

### Narrative carries the judgement, and the score confirms it

One finding from the assessment research explains why this pack is mostly prose.

When assessors judge a portfolio, **"assessors mainly relied on narrative feedback to inform their judgment… Grades and qualifications were merely used to confirm impressions based on narratives"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). The numbers were not the evidence. They were a check on a conclusion already formed from the prose.

Two further findings shape how you should expect the defence to go. **Anchoring is real**: assessors "were inclined to stick to their initial judgments even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence", and their final judgements were more elaborate than their first ones but not substantially different. **The order of your pack therefore matters more than its completeness**, which is why the departures and the what-this-does-not-establish page are not appendices. And assessors reaching the same outcome frequently differ in the reasoning behind it, driven by what they believe counts as trustworthy evidence and by performance theories that **"tended to deviate from the formal assessment criteria"** — which is precisely the argument for a closed specification that says what is marked and then says that nothing else is.

**Limits:** small qualitative think-aloud studies in a clinical education setting, using mock portfolios; the primary study is not by the author it is usually attributed to; several of the most-cited works in that programme are paywalled and were not consulted, and the substitution is recorded deliberately.

One last discipline comes from the delivery standard, and it is the cheapest thing in this occasion. **Once a specification is settled, a change to it is recorded as a decision rather than absorbed as an edit** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-fde-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-fde-delivery-practice.md), **internal, paraphrased, never linked, reporting no outcome and no evaluation of any kind**). **An edited artefact loses the fact that it changed, and with it the reason.** That is the whole argument for dating departures, and it is why the capacity statement travels unrevised.

## The workflow

**Put the gate in front of the release, make it fire once, and assemble the pack without tidying it.**

**Step 1 — deploy the increment as a hosted agent behind a promotion step.** You need a build, an artefact and a gate between the artefact and the recipient. If your slice began declaratively, record the move to hosted compute as a dated departure before you do anything else.

**Step 2 — wire the specification from [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md) into the promotion, and let it block.** Cheap deterministic checks on every commit; judged evaluation on the promotion. Both may run in one pass and produce their own results.

```yaml
# .pipelines/release-gate.yaml  -- the gate must be able to stop the release
stages:
  - stage: evaluate
    jobs:
      - job: local_checks          # no API calls; runs on every commit
          runs: evals/first-slice.local.py
      - job: judged_gate           # runs on promotion only
          dataset: evals/first-slice.jsonl
          split_strategy: full     # stated, not defaulted -- it decides what the score means
          repetitions: 5           # one sample from a stochastic system is not a measurement
          evaluators: [tool_call_success, tool_input_accuracy, tool_output_utilization]
          thresholds:
            tool_input_accuracy:
              value: 3
              justification: "docs/threshold.md -- set from hand-labelled traces, not inherited"
          fail_release_on: any_evaluator_below_threshold
          record: [label, reason, threshold, passed, score]   # reason makes it auditable
  - stage: promote
    dependsOn: evaluate
    condition: succeeded()         # THE GATE. If this never blocks, say so in the pack.
```

```yaml
# docs/gate-honesty.yaml  -- one of these two blocks is true. Fill in the true one.
blocked:
  date: 2026-07-24
  what: "tool_input_accuracy fell below threshold on S4-tool-down after the retry change."
  reason_string: "Called case_file_read with a stale case_id after the failure path."
  what_we_did: "Held the release. Fixed. Re-ran. Departure DEP-004 recorded and dated."
never_blocked:
  why_we_believe_it_would: ""
  smallest_regression_it_could_catch: ""    # if blank, the gate is decoration
  what_we_did_not_test: ""
```

**Step 3 — answer the power question before you defend anything.** State the smallest regression the gate could have caught. **If you cannot, write that in the pack rather than omitting the field** — an admitted limit is a finding and an unadmitted one is a defect.

**Step 4 — date every exclusion.** Go back through the ledger, the review threads and your own notes and find the day each exclusion became an exclusion. **Anything you cannot date was not decided.** This takes longer than anybody expects, because most of the dates are not written down anywhere, and it is the exercise that distinguishes a delivery from a result.

**Step 5 — reconcile the capacity statement against reality and do not revise it.** You predicted the review effort your rate of production would demand. Compare it with what happened. If demand never exceeded supply, establish whether the supply was adequate or the review was quietly narrowed to fit. **The version in the pack is the version written at [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md).**

**Step 6 — write the what-this-does-not-establish page last and specifically.** Not *this is an early increment*. Name the inferences: that it works at the volume the customer described; that it behaves on data nobody saw; that the same transformations run at serving as ran in your harness; that the recipient's colleagues would use it; that the exclusions are safe to exclude permanently. **This page is the pack's most valuable one and the first deleted before circulation**, which is why it is assessed here as it was at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md).

**Step 7 — have the agent argue the increment should not have shipped, then argue the gate proved nothing.** First: instruct it to argue that the boundary was drawn to make shipping possible rather than to make the increment valuable. Second: give it `gate-honesty.yaml` and the dataset and instruct it to argue that the gate could not have caught the regression you most fear, naming the case that is missing. Keep what is better than your own reasoning; record what you rejected. **An agent that congratulated you was not used as an instrument.**

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** That a gate is a claim about what you could have detected, not about what you did detect — and that the claim is checkable in an afternoon by anybody willing to ask which regression the case set would have missed. **You will also find out whether your gate was ever load-bearing**, because a gate nobody has seen block anything has the same evidential status as a check that has never been seen red.

## What good looks like

These are the assessed elements. Each is checked as present and adequate, or not.

- **Every departure from the slice definition is marked and dated**, and a departure discovered at shipping is recorded as a departure rather than as a revision.
- **The increment's recipient is named and was in a position to refuse it.** An increment received only by the delivery team is submitted with that stated as its finding.
- **Tests arrived with the code**, in the same change, and the pack shows this rather than asserting it.
- **The review record still says what was not read.** A pack in which everything turned out to have been read is checked against the time budget it was conducted under.
- **Something was refused**, in the ledger or in the review, and the pack names it.
- **The capacity statement is unrevised** and is reconciled against what the increment actually produced.
- **The untestable residue has a holder**, and the pack says whether that person actually looked.
- **Nothing in the pack asserts a magnitude that was not measured by a named instrument.**
- **The what-this-does-not-establish page exists and is specific**, naming inferences rather than gesturing at limits.
- **The gate is in one of its two honest states**: it blocked something and the pack says what, or it never blocked anything and the pack says why the candidate believes it would.
- **The pack states the smallest regression the gate could have caught**, or states that it cannot and treats that as a finding.
- **The split strategy and every threshold are named and justified**, and no evaluator's agreement with human judgement is asserted anywhere.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Whether the increment works is not marked. Whether the recipient was pleased is not marked. The quality, elegance or size of the code is not marked. The number of items shipped, refused or recorded is not marked. Whether the slice was the right slice is not marked. Whether the exclusions should have been excluded is not marked. The pack's presentation is not marked.

## How it goes wrong

- **The tidy pack** — the departures have become revisions, the refusals have become deferrals, and the unread portion of the review has quietly closed. *Warning sign:* the pack reads better than the fortnight went.
- **The undated exclusion** — everything excluded was excluded *at the start*, on inspection of nothing. *Warning sign:* item 2's second clause is blank.
- **The internal recipient** — the increment was received by the team that built it. *Warning sign:* the demonstration needed somebody to narrate it.
- **The retrospective specification** — the tests are present and were written after the code. *Warning sign:* no check in the pack has ever been seen red.
- **The softened capacity statement** — the version in the pack is more comfortable than the version written at [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md). *Warning sign:* it no longer names anybody.
- **The increment that ended the argument** — everyone agreed once it ran, and nobody wrote down what they had disagreed about. *Warning sign:* your pack contains no unresolved objection.
- **The decorative gate** — the evaluation runs on promotion, has never blocked anything, and is presented as though it had. *Warning sign:* you cannot name the regression it would catch.
- **The inherited threshold** — the pass line arrived with the tooling and is doing your release decisions for you. *Warning sign:* your threshold is a round number nobody chose.
- **The borrowed agreement** — the pack states or implies that an evaluator agrees with human judgement. *Warning sign:* you cannot cite where that agreement was published, because it was not.

## Exercises

**These are the assessment.** Unlike every other session in this module, the work below is marked — against the specification above and against nothing else. The exercises exist so that you arrive having already done the hard parts.

**1 · Date every exclusion, then count the undated ones.** Work through the exclusion list from [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md) and the ledger from [S042](/wiki/seminars/S042.md) and find the day each became an exclusion.

> **Scoring criterion.** Every entry carries a date or is explicitly marked undated. **An undated exclusion is submitted as undated**, not quietly given the date of the slice definition. The distinction being marked is not tidiness: **a dated exclusion is a decision and an undated one is an outcome**, and the pack that dates everything to day one has told the assessor that nothing was decided after day one, which nobody believes. Candidates routinely find that their most defensible exclusion is their least datable, and saying so is worth more than back-filling.

**2 · Make the gate fire, or explain why it never has.** Introduce a regression you believe your gate should catch, and record what happened.

> **What you should find.** Either the gate catches it, in which case you have the first honest state and a `reason` string to quote; or it does not, in which case **you have learned something about your case set that no amount of green runs would have told you.** The second outcome is not a failure of the exercise. **A gate that has never blocked anything is a report that runs after the decision**, and a pack saying so plainly scores better than one presenting an untested gate as a control. Note the published mechanism while you are there: results carry a `reason` field, and **the reason is what makes the judgement auditable rather than merely numeric.**

**3 · Go back to your own item 2, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 2 — what you decided not to ship, and on what day you decided it. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference.

> **Criterion.** Almost everybody can name an exclusion. **Far fewer can say when it was excluded, and an exclusion dated to the moment of shipping was not a decision, it was an outcome.** The criterion is that the date precedes the pressure that would have made the exclusion inevitable: an item excluded on the Monday you scoped the slice is a decision; the same item excluded on the Thursday before the demonstration is a description of what you ran out of time for, and both are legitimate, and only one of them is a boundary. **If your item 2 named the exclusion and not the day, you have just found the thing this occasion marks.**
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this occasion's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is. **School, university and laboratory evidence; no adult-professional study in its reference list.**

**4 · State the smallest regression your gate could have caught.** One sentence, before the defence.

> **Scoring criterion.** The answer passes if it distinguishes *we found no regression* from *we could not have found one*. **A gate whose case set could not detect the difference it exists to prevent will pass a broken increment and produce a green artefact saying so**, and nothing in the pipeline output reveals which happened — which is why the analysis belongs before the run rather than after it. If you cannot answer, write that in the pack. **An admitted limit is a finding; an unadmitted one is a defect.** The measurement source behind this requirement is analytical rather than empirical and its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions; **no number from it may appear in your pack.**

**5 · Write four inferences a reader could reasonably draw and you did not demonstrate.** Specific ones, naming the inference.

> **Model answers.** That it works at the volume the customer described; that it behaves on data nobody in the room has seen; **that the same transformations run at serving as ran in your evaluation harness**; that anybody would know if it stopped. The third and fourth are the ones packs miss, and both come from the production literature rather than from delivery practice: preprocessing that differs between build and serve is a **common failure that is hard to detect because the system keeps returning plausible answers**, and functional monitoring is worth nothing if nobody is watching whether the thing is up. **If you wrote "this is an early increment", you gestured at a limit rather than naming an inference**, and that is the sentence this page exists to replace.

**6 · Rehearse the counterfactual question.** Have a colleague name something inside your increment and ask why it was not an exclusion. Answer it once badly and once properly.

> **What to notice.** *Because it was needed* is the bad answer, and it is the honest instinct. **A candidate who concedes the thing could have been excluded and says what excluding it would have cost has answered properly**, and that is a pass. The question is not adversarial theatre: it is the only part of the format that reaches the cases you did not meet, which is why the assessment literature requires what-if questions in an entrustment discussion rather than retrospective explanation alone. **Note also what this occasion does not do:** it has one assessor and one observation, and the standard for a certification-like decision is agreement between multiple observers across multiple observations. **This occasion marks a specification. It does not certify you.**

## What this does not establish

This occasion shows that you can state the boundary of what you shipped and account for how it moved. **It does not show that the boundary was well drawn**, and it cannot.

**No source behind this stage supplies a basis for marking that.** The playbook names the minimal valuable slice as a commitment and offers no comparative evidence for it or for any other practice on the site. The internal corpus reports no failed engagement, no cost side, no defect data and no post-deployment record, so it cannot supply a case in which a badly drawn slice caused harm — because it supplies no harm.

**The design's addition is that a dated exclusion is a decision and an undated one is an outcome.** That is a claim about evidence rather than about outcomes, and it has no external warrant. So does the requirement that the capacity statement travel unrevised into the pack, which exists because a statement that can be edited at assembly will be. **So does the two-honest-states rule for the gate**, and the requirement that the pack state the smallest regression the gate could have caught.

**This occasion certifies nothing about your future responsibilities.** The assessment literature it borrows from is explicit that a certification-like decision requires agreement between multiple observers and multiple observations, precisely to avoid subjective bias. **There is one assessor here and one observation.** The same literature reports that assessors reaching identical outcomes frequently differ entirely in the reasoning behind them, and that they anchor on an initial judgement and do not substantially revise it when later evidence disagrees. **Those findings come from small qualitative studies using mock portfolios in a clinical education setting**, and several of the field's most-cited works were paywalled and not consulted for this repository.

**The platform publishes no validation of its own evaluators.** No agreement rates with human judgement, no calibration data, no threshold rationale. Many evaluators are preview; one consulted page is a superseded classic variant; the documentation was read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches. **A pack that claims an evaluator agrees with human judgement has asserted something nobody has published.**

**The production literature is one organisation's practice written by its own engineers.** Its default toolchain is that organisation's, both its worked pipelines are built in it, and alternatives are named briefly. It is strong evidence about how a large organisation operates production systems and **no evidence at all as a comparative evaluation of practices**.

**The measurement discipline is analytical rather than empirical**, its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions, and it addresses comparing scores rather than choosing what to score — so a tight interval around a badly chosen gate criterion is still meaningless.
