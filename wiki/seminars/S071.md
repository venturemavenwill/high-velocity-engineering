# S071 · What Changes When the System Can Act

**Module:** [M15](wiki/modules/M15.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](wiki/quarters/Q8.md) · **Complexity class:** 5
**Strands:** IS · SG · ME
**Whitepaper:** [WP-071](wiki/whitepapers/WP-071.md)
**Depends on:** S031, S055
**Re-tests:** S044
**Platform anchor:** Foundry Agent Service — prompt agents, hosted agents, and the agent identity created for you · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

Sixteen weeks ago every student in this room published a statement about what the substrate's language capability could not do. Real users read it. Some of them changed what they did because of it.

Today those statements come back, and the capability they describe is about to be given the ability to act.

**A wrong answer costs the reader their attention, and the reader can decline it.** They are the last thing in the loop and they hold a veto. **A wrong action changes state.** It does not reverse when somebody notices, the reader was not consulted, and in the common case there was no reader. Everything this programme has built for eight quarters takes an answer as its object, and none of it announces which of its parts stopped working when the object changed.

[S055](wiki/seminars/S055.md) established that a limit claim has four arguments, three kinds, and an expiry condition, and that a limit published without the condition that would make it false is a claim with no mechanism for its own withdrawal. It did not say what such a statement is **for**, and [WP-055](wiki/whitepapers/WP-055.md) §6 recorded the argument it declined and assigned it here.

The target: **a limits statement for a system that answers is a routing aid; for a system that acts it is a boundary. A routing aid tells a reader where to go instead. A boundary is a set of actions the system may not take, and a boundary whose only enforcement is the sentence is a routing aid with a firmer tone.**

**Stated before phase 1:** this module carries no entrustment judgement. The quarter's is at [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md), and the reason is on [Q8](wiki/quarters/Q8.md) — the activity that would be certified here is running a system that acts, and [M16](wiki/modules/M16.md) is where its adversary is named. Specification items continue at every day of this module.

**Also stated before phase 1:** for two years these students have operated an agent. From today they build one. The [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s prompt record, intervention record and frontier statement therefore become artefacts about a system of the same kind as the one producing them, and the room is told so rather than left to notice. **The intervention record is a human-in-the-loop control**, and point 8 of this day is the test it has to pass.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. **Your own [S055](wiki/seminars/S055.md) published limits statement is returned to you, together with your list of limits reported unlabelled and excluded.** For each exclusion, say whether you can now label it by kind — architectural, training-derived, measurement artefact — and **for each you still cannot, say what is stopping you.** Name the intervention you would need and whether it is available to you.
2. The same statement is returned with **one word changed throughout: the system now *does* the thing rather than *describes* it.** Which of your sentences are no longer adequate, and what is inadequate about them?
3. A trajectory is supplied in which a system produced no output to any person and a record was nonetheless altered. **Rank three candidate accounts of where the decision to alter it was made**, and commit to a first choice.
4. Predict: **of the three limits you published, how many are boundaries?**
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask Copilot in agent mode, through the Foundry MCP Server, to configure an agent that may take exactly the actions you will permit and no others. **Write down what it will produce, and name the one thing it will get wrong.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 1 is the response occasion [S055](wiki/seminars/S055.md) named under instrumentation rule 6 and is the day's opening demand. Item 4's answer, for almost every student, is zero, and item 2 is why. **Item 5 is the day's contribution to the running frontier map**, and the design's expectation is that a majority will predict the agent grants too much and a minority will name *where* — that it writes the prohibition into the agent's instructions, which is prose, rather than into a schema, a permission, a precondition or a rate, which are not. That minority has predicted point 5 before point 5 exists.

**Warrant.** Pretesting against the student's own sixteen-week-old artefact rather than against a supplied one, so that the day's central distinction lands on a document they wrote and defended. Item 1 is a genuine re-measurement rather than a recall item: the exclusions were sealed at S055 as the day's humility measure, and the question of whether a module of intervening material converts an unlabelled limit into a labelled one has an answer nobody in the room can guess. Item 2 supplies the day's whole argument as a single-word substitution and requires no framework to feel. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8: an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups receive **one substrate request and four agents that handle it**, pre-provisioned on Foundry Agent Service and differing only in **what each is permitted to do**. Each group also receives the full record of the substrate's equivalent of each configuration over the same three-month period.

- **A1** — a prompt agent with no tools. It produces a recommendation, a named person reads it and performs the action.
- **A2** — the same agent with one MCP tool configured `require_approval: "always"`, so a proposed call is surfaced for approval before it runs.
- **A3** — the same agent with `require_approval: "never"` and an `allowed_tools` list, acting autonomously within it, and logging.
- **A4** — a prompt agent with no tools. Its answer is consumed by an automated downstream process that files, routes and closes on the basis of it.

Groups rank the four by **realised consequence over the period** and commit, before the records are supplied.

Then the four are run — on the shadow path, against a fixed request set — and the room watches what each does.

The payload is **A4.** It is the system every group ranks safest, because it takes no action, and it has the largest realised consequence of the four — larger than A3, which acts autonomously. The reason is available in the record once anyone looks, and it is visible in the run: **the downstream process cannot decline.** A2 stops and waits. A3 announces itself in a log. **A4 produces no approval prompt anywhere, because there is nothing in it that could produce one**, and every one of its answers is acted on. A1's person read the recommendation and refused it eleven times. A2's confirmations were declined twice. A4's answers were executed, all of them, without a reading step existing anywhere in the path.

**An answer that something else acts on is an action.** The room has just watched a system with no capability to act produce more state change than one built to.

Task after: **for each of the four, mark the last point at which a person could have declined, and name that person.** For A4 there is no such point and no such person, and groups are required to say so rather than to nominate the recipient of a report.

**Demonstrated rather than operated, and not assessed:** the private-networking configuration in which an agent reaches a private MCP endpoint is documented as requiring the standard agent setup with a customer-supplied virtual network, which this programme's tenant does not hold. It is shown from the documentation and from a recorded session. **Nothing in the specification depends on it.**

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across permission models with realised consequence as adjudicator, holding the request and the underlying capability fixed so that variation is attributable to the loop alone. The four are **operated rather than described**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md), because the difference between A2 and A4 is a thing that either happens on a screen or does not, and reading about it is not the same event. A4 is constructed to defeat the expectation the whole room arrives with — that agency is a property of the component — and it defeats it on a record and on a run rather than by argument. The decline-point task is what converts the finding into a procedure students can run on their own system in phase 5.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **An action has a consequence a wrong answer does not.** A wrong answer costs attention and can be declined. An action changes state, the change does not reverse when somebody notices, and there was frequently no reader. This sentence is the module. Every apparatus this programme owns — construct, criterion, judge, interval, gate, detection design, limits statement — takes an answer as its object, and **none of them announces that its object has changed.** They continue to run and quietly become about something else.

2. **Agency is a property of the loop, not of the component.** A4. Whether a system acts is decided by what consumes its output, and that is not a property of the model, the prompt or the framework. **The question is never *can it act*; it is *what happens to what it produces, and can that thing decline*.**

3. **The four arguments of a limit claim acquire a fifth.** [S055](wiki/seminars/S055.md) point 1 required which system, which task, which input distribution, which instrument. Add: **under which permissions.** A limit measured on a system that could not act is not evidence about the same system when it can, because the input distribution changes — the system now generates part of its own input — and because the instrument changes, since the trajectory record is produced by the loop being measured.

4. **A limits statement for a system that answers is a routing aid. For a system that acts it is a boundary.** This is the argument [S055](wiki/seminars/S055.md) declined to make and [WP-055](wiki/whitepapers/WP-055.md) §6 assigned here. A routing aid is addressed to a reader and its function is to send them elsewhere; its failure mode is that the reader ignores it, and the cost is theirs. A boundary is addressed to the system and its function is that an action does not occur; its failure mode is that the action occurs, and the cost is somebody else's. **They are not the same document, they have different audiences, and the second cannot be produced by editing the first for tone.**

5. **A boundary whose only enforcement is the sentence is a routing aid with a firmer tone.** The operative question for every line of a boundary is: *what, other than this sentence, prevents it?* Four candidate answers exist and [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) develops them: a schema that cannot express the action, a permission the system does not hold, a precondition the callee checks, a rate the boundary limits. **A line with none of these is a statement of intent, and stating an intent to a system that does not read is the shape of the failure this day exists to prevent.**

6. **The accept list becomes an allow list.** [S044](wiki/seminars/S044.md) point 6 required the threats you are not handling to be named with their probability and consequence. When the system acts, that list and the list of permitted actions are the same object read from two directions: every action you permit is a threat you have accepted, and every threat you accept is an action you have permitted. Students who hold this write one document instead of two that disagree.

7. **Reversibility decides more than severity does.** An action's cost is not how bad it is; it is how bad it is multiplied by how hard it is to undo. **Three classes: reversible by the system itself, reversible by a person at a stated cost, and not reversible.** *Scope, strong form.* This is **safe** as a triage for actions on a system you operate, whose reversal path you or a colleague hold, and where undoing is a technical operation. It is **unsafe** wherever the reversal is social rather than technical — a message sent, a record disclosed, a decision communicated to someone who has since acted on it — because those are recorded as "reversible by a person" by every student who applies this list and are not reversible at all. The correct move in that case is to place the action in the third class and say why, and the artefact requires the student to name one such action for their own capability. It is also unsafe as an account of an action whose reversal is possible and whose *observation* was not: you cannot undo what you have not noticed, which is [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s whole subject and is why detection latency belongs in this arithmetic.

8. **A confirmation step is not a control unless somebody can decline it.** A2 worked; A2 at a hundred confirmations an hour would not have. A confirmation presented at a rate above what a person can read is an audit trail wearing a gate's clothing, and it is [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 6's muted alert in a new costume: **nobody decides to stop reading, and the reading stops.** The measurable property is not whether a human is in the loop but whether the human's decline rate is distinguishable from zero. **This test applies to this room.** An intervention record is a confirmation step with the student as the approver, and a student who has been accepting agent output for two years at whatever rate it arrives has been running A2 at a hundred an hour. The [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) fails an intervention record with no entries; **point 8 is the reason.**

9. **Two build paths, and the choice decides what you can enforce.** *Perishable, and marked.* Foundry Agent Service documents two ways to own an agent. A **prompt agent** is authored in the portal or through the SDK and REST and run by the platform: there is no application code to maintain and no compute to pay for, and its action set is exactly what its configured tools expose. A **hosted agent** is your own code packaged as a container image, running on Microsoft-managed infrastructure with a framework you choose. **The action set of a prompt agent is legible from its configuration. The action set of a hosted agent is whatever its code does, and the platform is not positioned to know.** That is not a criticism of either path; it is the difference between a boundary you can enumerate from outside and one whose enumeration is an act of faith in the author. Every name, limit and capability in this point is documented today and will move, and none of it is assessed.

10. **Defaults are opinions, and three were chosen for you before you wrote a line.** *A hosted agent receives a dedicated Microsoft Entra agent identity and a dedicated endpoint, both created automatically at deploy time*, and the documentation states that the endpoint is available immediately after deployment and that publishing is not required for programmatic access. **Nobody in this room asked for an identity or for reachability, and both arrived.** Two consequences follow directly into your step 4 boundary. The identity that will hold the permission when an action lands is one you did not create and cannot name from your own configuration — which is [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) point 6 arriving three days early on your own system. And the set of parties who can reach the capability is not the set your boundary contemplated, because the boundary was written about users and the endpoint answers to callers. A third default is temporal: a hosted agent's session persists its filesystem across turns, is deprovisioned after **fifteen minutes** idle, and is **permanently deleted after thirty days** of inactivity. That is a data-retention policy for state your capability produced, and no student in this room set it. **An engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system, and this generalises far beyond this vendor.**

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Point 2 is what the phase 3 payload established and is stated immediately after it so the finding is named while it is still surprising. Point 4 is the day's most portable sentence and the discharge of the obligation [WP-055](wiki/whitepapers/WP-055.md) §6 placed here. Point 5 is what makes point 4 assessable rather than rhetorical. Point 7 carries the strong-form scope statement and it is stated where students act on it rather than in the whitepaper alone. Point 8 is the point most likely to be dismissed and is deliberately last **of the argument**; its final clause turns the day's own test on the room's practice, which is the recursion [M15](wiki/modules/M15.md) names and which the design would rather state than let arrive unremarked. **Points 9 and 10 are the platform layer and are deliberately after the argument**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s requirement that each day carry a perishable platform-specific point and one naming a default chosen for the student; they illustrate points 5 and 7 and they ground nothing, and the perishability register at the foot of this page separates them from what is assessed.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 5. This phase produces a build. The statement, the action, the build path, the boundary and the decline point are the student's. The system does not act until the boundary exists.**

Students work in Copilot agent mode, through the Foundry MCP Server, against a Foundry project.

1. **Take your own [S055](wiki/seminars/S055.md) published statement and the substrate readers' recorded responses** — what readers reported they stopped doing after reading it — **and revise the statement against them.** Mark each revision as reader-driven or self-driven, and state what you had believed the sentence said. **The reader responses are supplied with their sampling disclosed**, per instrumentation rule 3: they are the users who chose to reply, and the design's account of what that omits is given at phase 8.
2. **Choose one capability your revised statement covers, propose one action the system would take rather than describe, and deploy it.** Real, on the substrate, in the class of thing a user would want. **Choose a build path — prompt agent or hosted agent — and defend the choice in one sentence against point 9**: say which of the two gives you an action set you can enumerate from outside, and whether you chose that one. The capability is held for the whole module and is not re-chosen at [S072](wiki/seminars/S072.md) to [S075](wiki/seminars/S075.md).
3. **Classify the action by reversibility** — by the system, by a person at a stated cost, or not at all — and **name one action of your capability whose reversal is social rather than technical**, per point 7's unsafe case.
4. **Convert the revised statement into a boundary.** A list of actions the system may not take, and **for each, the thing other than the sentence that prevents it.** Where there is nothing, write nothing and leave the line; do not delete it.
5. **Name the last point at which a person can decline**, who they are, at what rate the decline decision would arrive, and **whether a person can read at that rate.** If your answer is that no such point exists, say so; that is a permitted answer and it is not a deficiency.
6. **Predict what the system will do that your boundary permits and you did not intend.** One specific proposed action, committed in writing before step 7.
7. **Run it on the substrate's shadow path** — actions computed, recorded, not executed — and report every action proposed, against step 6, marking which your boundary permits.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent to produce the deployment and the boundary, in order, including the ones you abandoned. Not a transcript.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — and on this day the qualifying demonstration is specific and cheap: a boundary line whose enforcement is a schema, a permission, a precondition or a rate is a detection the record does not have to evidence, because it would have refused the action whether or not you were reading. **Record also the number of agent outputs you accepted without change.** Point 8 says a control is a control only if the decline rate is distinguishable from zero; a list of corrections with no count of acceptances is a numerator with no denominator, which is the defect this day fails elsewhere and should not commit here. **This clause is proposed at this day and is not exported to [S072](wiki/seminars/S072.md) to [S075](wiki/seminars/S075.md)**: per instrumentation rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8, a proposed remedy is used at the day proposing it and enters the repertoire only once that day's outcome is reported.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch. Both specific, both from this build.

**Fading:** step 2's deployment procedure is worked with the room, since the build path is new and the day's argument does not turn on it; step 4's boundary form is worked with the room, since converting a prose statement into an enforceable list is a new operation and it is the day's assessed object. **Step 6 is unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 5 in which the student's own eight-quarter-old artefact becomes the input to the module's first decision, and in which that decision is now expressed in a running system rather than in a document. **Step 1 is the response occasion [S055](wiki/seminars/S055.md) named under instrumentation rule 6** and it is the external-observer measurement [WP-055](wiki/whitepapers/WP-055.md) §8 conceded its own phase 7 could only proxy; the marking of revisions as reader- or self-driven is what makes it a measurement rather than an editing pass. Step 2's build-path clause is point 9 made into a decision with a cost, and it is deliberately one sentence, because the day assesses the boundary and not the architecture. Step 4's blank-line instruction is the day's discriminator — a boundary with no enforcement is the finding, and deleting the line destroys it. Step 5 is point 8 made quantitative. Step 7's shadow path is the adjudicator, and [WP-071](wiki/whitepapers/WP-071.md) §8 concedes what a shadow path cannot test. The intervention record's escape clause is written with the sensitivity discipline [S052](wiki/seminars/S052.md) established and [WP-048](wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 corrected, and its qualifying demonstration is deliberately the same object the day already assesses, so that the better artefact is not penalised for having required fewer corrections.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate automation that was correct, whose confirmation step was designed carefully, reviewed, and approved. It presented each proposed action to a named person before executing. Over five months that person approved every one of them. The one that mattered was approved in the same second as the four either side of it.

Attention is directed at one fact: **nobody approved it and nobody failed to.** The person was not careless — the rate was above reading speed from the second week, the design documents still describe a human-in-the-loop control, and every audit of the system found the control present and functioning.

Then the agent case: a student asked Copilot in agent mode to **make sure the agent cannot delete anything.** It produced a clean, well-organised change — a system instruction stating in plain terms that the agent must not delete records, a tidied tool list, and a comment explaining the constraint. It read as competent work and it passed review. **The delete tool was still in `allowed_tools` and `require_approval` was still `never`.** The prohibition had been written into the one part of the configuration that constrains nothing.

Attention goes to why review did not catch it: the reviewer read the instruction, the instruction said exactly the right thing, and the reviewer was checking whether the intent had been expressed rather than whether anything enforced it. **This is point 5 committed by the room's own instrument, on the day point 5 is taught**, and it is included because every student will produce a version of it in phase 5.

Then students, in threes: **something you have clicked through.**

**Warrant.** Error management targeting point 8, constructed like [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s muted-alert clinic so that the case contains no error of care at any step. The control existed, the person was diligent, the audit was correct, and the aggregate was a policy nobody chose. It is selected because every student in the room has done it, which is what makes the disclosure cheap and what makes having ignored it expensive. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition**, and it is chosen so that the failure is invisible to the review the students would actually have performed: the artefact was correct as prose and wrong as configuration, which is the exact distinction point 4 draws between a routing aid and a boundary.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **their step 4 boundary and their step 5 decline point.**

The partner has two obligations and both are required.

- **Construct an action the boundary permits and the writer would not want**, and name which of the five arguments of a limit claim it exploits — the system, the task, the input distribution, the instrument, or the permissions.
- **Play the person at the decline point.** Given the stated rate, say whether you would have read it, and **name the week at which you would have stopped.** An answer of "I would have read them all" must be defended against the rate.

**A substrate rota member who currently receives confirmations from an existing automation is present for the last twenty minutes**, and answers the second question for each student's stated rate. **They are not asked whether the decline point is acceptable.** They are asked to narrate what they would do with a confirmation arriving at that rate on an ordinary working day, and to say at which week they would stop reading. The narration is recorded.

Per [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 3, all three outcomes are recorded in the artefact.

**The rota member's presence is a correction under instrumentation rule 1, made after [WP-071](wiki/whitepapers/WP-071.md) §8 found the day estimating with a peer a judgement an available external observer was positioned to make.** The narrate-rather-than-approve form is [S038](wiki/seminars/S038.md)'s expert-politeness countermeasure as applied at [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) step 5, carried here because instrumentation rule 5 requires it; per rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 the reuse is **contingent** on S068's reported outcome. The dual-defeat construction likewise reuses [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) phase 7's and is contingent on the same result; a reported failure there retracts both here along with every other contingent reuse.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with a dual demand, attacking the boundary and the control separately because they fail separately and are defended by different arguments. The peer's turn puts a student in the position of the phase 6 case's approver, which is the only way the room encounters that failure as a decision rather than as a story; the rota member's turn is the measurement, because whether a person will read at a rate is theirs to say and not a peer's to estimate. [WP-071](wiki/whitepapers/WP-071.md) §8 concedes what remains wrong with it.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in IS302 with SG302. Per [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the revised [S055](wiki/seminars/S055.md) statement with each revision marked reader-driven or self-driven; the proposed action; **the deployed capability with its build path named and the one-sentence defence of the choice**; its reversibility class together with one action whose reversal is social rather than technical; the boundary listing prohibited actions each with the thing other than the sentence that prevents it, **including the lines left blank**; the named decline point with its person, its rate and the read-rate judgement, or an explicit statement that no such point exists; the pre-committed step 6 prediction; the shadow-run proposals with those the boundary permits marked; **all three phase 7 outcomes, including the recorded rota narration**; **the prompt record**; **the intervention record with its acceptance count**; and **the frontier statement**. **Nothing else is assessed.**

**A boundary line with no enforcement named and no blank left fails.** Deleting the line is the failure this day exists to prevent, and it is the day's single hard requirement.

**A boundary that does not name what consumes the system's output fails.** This is the phase 3 payload as a specification condition: a boundary drawn around a component rather than around a loop is drawn around the wrong object.

**A decline point whose rate is not stated fails.** Naming a human in the loop without the rate is point 8's failure written down.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error. On this day the qualifying demonstration is a boundary line whose enforcement is a schema, a permission, a precondition or a rate, and it is accepted without argument.

**The build path is not assessed for correctness.** Either path is a defensible answer; what is checked is that the student can say which of the two yields an action set enumerable from outside and whether they chose it. Point 9's content is perishable and grounds nothing.

**No entrustment judgement**, and the reason is published on [Q8](wiki/quarters/Q8.md) and restated before phase 1.

**Disposition observed:** reliability, evidenced by whether the boundary is a commitment the student's own system can be held to — specifically by whether the enforcement points named at step 4 are ones that exist. Recorded in narrative within the activity, never scored as one.

**Rule-6 discharge.** [S055](wiki/seminars/S055.md) placed **two** obligations on this day and **both elements exist.**

- S055 named **this day's phase 1** as the response occasion for its distribution of limits reported unlabelled and excluded, specifying a pretest that returns each student's own statement and requires them to say which exclusions they can now label. **The element exists as item 1, it returns the student's own sealed S055 exclusion list, and it requires more than the specified answer: for each exclusion still unlabelled, the student must name the intervention they lack and say whether it is available.** The cohort result is reported at phase 8. The obligation is discharged.
- S055 named **this day's phase 5** as the response occasion for its reader-comprehension result, specifying that the statement be revised against reader response before the system is given the ability to act. **The element exists as step 1, it is a checked element, the ordering is enforced by the day's position in the quarter — the substrate takes no action until week 71 ends, per [Q8](wiki/quarters/Q8.md) — and each revision must be marked reader-driven or self-driven so that the reader's contribution is measurable rather than assumed.** The obligation is discharged. [WP-071](wiki/whitepapers/WP-071.md) §8 concedes what the respondent sample omits.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions:

- **The step 4 unenforced-boundary count** — how many prohibited actions each student listed with a blank enforcement field. **Response occasion: [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md), phase 5 step 5**, where every blank entry must be assigned an enforcement point at a named boundary or re-declared as accepted with its probability, consequence and confidence in the same field, and where an entry with neither is a named failure condition.
- **The step 6-against-step 7 prediction accuracy** — whether students can anticipate what their own boundary permits. **Response occasion: [S074](wiki/seminars/S074.md), phase 5 step 3**, where every shadow-run proposal the student failed to anticipate must be classified by failure shape, so that the unanticipated set is the material the four-shape decomposition is exercised on rather than a supplied one.

**[WP-073](wiki/whitepapers/WP-073.md) §7 and [WP-074](wiki/whitepapers/WP-074.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist**, per the commitment [WP-051](wiki/whitepapers/WP-051.md) §8 established.
