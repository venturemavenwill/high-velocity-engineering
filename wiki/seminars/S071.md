## The situation

The system is live. The end date is fixed and near. Somebody has used the phrase *hand it over*, and everybody in the room nodded, and nobody has said what the phrase refers to.

Answer these in writing before the session opens.

1. Name the thing you are handing over. Not the system — the list of objects. How long is your list?
2. Which item on your list is currently held in a place that is not the repository?
3. Which item, if you are honest, has no plausible receiver at this customer?
4. What happens to that item when you leave?
5. If somebody accepted your handover, what would they have done? Describe the act.

**Item 5 is where the exercise usually collapses.** Most learners can name a handover and cannot name an acceptance, which means what they have designed is a departure with paperwork.

## What you must produce

A **transfer manifest**: one line per object. Each line names the object, where it currently lives, who currently holds it, the named receiving owner, the condition under which that owner has accepted it, and the consequence if they do not.

The manifest must contain **at least one line recorded as unaccepted**, with its consequence stated. If your first draft has none, you have written a wish list rather than a manifest.

## What good looks like

- The manifest contains objects the source corpus never mentions transferring: the infrastructure definitions, the pipeline, the runbooks, the evaluation harness, the cost model and the monitoring. **Those six are the ones that go missing**, because none of them is the product and all of them are how the product keeps working.
- Each receiving owner is a **role and a person**, and the person is one who could act. Enthusiasm is not authority.
- Each acceptance condition is something the recipient could evaluate with you out of the room.
- The unaccepted line is a real one — an item that genuinely has no receiver in this customer — and the consequence is stated in terms of what will stop working, not in terms of risk.
- Items held outside the repository are named as such. **A cost model on a laptop is an object with a location, and the location is the problem.**

## How it goes wrong

Five failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **Access mistaken for transfer** — permissions are granted, the manifest is marked complete, and nothing has moved. *Warning sign:* your acceptance condition is a thing you do rather than a thing they do.
- **The everything manifest** — every artefact of the engagement is listed at equal weight, so no receiver can tell what matters. *Warning sign:* your manifest has no order, or its order is the order you built things in.
- **The orphan you did not look for** — the evaluation harness and the cost model have no owner because they belong to nobody's job description on either side. *Warning sign:* every line has an owner and you found that easy.
- **The invisible location** — an object lives in a spreadsheet, a personal drive, a chat history or somebody's memory, and appears on the manifest as though it lived with the code. *Warning sign:* you cannot say the path.
- **The willing under-authorised owner** — the person who most wants the system is named against every line, and holds no budget, no access and no mandate. *Warning sign:* a single name appears against most of the difficult lines.

## Do it

Work from the supplied situation in [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md).

Build the manifest from the artefacts you already hold: the security work's unmitigated list, the impact assessment, the cost model, the monitoring, the measurement definitions and baselines. **Do not generate a list of what a handover should contain.** The list is a fact about this engagement, and a generic list is how the six items that go missing go missing.

Then use an agent for one specific job: give it your manifest and the description of the receiving side, and ask it to name the objects you have omitted. Record what it proposed, what you added and what you rejected and why. An agent asked to *review the manifest* will approve it; an agent asked to *name what is missing* has been given a task with an answer.

## Show it

Hand your manifest to another learner. They have one instruction: pick the line they believe you cannot defend, and ask you what the named owner would have to do to accept it.

**You may not answer with a document.** If your answer is that you will write something for them, that line is unaccepted and should be moved.

## What this does not establish

That a manifest improves anything. No source behind this programme reports whether any delivered system survived its delivery team, was maintained afterwards, or was quietly abandoned. The practice corpus records no sustainment evidence of any kind and no post-deployment record for any of its cases.

**The manifest's warrant is entirely negative.** It is built from an omission: the corpus specifies the removal of the delivery organisation's own assets in detail and says nothing about transferring the running system, its definitions, its pipelines, its runbooks, its harness, its cost model or its monitoring to anyone. Inferring a practice from an absence is weak, and this session is one of the places this programme does it.
