# S089 · The Practice Improving Itself

## The situation

Your organisation has a written practice. One of its pages describes how an engagement like this one is run. It does not describe what your team did.

Nobody has amended it. Nobody was asked to. **In the openly published handbook this practice descends from, an engineer is obliged not merely to follow the standard but to fix it when it is broken, and the route for doing that is a public change request that anybody can inspect.** The right is real. The uneven dates across that handbook's pages show it being exercised on some parts of the document and not on others, which is what a real right looks like from outside.

Answer these in writing before the session opens.

1. Name one occasion when you followed the written practice and it produced the wrong thing. Not *was unhelpful* — produced the wrong thing.
2. What did you do instead, and what would have happened if the next team did not know to?
3. Is your amendment a correction or a preference? How can somebody else tell?
4. What in the practice looks broken and is not, because you did not understand what it was for?
5. What will your amendment be ignored in favour of?

**Item 5 is the one that decides whether the amendment matters.** Advisory guidance inside a system of checklists loses to the checklists, every time, and an amendment that adds advice to a document full of gates has changed nothing while feeling like a contribution.

## What you must produce

Two amendments, one proposed and one declined.

The **proposed amendment** carries four things: the page or clause it changes, the occasion on which the current text produced the wrong outcome, the replacement text, and **the mechanism that will make it bind** — a gate, a required field, a check, a state that cannot be reached — or an honest statement that it will not bind and is advisory.

The **declined amendment** is a change you seriously considered and are not making, with the reason. Not a straw candidate. **A learner with nothing they declined has not been choosing.**

## What good looks like

- The occasion is specific, dated and locatable. **An amendment justified by a general impression is a preference.**
- The replacement text is text. Somebody could apply it without asking you what you meant.
- The binding mechanism is named honestly. Advisory is a legitimate answer, stated as one, and it comes with a prediction about how often the advice will be followed.
- **The amendment survives its author.** It does not require you to be present, does not encode your team's habits as universal, and does not describe your engagement's circumstances as though they were everybody's.
- The declined amendment is one you wanted. Declining something you did not want is bookkeeping.
- Where the practice contradicts itself, the contradiction is reported as a finding rather than resolved by picking a side. **Different sections of one standard disagreeing is a first-class defect, and the disagreement is more informative than either side of it.**

## How it goes wrong

Five failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **The preference in a correction's clothes** — the amendment makes the standard match what you would have preferred to do. *Warning sign:* your occasion is that the current text was annoying.
- **The advisory amendment** — a sentence of good sense added to a document of gates, changing nothing. *Warning sign:* your text begins with *teams should consider*.
- **Amendment as complaint** — the change describes what somebody else did wrong. *Warning sign:* your proposal would be read as being about a person.
- **The standard misread as broken** — the clause exists for a reason you were not present for, and your amendment removes a control. *Warning sign:* you could not answer item 4.
- **The genre change** — the amendment replaces a duty with a pointer to where the duty is described. *Warning sign:* your text is shorter and refers somewhere else. **A document that migrates from stating obligations to routing readers to them gains currency and loses self-containment, and a team reading only the current version learns process rather than duty.**

## Do it

Work from the supplied situation in [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md), and from your own record of this programme.

Find the occasion first. **Go back through what you produced at [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md), [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) and [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) and look for the moment you departed from a stated practice and were right to.** That departure is either an undocumented improvement or an error you have not yet noticed, and deciding which is the session.

Write the amendment as a change request against the text, in the form the practice's own amendment route accepts. **Doing it in the real form matters**, because a proposal written as a memo is a proposal that has not met the mechanism that would reject it.

Then find your declined amendment, and write the reason at the same length as the proposal. Reasons for declining are shorter than reasons for proposing in almost every document, and that asymmetry is where practices accumulate change nobody argued against.

## Show it

Read the occasion, then the replacement text, then the binding mechanism. In that order, and no summary.

One question from the room: **who has to agree, and what would they say?** A learner who has not thought about the person with the authority to reject the amendment has written a document rather than proposed a change.

## What this does not establish

**That practices improve when the people who use them may amend them.** The amendment right is verified — it is stated as an obligation in a published handbook, and its route is a public change request against a public repository. **Whether exercising it makes the practice better is not reported by anybody.**

What is observable is weaker and more interesting: the right is exercised unevenly. Some parts of that handbook are actively revised and some have not been touched in years, and nothing in its navigation distinguishes them. About half of it is substantive and about half is recipe, stub or link list, with no signal marking which. It contradicts itself in at least a dozen places and never notices — two pages disagreeing about the same rule, a worked example that does not follow its own template, a recipe recommending the approach its sibling comparison advises against. **That is the normal condition of a multi-author document maintained over years, and it is far more instructive to show than to hide.**

**The internal corpus behind this programme has no amendment mechanism at all**, and its own strata disagree with each other in at least as many places, including on team composition, on prototype sequencing and between a stated antipattern and the collection's own recipe. Nothing reconciles them. **This session is written against that**, on the argument that a standard nobody may amend is a standard nobody owns — which is an argument and not a finding.
