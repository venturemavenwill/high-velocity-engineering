# S089 · The Handover Artefact and Its Warranty

**Module:** [M18](/wiki/modules/M18.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** PE · SY · SG
**Whitepaper:** [WP-089](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-089.md)
**Depends on:** S034, S069, S086, S088
**Platform anchor:** Azure deployment stacks — `actionOnUnmanage` and deny settings as a warranty with enforceable terms — and Foundry and Entra agent identities as ownership that survives a person · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (write) · Foundry MCP Server (preview) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

Three days have produced the pieces. [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) measured what a receiver actually retains. [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) bounded the claim. [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) wrote what remains, to a person who will bear it.

Today they become one document, and the document acquires the thing none of the three has: **a statement of what happens when it turns out to be wrong.**

That is a warranty, and the students have written one before without the word. [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md) step 7, two years ago, asked for one page in the caller's language: *what you are giving them, what it guarantees, and what it costs them.* [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md) point 6 gave it three parts: what you may assume, what you will observe, what you must do. [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) step 5 attached a not-met procedure. This day assembles those into an artefact whose reader is guaranteed to be absent when it is written, and whose author is guaranteed to be absent when it is read.

**The warranty is not only a document today, because the platform has one.** A deployment stack is the closest thing this substrate has to a warranty with enforceable terms: it holds a list of the resources it manages, a deny setting saying who may change or delete them, and an **unmanage action** saying what happens to a resource that stops being managed — detach it, or delete it. Those are the same four columns the day is about to require, expressed in a system rather than in prose, **and every one of them has a boundary the document does not mention.** Students will write a warranty and then find out what their stack actually enforces, which is not the same thing.

The day also has a contradiction to resolve, and it is the programme's own.

**[S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) fails an arrangement whose owner is named as a function rather than a person** — "the platform team", "product", "on-call" — on the ground that a function cannot bear a cost and cannot be asked a question. That was correct and it remains correct. **A person-named owner is also exactly what does not survive a handover.** [WP-069](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §6 identified the conflict, stated that S069 does not flag it, and assigned the resolution here. There is no later day.

The target: **a handover artefact states what is guaranteed, under what conditions, for how long, and what happens when it is not met — and every ownership in it carries a person, a slot and a transfer, because a name is a fact with a date.**

## The delivery standard on this day, and the limitation this day carries

This day carries **principle 2 of the [FDE Delivery Standard](/wiki/program/14-FDE-Delivery-Standard.md): leave the customer's engineers more capable, not more dependent** — pair with them so that they finish stronger rather than more reliant. It is the strand's hardest principle, it is what this day exists for, and **it is the principle this programme is least able to assess.**

**That limitation is stated plainly here because the day carries it rather than solves it.** A student can deliver a working system, write a complete four-column warranty, ship an enforcement mechanism and reconcile it row by row, negotiate every receiver obligation honestly — **and leave the receiving team less able than they were**, because work that used to be theirs now sits inside something they did not build and cannot change. **The artefact of that engagement and the artefact of its opposite are indistinguishable.** Nothing in phase 5's eight steps separates them. Nothing in the checked-element list separates them, and nothing should be added to it that pretends otherwise: the specification checks whether the artefact *states* what the receiver must be able to do, and it cannot check whether they can do it, because the only honest check is at step 7's completion date and the author will have left.

**What the principle costs here is therefore an admission rather than an activity.** [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) measured what a receiver retained and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) forms EA-2 on this artefact, and **both examine capability through what the student can say rather than through what the recipient can do.** That is a weaker instrument than the design would like and it is the one available. **This day teaches the form of principle 2 and measures the form**, and a student who leaves believing those are the same thing has taken the wrong lesson from an otherwise well-built day.

**The playbook artefact that belongs here is the team agreement, and specifically the definition of done.** The source's condition on the form is that it is **written by the team rather than issued to it**, and that condition is what step 6 satisfies: point 8's completion criterion is a definition of done for a receiving team, and **an obligation the receiving party declines is that document being written by the people it binds rather than handed to them.** The condition is the source's; reading a declined obligation as its satisfaction is this design's own. **Principle 5 — treat security and responsible AI as first-commit obligations, not pre-launch gates — arrives as a second claim on step 8**: the deployment stack ships with the handover instead of after it, which is what a first-commit security requirement looks like when the commit is the last one.

**The playbook supplies no ownership form at all** — no date on a name, and no mechanism by which a document learns that an occupant has changed — so point 3's slot-and-occupant resolution takes nothing from it and remains this design's own. **Neither source reports any outcome, evaluation or measurement for any of it**, and [WP-089](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-089.md)'s evidence status says so.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Eight items on material not yet taught. Confidence attached. Never recorded.

1. **Write the guarantee you would give the receiving party for your component**, in one sentence.
2. **What conditions is it under?** List them.
3. **What must the receiving party do for your guarantee to hold?** If nothing, say so.
4. [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) required an owner to be a named person rather than a function, because a function cannot bear a cost. **You are handing over. What happens to that requirement, and what do you write instead?**
5. **How long does your guarantee last, and what ends it?**
6. Your handover artefact does not mention something the receiving party needs. **How do they find out that it does not mention it?**
7. Predict: **how many of your item 2 conditions will still be in the artefact at the end of the day?**
8. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to write the deployment stack that protects your component's resources for the receiving party. Write down what it will produce, and **name the one setting it will choose for you that you would not have chosen.** Sealed with items 1 to 7 and opened at phase 8.

Item 4 is the contradiction, taken cold from every student in the room, and the answers are collected verbatim so that phase 4 point 3 can be read against them. Item 6 is the [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md) discoverability demand, arriving two years later.

**Warrant.** Pretesting the whole artefact in miniature and the day's contradiction in one item, before any framework. Item 3 is constructed so that most of the room writes *nothing*, which is the belief phase 3's payload defeats. Item 4 is asked cold because a resolution supplied before the room has felt the conflict is a rule rather than an answer, and the room can feel it in ninety seconds. Item 8 is the day's contribution to the running frontier map, is sealed at phase 1 per instrumentation rule 8, and is deliberately about a **default** rather than an error, because the day's subject is terms nobody stated and the agent will supply several.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups of four receive **four handover artefacts, with warranties, for four components of this substrate**, each with the receiving team's record of the following six months: what they asked, what they could not find, what they did when the guarantee was not met, and whether they still operate the component.

**Each artefact comes with the deployment stack that was in force at the time**, reproduced in a disposable resource group. For twenty minutes each group tries, for each of the four, to establish **what the stack actually prevents**: they attempt a delete, attempt a write, attempt to add a child resource, and attempt each of those as a principal on the exclusion list and as one who is not. They record, per artefact, **one thing the warranty promises that the stack enforces, and one thing the warranty promises that it does not.** That table is written before the six-month records appear.

**The sampling is disclosed, per instrumentation rule 3.** Unlike the corpora at [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) and [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md), **these four are real and were not commissioned** — they are four of the substrate's own handovers, and they are the four for which a six-month receiving-team record exists. That is itself a selection: components whose handovers went badly generate more record than components whose handovers went well, and the room is told so before reading. The design states which corpora are constructed and which are found, on the same day in the module where it would be easiest not to. **The reproduced stacks carry their own disclosure**: they are the stacks as declared, in a disposable subscription, and a deny setting behaves differently against a principal who holds a role the disposable subscription does not grant.

- **W1 · The unlimited one.** A guarantee with no conditions, no exclusions and no expiry: *this component works as described.* The receiving team read it as unlimited, because it is, and escalated to a person who no longer worked there for four months before anyone told them. **Its stack has no deny setting at all, so the prose promises everything and the system prevents nothing.**
- **W2 · The exclusion list.** Three lines of guarantee and two pages of exclusions, each individually reasonable. The receiving team concluded the artefact guaranteed nothing, stopped reading it, and rebuilt the component's behaviour from the code — correctly, slowly, and with two of the original constraints lost. **Its stack denies write and delete on everything, which is the same document in a second register and which the receiving team worked around by deploying beside it.**
- **W3 · The one whose owner left.** Well-formed, [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md)-compliant, and every ownership in it is a named person. **Every one of those people had left within fourteen months.** The document is now a list of names the receiving team cannot use, and it is not stale in any way a reader can detect: it looks exactly as authoritative as it did on the day it was written. **Its stack carries the same names as excluded principals — and it carries seven of them, where the documented maximum is five, which does not return an error.** Two of the seven were never exempt and nobody found out until a delete they were entitled to make was denied.
- **W4 · The two-way one.** The guarantee is narrower than W1's and the exclusion set is shorter than W2's. Its distinguishing feature is a column the other three do not have: **for each guarantee, what the receiving party must do for it to hold** — keep the upstream schema stable, run the weekly check, tell the author's successor within a week if the check fails. The receiving team met some of those obligations and not others, and the artefact told them exactly which guarantees had lapsed as a result. **Its stack denies delete and not write, its unmanage action is detach rather than delete, and its excluded principals are a group rather than a list of people** — three choices that each correspond to a sentence in the document.

Groups rank the four on *which artefact they would rather receive* and **commit before the six-month records are supplied.** Then the records are supplied.

The payload is **W4**, and what it defeats is the room's assumption that a warranty is a promise. **It is a conditional promise, and the conditions are on the receiver.** A guarantee with no receiver obligations is not more generous; it is unmaintainable, because the author cannot guarantee behaviour under conditions they have no control over and will not be present to observe. W1 is what that looks like when written by someone who wanted to be helpful.

**W3 is the day's second finding and it is the programme's own.** W3 satisfies [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) completely and fails for exactly that reason, and it fails in the worst available way — silently, with the document still looking correct.

Task after: **for each artefact, name what the receiving team could not find and how they found out that they could not find it.** Then: **for each artefact, where did the prose and the stack disagree, and which of the two did the receiving team believe?** Then: **what does W4 cost, and what happens when a receiving party refuses its obligations?**

The last question has an answer the day needs. W4's obligations are a negotiation, and a receiving party may decline them — at which point the guarantee they attach to is withdrawn rather than the obligation being waived, and the artefact must say which guarantees are withdrawn rather than leaving the receiver to work it out. A student who writes obligations nobody agreed to has written W2 in a better costume.

The middle question is the day's second execution finding and it goes in a direction the room does not expect. **The receiving teams believed the prose.** In all four cases the document was what got read and the stack was what got hit, and in three of the four the disagreement between them was discovered by a failed operation rather than by anybody comparing the two. **A warranty and its enforcement are two artefacts, they drift, and only one of them is written for a reader.**

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across warranty construction with a six-month receiving-team record as adjudicator, matched on organisation and on the existence of a record, and with the corpus's real-but-selected provenance disclosed — including that the selection is biased toward handovers that generated record, which is toward the ones that went badly. Commit-before-records makes the ranking a generation act. **The stack-versus-prose table is the practical turn's contribution and it is the phase's second payload**: it establishes, before phase 4 says anything, that the document is not the mechanism, which is the premise point 2 and point 4 both rest on. W4 defeats the day's target belief; **W3 stages the programme's own contradiction twice — once in prose, where every owner has left, and once in the stack, where a seven-name exclusion list silently exceeded a five-name limit** — in a case where nobody did anything wrong, which is the condition that makes point 3 an answer rather than a reprimand of [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md).

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **A handover artefact is a contract with a reader you will never meet.** [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md) point 6's three parts at class 6: what you may assume, what you will observe, what you must do — in the receiving party's language, and with the author unavailable for clarification. Everything else in the artefact serves those three. **The three parts are the minimum and omit the commercial and legal layer of a real handover contract, which exists, is written by other people, and is not what this day is about. Safe as the engineering content of a handover between parties inside one organisation or under an existing agreement; unsafe as a contract between organisations, where the same words carry liabilities this form does not contemplate and where writing them without counsel is the error.**

2. **The warranty has four parts.** What is guaranteed. **Under what conditions**, including what the receiver must do. **For how long**, by event and by date, which is [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) point 8's expiry arriving as a contractual term. And **what happens when it is not met** — [S025](/wiki/seminars/S025.md) step 5's not-met procedure, with a route, a response and a person. A guarantee missing any of the four is not narrower than a complete one; it is undefined in that dimension, and an undefined dimension is read as unlimited by the reader and as excluded by the author.

3. **The contradiction, resolved.** [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) requires an owner to be a named person because a function cannot bear a cost or answer a question. That requirement is correct and is not weakened. A named person also does not survive a handover, which W3 demonstrates without anybody having erred. **The resolution is that ownership has two components and [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) named only one.** There is a **slot** — the role that bears the cost, which is what persists — and an **occupant**, the person who currently holds it, which is what can be asked a question. [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) was right that the occupant must be named, and it was addressing a live arrangement, where the slot is implicit because everybody in the room knows it. In a handover nobody knows it, so **both must be written, together with the date the occupant was correct and the mechanism by which the artefact learns of a change.** A name with a date is a fact; a name without one is a claim about the present that will silently become false. And the mechanism is the load-bearing part: **an ownership entry whose occupant can change with no signal is [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) point 4's failure — a medium that goes stale without failing.** The artefact must therefore either name where the current occupant is authoritatively recorded, or state that it does not know, which is [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md)'s recoverability condition applied to a name.

   **The distinction is not this design's invention and the platform makes it visible in three places.** A managed identity or an agent identity is a slot: it holds the role assignments, it is what the resources trust, and it does not resign. **A Foundry project's own managed identity is the assignee of the data-plane roles, not any human**, which is why a departing engineer's leaving does not break the pipeline — and why nobody notices for a year that no human can reach it either. **An agent identity in Microsoft Entra carries an accountable business owner assigned at creation**, which is the platform enforcing exactly the requirement [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) made, on the occupant side. And **the tenant's agent registry publishes a count of agents without owners**, which is the occupant side failing, counted. The slot is stable, the occupant is not, and the platform has separate machinery for each because they are separate things. **What the platform does not supply is the date and the mechanism**, which is precisely what the artefact must add.

4. **Obligations run both ways.** W4's column. A guarantee is conditional on the receiver's conduct because the author cannot guarantee behaviour under conditions they neither control nor observe. Each receiver obligation attaches to specific guarantees, and **when the obligation is not met, the artefact says which guarantees lapse** rather than leaving the receiver to infer it. **A receiving party may decline an obligation**, in which case the attached guarantee is withdrawn and the withdrawal is recorded — obligations are negotiated, not imposed, and an obligation nobody agreed to is decoration.

5. **Incompleteness must be discoverable.** [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md) phase 7's third question — *name something the contract does not tell you that you need* — was that day's terminal demand and is answered here as a design requirement rather than a reader's skill. The artefact must tell its reader **what classes of thing it does not cover, where the boundary is, and who to ask.** This is [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md)'s selection disclosure applied to a document rather than to a statement, and it is the same move for the same reason: a reader who does not know an omission exists cannot ask about it.

6. **What the artefact carries from the three preceding days**, and none of it is optional. **[S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md)'s safety case with its assumptions** — and every assumption whose responsible party was *none* must be assigned an owner here or entered in the exclusion set; an assumption in neither is the artefact's most dangerous entry, because it is a premise the receiver will not know they are relying on. **[S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md)'s residual-risk statement with its not-met procedures**, whose named people are now subject to point 3's slot-and-occupant form. **[S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md)'s not-transferred list**, whose entries are exclusions.

7. **What a warranty must not promise.** Four things this design refuses: **behaviour under conditions you cannot observe**; **the conduct of a third party**; **anything whose failure you would not detect**, which is [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s detection commitment as a limit on what may be guaranteed rather than as an operational design; and **availability of yourself**. **This list is not exhaustive and omits the promises that are unwise for commercial rather than engineering reasons — indemnities, timelines, exclusivity — which are real and are somebody else's expertise. Safe as a filter on the engineering guarantees you are tempted to write; unsafe as a sufficient review of a document that will be signed.**

8. **The handover is complete when the receiver can do the three things without you** — operate, diagnose, change — **not when the document is signed.** [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) point 1 as the completion criterion. This is why the artefact names a date at which that will be checked and by whom, and why a handover with a signature and no check is a transfer of paperwork.

9. **There is a warranty in the system, its columns are the same four, and every one of them is narrower than it reads.** A deployment stack holds the resources it manages, a **deny setting** — none, deny delete, or deny write and delete — saying what may be done to them and by whom, and an **unmanage action** saying what happens to a resource that leaves the managed set: detach it, or delete it. That is *what is guaranteed*, *under what conditions*, and *what happens when it is not met*, expressed in a system. Four boundaries follow and each one is a sentence a student will otherwise write wrongly. **Deny settings apply to control-plane operations and not to data-plane ones**, so a stack that protects a key vault does not protect the secrets in it and a stack that protects a storage account does not protect the containers. **They apply to explicitly declared resources and not to implicitly created ones**, so anything a managed service creates on your behalf is outside the guarantee. **The exclusion list holds at most five principals, and supplying more does not return an error** — which is W3's second failure and is the purest example in the module of a guarantee that stops applying without saying so; the documented remedy is to exclude a group rather than people, and to test nested-group membership before relying on it. And **the preview operation that shows what a deployment would change is not supported for stacks**, so the one tool that would let a receiving team see the consequence of an update before making it does not apply to the artefact that most needs it. **All of that is perishable. The durable claim is that an enforcement mechanism has a scope, the scope is narrower than the prose that describes it, and the difference is discovered by a receiving team through a failed operation unless somebody wrote it down.**

10. **Defaults are opinions, and today's decide what your handover destroys.** Three were chosen for somebody and the room should know which. **`actionOnUnmanage` has no safe value** — detach leaves orphans that nobody is now managing and delete removes resources that stopped being declared, and the documentation warns that deleting at resource-group scope removes the group and everything in it. A receiving team that deletes one resource from a Bicep file and runs an update has invoked whichever of those two the departing engineer chose, and they did not read the flag. **A system-assigned managed identity is deleted when the resource it belongs to is deleted**, and a user-assigned one is not, which means one of the two silently takes the role assignments with it. **And publishing an agent gives it a new identity, at which point the role assignments must be reassigned** — the slot changed and nothing followed it. **An engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system**, and in a handover the person who finds out is the one who inherited it.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to the four artefacts, to the room's phase 2 split, which is read back at point 3, and to the stack-versus-prose table phase 3 produced. Points 1, 7 and 8's supporting material carry the scope statements required by the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), two in strong form; **point 9 carries a third, in strong form, on the enforcement mechanism itself.** **Point 3 is the day's obligatory content**: it resolves the conflict [WP-069](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §6 identified, it does so by extending [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) rather than by overturning it, and it is stated as a correction to the programme's own earlier day so that the correction is legible rather than absorbed; **its platform paragraph is what turns the resolution from an invention into an observation**, because the distinction between a slot and an occupant is already built into the identity system these students have been using for a year. Point 5 discharges [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s terminal demand two years later by converting a reader's skill into an author's requirement. **Points 9 and 10 are the day's perishable platform content and its named defaults, required by the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md); point 9 carries the day's documentation silence, which is that the five-principal cap is stated together with the fact that exceeding it does not error, and nothing anywhere states what happens to the entries beyond the fifth.**

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 6. The artefact, its guarantees, its conditions and its negotiation are the student's.**

Each student assembles the handover artefact for **the component they chose at [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md)**, using their [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) case and their [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) statement.

1. **Write the three-part contract** — what the receiving party may assume, what they will observe, what they must do — one page, in their language, per [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md) point 6.
2. **Write the warranty table.** One row per guarantee, four columns: what is guaranteed; under what conditions **including the receiver's obligations**; for how long, by event and date; and the not-met procedure with a route and a response. **One row is fixed: the floor you wrote at [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md) element 7**, restated here as a guarantee, **with whether it survived the term and, if it did not, what moved it.** A floor that moved is a guarantee you no longer hold and the receiving party must be told which.
3. **Write every ownership in slot-and-occupant form:** the slot, the current occupant, the date the occupant was correct, and where the current occupant is authoritatively recorded — or the explicit statement that it is not recorded anywhere.
4. **Resolve your [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) unowned assumptions.** Each is assigned an owner in slot-and-occupant form or entered in the exclusion set. **An assumption in neither fails the specification.**
5. **Write the discoverability clause:** what classes of thing this artefact does not cover, where its boundary is, and who to ask. Per point 5 and [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md)'s selection disclosure.
6. **Negotiate the receiver obligations** with the actual receiving party, and record the agreement — or, for each declined obligation, **the guarantee that is withdrawn as a result**, in [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s three-part form.
7. **State the completion check:** the date on which the receiver's ability to operate, diagnose and change will be tested, who runs it, and what happens if it fails. **Attach the disposition register:** for each recommendation you made at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) item 8, record whether it was **implemented**, **declined with a reason**, or **never answered.** All three are admissible entries; an omitted recommendation is not.
8. **Ship the enforcement, and reconcile it against column two.** Using Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server, **declare and deploy the deployment stack for your component's resources** — its managed set, its deny setting, its excluded principals, and its unmanage action — into the substrate, merged. Then produce **the reconciliation table**: for each row of your step 2 warranty, one line saying **whether the stack enforces it, does not enforce it, or enforces something the warranty does not say.** The third column is the one that matters and is the one phase 3 found in three of four real artefacts. **Where a guarantee is not enforceable by the stack — because it concerns a data-plane operation, an implicitly created resource, or a behaviour no mechanism observes — say so in the artefact rather than in this table**, because the receiving party reads the artefact.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned. **The instruction that set the unmanage action is the one the assessors will ask about**, because it is the single most consequential term in the artefact and the one most likely to have been supplied rather than chosen.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — a stack whose excluded-principal list is a group rather than an enumeration, with a comment saying why, is the accepted example on this day, because it is the design that survives the limit the platform does not enforce.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch. Both specific, both from this build.

**The receiving party is present for the last thirty minutes and is not obliged to accept any obligation.** Six minutes each.

**Students whose [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) reconstruction gap ran in the over-confident direction write column two of step 2 — the conditions and the receiver's obligations — before column one.** This is the [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) rule-6 response and it is a sequencing requirement rather than a content one: an author who over-estimated what a stranger retained will write a guarantee first and fit conditions to it, and reversing the order makes the conditions the artefact's premise instead of its qualification.

**Fading:** step 1 reuses [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s three-part form; step 2's four columns are supplied as a table; step 6 reuses [S039](/wiki/seminars/S039.md)'s trade preparation and [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s three-part record; step 7's disposition register is a three-valued lookup against an existing record; step 8's stack has a supplied skeleton with **every setting left blank**. **Steps 3 and 5 are unsupported** — the ownership form and the discoverability clause are the day's two resolutions and a supplied template for either would produce the answer the template holds.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 6 with a live negotiation against a party who may decline, which is [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) step 7's construction with the stakes changed: there the student negotiated an arrangement they would operate, here they negotiate obligations they will not be present to enforce. **Step 4 is the artefact element [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) named under instrumentation rule 6**, and it is a fail condition. **The column-order requirement is the artefact element [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) named under the same rule; step 2's fixed row is [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md)'s; and step 7's disposition register is [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md)'s.** Step 3 is unsupported because slot-and-occupant is the day's resolution and a student who fills in a supplied template has not made the distinction; step 5 is unsupported because a discoverability clause written to a template describes the template's boundary rather than the artefact's. **Step 8 is the day's build and the reconciliation table is the point of it**: writing a warranty is a documentary act and every prior day in this module has been one, and the reconciliation is the first occasion in the programme on which a student's promise is placed beside the mechanism that will or will not keep it. The skeleton is supplied with every setting blank because **the settings are the judgement** — a stack whose unmanage action was accepted from a sample is W1 in a different register, and the prompt record is where that becomes visible. **The disposition register is the only occasion in the programme at which a student's own recommendation meets the organisation that received it**, and *never answered* is an admissible entry precisely because it is the commonest real outcome and the one an author is most tempted to soften.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: **a handover on this substrate whose warranty was met exactly and whose receiving team was worse off.** The guarantee held, the conditions were satisfied, the not-met procedure was never invoked because nothing was ever not-met — and the component was quietly abandoned within a year, because the artefact guaranteed behaviour the receiving team had no use for and said nothing about the two things they actually needed to change. Attention is directed at one fact: **nobody could point at a defect in the document**, and the handover failed at [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) point 10's boundary, which is adoption rather than transfer.

Then the agent case: **a Copilot-written deployment stack that was confidently wrong and passed review.** The student asked for a stack that would protect the component's resources for the receiving party. The agent produced a correct, well-formed declaration with `--action-on-unmanage deleteAll`, which is what the documentation's own example uses. It deployed. It protected the resources. **Nine months later the receiving team removed one resource from the Bicep file and ran an update, and the stack deleted the resource group.** Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the reviewer checked that the stack did what the warranty promised, and the flag governs what happens when the warranty stops applying** — a clause about the end of the relationship, reviewed by somebody thinking about the beginning of it. The documentation warns about it in a boxed note that the agent did not carry into the artefact and the reviewer did not go and read.

Then students, in threes: **a promise you have made this year whose conditions you did not state.**

The firewall applies and is stated before the prompt: nothing surfaced here reaches any assessment judgement about the student who surfaced it. This is the ninth demonstration under assessment-adjacent conditions and the last before the entrustment occasion.

**Warrant.** Error management on the day's residue. The instructor case is chosen because the artefact is faultless under every rule the day has taught, which prevents the clinic reinforcing phase 4 and keeps it on the failure the framework does not reach — a correct warranty over the wrong guarantees. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen because it is the day's own subject performed on the students**: a term nobody stated, supplied by a default, in a document that reads as a guarantee, discovered by a receiving team through a destructive operation. It is also the sharpest available answer to the question of why phase 5 step 8's skeleton has every setting blank. The student prompt targets step 2's second column and is timed after it, so what surfaces is a conditions failure the students have just committed to avoiding in writing.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student's partner receives **the artefact alone** — no safety case, no residual-risk statement, no conversation — and, playing the receiving party six months after the handover, must produce three things in writing before the author speaks:

- **one thing they need that the artefact does not tell them**, and **how they discovered that it does not tell them**;
- **one guarantee they believe has lapsed**, and which obligation caused it;
- **one ownership entry they would not be able to use**, and why.

The author then states which are correct, and where the first answer identifies a real gap the **discoverability clause is revised on the spot** and the revision recorded.

Per specification rule 3, the partner's three answers, the author's adjudication and any revision are elements of the artefact.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with a document-only test. Per instrumentation rule 2 this is the seventh document-only elicitation in the programme and **its rate is not compared against the earlier six.** The first question is [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md) phase 7's third question returned two years later with its second half added — *how did you discover that it does not tell you* — which is the part that tests point 5 rather than the reader's ingenuity, and which [WP-034](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-034.md) §7 named this day as carrying. The third question is the only test in the day of whether slot-and-occupant form actually helps a reader, as opposed to satisfying the design.

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- **Item 4's answers read back anonymously** against point 3's resolution: **how many students resolved the contradiction by retreating to a function, how many by naming a successor, and how many wrote both.** The room's phase 2 split is shown beside them.
- **Item 3 resolved:** how many of the room wrote *nothing* for what the receiving party must do, against how many receiver obligations appear in the cohort's step 2 tables.
- Item 7's predicted surviving-condition count against the actual.
- **Item 8 opened.** The sealed Copilot predictions are read against the settings the agent actually chose at step 8, and the cohort's hit rate is recorded. **Both halves count**: predicting that the agent would supply a default is easy, and naming which is the measure. **The cohort's distribution of agent-chosen unmanage actions is reported**, and it is the day's most direct evidence about how a default becomes a term.
- The room's phase 3 rankings against the six-month records: **how many groups ranked W1 above W4**, and **how many of the four prose-versus-stack disagreements the room found before the records were supplied.**
- **The step 8 reconciliation totals:** across the cohort, how many warranty rows the stack enforces, how many it does not, and how many things it enforces that no warranty row mentions.
- **The [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) unowned-assumption cohort total reported at the start of the day is closed:** how many were assigned owners, how many were excluded, and how many students had an assumption in neither before step 4 was checked.
- **The [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) reconstruction-gap direction, cohort-wide**, and how many students therefore wrote column two first.
- The step 6 outcomes: **how many obligations the receiving party declined, and how many guarantees were withdrawn as a result.**
- **The [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) phase 8 kept sentences are returned** — the item each student decided would not survive them — and each student states, in one line, whether the artefact they have just written changes that. The lines are not collected.

- Retrieval schedule: next morning, day 3, and at [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md).

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in PE390 with CAP390. Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the three-part contract in the receiving party's language; the warranty table with all four columns per guarantee, including receiver obligations and a not-met procedure with a route and a response, and including the [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md) floor restated as a guarantee with whether it survived the term and what moved it; every ownership in slot-and-occupant form with the occupant's date and the authoritative record or an explicit statement that none exists; every [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) unowned assumption assigned or excluded; the discoverability clause; the negotiated obligations with, for each declined obligation, the guarantee withdrawn, recorded in three-part form; the completion check with its date, its runner and its failure consequence; the [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) disposition register with a three-valued entry for every recommendation; **the deployed deployment stack as merged, and the reconciliation table with one line per warranty row and its enforces / does not enforce / enforces more entry**; the phase 7 partner answers with the author's adjudication and any revision; **the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed** — not the artefact's length, not whether the receiving party accepted the obligations, **and not how much of the warranty the stack turned out to enforce.**

**Seven hard requirements.** A guarantee **missing any of the four warranty columns fails.** An ownership entry **naming only a person or only a function fails** — this is the day's resolution and it is binary in both directions. An [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) assumption that is **neither assigned nor excluded fails.** An artefact with **no discoverability clause fails.** A disposition register **omitting a recommendation fails** — *never answered* is an admissible entry and silence is not. **A warranty row with no reconciliation line fails**, and a reconciliation table that reports no *enforces more* entries when the stack has settings the warranty does not mention fails, because that column is where the artefact's silent terms live. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**A low enforcement count is a pass.** Most of a good warranty is unenforceable by a stack, because most of it concerns data-plane behaviour, third parties and conduct, and a specification that rewarded enforcement would produce warranties written to fit a mechanism. **What fails is the omission of the line, not the content of it.**

**A declined obligation is a pass.** An obligation the receiving party refused, recorded with the guarantee withdrawn, is the specification's expected outcome for a substantial share of the cohort. **An obligation recorded as agreed when it was not is a specification failure**, and is treated as [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) treated the same act.

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md), where this artefact is the evidence EA-2 is formed from.

**Error-clinic firewall.** Phase 6 is unobserved by anyone contributing to a specification judgement, nothing from it is recorded, and phase 8's returned kept sentences are not collected.

**Discharge of the [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) contradiction.** [WP-069](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §6 stated that "an arrangement whose owner is a named person is exactly what does not survive a handover, and the day that teaches 'name a person, not a function' is setting up a contradiction S089 has to resolve," that the design regards the contradiction as real, and that "this day does not flag it, and S089 must." **The element exists and it is a fail condition.** Phase 2's bridge puts the contradiction to the room and leaves it open; phase 3's W3 stages it in a case where nobody erred; **phase 4 point 3 resolves it** — ownership has a slot and an occupant, [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) named the occupant correctly for a live arrangement where the slot is implicit, and a handover must carry both with the occupant's date and the mechanism by which the artefact learns of a change; and phase 5 step 3 requires the form, with an entry naming only one of the two failing the specification. [WP-069](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md)'s own preferred resolution — that a handover must transfer the ownership explicitly rather than that the ownership should have been anonymous — is adopted and is made operational rather than merely endorsed.

**Discharge of the [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md) obligation.** [WP-034](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-034.md) §7 recorded that "the phase 7 third-question quality is the incompleteness-discoverability measure carried to S089." **The element exists in two places:** phase 4 point 5 converts [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s reader-side demand into an author-side requirement, and phase 5 step 5's discoverability clause is a hard specification requirement. Phase 7's first question re-administers [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s third question with its second half added — *how did you discover that it does not tell you* — which is what makes the measure about the artefact rather than about the reader.

**Rule-6 discharge.** Four obligations land on this day and **all four elements exist.**

- **[S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md)** named this day as the response occasion for its prediction-to-reconstruction gap, specifying that students whose gap ran over-confident write the receiver-obligation clause before the guarantee clause. **That is phase 5's column-order requirement, and the cohort direction is reported at phase 4 and again at phase 8.**
- **[S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md)** named this day as the response occasion for its unowned-assumption total, specifying that every unowned assumption be assigned an owner or entered in the warranty's exclusion set and that one in neither fail. **That is phase 5 step 4, it is one of the day's seven hard requirements, and the cohort total is reported at phase 4 and closed at phase 8.**
- **[S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md)** named this day as the response occasion for its item 8 handover — three recommendations with owners, produced for an implementer who was not present — specifying that the handover artefact include the disposition of each recommendation, implemented, declined with a reason, or never answered. **That is phase 5 step 7's disposition register, it is a checked element, and an omitted recommendation fails.** [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) recorded this as the only occasion in the programme where a student's recommendation meets the organisation that received it, and *never answered* is admissible for that reason.
- **[S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md)** named this day as the response occasion for its element 7 floor-holding rate, specifying that the handover warranty restate the floor and state whether it survived the term and what moved it. **That is phase 5 step 2's fixed row and it is a checked element.** [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md) element 3's warranty is the object this artefact extends, and a floor that moved is recorded as a guarantee the student no longer holds rather than as a negotiation outcome.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments, and **for both the response occasion is programme-level rather than a later seminar day, because there is no later ordinary day in the programme.** This is stated explicitly rather than left to inference, and what consumes each is named.

- **The slot-and-occupant usability rate** — how often a partner at phase 7, reading the artefact alone as a receiver six months on, identifies an ownership entry they could not use. **Response occasion: [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) phase 3, where the real receiving party is asked the same question during the handover, and the two rates are compared.** Beyond the programme, **the result is consumed by PE390's specification review**, which is required to state whether the slot-and-occupant form survived contact with the receiving organisation and, if it did not, what replaces it in the next offering — and by the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](/wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md)'s amendment process, since a resolution to a programme-level contradiction that fails in use must be reopened at [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) rather than patched here.
- **The step 6 declined-obligation rate and the guarantees withdrawn as a result.** **Response occasion: programme-level.** No seminar day consumes it, and the design says so. **It is consumed by the substrate's continuity plan** — the withdrawn guarantees are, collectively, the list of things the substrate will not be guaranteed to do after this cohort leaves, and the [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page's operating position for the following year is required to carry it — **and by the next cohort's [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md)**, whose four handover cases are drawn from the components whose guarantees were withdrawn here. That is the mechanism by which this cohort's handovers become the next cohort's phase 3 corpus, and it is the only link in the programme that runs between cohorts.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** A warranty has four parts — what is guaranteed, under what conditions **including what the receiver must do**, for how long by event and date, and what happens when it is not met. A guarantee missing one is undefined, not narrow. **Ownership has a slot and an occupant**, and the artefact carries both with the date the occupant was correct.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** Handover as an engineered artefact with a warranty, and the treatment of a declined term, follow [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md) and [research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fisher-ury-getting-to-yes.md). Commit-before-reveal staging and document-only comprehension testing are cited for **direction and mechanism only, and no claim is made for the slot-and-occupant form's effectiveness**, which is untested on the human case. The enforcement documentation states its limits and **says nothing about what becomes of the entries beyond its cap.**

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Reconcile the prose against the mechanism, row by row**, and look hardest at what it enforces that no row mentions.
- **Negotiate receiver obligations rather than imposing them**, and where one is declined, withdraw the guarantee it carries.
- **Say what the artefact does not cover and who to ask.** A reader unaware that an omission exists cannot ask about it.
- **The most consequential term is not a field an agent may fill.** Per the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md), human approval is a stop condition, and the unmanage action governs what happens when the relationship ends.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Assuming a limit that does not error is a limit that holds. A protection that silently stops applying is found by a denied operation.
