# S089 · The Practice Improving Itself

**Whitepaper:** [WP-089](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-089.md)
**Retrieves from:** S024, S044, S086, S087
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

Your organisation has a written practice. One of its pages describes how an engagement like this one is run. It does not describe what your team did.

Nobody has amended it. Nobody was asked to. **In the openly published handbook this practice descends from, an engineer is obliged not merely to follow the standard but to fix it when it is broken, and the route for doing that is a public change request that anybody can inspect.** The right is real. The uneven dates across that handbook's pages show it being exercised on some parts of the document and not on others, which is what a real right looks like from outside.

**Answer these in writing before reading further.** Item 5 is the one that decides whether your amendment matters, and it is what this session is about.

1. Name one occasion when you followed the written practice and it produced the wrong thing. Not *was unhelpful* — produced the wrong thing.
2. What did you do instead, and what would have happened if the next team did not know to?
3. Is your amendment a correction or a preference? How can somebody else tell?
4. What in the practice looks broken and is not, because you did not understand what it was for?
5. What will your amendment be ignored in favour of?

Most people produce a real occasion for 1, a workaround for 2, and an assertion for 3. Item 4 is answered with something safe. **Item 5 is answered with nothing, because it requires you to imagine your own change losing** — and it does lose, reliably, in one specific way: advisory guidance inside a system of checklists loses to the checklists. An amendment that adds good sense to a document full of gates has changed nothing while feeling like a contribution.

## What you must produce

Two amendments, one proposed and one declined.

1. The **proposed amendment** carries four things: the page or clause it changes; the occasion on which the current text produced the wrong outcome, dated and locatable; the replacement text, as text; and **the mechanism that will make it bind** — a gate, a required field, a check, a state that cannot be reached — or an honest statement that it will not bind and is advisory, with a prediction about how often the advice will be followed.
2. The **declined amendment** is a change you seriously considered and are not making, with the reason recorded at the same length as the proposal. Not a straw candidate. **A learner with nothing they declined has not been choosing.**
3. **The annotation** — the sentence that will sit at the point of change recording what the text used to say and why it changed. Not a changelog entry somewhere else. **A superseded position that is invisible cannot be evaluated by the person who inherits the correction.**

## The material

### One source models the amendment right, the other cannot

The openly published engineering handbook takes an unusual position and states it as an obligation rather than a permission: an engineer **knows their playbook, follows their playbook, and fixes their playbook if it is broken** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md)). The route is a public change request against a public repository with a contribution guide.

**That claim is testable rather than rhetorical, and the test has a result.** Every page carries its own footer date, and across the whole site those dates run from 7 May 2021 to 26 September 2025. Some sections are actively revised; some have not been touched in years. **Nothing in the navigation distinguishes them.** About half the site is substantive practice, about a quarter is recipe pinned to a tool at a version, and a fifth is stub, link list or example instance — **with no signal marking which is which.** A reader who assigns by menu entry will assign badly.

**That is what a real amendment right looks like from outside: exercised unevenly, and invisibly so.** It is a better demonstration than a uniformly maintained document would be, because a uniformly maintained document tells you nothing about whether anybody has the right or merely the rota.

The handbook also contradicts itself in at least a dozen places and never notices. Its testing comparison table marks end-to-end tests as manual while three other pages assume they are automated. Its trade-study template says compare two or three options while its own index says two to four. Its GitOps secrets page recommends an external key vault and a sibling recipe encrypts secrets into the repository. Its branch-naming convention differs on three pages. **None of these are errors of fact. They are the normal residue of a document written by many hands over four years with no reconciliation pass**, and the honest teaching move is to hand them over as material rather than hide them. **Cite it by page and by that page's own footer date, never as a whole**; and note that it also carries four unsupported magnitude claims, which this repository records as specimens and reproduces nowhere.

**The internal corpus behind this programme has no amendment mechanism at all** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). It is a stratum spanning years and it contradicts itself in at least as many places — on team composition, on prototype sequencing, on whether design activity may be run as a separate workshop, and between a stated antipattern and its own recipe. **Nothing reconciles them.** Its stated prerequisites are followed immediately by an offer to proceed without them, which means they are not prerequisites. **It is internal material, paraphrased here and never named or quoted, and it records no declined engagement, no non-synthetic failure, no exclusion criteria and no outcome data of any kind.**

**So this session teaches an act that one source models and the other cannot.**

### This repository is the worked example, and it is the anchor for a reason

You are inside a body of work that corrects itself in public, and the record of it is the most useful specimen available because you can read the whole of it.

**Start with a stage that changed its name in front of you.** [Stage 9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md) was first drafted as *Withdraw* — close down, disengage, decommission, leave. A reviewer challenged it. **The challenge was checked rather than accepted, and the check went against the original design**: language about iteration, maturity, adoption and reinforcement overwhelms language about decommissioning across the whole corpus, and it is not close. The stage became *Compound*. **The renaming is recorded on the page rather than erased**, on a stated principle: a reader who cannot see the original position cannot evaluate the correction, and the correction is a better argument for the stage than the stage's own content is.

**Then a defect that was found and deliberately not fixed.** [The assessment architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) records that four consecutive occasions in Stages 6 and 7 used the same device — an offer the learner was expected to decline. The repetition was found by the authors of Stages 7 and 9 during construction. The second author corrected it at [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md) and [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md) rather than conceding it again. **The earlier four are unamended, and the page says so.** It also names the general risk: a device a learner can anticipate stops measuring judgement and starts measuring pattern recognition — and states that nothing establishes how many repetitions it takes.

**Then a design that caught itself violating the mechanism it was built on.** [WP-001](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-001.md) §4.3 records that the programme's opening session cited Kapur's assembly phase approvingly in §3.2 and **failed to implement its central condition** — that consolidation must explicitly prompt comparison against the learner's own failed attempt. Loibl and Leuders had tested exactly that: side-by-side presentation of correct and incorrect solutions produced no benefit, and only the explicitly-instructed comparison did ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). **The session was throwing away the mechanism it was built on, and the paper defending it cited that mechanism correctly while the session violated it.** The amendment is recorded at the point of change rather than applied silently, with the stated reason: **a design can cite a mechanism correctly, describe it accurately, and fail to implement it, and nothing in the citation catches that.**

**Then an error in the exemplar that propagated.** A research note written in the programme's final week found that the opening session and three later ones — plus the self-study edition derived from the first — rested a durable claim, *a connected tool is not an invoked tool*, on a stated evidence the documentation does not support ([research/06-microsoft-platform/learn-mcp-server-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/learn-mcp-server-docs.md)). **The claim survived and its evidence was replaced**, because the documentation supports it four separate ways: the trust-decline behaviour, the vendor's own *when the AI agent doesn't use the MCP tool when expected* section, its repository's admission about a named model, and both getting-started walkthroughs listing *allow the agent to use the MCP server* as a user step. The note records this as **a net improvement in warrant and a required correction to a cited mechanism.**

**Five files inherited a wrong evidence from one exemplar, and nothing but a full reading of the source caught it.** The mechanism of the spread is worth more than the error: each of those sessions was built by copying the exemplar's *shape*, and its unexamined factual claims came along with the shape. **A defect in an exemplar is not one defect. It is one defect per file built from it**, and the number is set by how well the exemplar worked — the better it is, the more widely its errors travel.

**And a convention that was wrong everywhere it rendered.** The repository's linking rule once read *workspace-relative*; the overwhelming majority of its internal links resolved to nothing on the public host, and **the validation command did not catch it, because the command resolved every link from the repository root rather than from the file containing it** ([AGENTS.md](/AGENTS.md)). Both the convention and the gate were fixed. **The old command was kept in the git history rather than in the file**, which is itself an amendment decision with a stated reason.

**And a defect class the gate could not see at all.** An edit tool taking raw parameters was given JSON-escaped text, and three files were left carrying literal backslash-u escape sequences and escaped newline pairs where an em dash and a paragraph break belonged. **Every other gate passed** — the links resolved, the headings parsed, the graph built, the evidence classes sorted — because none of them reads the prose. It was found by an author reading a page, and the gate gained a check for it afterwards.

**Then the new check failed on this session.** Describing the defect above required naming the sequences, and the first draft of this paragraph reproduced them literally, so the gate flagged the page that documents the gate. The obvious fix — exempt text inside code formatting — **would have reintroduced the original miss**, because one of the three damaged files carried its escape inside a fenced code block. The sequences are therefore named in words here rather than shown. **A detector strict enough to be useful will sometimes fire on a legitimate case, and the right response is to change the writing rather than to weaken the detector.** *A validation suite tells you about the defects somebody has already thought of, and its silence about everything else is not reassurance.*

**Read all six together and one pattern is unmistakable.** In every case the correction was cheap and the *detection* was expensive, and in most of them the thing that detected it was somebody reading a source, or a page, at the depth of its design conditions rather than at the depth of its claims. **That is the argument for a research layer, and it is the clearest instance of one paying for itself available anywhere in this programme.**

### A change binds or it does not, and the difference is a property of where it runs

The pinned agent configuration corpus supplies the vocabulary for the binding question more precisely than prose can ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)).

Its documentation states that **a check can be advisory at one tier while the nightly run treats the same check as authoritative** — the same check, different consequence, depending on where it runs. It states that behaviour conformance is **advisory by default** and lacks established calibration. It states that its regex-graded behavioural checks **test output shape and not correctness**. And it expresses maturity and retirement **through collection membership and metadata** rather than leaving them to be inferred from a file's age.

Four lessons follow directly for an amendment. **Advisory is a legitimate state, stated as one.** **The same text binds or does not depending on the tier it is enforced in, which means the amendment's binding is not a property of the words.** **A check that passes is not a check that verified the thing you care about.** And **retirement of a superseded clause must be an explicit state, or the clause simply persists.**

**Its limits are extreme and travel with every use.** Read at one commit; paths, counts, plugin names and package versions all moved during the reading. **Host and model dependence is total** — every artefact is inert without a host that loads it. **The repository has graders and no committed results at that revision**, and its own prose uses benefit vocabulary anyway. **A single vendor's opinion about agent configuration is not the field.**

### A practice corrects itself only where admitting error is survivable

One mechanism explains why most written practice is handed down and almost none is handed back.

Proposing an amendment on the grounds that the current text produced the wrong outcome is a public statement that you departed from the standard. That is speaking up when silence is cheaper — the construct Edmondson studies and Frazier synthesises ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md); [research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)). Edmondson's list of what people avoid is short and exact: **"Don't want to look ignorant? Don't ask questions. Don't want to look incompetent? Don't admit to mistakes or weaknesses. Don't want to be called disruptive? Don't make suggestions."** An amendment is all three at once.

And the construct **"seems to 'live' at the level of the group"** — so even inside one organisation there are teams where the amendment is routine and teams where it is career-limiting. **A programme that installs the disposition and says nothing about the environment has handed somebody a risk it did not help them assess.** This session does not solve that. It requires you to name who has to agree, which is the smallest useful version of the same question.

**Their limits:** the Edmondson note covers a 2019 trade book read in full and a 1999 paper that was **not read**, with case studies almost entirely secondary; the Frazier note is written from the official abstract of a closed-access meta-analysis. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude from either appears here.**

### The counterweight: reflecting on a practice is not improving it

One caution, because this session is otherwise a straight argument for amendment.

Effortful engagement with a domain is not automatically productive, and it explains least in exactly the domains that look like this one — unstable conditions, delayed feedback, contested criteria ([research/04-professional-formation/macnamara-deliberate-practice-limits.md](/research/04-professional-formation/macnamara-deliberate-practice-limits.md)). **No magnitude from that paper is reproduced here.** The transferable point is that a practice which amends itself frequently has not thereby improved, and that **an amendment rate is an activity measure** — the exact class of measure [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) spent a day separating from outcomes.

**Its limits:** a meta-analysis of correlational studies read from the official abstract; range-restricted samples; its professions estimate is the least stable of the set; a 2018 corrigendum exists and changed nothing substantive. **Direction and domain ordering only.**

The best available model of the behaviour this session teaches is the source that supplies its pedagogy. **Kapur's own account concedes that failure is sufficient but not necessary for the effect he describes, and his reference list contains no adult-professional study for the population he claims the cycle works in.** Those concessions are in the source, made by the author, against his own interest. **That is what an amendable practice looks like at the level of a single document**, and it is the standard your proposal is being read against.

## The workflow

**Write the amendment against this repository, in this repository's own form, because it is the practice you have the best evidence about.**

**Step 1 — find your occasion first, not your amendment.** Go back through what you produced at [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md), [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) and [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md) and find the moment you departed from a stated practice and were right to. **That departure is either an undocumented improvement or an error you have not yet noticed**, and deciding which is the session.

**Step 2 — locate the text.** Name the page and the clause. **An amendment to a practice in general is a complaint.** If you cannot find the clause, the practice does not say what you thought it said, and that is your finding instead.

**Step 3 — check item 4 before you write anything.** Find out why the clause exists. Read the paper that defends it, the decision record that produced it, or the session it belongs to. **A clause that looks broken because you were not present for its reason is a control you are about to remove.**

**Step 4 — write the replacement as text somebody could apply without asking you what you meant.**

**Step 5 — decide the binding, honestly, and put it in the file.**

```yaml
# .practice/amendments/AM-001.yaml   <- written in the practice's OWN change form.
# A proposal written as a memo has not met the mechanism that would reject it.

proposed:
  target:
    page: "/wiki/program/<page>.md"      # or the clause identifier in your own practice
    clause: "<the exact sentence being replaced>"
  occasion:
    what_happened: "<the wrong outcome the current text produced>"
    date: "<YYYY-MM-DD>"
    locatable_at: "<artefact, record or session where a reader can find it>"
    correction_or_preference: correction   # and say below how somebody else could tell
    how_to_tell: "<what a reader could check that distinguishes these two>"
  replacement_text: >
    <text. Applicable without asking the author what they meant.>
  binding:
    mechanism: advisory                    # gate | required-field | check | unreachable-state | advisory
    enforced_where: "<which tier. The SAME check can be advisory here and authoritative there.>"
    if_advisory_prediction: "<how often this advice will be followed, and what would show it>"
    what_it_will_lose_to: "<item 5. Name the gate, checklist or deadline that outranks it.>"
  annotation_at_point_of_change: >
    <the sentence that will sit in the amended text recording what it used to say and why
     it changed. NOT a changelog elsewhere. A superseded position that is invisible
     cannot be evaluated by whoever inherits the correction.>
  who_must_agree: "<named role with authority to reject>"
  what_they_would_say: "<their strongest objection, in their words>"

declined:
  change_considered: "<a change you wanted>"
  reason: >
    <at the SAME length as the proposal. Reasons for declining are shorter than reasons
     for proposing in almost every document, and that asymmetry is where practices
     accumulate change nobody argued against.>

contradictions_found:                       # report, do not resolve by picking a side
  - between: ["<clause A>", "<clause B>"]
    note: "Reported as a finding. Two sections of one standard disagreeing is a
           first-class defect and is more informative than either side of it."
```

**Step 6 — write the declined amendment at the same length as the proposal.** This is the step people skip and it is the one that makes the exercise a choice.

**Step 7 — go find a contradiction and report it without resolving it.** Two clauses in this repository, or in your own practice, that disagree. **Do not pick a side.** Report both with their locations. **Two sections of one standard disagreeing is a first-class defect, and the disagreement is more informative than either side of it.**

**Step 8 — attack your own amendment.** One prompt: *argue that this amendment is a preference wearing a correction's clothes; then argue that the clause it removes exists for a reason the author was not present for; then name the gate my amendment will lose to.* Read it for the reason you were not present for. Keep what is better than your own reasoning and record what you rejected. **Record separately that the agent has no access to the history that produced the clause**, so its answer to the middle question is a hypothesis and not a finding.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** Two things. **The first is the length asymmetry between your proposal and your declined amendment**, which you will feel while typing and which no amount of being told about produces. **The second is what happens at step 3**: a substantial share of learners discover that the clause they were about to amend exists for a reason recorded in a paper they had not read, and discovering that by reading is different from being warned about it.

## What good looks like

- The occasion is specific, dated and locatable. **An amendment justified by a general impression is a preference.**
- The replacement text is text. Somebody could apply it without asking you what you meant.
- **The binding mechanism is named honestly.** Advisory is a legitimate answer, stated as one, and it comes with a prediction about how often the advice will be followed and what it will lose to.
- **The annotation sits at the point of change**, and it records what the text used to say. A correction whose predecessor is invisible cannot be evaluated by whoever inherits it.
- **The amendment survives its author.** It does not require you to be present, does not encode your team's habits as universal, and does not describe your engagement's circumstances as though they were everybody's.
- The declined amendment is one you wanted, at the same length as the proposal. **Declining something you did not want is bookkeeping.**
- Where the practice contradicts itself, the contradiction is reported as a finding rather than resolved by picking a side.

## How it goes wrong

- **The preference in a correction's clothes** — the amendment makes the standard match what you would have preferred to do. *Warning sign:* your occasion is that the current text was annoying.
- **The advisory amendment** — a sentence of good sense added to a document of gates, changing nothing. *Warning sign:* your text begins with *teams should consider*.
- **Amendment as complaint** — the change describes what somebody else did wrong. *Warning sign:* your proposal would be read as being about a person.
- **The standard misread as broken** — the clause exists for a reason you were not present for, and your amendment removes a control. *Warning sign:* you could not answer item 4.
- **The genre change** — the amendment replaces a duty with a pointer to where the duty is described. *Warning sign:* your text is shorter and refers somewhere else. **A document that migrates from stating obligations to routing readers to them gains currency and loses self-containment, and a team reading only the current version learns process rather than duty.**
- **The silent correction** — the change is made and the superseded text is deleted, so the next reader inherits a position with no way to evaluate it. *Warning sign:* your amendment reads as though the clause had always said this.
- **Amendment rate as improvement** — the practice changes often and nobody can say whether it is better. *Warning sign:* somebody can tell you how many amendments were accepted this quarter. **That is an activity measure and [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) spent a day on it.**

## Exercises

**None of these is assessed.** The proposed amendment, the declined amendment and the contradiction report are carried to [S090](/wiki/seminars/S090.md), and the amendment crosses into [Q1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md) as the one thing this stage hands to the next engagement's practice.

**1 · Sort five changes into correction, preference, control-removal and genre change.**

- *Replace "review annually" with a named observable event and an observer.* · *Shorten the eight-phase day because it is long.* · *Remove the requirement that a whitepaper name an instrument for each prediction, since none is ever measured.* · *Replace the four evidence classes with a link to where they are defined.* · *Add "teams should consider recording provenance" to the reuse page.*

> **Model answers.** The first is a **correction** — it names an occasion class where the current text produces the wrong outcome, and the replacement is checkable. The second is a **preference** until somebody produces the occasion; length is not an outcome. The third is a **control removal** dressed as realism: the instrument requirement is what makes a prediction falsifiable, and removing it because nothing is measured removes the only thing that would let anybody notice. The fourth is a **genre change** — an obligation replaced by a pointer, gaining currency and losing self-containment. The fifth is an **advisory amendment** and it will lose to every gate on the page. **The general rule: a change is a correction only if somebody could name the occasion on which the current text produced the wrong thing.**

**2 · Find a contradiction in this repository and report it without resolving it.** Two clauses, two locations, no side taken.

> **What you should find.** They exist and they are findable. **Report the pair and the locations, and stop.** If you resolved it, you have exercised authority you were not given and you have destroyed the more useful artefact, which is the evidence that a standard maintained by several people over time disagrees with itself. **Two sections of one standard disagreeing is a first-class defect**, and the same finding holds for the two external corpora this session rests on.

**3 · Go back to your own item 5, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 5 — what your amendment will be ignored in favour of. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then write the honest version.

> **Criterion.** A pass names a specific competing mechanism, says why it outranks your amendment, and states what would have to change for the ranking to reverse. *"It will be ignored because people are busy"* fails — it names no mechanism and offers no remedy. *"It loses to the release checklist, because the checklist is a gate and my text is advice; it stops losing on the day it becomes a required field on the same form"* passes. **If you wrote nothing for item 5, keep the blank: an amendment whose author cannot name what it loses to has not thought about whether it will take effect.**
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is. **This session is a good place to notice that the source of that instruction is also the source the programme's own opening session was caught violating.**

**4 · Classify one check on the platform as advisory or authoritative, and find where that changes.** Using the pinned agent configuration corpus, find a check whose consequence differs by the tier it runs in.

> **What you should find.** That the corpus documents this about itself — a check can be advisory at one tier while the nightly run treats it as authoritative — and that **a green summary looks identical either way.** The transferable point is that your amendment's binding is not a property of its words. **If the corpus has changed at the revision you read, record the pin and the date; every count in this exercise is a count at a revision.**

**5 · Write the annotation, then delete it and read the clause again.** Write the sentence recording what the text used to say. Then remove it and read the amended clause as somebody arriving fresh.

> **What to notice.** That the clause reads better without the annotation and is worth less. **The annotation is the cost of being evaluable**, and every practice that has tidied its history has bought readability with the reader's ability to judge the correction. This repository states that trade-off explicitly and takes the other side.

**6 · Declining, timed.** Take the change you most want to make and spend as long writing the reason to decline it as you spent proposing it.

> **Scoring criterion.** A pass has a decline paragraph of comparable length to the proposal, giving a reason that is about the practice rather than about effort. *"Not worth the trouble"* fails. *"It would bind, and it would bind in the one place where a team that does not yet know why the clause exists would be blocked with no route to find out"* passes. **Reasons for declining are shorter than reasons for proposing in almost every document, and that asymmetry is exactly where practices accumulate change nobody argued against.**

## What this does not establish

**That practices improve when the people who use them may amend them.** The amendment right is verified — it is stated as an obligation in a published handbook, and its route is a public change request against a public repository. **Whether exercising it makes the practice better is not reported by anybody.** No source behind this programme measures it.

**What is observable is weaker and more interesting: the right is exercised unevenly and the unevenness is invisible.** Some parts of that handbook are actively revised and some have not been touched in years, with nothing in its navigation distinguishing them; about half is substantive and about half is recipe, stub or link list, with no signal marking which; and it contradicts itself in at least a dozen places without noticing. **That is the normal condition of a multi-author document maintained over years, and it is far more instructive to show than to hide.**

**The internal corpus has no amendment mechanism at all**, and its own strata disagree with each other in at least as many places. Nothing reconciles them. **This session is written against that**, on the argument that a standard nobody may amend is a standard nobody owns — which is an argument and not a finding.

**And the worked example is this repository, which is not evidence either.** Its self-correction record is real, it is public, and **nothing establishes that keeping it produced a better programme than tidying it would have.** The record shows that corrections were made and annotated. It does not show that the annotations were read, that the corrected design works, or that a reader ever used a superseded position to evaluate the change that replaced it. **The prediction register in every paper here names instruments for exactly these questions and not one of them has been resolved, because no cohort has run.** A practice that documents its own corrections and never measures whether documenting them helps is itself an amendable practice, and **this session is the place to say so.**
