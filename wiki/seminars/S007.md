## The situation

At [S004](/wiki/seminars/S004.md) you read the people who were invited. This session is about the ones who were not, and about the specific question that almost no discovery instrument asks: **who is worse off if this succeeds?**

Some absences are accidents of scheduling. Some are a decision. The two look identical on an attendee list and they are not the same object.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. List everyone whose work changes if this succeeds. Now mark which of them were in the room.
2. Who can stop this after it is built, and were they consulted before it was?
3. Who loses time, status, headcount or discretion if it works?
4. Who owns the data you will need, and has anybody asked them?
5. Of the absent parties, whose objection would be strongest? Write it at its strongest.

**Item 3 is asked in almost no discovery instrument in professional use**, including the one behind this programme — which is notable, because the same body of material makes a claim about headcount in its commercial section.

## What you must produce

An **absence map**: the affected-but-absent, what each would say if asked, and one specific ask that would bring the most consequential of them into the conversation.

## What good looks like

- At least one listed party bears a cost and holds no purchasing power. **Those are the ones a map assembled from the sponsor's contacts will never contain.**
- The strongest objection is written in that party's terms, and it is one you cannot immediately answer.
- Each party who could stop the work later is marked, with the stage at which they would do it.
- The ask is specific — a named person, a time, and what you want from them — and it has a possible answer other than yes.

## How it goes wrong

Four failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **The buyer's map** — only people with budget or authority appear, because those are the people the sponsor introduced you to. *Warning sign:* nobody on your map is worse off if the work succeeds.
- **Charitable ventriloquism** — you state each absent objection in the form you are best placed to answer. *Warning sign:* every objection on your page has a rebuttal beneath it.
- **The consulted formality** — the absent party is to be informed rather than asked. *Warning sign:* your ask has no possible answer other than agreement.
- **Deferred consultation** — the data owner, the security reviewer or the operator is scheduled after the design is settled. *Warning sign:* the phrase "we will bring them in once we know what we are building".

## Do it

Work from [Brief A](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md). The external assessor has never been in a conversation with the operator's technology function. The engineers who write the notes have not been asked why the notes are as they are. The back-office team's size has been raised twice this year in meetings none of them attended.

Map them. Then write the ask for the one whose absence most affects whether the work should happen at all, and hand it to somebody who plays that person and is briefed to decline once before considering it.

Method is yours. **Nobody will tell you what makes an ask answerable**, and choosing wrongly is the cheapest lesson available here.

## Show it

Read the strongest objection aloud, and have somebody else — not its author — read it back to the room.

If it sounds like something a person would actually say, in the words they would use, it may be honest. If it sounds like a position you had already defeated, you wrote a rebuttal and put quotation marks round it.

## What this does not establish

Nothing here shows that consulting absent parties improves an engagement, and the corpus behind this programme is silent on the question in a specific and diagnosable way: it has no data block at all, no question about who may see the data or whether anyone consented, and no question asking who loses if the work succeeds.

**That silence is an absence in a source, not a finding about practice**, and this session is built on it. The techniques for identifying affected parties come from a research literature with better standing than the stage that contains them, and even that literature establishes mechanisms rather than outcomes.
