## The situation

The request in [Brief A](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md) is specific. It names a technology, a document, a deadline and an outcome. Everybody in the room can picture it.

Nothing in it names a difficulty that anybody has.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. Write the request out verbatim. Underline every word that names a technology or a product.
2. What difficulty would remain if none of the underlined things existed?
3. When did that difficulty last actually occur? Who was present?
4. What is the current workaround, and who performs it?
5. What answer to item 3 would make you stop investigating this problem category altogether?

**Item 5 is the one people leave blank.** Once a problem category has been accepted, almost anybody will help you rank details inside it, whether or not the category matters to them.

## What you must produce

A **problem restatement with its evidence**: the difficulty stated in a sentence containing no technology, plus a datable last occurrence, the current workaround, and the name of the person who performs it.

## What good looks like

- The restatement contains no product, vendor or feature name, and would still be legible to somebody who had never heard of any of them.
- There is a specific occurrence with a date attached, **not a pattern described in the present tense.**
- The workaround is named and so is its performer, because a difficulty nobody has worked around is usually a difficulty nobody has.
- Your stated stopping condition is something an informant could actually say.

## How it goes wrong

Four failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **Premature zoom** — you begin ranking options inside a category before establishing that the category is consequential to anyone. *Warning sign:* your first three questions all accept the request's framing without testing it.
- **The obliging witness** — you ask whether your approach would help, and are told that it would. *Warning sign:* nothing anybody said surprised you.
- **Requirement by transcription** — the request is copied into a backlog item with the verbs changed. *Warning sign:* your restatement and the original request share their nouns.
- **The unanchored anecdote** — a difficulty described entirely in generalities. *Warning sign:* the account uses "usually" and "typically" and never a date.

## Do it

Work from [Brief A](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md). Write your restatement first and commit it before opening an agent.

Then ask an agent to produce its own restatement of the same request, and attack it: which of its sentences could survive with the technology removed, which nouns did it carry over unchanged, and what occurrence does it cite? Record what you asked, what you kept and what you rejected.

**An agent restating a request in fluent language has performed a translation, not an investigation**, and the point of the exercise is that the two are hard to tell apart on the page.

## Show it

Hand your restatement to a reader who strikes out every technology noun they find. Whatever survives is your actual problem statement.

If nothing survives, you have written the request again in a different register, which is the commonest outcome and is worth discovering here.

## What this does not establish

The techniques in this session — asking for the last occurrence, refusing to zoom before the category is established, treating a feature request as a symptom — have a practitioner literature behind them and no controlled evidence. Their direction is plausible and their magnitude is unknown.

The corpus of professional practice behind this programme goes further in the wrong direction: **its own discovery question bank asks the customer, in every section, to endorse the interviewer's solution category**, and ships specimen answers alongside the questions. Neither is an occasional slip; both are the template. This session teaches against its primary source on this point, and does so on the strength of an argument rather than a finding.
