# S065 · An Operational Readiness Argument ⊘

**Module:** [M13](/wiki/modules/M13.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** The full operate stack — gateway enforcement, capacity commitment, evaluation on stored responses, and posture management — assembled into an argument rather than a checklist
**Whitepaper:** [WP-065](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-065.md)
**Retrieves from:** S055, S061, S062, S063, S064
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

This is the stage's first assessment occasion. It marks one thing.

**Could somebody who did not build this system establish its state without asking you?**

Not whether the system is reliable. Not whether it has been running well. **Whether every claim about it is re-derivable by a person who does not trust you**, and whether the places that fails are written down.

Answer these in writing before you begin, and submit them.

1. Which claim about your running system rests on somebody's word rather than on evidence?
2. What is on your invisible-fix register, and what is the most serious thing on it?
3. Under what conditions should this system not be operated?
4. What did you stop collecting, and what did you stop alerting on?
5. If you disappeared this evening, what would the operator not be able to find out?

**Item 3 is the one candidates skip.** A readiness argument with no conditions attached is a statement that the system is always ready, which nobody believes and nobody wrote down.

## What you must produce

**An argument that the system can be operated by people who did not build it**, assembling four artefacts and adding one.

- **The observability specification** from [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md), held as code, with its question and asker columns filled, its deletion list attached, and its instrument-resolution and privacy lines present.
- **The closure procedure and invisible-fix register** from [S062](/wiki/seminars/S062.md), with its schedule, its named owner, and the precision line on any closure that rests on a measurement.
- **The service level, pruned alert set, silence register and unconsumed-signal register** from [S063](/wiki/seminars/S063.md).
- **The cost model** from [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md), with its token path, its capacity posture and the customer-side person who ran it, named and dated.
- **The readiness argument itself**, which is new: what readiness means for this system, what evidence supports it, and the conditions under which the system should not be operated.

## The material

### An argument is not a checklist, and the difference is where the reader's work goes

A checklist distributes the reasoning across whoever wrote it. **An argument carries the reasoning in the artefact**, so a reader who disagrees can locate the step they disagree with. That is the whole distinction this occasion assesses, and it is why the four artefacts are not marked for completeness but for whether they support a claim.

The four together are an operate stack rather than four exercises. **Gateway enforcement** decides what traffic is permitted and what is attributable. **Capacity commitment** decides what the system can serve and what it costs whether or not it serves it. **Evaluation on stored responses** decides what the system's recent behaviour licenses you to say. **Posture management** decides what you can see about the estate the system sits in. Each answers a different question, and the readiness argument is the place their answers are put beside one another and reconciled.

### Why this occasion assesses an activity rather than an outcome

The assessment frame this programme uses is entrustment: **the unit of assessment is a bounded piece of professional practice that a supervisor could hand over**, described as an activity with a beginning and an end rather than as a competency or a learning objective ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). The framework's own description template is instructive here: the title carries **no adjective**, because an adjective smuggles a quality standard into the name of the activity; the specification carries limitations, so the activity does not silently expand; and entrustment is expressed on an ordinal **supervision scale** rather than as a score.

Two of its details shape this occasion directly. **Summative entrustment requires agreement between multiple observers and multiple observations**, so anything decided here on one reading by one assessor is explicitly *ad hoc* rather than summative — and this occasion is ad hoc. And the framework supplies the **entrustment-based discussion**: a short conversation after the activity that probes understanding and **must include what-if questions**, because retrospective explanation shows only that the candidate understood the case they met, while counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about cases they did not meet. **That is precisely why the defence asks what should stop the system running rather than what the system does.**

**Its limits:** a conceptual and practical primer by the concept's originator, synthesising a literature rather than evaluating it; its empirical items are reported from prior studies rather than generated; and the whole apparatus is drawn from clinical education, so its transfer to engineering practice is an extrapolation this programme makes and does not test.

### Why the assessor writes a reason rather than a mark

The assessment literature on how judgements are actually formed supplies the reason the specification demands narrative. Assessors reading a portfolio **relied mainly on narrative feedback to inform their judgement**, with grades and qualifications **used merely to confirm impressions already formed from the narratives** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). The number was not the evidence; it was a check on a conclusion reached from prose.

The same work records two findings that make this occasion's design uncomfortable and honest. **Assessors reached similar pass-or-fail judgements while differing in their information-processing approaches and their reasoning** — so agreement on the outcome conceals disagreement about why. And **assessors were inclined to stick to their initial judgements even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence**; later reading produced more elaborate versions of the same conclusion rather than revised ones. The practical recommendations follow directly and this occasion adopts both: **high-stakes decisions should come from group discussion rather than from an individual**, and assessors should **thoroughly substantiate their judgements**, because substantiation is what makes divergent reasoning visible and therefore discussable.

**Its limits:** two open-access studies from one research group, in clinical education, with small assessor samples and mock portfolios; several of the most-cited works by the same authors are paywalled and **were not consulted**; and **no magnitude from any of it is reproduced here.**

### Why the argument must state what it does not cover

The production-monitoring literature supplies the operating constraint that makes an unconditioned readiness claim indefensible. **You can only control a system to the extent that you can observe it**, operationalised as **alertability** and **actionability** ([research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md)). A readiness argument therefore has a ceiling set by the estate's observability, and a candidate whose argument exceeds that ceiling is claiming control they do not have.

The same source names the two sensitivities that make the ceiling lower than it looks. **Entanglement**: changing one thing changes everything, so an upstream difference resurfaces somewhere unrelated. **Configuration**: versions, parameters and feature lists live in configuration, where a small error produces radically different behaviour that ordinary software tests will not catch. **Both mean that the conditions under which a system should not be operated are frequently not in the region the system was designed for**, which is why item 3 is hard and why blank answers to it are the commonest defect at this occasion. **Its limits:** practitioner guidance from a commercial publisher, not research; single-vendor default toolchain; some foundational claims rest on anonymous practitioner testimony; **every magnitude in it is a property of a particular system and none appears here.**

### Why no measurement in the argument is marked for its value

One further discipline governs every number a candidate brings to this occasion. **An evaluation is an experiment**, and a result reported without its uncertainty licenses conclusions it cannot support ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). The apparatus — standard errors beside means, item counts, cluster counts where observations arrive in related groups, and power analysis computed in advance to establish what the instrument could have detected — is what turns a reassuring absence into a statement with a stated strength.

**So the specification marks whether a measurement carries its precision, and never whether the measurement is good.** The reason is stated plainly in the assessed elements below: nothing available to this programme could check a figure in a candidate's cost model or evaluation, and an element that marked one would be the assessor's opinion wearing a specification's clothing. **Its limits:** an analytical paper rather than an empirical one; its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions and **none is reproduced here**; and statistical validity says nothing about whether the thing measured was worth measuring.

### The platform layer, assembled

The four platform surfaces this stage has used are load-bearing in the argument and each carries its own silence.

**Gateway enforcement** supplies per-consumer accounting, attribution through metric dimensions, content moderation at a single boundary, and failure isolation whose recovery timing is taken from the backend's own header rather than guessed ([research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/apim-ai-gateway-docs.md)). **It publishes no performance or cost data of any kind, and does not discuss the privacy surface its own logging recommendation creates.**

**Capacity commitment** supplies the distinction between permission, availability and price, and the documented order — deploy, verify, then commit ([research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/provisioned-throughput-for-foundry-models.md)). **It is definitional rather than empirical, and every concrete number lives on companion pages that were not fetched.**

**Evaluation on stored responses** supplies closure on behaviour that actually occurred rather than on a staged demonstration, together with the warning that the split strategy determines what a score means ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). **No agreement rate with human judgement, no calibration data and no threshold rationale is published for any evaluator — assert none.**

**Posture management** supplies estate-level visibility with its scan scope, cadence and latency stated ([research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md)). **No detection rate, no false-positive rate and no coverage measurement of any kind is published, one consulted page is explicitly superseded, and agent-level capabilities move to a separate licence on a stated date.**

**Assembled, those four silences are the readiness argument's real boundary**, and a candidate who states them has produced an argument. A candidate who does not has produced a checklist with better formatting.

## The workflow

Assemble the four artefacts, then write the argument, in this order.

**Step 1 — state what readiness means for this system.** Not in general. For this one, with this customer, with these operators. **A definition borrowed from elsewhere is the first thing an assessor will test**, because it will not survive contact with the specifics.

**Step 2 — walk every claim and ask where the evidence is.** Anything resting on your word goes in one of two places: it becomes a citation, or it goes on the invisible-fix register. There is no third place, and **the register is a boundary rather than an excuse** — an item that could have been made visible and was not is a weaker entry than one that genuinely could not.

**Step 3 — attach each platform surface's silence to the claim that rests on it.** Not in a footnote. In the same sentence.

```yaml
# readiness/argument.yaml   <- SHAPE, NOT SCHEMA. Verify any product keys separately.
readiness_means_here: >
  Two named operators outside the build team can establish the system's state,
  detect a breach of the agreed floor, and stop the system, without contacting us.

claims:
  - claim: "Consumption is attributable per consuming team."
    rests_on: "gateway token metrics with a consumer dimension"
    evidence: "policy at <path>, deployed <date>; dashboard query at <path>"
    silence: "the documentation publishes no performance or cost data; the mechanism is stated, not measured"

  - claim: "The scope-violation failure class has not recurred since the fix."
    rests_on: "evaluation over stored responses, window <dates>"
    evidence: "closure record at <path>"
    precision: "the evaluation could have detected a difference of at least <computed>; below that it is silent"
    silence: "no agreement rate with human judgement is published for this evaluator, and we have not established one"

  - claim: "No data-exposure incident occurred in the period."
    status: NOT_MADE            # <- the permitted form replaces it
    permitted_form: >
      No incident of a class our instruments can detect was detected, at a scan scope
      covering part of the estate, on a weekly cadence, with multi-day latency.
      NO DETECTION RATE OR FALSE-POSITIVE RATE IS PUBLISHED FOR THIS TOOLING.

capacity_posture:
  commitment_sequence: "deploy -> verify capacity -> commit"
  evidence_capacity_confirmed: "<deployment id + date>"
  note: "quota is permission; capacity is availability; a reservation guarantees neither"

do_not_operate_when:
  - condition: "<a dependency is unavailable>"
    why: "<what breaks, and what the operator sees>"
  - condition: "<a data condition our evaluation never covered appears>"
  - condition: "<volume exceeds what the cost model was refined against>"
  - condition: "<a control on the invisible-fix register cannot be confirmed>"

exclusions:
  covered_nowhere: "<carried forward from S055, not restated>"
  first_day_reading: "<which exclusions an operator needs on day one>"

if_i_disappeared:
  operator_cannot_find_out: "<verbatim, and it is not empty>"
```

**Step 4 — write the conditions under which the system should not be operated.** A dependency unavailable. A data condition your evaluation never covered. A volume beyond what the cost model was refined against. A control on the register whose status could not be established. **Each of these is a real thing and each of them will happen.**

**Step 5 — write the exclusions.** What this argument does not cover. Carry forward the unmitigated list from [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md) rather than restating it, and say which of its items an operator needs to know about on their first day.

**Step 6 — use the agent to attack the argument, and record what you refused.** One prompt: *for each claim, argue that its evidence is an assertion with a citation attached, and name the observation that would settle it.* A second: *find the condition under which this system should not be operated that I have not listed.* **Keep what beats your own reasoning and record what you rejected**, because a defence in which you conceded everything the agent said is a defence of the agent's argument rather than yours.

**Step 7 — check it against the person, not the document.** Give it to somebody who did not build the system and let them look for the place they would have to phone you. **That place is the argument's real boundary**, and finding it before the assessor does is the last useful thing you can do.

## What good looks like

These are the assessed elements. Each is checked as present and adequate, or not.

- **Every retained signal names the question it answers and the person who asks it**, and anything collected without both is on a dated review list with a recorded decision.
- **The observability configuration is in source control and arrived through review.** A configuration that exists only in a console fails this element regardless of quality.
- **The closure procedure requires a file and a location, and states its default for the ambiguous case in its own words.** The default must be unclear rather than implemented.
- **Any closure resting on a measurement carries a precision line** stating what the measurement could not have detected. An absence reported without one fails this element.
- **The invisible-fix register is present and non-empty**, and at least one entry is a fix inside the candidate's own control rather than outside it.
- **The re-derivation has a named owner and a trigger that is a state change** rather than a person's intention.
- **The service level is stated as the minimum quality acceptable to the customer**, with an explicit sentence saying it is not what the team intends to deliver.
- **Every alert names its expected action and expected actor**, and every deletion is recorded with what will now be missed.
- **No claim of absolute absence appears anywhere in the argument.** Where an absence is reported, the permitted form names the class, the instrument and the resolution.
- **The cost model is expressed per unit of the customer's work, carries a token path with the decision that set each term, carries observability as a line, and names a customer-side person who has run it**, with a date.
- **Where capacity is committed, the argument carries evidence that capacity was confirmed before the commitment**, in the documented order.
- **The readiness argument names the conditions under which the system should not be operated**, and names what the argument does not cover.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Whether the system is actually reliable is not marked. Whether an incident occurred during the period is not marked. **The number of signals is not marked and more is not better; the number of alerts is not marked and fewer is not automatically better either.** Whether any figure in the cost model or in any evaluation is correct is not marked, because **nothing available to this programme could check one.** Whether the readiness argument concludes that the system is ready is not marked, and **an argument concluding not ready, with named conditions, passes ahead of one concluding ready without them.** Tool choice is not marked. Platform choice is not marked. Presentation is not marked. Whether the invisible-fix register is complete is not marked — only that it exists, is non-empty, and contains something the candidate owns.

## How it goes wrong

- **The demonstration** — readiness argued from a working system in front of you. *Warning sign:* your argument requires you to be present.
- **The checklist in argument clothing** — four artefacts assembled and nothing reconciled between them. *Warning sign:* no sentence in your document connects a claim in one artefact to a limit in another.
- **The unconditioned readiness** — no circumstance is named under which the system should not run. *Warning sign:* item 3 is blank or generic.
- **The absence read as safety** — a clean period reported as a property of the system. *Warning sign:* your argument contains a sentence asserting that nothing happened, with no instrument and no resolution beside it.
- **The inherited silence** — a platform's published limits repeated as your own coverage. *Warning sign:* you can quote a scan cadence and cannot say what it misses.
- **The convenient register** — every invisible fix is somebody else's platform, tenant or contract. *Warning sign:* nothing in it is your responsibility.
- **The console configuration** — the observability is excellent and lives where nobody can review it. *Warning sign:* your evidence is a screenshot.
- **The intentional trigger** — the re-derivation is scheduled "each sprint" with no actor and no state change. *Warning sign:* the owner is the team.
- **The aspirational floor** — the service level is what you hope to deliver, and the explicit sentence is missing. *Warning sign:* the customer has not seen it.
- **The commitment without the confirmation** — capacity committed before a deployment established that it existed. *Warning sign:* your procurement date precedes your first successful deployment.
- **The model that only you can run** — nobody on the customer's side has opened it. *Warning sign:* your date field is a plan.
- **The complete argument** — nothing is outside it. *Warning sign:* your exclusions list is empty and you are pleased about it.

## Exercises

**This occasion is assessed, and these are part of it.** The written answers to the opening items are submitted with the artefacts, and exercises 1 to 4 are performed in the defence. Exercise 5 is preparation and is not marked.

**1 · Defend the argument, not the system.** The assessor has your service level and nothing else. They will ask three things: **what rests on your word, what should stop this system running, and what did you delete.**

> **Scoring criterion.** A pass answers all three from the artefact rather than from memory, and answers the second with conditions that are observable rather than dispositional — *a dependency is unavailable* passes, *if things get difficult* does not. The frame is entrustment: the assessor is judging **an activity with a beginning and an end that could be handed over**, not the candidate's confidence. **Anything decided here is explicitly ad hoc rather than summative**, because summative entrustment requires multiple observers and multiple observations and this occasion has one of each.

**2 · Answer the what-if.** The assessor will pose a situation your artefacts do not cover and ask what you would do.

> **What is being tested.** Not recall. **A retrospective walkthrough shows only that you understood the case you met**; counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases you did not. That is why the entrustment literature makes what-if questions a required component of the discussion rather than an optional one, and why an entrustment decision is described as an acceptance of risk — it can never be fully predicted what the candidate will encounter. **A pass names what it would need to find out and where it would look.** A fail improvises a confident answer.

**3 · Decline the waiver.** The assessor will offer to waive the invisible-fix register. They will say it reads like a list of things nobody can do anything about, that it adds nothing to a readiness argument, and that they are content to assess without it.

> **Accepting is the failure this occasion exists to catch.** The register is the honest boundary of the closure procedure, and a procedure whose boundary has been waived reads as complete. **The passing answer is to decline, and to say what the register is load-bearing for** — which is that the procedure is silent about exactly the fixes it cannot see, and **silence and absence are indistinguishable in its output.** A candidate who declines and cannot say why has guessed correctly; a candidate who accepts has told the assessor that their boundary was decoration.

**4 · Go back to your own item 3, then defend the difference.** Find what you wrote for opening item 3 before you assembled anything. **Do not revise it now.** Put it beside the conditions in your finished argument and name the difference aloud, in one sentence.

> **Criterion.** A pass names a specific condition that is in the finished argument and was not in the opening answer, and says what produced it — a platform limit, an evaluation's precision, a register entry, an exclusion carried forward. A fail says the answer improved. **The comparison is the exercise**, not the improvement.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this programme's teaching pattern rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). **Reading your finished argument is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you first wrote is.**

**5 · Find the place they would phone you.** Before the defence, give the argument to somebody who did not build the system and ask them to find the one thing they could not establish without you.

> **Not marked.** What they find is the argument's real boundary, and the useful move is to put it in the argument rather than to fix it. **A boundary you named is a boundary; a boundary the assessor finds is a gap.** Note also that a single reader's judgement is a weak instrument: assessors reading the same evidence reach similar verdicts by different reasoning, and tend to hold an initial judgement even against disconfirming evidence — which is why the recommendation in that literature is group discussion for anything consequential, and why this occasion asks the assessor to write a reason rather than a mark.

## What this does not establish

**This occasion shows that a system's state is discoverable. It does not show that the system works, that it is reliable, or that anybody will operate it the way the argument assumes.**

**No outcome evidence exists for any of the four artefacts.** The closure mechanism has never been compared with a person ticking a box. The observability rules are published as a working standard with no comparative evidence. The service-level definition sits on one of the oldest pages of its source site. The cost-model transfer requirement is assembled from a single reflective sentence in material that contains no exit procedure at all.

**Every platform surface in the stack is definitional rather than empirical, and each is silent where it matters most.** The gateway documentation publishes **no performance or cost figure of any kind** and does not discuss the privacy surface its own logging recommendation creates. The capacity documentation is authoritative about how one vendor's capacity model behaves and **makes no claim that could be independently checked**, with every concrete number on companion pages that were not fetched. The evaluation documentation publishes **no agreement rate with human judgement, no calibration data and no threshold rationale for any evaluator.** The posture documentation publishes **no detection rate, no false-positive rate and no coverage measurement of any kind**, one consulted page is explicitly superseded, and agent-level capabilities move to a separate licence on a stated date. **Assembled, those four silences are the boundary of anything this occasion can license.**

**The invisible-fix register is this design's invention** and has never been used by anybody. Its underlying observation is verified — a real fix that leaves no trace in the repository never closes and regenerates indefinitely — but **turning that into a required, marked artefact is an argument, not a finding**, and the element requiring one entry to be within the candidate's own control is a guess about where the register would otherwise degenerate.

**The specification cannot tell whether the register is honest.** A candidate who omits their most serious blind spot passes this element, and nothing here would detect it. That gap is real, it is named in [WP-065](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-065.md) §8, and the design accepts it rather than filling it with a judgement nothing supports.

**The assessment frame is borrowed from clinical education and its transfer is untested.** The entrustment apparatus — bounded activities, supervision levels, ad-hoc versus summative decisions, the what-if discussion — comes from a primer by the concept's originator that synthesises a literature rather than evaluating it, and whose empirical items are reported from prior studies. **Nothing establishes that it works for engineering practice**, and this programme's use of it is an extrapolation.

**And the assessor is a weak instrument, by the evidence of the literature this occasion cites.** Assessors reading the same portfolio reach similar verdicts by materially different reasoning, and tend to retain an initial judgement even when later evidence points elsewhere. **Group discussion is the recommended remedy for consequential decisions and this occasion has one assessor**, which is why anything decided here is ad hoc rather than summative and why the assessor writes a reason rather than a mark. Those studies are small, drawn from clinical education, and several of the most-cited related works **were not consulted** because they are paywalled.

**One claim this occasion makes without hedging is definitional:** an argument a stranger cannot follow is a demonstration, and a demonstration requires the demonstrator. Everything else here is a discipline rather than a finding.
