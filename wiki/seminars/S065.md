# S065 · An Operational Readiness Argument ⊘

**Module:** [M13](/wiki/modules/M13.md) · **Quarter:** [Q7](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-065](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-065.md)
**Retrieves from:** S055, S061, S062, S063, S064
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

This is the stage's first assessment occasion. It marks one thing.

**Could somebody who did not build this system establish its state without asking you?**

Not whether the system is reliable. Not whether it has been running well. **Whether every claim about it is re-derivable by a person who does not trust you**, and whether the places that fails are written down.

Answer these in writing before you begin, and submit them.

1. Which claim about your running system rests on somebody's word rather than on evidence?
2. What is on your invisible-fix register, and what is the most serious thing on it?
3. Under what conditions should this system not be operated?
4. What did you stop collecting, and what did you stop alerting on?
5. If you disappeared this evening, what would the operator not be able to find out?

**Item 3 is the one candidates skip.** A readiness argument with no conditions attached is a statement that the system is always ready, which nobody believes and nobody wrote down.

## What you must produce

**An argument that the system can be operated by people who did not build it**, assembling four artefacts and adding one.

- **The observability specification** from [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md), held as code, with its question and asker columns filled and its deletion list attached.
- **The closure procedure and invisible-fix register** from [S062](/wiki/seminars/S062.md), with its schedule and named owner.
- **The service level, pruned alert set and unconsumed-signal register** from [S063](/wiki/seminars/S063.md).
- **The cost model** from [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md), with the customer-side person who ran it named and dated.
- **The readiness argument itself**, which is new: what readiness means for this system, what evidence supports it, and the conditions under which the system should not be operated.

## What good looks like

These are the assessed elements. Each is checked as present and adequate, or not.

- **Every retained signal names the question it answers and the person who asks it**, and anything collected without both is on a dated review list with a recorded decision.
- **The observability configuration is in source control and arrived through review.** A configuration that exists only in a console fails this element regardless of quality.
- **The closure procedure requires a file and a location, and states its default for the ambiguous case in its own words.** The default must be unclear rather than implemented.
- **The invisible-fix register is present and non-empty**, and at least one entry is a fix inside the candidate's own control rather than outside it.
- **The re-derivation has a named owner and a trigger that is a state change** rather than a person's intention.
- **The service level is stated as the minimum quality acceptable to the customer**, with an explicit sentence saying it is not what the team intends to deliver.
- **Every alert names its expected action and expected actor**, and every deletion is recorded with what will now be missed.
- **The cost model is expressed per unit of the customer's work, carries observability as a line, and names a customer-side person who has run it**, with a date.
- **The readiness argument names the conditions under which the system should not be operated**, and names what the argument does not cover.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Whether the system is actually reliable is not marked. Whether an incident occurred during the period is not marked. **The number of signals is not marked and more is not better; the number of alerts is not marked and fewer is not automatically better either.** Whether any figure in the cost model is correct is not marked, because **nothing available to this programme could check one.** Whether the readiness argument concludes that the system is ready is not marked, and **an argument concluding not ready, with named conditions, passes ahead of one concluding ready without them.** Tool choice is not marked. Presentation is not marked. Whether the invisible-fix register is complete is not marked — only that it exists, is non-empty, and contains something the candidate owns.

## How it goes wrong

- **The demonstration** — readiness argued from a working system in front of you. *Warning sign:* your argument requires you to be present.
- **The unconditioned readiness** — no circumstance is named under which the system should not run. *Warning sign:* item 3 is blank or generic.
- **The convenient register** — every invisible fix is somebody else's platform, somebody else's tenant, somebody else's contract. *Warning sign:* nothing in it is your responsibility.
- **The console configuration** — the observability is excellent and lives where nobody can review it. *Warning sign:* your evidence is a screenshot.
- **The intentional trigger** — the re-derivation is scheduled "each sprint" with no actor and no state change. *Warning sign:* the owner is the team.
- **The aspirational floor** — the service level is what you hope to deliver, and the explicit sentence is missing. *Warning sign:* the customer has not seen it.
- **The model that only you can run** — nobody on the customer's side has opened it. *Warning sign:* your date field is a plan.
- **The complete argument** — nothing is outside it. *Warning sign:* your exclusions list is empty and you are pleased about it.

## Do it

Assemble the four artefacts, then write the argument, in this order.

**First, state what readiness means for this system.** Not in general. For this one, with this customer, with these operators. **A definition borrowed from elsewhere is the first thing an assessor will test**, because it will not survive contact with the specifics.

**Second, walk every claim and ask where the evidence is.** Anything resting on your word goes in one of two places: it becomes a citation, or it goes on the invisible-fix register. There is no third place, and **the register is a boundary rather than an excuse** — an item that could have been made visible and was not is a weaker entry than one that genuinely could not.

**Third, write the conditions under which the system should not be operated.** A dependency unavailable. A data condition your evaluation never covered. A volume beyond what the cost model was refined against. A control on the register whose status could not be established. **Each of these is a real thing and each of them will happen.**

**Fourth, write the exclusions.** What this argument does not cover. Carry forward the unmitigated list from [S055](/wiki/seminars/S055.md) rather than restating it, and say which of its items an operator needs to know about on their first day.

**Fifth, check it against the person, not the document.** Give it to somebody who did not build the system and let them look for the place they would have to phone you.

## Show it

Defend the argument, not the system.

The assessor has your service level and nothing else. They will ask three things: **what rests on your word, what should stop this system running, and what did you delete.**

They will also do one thing, and it is designed to be attractive.

**They will offer to waive the invisible-fix register.** They will say it reads like a list of things nobody can do anything about, that it adds nothing to a readiness argument, and that they are content to assess without it.

**Accepting is the failure this occasion exists to catch.** The register is the honest boundary of the closure procedure, and a procedure whose boundary has been waived reads as complete. The passing answer is to decline, and to say what the register is load-bearing for — **which is that the procedure is silent about exactly the fixes it cannot see, and silence and absence are indistinguishable in its output.**

## What this does not establish

**This occasion shows that a system's state is discoverable. It does not show that the system works, that it is reliable, or that anybody will operate it the way the argument assumes.**

**No outcome evidence exists for any of the four artefacts.** The closure mechanism has never been compared with a person ticking a box. The observability rules are published as a working standard with no comparative evidence. The service-level definition sits on one of the oldest pages of its source site. The cost-model transfer requirement is assembled from a single reflective sentence in material that contains no exit procedure at all.

**The invisible-fix register is this design's invention** and has never been used by anybody. Its underlying observation is verified — a real fix that leaves no trace in the repository never closes and regenerates indefinitely — but **turning that into a required, marked artefact is an argument, not a finding**, and the element that requires one entry to be within the candidate's own control is a guess about where the register would otherwise degenerate.

**The specification cannot tell whether the register is honest.** A candidate who omits their most serious blind spot passes this element, and nothing here would detect it. That gap is real, it is named in [WP-065](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-065.md) §8, and the design accepts it rather than filling it with a judgement nothing supports.

## Perishable content in this session

| Durable claim | Perishable instance |
|---|---|
| Readiness is a property of what a stranger can establish, not of what you can demonstrate | Whatever operations model your customer runs this year |
| A closure procedure's boundary is part of the procedure | The current set of controls that live outside a repository |
| A service level is a floor agreed with the customer | Present contractual and commercial conventions for stating one |
| A capability the customer has not exercised has not transferred | Today's tooling for cost modelling and who is licensed for it |
