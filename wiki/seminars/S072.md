# S072 · Runbooks, and the Operator Who Is Not You

**Strands:** PE · ME

## The situation

Something you built will fail at an hour when you are not available, in front of somebody who has never seen it before, who has been woken up, and who cannot ask you anything.

Everything you know about the system is the reason you cannot write for that person.

Answer these in writing before the session opens.

1. Take the last real failure this system had. Write down how you knew it had started.
2. Would your answer to item 1 be available to somebody who is not you? Say how they would get it.
3. At the first branch in your diagnosis, what evidence distinguishes the two paths?
4. What is the point at which the operator should stop and escalate, and to whom — by role, not by name?
5. What in your runbook is true only because of something you happen to remember?

**Item 5 has no honest answer from the inside.** That is the point of the session: you cannot audit your own context, so somebody else has to execute the document while you watch.

## What you must produce

**One runbook, for one failure that has actually occurred**, executed cold by a person who did not write it, while you are present and silent. What you hand in is the runbook and the execution record — where the executor stopped, what they asked that the document did not answer, and what they did that you did not intend.

Not a set of runbooks. One, executed, is worth more than a library that has never been read by anyone but its author.

## What good looks like

- **The entry condition is observable.** A person can tell whether they are in this runbook without judgement, from something on a screen.
- Each branch names the evidence that selects it, and the evidence is available to the operator at that moment.
- There is an explicit stop, with a named role to escalate to, and the escalation is a route somebody will actually answer.
- **It terminates.** There is a stated end state, and a way to know the incident is over rather than paused.
- The execution record contains at least one place where the executor stopped. **A runbook executed cold with no stops was executed by somebody who already knew the system.**

## How it goes wrong

Five failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **The runbook that assumes its author** — every step is correct and several are unperformable by anybody else. *Warning sign:* a step begins with "check that the pipeline looks normal".
- **The happy-path runbook** — it documents the procedure and not the failure, so it is a description of the system rather than a response to it. *Warning sign:* it contains no branch.
- **The escalation to a person** — the route ends at a name, a personal phone number or a private channel. *Warning sign:* the name is yours.
- **The unstartable runbook** — its trigger is a feeling. Something seems slow; the drafts look worse than usual. *Warning sign:* your entry condition contains a comparative with no reference value.
- **The rescue** — the executor stalls, you explain, everybody feels the session went well, and no defect was recorded. *Warning sign:* you spoke.

## Do it

Work from the supplied situation in [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md), and from the incident history you inherited from Stage 7. Pick a failure that happened, not one you can imagine.

Write the runbook. Then pair with a learner from a different engagement, hand it over, and **say nothing** while they run it. Your only permitted act is to record. If a stated safety condition fires — the executor is about to do something with a consequence outside the exercise — you stop the run rather than coach it, and you record that you did.

Afterwards, revise. **The revision is the deliverable and the first draft is the instrument.**

## Show it

Read out the single place where your executor stopped, and what they asked for. Then say what you changed.

The room is listening for one thing: whether your change addressed the question that was asked, or whether you added a paragraph of context that would have prevented that particular executor from asking that particular question. **The second is the commonest repair and it does not generalise.**

## What this does not establish

That an executed runbook produces a competent operator, or that a system with runbooks survives longer than one without. Nothing behind this programme measures either.

The session leans on a distinction — that being given the information required to perform an act is a different state from being able to perform it — which comes from a **practitioner change model published by the firm that owns its trademark and sells training in it.** Its evidence is that firm's own survey work; no independent evaluation of the model is cited anywhere in it. **The distinction licenses direction and mechanism only, and no magnitude of any kind.**

It also does not establish that your executor's difficulty predicts your recipient's. One person, once, on a prepared morning, is a demonstration and not a measurement.
