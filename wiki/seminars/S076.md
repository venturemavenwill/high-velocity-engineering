## The situation

Your handover is assembled and every line has a name against it. That tells you who has agreed to hold something. It tells you nothing about whether they can.

The rest of this stage is about the second question, and it is the half your source has no answer to.

Answer these in writing before the session opens.

1. Name one thing the back-office team leader must be able to do unaided after you leave. State it as an act, at a standard, under a real workload.
2. Have they ever done it? Say how you know.
3. If they cannot do it today, which is missing: that they have not been told it is theirs, that they do not want it, that they do not know how, or that they know how and cannot yet do it?
4. What would you do about each of those four, and are the four answers different?
5. The technology function is capable and unwilling. Which of your four does that call for?

**Item 4 is the session's whole content.** Almost everybody proposes training for all four, because training is the available response and the other three do not look like engineering problems.

## What you must produce

A **capability statement, one per receiving group.** For each group: the acts they must perform unaided, the standard each must reach, the workload under which it must hold, the current state, and the observation that would show the state had changed.

Not a training plan. **A training plan is a proposal; a capability statement is a target with an observation attached**, and the observation is what makes it possible to be wrong.

## What good looks like

- Each act is written as something a person does on an ordinary day, not as a topic. *Interprets a failing harness run and decides whether to pause the rollout* is an act. *Understands evaluation* is a topic.
- **The standard names the workload.** Somebody can do most things when it is the only thing they are doing.
- The current state is stated per group and is evidenced by something you observed, not by what somebody said in a meeting.
- Where an element other than instruction is missing, the statement says so and proposes something other than instruction.
- **At least one group has an act you believe they will not reach**, and that is written down rather than smoothed. Some people, for some changes, do not acquire the ability, and a plan that assumes everybody does is a plan with no failure state.

## How it goes wrong

Five failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **Training as the reflex** — every gap acquires a session, including the gaps that are about authority or unwillingness. *Warning sign:* your interventions are the same shape for all four elements.
- **The demonstration mistaken for capability** — you showed them, they followed, everybody was satisfied, and nobody has since done it alone. *Warning sign:* your evidence of current state is an event you ran.
- **Capability held by one person** — the group is recorded as capable because one member is. *Warning sign:* the same name appears as the evidence for every act in a group.
- **The closed feedback channel** — the recipient hits a defect in the system, has no route to report it, and appears to be failing. *Warning sign:* your statement has no line for what they do when the act does not work.
- **Willingness assumed from agreement** — the technology function said yes in a meeting where saying no was expensive. *Warning sign:* your only evidence of willingness is that nobody objected.

## Do it

Work from the supplied situation in [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md), which gives you two groups with opposite profiles: a back-office team leader who is willing and under-authorised, and a technology function that is capable and unwilling and has a legitimate grievance.

Write both statements. **Then write the third one nobody asks for**: the managed-service supplier who will hold the credentials, has attended nothing, and whose capability nobody has considered because they are not in anyone's stakeholder map.

Use an agent for one job only: hand it your capability statement and ask it to list the acts a recipient would have to perform that you have not named. Record what you added. As at [S071](/wiki/seminars/S071.md), the useful prompt asks what is missing rather than whether the document is good.

## Show it

Take one act from one statement. Another learner reads it and answers a single question: **who would they have to watch, doing what, to believe it?**

If they cannot answer, the act is a topic and needs rewriting. If they can, you now have the specification for the rehearsal at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md).

## What this does not establish

That the distinctions this session uses are true, or that acting on them improves anything.

They come from a **practitioner change model published by the consultancy that owns its trademark and sells the training and certification in it.** Its supporting evidence is that firm's own benchmarking survey work — practitioner self-report, no reproduced methodology, no independent replication — and **no independent evaluation of the model is cited anywhere in it.** The distinctions are used here because they are real distinctions that practice routinely conflates, and separating them changes what a team does. That is an argument about clarity, not a finding about effect.

**Direction and mechanism only. No magnitude, no rate, no proportion, no ordering strength.**

**One correction travels with the model.** It is widely summarised as holding that effort spent past the blocking element is wasted. **The book does not say that**, and this repository withdrew the claim on reading it. Its author's formulations are conditional — that a given effort will not by itself produce the change, or will not be productive until the blocking element moves — with one absolute restricted to the case of instruction in the absence of willingness. Use the conditional form.
