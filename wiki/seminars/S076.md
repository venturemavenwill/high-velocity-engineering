# S076 · Capability Is Not Documentation

**Module:** [M16](/wiki/modules/M16.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md) · **Complexity class:** 5
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** Custom instructions and agent configuration files as transferable capability — an executable convention beside the prose that describes it
**Whitepaper:** [WP-076](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-076.md)
**Retrieves from:** S072, S073, S075
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

---

## The situation

Your handover is assembled and every line has a name against it. That tells you who has agreed to hold something. It tells you nothing about whether they can.

Beside the handover sits a folder of documents. A runbook. A conventions page. An architecture decision record. A README saying what to do when the rejection log grows. Every one of them is well written, every one of them was written by you, and not one of them has ever been executed by anybody who was not in the room when it was drafted.

**Answer these in writing before reading further.** They are not rhetorical. Item 4 is the one this session is actually about.

1. Name one thing the back-office team leader must be able to do unaided after you leave. State it as an act, at a standard, under a real workload.
2. Have they ever done it? Say how you know, and say whether your evidence is something you observed or something somebody told you.
3. If they cannot do it today, which is missing: that nobody has told them it is theirs, that they do not want it, that they do not know how, or that they know how and cannot yet do it?
4. What would you do about each of those four, and are the four answers different?
5. The technology function is capable and unwilling, and has a legitimate grievance. Which of your four does that call for?

Most people answer 1, 2, 3 and 5 with some effort and produce four versions of the same answer to item 4, because the available response is training and the other three states do not look like engineering problems. **That collapse is the finding.**

## What you must produce

**A capability statement, one per receiving group**, and one executable convention for each of them.

1. **The acts** each group must perform unaided — written as things a person does on an ordinary day, not as topics.
2. **The standard** each act must reach, and **the workload** under which it must hold.
3. **The current state**, evidenced by something you observed rather than something somebody said.
4. **The observation that would show the state had changed** — who, doing what, watched by whom.
5. **The blocking element** per group, named as one of the four states, with a proposed response that is not automatically instruction.
6. **One convention per group expressed as configuration the tooling reads**, not as a page describing what people should do — with evidence, captured from a payload, that it was actually applied.
7. **The group nobody asked about.** There is always one.

Not a training plan. **A training plan is a proposal; a capability statement is a target with an observation attached**, and the observation is what makes it possible to be wrong.

## The material

### The two objects that are constantly confused

A document describes a capability. A convention enacts one. They look alike on a shelf and they behave completely differently the day after you leave.

Here is the difference stated exactly. A written standard is a claim about what people should do, and it is discharged by a person reading it, remembering it, and choosing to comply, on an ordinary Tuesday, under deadline, six months after anybody explained why it mattered. **The same standard expressed as configuration the tooling loads is discharged by the tooling.** Nobody has to remember. Nobody has to choose. The conformance question stops being *did they read it* and becomes *did it run*, and the second question has an answer you can look at.

This is the same claim [S046](/wiki/seminars/S046.md) makes from the delivery side — a specification an executor has not received is not a specification — arriving now from the transfer side. There, the executor was an agent and the failure was silent non-application. Here the executor is a team you will not be present to correct, and the failure is silent decay.

**The claim is not that documents are useless.** They discharge a different obligation. A document explains *why* the convention exists, which is what configuration cannot carry and what a recipient needs when the convention has to change. Prose without the executable form is an aspiration. The executable form without the prose is a rule nobody can amend safely. **Ship both, and know which one is doing which job.**

### An inspectable instance, and what it costs to read it honestly

The clearest available specimen of a standard expressed as configuration is an openly published agent configuration corpus, read at a pinned commit and recorded at [research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md). Its organising claim is that a coding agent's behaviour is an engineering artefact, and that if it is an artefact it should be authored, versioned, reviewed, packaged and distributed like any other.

Four artefact kinds sit in distinct trees and are applied by different rules. **Instructions** are constraints applied automatically to matching work — the closest analogue to a coding standard, and the kind this session is about. **Prompts** are user-facing entry points a person invokes. **Agents** are orchestrators and specialists with declared roles. **Skills** are reusable procedures with their own scripts and references. Authoring source, packaging manifest and generated output are kept apart, and the generated tree is declared non-canonical.

Two properties of that corpus are the teaching. The first is that **the standards themselves are subject to conformance checks** — there is machinery whose job is to test whether an instructions file has the shape it declares. That is what a standard looks like when somebody has taken the equivalence seriously: not only is the rule executable, the rule's own well-formedness is executable.

The second is the honesty, which is unusual and which you must reproduce. The corpus's own documentation records that its behavioural graders are regex-based and **test output shape rather than correctness**; that behaviour conformance is **advisory by default** and lacks established calibration; and that **a check can be advisory at one tier while a scheduled run treats the same check as authoritative.** Its transparency statement records that the project ships no model and no safety classifier, that host behaviour varies, and that human oversight remains necessary.

**Its limits travel with every use and they are severe.** Perishability is total on paths, counts, artefact names and package versions, all of which moved while the note was being written. **Host and model dependence is absolute** — every artefact is inert without a host that loads it, and a result observed on one client and one model is not a result about the corpus. Scale will overwhelm a small team, so select rather than adopt. And **a single vendor's opinion about agent configuration is not the field**; the artefact-kind taxonomy is a product decision, taught here as one instance of a durable idea.

**The durable idea, stripped of every path in it: a convention the tooling reads survives the person who wrote it. A convention only a person reads survives exactly as long as that person's attention.**

### Four states, and why they are not one problem

When a receiving group cannot do something, there are at least four distinct reasons, and the response to each differs. The practitioner vocabulary this session borrows separates them as **awareness** that the change exists and is theirs, **desire** to engage with it, **knowledge** of how, and **ability** to do it at the required standard under real conditions ([research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md](/research/05-fde-craft/hiatt-adkar.md)).

Two of the distinctions are worth more than the model they come from.

**Being told is not being aware.** The source's best-argued claim is that "sharing information does not always produce awareness", and that "Awareness is not achieved based on the messages sent, but rather how the messages are received and internalized by each person." Its consequence for a handover meeting is direct: the meeting happened, the slides were shown, and whether anybody now holds the fact is an open question you have not investigated.

**Knowing how is not being able.** "The presence of knowledge is often insufficient by itself." Ability is "turning knowledge into action" at "the required performance levels", and the source is unusually willing to say the uncomfortable thing: **"Some employees, depending on the change, may never develop the required abilities."** A plan that assumes everybody arrives is a plan with no failure state.

The same source supplies the failure mode that ruins handovers and is invisible while it happens: **"If the feedback channel is not open, then the employee will appear to be failing when the system is actually at fault."** A recipient who hits a defect and has nowhere to report it looks exactly like a recipient who cannot operate the system.

**The warrant is thin and must be stated every time.** This is a practitioner model published by the consultancy that owns its trademark and sells the training and certification in it. Every quantitative claim in it traces to that firm's own benchmarking surveys — practitioner self-report, no reproduced methodology, no sampling frame, no independent replication. **No independent evaluation of the model appears anywhere in it.** Its ordering argument is made from the meanings of the words and rests on nothing external. Use the distinctions because they are real distinctions that practice routinely conflates; do not use the model as evidence.

**One correction travels with it.** It is widely summarised as holding that effort spent past the blocking element is wasted. **The book does not say that.** Its formulations are conditional — that a given effort will not *by itself* produce the change, or will not be productive *until* the blocking element moves — with one absolute restricted to instruction in the absence of willingness. Use the conditional form.

### Information alone is the weakest of the available levers

The efficacy literature reaches the same place from a different direction and with a better warrant, and it is the source this module leans on hardest ([research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md](/research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md)).

Bandura's change model puts information first and immediately discounts it: **"factual information alone, much of which is usually redundant with what people already know, usually produces little change"**, with the efficacy-building work done by "repeated opportunities for guided practice in applying the skills successfully in situations simulating those they are likely to encounter in their everyday life". Of the four sources of efficacy belief, **only one is explicitly ranked**: "The most effective way of creating a strong sense of efficacy is through mastery experiences. They provide the most authentic evidence of whether one can muster whatever it takes to succeed."

Two further passages bear directly on a capability statement.

The first tells you what your handover meeting achieved: **"The multiple benefits of a resilient sense of personal efficacy do not arise simply from the incantation of capability. Saying something should not be confused with believing it to be so."** A recipient who said yes in a room has said yes in a room.

The second tells you why silence in that room is not agreement. **"Efficacy beliefs preempt expectancy-valence analyses"** — options in domains of low perceived efficacy are excluded wholesale, "with little thought to costs and benefits". A recipient who never objected to owning the evaluation harness may not have weighed and accepted it. They may never have considered it a thing they could hold, in which case your handover has a line with a name against it and no owner behind the name.

**The limits are specific.** The volume read is a conference collection edited by the field's founder, reporting no search strategy, no inclusion criteria and, across the editor's own chapter, no study whose result went the wrong way. Its empirical settings are schools, families, clinics, migration and addiction treatment; **it contains no study of adult professionals learning a technical craft**, so applying it here is an inference. The construct is almost always measured by self-report. And the foundational 1977 paper remains unread in this repository, so any claim resting on it alone is declared unverified. **Direction and mechanism only.**

### Write the act, not the topic

The assessment literature supplies the discipline that makes a capability statement checkable, and it is a writing rule rather than a theory ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)).

An entrustable professional activity is a unit of practice a supervisor could delegate whole. The granularity test is behavioural: could you hand this over and walk away, and would the recipient know when they had finished it? The description template is explicit that a title **"should not contain an adjective"** and **"should not be phrased as an educational objective—it is just an activity"**, and that a specification-and-limitations section must capture "no more and no less than what is exactly meant".

Applied here: *interprets a failing harness run and decides whether to pause the rollout* is an act. *Understands evaluation* is a topic wearing a verb. The adjective prohibition matters because an adjective smuggles a quality standard into the name of the thing, where it cannot be observed; the standard belongs in the specification and in the supervision level, where it can.

The same source records that **entrustment expires** — a decision "may expire" if the activity "has not been practiced for a long time" — which is the one property of a handover nobody writes down. **Its limits:** a clinical model with legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint, guidance from an advocate reporting adoption rather than effectiveness, and a full evidence standard expensive enough that any course adopts a reduced version and should say so.

### Why the convention has to be in place before you go

The change literature's most-quoted structural claim is that culture moves last rather than first: **"Culture changes only after you have successfully altered people's actions, after the new behavior produces some group benefit for a period of time, and after people see the connection between the new actions and the performance improvement"** ([research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md](/research/05-fde-craft/kotter-leading-change.md)). Its image for the failure mode is the right one for a handover: **"Shallow roots require constant watering."**

The same source names the thing that undoes a convention faster than any technical defect: **"Behavior from important people that is inconsistent with the vision overwhelms other forms of communication."** If your last fortnight consists of you bypassing the convention you are handing over, because you are quick and the deadline is close, the convention has been withdrawn in the only register that counts.

**Its warrant is practitioner counsel and its author says so.** The book carries no footnotes or endnotes by design — "I have neither drawn examples or major ideas from any published source except my own writing nor tried to cite evidence from other sources to bolster my conclusions" — and every extended case is pseudonymous and untraceable. Its numbers, including the multiplier in one of its own chapter headings, are constructed illustrations rather than measurements, and none is reproduced here.

### The absence this whole module is built on

The practice corpus behind this programme is detailed, experienced and in places genuinely wise about withdrawal — it mechanises the teardown of the delivery organisation's own assets down to a state change in an authoritative register ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)).

**And it contains no handover.** Nothing describes transferring the running system, its infrastructure definitions, its pipelines, its runbooks, its evaluation harness, its cost model or its monitoring to the customer. No named receiving owner, no exit criteria, no acceptance, no support taper. The one place the right instinct appears — that the customer should be left holding the cost-modelling capability because they will need it after the team disengages — sits as a reflection inside a business-value narrative rather than as a step in any procedure.

**The model on offer is withdrawal as estate hygiene, not withdrawal as transfer of a going concern.** Its question is *is our house clean?*, not *can they run it without us?* Only the second is an engineering problem.

**Its case studies all succeed, and it reports no engagement where capability transfer failed.** That absence is not evidence that transfer always works. It is evidence that nobody looked.

## The workflow

**Take one convention from your handover, express it as configuration the tooling loads, and prove it was applied — on the recipient's machine, not yours.**

**Step 1 — choose a convention that will decay.** Not a preference. Something that will silently stop happening within a month of your departure: every platform claim carries a fetch date; every change to the retrieval configuration names the evaluation run that cleared it; no runbook step is marked complete without the command that was run. **Pick the one whose absence would be hardest to notice.**

**Step 2 — locate the loading rule of the host the recipient actually uses.** Custom instructions are read from a declared path with a declared matching rule. Establish which path and which rule, on their editor and their version — not yours. A configuration corpus that is not loaded is a folder of opinions.

**Step 3 — write the instructions file.** Create `.github/instructions/handover.instructions.md`, with an `applyTo` glob covering the folders the recipient will actually work in:

```markdown
---
applyTo: "runbooks/**/*.md,eval/**/*.py"
---

# Conventions this team inherits

- Every claim about platform behaviour MUST carry a fetched documentation
  reference and the date it was fetched, in the same sentence as the claim.
- Every change under `eval/` MUST name the evaluation run that cleared it,
  by run identifier, and state the split strategy that run used.
- A runbook step MUST NOT be marked complete without the command that was
  executed and its exit status.
- If a required reference does not exist, write "not established" rather
  than answering from general knowledge. Do not infer a value.
```

**Step 4 — verify arrival, on their machine.** Send one prompt from the recipient's editor, then inspect the chat output channel and confirm the instruction text appears in the request payload. **An instructions file you have not observed in a payload is a file, not a specification.** Record the host, the host version, the model and the date, because the artefact is inert without a host that loads it and a result on your machine is not a result on theirs.

**Step 5 — break it deliberately, and watch what happens.** Ask for a change under `eval/` and supply no run identifier. A convention that is loaded produces a refusal or a marked gap. A convention that is present and unloaded produces a fluent, helpful, entirely non-conforming answer, with nothing in the output indicating which of the two you got. **That indistinguishability is the point of the step.**

**Step 6 — hand the keyboard over and do not take it back.** The recipient writes the second instructions file, for their own second convention, while you watch. You may answer a direct question. You may not type. If they cannot get it loaded, you have found a real capability gap, and it is worth more than the file would have been.

**Step 7 — attack your own convention rather than improving it.** One prompt: *here is an instructions file and the folder it applies to. Describe three ordinary tasks this team performs weekly that fall outside the glob, and one case where following this rule literally would produce a worse outcome than ignoring it.* Read it for the case you did not think of. Keep what is better than your own reasoning and record what you rejected. **Record separately that this instrument is not independent of the tooling being configured, and that an agent which endorsed your convention was not used as an instrument.**

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** The gap between a rule that exists and a rule that runs, on the recipient's hardware, in one afternoon. You can hold a well-written conventions page and an unloaded instructions file side by side and see they are the same object — a description of intended behaviour that no machine will enforce. **You cannot argue anybody out of that, and you do not have to.**

## What good looks like

- Each act is written as something a person does on an ordinary day, at a standard, under a stated workload. **No adjectives in the name of the act.**
- **The current state is evidenced by something you observed**, not by what somebody said in a meeting — and the statement says which.
- The blocking element is named per group as one of the four states, and where it is not knowledge, the proposed response is not instruction.
- At least one convention exists in executable form, **with a captured payload showing it applied on the recipient's host**, dated, with host and model recorded.
- The prose explaining why the convention exists is shipped beside it, and the statement says which document is doing which job.
- **At least one group has an act you believe they will not reach**, written down rather than smoothed. Some people, for some changes, do not acquire the ability.
- Every group has a line for what the recipient does when the act does not work, because a closed feedback channel makes a broken system look like a failing operator.

## How it goes wrong

- **Training as the reflex** — every gap acquires a session, including the gaps that are about authority or unwillingness. *Warning sign:* your interventions are the same shape for all four states.
- **The document mistaken for the convention** — a well-written page describes what the team should do, and nothing loads it. *Warning sign:* you cannot say which process reads your standard.
- **The convention that never arrived** — the file exists at the right path, the host is not configured to load it, and the tooling behaves exactly as before. *Warning sign:* you have not seen the text in a payload.
- **The demonstration mistaken for capability** — you showed them, they followed, everybody was satisfied, and nobody has since done it alone. *Warning sign:* your evidence of current state is an event you ran.
- **Capability held by one person** — the group is recorded as capable because one member is. *Warning sign:* the same name appears as the evidence for every act in a group.
- **Willingness assumed from agreement** — the technology function said yes in a meeting where saying no was expensive. *Warning sign:* your only evidence of willingness is that nobody objected.
- **The convention you yourself stopped following** — in the last fortnight, under deadline, you bypassed it because you were faster without it. *Warning sign:* your own recent commits do not conform to the file you are handing over.

## Exercises

**None of these is assessed.** The capability statement is carried to [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md), where its acts become the specification for the rehearsal, and to [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), where the stage is judged as an entrustment decision.

**1 · Convert four topics into acts.** Rewrite each so a reader can name who would have to be watched, doing what, to believe it.

- *Understands the evaluation harness.*
- *Is comfortable with the deployment pipeline.*
- *Knows the escalation process.*
- *Has good judgement about model changes.*

> **Model answers.** *Runs the harness against the current suite, reads a failing check, and decides whether to pause the rollout — during a release window, with the release manager waiting.* · *Takes a change from branch to production unaided, including the approval step, and can state what it did not deploy.* · *Raises an incident, names the on-call owner from the register, and states the customer-visible impact — without asking anybody who it is.* · The last one cannot be rewritten as one act, and that is the answer. **"Good judgement" is a summary of many acts, and it is exactly the sort of adjective the specification prohibits in a title.** Break it into the two or three decisions you actually mean, or admit you cannot observe it.

**2 · Diagnose four groups, then check yourself.** For each, name the blocking state and one response that is not instruction:

- A team leader who has never been told the rejection log is now theirs.
- A technology function that understands the system perfectly and has a live grievance about not being consulted.
- An operator who wants the responsibility and has never seen the runbook.
- An engineer who has read everything, practised twice, and freezes when the pipeline fails under time pressure.

> **Model answers.** Awareness; willingness; knowledge; ability — and the fourth is the interesting one, because it looks like a knowledge gap and is not. The source is candid that what is "difficult to ascertain during this phase" is "whether the barrier to change is ability or incomplete knowledge", and the only thing that settles it is watching. **Non-instruction responses:** name them on the register and have their own manager say it; ask what was done to them and what would have to be true now; nothing — the third really is instruction; supervised practice under real conditions with the mistake permitted.

**3 · Go back to your own item 4, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 4 — your four responses to the four states. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then write the honest version.

> **Criterion.** A pass requires that **at least two of the four responses are not instruction**, and that the response to unwillingness is not a better explanation. Four variations on *run a session* fails, however well the sessions are designed. *Explain it again more clearly* fails, because it is instruction wearing a different verb.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**4 · Load a convention and prove it, on someone else's machine.** Using the workflow, put one instructions file in place on another learner's editor and capture the payload that shows it arrived. Then have them break it deliberately.

> **What you should find.** That the loading rule is host-specific and version-specific, and that the failure mode when it does not load is silence rather than error. **If you cannot obtain a payload, record that as the finding** — you have discovered that your recipient's environment gives you no way to verify a standard is in force, which is a more important fact about the handover than the convention itself.

**5 · Find the group nobody asked about.** List every party who will touch the running system after you leave, including the ones outside anybody's stakeholder map: the managed-service supplier holding credentials, the platform team who own the subscription, the auditor who samples the compliance record.

> **Scoring criterion.** A pass names at least one group appearing in no handover document, and states what act they must perform. **A fail lists only the groups who attended your sessions**, which is the same defect as evidencing capability from an event you ran. The managed-service supplier is the usual answer, and is usually holding the credentials.

**6 · Turn the instrument on your own last fortnight.** Take the convention you are handing over and check your own recent work against it, mechanically.

> **What to notice.** That you probably stopped following it under deadline, and that nobody mentioned it. Behaviour by the person who wrote the rule overwhelms the rule. **The point of this exercise is not contrition.** It is that the convention has already been tested on the person most motivated to keep it, and it failed — which tells you something about how long it will survive with people who are less motivated, without you having to guess.

## What this does not establish

**That any of this improves anything.** Nothing behind this programme measures whether a capability statement, an executable convention or a diagnosed blocking element makes a delivered system more likely to survive.

**The four-state vocabulary is a commercial model with no independent evaluation.** Its ordering claim is argued from the meanings of the words and rests on nothing external; its quantitative claims come from its own vendor's self-report surveys. The distinctions are used here because they are real and routinely conflated. That is an argument about clarity, not a finding about effect.

**One correction travels with the model.** It is widely summarised as holding that effort spent past the blocking element is wasted. **The book does not say that**, and this repository withdrew the claim on reading it. Its author's formulations are conditional, with one absolute restricted to instruction in the absence of willingness. Use the conditional form.

**The efficacy literature is well warranted and about the wrong population.** Its settings are schools, families, clinics and treatment programmes. It contains no study of adult professionals learning a technical craft, and its foundational paper is unread in this repository.

**The change literature is practitioner counsel by its author's own statement**, with untraceable cases and no cited evidence.

**The practice corpus contains no handover at all**, and this module is written past it. **It also reports no engagement where capability transfer failed** — which is an absence of looking, not evidence of success.

**The platform material is the most perishable content here.** The configuration corpus was read at one commit with one host and one model, and every path and count in it moved during the reading. **Direction and mechanism only. No magnitude, no rate, no proportion, no ordering strength.**
