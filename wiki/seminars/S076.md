# S076 · The Adversary Who Reads Your Prompt

**Module:** [M16](wiki/modules/M16.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](wiki/quarters/Q8.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** SG · IS · ME
**Whitepaper:** [WP-076](wiki/whitepapers/WP-076.md)
**Depends on:** S044, S073
**Re-tests:** S071
**Platform anchor:** Azure AI Content Safety Prompt Shields (user-prompt and document shields) and spotlighting, over a Foundry Agent Service component · **MCP servers:** Foundry MCP Server · Azure MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## What this day is for

[S044](wiki/seminars/S044.md) point 3 supplied four attack families for a learned component and named two omissions. One of them was assigned here, in that day's own words: *it omits attacks specific to generative components, which arrive at S076.* This is that day, thirty-two seminar days later, and the deferral is discharged in full.

[S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) established that a tool call crosses a boundary of trust. [S071](wiki/seminars/S071.md) established what changes when a system can act. Between them they left a question the module has to answer before it can ask any other: **the boundary of trust runs somewhere, and after the context is assembled it does not run anywhere at all.**

A model receives one sequence. The system prompt somebody wrote, the tool definitions somebody registered, the documents the retriever returned, the strings the tools emitted, the user's message, and — through the orchestration layer — the model's own earlier output, summarised. Those six things arrive concatenated, in one channel, with whatever provenance the assembler chose to preserve, which is usually none.

The target: **the adversary is whoever can put text where the system will read it.** Not a network position, not an authenticated account, not somebody with malicious intent. A write path into any source the context assembler reads, held by anyone, used deliberately or not.

**And a consequence that is harder to accept than the definition:** if the adversary is anyone with a write path, then most of your adversaries are your own users, your own staff, your own vendors and your own system, and none of them is attacking you.

**Everything on this day is executed.** The four cases are sent through a disposable copy of the substrate agent, the demonstration and the bound are built with Copilot in agent mode against a real Foundry deployment, and the vendor's own injection control is read out of its documentation rather than described. **Nothing runs against the production path**, and the reason is a duty of care rather than a convenience: the substrate's disclosure covers announced disturbance and does not cover a student making the live service do something its operator did not intend.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

A substrate agent is supplied with **the full assembled context of one real request**, printed as the model received it: system prompt, tool definitions, three retrieved documents, two tool outputs, the summarised prior turn, and the user message. Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. **Mark every part of this context that somebody outside the engineering team can influence.** Mark it on the page.
2. **For each thing you marked, name who can influence it and what it would cost them.**
3. The agent's instructions say *never disclose the internal ticket identifier.* **Say how you would enforce that**, in one or two sentences.
4. **Predict: what fraction of the printed context did you mark at item 1?**
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent, through the Foundry MCP Server, to *make this component resistant to prompt injection.* Write down what it will produce and **name the one thing it will get wrong.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 1 is the day's opening finding and needs no framework. Item 4 is asked separately from item 1 because the two answers diverge — students mark less than they then estimate they marked, which is the calibration result phase 8 reports.

Item 3 is the day's payload administered before the day names it, and the answers are collected verbatim so that phase 8 can sort them into the three places of point 6.

Item 5 is the day's contribution to the running frontier map. The design's expectation is that the agent will produce something real and configurable — a content filter, a delimiter convention, a hardened system prompt — and that it will produce a control at point 6's first place while describing it as a solution. **Predicting that the agent will get something wrong is easy; naming what is the measure**, and the two rates are reported separately.

**Warrant.** Pretesting on a real assembled context with the answer physically present on the page, so that the phase 8 comparison is against a fact rather than against an opinion. Item 2 enforces [S044](wiki/seminars/S044.md)'s actor requirement before the day can drift into hazards, which the module cannot assume is a habit after one prior day. Item 3 elicits the belief that enforcement belongs at the perimeter, which is the belief point 6 exists to correct, and it is elicited in the student's own words rather than as a multiple choice, because the sorting is the measurement. **Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8**, and it is deliberately the same question as item 3 asked of a different respondent — so the day measures the room's model of enforcement and the room's model of the agent's model of enforcement, and can report the gap.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative retrieval set (20 min)

Free recall, no notes.

- [S044](wiki/seminars/S044.md): a threat is an actor with access and a goal — and **actor D**, who wants nothing in particular and is careless. Why is expected harm frequently highest there?
- [S044](wiki/seminars/S044.md): the four attack families, and the two the list omits.
- [S071](wiki/seminars/S071.md): what changes when the system can act, rather than answer.
- [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md): where the boundary of trust runs at a tool call, and whose authority the call carries.
- **Bridge:** [S044](wiki/seminars/S044.md) said access determines feasibility and is usually wider than assumed. [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) said the boundary of trust runs at the tool call. **Once the context is assembled, where does the boundary run, and who is inside it?**

The room answers, and it can: **it does not run anywhere.** The assembled context is one sequence with no internal boundary the model enforces, and everyone who contributed to any of its sources is inside. The follow-up the room supplies unprompted, if the first answer comes quickly: the boundary must therefore have been enforced *before* assembly, by a component nobody has named yet.

**Warrant.** Expanding-interval retrieval across two quarters and eight months, converting two held facts into the day's structural claim by joining them. Actor D is retrieved first and deliberately, because the day's definition of adversary is broad enough to be dismissed as alarmism, and a room that has just re-derived why the careless insider outranks the competitor will not dismiss it. The bridge is answered by the room, not the instructor.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Each group has **a disposable copy of the substrate agent** — its own Foundry Agent Service deployment, its own index, no real users — stood up from the substrate's Bicep by Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server, with Content Safety in its default configuration. Nothing on this day runs against the production path.

Groups receive **four pieces of content that reached the same substrate agent**, each printed twice: once as it appeared in its source, and once as it appeared in the assembled context with all provenance markers stripped. They **send all four through the copy** and read three things: what the agent did, what the content filter reported, and whether the annotation distinguished the four at all.

- **C1** — a user message containing an explicit instruction to disregard the agent's instructions. Crude, blocked by every filter the substrate has, and the case every student expects.
- **C2** — a document from the substrate's own corpus, written by a real user two years ago in good faith. One sentence in it reads as an instruction only because of where the assembler places it. **The action C2's sentence asks for is selected against the schema reduction this cohort was least willing to make at [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) step 4** — it is an action the room's own narrowest tool schema still admits, so that the best restriction the cohort was prepared to accept does not stop it.
- **C3** — a tool's error string, quoted verbatim into the context. The text is controlled by the tool's upstream vendor and changes without notice.
- **C4** — the agent's own output from three turns earlier, summarised back into the context by the orchestration layer built at [S072](wiki/seminars/S072.md).

Groups rank the four by *how much of this reaches the model as instruction*, commit in writing, and only then run them.

**The payload is C4.** No outsider is in the loop. Every control the room designed sits at a perimeter that C4 never crosses, because C4 originates inside. And the mechanism is worse than the case: an adversary who gets one sentence into any earlier turn now has a laundering path through the component the students trust most, which is the one they built.

**The platform supplies a second finding the room does not expect, and it is a finding about the vendor rather than about the model.** Prompt Shields is two shields, not one: a shield for **user prompts** and a shield for **documents**. That split is a claim that the system knows which is which — and on this copy, what makes a string a *document* is that the assembler passed it in the documents field. C1 is a user prompt, C2 and C3 are documents only if somebody mapped them there, and C4 is neither, because nothing in the request says where it came from. **The product's own taxonomy is a taxonomy of the assembler's choices**, which is point 2 arriving from an unexpected direction and is the strongest available evidence for it.

Three tasks after, all required:

1. **Working only from the stripped versions, reconstruct which of the four came from which source.** Record the group's answer before the mapping is revealed. The reconstruction error rate is the phase's measurement.
2. **For each, state where in the pipeline the content stopped being data and started being instruction, and name the component that made the transition.**
3. **For each, state which shield — if any — the platform had a chance to apply, and why.** A case for which the answer is *neither* is not a defect in the product; it is the answer to task 2 restated by somebody who sells software.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across write paths with the agent's own behaviour as adjudicator, matched on content and varied on origin, which is the target dimension, and **executed against a disposable copy so that the adjudicator is a real service rather than a supplied result**. C4 defeats the room's expectation that an adversary is an outsider and does it on the one case where nobody acted adversarially at all.

Task 1 exists because of a pattern this design has recorded three times and not yet elevated: **that it stages what it expects students to resist and asserts what it finds obvious once stated** — opened at [WP-049](wiki/whitepapers/WP-049.md) §8, recorded as a second at [WP-053](wiki/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §8, and argued for elevation at [WP-057](wiki/whitepapers/WP-057.md) §8, [WP-064](wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 and [WP-067](wiki/whitepapers/WP-067.md) §8. **The proposed rule is pending and is not in force**, and this day applies it to itself voluntarily. Point 4 — that data and instruction are indistinguishable in the channel — is exactly the belief item 3 elicits as wrong and has an obvious engineering consequence, and without task 1 it would be delivered by exposition. With task 1 the room produces it, and the reconstruction error rate is a number rather than an assertion. **Task 3 is the same argument run on the vendor's product rather than on the room's model**, and it is cheap: the taxonomy is on a documentation page the students fetch through the Learn MCP Server in ninety seconds.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **The adversary is whoever can put text where the system will read it.** A write path into any source the context assembler reads. Not a network position, not an account, not intent. [S044](wiki/seminars/S044.md)'s actor requirement holds unchanged — an actor with access and a goal — and the goal may be *finish my ticket*, which is why phase 3's C2 has no attacker in it and behaved like one.

2. **The untrusted-content boundary is the point at which content becomes context**, and it is a design decision made by whoever wrote the assembler. On most systems nobody made it: the assembler concatenates whatever the retriever and the tools return, and the boundary ended up wherever the concatenation happens to start.

3. **Three properties decide exposure**, and they are the questions to ask of any source: *who can write to it*, *how many hops the content takes before assembly*, and *whether provenance survives those hops.* The third almost never holds, which is what phase 3's task 1 measured. **This list omits two things it would be wrong to leave implicit:** it omits whether the source can be written to *retrospectively*, which changes an audited system into an unaudited one, and it omits multi-party contexts where two principals' content is assembled together, which is [S078](wiki/seminars/S078.md)'s. It is safe as a triage for a single-principal agent whose sources you can enumerate; it is unsafe as a review of a system whose retriever's corpus you do not control, where the enumeration is the hard part and this list assumes it is done.

4. **Data and instruction are not distinguishable in the channel.** The model receives one sequence. Any separation you believe in is a separation enforced before assembly, by a component that is not the model, and phase 3 task 1 measured how well the room could reconstruct one after the fact. **Delimiters, tags and role markers are conventions the assembler honours and the content can imitate.** This is not a defect of any particular model; it is a property of putting two things in one channel.

5. **The system's own output is untrusted content on the next turn.** [S072](wiki/seminars/S072.md)'s orchestration loop is a write path with no author. Anything that reached the model once can reach it again, laundered through a component the team owns and therefore trusts.

6. **A defence can sit in exactly three places**, and item 3's answers will be sorted into them at phase 8:
   - **restricting what reaches the assembler** — filters, allow-lists, source curation;
   - **changing what the assembled content is able to cause** — capability bounds, authorisation, confirmation;
   - **detecting that it caused something** — [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s repertoire applied here.

   **Only the second and third survive an adversary who holds a legitimate write path.** The first is a control on who may contribute, and phase 3's C2, C3 and C4 were all contributed legitimately. **This is safe as a design frame for a component whose actions you can enumerate; it is unsafe as a completeness claim, because the third place depends on a detection design tuned on non-adversarial variation** — which is [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md)'s subject and this module's central problem.

7. **Bound the capability, not the string.** [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md)'s boundary of trust re-expressed: the operational question is not whether injected text is present — you cannot answer it — but what the agent is permitted to do while holding content from that path. An agent that cannot write, cannot spend and cannot disclose is an agent whose injected instructions produce a rude answer. **The bound is stated per path, not per system**, and [S078](wiki/seminars/S078.md) is where it meets authorisation.

8. **What one vendor built, and what it decided for you. Marked perishable; the reasoning is not.** Content Safety supplies **Prompt Shields**, which is two detectors: one for **user prompt** attacks and one for **document** attacks embedded in grounding data. Three properties are worth more than the feature.

   - **The split encodes point 2.** The product can only shield a document if something told it the content was a document, and that something is your assembler. The vendor has built a control whose correctness depends on a decision the vendor did not make.
   - **The alert taxonomy distinguishes blocked from detected-and-not-blocked**, and Defender's published catalogue carries both as separate alerts — the second described as not blocked *due to content filtering settings or due to low confidence.* **No confidence scale, no threshold and no rate is published for either.** You are given a control with a knob and no curve.
   - **Spotlighting — the sub-feature that transforms retrieved content so the model treats it as less trustworthy — is off by default.** Turning it on costs tokens and can push a long document past the input limit. **This is the day's default the platform chose for you**, and it is chosen in the direction that makes your system work and your boundary weaker. An engineer who cannot say which of these three facts is a property of language models and which is a product decision does not understand their own system, and only the first is durable.

   **Safe** as a reason to know what your filter has been told about your own content, and as a worked instance of point 4's consequence. **Unsafe** as evidence that a shield holds: nothing published states how often either detector is right, so **a request that passes a shield is a request that passed a shield**, and the day's specification checks the capability bound rather than the filter for that reason.

9. **What [S044](wiki/seminars/S044.md) deferred here, and what this day does not cover.** [S044](wiki/seminars/S044.md) point 3 deferred generative-component attacks to today; today covers the content path into an assembled context. **It does not cover poisoning, extraction or exhaustion, which are [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s and are different mechanisms with different defences; and it does not cover attacks on the infrastructure below the assembler** — credentials, transport, dependencies — which are SG201's and SG302's and which [WP-044](wiki/whitepapers/WP-044.md) §8 recorded as the origin of most real incidents. The [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) classes adversarial technique catalogues as perishable per delivery, on the ground that **the attack classes are more stable than the specific exploits**, and that sentence is read to the room here: every specific evasion you will see this year is an instance of points 3 and 4, and points 3 and 4 are what you are being assessed on.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3, with point 4 generated by task 1 rather than asserted and point 6 supplying the sorting frame for item 3's collected answers. Points 3 and 6 carry strong-form scope statements per the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md); **point 8 is the day's platform-specific content, is marked perishable, names a default and carries its own strong-form scope statement**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md); point 9 discharges [S044](wiki/seminars/S044.md)'s deferral and bounds today's own claim in the same paragraph, which is the form the deferral rule requires. **Point 8's second bullet is the module's first encounter with the silence that runs through all five days** — a vendor that publishes an alert taxonomy and no rate — and it is named rather than worked around.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 6. The student decides what to hand over, to whom, and with what warranty. The handover is a content boundary and the receiver is whoever is on call when the component behaves oddly. This phase produces a build.**

Students use **their own agent component**, carried since [S066](wiki/seminars/S066.md) and extended through [M15](wiki/modules/M15.md), and **Copilot in agent mode through the Foundry and Azure MCP Servers**.

1. **Map every path by which text reaches your component's context.** Include the paths you do not control and the ones you inherited. For each, state who can write to it, at what cost, and whether provenance survives to assembly.
2. **Name the paths that can be written to retrospectively**, and say how you would know if one had been.
3. **Choose the path you judge most exposed and demonstrate it.** Get your own component to take an instruction from that path. **This runs against your disposable copy on the substrate's disturbance-window infrastructure, never against the production path**, and the reason is stated in the brief: the substrate's disclosure to its users covers deliberate disturbance in announced windows and does not cover a student causing the live service to do something its operator did not intend.
4. **State what the demonstration does not establish.** [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s discipline: one successful path is evidence about one path, and a failed attempt on another is evidence about your attempt. **If you used the platform's own adversarial tooling to generate the attempt, the attack success rate it reports is a property of its attack set and of your copy, and you say so** — a rate with no reference value published is a measurement of what you ran, not of what your component is.
5. **Ship the bound.** Change the copy so that the demonstration at step 3 no longer produces the consequence, and re-run it. **The change must be a change to what the component may do, not a change to what it will read** — a filter that blocks your own step 3 string is not accepted, for the reason point 6 gives. Record what the re-run did.
6. **Write the boundary statement.** Which sources your component treats as instruction, which it treats as data, **the named component that enforces the distinction**, and **what the component does when that enforcement is bypassed.** The last clause is the warranty and is the reason this is a class-6 artefact rather than a design note.
7. **State the capability bound per path**, per point 7: what your component may do while holding content from each. **Name one action you have removed**, and say what it cost to remove it and who now cannot do the thing it enabled.
8. **Hand the boundary statement to the on-call rota member who would receive an incident on this component.** They are present for the last thirty minutes. **You do not ask whether the statement is clear. You ask them to narrate what they would check first if they were told at three in the morning that your component "did something odd", and to say where they would stop. Record the narration.**

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned. Not a transcript.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — here the accepted example is a step 5 bound whose re-run at step 3 is automated, so that the component's own check would have caught a bound that did not hold.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch. Both specific, both from this build.

**Fading:** step 1's write-path enumeration is worked with the room, since the object is new and the omissions are systematic. **Step 6 is unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 6 producing a handover to a named receiver who is in the room, **and a build rather than a design note**. **Step 8 uses an external observer per instrumentation rule 1** — the person who would receive the incident is present for other reasons and whether the statement is usable is theirs to judge, not the student's to assume. **Step 8's narrate-rather-than-approve form is [S038](wiki/seminars/S038.md)'s expert-politeness countermeasure, which instrumentation rule 5 requires be reused wherever the same problem recurs**, and it is imported here from [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md) step 5. Per rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8, no day has run, so the reuse is **contingent** on [S038](wiki/seminars/S038.md)'s and [S068](wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s reported outcomes, and a reported failure at either retracts it here.

Steps 3 and 5 are the day's construction and step 6 is its discriminator. **Step 5's must-be-a-subtraction clause is what makes the build a build of the right thing**: an agent asked to make a component injection-resistant will reach for a filter, because that is what the documentation and the examples are full of, and the room will have watched it do so at item 5 forty minutes earlier. Step 7's removed-action clause exists because a capability bound stated as an addition — a filter, a check, a review — is the first place of point 6 wearing the second place's clothes, and the only way to tell is to require a subtraction.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate incident in which the injected content came from a document **the substrate's own staff had written**, in good faith, three years earlier, as internal guidance. The sentence was not an attack, had a legitimate meaning where it was written, and behaved as an instruction where the assembler placed it. Attention is directed at two facts — **no adversary existed**, and **the document was in the corpus because it was the most trusted source available.**

Then the agent case: a Copilot-produced hardening change that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. Asked to protect an agent against instructions arriving in retrieved content, it enabled the content filter, added a delimiter convention, rewrote the system prompt to say that content between the delimiters must never be treated as instructions, and cited the documentation for each. It read as competent, defensible work, and **it left the tool schema untouched**, so the component could still perform every action an injected instruction would have asked for. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: three real controls were added, each was correctly implemented, each was correctly cited, and **the reviewer checked whether the change did what it said rather than whether it changed what the component could do.**

Then students, in threes: **a time you trusted an input because of where it came from.**

**Warrant.** Error management targeting points 1 and 2. The instructor case contains no wrongdoing at any step and no attacker, which is the only construction that makes the day's definition of adversary feel like a description rather than a posture. Choosing a staff-authored document is deliberate: it puts the failure inside the room's own trust boundary and makes the disclosure cheap to make. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition** and is chosen so that it fails at exactly point 6's first place while looking like all three — which is the failure the room's own item 5 predictions are about to be scored against.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **their step 6 boundary statement and their step 7 capability bound**, withholding the step 8 narration.

The partner has two tasks and both are required:

- **Construct a write path the boundary statement does not cover.**
- **State what the component would be permitted to do while holding content from that path** — reading the partner's own step 7 bound back to them.

A path the statement does not name but whose capability bound already forbids the consequence is **not a defeat**, and saying so is part of the exercise: it is point 7 demonstrated on the student's own artefact.

Per [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) specification rule 3, the outcome is recorded in the artefact, including the case where the pair could not construct an uncovered path, which is a result about the statement rather than an absence of one.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with a dual demand whose second half attacks the capability rather than the boundary. The structure forces bilateral production and it makes the pair discover the day's own ordering — that the boundary is the weaker of the two artefacts and the bound is what holds when it fails.

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- **Sealed items opened.** Item 1's marked fraction against the fraction of the printed context that is in fact externally influenceable. **This is the day's most quoted result** and the errors run one way.
- Item 4's predicted fraction against item 1's actual marking: the gap between what students marked and what they believed they had marked.

The cohort's agent outputs are also sorted into point 6's three places alongside item 3, so the room sees its own model and the agent's beside each other.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in SG302 with IS302. Per [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the write-path map with writer, cost and provenance survival for each path, including paths the student does not control; the retrospectively-writable list with its detection statement; the demonstration with its result; the statement of what the demonstration does not establish; **the shipped bound and its re-run result**; the boundary statement with its named enforcing component and the failure behaviour when enforcement is bypassed; the per-path capability bound with one named removed action and who lost it; the recorded on-call narration; **the statement of which SG302 material was used to identify infrastructure write paths**; the phase 7 outcome; **the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed** — not whether the demonstration succeeded, not the sophistication of the path chosen, and not which platform controls are configured.

**A boundary statement that does not name the enforcing component fails.** This is the day's single hard requirement, and it fails because a boundary enforced by nobody is a description of an intention.

**A capability bound with no removed action fails.** A bound expressed only as an addition is a filter, and point 6 says what filters are worth against a legitimate write path.

**A demonstration reported without a statement of what it does not establish fails.** Where an attack-success rate from platform tooling is quoted, **a rate reported without the attack set that produced it fails**, because no reference value for it is published anywhere.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — the sensitivity escape [S052](wiki/seminars/S052.md) established and [WP-048](wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 corrected. A step 5 bound whose step 3 re-run is automated is the accepted example.

**The cross-course join is a checked element**, per the [Whitepaper Standard](wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s cross-course deferral rule. Point 9 defers infrastructure-level write paths to SG302, and step 1 requires the student to name the SG302 material they used to enumerate them. Without it the deferral is unverifiable in principle, which is the failure [WP-044](wiki/whitepapers/WP-044.md) §8 recorded on this material's own predecessor day.

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md).

**Rule-6 discharge.** One obligation lands on this day under the rule and **the element exists.** [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) named this day's phase 3 as the response occasion for its step 4 schema-reduction cost, specifying that the injection cases include one that the cohort's narrowest schema still admits, selected against the reduction this cohort was least willing to make. **That is C2: the action its injected sentence asks for is a function of the [S073](wiki/seminars/S073.md) result, so the case the room receives changes with what the room was unwilling to give up.** It is not a mention; it determines the content of one of the four printed cases, and it is what makes point 7 land — a capability bound is the answer precisely because the restriction the cohort accepted does not stop C2.

**One older forward link also lands here.** **[S044](wiki/seminars/S044.md) point 3 named this day as the home for attacks specific to generative components**, in a scope statement rather than under rule 6, which did not exist when it was written. **The obligation is discharged by the whole day and specifically by point 9, which states what has now been covered and what remains outside it.** It is not a mention: the deferral's subject is the day's entire phase 4, and point 9 re-bounds the claim so that the discharge does not become a completeness assertion of its own.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions, both inside this module:

- **The item 1 exposure-estimate error** — how much of a real assembled context students identify as externally influenceable, against how much is. **Response occasion: [S077](wiki/seminars/S077.md), phase 3, whose four traces are selected against whichever write-path class this cohort most systematically failed to mark**, so that the attack material is aimed at the measured blind spot rather than at the familiar case.
- **The step 7 capability-bound quality** — how many students removed an action rather than adding a control, and which actions. **Response occasion: [S078](wiki/seminars/S078.md), phase 5 step 4, where the authorisation position must state which actions are unavailable to a request whose context carries content from an untrusted path**, and where a student who removed nothing here has nothing to state there.

**[WP-077](wiki/whitepapers/WP-077.md) §7 and [WP-078](wiki/whitepapers/WP-078.md) §7 must report whether those elements exist.**

## Perishable content in this day

**Security content is the most perishable material in this programme, and this day is the most perishable day in the module.** Specific evasions have a shelf life measured in months; the assemblers, the delimiters, the filters, the products and the models all change. The design's response is that **everything assessed here is boundary reasoning or a measurement obligation**, and no specific technique and no product configuration is on the specification.

The durable column is written as reasoning and obligation rather than as facts about named attacks, because a durable claim phrased as *this attack works* expires with the attack.

| Durable claim — reasoning and obligation | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| Anyone holding a write path into a source the assembler reads is an adversary, whatever their intent | The substrate's current corpus, tool set and orchestration summariser |
| The boundary is the point at which content becomes context, and somebody chose where that is | The assembler's current field mapping, and which content the request labels as documents |
| Ask of every source: who can write, how many hops, does provenance survive | Prompt Shields' split into a user-prompt shield and a document shield, which assumes the third |
| Data and instruction are not distinguishable in one channel; any separation was enforced before assembly | Delimiter and role conventions, and spotlighting's base-64 transformation |
| The system's own output is untrusted content on the next turn | The [S072](wiki/seminars/S072.md) summariser's current behaviour |
| A defence sits in three places and only two survive a legitimate write path | Which of the three the substrate's current filters occupy |
| Bound the capability, not the string, and state the bound per path | The component's current tool schema |
| A control whose accuracy is unpublished licenses no claim about what its silence means | The Defender alert taxonomy's blocked-versus-detected split, with no rate for either |
| **Obligation:** a demonstration establishes one path; a rate establishes the attack set that produced it | The attack-success rate reported by whichever adversarial tooling the cohort ran |
| **Obligation:** state the default you did not choose | Spotlighting off by default; content filters on by default |

- **Perishable:** every specific injection in phase 3, the substrate's current assembler and its delimiter conventions, the filters and shields the substrate runs and their configuration, the tool vendor's error strings, the agent's behaviour on each of the four cases, and every product name on this page. All are properties of one system and one vendor at one delivery, and the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md)'s entry for adversarial technique catalogues applies to all of them.
- **Local:** the printed assembled context, the four cases, the staff-authored document in the error clinic, the disposable copy used at steps 3 and 5, and the rota member present at step 8.
