# S013 · Facilitation as a Technical Skill

**Module:** [M03](/wiki/modules/M03.md) · **Quarter:** [Q2](/wiki/quarters/Q2.md) · **Complexity class:** 2
**Strands:** PE
**Platform anchor:** GitHub Copilot custom chat modes and instructions files — constraining what an agent may ask and in what order, as facilitation encoded and inspectable
**Whitepaper:** [WP-013](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-013.md)
**Retrieves from:** S004, S011
**Entry state:** either

---

## The situation

You are the most technically expert person in the room. You have been asked to run the session because of that.

Everything you know about how to be useful in a technical conversation is now working against you.

At the same time, you have a second facilitator you did not think of as one. The agent in your editor asks questions, supplies options, fills silences and corrects errors, constantly, and nobody has ever written down what it is allowed to do. **Its habits are as invisible as yours and considerably more consistent.**

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. A participant states something about their own pipeline that you know to be wrong. What do you do, and when?
2. You ask a question. Nobody answers for eight seconds. What do you do?
3. Half the room is remote. Name the specific thing that will go wrong, and the specific person whose job it is to prevent it.
4. Somebody has not spoken all morning. List three different reasons that might be true, only one of which is about them.
5. You have run the session, well, four days in a row. What is the fifth day worse at?

**Item 2 is where most engineers fail.** The instinct is to rescue the silence by offering options, and the options are the answer. Item 4 is the one the session will spend most of its time on, because two of your three reasons will be about the person and the third will be the one that matters.

## What you must produce

Two artefacts.

A **facilitation plan** for one segment of your charter's session, naming:

1. **Who is meant to speak** — not a role, a person, and the thing only they can say.
2. **What makes speaking cheap for them** — what the segment does before they are asked, so that answering is not an interpersonal risk.
3. **What you will not do** — the specific interventions you are forbidding yourself, written down before you are under pressure.
4. **The remote provision** — if anybody is remote, the named person whose only job is their experience, and what that person is doing while you are facilitating.
5. **The binding amendment** — one item of guidance that currently lives in advisory material, rewritten as a line of your plan.

A **facilitation mode**, on the assigned platform, in which:

6. The same forbidden list is expressed as constraints an agent must obey.
7. The ordering rule is stated as a rule rather than as a sequence of prompts.
8. One constraint is demonstrated to fail, and the failure is recorded.

## The material

### The expert's helpfulness is the failure mechanism

The interviewing literature is blunt about this and it is worth reading its list of ordinary conversational instincts as a list of defects: taking half the airtime, waiting for a turn to speak, sharing a related experience, smoothing over silence, supplying possible answers, and correcting an error ([research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md](/research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md)). Every one of those is good behaviour in a technical discussion between peers and damage in an elicitation.

The correction is the sharpest case because it is the one that feels most like competence. The source's instruction is explicit: do not correct a mistaken product belief or repair a workflow during data gathering, because either action shifts authority to the interviewer and may convert research into support. A genuinely helpful correction can be offered afterwards, **if it benefits the participant rather than the interviewer's need to be right.**

The supplied option is subtler and more common. A question followed by examples is not the same question; the examples become the response set. The source names the whole posture required to avoid it — treat the participant as expert and the interviewer as novice — and is clear that this is not passive: it requires preparation, full attention and freedom from avoidable distractions.

**Its limits.** A first-person practitioner synthesis. **It reports no controlled trials, no sampling framework for its cases, no measured causal effects, no validated saturation threshold and no universal staffing rule.** Its own empathy claim is supported by an analogy in a footnote rather than by a study. This is the 2013 first edition; the later edition has not been read here, so nothing from its expanded remote-research treatment is available. Its release, consent and incentive examples are jurisdiction-specific and dated.

### Silence is an instrument, and it is not a trick

The same source supplies the drill and the warning that must travel with it. Ask the shortest clear question and stop. Silence before an answer allows thinking; silence after an answer allows the next paragraph. Do not soften anxiety by adding choices or trailing examples.

Then the warning, and it is not decorative: **silence has different meanings across people and cultures.** It can create shared readiness, signal effort, expose confusion, or produce stress. The interviewer must read cues and adjust rather than apply waiting as a mechanical technique. **A facilitator who waits through every silence has replaced one habit with another**, and the second is harder to notice because it looks like discipline.

### What speaking costs, and why encouragement does not change it

Here is the mechanism underneath all of the above, and it is the reason structure beats encouragement.

People withhold what they know at work not from personality but from a rapid, largely unconscious risk calculation. The construct is psychological safety — the belief that the work environment is safe for interpersonal risk taking — and its author is emphatic about what it is not: not niceness, not comfort, not a personality trait, not another word for trust, and not lowered standards ([research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md](/research/04-professional-formation/edmondson-psychological-safety.md)). Her formulation of the last point is worth memorising: it is "about candor, about making it possible for productive disagreement and free exchange of ideas", and "nice, in short, is not synonymous with psychologically safe".

Two further pieces of that account bear directly on a facilitator. The first is the **asymmetry of voice and silence**: voice benefits the organisation, later, uncertainly; silence benefits oneself, immediately, certainly. The second is that **silence is invisible** — the moment passes and no one is the wiser except the person who held back. It follows that you will never receive feedback about the thing your session failed to elicit.

The construct lives at the level of the group rather than the person, and the same organisation contains pockets of high and low safety at once. Which means the lever is not exhortation. **Telling a room there are no wrong answers does not change what a wrong answer costs**, and the room knows that better than you do.

**The limits here are unusually important and this session will not smooth them.** The claims above rest on a trade book by the construct's originator, read in full for this repository; **the empirical paper it restates has not been read here**, and a summary written two decades later by the same author is not the paper. The book's narrative review reports no disconfirming study across the literature it surveys, which is not a state of evidence any review can establish. Its leadership framework is drawn partly from consulting experience and **no trial of it is reported anywhere.** And it contradicts itself on comfort: its introductory gloss asserts comfort twice while its own chapter denies it and names a comfort zone as a failure state.

The quantitative synthesis is what makes this worth curricular time in a programme sceptical of disposition training ([research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md](/research/04-professional-formation/frazier-psychological-safety-meta-analysis.md)). It aggregates many independent samples across individuals and groups; it **ranks** antecedents rather than listing them; it tests **incremental validity** against named alternatives — positive leader relations and work engagement — which is the sceptic's question asked properly; and it **tests rather than assumes** that the construct behaves the same way at individual and group level.

**Its limits, and they bound what may be said.** This repository's note is written from the abstract, which **reports no numerical effect sizes at all**, so no correlation, no ranking and no magnitude may be taken from it, and none is asserted here. The pooled literature is predominantly cross-sectional and self-report, so **correlation does not establish that raising safety raises anything.** And whether homology across levels was supported is not recorded either way.

### The team is part of the reasoning apparatus

Facilitation is usually taught as a solo skill. The design-research literature treats it as a staffing decision, and that reframing is the more useful one.

An interpretation session replays a field visit to a cross-functional team soon after it happens, with **defined roles that keep the session moving and make participation active**, and external representations treated as working surfaces rather than as documentation produced after thinking ([research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md)). The stated reason is the one to carry: notes and models hold the conversation where everyone can inspect and challenge it, and **they reduce dependence on memory, status and rhetorical force.** A facilitator arguing from recall is arguing from authority.

The same source insists that project management and interpersonal facilitation are part of the method rather than administrative support around it, and that field visits, interpretation, ideation, rooms, materials **and rest** all need scheduling.

**Its limits.** A method text resting on practitioner experience with no comparative experimental evidence; its role structures and timings are heuristics; and it states explicitly that observation is not neutral — the method manages subjectivity through partnership, interpretation, team review and multiple representations, and does not eliminate it.

### The wellness document is an incident record

The corpus contains guidance telling facilitators to protect sleep and meals, to avoid consecutive intensive engagements, to rotate the demanding roles between people, and to watch colleagues for signs of difficulty ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). Read it for what it is: **an incident record written in the grammar of advice.** Nobody writes those instructions prospectively.

Two properties of it are the teachable part. **Its justification has been moved onto effectiveness** — rest is defended by its contribution to delivery quality rather than as something owed to the person. And **its remedies appear nowhere in the documents that schedule the work**: not in the lifecycle, not in the timeline, not in the checklist, not in the readiness gate. **Advisory guidance inside a system of checklists is guidance that loses.**

The same source names hybrid delivery as the hardest setting, states the core risk as a two-tier experience, and requires a dedicated person whose only job is the remote participants' experience. That is a role, not an attitude, and a facilitator who is also looking after the remote participants is not looking after the remote participants.

**Its limits.** Access-controlled, white-labelled, uncheckable by any public reader. A stratum contradicting itself in many places, with no failed session, no comparison and no outcome anywhere. The facilitation material is codified experience, and it is good, and it is unwarranted.

### Facilitation encoded, and therefore inspectable

Now the platform, and its contribution is precise: **it is the only place where you can watch your own facilitation rules being obeyed and broken.**

An instructions file is a constraint applied automatically to matching work — the closest analogue to a coding standard — and a chat mode is a named configuration that fixes what an agent may do and in what order. The published agent-configuration corpus separates these artefact kinds deliberately: **instructions** as automatically applied constraints, **prompts** as user-facing entry points, **agents** as orchestrators with declared roles, and **skills** as reusable procedures ([research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/hve-core-repository.md)). Its organising claim is the durable one: **a coding agent's behaviour is an engineering artefact, and if it is an artefact it should be authored, versioned, reviewed and distributed like any other.**

Three of its declarations transfer straight into a facilitation plan. **Execution completion and acceptance outcome are different results** — a segment that ran is not a segment that worked. **An agent may not record a human's approval**, and a workflow must stop when a required human review is absent; human review is a stop condition, not a checkbox the workflow can satisfy for itself. And its own graders are honest in a way most are not: the behavioural checks **test output shape and not correctness.**

That last one is the lesson for this session. A facilitation mode can enforce that you did not supply an example. **It cannot enforce that the question was good**, and a learner who leaves believing otherwise has learned the wrong thing from a very good instrument.

**Its limits.** Read at a pinned commit at which one of its own checks was failing; perishability extreme, with paths, counts and versions moving while it was read; host and model dependence total, so every artefact is inert without a host that loads it and a result on one client is not a result about the corpus; it contains benefit vocabulary its own evidence does not support; **a grader exists and no result corpus does**; and nothing in it licenses a claim about learning, because it was not built for a cohort and no learner study exists.

## The workflow

**Encode your forbidden list as an agent constraint, then break it on purpose.**

You will use a custom chat mode and an instructions file in your editor's Copilot configuration. Everything here runs locally against a repository you control; no customer material goes anywhere near it.

**Step 1 — write the facilitation plan first, on paper.** The forbidden list comes before the file. A prohibition derived from what the tooling makes easy to express is not a prohibition, it is a feature list.

**Step 2 — create the mode and give it one job.** Not "help me facilitate". A single segment, with the person who is meant to speak named by the thing only they can say.

**Step 3 — write the constraints as prohibitions, not as preferences.** "Prefer open questions" is unenforceable and unfalsifiable. "Never append an example, an option list, or a completion to a question" can be checked by reading the transcript.

**Step 4 — state the ordering as a rule rather than as a script.** A script is a sequence you will abandon. A rule survives the abandonment.

```markdown
---
# .github/chatmodes/discovery-segment.chatmode.md
# Illustrative. Frontmatter keys and file locations for chat modes and
# instructions files change; verify against current documentation.
description: 'Elicitation for one discovery segment. Constraints, not suggestions.'
tools: ['search', 'fetch']
---

# Segment: how a deployment actually fails today

Who is meant to speak: the on-call engineer who carried the pager last quarter.
The thing only they can say: what they did at 02:00 when the pipeline stalled.

## Ordering rule

Establish that the category is active and consequential BEFORE any detail
inside it. Never ask about a tool, a threshold or a preference until a
specific past occurrence has been reconstructed.

## Prohibitions — these are constraints, not preferences

- NEVER append an example, an option list, or a sentence beginning "for
  example" to a question. Ask, then stop.
- NEVER correct a factual error in what the participant reports during the
  segment. Record it under DEFERRED and raise it after, only if the
  correction helps them rather than me.
- NEVER ask whether a category of tooling would help. That question asks
  the participant to endorse the asker's solution.
- NEVER ask more than one question per turn.
- If the participant gives a compliment, a generic promise or a feature
  request, do not accept it as an answer. Ask for the last real occurrence.

## Stop conditions

- If a question would require a person who is not present to answer it,
  STOP and report who is missing. Do not answer on their behalf.
- Human review is a stop condition. Do not record that a human approved
  anything, and do not tick a review box on anyone's behalf.

## Deferred

Maintain a DEFERRED list. Corrections, tangents and interesting-but-
irrelevant threads go here, visibly, and are raised at the end.
```

**Step 5 — run the segment against a hostile participant.** Play somebody who answers in compliments, generic future promises and feature requests. Watch which prohibition the agent breaks first. Record it.

**Step 6 — break a constraint on purpose and see whether anything notices.** Ask the agent a question in a form your own instructions forbid. Then check: did the constraint hold, did it hold silently, or did nothing happen at all? **Whichever it is, that is a fact about your host and model rather than about your file**, and it is the fact worth carrying.

**Step 7 — attack your own plan with the agent.** Give it your forbidden list and one instruction: name the three situations in which obeying this list would produce a worse session than breaking it. Then ask it which of your prohibitions is unenforceable by reading a transcript. Record what you kept, what you tightened and what you removed.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** That a prohibition has a truth value. In a room, "I didn't supply options" is a memory you have every reason to be generous about. In a transcript, it is a line of text or it is not. You will also watch the harder fact arrive: **a constraint can hold perfectly and the questions can still be bad**, which is the difference between output shape and correctness, and it is the thing a green check will never tell you.

## What good looks like

- The forbidden list contains something you are actually inclined to do. A prohibition against a thing you would never have done is not a prohibition.
- **The plan makes speaking cheap by structure rather than by encouragement.** Telling a room to feel free to interrupt does not change what interrupting costs.
- Silence is planned for as an instrument, not as an emergency — and the plan says what you will do if the silence is distress rather than thought.
- The remote provision names a person and their sole job. **A facilitator who is also looking after the remote participants is not looking after the remote participants.**
- The plan says what the segment is for in one sentence, and that sentence is about what will be learned rather than what will be covered.
- Every constraint in the mode file is checkable by reading a transcript. Anything that is not has been rewritten or removed.
- The binding amendment is a line in your plan, not a line in a separate document.

## How it goes wrong

- **The expert in the chair.** *Warning sign:* you have used the words "actually" or "just to be precise". You correct a factual error during the session and authority transfers to you; everything after that is a conversation with you rather than about them.
- **The rescued silence.** *Warning sign:* your question ended with "for example" or with a list. You fill a pause with example answers and the response set narrows to the examples.
- **The two-tier room.** *Warning sign:* nobody remote has spoken unprompted since the first ten minutes. The people in the room have a session and the people on the call have a broadcast.
- **Encouragement instead of structure.** *Warning sign:* the invitation to speak and the shape of the question disagree. You tell people there are no wrong answers, then ask one that has an obviously right one.
- **Wellness as advice.** *Warning sign:* the guidance about your capacity lives somewhere that does not schedule your time. You know you should not run five of these in a row, and the schedule was made by somebody reading a different document.
- **The mechanical wait.** *Warning sign:* you waited through a silence in which somebody was visibly struggling, because waiting is the technique. Silence is read differently by different people, and a rule applied without reading the room is a habit rather than a skill.
- **Shape mistaken for quality.** *Warning sign:* your constraints all held and you cannot say what you learned. An enforceable prohibition tells you the question had the right form. **It cannot tell you the question was worth asking.**

## Exercises

**None of these is assessed here.** One property of the plan is assessed at [S015](/wiki/seminars/S015.md): whether the prohibitions survived the assigned difficulty, or the breach was recorded by you rather than found by the assessor.

**1 · The silence drill.** In threes: one facilitates, one participates, one observes. The facilitator must leave a deliberate pause before their question and after the answer, and may not supply an option, an example or a completion. The observer records every supplied option, every interruption and every occasion the facilitator answered their own question. Rotate.

> **What to notice.** Most facilitators break the rule inside their first three questions, and almost none of them notice. **The observer's tally is the only place the failure exists** — it is invisible to the facilitator and unremarkable to the participant, which is precisely why it survives whole careers.
>
> **And carry the warning.** Silence is read differently by different people and in different cultures. The drill can teach a bad habit if you leave with "always wait" rather than "never rescue".

**2 · Classify five prohibitions by enforceability.** For each, say whether a reader of the transcript could tell that it had been broken.

- *Ask open questions.*
- *Never append an example to a question.*
- *Be curious.*
- *Never correct a factual error during the segment.*
- *Make the participant feel comfortable.*

> **Model answers.** Enforceable: the example rule and the correction rule — both are visible in the text. Unenforceable: open, curious, comfortable. **The unenforceable three are not bad advice; they are not prohibitions.** A plan made of them cannot be broken and therefore cannot be kept, and under pressure you will discover you have no list at all.

**3 · Read the wellness guidance as evidence.** Read the corpus's facilitator wellness material and write down two things about it: what its justification has been shifted onto, and where its remedies do not appear.

> **Model answer.** Its justification has been moved onto delivery effectiveness — rest is defended by its contribution to quality rather than as something owed to the person. Say what that framing makes arguable that would otherwise not be: a person can now be asked to trade their rest against a delivery outcome, and the trade has an agreed exchange rate. Its remedies appear in none of the artefacts that schedule work.
>
> **Then change one thing in your own plan so the guidance binds.** A line in your plan, not a line in a separate document. **Advisory guidance inside a system of checklists is guidance that loses**, and your plan is the checklist.

**4 · Run the mode and break it.** Using your chat mode, run the segment against a hostile participant, then deliberately issue a question in a forbidden form.

> **What to notice.** Three outcomes are possible and each teaches something different. The constraint holds and says so — you have a check. The constraint holds silently — you have a check you cannot audit. Nothing happens — you have a file, and the file is inert without a host that loads it and a model that complies. **The third case is the common one and it is not a failure of your writing.**

**5 · Go back to your own item 4.** Find the three reasons you wrote for why somebody had not spoken all morning. **Do not rewrite them yet.** Put them beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then rewrite the plan's second field.

> **Criterion.** A passing set contains at least two reasons that are conditions rather than properties of the person — who else is in the room, what was asked and of whom, what an answer would cost this person in front of these people, whether the segment has already established that being wrong is survivable. "They're shy", "they're not engaged" and "they're not technical" are all one reason wearing three coats, and all three are about them.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: presenting a correct answer beside a learner's own produced no benefit, and only the group **instructed to compare them** learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference is. **Its limits: school, university and laboratory evidence, with no adult-professional study in its reference list.**

**6 · Run your segment for ninety seconds in front of two peers**, then stop and hand them your forbidden list. Their only job is to tell you which item you broke.

> **Scoring criterion.** If you broke none, run it again with a harder participant — **a forbidden list that survives an easy room has not been tested.** If you broke one and had already noticed, say when you noticed; the gap between breaking and noticing is the thing that shrinks with practice, and it is the only part of this that improves.

## What this does not establish

Nothing here shows that facilitated sessions produce better discovery than unfacilitated ones.

The corpus offers **no comparison, no outcome and no failed session.** The facilitation material is codified experience, and it is good, and it is unwarranted. It is also access-controlled, so no public reader can check the paraphrase against its source.

The claim that people withhold what they know because of a rapid, largely unconscious calculation about interpersonal risk — rather than because of personality — rests on a trade book by a management researcher that was **read in full for this repository**. That book restates an earlier empirical paper which **has not been read here**, and the restatement is a summary written two decades later by the same author. Its narrative review reports no disconfirming study across the literature it surveys, which is not a state of evidence any review can establish, and **its leadership framework is drawn partly from consulting experience with no trial of it reported anywhere.** **Direction and mechanism only.** No claim about magnitude is made here or anywhere in this wiki.

**The quantitative synthesis is the strongest evidence in the session and it does not say what people assume.** It aggregates a large literature and tests the construct against named alternative explanations, moderators and levels of analysis — but **this repository's note is written from the abstract and reports no effect sizes at all**, so no correlation and no ranking may be quoted. The pooled evidence is predominantly cross-sectional and self-report, so **it does not establish that raising psychological safety raises anything.** Whether homology across individual and group levels was supported is not recorded either way — which matters here, because this session applies a group-level construct to a room of strangers meeting once. **That transfer is unwarranted and is design reasoning.**

The claim that silence is an instrument rather than a failure comes from a practitioner interviewing manual which states plainly that it reports no controlled comparison and no validated threshold. It also warns that silence is read differently by different people and in different cultures, and must be adjusted to rather than applied mechanically. **Carry that warning; the drill above can teach a bad habit if the warning is dropped.** The manual is the first edition; the later edition has not been read here.

**The design-research material establishes mechanism and not outcome**, resting on practitioner experience with no comparative experimental evidence, and it states explicitly that its method manages subjectivity rather than eliminating it.

**The agent-configuration corpus establishes that behaviour can be authored, versioned and checked, and nothing else.** It was read at a pinned commit at which one of its own checks was failing. Its host and model dependence is total, so a result observed on one client and model is not a result about the corpus. Its own documentation states that its behavioural checks test output shape rather than correctness, and that a gate may be advisory in one tier and authoritative in another. **A grader exists; no result corpus does.** Nothing in it licenses a claim about learning.

**No effect size is asserted anywhere in this session.** Direction and mechanism only.
