# S013 · Facilitation as a Technical Skill

**Strands:** PE
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-013](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-013.md)

**Entry state:** either

## The situation

You are the most technically expert person in the room. You have been asked to run the session because of that.

Everything you know about how to be useful in a technical conversation is now working against you.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. A participant states something about their own pipeline that you know to be wrong. What do you do, and when?
2. You ask a question. Nobody answers for eight seconds. What do you do?
3. Half the room is remote. Name the specific thing that will go wrong, and the specific person whose job it is to prevent it.
4. Somebody has not spoken all morning. List three different reasons that might be true, only one of which is about them.
5. You have run the session, well, four days in a row. What is the fifth day worse at?

**Item 2 is where most engineers fail.** The instinct is to rescue the silence by offering options, and the options are the answer.

## What you must produce

A **facilitation plan** for one segment of your charter's session. For that segment it names:

- **Who is meant to speak** — not a role, a person, and the thing only they can say.
- **What makes speaking cheap for them** — what the segment does before they are asked, so that answering is not an interpersonal risk.
- **What you will not do** — the specific interventions you are forbidding yourself, written down before you are under pressure.
- **The remote provision** — if anybody is remote, the named person whose only job is their experience, and what that person is doing while you are facilitating.

## What good looks like

- The forbidden list contains something you are actually inclined to do. A prohibition against a thing you would never have done is not a prohibition.
- **The plan makes speaking cheap by structure rather than by encouragement.** Telling a room to feel free to interrupt does not change what interrupting costs.
- Silence is planned for as an instrument, not as an emergency.
- The remote provision names a person and their sole job. **A facilitator who is also looking after the remote participants is not looking after the remote participants.**
- The plan says what the segment is for in one sentence, and that sentence is about what will be learned rather than what will be covered.

## How it goes wrong

- **The expert in the chair** — you correct a factual error during the session, and authority transfers to you. Everything after that is a conversation with you rather than about them. *Warning sign:* you have used the words "actually" or "just to be precise".
- **The rescued silence** — you fill a pause with example answers, and the response set narrows to the examples. *Warning sign:* your question ended with "for example" or with a list.
- **The two-tier room** — the people in the room have a session and the people on the call have a broadcast. *Warning sign:* nobody remote has spoken unprompted since the first ten minutes.
- **Encouragement instead of structure** — you tell people there are no wrong answers, then ask a question that has an obviously right one. *Warning sign:* the invitation to speak and the shape of the question disagree.
- **Wellness as advice** — you know you should not run five of these in a row, and the schedule was made by somebody reading a different document. *Warning sign:* the guidance about your capacity lives somewhere that does not schedule your time.

## Do it

Two exercises, in this order.

**First, the silence drill.** In threes: one facilitates, one participates, one observes. The facilitator must leave a deliberate pause before their question and after the answer, and may not supply an option, an example or a completion. The observer records every supplied option, every interruption and every occasion the facilitator answered their own question. Rotate.

**Second, the wellness reading.** The corpus contains guidance telling facilitators to protect sleep and meals, to avoid consecutive intensive engagements, to rotate the demanding roles between people, and to watch colleagues for signs of difficulty. Read it for what it is: **an incident record written in the grammar of advice.** Nobody writes those instructions prospectively.

Then notice two things about it and write them down.

- **Its justification has been moved onto effectiveness.** Rest is defended by its contribution to delivery quality rather than as something owed to the person. Say what that framing makes arguable that would otherwise not be.
- **Its remedies appear nowhere in the documents that schedule the work** — not in the lifecycle, not in the timeline, not in the checklist, not in the readiness gate. **Advisory guidance inside a system of checklists is guidance that loses.**

Now change one thing in your own facilitation plan so that the guidance binds. A line in your plan, not a line in a separate document.

## Show it

Run your segment for ninety seconds in front of two peers, then stop and hand them your forbidden list.

Their only job is to tell you which item you broke. **If you broke none, run it again with a harder participant**, because a forbidden list that survives an easy room has not been tested.

## What this does not establish

Nothing here shows that facilitated sessions produce better discovery than unfacilitated ones.

The corpus offers **no comparison, no outcome and no failed session.** The facilitation material is codified experience, and it is good, and it is unwarranted.

The claim that people withhold what they know because of a rapid, largely unconscious calculation about interpersonal risk — rather than because of personality — rests on a trade book by a management researcher that was **read in full for this repository**. That book restates an earlier empirical paper which **has not been read here**, and the restatement is a summary written two decades later by the same author. **Direction and mechanism only.** No claim about magnitude is made here or anywhere in this wiki.

The claim that silence is an instrument rather than a failure comes from a practitioner interviewing manual which states plainly that it reports no controlled comparison and no validated threshold. It also warns that silence is read differently by different people and in different cultures, and must be adjusted to rather than applied mechanically. **Carry that warning; the drill above can teach a bad habit if the warning is dropped.**
