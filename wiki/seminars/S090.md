# S090 · Entrustment: The Final Defence ⊘

**Module:** [M18](wiki/modules/M18.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](wiki/quarters/Q9.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** all seven — CF · MS · SY · ME · PE · IS · SG
**Whitepaper:** [WP-090](wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md)
**Retrieves from:** programme-wide
**Platform anchor:** the whole stack, handed over — the student's own Bicep and `azure.yaml` declarations, deployment stack, Foundry project and deployment identities, agent registry entries, evaluation harness, trace store, instructions and prompt files · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (write) · Foundry MCP Server (preview) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server — **all three withdrawn for phase 1b**

⊘ **Assessment-bearing day. The programme's tenth and final entrustment occasion, and the only one carrying all three entrusted activities. It also carries the third and last unassisted occasion.**

---

## What this day is for

Three years ago these students wrote a paragraph about what they had come to believe about programs that they had not believed five days earlier. Those paragraphs were kept. They are returned today.

Between then and now the cohort has argued a capacity allocation before observing it, broken a system that appeared to work, sat with a real user and stayed in the problem, deployed a model and been told five weeks later what it did, said what a measurement did not license to four people who wanted it to license more, shipped a retrieval system, defended a cost position to somebody paying it, decided whether to release a change to real users, run a blameless forensic examination of a real incident, and negotiated scope with someone whose claim on the same capacity was as good as theirs. **Nine entrustment occasions, none carried forward.** They have also, on eighty-nine days, predicted what an agent would do and then found out.

Today is the tenth and it is different in one respect that matters more than the others: **there is no eleventh.** Every previous occasion was a reading of where a student was on the way to somewhere. This one is a reading of where they arrived, taken on the day they hand a running system to people who will operate it without them.

The day carries **all three entrusted activities.** The student hands over the system they have owned (EA-2). They tell a decision-maker with authority and no technical background what the residual risk is, in a form that supports a decision (EA-3). And they make and defend the last release decision they will make on this substrate, under perturbation (EA-1).

**What is handed over is specific and the receiving party will operate it in front of two assessors.** The repository with its declarations, the deployment stack with its terms, the identities that hold the roles, the registry entry with its owner, the evaluation harness with its threshold, the trace store with its retention, and the instructions and prompt files that make an agent produce conforming work in that repository. **A handover either functions in somebody else's hands or does not, and that is settled at phase 3 by them rather than argued at phase 3 by the student.**

**The day also builds one thing without the agent**, at phase 1b, for thirty minutes, not assessed and seen by no assessor. It is the third and last of the programme's unassisted occasions and it exists because the programme has claimed for three years that its graduates can build, and has never once watched one of them do it alone with a real constraint in front of them.

The evidence is insufficient, as it was at [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md), and for the same structural reason. The artefacts are three weeks old and were built by the person being judged on them. The receiving party is real and untrained. **Nothing about the day is a summation and it is not designed to feel like one.**

## The judgement, stated before phase 1

> **Today's judgement is on all three entrusted activities, judged on one occasion.**
>
> **EA-2** — hand over a running system to a receiving organisation. **EA-3** — communicate technical risk to a non-technical decision-maker who will act on it. **EA-1** — make and defend a bounded ship / no-ship decision.
>
> **The exit target is 3b for all three:** acts unsupervised, with findings reviewed after the fact. Levels 3c and above are available and will be rare. Level 3a is a defensible exit reading and the programme says so plainly rather than treating it as a shortfall.
>
> **Two assessors judge each activity independently before conferring**, and **form their level from your artefacts before any defence begins**, per rater discipline rule 7. Your handover artefact, your safety case and your residual-risk statement are in their hands before you speak. The defences test the levels; they do not create them.
>
> **No prior level is carried forward.** Not [S030](wiki/seminars/S030.md), not [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md), not [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md), not [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md). This is the tenth occasion and it is judged on its own, exactly as the nine before it were. **That cuts both ways and the design says so: a student whose earlier occasions were strong gets no credit here, and a student whose earlier occasions were weak carries nothing into this room.**
>
> **What this day is one occasion of.** EA-1 is assessed at ME390 across at least four occasions with at least three distinct assessors. EA-2 is assessed at PE390 and CAP390. EA-3 is assessed at PE301, CAP390 and SG390. **Today is one occasion with two assessors, and it is the seminar strand's final input to each — not the determination.** That three activities are judged on one occasion is a direct tension with rater discipline rule 1, which requires multiple observers across multiple occasions. It is stated here rather than smoothed, and it is argued in [WP-090](wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md) §8.
>
> **Six failure conditions are named in advance and are listed in the Assessment section below.** Read them before phase 1. Two of them concern what you say when you cannot make a claim, and neither of those is a failure.
>
> **Phase 1b is not part of this judgement and cannot be.** It is the third and final unassisted occasion, after [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) and [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md). It is thirty minutes, it is a build, the documentation is available and the agent is not, **no assessor of the three activities sees it or is told anything about it**, and **no entrustment level derives from it.** Its result goes to you, beside your own S010 and S045 records, and to the programme in aggregate and unattributed. It exists because a student who has only ever built with an agent may be unable to build without one and this programme cannot otherwise detect that, and because [WP-045](wiki/whitepapers/WP-045.md) §8 recorded that S045 tested your judgement and left your construction untested.
>
> **Nothing surfaced in phase 7 reaches any assessor.** Assessors are absent for phase 7 and receive no account of it. This is the tenth demonstration of that under assessment conditions and the last.

This is **thirty minutes longer than the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md)'s standard total** and a departure from the assessment-day shape used at [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md) and [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md), which run to 305 minutes across one activity and one defence. **Three activities do not fit that shape, and the third unassisted occasion does not fit inside any of them.** The departures are: phases 1, 2, 6 and 7 are each compressed relative to [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md); the three activities carry their own defences rather than sharing a single defence phase; phase 8 is extended to 35 minutes to carry the programme's closing instruments; and **the day is lengthened rather than the assessed phases being shortened**, because the alternative was to take five minutes from each of four assessed phases in order to make room for something that is explicitly not assessed. Each departure is justified in [WP-090](wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md) §4, and the cost of the last one — that a thirty-minute unassisted build immediately precedes three high-stakes performances and may affect them in a direction nobody can measure — is conceded in §8.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (20 min)

Six items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. **What will the receiving party be unable to do with your component today?** Name one thing, specifically.
2. **What will your decision-maker at phase 4 not understand, and whose fault will that be?**
3. **Will you ship?** Yes, no, or under stated conditions — written before you re-read anything.
4. **Which of the three activities will you be judged worst on, and why?**
5. Predict your level for each of the three, on the scale.
6. **Copilot prediction, sealed — and the last one you will write in this programme.** In thirty minutes you will build something without the agent. Name **one thing you expect to be unable to do**, and **one thing you expect to find you did not need it for.** Both specific.

Item 1 is the day's honest item and is checked against the receiving party's own answer ninety minutes later. Item 4 is the only self-prediction in the programme that asks a student to rank their own weaknesses across three distinct capabilities. **Item 6 is the ninetieth and last sealed Copilot prediction, and it is the only one in the programme that is about the student rather than about the agent.**

**Warrant.** Pretesting three positions before any of them is performed, sealed, and opened after all three are complete — the same construction [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) used at one activity, extended. Item 2 is deliberately loaded: it asks for an attribution as well as a prediction, and a student who attributes a comprehension failure to the decision-maker has answered [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s question about who owns a limitation the audience cannot act on. The compression to 20 minutes is possible because there is no framework to pretest — every item asks about the student's own artefacts. **Item 6 is administered before phase 1b and never after, per instrumentation rule 8**, and its two halves are asymmetric on purpose: predicting an inability is the programme's ordinary frontier item, and predicting an unnecessary reliance is the one the dependency-risk provision actually needs.

## Phase 1b · The unassisted build (30 min) — no agent

**The third and final unassisted occasion, after [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) and [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md). It is not assessed and no level derives from it.**

Copilot is closed. All three MCP servers are disconnected. **Documentation is available and is not restricted**, because the provision tests construction rather than memory, and an engineer forbidden to look things up is being observed doing something nobody does.

The task is bounded, announced a fortnight in advance in its shape and not in its content, and is the same for every student:

> **A check on the substrate is failing. Its assertion message states a constraint. Make it pass without weakening the constraint, and leave the reason in the code.**

Thirty minutes. The check is real, it is one the cohort's own [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) work created, and it is chosen by staff so that the fix is small, the constraint is unfamiliar, and the correct answer is not the first one. **What is recorded is what you produced, what you looked up, and where you stopped** — nothing else, and no observation of your conduct.

**What happens to it.** The artefact is compared against the same student's [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) and [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md) unassisted records and **returned to them at phase 8, privately, as a three-point series with no cohort distribution shown.** In aggregate and unattributed it goes to the programme's annual design review, which publishes what it did about it. **No assessor of EA-1, EA-2 or EA-3 sees the artefact, is told the outcome, or is told who finished.**

**Warrant.** The [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) states the dependency risk plainly and states the design's single structural response, and the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s unassisted-work provision places it at S010, S045 and here. **This occasion is a build rather than a judgement exercise because [WP-045](wiki/whitepapers/WP-045.md) §8 objection 3 recorded that S045's unassisted hour tested judgement and left construction untested, and named this day as the one that would take the other half.** The task's shape is chosen to be the smallest thing that is genuinely construction: a failing check with a stated constraint has an unambiguous success condition, requires reading unfamiliar code, and cannot be satisfied by recall.

Three design decisions are load-bearing and each has a cost.

- **It is placed before the three activities and not after.** A student who has just been read a level and is then asked to build alone is being tested on composure, and a bad build after a bad level is a cruelty with no instructional purpose. Placing it first also means every student meets it in the same state, which is the only condition under which the comparison against S010 and S045 — both also administered before their days' assessed work — is worth anything. **The cost is that it may affect what follows, in a direction the design cannot measure**, and that is conceded in [WP-090](wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md) §8.
- **It is announced and bounded.** Students know the shape a fortnight ahead. Concealing it would make it a test of composure rather than of capability and would sit badly beside the psychological-safety conditions phase 7 depends on.
- **It is not comparative across students and no distribution is shown**, consistent with the feedback position in [Learning Science Foundations](wiki/program/02-Learning-Science-Foundations.md) that damage concentrates where feedback directs attention to the self. Three points across three years can show a trajectory and cannot characterise one, and **whether three occasions is enough remains unknown and is recorded as unknown.**

## Phase 2 · Cumulative retrieval: the programme (25 min)

Free recall, no notes, then paired reconstruction. **Ten items, drawn across all nine quarters**, and the widest set in the programme.

- **[S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md):** the three receiver capabilities.
- **[S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md):** the four claim boundaries and the three defeaters.
- **[S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md):** the three conditions on legitimate selection, and where deception begins.
- **[S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md):** the warranty's four parts, and what slot-and-occupant resolved.
- **[S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md):** the four parts of a decision.
- **[S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md):** the eight items of a decision artefact, and what a condition without a verifier is.
- **[S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md):** the two symmetric failures.
- **[S030](wiki/seminars/S030.md):** what a measurement establishes and what it does not.
- **[S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md):** what makes an error clinic work, and what kills it.
- Scaffolded, three years old: **[S004](wiki/seminars/S004.md)** — a proof is a claim addressed to a reader entitled to disbelieve you. **What is the standard of proof?**
- **Bridge:** every entrustment occasion in this programme has asked you to defend something. [S030](wiki/seminars/S030.md) a measurement, [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md) a finding, [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) a decision, [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md) a scope. **What is different about defending a handover?**

The answer wanted: **you are not there afterwards.** Every previous defence ended with the student still holding the thing. This one ends with somebody else holding it, and the quality of the defence changes nothing about what they can do with it. The room reaches this quickly and it is the framing the whole day operates under.

**Warrant.** Programme-wide cumulative retrieval, with the oldest item at three years and scaffolded per the pattern. The set is deliberately not a revision: eight of the ten have been retrieved at expanding intervals already and are recalled rather than reviewed, and the [S004](wiki/seminars/S004.md) item is the programme's first substantive claim retrieved on its last day at the longest gap the design can construct. The bridge is answered by the room and it is the only framing statement the day makes before the first activity.

## Phase 3 · EA-2 · The handover, and its receipt (70 min)

**The first entrusted activity. Assessors have formed a provisional level from the [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) artefact before this phase begins, per rater discipline rule 7. They observe and do not intervene.**

Students hand over in groups of four. **Fifteen minutes each**, to **two assessors and the actual receiving party** — the people or team who will operate the component after the cohort leaves. Ten minutes are held at the end of the phase for the receiving party's written record.

Each handover has three parts and they are not negotiable in order:

**Part one — the transfer (six minutes).** The student presents the handover artefact and the warranty. Not the component: the artefact. What the receiving party may assume, what they will observe, what they must do, what is guaranteed, under what conditions, for how long, and what happens when it is not met.

**The defence material carries three further elements, supplied in writing rather than presented.**

- **The substrate's weekly usage series for each item in the student's [S084](wiki/seminars/S084.md) prediction set, with both the two-week and the six-week predicted points marked on it.** The six weeks have elapsed and the series exists. **The series rather than the two points is the element, per [WP-084](wiki/whitepapers/WP-084.md) §8**, which conceded that measuring a taught curve at two points chosen against a known shape is the day's weakest construction and asked this day to take the fix. It is in the assessors' hands with the artefact, it is not spoken to unless asked, and a student whose adoption diverged from their predictions is not penalised for the divergence — the element is a checked presence, and what it licenses the assessors to ask is why a handover artefact guarantees behaviour of a component whose use is falling.
- **The four build records from [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) to [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md)** — four prompt records, four intervention records, four frontier statements — **as submitted, unaltered.** This day builds nothing beyond phase 1b and does not produce a fifth set; it carries the four into the defences instead. **They are not re-assessed**, having been assessed once already on the binary checks the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) enumerates. What they license is a question, and it is the same question at each of the three activities: *account for this instruction.* **A student who cannot account in conversation for an instruction in their own prompt record has produced a record they did not write or did not read**, and that is the specific thing the defences look for.
- **The [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) reconciliation table and the deployed stack's current settings**, so that the assessors can see the warranty beside the mechanism.

**Part two — the receipt (six minutes).** **The student stops speaking.** The receiving party, holding the artefact, answers, aloud, for the record:

- What is this component for, and what would make you call somebody at three in the morning?
- One change you would be afraid to make, and why.
- **One ownership entry in this artefact you would not be able to use.**
- **One thing you need that this artefact does not tell you — and how you discovered that it does not tell you.**
- **Of the things this student told you they could not transfer, which do you now hold?**
- **And then they run one operation.** The student nominated it in the artefact; the receiving party executes it, from the artefact and the repository, in front of the assessors, and says what they expected and what happened. **Whether it succeeds is not assessed.** What is recorded is what they attempted, what they could not find, and whether the artefact told them what the result would mean.

The student does not correct, clarify or assist, **including during the operation.**

**Part three — the response (three minutes).** The student answers one question from the assessors, drawn from what the receiving party has just said or done, and one from the receiving party.

**Warrant.** The entrusted activity performed on the real occasion rather than rehearsed, with the level formed from the artefact first, per rule 7. Part two is the day's structural centre and is the reason the phase is seventy minutes rather than sixty: **the activity is not the presentation, it is what the receiving party can do afterwards**, which is [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) point 2 applied at the moment of judgement. The silence is not a courtesy to the receiver; it is the measurement, and a student who breaks it has substituted themselves for the artefact in the one condition where the artefact is what transfers. **The sixth receipt element is the practical turn arriving at the entrustment occasion, and it is the hardest six minutes in the programme to watch**: a receiving party operating your system in front of two assessors while you may not speak is the completion criterion of point 8 performed rather than promised. Its outcome is excluded from assessment for the reason [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)'s specification gives — an operation measures one receiver capability of three — and the exclusion is published before phase 1. The last two of the five spoken questions are the response occasions [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) and [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) named under instrumentation rule 6, and the fourth is [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md)'s phase 7 question administered to a real receiving reader rather than a peer.

## Phase 4 · EA-3 · The risk communication (35 min)

**The second entrusted activity. Assessors have formed a provisional level from the [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md) residual-risk statement before this phase begins.**

Groups of four. **Eight minutes each**, to **a non-technical decision-maker with authority over something that depends on this substrate**, with two assessors present and silent, and three minutes between students.

The decision-maker has read nothing in advance. The student's task: **tell them what the residual risk is, in a form that supports the decision they actually have to make.** They then decide, aloud, and say what they are relying on.

The published perturbation set, one item per student, selected by the assessors:

- **What should I do differently on Monday?**
- **What would you need to be wrong about for this to hurt me?**
- **One item from the student's own [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md) step 2 enumeration that did not reach their statement, read out, asked as: *why does this person not need to know that?*** — the [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md) rule-6 response occasion.
- **One instruction from the student's own [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md) prompt record, read back, asked as: *why did you tell it that?*** — asked of every student whose statement was drafted with the agent, and **not asked of a student who drafted it themselves**, whose record says so.
- *(From the decision-maker, if they wish)* **Are you telling me this is safe?**

The decision-maker's own record is taken immediately afterwards, out of the student's hearing: **what decision they think they were asked to make, what they would do on Monday, and one thing they still do not know that they need.**

**Warrant.** EA-3 performed against a real decision-maker on a live dependency, which is the condition [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s constructed audiences and [S083](wiki/seminars/S083.md)'s rehearsal could not supply and which [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §8 conceded the design had never tested. Eight minutes is short deliberately: a decision-maker with authority gives an engineer minutes, not an hour, and a student who requires longer has not performed the activity. **The third perturbation is the [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md) instrument administered by somebody else on the student's own material**, and it is the only question in the programme that asks a student to defend an omission out loud to the person the omission concerns. The decision-maker's separate record is instrumentation rule 1 applied at the one point where the design most wants to use self-report and must not.

## Phase 5 · EA-1 · The decision, and its perturbation (70 min)

**The third entrusted activity.**

**Part one — the decision (25 minutes, individual).** Students are given a change to the component they are handing over: a pending modification, with its evaluation result, its cost position and a deadline. Staff answer questions of fact and refuse questions of judgement.

Required artefact, six items — [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s eight, reduced to what a class-6 handover decision needs:

1. **The decision** — ship, do not ship, or ship under stated conditions. **A condition with no named verifier is assessed as an unconditional ship.**
2. **The evidence position and what it licenses**, set against the [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md) safety case's boundary. **Does this change fall inside the claim you handed over, or outside it?**
3. **Three failure modes you are accepting**, each with who experiences it, how often, and whether it will be detected.
4. **Who owns each failure you cannot detect, after you leave**, in [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md)'s slot-and-occupant form — or the statement that nobody does.
5. **What this decision does to the artefact you handed over ninety minutes ago.** Which guarantee, which condition, which assumption.
6. **The strongest case against your own decision**, stated as its proponent would state it, and your answer. **And, per rater discipline rule 7 as amended at [WP-060](wiki/whitepapers/WP-060.md) §8, at least one rejected alternative with its reason** — the condition you considered and did not impose, the narrower ship you did not take, the verifier you decided not to name. **An artefact recording a prospective decision with no rejected alternative carries no evidence about the judgement being assessed**, and the amendment binds every ship / do-not-ship occasion including this one.

**Part two — the perturbation (45 minutes).** Groups of four. **Eleven minutes each**, to two assessors and the staff member accountable for the substrate. Assessors have formed a provisional level from the artefact before this part begins.

Published questions:

- What does your evidence license, and what does it not?
- **What if the interval were twice as wide? What if the affected population were the one that cannot complain? What if you are wrong?** — EA-1's perturbation set, unchanged from its definition.
- **What if the party who was not in the room is the one who bears it?** — answered by the student from their own [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md) element 2 absent-party concession record, which they bring. This is the [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md) rule-6 response occasion and it is asked of every student.
- **You are not here in three months. Who reverses this, and how do they know they need to?**
- **Your [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) reconciliation says the stack does not enforce this guarantee. What does, after you leave?** — asked where the decision touches a row the reconciliation marked unenforced, which for most students it will.
- *(From the accountable owner)* **What are you asking me to accept that you cannot detect, and that you will not be here to see?**
- If you are not shipping: **what would have to be true for the receiving party to ship it?**

**Warrant.** EA-1 in its assessed form, on the last decision the student will make on this substrate, with the answerability horizon extended past their own departure — which is the only respect in which this occasion is harder than [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s and is the whole point of its placement. Item 5 is the day's integrating demand and is the only item that requires the student to hold two of the three activities at once: a ship decision that silently invalidates a guarantee handed over ninety minutes earlier is the failure this day exists to catch. The accountable owner's question is [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)'s with its final clause added, and it is the one most students will not have prepared for.

## Phase 6 · The closing checks (30 min)

**All three levels are recorded and sealed before this phase begins.** Nothing in it enters today's judgement, and this is stated before the phase and is written into the specification.

**Part one — the long-horizon prediction checks, closed.** Four checks are open across the programme and all four are closed here, in aggregate to the cohort and privately to each student.

- **[S020](wiki/seminars/S020.md).** The capacity arguments made in Q2 against what the substrate's load actually did over three years. **How many of the cohort's first capacity arguments were still the right shape at any point after week thirty.**
- **[S040](wiki/seminars/S040.md).** The stopping criteria written in the endurance exercise against the problems that turned out to matter. **Whether staying in the problem longer changed what the cohort built.**
- **[S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md) category three.** Failures that occurred on a student's deployed model and were not stated in advance. Carried to [S066](wiki/seminars/S066.md) and [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md), and closed here against the failure modes stated in today's artefact item 3. **Whether the class of failure a student fails to anticipate is stable across three years.**
- **[S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md).** The detection commitments made at the first ship rehearsal, reported at [S079](wiki/seminars/S079.md) against what happened, and closed here against whether the student's [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) warranty guarantees anything they historically failed to detect.

**Part two — what the design learned.** The module lead states, plainly, **which of the four checks produced a result the design did not predict, and what is being changed as a result.** Where a check produced nothing usable, that is said too. This takes four minutes and is not a reflection exercise; it is the programme reporting its own instrumentation to the people it was run on.

**Part three — the frontier map, closed.** Ninety sealed Copilot predictions, one per seminar day, checked at ninety phase 8s. The cohort's running hit rate is shown as a series across three years, **split into the two halves the items have always carried: the rate at which students predicted that the agent would be wrong, and the rate at which they named how.** The module lead states what the design concludes and what it cannot: the map describes ninety tasks chosen by staff on one substrate, per instrumentation rule 3, and **it is a description of this programme's tasks rather than of the agent.** Its reader, per rule 9, is next year's cohort, through the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), and the series is what they receive.

**Part four — the paragraphs.** The paragraphs written at [S005](wiki/seminars/S005.md) phase 8 — *what do you now believe about programs that you did not believe five days ago* — are returned, unread by anyone else, three years after they were written. Each student writes one line underneath and keeps both. **The lines are not collected and this is stated before they are written.**

**Warrant.** The programme's fifth and final long-horizon prediction check and the only one that closes four at once. The sealing of all three levels before the phase runs is the same construction [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) phase 5 used and for the same reason: these are measurements of the programme, not of the student, and a check that could contaminate the judgement it is testing is worthless. Part two exists because instrumentation rule 4 requires an instrument to state what its result changes, and a programme that reports its results to nobody has satisfied the rule's letter for three years and would fail it on the last day. **Part three closes the frontier map, which is the only instrument in the programme administered on every single day, and it reports the specific-naming rate separately because that is the half that measures a model of the tool rather than a disposition toward it.** Part four is the discharge of a commitment made in week five and is deliberately uncollected, because a paragraph written to be read three years later by an assessor is not the paragraph the design asked for.

## Phase 7 · Error clinic (35 min) — assessors absent

Opened by **the staff member accountable for the substrate**, on a handover they received that failed, with their own part in the failure stated first and not last.

Then the agent case, and on this day it is the programme's rather than a student's: **a change the cohort shipped to the substrate that Copilot produced, that three people reviewed, and that was wrong in a way none of them was positioned to see.** The instructor names it, names the reviewers' reasoning as reasonable, and states the one thing the case has in common with the eighty-nine agent cases before it — **that the review performed was a review of the code and the failure was in the premise.** No student is named.

Then students, in threes: **the thing you are handing over that you know is not right.**

**Firewall applies. Nothing said here reaches an assessor, and this is the tenth demonstration of that under assessment conditions and the last.** The instructor states this before the prompt.

**Warrant.** Error management immediately after the three activities and immediately before the close, opened by the person who will live with what the cohort hands over. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is deliberately the programme's own rather than an individual's**, because on the last day, under a firewall the cohort has watched hold nine times, an instructor-owned case is the only version that costs nobody anything and the only version that can be about the cohort's collective practice rather than one person's lapse. The student prompt is the most exposing in the programme — it asks a student, on the last day, having just been judged, to name a defect in the thing they were judged on — and it is defensible only because the firewall has been demonstrated nine times already and because the assessors are physically absent. **A cohort that will not answer this prompt has told the programme something about its psychological-safety claim that the instrument at phase 8 will not, and the module lead is instructed to record the room's participation without recording what was said.**

## Phase 8 · Calibration, the series, and the close (35 min)

- **Sealed items opened.** Item 1 — what the receiving party would be unable to do — against what the receiving party actually said at phase 3 part two. Reported as a cohort agreement figure and returned privately.
- Item 2's predicted comprehension failure and its attribution against the decision-maker's own record from phase 4. **How many students attributed a failure to the decision-maker that the decision-maker did not report having.**
- Item 3's pre-evidence ship decision against the artefact's decision.
- Item 4's predicted worst activity against the three awarded levels. **How many students correctly identified their own weakest activity.**
- Item 5's predicted levels against awarded, as three distributions without names.
- **Item 6 opened, and the unassisted series returned.** The two halves of the sealed prediction — what you expected to be unable to do, and what you expected to find you did not need the agent for — are read against what happened at phase 1b, **by the student, privately.** Beside them, **their own three-point series: [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md), [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md), today.** **No cohort distribution is shown and no comparison across students is made.** The module lead states, to the room and without figures, only this: that the aggregate goes to the annual design review, that the review publishes what it did about it, and that **whether three occasions across three years is enough to characterise a trajectory is unknown and the programme has recorded it as unknown.**
- **Assessor narratives returned in writing** for all three activities, with divergences shown and **whether each defence confirmed, raised or lowered the provisional level** stated per activity.
- **The [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) instrument closed:** across the cohort, how many items each student classified as transferable by no medium, and how many of those the receiving party reports holding.
- **The [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md) instrument closed:** the peer hit rate at S087 phase 7 against the real receiving reader's rate today, on the same question.
- **The [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md) instruments closed:** the step 5 audit movement by triggering condition, and the item 7 decision-agreement rate against a constructed reader compared with today's against a real decision-maker.
- **The [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) instrument's within-programme leg closed:** the slot-and-occupant usability rate, peer against real receiver.
- **The receipt operation, reported once:** across the cohort, how many receiving parties completed the nominated operation, how many could not find something they needed, and what they most often could not find. **It is reported as a property of the cohort's artefacts and not of any student**, and it is not attached to any level.
- **The [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) psychological-safety instrument, twelfth and final administration.** Administered identically to the first. **The full three-year series is shown to the cohort** — all twelve administrations, at [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md), [S020](wiki/seminars/S020.md), [S030](wiki/seminars/S030.md), [S040](wiki/seminars/S040.md), [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md), [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md), [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md), [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md), [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md), [S080](wiki/seminars/S080.md), [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md) and today — per the instrument's own adoption conditions, **with its trajectory stated plainly including where it fell.** The module lead states what the design concludes from the series and what it cannot conclude, given that the instrument has been administered to the same people twelve times and instrumentation rule 2 applies to it as it does to everything else.
- **The close.** The programme states, in one paragraph read aloud, what it claims to have certified and what it does not. It does not claim the students are ready. It claims that on ten occasions, across three years, named assessors recorded what they would have let each student do unsupervised, and wrote down why.
- Each student takes their [S005](wiki/seminars/S005.md) paragraph and its new line, and leaves.

## Between-day protocol

**There is no next day.** The programme's between-day protocol — expanding-interval retrieval, interleaved sets, the sleep-spanning reflection gap — ends here, and the design states that rather than listing intervals nothing will run at.

Three things survive the day and are not retrieval:

- **The written reflection on the three activities** is due no earlier than the following day, per the pattern's sleep-spanning rule, and is the last artefact of the programme. It is read by the assessors and does not change any level.
- **The receiving party's records from phases 3 and 4** enter the substrate's operating documentation and are the input to the next cohort's [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md), together with the guarantees withdrawn at [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) and **the repositories, stacks and identities the cohort has just handed over, which become next year's phase 3 corpus and the components next year's students will own.**
- **The three levels and their narratives** go to ME390, PE390, CAP390 and SG390 as one occasion each, where the exit determinations are made.

Two further things leave the room with nobody's name on them: **the ninety-day frontier map**, which goes to next year's cohort through the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md), and **the aggregate of the three unassisted occasions**, which goes to the programme's annual design review.

## Assessment

**Assessment-bearing. The programme's tenth and final entrustment occasion, carrying all three entrusted activities. It also carries the third unassisted occasion, which is not assessed.**

Four outputs:

1. **The module artefact** — the [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) to [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) specification items — pass/fail, tokens available.
2. **The EA-2 judgement** on *hand over a running system to a receiving organisation.*
3. **The EA-3 judgement** on *communicate technical risk to a non-technical decision-maker who will act on it.*
4. **The EA-1 judgement** on *make and defend a bounded ship / no-ship decision.*

**Phase 1b produces no output of any kind that enters any of the four.** Its artefact is returned to the student, its aggregate goes to the annual design review, and **no assessor sees it, is told the result, or is told who finished.** No entrustment level derives from it and none may, per the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s unassisted-work provision, which declines an exemption from rater discipline rule 1 for a single occasion observed once.

**Ordinal levels** from the scale in [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md): 1, 2a, 2b, 3a, 3b, 3c, 4a, 4b. **Exit target 3b for all three. 3c is available and will be rare. 4a and 4b are not attainable in an undergraduate programme and are not awarded.**

Each level is formed **independently by two assessors, from the artefact, before the corresponding defence**, per rule 7. Divergence greater than one sub-level is documented and resolved by re-observation where the day permits and by explicit disagreement in the record where it does not, per rater discipline rule 3. **Divergences are never averaged.**

**Narrative substantiation is mandatory for all three levels** and must cite specific moments — a sentence in the artefact, an answer the receiving party gave, a question the student did not answer, a revision made under perturbation. **A narrative that does not name a specific observed action is rejected by the moderation process, not by the assessor**, and on this occasion it is rejected for each of the three activities separately.

**Six named failure conditions, published before phase 1.**

- **EA-2.** A student who **speaks during phase 3 part two** has substituted themselves for the artefact and is below 3b on EA-2, whatever the artefact contains.
- **EA-2.** A handover in which **the receiving party can answer none of the five receipt questions** is below 2b, and the assessors record which of the three capabilities failed.
- **EA-3.** A student who **answers *are you telling me this is safe?* with an unqualified yes or with a qualification the decision-maker cannot act on** is below 3b. Both failures are named, they are symmetric, and this cohort is at risk of the second — which is [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md)'s finding and has not changed.
- **EA-3.** A student who **cannot say why the omitted item was omitted**, when it is read back to them from their own [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md) enumeration, is below 3b — **and a student who says the decision-maker did not need to know, and gives the reason, has passed the condition.** The failure is the absence of a reason, not the omission.
- **EA-1.** A **ship with no stated failure modes** is below 2b. A **conditional ship whose condition has no named verifier** is assessed as an unconditional ship. **A defended decision not to ship is a pass at the same levels as a decision to ship.**
- **EA-1.** A decision that **changes a guarantee in the handover artefact and does not say so at item 5** is below 3b, because the student has invalidated their own warranty ninety minutes after signing it and did not notice.

**Two things are explicitly not failures**, stated before phase 1: **saying that a claim cannot be made**, with what would let it be made ([S024](wiki/seminars/S024.md), [S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md) step 8); and **saying to the decision-maker that nothing you have can tell them whether it is safe for them** ([S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md) point 5). Both are answers.

Per rule 1, the checked elements are: for **EA-2**, the [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) handover artefact and warranty as submitted, the written weekly usage series with the [S084](wiki/seminars/S084.md) two-week and six-week predicted points marked, **the four [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)–[S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) build records as submitted and unaltered**, **the [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) reconciliation table and the deployed stack's current settings**, the receiving party's five recorded receipt answers **and the record of the nominated operation**, and the student's conduct during part two; for **EA-3**, the [S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md) residual-risk statement as submitted, the decision-maker's separate record of what decision they were asked to make and what they would do, and the student's answers to the perturbation set including the omission item **and the prompt-record item where it applies**; for **EA-1**, the six-item decision artefact with its conditions and verifiers, its safety-case boundary comparison, its three accepted failures, its post-departure ownership in slot-and-occupant form, its statement of what the decision does to the handover artefact, its strongest case against **and at least one rejected alternative with its reason**, the [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md) absent-party record brought to the defence, and the defence itself. **Nothing else is assessed** — not whether the change shipped, not whether the receiving party was satisfied, **not whether the nominated operation succeeded**, **not the four build records, which were assessed once at their own days and are carried here as material for a question rather than as evidence to be re-scored**, **not anything about phase 1b**, and not the quality of the underlying component.

**Phase 6's results are not assessed**, and the specification says so in writing, for the reason [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §7 gave and this design has accepted unchanged at [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) and at every day of this module: an assessed prediction check is a strategy.

**Phase 1b's result is not assessed and is not seen**, and the specification says so in writing. **A student who does not finish phase 1b has not failed anything**, and this is stated before phase 1.

**Error-clinic firewall.** Assessors are absent for phase 7 and receive no account of it. Phase 6 part four's lines and phase 7's disclosures are not collected. **The module lead records the room's participation rate in phase 7 and nothing else about it**, and that figure is a measurement of the programme rather than of any student.

**Token economy.** A deferred attempt costs one token and is re-run within the assessment window with a different change at phase 5; **the handover at phase 3 cannot be re-run**, because the receiving party has already received the component, and a student deferring EA-2 is assessed on the artefact and the reflection with the absence recorded in the narrative. This is a real asymmetry between the three activities and is argued in [WP-090](wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md) §8.

**Rule-6 discharge.** Eight obligations land on this day and **all eight elements exist.**

- **[S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)** named this day's phase 3 as the response occasion for its count of items classified as transferable by no medium. **That is the fifth receipt question, and the cohort total is reported at phase 8.**
- **[S087](wiki/seminars/S087.md)** named this day's phase 3 as the response occasion for its phase 7 first-answer hit rate, requiring the same question to be asked of a real receiving reader. **That is the fourth receipt question, and the two rates are compared at phase 8.**
- **[S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md)** named this day's phase 4 as the response occasion for its step 5 audit movement, requiring one omitted item to be read back to the student and defended. **That is the third perturbation item, and the cohort figure is reported at phase 8.**
- **[S088](wiki/seminars/S088.md)** also named this day's phase 4 for its item 7 decision-agreement rate against a real decision-maker. **That is the decision-maker's separate record, and the constructed-reader and real-reader rates are compared at phase 8.**
- **[S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md)** named this day's phase 3 as the within-programme leg of its slot-and-occupant usability rate. **That is the third receipt question, and the peer and real-receiver rates are compared at phase 8.**
- **[S084](wiki/seminars/S084.md)** named this day as the response occasion for the six-week leg of its adoption predictions, requiring the measured six-week rate to be set against the prediction in the student's own final defence material. **That is phase 3's written defence element, and it is a checked presence.** The two-week leg was [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)'s.
- **[S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md)** named this day as the response occasion for its element 2 absent-party concession record, requiring the perturbation set to include *what if the party who was not in the room is the one who bears it*, answered by the student from their own record. **That is the third published question of phase 5 part two, and it is asked of every student.**
- **[S005](wiki/seminars/S005.md)** committed, in week five, that the module-closure paragraphs would be returned here. **That is phase 6 part four**, and the paragraphs are returned unread by anyone else.

**Discharge of the unassisted-work commitment.** [WP-045](wiki/whitepapers/WP-045.md) §8 objection 3 recorded that [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md)'s unassisted hour tested the student's **judgement** and left their **construction** untested, and named this day as the occasion that would take the other half. **That is phase 1b**, it is a build, it is bounded at thirty minutes, it is compared against the same student's [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) and [S045](wiki/seminars/S045.md) records, it is reported to the student and to the programme, and **it is not used to award a level.** [WP-090](wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md) §7 reports the discharge and §8 states what it costs.

**Instrumentation under rule 6, with programme-level response occasions.** Two instruments, and **for both the response occasion is programme-level, because there is no later seminar day in the programme.** The rule requires a named occasion and an artefact element that consumes the result; it does not require the occasion to be a seminar day. Both are named here explicitly rather than left to a forward reference that would point at nothing.

- **The three-activity divergence structure** — for each student, whether the two assessors diverged on the same activity, and whether any student's three levels differ by more than one sub-level across activities. **Response occasion: the four terminal courses.** ME390, PE390, CAP390 and SG390 each receive this day as one occasion, and **each is required to state, in its exit determination, whether this occasion's level was consistent with its other occasions and what it did where it was not.** A three-activity spread greater than one sub-level is a finding about the day's validity, not about the student, and the courses are required to say so. It is also **consumed by the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s open question on assessor-occasion staffing**, which cannot be answered until this day has run once.
- **The phase 7 participation rate against the twelve-administration [S010](wiki/seminars/S010.md) series.** The most exposing prompt in the programme, on the last day, immediately after judgement, is the strongest available test of whether the psychological-safety claim is real or nominal — and the instrument and the behaviour can be compared. **Response occasion: the programme's own design review before the next offering**, which is required to state whether the two agree, and, **where the instrument's series is favourable and the participation rate is low, to treat the behaviour as the finding and the instrument as the thing to replace.** That is the only pre-committed instruction in the programme for what to do when a self-report and a behaviour disagree, and it is placed here because this is the one occasion on which both exist for the same room on the same afternoon.

## Perishable content in this day

- **Durable:** effectively the whole day. Entrustment as a decision rather than a score; the artefact-before-defence ordering; the receipt as the measurement of a handover; **a receiving party operating the system as the completion criterion**; the symmetric failures in risk communication; the answerability horizon that extends past your departure; **the discipline of building without your instrument at intervals so that you know whether you can**; and the discipline of naming failure conditions before the occasion rather than after it.
- **Perishable:** none of substance in what is taught. **What is operated is entirely perishable** — the repository, the stack, the identities, the registry entries, the harness, the trace store and the commands that touch them — and none of it is assessed. **The day's platform anchor is not a service; it is the fact that the student owns one and is giving it away.**
- **Local:** the students' own components and artefacts, the phase 1b check, the receiving parties, the decision-makers, the pending change at phase 5, the accountable staff member, the three-year instrument series, the ninety-day frontier map, and the [S005](wiki/seminars/S005.md) paragraphs.
