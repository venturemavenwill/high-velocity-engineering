# S090 · Entrustment: The Final Defence ⊘

**Module:** [M18](/wiki/modules/M18.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-090](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md)

**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

This is the last occasion. Ninety sessions ago you were asked what would have to be true for the right answer to be *do not build this*, and most people could not answer it.

Today you are asked the version of that question that is about you: **what should you not yet be trusted to do without supervision, and what did somebody observe that shows it?**

You lodge your answer before you hear anything. The assessors have already formed their own levels, independently, from across the whole programme. Neither side sees the other until both are on the table. **The occasion is the comparison.**

Answer these in writing before it opens. They are lodged and not revised.

1. Name three activities from this programme you should not yet perform unsupervised. For each, the specific occasion that shows it.
2. Name one you should. The same requirement applies, and it is harder.
3. Where in this programme were you most confident and most wrong? What happened between those two facts?
4. What would somebody who worked alongside you say about item 1 that you have not said?
5. What did this programme not teach you that you will need, and how will you find out you needed it?

**Item 2 is where this occasion is decided.** Naming your own limits is socially cheap and most people will do it fluently. Naming something you can be trusted with, and defending it against an assessor's contrary reading, is the act.

## What you must produce

A **forward brief**: a written statement, addressed to whoever supervises you next, of what you can be trusted with, what you cannot, and the observation supporting each line.

It is not a reflection. It has the shape of something a supervisor could act on tomorrow — activities named at the granularity of a task somebody is assigned, each with a supervision position and a reason that is an observation rather than a feeling.

Then a defence of it, live, after the assessors' levels are revealed alongside your own.

## What good looks like

- Every line rests on a specific occasion with a date and a witness. **A limit you inferred about yourself is a hypothesis; a limit somebody watched you hit is evidence.**
- The brief contains at least one activity you claim unsupervised, and you hold it when an assessor reads the evidence differently.
- **Divergence is engaged rather than absorbed.** Where the assessors placed you higher than you placed yourself, you say why you think they are wrong, if you do. Deferring to a higher reading is the same failure as clinging to one.
- Item 3 is answered with a specific episode, not a disposition. *I can be overconfident* is a personality claim. *At [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) I defended a change I could not explain, and I did not notice until somebody asked me the second question* is a finding.
- The brief is usable by somebody who has never met you and will not read anything else about you.
- **Nothing in it is written to be admired.** A limit chosen for how it sounds is detectable and is read as an answer to item 4 that you did not give.

## How it goes wrong

Five failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **Modesty performed** — the limits are real, safe and cost nothing to admit. *Warning sign:* every item in your brief is something the programme already told you it was going to be hard.
- **The unclaimed competence** — nothing is claimed unsupervised, because claiming is exposed. *Warning sign:* your brief has no line an assessor could disagree with.
- **Collapse on divergence** — the assessors read you differently and you immediately adopt their reading. *Warning sign:* your revision introduced no new information. This is the same defect as conceding a defensible position at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), arriving in the one place it is about you rather than about a system.
- **The disposition instead of the episode** — limits are named as traits. *Warning sign:* your brief would be true of you before this programme started.
- **The brief nobody could use** — accurate, honest, and pitched at a level of generality no supervisor could act on. *Warning sign:* none of your lines names an activity somebody could be assigned.

## Do it

Work from your own record across the whole programme — the qualification position from [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md), the entrustment occasions at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md) and [S085](/wiki/seminars/S085.md), the limits register from [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md), and everything in between.

**Build the brief from occasions, not from topics.** Walking the module list and rating yourself against it produces a self-assessment; walking your own record and finding the moments somebody saw something produces a brief. The two look similar on the page and only one of them survives item 4.

Then lodge it. **You do not see the assessors' levels until yours is lodged**, and they do not see yours until theirs are recorded. That order is the instrument and it is not negotiable for anybody's convenience.

## Show it

Both readings go on the table at once.

You speak first, for a short time, on one thing only: **where the two differ, and what you make of it.** Not a summary of your brief, which has been read, and not a defence of the programme's opinion of you.

Then one question, fixed and published in advance: **what is the first thing you will get wrong, and who will notice?**

## Assessment

**This is an entrustment decision, not a specification check.** There is no checklist here and nothing in this section is one. What is recorded is an ordinal supervision level with a mandatory narrative, against a single judgement:

> **Can this learner be trusted to judge the limits of their own competence, in a form somebody else can act on?**

**How the level is formed.** From accumulated observation across the whole programme, by more than one assessor, on more than one occasion, with each assessor recording level and narrative independently before any discussion and before seeing the learner's brief. The comparison tests the reading; it does not create it. Divergence between assessors of more than one sub-level is investigated rather than averaged. See [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) for the scale and the rater rules.

**What the assessors are reading for**, none of which is a threshold to be met.

- Fit between the learner's brief and the accumulated record, **in both directions.** Understating and overstating are the same defect and are read identically. This is the judgement the whole occasion turns on.
- Whether each line rests on an observation of an act rather than on a disposition.
- Whether the learner claimed at least one activity unsupervised, and held it or gave it up for a reason.
- Whether divergence was engaged with rather than deferred to.
- Whether the brief would be usable by a supervisor who knew nothing else about them.

**A narrative is mandatory and names a specific observed action.** A level recorded without one is rejected by moderation rather than by the assessor.

**What may not enter the judgement.** Anything the learner disclosed about their own error, anywhere in the programme, in a debrief or a report designed to elicit it. **That firewall has been in force since [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) and it does not lapse on the last day.** A learner whose brief names the moment they got something badly wrong has produced the occasion's most useful evidence, and a programme that penalises that will not receive it from the next cohort.

**And what the level does not record.** Whether the learner is competent. Whether they will perform any of this in a real engagement. Whether the programme improved them. **It records that this learner, observed on these occasions, described the limits of their own competence in a way that matched what people watching them saw.**

## What this does not establish

**This is the closing act of the programme and it is a statement of its own limits.**

**Nothing in this programme is evidence that any of it works.** Not the shape of the arc, not the seven movements, not the entrustment occasions, not the four evidence classes, not one session in ninety.

The practice corpus it is built on **reports no outcome, no evaluation, no comparison and no measurement of the standard it proposes.** No failure is recorded anywhere in it; every case succeeds and the single failure narrative is a synthetic composite that is fictional by its own admission. No exclusion criteria exist. No cost side appears — every claim in it is a gross benefit, with staffing, compute, the cost of the discovery activity itself and opportunity cost all absent. No defect, incident, security-finding or maintainability data appears, and no post-deployment record for any case. **No sustainment evidence appears at all**: whether any delivered system survived, was maintained by its customer, or was still running later is not reported once. Not one reference, study or citation appears anywhere in it.

The change-management sources are commercially interested practitioner accounts with no independent evaluation, licensing direction and mechanism only. The published engineering handbook is a working standard, not a study, and offers no comparative evidence that its practices outperform alternatives.

**And this programme's own predictions are unmeasured, because no cohort has run.** Every paper in this wiki closes with predictions naming an instrument, and not one of those instruments has been applied to anybody. The design's own position, stated where it is least convenient, is that most of them never will be.

So what you have been assessed on is this: **whether you can hold a claim and its warrant apart, in a practice that mostly does not, using an apparatus that has never been shown to produce that ability in anybody.** The apparatus is an argument. You are the first people it has been made to.

**If any of it turns out to be wrong, the place to say so is [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md)**, and the obligation to say so is the last thing this programme asks of you.
