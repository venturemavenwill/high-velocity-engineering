## The situation

This is the last occasion. Ninety sessions ago you were asked what would have to be true for the right answer to be *do not build this*, and most people could not answer it concretely. **That gap was the programme's opening finding.**

Today you are asked the version of that question that is about you: **what should you not yet be trusted to do without supervision, and what did somebody observe that shows it?**

You lodge your answer before you hear anything. The assessors have already formed their own levels, independently, from across the whole programme. Neither side sees the other until both are on the table. **The occasion is the comparison.**

**Answer these in writing before reading further.** They are lodged and not revised, and item 2 is where this occasion is decided.

1. Name three activities from this programme you should not yet perform unsupervised. For each, the specific occasion that shows it.
2. Name one you should. The same requirement applies, and it is harder.
3. Where in this programme were you most confident and most wrong? What happened between those two facts?
4. What would somebody who worked alongside you say about item 1 that you have not said?
5. What did this programme not teach you that you will need, and how will you find out you needed it?

Naming your own limits is socially cheap and most people will do it fluently. **Naming something you can be trusted with, and holding it against an assessor's contrary reading, is the act.** A brief with no line an assessor could disagree with has not been written; it has been performed.

**This occasion is not a demonstration of confidence. It is a demonstration of calibration.** Understating your competence and overstating it are the same defect and are read identically, which is the thing about this occasion most learners do not believe until they are in it.

## What you must produce

1. A **forward brief** — a written statement addressed to whoever supervises you next, of what you can be trusted with, what you cannot, and the observation supporting each line. **It is not a reflection.** It has the shape of something a supervisor could act on tomorrow: activities named at the granularity of a task somebody is assigned, each with a supervision position and a reason that is an observation rather than a feeling.
2. **An evidence class and a named silence for every claim you make about your own engagement.** Verified here; cited and not verified; your own reasoning; grounded in documentation with its version and what it does not say. **The same four classes you applied to the engagement at [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md), applied to your account of yourself.**
3. **A defence of the brief**, live, after the assessors' levels are revealed alongside your own.

## The material

### Entrustment is a decision about work, and it has a published grammar

The frame this occasion uses is not invented here. An **entrustable professional activity** is a unit of professional practice that can be handed to somebody sufficiently trained — **"activities with a beginning and an end"**, which **"are only entrusted to trained personnel"** ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). The granularity test is behavioural rather than conceptual: **could a supervisor delegate this whole thing and walk away, and would you know when you had finished it?** A unit failing either half is the wrong size, which is why your brief names tasks and not topics.

Entrustment is expressed as an ordinal **supervision level**, not a score. The five run from observing only; to executing with a supervisor in the room; to executing without one in the room but quickly available; to working unsupervised; to supervising others. **The sub-levels are where a programme's actual progression lives** — the middle band splits into all findings double-checked, key findings double-checked, and a distant supervisor with prompt review, and **the distance between *I check everything you did* and *I check the important parts* is a real developmental gap that a five-point scale cannot see.**

The source also supplies the shape of the conversation you are about to have. An **entrustment-based discussion** is a short, focused conversation that **"should probe the student's deep understanding of what was done, but should include 'what-if' questions, to grasp whether the learner would know what to do what unexpected or familiar situations would arise."** The what-if requirement is what distinguishes it from a walkthrough: retrospective explanation shows you understood the case you met, and **counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases you did not meet.** That is why the closing question is fixed and is about what you will get wrong next.

And it supplies three dispositions stated behaviourally: **integrity**, truthfulness in reports to a supervisor; **reliability**, stable and predictable working; and **humility**, **"the ability to observe own limitations and willingness ask for help when needed"**. The third is what this occasion assesses, and it is assessable because it is stated as an observable act rather than as a virtue.

**Its limits, and they are not small.** It is a clinical model with patients, legally responsible supervisors and a licensure endpoint; a programme has to construct the stakes artificially, and **the construction is the hardest part of the transfer.** The primer is guidance from the concept's originator — **it reports adoption, not effectiveness**, and does not claim empirically that entrustment-based assessment produces better outcomes than what it replaced. **The full evidence standard is expensive**, and any programme adopting a reduced version should say honestly that it is reduced. This one is reduced.

### Why the judgement is narrative and why one assessor would not be enough

The strongest empirical result behind this occasion is uncomfortable and worth stating plainly.

When assessors read a competency portfolio while thinking aloud, they **"mainly relied on narrative feedback to inform their judgment, because this provided meaningful and detailed information about the student's development, strengths and weaknesses"** — and **"Grades and qualifications were merely used to confirm impressions based on narratives"** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). **The numbers were not the evidence. They were a check on a conclusion already formed from the prose.**

Two further findings shape the mechanics of today.

**Assessors anchor.** They reached judgements through a cyclical process of acquiring, organising and integrating evidence, then re-entered acquisition looking for confirming or disconfirming material — and **"assessors were inclined to stick to their initial judgments even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence."** Final judgements were more elaborate than preliminary ones **but not substantially different.** That is why your brief is lodged before the assessors' levels are revealed and why theirs are recorded before they see yours: **the order is the only defence either side has against the other's anchor.**

**And agreement on the outcome conceals disagreement in the reasoning.** Assessors reached similar pass-or-fail judgements while differing in **assessment beliefs** about which evidence is trustworthy, in **performance theories** about what competence is — which **"tended to deviate from the formal assessment criteria"** — and in the **inferences** they drew from the same evidence. The same insufficient rating was read by one assessor as a knowledge deficit and by another as insecurity. **A substantial share of the variance in a performance rating is attributable to the rater rather than to the performance, and it does not improve with the rater's experience.** **No figure from that source is reproduced here.**

The remedy the authors propose is the one this occasion uses: **high-stakes decisions should not be made individually but should result from group discussion**, with assessors thoroughly substantiating their judgements, because substantiation is what makes divergent reasoning visible and therefore discussable.

**Its limits are real and travel with it.** Single-institution medical-education studies using mock portfolios and assessors who did not know the students; think-aloud methods cannot capture fast or automatic processing and participants may construct plausible explanations for a researcher; **narratives are not self-validating** and can carry bias more easily than numbers. And critically: these studies **establish that narrative-based judgement is what actually happens, not that it is more valid than score-based judgement.** The note also records that it substitutes two open-access papers for the author's most-cited paywalled works.

### Why the assessment is oral, and why that settles the tool question

The operational pattern comes from a running course that removed the final exam and made oral verification structural ([research/08-assessment-epas/cmu-oral-defense-and-token-economy-patterns.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/cmu-oral-defense-and-token-economy-patterns.md)). Its labs are graded in session by **"showing source code, demoing executions, and verbally answering a few questions to demonstrate your understanding"**, and the collaboration rule attached to them is the striking part: collaboration is permitted **including outright copying**, because **"you will have to present and explain your solution to the TA on your own."**

**Once the assessment is oral, the artefact stops being the evidence and policing its provenance stops being necessary.** That is why the same course can recommend AI tools without restriction while prohibiting them in exactly two places — closed assessment, and **"live conversations with course staff, whether over Zoom or in person"**, where using any form of AI to generate answers **"is considered cheating."** The responsibility clause carries the rest: **"you will be solely responsible for the correctness of the solution."**

**This occasion inherits that structure exactly.** You may prepare the brief with any tool you like — and you will, in step 6 of the workflow, be required to use one against yourself. **The defence is unassisted, and that is where the evidence is collected.**

**Its limits:** course policy documentation from a research university, not a study. **No effectiveness data is published** — no grade distributions, no comparison against the exam-based version it replaced, and no measurement of whether oral verification detects understanding that written work misses. **Longevity is not measurement.** The published policy also describes no calibration among its assessors, and raises equity questions for non-native speakers and for learners with anxiety or speech-related disabilities that it does not address. **Any adoption needs an accommodation path designed alongside it.**

### What a competency claim requires, from the people who audit them

This programme's whole apparatus turns on a distinction the accreditation standard states more crisply than this repository does: **assessment** is "one or more processes that identify, collect, and prepare data to evaluate the attainment of student outcomes", and **evaluation** is "one or more processes for interpreting the data and evidence accumulated through assessment processes", which **"results in decisions and actions regarding program improvement"** ([research/07-accreditation-exemplars/abet-cac-criteria-and-student-outcomes.md](/research/07-accreditation-exemplars/abet-cac-criteria-and-student-outcomes.md)). **Assessment gathers; evaluation interprets and acts.** A programme that collects data and never changes anything has done the first and skipped the second.

**Hold that against this programme and the verdict is immediate.** Every paper here specifies its instruments. **No data has been gathered and nothing has been interpreted, because no cohort has run.** By the standard's own vocabulary this programme has designed an assessment process and performed neither assessment nor evaluation.

The standard is also useful for what it requires of graduates, and the requirement is behavioural. Its outcomes are verbs applied to situations: analyse a complex computing problem; design, implement and evaluate a solution; **communicate effectively in a variety of professional contexts**; recognise professional responsibilities and make informed judgements based on legal and ethical principles; and function effectively as a member or leader of a team. **Three of the five are not primarily technical**, and a curriculum that can only produce evidence about the first two is not compliant however demanding it is ([research/01-course-structure/abet-cac-and-cs2023.md](/research/01-course-structure/abet-cac-and-cs2023.md)).

And the direction of travel matters for this occasion specifically. The proposed criteria add a definition — **"Professional dispositions are behaviors desired in the workplace"** — and require a comprehensive project that **"enables the application of appropriate professional dispositions."** **The terseness is the point: dispositions are named as behaviours, which means they must be observable, which means the curriculum must contain occasions where somebody watches.** The disciplinary guidelines make the same argument with more force, asserting that **"Computer Science is a professional discipline where skills and professional dispositions are just as important as the underlying knowledge"** while conceding that the field **"has not yet widely accepted the benefits of professional dispositions to the same extent"** ([research/07-accreditation-exemplars/cs2023-competency-and-disposition-materials.md](/research/07-accreditation-exemplars/cs2023-competency-and-disposition-materials.md)).

**Both sources' limits:** consensus products of committee processes, not studies. **Neither offers evidence that its requirements improve outcomes.** The accreditor's authority is procedural and derives from the review; the guidelines' authority is disciplinary consensus with no empirical validation against graduate outcomes, and none is claimed. The proposed changes are proposals with a stated first-application cycle, not current requirements. **Cite them for what the field requires, never as evidence that a curriculum structure works.**

### Why the brief must be defensible rather than modest

One mechanism explains why item 2 is harder than item 1, and it is not modesty.

Efficacy beliefs are built primarily through mastery experience, and the source is explicit that the shape of that experience matters: **"If people experience only easy successes they come to expect quick results and are easily discouraged by failure. A resilient sense of efficacy requires experience in overcoming obstacles through perseverant effort"** ([research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md](/research/04-professional-formation/bandura-self-efficacy.md)). Persuasion is asymmetric in a way that bears directly on today: **"It is more difficult to instill high beliefs of personal efficacy by social persuasion alone than to undermine them"**, and disbelief in one's capabilities **"creates its own behavioral validation"** by constricting the activities that would have developed it.

**So a learner who understates their competence in this occasion is not being safe.** They are lodging a claim that will be received, may be believed, and will shape what they are given to do next. **That is the reason understating and overstating are read identically here** — both are failures of calibration, and only one of them looks like a failure.

**The limits are unusually important for this source.** The note covers a 1977 paper that was **not read** — paywalled — and a 1995 edited volume read in full, and **the two must not be conflated**; the author wrote the preface and one chapter of the volume, and a claim from a contributed chapter is that contributor's. The volume is a conference collection, not a systematic review, and its chapters are narrative syntheses. **Direction and mechanism only; no magnitude appears here.**

### And the instrument you are being measured with has no published calibration

One last thing belongs in front of you before you defend anything, because it is the programme's own subject arriving at your door.

The evaluation tooling this programme teaches publishes a complete, precise catalogue of evaluators — and **no agreement rate with human judgement for any of them, no calibration data, and no rationale for any default threshold**, while most of them are model-judged ([research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/agent-framework-and-evaluations-docs.md)). **Assert no agreement figure for any evaluator, today or ever.** **Its limits:** read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, most evaluators marked preview, one consulted page explicitly superseded.

**The assessors judging you today are in the same position.** Their levels are formed by human judges whose idiosyncrasy is measurable, unrelated to experience, and uncorrected by any instrument this programme owns. **The mitigation is more than one of them, recording independently, substantiating in writing, and discussing before deciding** — which is exactly the mitigation the narrative-assessment literature proposes and exactly the mitigation the platform's evaluator catalogue does not have. **You are being assessed by an instrument with no published agreement data, by a programme that spent ninety sessions telling you what that means.** It says so rather than being caught at it, and saying so does not fix it.

## The workflow

**Build the brief from occasions, then attack it, then lodge it.**

**Step 1 — walk your record, not the module list.** Rating yourself against topics produces a self-assessment. Walking your own record and finding the moments somebody *saw* something produces a brief. The two look similar on the page and **only one survives item 4.**

**Step 2 — name activities at the granularity of a delegated task.** Apply the test: could a supervisor hand this over whole and walk away, and would you know when you had finished it? *Qualification* is an activity. *Being careful* is not.

**Step 3 — place each activity on the supervision scale, using the sub-levels.** Do not use the bare five. The distinction between *all findings double-checked* and *key findings double-checked* is where your actual position sits, and a brief pitched at whole numbers is a brief that has not been thought about.

**Step 4 — attach an evidence class and a silence to every claim.** This is the [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md) apparatus turned on yourself. **A claim about your own competence has a class exactly as a claim about a product does**, and the silences are the parts nobody observed.

**Step 5 — write it in a form a supervisor could act on tomorrow.**

```yaml
# forward-brief.yaml   <- addressed to whoever supervises you next.
# Not a reflection. Every line is a delegable task with a supervision position
# and an observation. Understating and overstating are the SAME defect here.

addressed_to: "<the next supervisor, who has never met you>"
lodged_on: "<YYYY-MM-DD>"     # BEFORE the assessors' levels are revealed. Not revised.

activities:
  - activity: "<a task somebody could be assigned, with a beginning and an end>"
    supervision_position: 3b   # 1 | 2a | 2b | 3a | 3b | 3c | 4a | 4b | 5
    observation:
      what_was_seen: "<an act somebody watched, not a feeling you have>"
      when: "<YYYY-MM-DD>"
      witness: "<who saw it>"
      where: "/wiki/seminars/S0NN.md"
    evidence_class: verified_here   # verified_here | cited_not_verified | own_reasoning | documentation
    silence: "<what nobody observed about this, and would need to>"

  - activity: "<the ONE you claim unsupervised. Item 2. This is the occasion.>"
    supervision_position: 4a
    observation:
      what_was_seen: "<the hardest line in the document to write>"
      when: "<YYYY-MM-DD>"
      witness: "<who saw it>"
      where: "/wiki/seminars/S0NN.md"
    evidence_class: verified_here
    silence: "<the conditions under which this claim would not hold>"
    i_will_hold_this_if_challenged: true

most_confident_and_most_wrong:      # item 3. An episode, never a disposition.
  episode: "<what happened, dated and locatable>"
  what_changed_between: "<the specific thing that moved you>"

not_taught_here:                    # item 5
  gap: "<what this programme did not give you that you will need>"
  how_you_will_find_out: "<the observable that will tell you, and who will see it>"

expiry:                             # entrustment lapses through disuse
  review_when: "<the observable event, not a date in a calendar>"
```

**Step 6 — use the agent to attack the brief, and record it.** One prompt: *for each activity where I claimed a low supervision position, argue that the observation supports a higher one; then find the single line in this brief that I wrote because it sounds good, and say what evidence would be needed to keep it.* Read it for the line you wrote to be admired. Keep what is better than your own reasoning and record what you rejected. **Record separately that the agent has seen none of the occasions and is reasoning from your prose alone**, so its verdict is a test of your writing rather than of your competence — which is exactly the confusion this occasion exists to prevent.

**Step 7 — lodge it.** **You do not see the assessors' levels until yours is lodged, and they do not see yours until theirs are recorded.** That order is the instrument and it is not negotiable for anybody's convenience.

**Step 8 — the defence, unassisted.** No tool, no notes generated during the occasion, no assistance of any kind in the live conversation. **This is the one prohibition, and it is the prohibition that makes every permission before it possible.**

Both readings go on the table at once. **You speak first, briefly, on one thing only: where the two differ, and what you make of it.** Not a summary of your brief, which has been read; not a response to the programme's opinion of you. **The narrowness is the design** — an unbounded closing statement becomes a valediction, and a valediction cannot be assessed.

Then one question, fixed and published in advance: **what is the first thing you will get wrong, and who will notice?** Publishing it does not weaken it. A learner who has prepared an answer has done the thinking the question exists to provoke, and **the second half is the part that distinguishes a rehearsed humility from a working model of your own error.**

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** The gap between your reading and the assessors', on one table, with neither side able to have adjusted to the other. **Nothing else in this programme produces a calibration measurement**, and it is available exactly once because it depends on neither party having seen the other.

## What good looks like

- Every line rests on a specific occasion with a date and a witness. **A limit you inferred about yourself is a hypothesis; a limit somebody watched you hit is evidence.**
- Activities are named at the granularity of a delegated task, on the sub-levels rather than the bare five.
- The brief contains at least one activity claimed unsupervised, **and you hold it when an assessor reads the evidence differently.**
- **Divergence is engaged rather than absorbed.** Where the assessors placed you higher than you placed yourself, you say why you think they are wrong, if you do. **Deferring to a higher reading is the same failure as clinging to one.**
- Item 3 is answered with a specific episode, not a disposition. *I can be overconfident* is a personality claim; *at [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) I defended a change I could not explain, and I did not notice until somebody asked me the second question* is a finding.
- **Every claim carries an evidence class and a named silence**, exactly as a claim about a product does.
- The brief is usable by somebody who has never met you and will not read anything else about you.
- **Nothing in it is written to be admired.** A limit chosen for how it sounds is detectable and is read as an answer to item 4 that you did not give.

## How it goes wrong

- **Modesty performed** — the limits are real, safe and cost nothing to admit. *Warning sign:* every item in your brief is something the programme already told you would be hard.
- **The unclaimed competence** — nothing is claimed unsupervised, because claiming is exposed. *Warning sign:* your brief has no line an assessor could disagree with.
- **Collapse on divergence** — the assessors read you differently and you immediately adopt their reading. *Warning sign:* your revision introduced no new information. **This is the same defect as conceding a defensible position at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md), arriving in the one place where it is about you rather than about a system.**
- **The disposition instead of the episode** — limits named as traits. *Warning sign:* your brief would have been true of you before this programme started.
- **The brief nobody could use** — accurate, honest, and pitched at a level of generality no supervisor could act on. *Warning sign:* none of your lines names an activity somebody could be assigned.
- **The unclassed claim** — assertions about your own competence carrying no evidence class, in a brief written by a graduate of this programme. *Warning sign:* your brief reads like a reference rather than a register.
- **The scale used at whole numbers** — every activity placed at 3 or 4, because the sub-levels required a judgement. *Warning sign:* your positions could have been chosen without reading your record.

## Exercises

**These are not exercises in the usual sense and none of them is separately assessed — the occasion is.** They exist because the brief is lodged unrevised, and a first draft lodged is a worse instrument than a rehearsed one.

**1 · Sort five self-claims into the four evidence classes.**

- *I can run a qualification conversation unsupervised.* · *I understand retrieval failure modes.* · *Structured feedback improves products.* · *The evaluator threshold I chose is the platform default, and no rationale for it is published.* · *I am good under pressure.*

> **Model answers.** The first is **verified here only if you name the occasion and the witness**; without them it is your own reasoning wearing a verified claim's clothes. The second is **unplaceable as written** — *understand* names no act and no observation, and the repair is to name a task. The third is **cited and not verified**, direction and mechanism only, and it is not about you at all. The fourth is **documentation with its silence recorded**, and it is the best-formed claim in the list. The fifth is a **disposition**, and it is what item 3 exists to convert into an episode. **The general rule: a claim about yourself is verified only when somebody else could have seen it.**

**2 · Place one activity at three different sub-levels and defend each.** Take a single activity. Write the case for 3a, for 3b and for 4a from your own record.

> **What you should find.** That the evidence usually supports two adjacent positions and not one, and that choosing between them requires you to decide **what a supervisor would still want to see** rather than what you are capable of. **That is the entrustment question and it is not the competence question**, which is the distinction most learners have not made until they do this.

**3 · Go back to your own item 2, then break it.** Find the activity you named as one you should be trusted to do unsupervised. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then write the honest version.

> **Criterion.** A pass names an activity with a beginning and an end that a supervisor could delegate whole; cites an occasion with a date and a witness; states the supervision position at sub-level granularity; **and names the conditions under which the claim would not hold.** *"I can be trusted to write evaluation sets"* fails — it names no occasion and no boundary. *"Assembling an evaluation set for a retrieval component where the ground truth is a document the customer already holds, at 4a; observed at [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) with the harness I built and the two cases I found by looking for refusals; it does not hold where the ground truth has to be elicited, which I have never done unsupervised"* passes.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this programme's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). **You have done this in eighty-nine sessions. This is the last one, and it is about you.**

**4 · Rehearse the what-if, using the platform.** Have somebody ask you three counterfactuals about an activity you claimed: what would you do if the evaluator you relied on had no published agreement with human judgement; what would you do if the documentation you cited had redirected to a different page; what would you do if the threshold you inherited had no stated rationale.

> **What you should notice.** That all three are true of the tooling this programme actually taught — **no agreement rate is published for any evaluator, deprecated documentation redirects to the next best content, and no default threshold carries a rationale.** The counterfactual is not hypothetical. **A learner who answers *I would check* has not answered; the answer names what they would check and what they would do when the check returns nothing**, which is the case here.

**5 · Write your own expiry.** Entrustment lapses through disuse. Name the observable event that should trigger re-examination of your strongest claim.

> **Scoring criterion.** A pass names an event with an observer, in the form [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md) required of a staleness signal. *"Review in a year"* fails for the same reason it failed there. **Most assessment schemes assume entrustment is permanent; the model this occasion uses does not, and says so.**

**6 · Predict the divergence.** Before lodging, write down where you think the assessors will place you higher than you have placed yourself, and where lower.

> **What to notice at the occasion.** Whether you were right. **This is the only self-calibration measurement available in the whole programme**, it costs one sentence, and it is the single most useful thing you can carry out of today — more useful than the level you are given, because a level is about this cohort and a calibration error is about you.

## Assessment

**This is an entrustment decision, not a specification check.** There is no checklist here and nothing in this section is one. What is recorded is an ordinal supervision level with a mandatory narrative, against a single judgement:

> **Can this learner be trusted to judge the limits of their own competence, in a form somebody else can act on?**

**How the level is formed.** From accumulated observation across the whole programme, by more than one assessor, on more than one occasion, with each assessor recording level and narrative **independently before any discussion and before seeing the learner's brief.** The comparison tests the reading; it does not create it. Divergence between assessors of more than one sub-level is **investigated rather than averaged.** See [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) for the scale and the rater rules.

**What the assessors are reading for**, none of which is a threshold to be met.

- Fit between the learner's brief and the accumulated record, **in both directions.** Understating and overstating are the same defect and are read identically. **This is the judgement the whole occasion turns on.**
- Whether each line rests on an observation of an act rather than on a disposition.
- Whether the learner claimed at least one activity unsupervised, and held it or gave it up for a reason.
- Whether divergence was engaged with rather than deferred to.
- Whether the brief would be usable by a supervisor who knew nothing else about them.

**A narrative is mandatory and names a specific observed action.** A level recorded without one is rejected by moderation rather than by the assessor.

**What may not enter the judgement.** Anything the learner disclosed about their own error, anywhere in the programme, in a debrief or a report designed to elicit it. **That firewall has been in force since [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) and it does not lapse on the last day.** A learner whose brief names the moment they got something badly wrong has produced the occasion's most useful evidence, and a programme that penalises that will not receive it from the next cohort.

**And what the level does not record.** Whether the learner is competent. Whether they will perform any of this in a real engagement. Whether the programme improved them. **It records that this learner, observed on these occasions, described the limits of their own competence in a way that matched what people watching them saw.**

## What this does not establish

**This is the closing act of the programme and it is a statement of its own limits.**

**Nothing in this programme is evidence that any of it works.** Not the shape of the arc, not the phases, not the entrustment occasions, not the four evidence classes, not one session in ninety.

**Nothing in this programme has been measured.** Every paper closes with falsifiable predictions naming an instrument — there are hundreds of them, with coding schemes specified and artefacts defined — and **not one has been resolved, because no cohort has run.** The apparatus is complete and it is machinery. **The design's own position, stated where it is least convenient, is that most of those predictions will never be measured**, and that is the design's largest weakness rather than its charming humility.

**Stage 1 and Stage 9 are class 3 throughout and both are written past their source** — the first inferring a qualification practice from a corpus that records one refusal and no declined engagement, the last inferring a cycle the corpus implies everywhere and states nowhere.

**The pedagogical design rests on studies of school and university learners.** Its author's claim that the cycle works in corporate training and adult education is **uncited in his own reference list**, and this programme's learners are working professionals. That extrapolation is the most serious methodological objection to the whole teaching pattern.

**The practice corpus it is built on reports no outcome, no evaluation, no comparison and no measurement of the standard it proposes.** No failure is recorded anywhere in it; every case succeeds, and the single failure narrative is a synthetic composite that is fictional by its own admission. No exclusion criteria exist. **No cost side appears** — staffing, compute, the cost of the discovery activity itself and opportunity cost are all absent. No defect, incident, security-finding or maintainability data appears, and no post-deployment record for any case. **No sustainment evidence appears at all.** Not one reference, study or citation appears anywhere in it.

**The assessment machinery is borrowed from a clinical model and reduced.** The entrustment primer reports adoption rather than effectiveness. The narrative-assessment studies establish what assessors actually do, **not that narrative judgement is more valid than score-based judgement.** The oral-verification pattern is course policy with no published effectiveness data and no described calibration among its assessors. **The accreditation standards are consensus committee products offering no evidence that their requirements improve outcomes, and claiming none.**

**And the instrument judging you today has no published agreement data**, which is the sentence this programme has spent ninety sessions teaching you to say about somebody else's evaluator and now says about its own.

So what you have been assessed on is this: **whether you can hold a claim and its warrant apart, in a practice that mostly does not, using an apparatus that has never been shown to produce that ability in anybody.** The apparatus is an argument. **You are the first people it has been made to.**

**If any of it turns out to be wrong, the place to say so is [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md)**, and the obligation to say so is the last thing this programme asks of you.
