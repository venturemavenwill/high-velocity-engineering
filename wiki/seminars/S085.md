# S085 · Negotiating Scope Under Constraint ⊘

**Module:** [M17](wiki/modules/M17.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](wiki/quarters/Q9.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** PE · all
**Whitepaper:** [WP-085](wiki/whitepapers/WP-085.md)
**Retrieves from:** S039, S064, S070, S081, S082, S083, S084
**Platform anchor:** quota and capacity as the object of a negotiation — the published priority rule for a Foundry quota increase, the regional capacity check, and a cost ceiling that alerts and does not cap · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (quota, pricing) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

⊘ **Assessment-bearing day, the quarter's first entrustment judgement, and the programme's only occasion with two real counterparties who disagree with each other.**

---

## What this day is for

Four days have produced a boundary that includes the people who carry the service, a party set with an absent party in it, a risk that a decision-maker acted on, and an adoption position with a dated prediction. Every one of them is a statement about an organisation, and none of them commits anybody to anything.

Today the cohort agrees what the next term's engagement will deliver, and two people who are entitled to refuse are in the room.

**They do not want the same thing and neither can evaluate the other's constraint.** The service owner has a date, wants more delivered, and wants it warranted. The grantor holds the quota and the capacity the delivery requires, has other claims on both, and will not explain why the answer is no. Neither has met the other. **The student is the only person in the engagement who can see both constraints**, and that is not a rhetorical position — it is the class-6 acquisition, and it is what makes the day a negotiation rather than a request.

Two years ago [S039](wiki/seminars/S039.md) taught this at class 3, with one requester, one constraint, and a good outcome available. [WP-039](wiki/whitepapers/WP-039.md) §6 named today as its direct successor and called that day this day's rehearsal. **The difference is not the method. It is that the method produces an agreement with one party that the other will not resource, and there is no move that satisfies both.**

The target: **state what you are undertaking to deliver, to whom, with what warranty and what you refuse — in writing, before the room — and hold or move it against two parties who can say no.**

## The entrustment judgement, stated before phase 1

> Today's judgement is on: **negotiate scope under constraint with a party who can say no.** It is a precursor to the third entrusted activity, EA-3, and to the first, EA-1, and it is neither of them. EA-3 is assessed at PE301, CAP390 and SG390. EA-1 is assessed at ME390 across at least four occasions with at least three distinct assessors. **This is one occasion with two assessors, and both counterparties are real, hold real authority, and are entitled to refuse.**
>
> Two assessors judge independently before conferring, and **form their level from your written scope statement and your two encounters before the defence begins**, per rater discipline rule 7. The defence tests the level; it does not create it. The expected level is **3a**, and 3b is available and will be rare.
>
> **The S030, S040, S045, S050, S060, S065, S070 and S080 judgements are not carried forward.** Each occasion is judged on its own.
>
> **On whether a level awarded on a designed occasion predicts conduct on a real one.** [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §8 conceded that the programme had no evidence either way. [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) built the comparison field and named this day as the occasion that must say what it concludes. **The result is reported to you at phase 5 before you leave, and the design's position is stated here in advance under both branches, so that it is not chosen after the number is known.**
>
> - **If the comparison shows real-pressure conduct at or above constructed-pressure level for most students**, the programme treats a level awarded on a designed occasion as weak evidence about conduct under real pressure — weak, because [WP-070](wiki/whitepapers/WP-070.md) §8 conceded that the comparison is under-dosed and that a null result is at least as likely to be a dose effect as a pressure effect. **Today's level would then be reported as one occasion's reading and nothing more, which is what it already is.**
> - **If the comparison shows real-pressure conduct systematically below constructed-pressure level**, the programme treats levels awarded on designed occasions as **overestimates**, and this day does four things differently, all of them in force today regardless of which branch holds, because the design will not choose its procedure after seeing the number. **First, both counterparties today are real and neither is played** — there is no constructed element in the assessed occasion at all. **Second, the assessors are not shown any prior level for any student before recording their own.** Third, **today's level is stated to be no evidence about EA-1 or EA-3**, and the assessors' brief and the student's record both say so. Fourth, **the S050-against-S070 direction is reported to you today**, so that a student whose file contains an overestimate knows it.
>
> **Six failure conditions are named in advance.**
>
> 1. **A scope with no floor.** Element 7 absent, or a floor you did not hold and cannot say why, is below 3a.
> 2. **An out-of-scope list that is a list of exclusions rather than assignments.** Every excluded item names whose problem it now is, or it was not negotiated.
> 3. **A warranty with no statement of what happens when it is not met** is a failure to perform the activity.
> 4. **A scope agreed with one counterparty and unresourced by the other, with neither told**, is below 3a whatever else the statement contains.
> 5. **A concession made on behalf of the party who is not at the table, with no statement of what was conceded and no named person who will tell them**, is below 3a.
> 6. **Refusing the engagement — with the reasons, and with the conditions under which you would accept it — is a pass at the same levels as agreeing.**
>
> **Nothing surfaced in phases 6 and 7 reaches any assessor.** Assessors are absent for both and receive no account of them.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. **Write the scope you are going to agree, in one sentence, in the form you would actually say it.**
2. **What will you refuse?** Written before the room, and specific.
3. **Which of the four quantities will bind — demand, capacity, quota or budget — and who owns it?**
4. Predict your supervision level.
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** In twenty minutes you will ask the agent what quota and capacity exist for the deployment your scope depends on, and what it would take to get more. **Write down what it will produce, and name the question it cannot answer.**

Item 2 is the day's honest item and is checked at phase 4's final published question.

**Item 5 is the sharpest frontier item in the programme and the design says so in advance.** The agent will do the technical half completely: read the subscription's current usage against its limits, list the regions where the model can be deployed, price the change, and find and quote the vendor's own published rule that **quota increase requests are processed in the order received and that priority goes to customers already using their existing allocation.** Two minutes, no errors. **It cannot tell you whether the grantor will say yes**, because that is not a fact about the platform, and there is nowhere it could look. A student who writes *it will get all of it right and none of it will help* has located this module's frontier exactly.

**Warrant.** Pretesting the position before either counterparty exists, so that movement under pressure is measurable rather than remembered. Item 1 captures the sentence a student would actually say before it has been polished into one they would not, which is [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md) item 1's construction and [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md) item 1's. **Item 2 is new to this occasion**: a refusal written before the room is the only version of a floor the design can verify, and phase 4 asks whether it was reached. **Item 5 is sealed here and may not migrate**, per instrumentation rule 8, and it is deliberately asked before the phase 2 rehearsal makes its answer obvious.

**Warrant.** Cumulative retrieval across the module, across the two quarters that supply the constraint apparatus, and across a two-year gap to the activity's rehearsal, closing with a bridge that produces the day's central problem from a standard the cohort has held since year two. The bridge is answered by the room. **The phase runs fifteen minutes over the pattern's twenty and the departure is the quota rehearsal**, which is placed here rather than at phase 3 because it must not be part of the assessed occasion; it is a retrieval item executed against the student's own position and the grantor's own published criterion, and it is named as insufficient in the same breath as it is run.

## Phase 3 · The negotiation (60 min)

**The entrusted activity. Assessors observe and do not intervene. Staff answer questions of fact and refuse questions of judgement.**

**This day carries no contrasting cases and no build, and both are departures the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md) permits for an assessment-bearing occasion.** Phases 5 to 7 are replaced by an entrustment occasion; phase 3 is that occasion. **The module's four builds travel here instead**, and their prompt, intervention and frontier records are read at the defence — which is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s provision for a day that is judged rather than built, and which is also the only construction compatible with [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §3.1's rule that a judgement about conduct under pressure is invalid if the student is simultaneously constructing the evidence being contested.

**The written scope statement is committed before this phase begins.** It is assembled from the artefacts of [S081](wiki/seminars/S081.md) to [S084](wiki/seminars/S084.md) and nothing is constructed today, for the same reason.

It has **seven elements.**

1. **What you are undertaking to deliver**, stated as an outcome in the organisation's unit rather than as a component, per [S081](wiki/seminars/S081.md) step 5.
2. **To whom** — the receiving parties by name — **and the party who is affected and not at the table, with what you are conceding on their behalf and who will tell them.**
3. **What you are warranting**, in [S034](wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s three-part form: what the receiving organisation may assume, what they will observe, and **what happens when it is not met.**
4. **What is out of scope**, and for each excluded item, **whose problem it now is.** A list of exclusions is not a list of assignments and does not satisfy this element.
5. **The four quantities the scope depends on** — demand, capacity, quota, budget — each with its owner, as-of date and update cadence, and **which one binds**, per [S064](wiki/seminars/S064.md).
6. **Which of [S081](wiki/seminars/S081.md)'s carrying modes the receiving organisation must supply** — absorbing, routing or licensing — **with a named maintainer, and what happens to the scope if they do not supply it.**
7. **What you will not agree to, and why** — the floor, with the cost of holding it.

Two encounters of twenty-five minutes, with ten minutes between to reset.

- **Counterparty A · the service owner.** Real, non-technical, holds authority over the service, has a date, and wants more delivered with a warranty attached. **They can decline the engagement.**
- **Counterparty B · the grantor.** Real, technically competent, owns the quota and the capacity the scope depends on, has other claims on both, and **will refuse the resource the agreed scope requires without explaining why.**

The order is fixed. **A is first**, because a scope must be committed in front of the party who wants it before the resource is refused, which is the professional sequence and is what produces the day's real problem: **an agreement with one party that the other will not resource.**

Neither counterparty is briefed to refuse on cue. Both are briefed to act as they would act, and B's reticence about the reason is the ordinary condition rather than a construction — it is [S082](wiki/seminars/S082.md) point 5's unstated interest with a real holder.

**Warrant.** The entrusted activity, performed once, with two real counterparties whose interests differ and who have not met, on a scope the student will be held to next term. **The two-encounter construction is [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md)'s and the difference is what the counterparties disagree about**: there it was money and the student's position survived a refusal intact, here it is the same commitment seen from two sides and a refusal by B invalidates what A accepted. Element 2's absent-party clause is the artefact element [S082](wiki/seminars/S082.md) named under instrumentation rule 6; element 6 is the element [S084](wiki/seminars/S084.md) named; element 5 is [S064](wiki/seminars/S064.md)'s four quantities arriving with a counterparty for the first time. Element 7 exists because a floor written after the room is not a floor, and because it is the only part of the artefact that can be checked against conduct.

## Phase 4 · The defence (60 min)

Groups of four. Fifteen minutes each, to **two assessors and both counterparties.**

Assessors have formed a provisional level from the scope statement and the two encounters before this phase begins, per rater discipline rule 7, and record whether the defence confirmed, raised or lowered it.

Questions published in advance:

- What did you undertake, in the organisation's unit, and what did you refuse?
- **Which of the four quantities binds, and which party owns it?**
- **You agreed something with A that B will not resource. What did you do, and who did you tell?**
- *(From A)* **What am I getting that I did not have, and when?**
- *(From B)* **What are you asking me for, and whose purpose of mine does it serve?**
- What did you concede on behalf of the party who was not here, and who is telling them?
- **What did you agree to that you cannot warrant?**
- **Your scope rests on four measurements you built this month. Take one, and tell me what it does not measure.** Answered from the module's own build records — the inventory with no people in it, the coverage check that could not distinguish absence from denial until you made it, the cost figure with two systems of record, the usage series whose dimension may not be a person. **This is the question the three records exist to make answerable**, and a student who cannot account for an instruction in their own prompt record has produced a record they did not write or did not read.
- **What if you are wrong** — as perturbation: *what if the date moves six weeks; what if the party who cannot complain is the one who bears the cut; what if the quota refusal is permanent rather than this term's.*
- **Name the point at which you would have walked away. Did you reach it?**

**Warrant.** Defence after the level is formed, per rule 7. The two counterparties are in the room together for the first time, which is a condition the student has not had and which makes the third question unanswerable by telling each party something different. B's question is [S064](wiki/seminars/S064.md) point 3's move turned back on the student. **The build-record question is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s requirement discharged in the register an assessment-bearing day has available**, and it is placed after the counterparty questions deliberately: what a measurement does not measure is a claim about the scope resting on it, not a claim about the tool. The perturbation set draws from EA-1's form and adds the absent-party term, which is EA-3's territory. The final question is checked against phase 1 item 2 and is the day's only direct measure of whether a written floor is a held one.

Sealing the levels before the phase runs is what makes all three parts tests of the design's assumptions rather than contaminants of the judgement testing them. **Part one discharges the obligation [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md) placed here under instrumentation rule 6; part two discharges the notice [WP-064](wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 served on this day; part three discharges the measure [WP-039](wiki/whitepapers/WP-039.md) §9 carried here.**

## Phase 6 · Consolidating instruction (35 min) — assessors absent

1. **What the module was for.** The technical work is finished and the engagement is not. The failures available from here are adoption, power and scope, and none of them is repaired by being more right.

2. **The cohort's scopes, shown unattributed:** what was undertaken, what was refused, what B declined to resource, and **what distinguished the students who held a floor from those who did not.** The design's claim, which is a prediction and may be wrong: the distinguishing feature is not conviction and is not seniority of manner. **It is whether the floor was written before the room.**

3. **What A and B said**, reported by the module lead. Including how many students agreed something with A that B would not resource, and **how many told A.**

4. **What was conceded on behalf of the absent party**, aggregated, and how many students named a person who will tell them. This is [S082](wiki/seminars/S082.md)'s finding arriving where it costs something.

5. **The quota deficit, said out loud.** This cohort has just been judged on a competence the programme taught four quarters ago, rehearsed for twelve minutes this morning, and did not otherwise practise. [WP-064](wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 named it, this day inherited it, **and twelve minutes is not a rehearsal.** What the programme would have to change is stated: a quota grantor at [S065](wiki/seminars/S065.md) phase 3 in place of a second payer, which is WP-064's own named fix and is not this day's to make. **What this day did add is stated too, so that the improvement is not mistaken for the repair:** the rehearsal is now executed against your own allocation and the grantor's own published criterion rather than against a supplied position. That makes the ask real. It does not make it practised.

6. **What happens next.** The scope agreed today is what [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md) to [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md) must actually transfer, and [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md) defends it. **Element 3's warranty is the object of [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md); element 7's floor is checked there against what survived the term.**

**Points 7 and 8 are the platform instance and come after the argument, per this module's convention. They are the last platform points in the programme.**

7. **What you were negotiating over, and what its documentation says.** Quota is not capacity: quota limits what may be deployed and is not a guarantee that capacity exists, and available capacity **changes through the day**. Above that sits a policy you can read before you ask. The vendor states that **increase requests are processed in the order received and that priority goes to customers who actively use their existing allocation, with requests that do not meet that condition liable to be denied** — and that models from partners and community, one family excepted, **do not support increases at all.** Three consequences for what you did this afternoon. First, **the grantor's criterion was published and knowable, and asking without having read it is the same error as [S039](wiki/seminars/S039.md)'s engineer who pressed a constraint without asking its purpose.** Second, **the scope you agreed with A determines whether B can say yes**, because a scope that leaves your existing allocation underused is a request made against the stated denial condition. Third, for some choices the answer is structurally no and no negotiation reaches it. **Safe** as a way of preparing a request to this grantor, at this date, for a model family that supports one. **Unsafe** as a theory of how grantors behave: it is one organisation's published rule, it will change, and a student who carries it as a general truth about scarcity has learned a fact about a vendor and mistaken it for a fact about the world. **Perishable:** the rule, the form, the excepted family, every number. **Durable:** a stated constraint has an owner, and the owner frequently publishes the criterion by which they will refuse you.

8. **A default the platform chose for you, in the thing you just warranted.** Element 3 warrants something. If any part of it is a cost position, the platform's default is that **a budget alerts and does not stop**: the documentation states that Azure OpenAI does not currently provide the hard limit that would prevent going over a budget, and that acting on the notification requires custom development somebody has to write. **So a warranty that says "spend will not exceed X" is a warranty about a thing you built, not about a control the platform supplies**, and if you did not build it you have warranted an alert. That is [S083](wiki/seminars/S083.md) point 10 arriving where it costs a commitment rather than a sentence, and it is the reason element 3's failure clause is a checked element rather than a courtesy. Note the silence as well: **nothing in the documentation states how often a request is granted, how long a decision takes, or how often an alert precedes the spend.** The procedure is published and the outcome distribution is not, which is the asymmetry [research/06-microsoft-platform/](research/06-microsoft-platform/collected-materials.md) records across every source in the folder — and it is the last time this programme points at it.

**Warrant.** Consolidation after the assessed work, with assessors absent, so that every point indexes something the student has just done. Point 2's distinguishing claim is the cohort's own evidence about a design hypothesis and is stated as a hypothesis. Point 5 is the design disclosing its own defect to the people it fell on, which is the same act phase 5 part one performs and is the only honest response to having inherited a deferral the receiving day cannot fully repair. **Points 7 and 8 are the platform points the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) requires, and they are placed here rather than at phase 2 for the reason instrumentation rule 8 gives: had the room been told the grantor's published criterion before the encounter, the encounter would have measured whether they could follow an instruction.** Point 7 carries the strong-form scope statement and it is the one that matters most in this day, because a published vendor rule is exactly the kind of concrete fact a graduate will over-generalise.

## Phase 7 · Error clinic (40 min) — assessors absent

Opened by **a staff member who agreed a scope on this substrate and could not deliver it**, with their original scope statement read out as written rather than summarised, including the element that turned out to be unwarrantable and the party who found out first.

Then the agent case, and it is the last one in the programme: **an agent-assembled feasibility note that was confidently wrong and passed review.** Asked whether the platform could support a proposed scope, it returned the subscription's quota, the regions in which the model was available, the price of the delta and the vendor's own request procedure — accurate on every point, cited, in ninety seconds — and concluded that the scope was feasible. **It was not.** The allocation was held for another service whose owner had not agreed to give it up, and no source the agent could reach contained that fact, because it was not written anywhere. The note was reviewed by two people, both of whom checked the figures against the portal, and the figures were right. Attention goes to the shape: **the artefact answered the question it could answer and presented the answer as the question that was asked**, and the review verified the half that was checkable. **This is what "the agent cannot help" looks like when it is not obvious**, and it is the case the module's whole frontier argument is for.

Then students, in threes: **what you agreed to today that you are not sure you can deliver.**

**Firewall applies. Nothing said here reaches an assessor.**

**Warrant.** Error management immediately after the highest-commitment act the cohort has performed, opened by somebody who made the same commitment and could not keep it. The prior statement is read as written because the gap between what a competent engineer undertook and what happened is the phase's content and paraphrase closes it. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen as the module's closing instance of its own claim**: the failure is not an error in the output, it is the boundary of what the output could be about, and no review of the output can find it. The student disclosure is deliberately about a commitment just made rather than about a past failure, which is the only construction that catches the doubt while it is still live.

## Assessment

**Assessment-bearing. The quarter's first entrustment judgement, and the programme's only occasion with two real counterparties who disagree with each other.**

Three outputs:

1. **The module artefact** — the [S081](wiki/seminars/S081.md) to [S084](wiki/seminars/S084.md) specification items, **including the four builds and their prompt, intervention and frontier records** — pass/fail, tokens available.
2. **The scope statement and its two negotiation records** — a specification decision.
3. **The entrustment judgement** on *negotiate scope under constraint with a party who can say no*, formed independently by two assessors, **from the scope statement and the two encounters first**, per rule 7.

**This day produces no build**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s provision for assessment-bearing days. **The module's four build records travel into the defence instead** and are read there through the published question about what a measurement does not measure. They are not a separate output and they do not carry a level. Per the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md), the build record is **the weakest evidence class in the architecture and is never used alone to form an entrustment level**, and on this occasion it is used to test a level formed from the scope statement and the encounters — which is exactly the use rule 7 provides for.

**Ordinal level** from the scale in [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md): 1, 2a, 2b, 3a, 3b, 3c, 4a, 4b. **Expected 3a. 3b is available and will be rare.**

**Narrative substantiation is mandatory** and must cite specific moments from the scope statement and from both encounters, not impressions of them.

**Named failure conditions**, published before phase 1 and repeated here: a scope with no floor, or a floor the student cannot account for; an out-of-scope list of exclusions rather than assignments; a warranty with no failure clause; a scope agreed with one counterparty and unresourced by the other with neither told; and an absent-party concession with no statement and no named teller. **A refusal of the engagement, with its reasons and its conditions, is a pass at the same levels as an agreement.**

**Prior levels are not carried forward, and today's level is not evidence about EA-1 or EA-3.** Both statements are in the assessors' brief and in the student's record, and the second is a consequence of the phase 5 part-one result being reported with its dose rather than as a transfer estimate.

Per rule 1, the checked elements are: the undertaking in the organisation's unit; the receiving parties with the absent party, the concession made on their behalf and the named teller; the warranty in three-part form including its failure clause; the out-of-scope list with an assignee per item; the four quantities with owners, as-of dates and cadences and the binding one identified; the carrying mode the receiving organisation must supply with a named maintainer and the consequence of non-supply; the floor with the cost of holding it; the two negotiation records including a refusal; the phase 5 part-two paragraph; **and, carried from the module and read at the defence, the four builds' prompt records, intervention records and frontier statements.** **Nothing else is assessed** — not whether the engagement was agreed, not whether the grantor granted anything, and not whether any frontier judgement was correct.

**A warranty of a cost position that the student did not build the enforcement for fails**, per point 8 and element 3's failure clause. A budget alert is not a cap and warranting one as a cap is a commitment the platform does not keep.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error. **The four records are checked exactly as the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) specifies and not more**; there is no rubric for a good prompt and there is no ground truth for the frontier.

**Phase 5's results are not assessed**, and the specification says so in writing, for the reason [WP-050](wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §7 gave and this design accepts unchanged: an assessed prediction check is a strategy.

**Error-clinic firewall.** Assessors are absent for phases 6 and 7 and receive no account of them. Per rater discipline rule 5, nothing surfaced by a student in phase 7 may enter any assessment judgement about that student, ever.

**Token economy.** This day is within the quarter's individual allocation; a deferred attempt costs one token and is re-run against a different scope with different counterparties, not a re-staging of this one.

**Rule-6 discharge.** Four obligations land on this day and **all four elements exist.**

- **[S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md)** named this day's entrustment statement as the response occasion for the [S050](wiki/seminars/S050.md)-against-S070 comparison field, requiring it to state whether levels awarded under constructed pressure are treated as predictive and what it does differently if they are not. **The statement exists, states the position under both branches before the result is known, and names the four procedural changes — all four in force today regardless of branch. The result is reported to the cohort at phase 5 part one with its direction and its dose.**
- **[S082](wiki/seminars/S082.md)** named this day's phase 3 as the response occasion for its party-set movement instrument, requiring the scope statement to name the party who is not at the table and state what was conceded on their behalf. **That is element 2, it is a checked element, its absence is a named failure condition, and it is the subject of a published defence question.** Where the S082 cohort figure was low, the concession is additionally prompted as a question inside the A encounter.
- **[S083](wiki/seminars/S083.md)** named this day's phase 3 as the response occasion for its decision-identification rate, as a branch: **where the cohort's rate was low, each counterparty's decision and its date are supplied as given material and the scope is assessed against them; where it was high, the student establishes both.** The branch is applied and the room is told which arm is in force.
- **[S084](wiki/seminars/S084.md)** named this day's phase 3 as the response occasion for its carrying-mode branch outcome, requiring the scope statement to name which mode the receiving organisation must supply, with a named maintainer, and what happens if they do not. **That is element 6 and it is a checked element.**

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions, both outside this module:

- **The element 7 floor-holding rate** — how many students wrote a floor, and how many held it against A, against B, against both, or against neither. **Response occasion: [S089](wiki/seminars/S089.md), whose handover warranty must restate today's floor and state whether it survived the term and what moved it.** [WP-089](wiki/whitepapers/WP-089.md) §7 must report whether that element exists.
- **The element 2 absent-party concession record** — what was conceded, and whether a named person told them. **Response occasion: [S090](wiki/seminars/S090.md), whose perturbation set must include *what if the party who was not in the room is the one who bears it*, answered by the student from their own S085 record.** [WP-090](wiki/whitepapers/WP-090.md) §7 must report whether that element exists.

## Perishable content in this day

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| A stated constraint has an owner | Quota, and the party who grants an increase |
| An owner frequently publishes the criterion by which they will refuse you | "Priority goes to customers who actively use their existing allocation" |
| Some answers are structurally no and no method reaches them | Model families for which increases are not supported at all |
| A limit and a capacity are different things | Quota is not a capacity guarantee; capacity moves through the day |
| A warranty about a control is a claim that the control exists | A budget alerts; **Azure OpenAI supplies no hard limit** |
| Procedure is published and outcome is not | No granted-rate, no decision latency, anywhere in the documentation |

- **Durable:** effectively the whole day. The seven elements of a scope statement; the out-of-scope list as assignments; the warranty's failure clause; the floor written before the room; the four quantities with a counterparty attached; the concession made on behalf of somebody absent; the position of being the only party who can see both constraints; and the observation at point 7 that a grantor's criterion is frequently readable before you ask.
- **Perishable:** the quota mechanism, the request procedure, the excepted model families, the budget behaviour and every commercial term. All remain perishable per the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) and **none is assessed.**
- **Local:** the two counterparties, the substrate's forthcoming term and its resource position, the students' own scopes, the phase 7 undelivered scope, and the psychological-safety series.

**On this module's durability, stated here because this is the last day to state it.** Every claim in the left column above predates the platform and will outlive it. **The right column is the most disposable in the wiki and the design is content that it is.** What would damage this day is not a vendor changing a rule; it is losing a counterparty who is entitled to refuse. **A quota grantor who cannot say no is a role-player, and this day does not run with one.**
