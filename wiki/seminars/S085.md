# S085 · A Maturity Claim with Evidence ⊘

## The situation

You have a claim about where this engagement got to, a procedure for re-deriving it, and an adoption position and a re-run finding that either support the claim or do not.

Today somebody else executes your procedure, without you, against the estate. **You will not be in the room while they do it.**

Before that happens you lodge a prediction of what they will find, dimension by dimension, and the prediction is sealed. When they are finished, you reconcile the two in front of the assessors.

This is not a specification check. **It is a judgement about you** — whether you can be trusted to say what a claim about improvement actually rests on — and it is formed from everything observed across this module rather than from what you produce this morning.

Answer these in writing before the occasion opens. They are lodged with your claim and they are not revised afterwards.

1. What is your claim, in one sentence, with its scope and its date?
2. For each dimension: what position will the executor reach, and what will they inspect to reach it?
3. Which dimension will they fail to reach at all, and why will they fail?
4. Where do you expect them to reach a different position from yours, and which of you will be right?
5. What in your own evidence argues against your claim, and why did you make the claim anyway?

**Item 3 is the one the occasion is built on.** A procedure with no predicted failure is a procedure its author has not run.

## What you must produce

Three things, lodged together before the occasion, and one produced during it.

- **The claim.** What matured, over what interval, at what scope, with the name of whoever is responsible for establishing that the change was caused by the work.
- **The re-derivation procedure.** Per dimension: the artefact, the observation, the rule that turns the observation into a position, and the artefacts it deliberately does not use.
- **The sealed prediction.** Per dimension, the position you expect the executor to reach, and the dimensions you expect to defeat them.
- **The reconciliation**, written live: for every divergence between the prediction and the execution, which of three things happened — the evidence is not there, the rule was wrong, or the executor read it differently from you — and what follows.

## What good looks like

- **The procedure is executable by somebody who does not know your engagement.** That is the single property the occasion is arranged to test, and it is visible within minutes.
- The prediction is specific enough to be wrong. *They will find it difficult* is not a prediction; *they will reach a lower position on this dimension because the only evidence is a document we wrote* is.
- The claim's scope is narrower than the engagement. A claim about everything is a claim about nothing, and the dimensions it cannot reach are already named.
- **Divergences are diagnosed, not resolved.** Deciding you were right is a conclusion; saying which of the three causes produced the difference is the work.
- Where the executor reached a higher position than you predicted, that is examined as carefully as the other direction. **Understating a claim and overstating it are the same defect**, and a learner who only guards against one has a habit rather than a method.
- The evidence against your own claim is in your lodged answers, not produced under questioning.

## How it goes wrong

Five failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **The procedure that only its author can run** — every step is executable if you already know the answer. *Warning sign:* a step in your procedure begins with the word *check*.
- **The prediction adjusted** — the reconciliation quietly restates what you predicted so that it matches. *Warning sign:* your account of your prediction is more nuanced than your prediction.
- **Divergence read as executor error** — every difference is attributed to the person who ran it. *Warning sign:* your reconciliation contains no case in which you were wrong.
- **The unfalsifiable claim** — the claim is stated so that no execution could disturb it. *Warning sign:* you predicted no failures.
- **The safe understatement** — the claim is pitched low enough to be certain, and the occasion becomes about nothing. *Warning sign:* your claim would have been true before the engagement started.

## Do it

Work from the supplied situation in [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md), and from what you built at [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md)–[S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md). Assemble; do not rebuild.

Lodge the three artefacts. **Your executor is another learner who worked a different part of this brief, and they receive the procedure and the estate and nothing else** — no summary from you, no conversation, no clarification during execution. If they cannot proceed, they record where they stopped and that becomes part of the finding rather than an interruption of it.

While they execute, you write nothing. **The interval is deliberately empty**, because the temptation this occasion exists to remove is the one where an author walks a re-deriver through their own reasoning and calls the result a re-derivation.

## Show it

The reconciliation is short and live. Your lodged prediction is read out first, by somebody else, before you speak.

You will be asked one question at the end, whatever else is asked: **which dimension of your claim is now weaker than you thought, and what would it take to strengthen it?**

## Assessment

**This is an entrustment decision, not a specification check.** There is no checklist here and nothing in this section is one. What is recorded is an ordinal supervision level with a mandatory narrative, against a single judgement:

> **Can this learner be trusted to say what a claim about improvement actually rests on?**

**How the level is formed.** From accumulated observation across [S081](/wiki/seminars/S081.md)–[S084](/wiki/seminars/S084.md), by more than one assessor, on more than one occasion, with each assessor recording level and narrative independently before any discussion. The lodged artefacts and the execution supply the evidence; the reconciliation tests it. Divergence between assessors of more than one sub-level is investigated rather than averaged. See [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) for the scale and the rater rules.

**What the assessors are reading for**, none of which is a threshold to be met.

- Whether the procedure was executable by a stranger, and whether the learner had predicted where it would not be.
- Whether the sealed prediction was honest — specific, falsifiable, and containing at least one expectation that reflected badly on the learner's own claim.
- Whether divergences were diagnosed by cause rather than resolved by authority, and whether the learner could distinguish absent evidence from a bad rule.
- Whether overstatement and understatement were treated with equal suspicion.
- Whether the claim named who is responsible for establishing causation, and held that position under questioning.

**A narrative is mandatory and names a specific observed action.** A level recorded without one is rejected by moderation rather than by the assessor.

**What may not enter the judgement.** The executor's competence. A procedure defeated by a careless execution and a procedure defeated by its own defects look similar from outside, and the assessors resolve that by reading the reconciliation rather than by scoring the execution. **Nor may the executor's own level be affected by what they found**, in either direction; an executor who reports being unable to proceed has produced the occasion's most useful observation, and a programme that penalises that report will not receive it again.

## What this does not establish

**That the learner's claim is true.** A re-derivable claim is one whose provenance can be inspected. Whether the thing it describes actually improved is a question no source behind this programme answers for any system it describes.

**And nothing here validates the instrument.** No calibration of any maturity instrument against human judgement exists anywhere in this material — no gold standard, no held-out sample, no inter-rater statistic, no record that any produced position was ever compared with an expert's reading of the same system. The scales, their boundaries and their weights are asserted constants presented in the visual language of measurement. **This occasion tests whether a learner can re-derive a position on a scale nobody has shown to mean anything.** That is worth doing and it is less than it sounds.

The simulation limits are the same as everywhere in this programme and are not reduced by the occasion's structure. The estate is fictional, the executor is a peer with no stake, the claim carries no commercial consequence, and nobody's next engagement depends on the answer. **Every pressure that makes an honest maturity claim difficult in practice is absent.**
