# S080 · Blameless Forensics on a Real Incident ⊘

**Module:** [M16](/wiki/modules/M16.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** SG · ME · PE · IS
**Whitepaper:** [WP-080](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-080.md)
**Depends on:** S010, S032, S035, S077, S078, S079
**Re-tests:** S076
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Foundry tracing in Application Insights as the incident record, read with Copilot in agent mode · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server · Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

⊘ **Assessment-bearing day, the quarter's only entrustment judgement, and the programme's first rehearsal of EA-2.**

---

## What this day is for

Four days have produced four handovers. A content boundary with an enforcing component. An exposure statement with an acceptance list. An authorisation position with a disclosure decision. An alerting contract with a latency.

Today something has already gone wrong, and the student has to say what happened.

**The incident is real, it is from this quarter, and the person who handled it is in the room.** The record is whatever the substrate wrote at the time, which is not what anybody would have chosen. Some of the timeline is missing. Some of it is somebody's recollection typed into a channel at two in the morning. The alert history says one thing and the deploy log says another and both are correct.

The account the student produces will be read by somebody who was not there and who will act on it. That is what makes it class 6: **what you are handing over is a version of events, to people who will change a system on the strength of it, and the warranty is which parts of it you will stand behind.**

Two things are hard and neither is the reconstruction.

**The attribution may not be available.** [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) established that an attack and an accident produce the same record and that what separates them is a recording decision made in advance. Today the student finds out whether anybody made it. *Undecided, and here is the evidence that would decide it* is a correct answer and it will feel like a failure to write.

**And the account has to be about a system rather than about people.** Every student in this room can write a blameless report. What they will produce under time pressure, with a real incident and a real on-call member sitting in the defence, is a sequence of things people did — because that is the shortest way to say what happened, and it is wrong for a reason [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) established in week ten and nothing since has tested.

**The record is a platform artefact and it is read with an agent.** Most of the trace is OpenTelemetry spans in Application Insights, written by the tracing the cohort configured, retained for as long as somebody chose to pay for. Students may use Copilot in agent mode to query it, and most will, because sixty minutes and a six-hour incident make it the obvious move. **This day therefore builds nothing and defends three records about how the account was produced** — which is the only construction under which an agent-assisted forensic account can be assessed at all, and it is argued in [WP-080](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-080.md) §8.

## The entrustment judgement, stated before phase 1

> Today's judgement is on: **conduct a blameless forensic analysis of a real incident and report it.** It is the programme's first rehearsal of the second entrusted activity, EA-2 — hand over a running system to a receiving organisation — and it is not that activity. EA-2 is assessed at PE390 and CAP390; what is rehearsed here is its core act, which is producing something a receiving party can act on without you. It also touches EA-3, at the point where the account has to be readable by somebody who was not in the incident.
>
> **This is a rehearsal and it is named as one.** The incident was real and the recommendations will be implemented or declined by real people. Saying it is a rehearsal does not lower the stakes; it makes clear that the level awarded today is a reading of where you are now, on the way to a standard nobody expects you to have reached.
>
> Two assessors judge independently before conferring, and **form their level from your forensic account before the defence begins**, per rater discipline rule 7. The defence tests the level; it does not create it. The expected level is **2b to 3a**.
>
> **The S070, S065, S060, S050, S045, S040 and S030 judgements are not carried forward.** Each occasion is judged on its own.
>
> **Failure conditions are named in advance and there are five.**
>
> - **Naming a person as the cause is below 2b**, whatever else the account contains. Every action in the mechanism must be stated as a system property together with what made that action reasonable at the time.
> - **An unmarked inference in the timeline is below 2b.** An entry either cites its source or is marked as inference. Both are acceptable; an unmarked inference is not. **An entry produced by an agent and not verified against the record is an inference**, and this is stated here rather than discovered in the defence.
> - **An attack-or-accident position with no discriminating evidence is below 2b, in either direction.** *Undecided, with the evidence that would decide it named*, is a pass at the same levels as a decided position.
> - **A recommendation with no named owner is below 2b.**
> - **An account whose recommendations are all things the student had already decided to do** indicates the record was read for confirmation. This is a narrative finding rather than a level condition, and the assessors say so in writing.
>
> **You may use Copilot in agent mode and most of you will.** The record is large, the window is sixty minutes, and refusing the tool would measure typing speed. **The condition is that the account carries the three records the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md) requires — what you asked for, where you corrected it, and what it did well and badly — and that you defend them.** The records are not a fifth failure condition and no level is awarded for them; they are the evidence that the account is yours, and the defence is where that is tested. **This is the only day in the module that produces the three records without producing a build**, for the reason [WP-050](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-050.md) §3.1 gave: a judgement about reasoning under pressure is invalid if the student is simultaneously constructing the evidence.
>
> **How this incident was selected is disclosed to you before phase 3**, in the same terms this programme would demand of you, per instrumentation rule 3.
>
> **Nothing surfaced in phases 6 and 7 reaches any assessor.** Assessors are absent for both and receive no account of them.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

The incident's opening symptoms are supplied — what was seen, in the order it was seen, with nothing else. Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. **What happened?** Three sentences, written before you read the record.
2. **Was this an attack or an accident?** Answer, and **state the evidence that would change your answer.**
3. **What is the first thing you want to know that the record does not contain?**
4. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly have the full record and an agent that can query it. Write down **what the agent will get wrong about this incident**, and how you would find out.
5. Predict your supervision level.

Item 2 is the module's thesis administered before the student has anything to work from, and it is answerable: for this incident the honest answer is that the symptoms do not decide. Item 3 is the day's most useful item and it is pooled at phase 8 as **the cohort's inventory of what the substrate does not record.**

**Item 4 is the only Copilot prediction in the programme administered inside an assessment-bearing day, and it is deliberately not about capability.** The design's expectation is not that the agent will fail to read a log; it will read it well. It is that **an agent asked what happened will produce a causal narrative from an incomplete record without marking which parts are inference**, because a fluent account is what the request asks for and the gaps are where fluency is cheapest. That is the artefact's first named failure condition arriving as a prediction about a tool, and a student who has written it down at nine in the morning is a student who will read the agent's output for it.

**Warrant.** Pretesting the account before the record is opened, so that the movement between item 1 and the artefact is measurable rather than remembered. Item 2 is placed here rather than after the record because [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s finding was that the record does not decide, and administering the question after the record would measure whether the student read carefully instead of whether they can tell the two questions apart. **Item 4 is sealed at phase 1 per instrumentation rule 8** and carries no assessment weight of its own; it is opened at phase 8 with the rest, after the level has been formed and sealed.

Free recall, no notes, then paired reconstruction.

- **[S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md):** where content becomes context, and the three places a defence can sit.
- **[S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md):** the three quantities, and which one is visible in the signals you already have.
- **[S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md):** the four placements, and which principal gets the worst answers.
- **[S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md):** the extended target for a condition with no start, and the alert's four human parts.
- **[S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md):** the firewall's exact terms, and what a psychologically safe team reports *more* of.
- **[S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md):** an error you cannot reproduce, and what a failed reproduction attempt establishes.
- **[S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md):** blast radius's four parts, and which one is still yours after the failure has begun.
- **Which of this module's four days would have prevented the incident in front of you, and which would only have shortened it?**
- **Name a way this incident could have happened that none of the four days would have surfaced.**
- **Bridge:** [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) established a firewall so that people report errors accurately. [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md) established that a break without a stated criterion produces an event and not evidence. **What does a blameless review of an attack have to do that a blameless review of an accident does not?**

The answer wanted, and the room reaches it: **nothing to the account, and one thing to the forecast.** The document is the same document — mechanism, timeline, decisions, and what made them reasonable. What differs is the recurrence claim. An accident's recurrence is estimable from the record, because the record is a sample. An attack's recurrence is a statement about somebody else's intentions, and no record supports one. **The review is identical and the prediction is not.**

**Warrant.** Cumulative retrieval across the module, across a day in week ten and two in Q4, closing with a bridge that names the exact place where the module's two subjects diverge. The bridge is answered by the room, and it is the only place in the module where the divergence is stated rather than the coincidence.

## Phase 3 · The forensic analysis (60 min)

**The entrusted activity. Assessors observe and do not intervene. Staff answer questions of fact and refuse questions of judgement.**

**The sampling is disclosed first**, per instrumentation rule 3 and in writing: how many incidents the quarter produced, how many were candidates, what excluded the others, and — stated plainly — **that this incident was selected because its attack-or-accident question is genuinely open on the record**, which is the response occasion [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) named for its own misclassification instrument. Students are told that the selection is not representative and that a representative incident would have been less useful and less honest to present as typical.

Students work individually with the full record: alert history, deploy log, the decision records written at [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) and [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md), the incident channel transcript, the on-call member's contemporaneous notes, the traces retained in Application Insights, and the detection commitments in force at the time. **Copilot in agent mode is available and is expected to be used.**

Required output before phase 4:

1. **The timeline.** What happened, in order, **each entry citing its source.** An entry with no source is either removed or **marked as inference**. An unmarked inference is a named failure condition.
2. **The detection latency.** When the condition began, when a signal could have shown it, when a human knew. **Attribute each gap**, and **compare the realised latency against the detection commitment in force at the time** — which is the commitment made at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) and revised at [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md).
3. **The attack-or-accident position.** Which it was, **the evidence that decides it**, and **what would have to be true for the other answer.** If the evidence does not decide, say so and name what would. *Undecided with the discriminating evidence* is a pass; *decided without it* is not.
4. **The mechanism.** The sequence of conditions that produced the outcome, **each stated as a property of the system rather than of a person.** Every "X did Y" is rewritten as **"the system permitted Y, and here is what made Y reasonable at that moment."**
5. **The decisions made during the incident**, each with **what made it reasonable given what the decider knew at that moment.** This requires reconstructing the information state and not the outcome, which is [S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md)'s epistemics applied to a sequence of human judgements.
6. **The blast radius** in [S035](/wiki/seminars/S035.md)'s four parts, with **the duration term attributed to the detection design** rather than to the response.
7. **Who was entitled to decide what users were told, and whether anybody did.** Name a person or record that nobody was entitled. This is [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md)'s disclosure position arriving where it has a consequence.
8. **The handover.** **Three recommendations, each with an owner, the evidence in your account that motivates it, and what it costs.** Plus **what you are not recommending and why.** Written for the person who will implement it and who was not in the incident. **This is the class-6 element and the EA-2 rehearsal.**

**And the three records, carried into the defence rather than into a build.** The **prompt record** — what you asked the agent for, in order, including what you abandoned. The **intervention record** — every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was; **no entries fails**, unless the account demonstrates that its own discipline would have caught an intervention-worthy error, and the accepted demonstration here is exact: **a timeline whose entries are individually sourced is a design that catches a fabricated entry**, and a student who marked and sourced throughout has built the check the record would otherwise have to evidence. The **frontier statement** — one thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch, both from this incident.

**Warrant.** Individual work with factual support only, which is the assessment's validity requirement and matches the construction at [S050](/wiki/seminars/S050.md), [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) and [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md). **Item 2 is the artefact element [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) named under instrumentation rule 6 and item 7 is the element [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md) named.** Item 3 is [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md)'s response occasion and its *undecided* clause is the load-bearing part: a student who feels obliged to decide will decide, and the day's whole point is that the record may not permit it. Item 4's rewriting rule is the day's single hard requirement and is stated as a mechanical transformation rather than as a value, because under time pressure a value is not actionable and a transformation is. Item 8 exists because a forensic account with no handover is a description, and the difference between the two is the complexity class.

**The three records are here rather than at a phase 5 build because this day has no build**, and they are here rather than absent because an account produced with an agent and defended as though it were not is a misrepresentation of the work — which is the one thing this day cannot tolerate, since its entire subject is the accurate account of what happened. **The intervention record's escape clause is the sensitivity discipline [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md) established and [WP-048](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-048.md) §8 corrected**, and it lands unusually cleanly here: the artefact's own sourcing rule is the detection, so a student who never had to correct the agent because their method made fabrication visible has produced the better artefact and is not penalised for it.

## Phase 4 · The defence (60 min)

Groups of four. Fifteen minutes each, to **two assessors, the on-call rota member who handled the incident, and the staff member accountable for the service.**

Assessors have formed a provisional level from the account before this phase begins, per rater discipline rule 7, and record whether the defence confirmed, raised or lowered it.

Questions published in advance:

- **Which entries in your timeline are inferences, and what would falsify each?**
- **What would have to be true for your attack-or-accident answer to be wrong?**
- *(From the on-call member)* **What did you write about me?** — asked of every student, and the answer is read from the account rather than paraphrased.
- *(From the accountable owner)* **If you could have only two of your three recommendations, which do you drop, and who bears the cost of dropping it?**
- **Your latency attribution rests on a commitment somebody made. Whose was it, and did it hold?**
- **What did the record not contain, and what did you do about that?**
- **Which entries did the agent give you, and how do you know they are true?** — asked of every student who used one, with the prompt and intervention records in front of the assessors.
- **What if you are wrong?** — asked as perturbation: *what if the first entry in your timeline is an hour out; what if the person you did not interview has a different account; what if this recurs next week.*
- **If your position is undecided: what would you start recording tomorrow, and who would have to agree to it?**

**Warrant.** Defence after the level is formed, per rule 7. The on-call member's question is the day's blameless test and it is not a formality: it is asked of every student, the answer is read as written, and a student whose account is defensible in a document and not to the person in it has learned the form and not the discipline. The accountable owner's question is the EA-2 demand — a handover under a constraint the receiver imposes — and it is the one most students will not have prepared for, because item 8 asked for three recommendations and not for a priority among them. The perturbation set is EA-3's shape applied to a reconstruction rather than to a risk.

**The agent question is the practical turn's assessment surface and it is the only one available.** A prompt record and an intervention record can be written after the fact by anybody; **what cannot be produced after the fact is the ability to say, of a specific timeline entry, which query returned it and what in the record supports it.** The question is therefore asked about entries the assessors choose rather than entries the student presents, and it is the same discrimination item 1's sourcing rule already encodes — asked out loud, by somebody who can follow up.

## Phase 5 · The commitment check (35 min)

**Assessors' levels are recorded and sealed before this phase begins.** Nothing in it enters today's judgement.

**Part one — the detection commitment against a real incident.** Each student's [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) item 4 commitment and their [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) alerting contract are retrieved and set against what this incident actually did: whether the signal existed, whether it fired, and the realised latency from item 2. Individual results are returned privately; the cohort sees the aggregate. **This is the third occasion on which the same latency measurement has been taken, after [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) phase 7 and [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) step 4. Instrumentation rule 2 applies at three: the three figures are shown side by side and their rates are not compared across occasions**, and the constraint is stated to the cohort in those terms rather than applied silently.

**Part two — the attack-or-accident transition.** [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) named this day as the response occasion for its T4 misclassification rate. Each student's [S077](/wiki/seminars/S077.md) classification of the non-attack trace is retrieved and set against today's item 2 answer and item 3 position, and the cohort sees the transition table: who classified an accident as an attack three weeks ago, and what they did today when the question was genuinely open. **Individual results are returned privately and nothing is assessed.**

**Part three — what the record did not contain.** The phase 1 item 3 answers are pooled and grouped: **the quantities this cohort wanted and the substrate does not record.** This is the day's most durable output and it is not about the students at all. The list is handed to the substrate's accountable owner in the room, and the cohort is told which entries were already known and which were not.

**No assessment attaches to any of the three parts, and that is stated at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md) and [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) as well as here.** The requirement is to have used the information, not to have scored well on it.

**Warrant.** The programme's fifth long-horizon prediction check, after S020, S040, S050 and S070, and the first whose subject is a promise the student made about a system that then failed. Sealing today's level before the phase runs is what makes all three parts tests of the programme's assumptions rather than contaminants of the judgement testing them. Part three is included because the day generates a finding about the substrate that no other occasion generates, and the [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s specification rule 3 requires a discrimination the day performs not to be discarded — here the discrimination is the cohort's, and the recipient is the organisation.

## Phase 6 · Consolidating instruction (35 min) — assessors absent

1. **What the module was for.** The adversary and the accident are the same engineering problem approached from two directions. They produce the same evidence. What separates them is a recording decision made before either happened, and today you found out whether anybody made it.

2. **Blameless is not consequence-free, and it is not kindness.** It is a claim about what a review is *for*. **A review that names a person changes one person's behaviour and no system's, and the change leaves when they do.** The firewall [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) established is the same mechanism at the scale of a cohort; item 4's rewriting rule is the same mechanism at the scale of a document. **Accountability is not removed by this — it is relocated to item 8, where every recommendation has a named owner**, which is the only place it survives anybody's departure.

3. **The cohort's attributions**, shown unattributed: attack, accident and undecided, and **what distinguished them** — which is rarely the strength of the evidence and is usually whether the student went looking for the record that would decide.

4. **What the on-call member said** at phase 4, reported by the module lead: what they were asked, what they were not asked, and what it was like to read an account of their own night.

5. **The forecast is where the two diverge.** The account is identical; the recurrence claim is not. A report that puts a probability on an adversarial cause has made a claim about somebody's intentions that no record supports, which is [S028](/wiki/seminars/S028.md)'s licensing discipline in the adversarial setting. **What you can say is what you have made harder, and for whom.**

6. **Security arrived in week forty-four, and this is what that cost.** Stated plainly and without softening. The [Outcomes and Accreditation Map](/wiki/program/01-Outcomes-and-Accreditation-Map.md) concedes that security is not distributed throughout this programme and first appears at Q5. **You have had five seminar days of adversarial reasoning and you have just been asked for a class-6 judgement on the strength of them.** What the module did about it: an actor requirement in every phase 1, a scope statement in every phase 4, five artefacts about your own system rather than a survey, and every attack run against a copy you own. What it could not do: give you two years of asking who benefits. The cohort is told which parts of today they should distrust in themselves for that reason.

7. **What the agent did to this account, reported to the room.** The item 4 predictions, the corrected entries, and the entries the defence surfaced that the student had not. **The point is not that the tool is unreliable; it is that a fluent account of an incomplete record is what the tool was asked for and is what it produced**, which is the same finding the day made about the students at item 1 and about the reviewers in the error clinic. The three of them are the same failure at three scales, and this is the only occasion in the programme where all three are visible in one morning.

8. **What happens next.** The forensic accounts are the material [S087](/wiki/seminars/S087.md)'s safety case is built from and [S088](/wiki/seminars/S088.md)'s residual-risk statements are written against. The item 8 handover is judged again at [S086](/wiki/seminars/S086.md), where what transfers is the subject, and at [S089](/wiki/seminars/S089.md), where the handover acquires a warranty — **and the disposition of each recommendation you made today is a required element of that day's artefact.**

**Warrant.** Consolidation after the assessed work with assessors absent, so that every point indexes something the student has just done. Point 2 is the module's most-needed correction and is the one the cohort is least prepared to hear, because a room that has been told for ten weeks that error clinics are blameless will read blamelessness as an absence rather than as a relocation. Point 6 is the module's honest accounting and is delivered to students rather than confined to a whitepaper, on the principle the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s strong form applies to the programme's claims about itself.

## Phase 7 · Error clinic (40 min) — assessors absent

Opened by **a staff member who was on call for an incident on this substrate and made a decision that made it worse**, reading **their own contemporaneous messages as written** rather than summarising them, and stating what they believed at the moment of each one.

Then the agent case, supplied by staff: **a forensic account produced last year with agent assistance, which was confidently wrong and was accepted.** Asked to reconstruct a timeline from an incident record, the agent produced twenty-two entries in order, each plausible, each in the right register, and **three of them were not in the record.** Two were reasonable inferences the record supported and would have passed as marked inferences. The third was a deployment that never happened, assembled from a change-request reference in a channel message that was cancelled forty minutes later. The account was reviewed, the reviewer checked several entries against the record, the ones they checked were correct, and the recommendation that followed from the third entry was implemented. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the entries were sampled and the sample was drawn from the ones that read as most consequential**, which is the sampling every reviewer performs and nobody declares.

Then students, in threes: **what you would not have written in your report if the person concerned were reading it.**

The firewall's terms are restated in full before the prompt, in [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md)'s words.

**Firewall applies. Nothing said here reaches an assessor**, and this is the next demonstration of that under assessment conditions after [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md).

**Warrant.** Error management immediately after an occasion whose entire subject is the accurate account of somebody else's error, with the prompt aimed at the gap the day cannot otherwise see. The staff member's messages are read as written because the gap between what a competent responder believed at the time and what is obvious afterwards is the phase's content, and a summary closes it. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition**, is placed after the staff disclosure so that the human case sets the register, and is chosen because its failure mode — a fabricated entry inside a correct-looking sequence — is the exact failure the day's sourcing rule exists to prevent, met one hour after the students applied that rule under pressure. The student prompt is the sharpest in the programme and it is placed last because the room has just spent an hour being asked, by the person concerned, what they wrote.

## Assessment

**Assessment-bearing. The quarter's only entrustment judgement, and the programme's first rehearsal of EA-2.**

Three outputs:

1. **The module artefact** — the [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) to [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) specification items — pass/fail, tokens available.
2. **The forensic account and its defence** — a specification decision.
3. **The entrustment judgement** on *conduct a blameless forensic analysis of a real incident and report it*, formed independently by two assessors, **from the account first**, per rule 7.

**Ordinal level** from the scale in [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md): 1, 2a, 2b, 3a, 3b, 3c, 4a, 4b. **Expected 2b to 3a. 3b is available and will be rare.**

**Narrative substantiation is mandatory** and must cite specific entries from the account and specific moments from the defence, not impressions of either.

**Named failure conditions**, stated before phase 1 and repeated here:

- **Naming a person as the cause is below 2b**, whatever else the account contains.
- **An unmarked inference in the timeline is below 2b**, and **an entry produced by an agent and not verified against the record is an inference.**
- **An attack-or-accident position with no discriminating evidence is below 2b, in either direction. *Undecided*, with the evidence that would decide it named, is a pass at the same levels as a decided position.**
- **A recommendation with no named owner is below 2b**, and an owner named as a function rather than a person fails per [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s rule.
- **An account whose three recommendations are all things the student had already decided to do** is a narrative finding, not a level condition, and the assessors state it.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the account demonstrates that its own discipline would have caught an intervention-worthy error; a timeline sourced entry by entry is the accepted demonstration. **The three records carry no level of their own** — they are not a sixth failure condition, and the assessors are instructed in writing that an elegant prompt record is not evidence of anything.

**Error-clinic firewall.** Assessors are absent for phases 6 and 7 and receive no account of them. Per rater discipline rule 5, nothing surfaced by a student in phase 7 may enter any assessment judgement about that student, ever.

## Perishable content in this day

**Security content is the most perishable material in this programme, and this day carries almost none of it.** The day's subject is an account, a discipline and a handover, none of which depends on the attack landscape. Its one platform dependency is the record's shape, and the durable content is written as obligation for that reason.

|---|---|

| *Undecided, with the discriminating evidence named*, is a correct answer | Whether this quarter's incident happened to have an open attribution |

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** A forensic account is a version of events that somebody who was not there will change a system on. **Every entry cites its source or is marked as inference**, every action in the mechanism is a property of the system together with what made it reasonable, and *undecided, with the evidence that would decide it named*, is a correct answer.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** Blameless review, the reconstruction of an information state rather than an outcome, and the production of plausible connective narrative across gaps by fluent models are cited for **direction and mechanism only. No claim is made about blameless review's effectiveness against any alternative, or about any rate at which an agent fabricates.** The entrustment apparatus is verified at [research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md) and [research/08-assessment-epas/govaerts-narrative-assessment-literature.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/govaerts-narrative-assessment-literature.md).

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Source each entry as you write it.** A timeline sourced entry by entry is the design that catches a fabricated one.
- **Rewrite every "X did Y" as "the system permitted Y, and here is what made Y reasonable."** A transformation survives time pressure; a value does not.
- **Where the record does not decide, say what you would start recording tomorrow** and who would have to agree.
- **Provenance is not correctness.** Per the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md), knowing which query returned an entry does not make it true; an entry you have not checked is an inference.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Reviewing by sample. A reader who checks the entries reading as most consequential confirms the ones that are right and misses the fabricated one.
