# S067 · Attribution, and Who Is Responsible for It

**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-067](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-067.md)

**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

The number moved. Somebody is going to say your system moved it.

They may be right. Nothing you currently hold establishes that they are, and in most delivery organisations **nobody is assigned the job of establishing it.** Cost is forecast by named engineers against named telemetry. Return is chosen by the customer from a menu, measured with instrumentation the customer may not possess, over a period nobody specifies. The causal step between the two is performed by nobody and reviewed by nobody.

The framework this module teaches from does something sharper and worse. **Several of its metric definitions define themselves as the change *attributable to* the system.** A measure defined that way cannot be evidence that the system caused a change, because the attribution is inside the definition. The framework also poses the confounding question correctly — how would you separate the value of the system from the value of the learning, if a previous solution already exists — **and answers it nowhere.**

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. Who, by name, is responsible for establishing that your system caused the change?
2. What else could have caused it?
3. A previous solution already exists. How do you separate its effect from yours, and both from the learning that happened alongside them?
4. Which of your metric definitions contains the word *attributable*, and what work is that word doing?
5. What would you accept as evidence that your system did **not** cause the change?

**Item 1 is the session.** Most answers are a role nobody holds, a team, or a silence.

## What you must produce

**An attribution statement.**

- **A named person** responsible for establishing causation. A human being with a name, not a role and not a team.
- **The competing explanations**, listed, with at least one that is genuinely plausible.
- **What was done to separate them** — and where nothing was done, what the claim has been downgraded to.
- **The rewritten definitions**: any metric defined as the change attributable to the system, restated so that the attribution is a conclusion rather than a premise, with the original retained beside it.
- **The disconfirming evidence**: what you would accept as showing the system was not the cause.

## What good looks like

- **Attribution has a name attached.** **A claim whose attribution is assigned to nobody is not a claim** — it is a sentence somebody will quote.
- **The competing explanations include at least one that would be awkward for you.** A list containing only implausible alternatives is a rhetorical device.
- **Every definition that presupposed attribution has been rewritten**, and the original is retained beside it so that a reader can see what changed. **A measure defined as the change caused by the system assumes the conclusion and cannot then supply it.**
- **Where the previous solution's effect could not be separated, the claim is stated as an association** and says so in the sentence, not in a footnote.
- **The learning confound is addressed explicitly.** The customer got better at the problem while using your system. Some of the change is theirs.
- **The disconfirming evidence is stated and could occur.**
- **The asymmetry is disclosed**: where one side of a ratio comes from telemetry and the other from a workshop, the document says which is which.

## How it goes wrong

- **The circular metric** — a measure defined as the change caused by the system, then offered as evidence the system caused a change. *Warning sign:* the word *attributable* appears in the definition and nobody has noticed it is doing the work.
- **The unassigned responsibility** — everybody agrees causation matters and nobody owns it. *Warning sign:* your governance record has an owner for every risk and none for the attribution.
- **The co-timed change** — a reorganisation, a policy change, a price change, a seasonal shift. *Warning sign:* you cannot say what else happened in the period.
- **The learning confound claimed as yours** — the customer improved at the task while using the system, and the whole change is claimed. *Warning sign:* you delivered training inside the measurement window and it is not in the list.
- **The engineered denominator** — the cost side is instrumented and the return side is elicited, and the ratio is presented as though both were measured. *Warning sign:* one side of your ratio comes from telemetry and the other from a menu.
- **The role that nobody holds** — attribution assigned to "the programme" or "the steering group". *Warning sign:* you cannot email the owner.
- **The unexaminable separation** — a method for separating causes is asserted and not described. *Warning sign:* your document says "controlled for".

## Do it

**First, find the word.** Search every metric definition you have been handed for language that assumes causation — *attributable to*, *driven by*, *resulting from*, *enabled by*. Rewrite each one so the measure describes an observable quantity and the attribution becomes a separate claim. **Keep the original next to it.** This is the same discipline [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) installed for inferred security attributes, arriving in a financial document: **uncertainty relocated is uncertainty preserved; uncertainty stripped is a guess wearing a fact's clothes.**

**Second, take the confounder list from [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md) and make it worse.** Add the ones you left out because they were inconvenient. **The list is not an admission; it is the input to everything that follows**, and a short list means the analysis has not started.

**Third, deal with the previous solution and with the learning.** Something was in place before. People got better while using what you built. Say what portion of the change either could account for, or say you cannot separate them — **and if you cannot, the claim is an association and the sentence has to say so.**

**Fourth, find a name.** Go and ask somebody to own the causal claim. **This is uncomfortable and it is the point:** you are asking a person to be accountable for a judgement that most organisations leave unowned precisely because it cannot be made safely. If nobody will take it, **that refusal is your finding** and it goes in the statement.

**Fifth, write the disconfirming evidence and check that it could occur.**

**Sixth, disclose the asymmetry.** Where your ratio has an instrumented denominator and an elicited numerator, say so in the document that carries the ratio, not in an appendix. **A ratio whose denominator is well instrumented and whose numerator is not is not a well-instrumented ratio, but it will feel like one.**

## Show it

Take the attribution statement to the person you named and ask them to accept it.

Watch what they ask for. **The thing they want before they will put their name to it is the work the statement is still missing**, and it is usually the same thing: a way of ruling out one competing explanation that you had listed and not addressed.

If they decline, record the decline and what they said. **A refused attribution, recorded, is a stronger artefact than an unowned claim** — and it is the honest state of most value claims in this industry.

## What this does not establish

**Nothing here establishes that any particular claim is causal.** This session teaches who is responsible for the question and how to downgrade when it has not been answered. It does not teach a method for answering it, and it does not have one.

**The framework it corrects poses the confounding question and answers it nowhere.** There is no worked separation anywhere in the corpus, no case in which a previous solution's contribution was distinguished from the new one's, and no reported instance of any metric being measured at all. **The correction taught here is therefore a discipline imposed on an absence.**

**The named-person requirement is written past the source.** Nothing in the corpus assigns responsibility for causation to anybody, and the recommendation that it should be assigned comes from this repository's own reading of the material rather than from the material. **It is design reasoning and it has never been tested on an organisation.**

**And the requirement has a cost the session cannot offset.** A person who accepts the attribution has accepted an accountability that outlasts the engagement, and this programme has no evidence that anybody benefits from taking it. **A learner should expect refusal to be the common answer** and should treat a recorded refusal as a successful outcome of the exercise rather than a failed one.
