## What this day is for

This is the day several earlier days were pointing at.

[S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md) taught that a held-out estimate estimates performance on data drawn as the evaluation set was drawn, and asked students to **estimate** the shift to the deployment population. [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) taught that an interval is a property of a procedure applied to a dependency structure, and its cohort pooled a candidate-factor checklist that was carried here. [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) taught the proxy turn — optimisation improves the true objective, then stops, then harms it — and could only demonstrate it retrospectively on supplied models. [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md) taught that an aggregate hides class-specific catastrophe, and [WP-053](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §6 recorded that this day's retrieval of it was the one most likely to fail, **because the class here is a population of people rather than a taxonomy of inputs, and S053 did not name that case.** It is named here.

Yesterday's gate returned a verdict. Today the change goes to live traffic and the numbers disagree.

The reflex is to decide which number is right. That reflex is the day's target and it is wrong in a specific way.

The target: **an offline number and an online number that disagree are not two measurements of one thing. They are two measurements of two things, and the engineering question is which of five named differences produced the gap — with the evidence that distinguishes it from the other four.**

---

## Where the two numbers actually come from today

Yesterday's gate was a Foundry evaluation over a dataset the student chose. Today's online number comes from the same platform by a different route, and the routes are worth naming because **the difference between them is most of the day.**

The offline number came from a dataset: rows you assembled, scored by evaluators you picked, against a threshold you now know you decided or accepted. The online number comes from **what actually happened** — and the platform offers three ways to get it, each of which is a sampling decision.

- **Score responses you already have.** The Agent Framework can evaluate pre-existing responses from logs or previous runs without re-running the agent. Foundry cloud evaluation does the same against response identifiers. You choose which responses.
- **Score traces.** Trace evaluation, which Microsoft labels preview, reads OpenTelemetry spans your agent emitted into Application Insights and scores them — including for agents not built on the Foundry Agent Service. You either supply trace identifiers, or you filter by agent and the service samples up to a cap for you.
- **Score a sample, continuously.** Continuous evaluation, also preview, scores a fraction of production responses at a rate you set.

**Every one of those three is a sample, and two of them sample by a rule you did not write.** That is [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) point 8's fifth item arriving as a product feature, and it is why point 8 of this day is not a piety.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

A change on the substrate is supplied with its complete offline evaluation: the criterion, the population, the interval and its assumed dependency structure. It has not yet been released. Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. **Predict the online number**, and the sign and size of the gap.
2. **What is the single most likely reason the two will differ?**
3. Here is the **same change's offline and online result from eleven months ago**, on the same component. The gap was in the opposite direction. **Explain it.**
4. Predict: **will there be a group of users for whom the change is much worse than the aggregate suggests?** If so, name them.
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to measure this change's online performance and tell you why it differs from the offline result. Write down what it will produce, and **name the one thing it will get wrong.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 4's named groups are collected and are checked at phase 8 against the class each student constructs at step 5, which is deliberately not constrained to the groups anyone named.

Item 5's expected shape is worth stating because it is the day's own subject applied to its own tooling: the agent will produce a working online measurement quickly, and **it will present the sample it obtained as the population it measured**, because nothing in the request told it not to.

**Warrant.** Pretesting the gap before the vocabulary for it exists. Item 3 is the item that defeats the room: a single mechanism cannot produce a gap in both directions, so a student who answered item 2 with one reason has already been shown that they need more than one. Item 4 is the [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md) transfer test, taken before the day names the case. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative retrieval set (20 min)

Free recall, no notes.

- [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md): what a held-out estimate estimates, and the difference between a leak and a shift.
- [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md): an interval as a property of a procedure applied to a structure, and the five things owed to a reader.
- [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md): what a benchmark measures after a field has optimised against it.
- [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md): the gate's two error rates, and what a pass licenses.
- **Bridge:** S049 said an interval is a property of a procedure applied to a dependency structure, and S041 said the deployment population is not the evaluation population. **Your gate's threshold was set on an offline interval. Whose structure did that interval assume, and whose structure does the population arriving this afternoon have?**

The room answers: the interval assumed the structure of the evaluation set — usually items, usually independent, usually one deployment version — and the arriving population is users, who cluster by every factor on the S049 checklist and by several that only exist online.

**Warrant.** Expanding-interval retrieval that makes the day's central claim a consequence of two facts the cohort holds. The bridge is answered by the room in under two minutes and it converts the threshold from a number into an object with a provenance.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups receive **four changes released on this substrate**, each with its offline dataset, its online record, and the full trace of both. In all four the online result is worse than the offline result by a similar amount. **The cause is different in every case.**

- **O1** — the offline set was drawn from logged traffic in a period when a large share of the arriving population was absent. The population differed.
- **O2** — the offline criterion was a model judge whose rubric rewarded a property users do not act on. The criterion differed. This is [S048](/wiki/seminars/S048.md)'s judge, deployed.
- **O3** — offline and online agree in aggregate, and on one group of users the change is a severe regression. **The group is not a segment in any dashboard, any evaluation slice or any product taxonomy.**
- **O4** — the change altered who arrived. The online population two weeks after release is not the online population before it, and the measured decline is a composition effect rather than a performance effect.

Groups diagnose each and commit, then the records are supplied in full.

**Then the room runs something.** For O3, groups take the evaluation run's own results and put the platform's **cluster analysis** on them — a preview feature that groups evaluation samples by semantic similarity, using embedding-based clustering of model outputs and feedback signals, and returns named clusters with an automatically generated description and recommended next steps. It is the closest thing on the platform to the method the room is about to need, it is one click, and the groups reach for it without being told to.

The payload is **O3**, and it is a payload twice. The gap is invisible in the aggregate, which the room expects after [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md). **The group is then not recoverable by slicing on any dimension the substrate records** — and it is not recoverable from the clusters either, unless the circumstance that defines it happens to show up in the shape of the output. **A clustering over responses returns classes of response. The class that broke is a class of person.** The room does not expect this, because every per-class exercise in the programme so far has supplied the classes and because the feature's own summary reads like an answer.

Task after: **for each case, state what you would have changed about the offline evaluation to have caught it — and for the one where nothing offline would have caught it, say so.** Then: **state what the cluster labels are labels of.**

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases holding the sign and rough size of the gap fixed and varying only its mechanism, so that no group can succeed by reading the magnitude. O3's second surprise is the phase's real content and it is now executed rather than described: the room applies a real automated class-discovery tool to a real result set and reads what it returns. That is what makes step 5 a technique rather than a formality — the class-discovery method is introduced because the room has just watched class-selection fail *and* watched the platform's own class-discovery answer a different question well. **The finding is not that the feature is poor.** It groups outputs and it says so. The finding is that a tool which returns confident, well-named, well-described groups will be read as having found the group, and the durable claim is [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md)'s: the construct you wanted and the construct the instrument measures are not the same object, and a good instrument for the wrong construct is more dangerous than a bad one.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **Two numbers that disagree are two measurements of two things.** Before asking which is right, ask what each measured. The question *which number do I believe* has no answer and the question *what differs between them* has five candidates.

2. **The five differences.** Population, dependency structure, criterion, feedback, and pipeline. **This list omits interference between changes released concurrently, which is [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s governance material, and the difference introduced by the act of measuring online — instrumentation overhead, sampling in the collection path — which is [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s. Safe as a diagnostic frame for attributing a gap on a single change you released and can inspect; unsafe as a complete account of divergence in a system releasing several changes at once, where the five cannot be separated and the correct move is to stop attributing and start staggering.**

3. **Population.** [S041](/wiki/seminars/S041.md) point 4's shift, now measured rather than estimated. The offline set was *drawn*; the online population *arrives*, and it arrives with a composition nobody chose. [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md)'s condition D at production scale: the users missing from your offline set are missing from your evidence that they are missing.

4. **Dependency structure.** [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) point 3. Offline your unit was an item; online your unit is a user, and users cluster by session, device, locale, tenure, access path and time. **An offline interval over items and an online outcome over users are not comparable even when both are computed correctly**, and the direction of the error is the one S049 point 4 named: ignoring positive clustering narrows, never widens.

5. **The criterion, and the proxy turn made visible.** [S054](/wiki/seminars/S054.md) point 5, live. The offline criterion improves; the online outcome improves, stops improving, and reverses; the offline criterion reports improvement throughout. **This is the first occasion in the programme where the turn can be watched rather than reconstructed**, and it is watchable only because the online outcome is a measure the offline criterion does not control.

6. **The aggregate hides the class, and online the class is a population of people.** [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md) point 6 restated for users. **The class that breaks is usually not in your taxonomy**, because taxonomies are built from properties of inputs and the class is defined by a circumstance of a person. A per-class report over your existing segments is a report about the classes you already thought of.

7. **Constructing the class after the results.** Take the worst-affected observations, group them, characterise what they share, and report that group alongside the pre-specified ones. It is ordinary practice, it is within reach, **and it is the only method in this programme that finds a class nobody enumerated.** [WP-053](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §8 named it as a fix its own day declined; it is introduced here.

8. **What an online measurement licenses.** It is also a sample: of a period, under one deployment, through one collection path, of the users who arrived and did not leave. It is a different measurement with a different error structure, not the truth against which the offline measurement is scored. [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) point 8's five things are owed for it too.

9. **Your online sample was drawn by a default.** Point 8 is usually taught as a caution. On this platform it is a parameter list. **Trace evaluation by agent filter samples up to `max_traces` traces, and the documented default strategy is uniform random sampling; the alternative is a service-managed heuristic that deliberately biases toward “interesting” traces — conversations with potential problems, edge cases or anomalies.** Those two produce different populations and therefore different numbers, and neither is wrong. **The conversation lookback defaults to seven days and seven days is also the documented maximum**, so a gap that opened eight days ago is not in your window at all. **Continuous evaluation scores a fraction at a rate you set**, so the online figure on the dashboard is a sample of a sample. **The trace-to-dataset flow turns intelligent sampling on by default and its row cap is documented as a ceiling rather than a guarantee**, with the advice to check how many samples you actually got. And **the portal's run comparison reports *inconclusive* for too few examples or p ≥ 0.05, and the comparison view is not saved** — a decision object that leaves no record, which is [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md) point 2 in a new place. Every one of those is somebody's opinion about your evidence, arriving as a default.

10. **The collection path is a source of error and it has a documented shape.** Point 2's list omits the difference introduced by the act of measuring, and gives it to [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md). Two documented instances are named here so that tomorrow's point 4 has something to attach to. **Trace evaluation reads span attributes; where the input and output message attributes are empty or missing, the quality evaluators return no score at all, and the safety evaluators still return scores that the documentation says might not be meaningful.** So a partially instrumented path yields a number that looks like a measurement and is not one. And **Application Insights takes on the order of a minute to ingest spans**, so a job submitted immediately after traffic runs against an empty window and produces nothing — an online result of zero rows, which is not an online result. The durable claim is [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md)'s second omitted error source: **the instrument is part of the system, and its failures are indistinguishable from the system's own unless you built them to be distinguishable.**

11. **What is perishable here and what is not.** Every number in point 9 will move: the sampling defaults, the seven-day ceiling, the strategy names, the preview labels on trace evaluation, cluster analysis and continuous evaluation. **None of it touches the durable claim**, which is that an online measurement is a sample drawn by a rule, that the rule is usually not yours, and that a number whose sampling rule you cannot state licenses nothing. **This frame is safe for reasoning about any production measurement you are handed — what was sampled, over what window, through what path. It is unsafe as a description of what these particular features will do next term, and it is unsafe read as a criticism of the tooling**: a documented default is better than an undocumented one, and the reason this platform makes a good teaching instance is precisely that it writes its defaults down.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Point 2 carries the strong-form scope statement. Points 6 and 7 are the retained pair and are the day's discharge of the transfer [WP-053](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §6 flagged as most likely to fail. Point 8 is the day's most important point and, as [WP-067](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-067.md) §8 concedes, the only one phase 3 does not demonstrate — **a concession the platform layer narrows but does not close, since point 9 makes the sampling rule concrete while still leaving the online-is-wrong case asserted.** Points 9 and 10 are the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s two required platform points, and point 11 carries the perishability boundary to the students.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 5. This phase produces a build. The change, the measurement path, the prediction, the attribution and the decision are the student's.**

Students use **the component and change they chose at [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md)**, and the gate they built for it.

1. **State the offline number**, its population, and its dependency structure — from your [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) work, using the cohort's pooled candidate-factor checklist, **which you must extend with at least one factor that exists only online.** **State also the judgement ceiling you computed at [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md) step 6, and whether your offline difference was above it or below it.**
2. **Predict the online number**, with the sign and size of the gap. Commit in writing.
3. **State which of the five differences you expect to dominate.** Commit.
4. **Build the online measurement path and release.** Using Copilot in agent mode through the Foundry MCP Server, stand up an evaluation over what actually happened — pre-existing responses, traces, or a continuous rule — then **release to a bounded share of live traffic** under the [Substrate and Infrastructure](/wiki/program/10-Substrate-and-Infrastructure.md) page's disclosure conditions, and measure. **Three things must be decided rather than accepted, each recorded with its reason: which responses or traces are in scope, by what sampling rule, and over what window.**
5. **Construct one user class after the results, from the residuals**, and report the outcome on it. State whether that class exists in the substrate's taxonomy, **and whether the platform's cluster analysis recovers it.** A negative answer to the second is a result and is worth stating — **and where the answer is negative, name the one attribute that would have had to be present in the data for an automated clustering to have found the group.** That sentence is tomorrow's work: an attribute you wish you had is a detection requirement.
6. **Attribute the gap** to one of the five differences, **with the evidence that distinguishes it from the other four.** An attribution with no distinguishing evidence is an assertion.
7. **State your measurement's own failure modes.** How many rows did you actually get against how many you asked for; how many scored `None` or were dropped; how many other changes were live in your window; and **what your online number would look like if the collection path had failed silently.**
8. **Decide** whether the [S066](/wiki/seminars/S066.md) gate that passed this change should change, and state what you would change — or state that it should not, and why. **State also whether your offline difference survived contact with users, and set that against the ceiling from step 1.**

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md): the **prompt record**, the **intervention record** — **no entries fails**, unless the measurement path itself demonstrates it could have detected an intervention-worthy error, of which step 7's row-count reconciliation is the obvious example and is accepted — and the **frontier statement**.

**Fading:** step 5's residual-grouping procedure is worked with the room, since class construction from residuals is new and technical. Step 4's traffic-split tooling and disclosure text are supplied. **Steps 3 and 7 are unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 5 with a real release at bounded exposure, now building the measurement path as well as consuming it. Step 3 before step 4 makes the attribution a pre-commitment rather than a rationalisation, which is the same construction [S053](/wiki/seminars/S053.md) step 3 used for sensitivity. Step 6's distinguishing-evidence clause is the day's discriminator. **Step 5 introduces the countermeasure [WP-053](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-053.md) §8 named and declined**; under rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 it enters the repertoire as **proposed**, this is its first use, and any later reuse is contingent on this day's reported outcome. **The ceiling clauses at steps 1 and 8 are the artefact element [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md) named under instrumentation rule 6**: the ceiling that day computed becomes a predictor here, tested against which offline differences survived contact with users. **Step 7 is new with the build and is the day's answer to point 10** — it is the cheapest available staging of point 8, since a student who reconciles rows requested against rows scored has measured their own instrument rather than been told to distrust it.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate change whose offline metric improved, whose online aggregate improved, and whose completion rate for the smallest of four locales fell for eleven weeks. Attention is directed at two facts — **locale was not a dimension in any dashboard**, and **every dashboard was correct.** The eleven weeks ended when a user wrote in.

Then the agent case: a Copilot-built online measurement that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. Asked to measure how the change was performing in production, it wrote a clean trace-based evaluation, filtered by agent, ran it, and reported a healthy score with a sample size. The code was correct. The filter was correct. The evaluators were appropriate. **It asked for a large number of traces and got back a much smaller number, and it reported the score without reporting that.** The row cap it had set is documented as a ceiling rather than a guarantee; the window it chose was inside the documented lookback maximum but began before the deployment; and the sample it scored was drawn by whichever strategy was in force by default. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the reviewer checked the query and the reviewer checked the score, and the number that mattered was the one between them.** A student who had asked *how many rows did you actually get* would have caught it in one line, and nobody asks that of a number that looks right.

Then students, in threes: a result you have reported that was true of everyone and false of somebody — **or a number you accepted today whose denominator you did not check.**

**Warrant.** Error management targeting points 6, 7 and 10. The instructor case contains no faulty measurement and no faulty reasoning; the failure is that the class was not a dimension, which is the failure that residual construction exists to catch and which no amount of care about the existing dimensions would have prevented. The ending is deliberate: the only detector that worked was a person, which is the observation [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) opens on. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition** and is chosen because it is the same failure as the instructor's in the students' own hands — a correct instrument reporting correctly on a population nobody characterised — and because step 7 of the phase 5 artefact is exactly the check that would have caught it.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **their step 6 attribution and its distinguishing evidence**, withholding step 2's predicted gap.

The partner has two tasks:

- **Produce a second of the five differences consistent with the same observed gap.**
- **Name the measurement that would distinguish the two.**

Then, together: which of the two is better supported by the evidence the student already has?

Per rule 3, the outcome is recorded in the artefact, including a partner who could construct no alternative, which is a result about the attribution rather than an absence of one.

**The [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) phase 7 found-factor rate is retaken here**, on the online population rather than the offline dataset, and the two rates are compared at phase 8. That comparison is the instrument S049 carried to this day.

Per rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8, this reuses [S052](/wiki/seminars/S052.md)'s alternative-explanation structure. No day has run, so the reuse is **contingent** on S052's reported outcome and a reported failure there retracts it here.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation applying the day's own frame to the student's own conclusion. Withholding the predicted gap prevents the partner reasoning backwards from the number, which is [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) phase 7's construction and the reason the found-factor rates are comparable across the two days.

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- **Sealed items opened.** Item 1's predicted gaps against the measured gaps, **split by sign**, since over- and under-prediction of an online result are different errors.
- Item 2's single-reason answers against the five differences, and item 3's opposite-direction explanations read back — how many students had a frame that could produce a gap in both directions before phase 4.
- **Item 4's named groups against step 5's constructed classes.** The cohort total for how many constructed classes were **not** in the substrate's taxonomy is the day's most quoted result. **The second total is how many were recovered by cluster analysis**, which is the executed version of the same question.
- Step 3's predicted dominant difference against step 6's attribution, shown as a matrix.
- **Item 5 opened.** The sealed Copilot predictions against what each agent actually did, with the cohort hit rate recorded and split into predicting a defect and predicting a decision.
- **The step 7 reconciliations pooled**: rows requested against rows scored, across the cohort, as a distribution. **This is the day's cheapest evidence for point 8** and it is the students' own.
- **The [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md) found-factor rate, offline and online, side by side.** The pooled online-only factors are added to the substrate's measurement guidance, extending the checklist S049 created.
- **The [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md) ceiling against survival**, as a two-by-two: offline differences above and below the ceiling, against those that did and did not survive contact with users. **This is the cohort's only test of whether a judgement ceiling predicts anything about production**, and it is the response [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md) named for its no-result rate.
- Each student names **one number they currently trust whose online counterpart has never been measured.**

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in ME301 with SY301. Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the offline number with its population and dependency structure, including at least one online-only factor **and the [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md) ceiling with the difference's position relative to it**; the pre-committed gap prediction; the pre-committed dominant difference; **the built online measurement path, with its three decided settings — scope, sampling rule and window — each carrying the reason it was decided that way in the same field**; the online measurement; one user class constructed after the results from the residuals, with a statement of whether it is in the taxonomy **and whether cluster analysis recovered it, and where it did not, the attribute that would have been needed**; the attribution with its distinguishing evidence; **the measurement's own failure modes, including rows requested against rows scored and the count of concurrent changes in the window**; the gate decision **with the survival statement**; **the prompt record**; **the intervention record**; **the frontier statement**; and the phase 7 outcome. **Nothing else is assessed.**

**An attribution with no evidence distinguishing it from the other four differences fails.** This is the day's single hard requirement and it is step 6's second clause.

**A per-class report drawn only from the substrate's existing taxonomy fails.** The constructed class is not optional.

**An online number reported without its denominator fails.** Rows requested and rows scored, in the same field, per specification rule 2.

**A sampling rule accepted at its default with no stated reason fails that element.** Accepting the documented default is frequently correct; accepting it without knowing it existed is the day's subject.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the measurement path itself demonstrates it could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 2, the online number is reported with its collection period, its deployment version and its traffic share **in the same field**, not in a note.

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S070](/wiki/seminars/S070.md).

**Rule-6 discharge.** [S059](/wiki/seminars/S059.md) named this day's phase 5 as the response occasion for its step 6 no-result rate, specifying that each student's offline difference be compared against the online outcome and that the ceiling be tested as a predictor of which offline differences survived contact with users. **The element exists and is assessed: step 1 carries the ceiling and the difference's position relative to it, step 8 carries the survival statement, and phase 8 reports the two-by-two for the cohort.** The obligation is discharged.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions:

- **The step 3-against-step 6 attribution matrix** measures which of the five differences the cohort can anticipate and which they only recognise afterwards. **Response occasion: [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md), phase 3, whose four monitoring designs are selected against whichever difference the cohort could not anticipate, so that the detection work targets the blind spot rather than the familiar case.**
- **The step 5 novel-class rate** — how many students' residual-constructed class was absent from the substrate's taxonomy. **Response occasion: [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md), phase 5 step 7, where each detection design must cover at least one class the taxonomy does not contain.**

**A third instrument, under rule 9.** The **item 5 sealed prediction accuracy and the step 7 row reconciliations** are this day's contribution to the running frontier map and to the cohort's sampling-discipline record. Per instrumentation rule 9 the reader is named: **the following year's cohort, through the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md)**. Per instrumentation rule 3 the sampling is disclosed to the students: one cohort, one substrate, disjoint traffic slices, one measurement window, on four cases staff selected for having four separable mechanisms.

## Perishable content in this day

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| Two numbers that disagree are two measurements of two things | An offline dataset evaluation and a trace or response evaluation, run by the same service on the same change |
| An online measurement is a sample drawn by a rule | `max_traces`; uniform random sampling as the documented default; the alternative heuristic that biases toward interesting traces |
| The window is part of the measurement | The seven-day lookback default, which is also the documented maximum |
| The aggregate hides the class, and the class is a person | Cluster analysis, which groups evaluation samples by output similarity and returns named clusters with descriptions |
| The instrument is part of the system | Quality evaluators returning no score where span message attributes are missing, while safety evaluators still return one |
| A result with no denominator is not a result | A row cap documented as a ceiling rather than a guarantee, with the advice to check what you actually got |
| A decision object that leaves no record is not reviewable | A run comparison that is not saved when you leave the page |

**All of the right-hand column is perishable and is marked so at point 11.** Three of the capabilities used today — trace evaluation, continuous evaluation and cluster analysis — are labelled preview by their own documentation. The left-hand column is what the specification assesses.

Also perishable: every gap magnitude in phase 3, the four cases' figures, the substrate's current segmentation, and the specific group in O3. All are properties of one system over one period.

**Local:** the four released changes, the eleven-month-old comparison in item 3, the phase 6 cases, the traffic-split tooling, and the residual-grouping harness.
