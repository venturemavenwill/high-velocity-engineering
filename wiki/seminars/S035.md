## The situation

This is an assessment occasion. You have run an investigation and you now have to write what you found, to somebody who wanted a different answer, in a form that does not tell them what to do.

The last clause is the one candidates fight. **A finding of fact and a recommendation are different documents**, and the reason to keep them apart is not modesty: a recommendation absorbs the evidence into a conclusion, and the reader who disagrees with your conclusion loses access to your evidence along with it.

Answer these in writing before you begin, and submit them.

1. What was the question, as chartered, and did the investigation answer it?
2. What did the investigation cost — your time, other people's time, and anything you consumed?
3. What is the cheaper investigation you can now see, having done this one?
4. What in this finding will the sponsor dislike, and what will they do with it?
5. What has the spike's code done since the charter closed?

**Item 3 is not a trick.** Almost every completed investigation reveals a cheaper one, and naming it is how a practice gets less expensive rather than more thorough.

## What you must produce

A **feasibility finding** in five parts, submitted with the charter it closes, and defended in front of an assessor who has read the charter and nothing else.

- **Goal** — the chartered question, unchanged, or the change recorded with its date and reason.
- **Method** — what you did, in enough detail that somebody else could do it again and get a comparable result.
- **Evidence** — what you observed, separated from what you concluded. Includes the inputs, who chose them, and the ones that did not work.
- **Conclusions** — statements of fact, each traceable to a line in the evidence.
- **Next steps** — the options now open, with what each would cost. **Not a recommendation.**

Submitted alongside it: the [S032](/wiki/seminars/S032.md) pre-mortem record, the [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md) question set with its outcomes, the [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md) data reality record, and the disposal statement for the spike's code.

## The material

### A finding of fact and a recommendation are different documents

Candidates fight this and the fight is worth having in the open, because the reason is not modesty.

**A recommendation absorbs its own evidence.** Once a document says *we should proceed*, every observation in it becomes an argument for proceeding, and a reader who disagrees with the conclusion has no way to keep the observations. They have to reject the whole document or accept the whole document, and in practice they reject it — which means the fortnight you spent finding things out has produced nothing that survives disagreement.

A finding of fact fails differently and better. A reader who rejects your reading of the evidence still has the evidence. **The document's value survives its author being wrong**, which is the only kind of value an investigation can honestly claim.

This is not a preference. The published protocol behind this module states it flatly: **"The goal of a spike should be fact-finding, not decision-making or recommendation"** ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md), page read 2026-07-30). The same source supplies the five-part shape this session assesses — goal, method, evidence, conclusions, next steps — and **the separation of evidence from conclusions is the entire reason the shape has five parts rather than three.** That publication offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices, contradicts itself in at least a dozen places without noticing, and asserts four unsupported magnitude claims which this repository records as specimens and reproduces nowhere.

### The two sentences that look like observations and are not

Go through your evidence section and mark every sentence as something you observed or something you concluded. The exercise is harder here than it was at [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md), because a technical investigation produces conclusions that feel like observations.

*The run failed.* That is an observation, if you can say what the failure was.

*The latency was unacceptable.* That is a conclusion. The observation is a measurement; the unacceptability is a judgement against a threshold somebody chose, and the threshold belongs in the conclusions section beside the person who set it.

*The extract was incomplete.* That is a conclusion, and a compound one. The observation is that the extract contained fewer records than described, or that a field was empty in some proportion of rows. **Incompleteness is a comparison against an expectation, and the expectation is the part a reader might disagree with.**

**Two of those three are conclusions**, and a candidate who cannot see which two has not yet separated the sections.

### The price clause, and why the module's question has one in it

The stage's question is *is it feasible, and how cheaply can you find out?* — and the second half is assessed here because nothing else in the programme assesses it.

A proof whose price is unstated **cannot be compared to the alternative you did not run.** That is the whole argument, and it is why the finding must state its cost in the units it was actually paid in: your time, other people's time, anything consumed, and the calendar the investigation occupied while somebody was waiting.

The internal practice corpus is instructive here by its absence. **It reports no cost side of any kind** — every claim in it is a gross benefit, and the cost of the discovery activity itself is never counted anywhere ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). It also records no failed engagement, and its only failure narrative is synthetic and labelled fictional, so it cannot supply a case in which a cheap proof averted anything. **A practice that never counts what its own methods cost cannot say which of them is worth running**, and this session's price clause is the repair. It is access-controlled and paraphrased here, never quoted, and it carries not one external citation in roughly ninety documents.

### The smallest artefact that can answer the question, and no larger

The platform work in this session is a discipline exercise rather than a build.

The hosting fork on this platform is unusually clean and it is the one to reason from. **Prompt agents** use "declarative config in portal or code", are "managed by Foundry with no agent compute", and suit "agents that don't include custom code or require custom hosting". **Hosted agents** run "your code (containerized)" on managed container compute ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). **The line between them is whether you are running code, and therefore whether you are paying for and operating compute.**

For a feasibility artefact that is the only question worth asking: **does answering my chartered question require my code?** If it does not, a prompt agent is the artefact, and reaching for the containerised option is scope the charter did not authorise. If it does, the charter's cost line changes and the finding must say so.

The same documentation family supplies two constraints a feasibility artefact should not quietly cross. The **basic setup "lacks network isolation"** and is positioned for rapid prototyping, while the standard setup provides fine-grained control over data, security and networking — **so a feasibility artefact built on the fast topology is not a production topology, and the transition between them is a deliberate act rather than a configuration detail found at the end.** And the selection criteria for any of this are stated as **"your organization's technical capabilities, timeline, and control requirements"**, which is the same triple your charter's bound was written against.

**The limits travel with all of it.** This is first-party documentation with a selection-guidance purpose, so **its authority is definitional**: it says what the products are, not that choosing them this way works. It publishes **no adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no evidence that its recommended mappings produce better outcomes.** It was read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches, with several comparison tables truncated. **And it is highly perishable** — product names and capability boundaries are actively changing, so every platform statement in your finding carries the date you read it.

**The durable claim underneath is the one the assessor is listening for: an artefact built larger than its question cannot be read as evidence about that question**, because you can no longer say which part of it produced the result.

### What a small run licenses, and what it does not

A feasibility run is an experiment and inherits every limit of one.

A reported result needs to say how many cases it rests on, and where the cases arrive in related groups — several questions about one document, one question in several forms, several runs of one prompt — **a naive treatment will make the result look more precise than it is** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/miller-adding-error-bars-to-evals.md)). The discipline that belongs before the run is the question *what difference could this have detected*, and without it **the sentence *we found no problem* and the sentence *we could not have found this problem* are the same sentence** — and the finished document does not distinguish them.

**A finding from a small sample that states no uncertainty overclaims**, and it overclaims in the direction that flatters whoever ran it. Two further disciplines from the same source transfer directly. **Where two options were exercised on the same cases, compare them case by case rather than by their separate totals** — the options agree about which cases are hard, and the pairing removes that shared variation at no cost. And **never suppress variance by changing a setting whose purpose is something else**; making a comparison look cleaner by reducing the randomness of one side shifts what is being measured rather than measuring it better.

**That source is analytical rather than empirical**, its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions, and it addresses comparing scores rather than choosing what to score — so a tight result around a badly chosen question is still meaningless. **No number from it appears here.**

### Why this is assessed as a narrative and not as a score

The assessment literature this programme's architecture rests on is specific about what a judgement of this kind should record.

When assessors read an accumulated evidentiary record, **they rely mainly on narrative to form their judgement, and use grades and qualifications to confirm impressions the narrative already produced** ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). The numbers are not the evidence; they are a check on a conclusion already reached from prose.

Two further findings from that work shape how your defence will actually go, and you should know both.

**Assessors anchor early.** They form a reading and then look for confirming or disconfirming evidence, and they are **inclined to stick to initial judgements even when confronted with seemingly disconfirming evidence** — final judgements were more elaborate than preliminary ones but not substantially different. **The practical consequence is that your opening matters more than it should**, and the assessor knows this about themselves, which is why the specification lists elements rather than inviting an overall impression.

**Agreement on the outcome conceals disagreement in the reasoning.** Assessors reached similar pass-and-fail judgements while differing in what they treated as trustworthy evidence and in what they thought competence was. The recommended repair is **group discussion with substantiated judgements** rather than better rating scales, because standardising the instrument does not standardise the judgement.

**The limits are important and are not small.** These are **think-aloud studies from medical education**, from a single institution and portfolio type in the primary source; the method cannot capture fast or automatic processing and carries the risk that participants construct explanations for the researcher. **This programme's transfer of the finding to an engineering assessment is an extrapolation and is recorded as one.** No figure from that literature is reproduced anywhere in this wiki.

### The defence, and why it asks about what did not happen

The assessor has your charter and nothing else, and the conversation is a short focused one after the activity — the form the entrustment literature calls an entrustment-based discussion. **Its defining requirement is that it probe deep understanding of what was done and include "what-if" questions**, to establish whether the candidate would know what to do in situations that did not in fact arise ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)).

**The what-if requirement is what distinguishes a defence from a walkthrough.** Retrospective explanation shows you understood the case you met. Counterfactual questioning is the only available evidence about the cases you did not meet — and the framework is explicit about why that matters: an entrustment decision **implies the acceptance of risk**, because it can never be fully predicted what a person will encounter.

So the three questions are not arbitrary. *What did this cost* tests whether you counted. *What would you do more cheaply now* tests whether the investigation taught you anything about investigating. *What did you stop before finding out* tests whether you understand that a time-boxed investigation stops by design rather than by exhaustion. **A candidate who answers nothing to the third has misunderstood the instrument.**

**That source is a conceptual and practical primer by the concept's originator**, synthesising a decade of medical-education literature. It is guidance rather than evaluation; it tells you what to build and what its builders learned, and does not claim to have measured the result. Its transfer to engineering is this programme's inference.

## The workflow

**Build the smallest artefact that can answer your chartered question, run it, and assemble the finding. The discipline being assessed is the *and no larger*.**

**Step 1 — write the question the artefact must answer, from the charter, unchanged.** If it changed, record the change with its date and reason. **A question edited silently is a question selected in the presence of the answer.**

**Step 2 — decide the hosting fork on one criterion.** Does answering this question require your code? If not, the artefact is a **prompt agent** — declarative configuration, no agent compute. If it does, it is a **hosted agent** running your container, **and your charter's cost line changes**, which the finding must say. Reaching for the containerised option because it is more capable is scope the charter did not authorise, and it is the thing this step exists to catch.

**Step 3 — write the configuration and keep it hostile to growth.**

```yaml
# agents/feasibility-probe.agent.yaml
# The smallest artefact that can answer F2 from docs/feasibility/questions.yaml, and no larger.
name: feasibility-probe
kind: prompt                 # declarative config, no agent compute.
                             # If this ever needs to run our code it becomes a hosted agent,
                             # and the charter's cost line changes. Say so in the finding.
model: <deployment-name>     # record the catalogue entry AND the date you read it
instructions: |
  You answer exactly one question and refuse every other request.

  QUESTION: For the case supplied, name the passage a handler would need,
  or state "NO SUCH PASSAGE IN THE SUPPLIED CONTEXT".

  - Answer ONLY from the supplied context. Never from general knowledge.
  - If the context does not contain the answer, say so. Do not construct one.
  - Do NOT recommend anything. Do NOT say whether this approach is viable.
  - Output exactly two lines: PASSAGE or NO SUCH PASSAGE, then the source id.
tools: []                    # empty on purpose.
                             # A tool added here is scope the charter did not authorise,
                             # and it makes the result unattributable to the question.
topology: basic              # prototyping topology. It lacks network isolation.
                             # Recorded here so the finding cannot present it as production.
```

**The empty tool list and the refusal instruction are the assessed discipline, not the configuration.** An artefact that can also summarise, also route and also explain is an artefact whose result you cannot attribute to your question.

**Step 4 — run it over the input set you did not choose**, with the sampling frame from [S033](/wiki/seminars/S033.md) stated before you look at the outcome. Record the inputs, who assembled them, and the ones that produced nothing.

**Step 5 — write the evidence section, then mark every sentence.** Observed or concluded. Move the second kind out. **Do this before you write the conclusions**, because a conclusions section written first will pull observations into itself.

**Step 6 — write what the run could not have detected.** One sentence, in the evidence section, naming the case type your inputs cannot cover and the difference the run would have missed.

**Step 7 — write the cost, including other people's time**, and the disposal, as an act with a date. **If code was retained, say so plainly, name the review it went through and under whose authority.** Retention reported honestly is a pass; retention omitted is the element's failure.

**Step 8 — attack the finding, at two named targets.** Give the agent your charter and your finding and ask it to argue **that the investigation was unnecessary** — that the question was already settled by something the team knew, or that a cheaper instrument existed. Then give it your evidence section alone and ask it to **name the sentences that are conclusions.** Keep what is better than your own reasoning; record what you rejected and why. **An agent that congratulated you was not used as an instrument.**

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** That an artefact grows without anybody deciding to grow it. You will feel the pull towards adding a tool, then towards a second question, then towards making it demonstrable — and each step is individually reasonable and collectively converts a probe into a prototype. **The empty tool list is the only place in this module where you can watch that happen and decline it**, and declining it is what makes the run's result attributable to the question you chartered.

## What good looks like

These are the assessed elements. Each is checked as present and adequate, or not.

- **The finding states facts and does not recommend.** A conclusion beginning *we should* is a failed element regardless of how good the reasoning behind it is.
- **Evidence is separable from conclusion.** A reader can reject a conclusion and still use the evidence, and the document's structure is what makes that possible.
- **The bound was declared before the work and its outcome is reported** — respected, exceeded, or reached without an answer. **Reached without an answer is a pass.**
- **The cost of the investigation is stated**, in the units it was actually paid in.
- **At least one question was answered negatively**, or the finding states that none was and says why that should be treated as suspicious.
- **The blocking absences from [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md) are carried into the finding**, unsmoothed, and are not converted into risks with mitigations.
- **The disposal is reported as an act.** Where the code was retained, the finding says so, says who reviewed it as production code, and says under whose authority.
- **The artefact is no larger than its question.** Every capability in the submitted configuration is required by the chartered question, and the finding names the topology it ran on. **A prototyping topology presented as production is a failed element.**
- **The finding names the decision it enables and the role who takes it.**

**Nothing else is assessed.** Whether the thing turned out to be feasible is not marked. The quality of the spike's code is not marked. Whether the artefact worked is not marked. Which model you chose is not marked. The number of questions in the set is not marked. Whether the sponsor accepted the finding is not marked. The document's presentation is not marked. Whether your conclusions match anybody else's is not marked.

## How it goes wrong

- **The recommendation in the conclusion** — the finding tells the reader what to do, and the evidence becomes decorative. *Warning sign:* your conclusions section is shorter than your next-steps section.
- **The rescued bound** — the limit was extended, quietly, because the answer was nearly there. *Warning sign:* your method describes more work than your charter authorised and does not say so.
- **The absent cost** — the finding reports what was learned and not what it took. *Warning sign:* your document would read identically if the investigation had taken substantially longer.
- **The confirmed set** — every question came back yes. *Warning sign:* you are pleased with the finding.
- **The artefact that grew** — a tool was added, then a second question, then a demonstration mode, each individually reasonable. *Warning sign:* you can no longer say which part of the artefact produced the result.
- **The smoothed data** — [S034](/wiki/seminars/S034.md)'s blocking absence has become a line in a risk table. *Warning sign:* a thing that was blocking now has a mitigation.
- **The quiet retention** — the code was kept and the finding does not mention it. *Warning sign:* there is no disposal line at all.
- **The unreadable evidence** — the evidence section is a narrative in which observations and inferences are interleaved. *Warning sign:* a reader has to trust you in order to use it.

## Exercises

**These are assessed, and they are the submission.** Exercises 1 to 4 form the finding and its accompanying records; exercise 5 is the defence. **The assessed elements are the ones listed under *What good looks like*, and nothing else is marked.**

**1 · Separate the sections.** Take your evidence section and mark every sentence *observed* or *concluded*. Move the concluded ones. Submit both versions.

> **Scoring criterion.** An element passes if a reader can **reject a conclusion and still use the evidence.** The test is applied by removing your conclusions section entirely and asking whether the remaining document still supports somebody else reaching a different conclusion. **Sentences most often misfiled: anything containing *unacceptable*, *incomplete*, *insufficient*, *too slow* or *not viable* — each is a comparison against a threshold somebody chose, and the threshold and the chooser belong in the conclusions.**

**2 · Put the negative first.** Find the question that came back no and open with it. If none did, write the paragraph explaining why an investigation that confirmed everything is a weaker instrument than one that did not, and what you would change about your question set.

> **Scoring criterion.** The paragraph passes if it identifies a **mechanism** rather than expressing suspicion. **"Every question was chosen by the person who wanted the design to work, and none of them had an outcome that would have been inconvenient" passes.** "This seems too good" fails. **A confirming result is not a defect and this element does not treat it as one** — what is assessed is whether you can say why it is weaker evidence than a disconfirming one.

**3 · State the price.** Write what the investigation cost in the units it was paid in, including the time of people who were not on your team.

> **Scoring criterion.** The element passes if the finding **would read differently had the investigation taken substantially longer.** A document that is indifferent to its own duration has not stated a price. **The corpus this programme draws on reports no cost side of any kind anywhere** — every claim in it is a gross benefit — which is precisely why this element exists ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md), **access-controlled and uncheckable by any reader of this page**).

**4 · Build it no larger.** Submit your agent configuration alongside the finding, with the tool list and the refusal instruction intact.

> **Scoring criterion.** The element passes if **every capability in the configuration is required by the chartered question**, and if the finding says which topology it ran on. **A prototyping topology presented as though it were production is a failed element**, because the documentation states plainly that the basic setup lacks network isolation and a finding that omits this has made a production claim it cannot support. **What is not assessed: whether the artefact worked, how elegant the configuration is, or which model you chose.**

**5 · Go back to your own item 5, then defend it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 5 — what the spike's code has done since the charter closed. **Do not rewrite it.** Put it beside the criterion below, write one sentence naming the difference, and submit both. The assessor will ask about the difference.

> **Criterion.** A disposal statement passes if it reports **an act, a date and a location**, and if any retention names a reviewer and an authority. **"The branch was deleted on the fourth" passes. "The code is not being used" fails**, because it describes a present state rather than an act and nothing in it can be checked next month. **Retention reported honestly is a pass.** The rule this module teaches is enforced by disclosure and not by resolve, which is why concealment fails the element and retention does not.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**6 · Answer the three questions, and expect a fourth you cannot prepare.** The assessor holds your charter and nothing else, and will ask what this cost, what you would do more cheaply now, and what you stopped before finding out. **They will then ask a what-if question about a situation that did not arise.**

> **What is assessed and what is not.** The three questions are assessed as elements. **The what-if is assessed as part of the defence and never on whether your answer is right**, because there is no right answer to a situation that did not occur — what is being established is whether you can reason about the case you did not meet. This is the form the entrustment literature specifies for a post-activity discussion: probe deep understanding, and **include what-if questions**, because an entrustment decision implies accepting risk about what a person will encounter ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md), **guidance from medical education rather than an evaluation of it; its transfer to engineering is this programme's inference**). **A candidate who says nothing stopped has misunderstood a time-boxed investigation.**

## What this does not establish

This assessment shows that you can bound an investigation, report it as fact and disclose its price. **It does not show that the investigation was the right one.**

Nothing in the material behind this module establishes which instrument settles which uncertainty most cheaply. The playbook supplies the protocol and **no comparative evidence for it** — true of that publication throughout, which also contradicts itself in at least a dozen places without noticing and asserts four unsupported magnitude claims recorded here as specimens and reproduced nowhere. The practice corpus supplies **no cost side anywhere**, so it cannot rank instruments by price, and **no failure**, so it cannot show one being averted; it is access-controlled and carries not one external citation in roughly ninety documents.

**So this session assesses the form, the disclosure and the discipline, and not the judgement.** That is a real limitation. A candidate could produce a perfectly formed finding about an unimportant question and pass every element here. The judgement is assessed at [S040](/wiki/seminars/S040.md), where the defence is of the price of the whole proof rather than of one investigation, and the gap between the two occasions is deliberate.

**The measurement discipline is analytical rather than empirical.** Its illustrative magnitudes rest on stylised assumptions and it addresses comparing scores rather than choosing what to score — so a tight result around a badly chosen question is still meaningless, and no number from it appears here.

**The assessment design's warrant comes from medical education and its transfer is an extrapolation.** The finding that assessors rely on narrative and use scores to confirm impressions comes from think-aloud studies at a single institution with one portfolio type; the method cannot capture fast or automatic processing and carries the risk that participants construct explanations for the researcher. The entrustment framework is **guidance by the concept's originator rather than an evaluation of it** — it states what to build and what its builders learned, and does not claim to have measured the result. **No figure from either is reproduced anywhere in this wiki.** That this transfers to an engineering defence is this programme's inference and is recorded as one.

**The platform claims are first-party, definitional and highly perishable.** The prompt-agent and hosted-agent distinction, the network-isolation statement and the selection criteria are what the vendor says its products are, not evidence that choosing them this way works. There is **no adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no outcome evidence of any kind**, the material was read through search-returned excerpts rather than full page fetches, and product names and capability boundaries are actively changing. **Every platform statement in your finding carries the date you read it or it carries nothing.**

**And the strongest claim in the session has no warrant at all.** That an artefact built larger than its question cannot be read as evidence about that question is **design reasoning**, held because attribution fails when capability is added, and offered as an argument.
