**Whitepaper:** [WP-030](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-030.md)

## The situation

This is an assessment occasion and it is the stage's class boundary.

Every previous assessment in this programme told you what to produce. **This one does not.** You choose the scope at which your design is written, and the scope you choose determines the standard you will be marked against.

Answer these in writing before you begin, and submit them.

1. What decision does this design commit you to, and for how long?
2. At what scope should it be written — a task, a feature, a milestone, or an investigation?
3. **Name the heading your chosen scope requires that the scope below it does not.**
4. Who runs this after it exists?
5. What in your design is still open, and are you allowed to leave it open?

**Items 3 and 4 are the same question.** The published convention this session uses escalates from feature scope to milestone scope by adding exactly one heading, and that heading is about operation. A design that leaves the scope of a single piece of work **acquires a runtime owner**, and that acquisition is the whole content of the escalation.

## What you must produce

- **One design document**, at the scope you chose, following that scope's heading list.
- **A one-paragraph defence of the scope choice**, submitted with it.
- **The constraint register from [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md)**, attached as the design's frozen inputs.
- **One architecture decision record** for the design's largest irreversible choice, in which the rejected option has been argued at its strongest by an adversarial reading you did not write yourself.

The document is marked as in progress while it is in progress, and the mark is removed when it is not.

## The material

### Four scopes, and the single heading that separates two of them

The convention this session uses publishes **four design-review templates**, each with an explicit heading list and each requiring a `[DRAFT/WIP]` prefix in the title while the document is in progress ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md), pages read 2026-07-30).

The **task** template is short: overview and problem statement, goals and scope, non-goals, **proposed options**, technology choices, open questions, resources. Note what it has that the others do not — options, because a task-sized decision is usually a choice between two ways of doing one thing.

The **feature or story** template is the working default: overview and problem statement, goals and in-scope, non-goals and out-of-scope, proposed design, technology, non-functional requirements, dependencies, risks and mitigation, open questions, resources.

The **milestone or epic** template is **the same list plus exactly one heading — operationalisation — inserted between non-functional requirements and dependencies.**

The **technical spike** template is a different shape entirely: goal, method, evidence, conclusions, next steps. It also carries a constraint that binds what you may do with its output: **"The goal of a spike should be fact-finding, not decision-making or recommendation"**, and separately that **the code written for a spike is not going to serve as the code for the final solution.**

**The one-heading escalation is the claim this session is built on**, and it is worth stating precisely: leaving the scope of a single piece of work adds exactly one obligation, and that obligation is to say who runs the thing afterwards. **It is precise, attractive, checkable in minutes against any design document you have ever written, and it is a convention rather than a finding.** Nobody has shown it holds. It is taught because a claim you can check against your own artefacts is worth more than one you cannot.

### The decision record has a specific shape, and two of its fields are doing real work

The same convention publishes an architecture decision record with named fields: an ascending number and a title; a date; a status drawn from **"[Proposed/Accepted/Deprecated/Superseded]"**; a context section that must be **"a value-neutral description of the forces at play"**; a decision section whose sentence begins **"We will…"** or **"We have agreed to…"**; and a consequences section. Records live under a fixed directory and are **"added as pull request in the proposed status"**, so a record enters review as a proposal rather than arriving as a settled fact. Where several are written in parallel, the collision rule is to give a record the identifier of the work item it relates to.

Two of those fields are the ones that matter for this assessment. **The value-neutral context is the hardest sentence in the format to write honestly**, because it must describe forces without implying the conclusion, and a context written after the decision almost always leaks the decision. And **the status vocabulary contains no value for "we changed our mind"** — a decision is superseded by a new record rather than edited, which is why the record is durable and the design document is not.

The surrounding process supplies two more rules worth carrying. Design documents live in a reviewable directory and go through pull request, with the instruction that **"After two round trips of question/response, resort to synchronous communication for resolution"**. And the standard states the maintenance rule bluntly: **"Artifacts that are not up to date are `bugs` in the design."** Where a review does not converge, the published rule is **"Disagree and commit!"** — a rule about proceeding, not about agreeing.

**The limits on all of this, and they are substantial.** The publication offers **no comparative evidence for any of its practices and does not claim to.** Its pages carry footer dates spread across several years, roughly a fifth of the site is stubs or link lists, and **it contradicts itself in at least a dozen places without noticing** — including on the very topic this session's sibling covers, where its comparison template and index disagree and its own worked example fails both. It also asserts **four unsupported magnitude claims**, one of them a cost outcome offered with no evidence, no baseline and no source. **This repository records those four as specimens and reproduces none of them.** Cite the publication by page and by the date that page was read, never as a whole.

### Why a design at the wrong scope is an assessment target rather than a style preference

Writing at a larger scope than the decision requires is failed here for the same reason as writing at a smaller one, and the reason is not tidiness.

An oversized design document commits reviewers' attention to headings the decision does not need, which is a cost. More importantly, **it manufactures the appearance of an operational commitment nobody made**: a design carrying an operationalisation section implies somebody accepted a runtime obligation, and if nobody did, the document has created a fiction that will be discovered at handover. An undersized one does the opposite and is the commoner failure — the decision commits somebody to running something and the document has no place to say who.

The production-systems literature supplies the same distinction from a different direction, and its version is sharper because it is about cost rather than ceremony: **ship pipelines, not models, once a thing has users — and do not build a pipeline for an experiment that has none** ([research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/crowe-machine-learning-production-systems.md)). Scope follows commitment, not ambition. **That book is a survey by its own description, offers no comparative evidence for any recommended practice, has a non-neutral toolchain, and every figure in it is a property of a particular system; none is reproduced here.**

### The assessment is a specification check, and that choice has a literature behind it

Seven elements are checked as present and adequate, or not. Nothing outside the list is considered — not the quality of the architecture, not the technology, and not whether the assessor would have chosen your scope.

That last exclusion is the one worth understanding, and it follows from the entrustment frame this programme's assessment architecture is built on ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). An entrustable professional activity is a unit of practice that can be handed over; the judgement is whether a supervisor would let this person do this unsupervised. **Writing a design at the scope its decision warrants is a plausible activity. Choosing the scope an assessor prefers is not** — it is agreement, and marking agreement measures compliance rather than judgement.

The same reasoning explains why the scope defence is a paragraph rather than a score. Judgements of this kind carry their useful information in the *reasons*, which is the argument the narrative-assessment literature makes for recording reasoned narrative rather than a mark ([research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/govaerts-narrative-assessment.md)). Your defence is assessed on whether it engages the decision the design actually carries, not on whether it persuades.

### Your instrument is unreliable in a known direction, and this is the last time this stage says so

You will use an agent to attack your own decision record, and one warning belongs beside that instruction.

Capability in these systems is **jagged** — the boundary between what they handle well and what they do not is uneven and does not track how hard a task feels to a person ([research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md](/research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md)). Outside that boundary you get a persuasive wrong answer in the same register as the correct ones. **That note rests on an abstract; its shape is usable and no number in it is, and none is asserted.**

The design consequence is the one this stage has made four times and makes once more here. **The agent is given the task of arguing a position, which it is good at, and is given no authority over the decision, which it is not.** An adversarial reading that fails to move you is still worth having in the record; an adversarial reading you did not ask for is a decision record with one voice in it.

The platform documentation is worth one sentence of caution too. Agent mode determines its own steps and iterates rather than stopping after a single response, and requests confirmation before running a terminal command or a tool that is not built in — **with that confirmation being a dismissible default rather than a boundary**, auto-confirmable per session, per solution or permanently ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). The same family carries the fact your consequences section most needs: **a decision taken at the start binds at the end** — publishing route is constrained by build choice — which is exactly the class of consequence a decision record exists to make visible before it is irreversible. **Highly perishable; read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches; no adoption, cost or outcome data of any kind.**

## The workflow

**Write the decision record before the design, and make the agent argue the option you are about to reject.**

**Step 1 — choose the scope and write the defence before you write anything else.** Doing it in this order is the only protection against choosing a scope because it suited the document you had already begun.

**Step 2 — open the decision record in the proposed status, as a pull request.** Number it, date it, and write the context section first — value-neutral, forces only, no conclusion. **If your context makes the decision obvious, you wrote it after deciding**, and the record is a ratification rather than a proposal.

```markdown
<!-- doc/adr/0014-retrieval-boundary.md  (opened as a PR in Proposed status)   -->
<!-- The convention names the fields, not the heading depth. Depth is yours.   -->
# 14. Retrieval boundary for the determinations service

Date: 2026-07-31
Status: Proposed        <!-- Proposed | Accepted | Deprecated | Superseded -->
Work item: 4471         <!-- collision rule: use the work item's identifier -->

### Context
<!-- Value-neutral. Forces only. If a reader can tell what we decided from
     this section alone, it was written after the decision and must be redone. -->
- The permission estate is owned outside this engagement; its authority is
  recorded at docs/constraints/register.yaml#C-014 (status: named).
- The production topology requires network isolation; the topology we can
  provision does not have it.
- An incumbent service already answers a narrower version of this question.

### Decision
We will ...

### Consequences
<!-- Include the ones that bind later from a choice made now. -->
- Positive:
- Negative:
- Binding: publishing route is constrained by this choice and cannot be
  revisited after the first release without a superseding record.

### Rejected options
#### Option B - <name>
**Argued at its strongest by an adversarial reading, not by its opponent.**
<!-- Paste the agent's best case here, edited only for accuracy.
     Then, beneath it, the single observation that settled the disagreement. -->
- Strongest case:
- The observation that decided against it:
- What would have to change for this record to be superseded:
```

**Step 3 — make the agent argue the rejected option at its strongest.** Give it the context section and the rejected option only. Withhold your decision and your reasoning. **An agent given your conclusion will elaborate it; an agent given only the forces may not reach it**, and the difference between the two runs is the value of the exercise.

**Step 4 — paste the strongest version into the record, edited only for accuracy.** Do not soften it. A rejected option recorded weakly is a record that cannot be reopened honestly when the constraint moves, and constraints move.

**Step 5 — write the observation that settled it**, and the condition under which this record would be superseded rather than edited. **The status vocabulary has no value for "we changed our mind"**, which is the format telling you that a reversal is a new record and a new date.

**Step 6 — write the design at the chosen scope**, using that scope's heading list exactly. Carry the non-functional measurements and verification methods from [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) and [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md) through unchanged. **A threshold that changed between the requirement and the design is either an error or a decision, and if it is a decision it gets its own record.**

**Step 7 — run the mechanical check.** Go through the constraint register entry by entry and, for each frozen input, point at the place in the design where it is accommodated. **Entries with nowhere to point are either constraints your design violates or constraints that were never real.** Both are findings and both belong in the document.

**Step 8 — attack your own scope choice.** Give the agent your design and ask it to argue that it should have been written at the scope above and then at the scope below, naming which headings would appear or disappear. Then hand the design to a person who would have chosen differently and ask which headings they missed. **What they name is the scope argument, and it is the argument you are defending.** If they miss nothing, either the scopes are closer than you thought, or you wrote at the larger one and did not notice.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** That an adversarial reading of your rejected option is either specific or empty, and that you cannot tell which until you have one. **You will also watch the context section fail**: almost every first draft leaks the decision, and the leak is visible the moment somebody who does not know the answer reads it and can state the answer.

## What good looks like

These are the assessed elements. Each is checked as present and adequate, or not.

1. **The scope is chosen, defended, and correct for the decision the design carries.** A design that commits somebody to running something is written at the scope that has an operational heading. One that does not, is not — and writing at a larger scope than the decision requires is failed for the same reason as writing at a smaller one.
2. **Non-functional requirements appear as inputs**, with the measurements and verification methods from [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md) and [S023](/wiki/seminars/S023.md) carried through unchanged. A threshold that changed between the requirement and the design is either an error or a decision, and if it is a decision it has a record.
3. **The frozen inputs are attached, and each names its authority** or is explicitly marked as having none.
4. **Open questions are present and actually open.** Each names who would close it and what would close it. A question with neither is not open; it is abandoned.
5. **Risks name mitigations, and at least one risk is carried unmitigated with that stated.** An honest unmitigated list is worth more than a complete mitigated one.
6. **The in-progress marking is correct at the moment of submission.** Trivially checkable and routinely wrong.
7. **Where the design meets an existing system, the boundary decision from [S027](/wiki/seminars/S027.md) is named and its standing cost is stated.**
8. **A decision record exists for the design's largest irreversible choice**, with a value-neutral context, a status from the published vocabulary, a consequences section naming what binds later, and **the rejected option argued at its strongest by a reading the candidate did not write** — with the observation that settled it, and the condition under which the record would be superseded.

**Nothing else is assessed.** Not the quality of the architecture, not the technology chosen, not the length of the document, not whether anybody accepted the design, not whether the design is one an assessor would have produced, not whether the decision recorded was the right one, and **not whether the scope you chose is the scope an assessor would have chosen** — only whether you defended it against the decision the design actually carries.

## How it goes wrong

Eight failure modes, one for each assessed element, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **The essay at every scope** — one document shape for all decisions, so a task-sized choice arrives with a milestone's ceremony and a milestone-sized choice arrives with a task's. *Warning sign:* your defence of the scope choice is about the size of the document.
- **The milestone design with no operational owner** — the design commits somebody to running the thing and nobody is named. This is the failure the escalation exists to catch and it is the commonest one. *Warning sign:* item 4 was answered with a team that has not agreed.
- **The open question that is a decision nobody wants to take** — it is listed as open because listing it is easier than resolving it, and it names nobody who could close it. *Warning sign:* the same question was open at [S024](/wiki/seminars/S024.md).
- **The fully mitigated risk register** — every risk has a mitigation, several of the mitigations are the word *monitor*, and nothing is carried. *Warning sign:* nothing in your design worries you.
- **The perpetual draft** — the in-progress marking has been on the document for weeks and has stopped meaning anything. *Warning sign:* somebody asks whether it is finished.
- **The design with unauthored constraints** — the frozen inputs are attached and none of them has an authority, so the design is built on the register's weakest entries. *Warning sign:* the attached register is the first version, unchanged since [S026](/wiki/seminars/S026.md).
- **The context that leaks the decision** — the record's context section is written after the choice and a reader can state the conclusion from it alone. *Warning sign:* your context contains a comparative adjective.
- **The straw rejection** — the rejected option is recorded in the weakest form anybody has ever put it in, so the record cannot be reopened honestly when the constraint moves. *Warning sign:* the adversarial reading is shorter than your own reasoning for the option you chose.

## Exercises

**Elements 1 to 8 above are assessed. These exercises are not**, and they are the preparation the specification assumes.

**1 · Place four decisions at a scope.** For each, name the scope and the heading that decides it.

- *Choosing between two libraries for one component.* · *Introducing a retrieval layer that a department will depend on daily.* · *Finding out whether a protocol integration is feasible at all.* · *Changing a threshold in a component already running.*

> **Model answers.** Task, milestone, spike, and — the trap — the fourth is not a design document at all but a decision record, because nothing is being designed and something is being changed. The second is milestone scope, and the heading that decides it is operationalisation: a daily dependency acquires a runtime owner. The third is a spike, and it carries a constraint the others do not — **its goal is fact-finding, not decision-making or recommendation**, and its code is not the final solution's code. **If you placed the fourth at task scope, you sized by effort rather than by commitment.**

**2 · Make the agent argue against you, and measure whether it could.** Run workflow step 3 twice: once giving the agent your context and your decision, once giving it the context alone. Compare the two rejected-option arguments.

> **What you should find.** The run that received your decision produces an argument shaped to lose. The run that received only the forces produces one you have to answer. **An instrument given your conclusion elaborates it**, which is the same failure [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md) named about an instrument that agrees with you, arriving for the fifth and last time in this stage. Note what the agent has not done: it has decided nothing, and the observation that settled the choice is still yours to write. **Capability here is jagged and the failure mode is a confident wrong answer, so the argument you paste in is edited for accuracy and never for strength.**

**3 · Go back to your own item 3, then break it.** Find what you wrote for opening item 3 — the heading your chosen scope requires that the scope below does not. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference.

> **Criterion.** A passing answer names one heading, says what obligation it creates, and names the person who would have to accept that obligation. "More detail about operations" fails on all three. "Operationalisation, which commits a named team to running this after handover, and that team has not agreed" passes — and, note, passing it may tell you that you chose the wrong scope, which is a better outcome than passing the exercise.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). Reading the criterion is not the exercise. Naming the difference between it and what you wrote is.

**4 · Break your own context section.** Give the context section, alone, to somebody who does not know your decision, and ask them what you decided.

> **What to notice.** Most first drafts leak. The leak is usually a comparative adjective, an ordering of the forces that puts the winning one last, or a force stated in the vocabulary of the option that satisfies it. **A value-neutral context is the hardest sentence in the format to write honestly**, and it is the field that determines whether the record can be reopened by somebody who was not there.

**5 · Point every frozen input at the design.** Run workflow step 7 and list every register entry with nowhere to point.

> **Scoring criterion.** The list passes if each entry on it is classified as *the design violates this* or *this was never a real constraint*, with a sentence for each. **Both are findings and both belong in the document.** An entry left unclassified is the third possibility — that you did not check — and it is the one the assessment cannot distinguish from the other two.

**6 · Name what a specification cannot see.** Closed book: this assessment checks eight elements and nothing else. Name two things a candidate could do that would be bad engineering and would still pass.

> **Answer.** Write a technically poor design that satisfies every element; and record a decision that is wrong, argued impeccably, with a rejected option that was in fact the right one. **Both pass, deliberately.** Marking architecture quality would mark the assessor's preferences, and marking whether the decision was correct requires an outcome that does not exist at submission. **The specification assesses the reasoning and its record, and it says so rather than implying more.**

## What this does not establish

Nothing shows that any of these four scopes is the right granularity, or that four is the right number of them. **They are one organisation's published convention, read on 2026-07-30**, and the publication offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices and does not claim to.

**The claim this session is built on — that leaving feature scope adds exactly one obligation, and that obligation is an operational owner — is precise, attractive, checkable in minutes against any design document, and is a convention rather than a finding.** It is taught because a claim you can check against your own artefacts is worth more than a claim you cannot, not because anybody has shown it holds.

The wider material this stage draws on **reports no engagement in which a design was found to have been written at the wrong scope**, because it reports no engagement that went badly at all. There is no observed instance of this failure anywhere in the sources, only the specification that would catch it.

**The publication supplying every convention here offers no comparative evidence for any of its practices.** Its pages carry footer dates spread across several years, roughly a fifth of it is stubs or link lists, it contradicts itself in at least a dozen places without noticing, and it asserts four unsupported magnitude claims. **This repository records those four as specimens and reproduces none.** Cite it by page and by the date that page was read.

**The assessment frame is borrowed and its transfer is untested here.** The entrustment literature comes from clinical education, and the narrative-assessment argument with it; both licence direction and mechanism only. **No claim is made that specification checking produces better engineers than any other form of assessment**, because none of the sources behind this programme measures that.

**Your instrument's limits are direction-only.** The jagged-frontier note rests on an abstract; no magnitude from it is usable and none is asserted. The platform documentation was read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, publishes no measure of how varied an agent's output is across repeated prompts, and is **highly perishable**.

**And nothing here establishes that an adversarially argued rejected option produces better decisions.** The requirement is defended on a different ground: that a record whose rejected option was recorded weakly cannot be reopened honestly when the constraint moves. That is an argument about the artefact's future usefulness, not a finding about decision quality.
