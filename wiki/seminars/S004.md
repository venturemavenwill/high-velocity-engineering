# S004 · Reading a Room You Were Invited Into

**Module:** [M01](/wiki/modules/M01.md) · **Quarter:** [Q1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md) · **Complexity class:** 1
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-004](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-004.md)
**Retrieves from:** S002, S003
**Entry state:** either

---

## The situation

You have been given an attendee list and an agenda. Both were written by somebody who has already decided what this session is.

The list is evidence. It tells you what the customer thinks you are, what they believe the problem is, and who they were unwilling or unable to release for a morning. Read it before you read the agenda.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. List everyone who will be present. Next to each name, write what that person can decide alone.
2. Which of them is worse off if this succeeds?
3. What did the invitation ask you to bring, and what does that tell you about what they think you are?
4. How would you find out what this room already knows, without asking them?
5. What would you see in the first minutes that would tell you to abandon the agenda?

**Most decision-rights columns end up repeating job titles.** Seniority is what an attendee list makes visible and authority is what you need, and they are not the same column.

## What you must produce

A **room reading**: attendance set against decision rights, one indirect probe for what the room can actually do, and a written pre-commitment naming a signal you might see and the change you will make if you see it.

## What good looks like

- At least one attendee is identified as able to stop the work, and it is not the sponsor.
- The probe is a task or an artefact request — something the room does — rather than a question about how confident they feel.
- **The pre-commitment is written before the room and names both the signal and the change**, specifically enough that somebody else could tell whether it fired.
- The reading distinguishes what the invitation asked for from what the engagement needs, and says what you will do about the gap.

## How it goes wrong

Four failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **The seniority proxy** — attendance is read as authority, so the most senior person present is treated as the decision. *Warning sign:* your decision-rights column is a second copy of your job-title column.
- **The direct literacy question** — you ask the room how comfortable they are with the tooling. *Warning sign:* everybody reports being comfortable, and the answers arrive in the order of seniority.
- **Demonstration drift** — the invitation asked for an overview of a product and you agreed to give one, so the session becomes a tour. *Warning sign:* the agenda was finalised before anybody understood the customer.
- **Reading after the fact** — the signals are all identified in the debrief, where they are obvious. *Warning sign:* your pre-commitment has the same timestamp as your notes.

## Do it

Work from [Brief A](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md). Construct the attendee list you would expect the operations director to assemble, then construct the one the engagement actually needs, and mark every difference.

Design the indirect probe. It must be answerable by doing something small and real — showing a record that was returned, walking through the handheld application, opening the last rejected submission — and it must not require anybody to assess their own capability out loud.

Write the pre-commitment last, and date it.

## Show it

Read your pre-commitment to somebody who has not seen the brief. They have one job: to say whether they could tell, from the outside, if the signal had fired.

If they could not, the signal is a feeling and not a signal. **A trigger nobody else could recognise is a trigger you will decline to notice when it is inconvenient.**

## What this does not establish

Nothing here shows that a room read well produces a better engagement. The material behind this session is a catalogue of observable warning signs assembled by experienced facilitators — an agenda settled before the customer was understood, a room of managers with few hands-on engineers, nobody asking for an environment to work in — and it is offered without a single measurement, a single declined engagement or a single recorded failure to compare against.

**It is hard-won operational knowledge and it is not evidence.** The two facts sit in the same documents in the same register, which is the reading skill this stage is actually training.

## Perishable content in this session

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| An attendee list is evidence about the customer, not logistics | The specific job titles that currently signal decision rights in a given sector |
| Capability is assessed by what a room does, not by what it says about itself | The tools the probe asks the room to open, and their current interfaces |
| A trigger a stranger could not recognise will not fire | The vocabulary this term's cohort uses to describe an agent, which will date faster than the probe |
