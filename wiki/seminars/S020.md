# S020 · A Discovery You Can Act On ⊘

**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none

## The situation

This is an assessment occasion and it is the stage's class boundary. Everything before it told you what counted as done. **This one does not.**

You assemble the discovery, you say when it was enough, and you defend the stopping rule rather than the finding.

Answer these in writing before you begin, and submit them.

1. What decision is waiting on this discovery, and who takes it?
2. What would it cost to be wrong about the central claim?
3. State your stopping rule **now**, before you look at what you have.
4. Which uncertainty in this engagement can a conversation not resolve?
5. If you stopped today, what is the first thing that would surprise you later?

**Item 3 is the assessment.** A stopping rule written after the evidence arrived is a description of what you happened to do.

## What you must produce

A **discovery packet**, assembled from the stage's work and defended in front of an assessor who has read only your charter and your stopping rule.

- **The problem**, stated in the customer's terms and as something they experience.
- **Who was studied and who was not**, by position rather than by name.
- **The observation record** from [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md), with observed, reported and inferred still separated.
- **The consolidated finding** from [S018](/wiki/seminars/S018.md), with its preserved contradiction intact.
- **The disconfirmation question and its answer** — what result would show this had not worked, and what they said.
- **Who loses if this succeeds.**
- **The data block** — provenance, access, correctness, labelling, consent.
- **The commitment ledger** from [S019](/wiki/seminars/S019.md), containing at least one refusal or recorded non-advancement.
- **The stopping rule**, dated before the evidence that satisfied it, with the evidence named.
- **The routing** — for each uncertainty a conversation could not resolve, the mechanism that could: an observation, a spike, representative data, an evaluation, a policy review.

## What good looks like

These are the assessed elements. Each is checked as present and adequate, or not.

- **A decision changed**, and the packet says which one and in what direction. A discovery that confirmed everything is submitted with that stated as its finding.
- **Observation, report and inference are separated** throughout the packet, not only in the record they came from.
- **The stopping rule was stated before the evidence that satisfied it**, and the ordering is verifiable from your submission.
- **The stopping rule is tied to the decision waiting on it**, not to an amount of effort spent.
- **An absence is recorded rather than smoothed** — a refusal, a non-advancement, a population not sampled, or a question nobody could answer.
- **At least one uncertainty is routed away from conversation** to a mechanism that could actually settle it.
- **The central claim names what would withdraw it.**

**Nothing else is assessed.** The number of informants is not marked, whether the customer committed is not marked, whether the finding is correct is not marked, the artefact's presentation is not marked, and whether your stopping rule matches anybody else's is not marked.

## How it goes wrong

- **The retrospective rule** — the stopping rule was written to fit the evidence you already had. *Warning sign:* your rule is satisfied exactly and with nothing to spare.
- **The effort rule** — the rule is about how much discovery you did rather than about what the decision needs. *Warning sign:* your rule contains a count of conversations.
- **The confirmed packet** — everything you learned agreed with what you brought. *Warning sign:* nothing in the packet surprised you, and you have not said so.
- **The collapsed separation** — observation, report and inference were separate in [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) and have merged in the summary. *Warning sign:* your problem statement contains an inference stated as an observation.
- **The conversational answer to a technical question** — a feasibility, capability or data-quality uncertainty is supported only by what somebody told you. *Warning sign:* your evidence for something the system must do is an interview.
- **The smoothed absence** — the refusal is in the ledger and not in the packet's account of what you learned. *Warning sign:* the packet reads better than the engagement went.

## Do it

Assemble it, then attack it in this order.

**First, the stopping rule.** Write it before you open anything else, timestamp it, and submit it separately. Tie it to the decision: *we stop when we can answer whether X, because that is what the sponsor's decision turns on.* Then check it against the cost of being wrong from item 2. **A cheap wrong answer justifies a short discovery; an expensive one does not.**

**Second, the routing.** Go through your uncertainties and mark each one as answerable by conversation or not. Whether people want the outcome, whether they will fund it, whether authority exists, whether adoption is plausible — those are conversational. Whether the system can be built, whether the data supports it, whether the model behaves, whether the integration holds — those are not, and no amount of interviewing will settle them. **Route them. A packet that answers a technical question with testimony has misrouted, and that is the most common failure at this session.**

**Third, the absences.** Find every place where the packet is smoother than the engagement. The refusal, the person you could not reach, the question nobody answered, the contradiction you would like to have resolved. **Put each one back.**

Then have an agent argue that your discovery was insufficient and that the decision should not be taken. Keep what is better than your own reasoning; record what you rejected and why. **An agent that endorsed your packet was not used as an instrument**, which is [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s claim returning at the end of the stage.

## Show it

Defend the stopping rule, not the finding.

The assessor has your rule and your charter and nothing else. They will ask three things: **what decision turns on this?**, **what would it cost to be wrong?**, and **what did you stop before finding out?**

The third question has an answer. **A candidate who says "nothing" has not understood the class boundary.**

## What this does not establish

This assessment shows that you can state a sufficiency criterion, meet it, and record what you did not learn. **It does not show that your criterion was right.**

Nothing in the material behind this stage establishes when discovery is enough. The practice corpus reports no engagement in which discovery was judged insufficient, and none in which it was judged excessive. The three interviewing sources all decline to supply a threshold: one says explicitly that stopping should be governed by the decision, the segment and whether new information is still appearing, and **not by a numeric rule**; the others give conversation counts and durations as the authors' own heuristics and warn against promoting them.

**So this session assesses the existence and the timing of the rule, and not its content.** That is a real weakness and it is the honest position available: a programme that marked the rule's content would be asserting a threshold that none of its sources supports.
