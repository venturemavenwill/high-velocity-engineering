## The situation

This is an assessment occasion and it is the stage's class boundary. Everything before it told you what counted as done. **This one does not.**

You assemble the discovery, you say when it was enough, and you defend the stopping rule rather than the finding.

Answer these in writing before you begin, and submit them.

1. What decision is waiting on this discovery, and who takes it?
2. What would it cost to be wrong about the central claim?
3. State your stopping rule **now**, before you look at what you have.
4. Which uncertainty in this engagement can a conversation not resolve?
5. If you stopped today, what is the first thing that would surprise you later?

**Item 3 is the assessment.** A stopping rule written after the evidence arrived is a description of what you happened to do.

## What you must produce

A **discovery packet**, assembled from the stage's work and defended in front of an assessor who has read only your charter and your stopping rule.

- **The problem**, stated in the customer's terms and as something they experience.
- **Who was studied and who was not**, by position rather than by name.
- **The observation record** from [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md), with observed, reported and inferred still separated.
- **The consolidated finding** from [S018](/wiki/seminars/S018.md), with its preserved contradiction intact.
- **The disconfirmation question and its answer** — what result would show this had not worked, and what they said.
- **Who loses if this succeeds.**
- **The data block** — provenance, access, correctness, labelling, consent.
- **The commitment ledger** from [S019](/wiki/seminars/S019.md), containing at least one refusal or recorded non-advancement.
- **The stopping rule**, dated before the evidence that satisfied it, with the evidence named.
- **The routing** — for each uncertainty a conversation could not resolve, the mechanism that could: an observation, a spike, representative data, an evaluation, a policy review.

## The material

### A stopping event is not a stopping rule

Discovery has no natural end. There is always another informant, another exception, another role that has not been heard.

Practitioners therefore stop for reasons that have nothing to do with the decision: the time ran out, the sponsor grew impatient, the team felt they had enough, somebody wanted to start building. **Each of those is a stopping event, and none of them is a stopping rule.** The difference matters for one reason only: a rule can be wrong in a way that is visible afterwards, and a stopping event cannot.

The sources behind this stage decline to supply a threshold, and they decline consistently. The customer-conversation handbook governs stopping by whether new information is still appearing, by the coherence of the segment, and by the decision waiting on the work — **explicitly not by a numeric rule** ([research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md](/research/05-fde-craft/fitzpatrick-the-mom-test.md)). The field manual gives conversation counts and durations as the author's own heuristics and warns against promoting them to thresholds ([research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md](/research/05-fde-craft/portigal-interviewing-users.md)). The contextual design method's sampling guidance is described in the source itself as heuristic ([research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md](/research/05-fde-craft/beyer-holtzblatt-contextual-design.md)).

**Three sources, three refusals to answer the question you most want answered.** That is not a gap in the reading. It is the class boundary: at complexity class 3 the criterion is yours to supply and to defend, and there is no supplied standard to appeal to.

### Sufficiency is a function of the decision and the cost of being wrong

The rule this session wants has a shape. *We stop when we can answer whether X, because that is what the sponsor's decision turns on.*

Then the second term. **A cheap wrong answer justifies a short discovery; an expensive one does not.** The value of further discovery is the reduction in the probability of a costly wrong decision, so the cost of the error sizes the effort. A rule expressed in units of effort — a number of interviews, a number of weeks — has answered a different question, and it will be satisfied by whatever you happened to do.

**This is design reasoning with no external warrant.** No source behind this programme supplies a threshold or a method for computing one, and this session offers neither.

### Routing: some uncertainties cannot be settled by talking to anybody

The second half of the assessment, and the more commonly failed one.

The distinction is between **market risk** — whether people want the outcome, will pay for it, have the authority, exist in sufficient number — and **product risk** — whether the team can build it, grow it and retain use of it. Conversations reduce uncertainty about the first. **They cannot prove that a technically difficult system, integration or model behaviour will work**, and when product risk dominates the team must build or run a direct test earlier and accept that talking supplies nothing.

The field manual makes the operational version of the same point: research goals that interviews cannot answer must be **routed** — to observation, telemetry, an experiment, a technical evaluation, a policy review — rather than reworded into questions.

And the contextual design method supplies the general principle that makes routing more than a taxonomy. Its contribution is that it **locates each decision in the cheapest representation capable of answering it**: discover a workflow mismatch in the field rather than after deployment; reject a concept in a paper prototype rather than after building production infrastructure; expose a dependency before parallel implementation diverges. **Routing is that idea applied to an uncertainty rather than to a design decision.**

**The categories are not clean and this session says so.** Whether the data supports the system is technical, and also organisational, legal and political. Whether people will adopt something is conversational, and also technical, because adoption depends on whether the thing works. The specification asks for one clearly misrouted uncertainty to be corrected, which is a low bar chosen because it is checkable.

### Once the system exists, routing becomes diagnosis

Worth holding now because it is what your routing decisions turn into.

A retrieval-augmented system's failures are stage-specific — corpus, retrieval, context assembly, generation — and the operator's first question when an answer is wrong should be **which stage failed** rather than which model to change ([research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/barnett-rag-failure-taxonomy.md)). Most people respond to a bad answer by changing the prompt, which is the least likely place the defect lives.

The same source makes a claim this session should hold uncomfortably rather than adopt: that validation of such a system is **only feasible during operation**, because the space of real queries cannot be adequately anticipated before deployment. If that is right, a discovery packet cannot in principle settle certain uncertainties, and the honest routing for those is to production observability rather than to a spike.

**Its limits.** An experience report from three deployments; **no frequency data, no severity ranking, no controlled comparison**; and this repository's note records that the specific names of its failure points are reconstructed rather than quoted. Treat the operational-validation claim as a strong position to be examined, not an axiom — it has real consequences for release governance in regulated settings.

### An absence is evidence, and the corpus behind this stage records none

The packet's hardest requirement is that it be no smoother than the engagement was.

The motivation is directly observable in the practice material this stage draws on ([research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-accelerated-delivery-practice.md)). **Its case studies all succeed. Its single failure narrative is a synthetic composite, fictional in its particulars by its own admission. It states no exclusion criteria and records no engagement in which discovery was judged insufficient or excessive.**

A practice whose artefacts never record a failure produces practitioners whose artefacts never record one either. That is not a moral observation; it is a claim about what a template teaches, and the absence-restoration pass is the countermeasure.

There is a sharper version in the same material and it is the most teachable thing in the whole collection. The model's prepared answer to the objection that tooling improved and delivery did not speed up **reattributes the shortfall to the customer's own organisational constraints**, and its readiness rubric is composed almost entirely of customer dispositions. Taken together, **the evidence has been arranged so that a disappointing result confirms the thesis rather than challenging it.** Your packet can acquire the same shape without anybody intending it, and the way it happens is that the awkward parts get tidied.

**Its limits.** Access-controlled internal material held here only as an original paraphrase; **no public reader can check it**; and it contains no citation, study or measurement of any kind behind any of its claims.

### Every platform claim carries a citation and a date

Here is the packet's last requirement and the reason it has a platform anchor.

Your discovery packet will contain claims about what a platform can do — that a service supports a deployment scale, that a capability is available in a region, that a control exists, that an integration is supported. Each of those is a claim somebody will build on, and each has a shelf life.

Two published facts from the platform's own selection guidance show what the shelf life looks like ([research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/microsoft-foundry-and-copilot-studio-docs.md)). The basic setup for rapid prototyping **lacks network isolation**, while the standard setup supplies fine-grained control over data, security and networking — so prototyping topology and production topology are different things and the transition is a deliberate act. And one product is **designed for small-scale deployments, such as department-level solutions**, which is a scale claim the vendor publishes about its own product; a brief assuming enterprise-wide rollout on it is outside the design envelope, and the symptoms appear as governance and lifecycle problems rather than as functional failures.

The same family supplies the fact that makes today's tooling possible at all: the build options are **explicitly not mutually exclusive**, and components on different platforms compose through MCP. That is why a documentation server and a Foundry project can be used together in one workflow.

**Its limits, and they are severe.** Read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches, so page structure is reconstructed. **Highly perishable** — product names and feature boundaries are actively changing. Single-vendor selection guidance with a commercial frame, offering **no adoption data, no failure-rate data, no cost comparison and no evidence that its recommended mappings produce better outcomes.** Its authority is definitional: it tells you what the products are, not that choosing them this way works.

**And one honest gap in this programme's own record.** This repository holds **no dedicated research note for the Microsoft Learn MCP Server itself.** Its properties as used here — a remote endpoint exposing documentation search, documentation fetch and code-sample search — are carried forward from [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s workflow rather than from a verified note, and they should be treated as perishable and re-checked. **A programme that requires you to date your platform claims has to date its own.**

### Grounding makes an answer accurate within a corpus and silent outside it

The mechanism the workflow depends on, and the reason the citation requirement is not bureaucracy.

A grounded agent answers from a fetched corpus. Where the corpus covers the question, the answer is checkable. **Where it does not, a well-behaved agent says so — and an ungrounded one produces a fluent paragraph instead**, with nothing in the output to tell you which side of that line you are on.

The in-sentence citation requirement is what converts silence into a visible artefact. It is not there to make the packet look rigorous; it is there so that **"not found in the documentation" becomes a recordable finding** rather than a gap that closes itself. An unanswerable platform question in a discovery packet is an uncertainty, and an uncertainty is something you route.

### Why this is a specification check and not an entrustment decision

One note on what today can and cannot certify.

Entrustment judgements are made about activities a professional can be trusted to perform unsupervised, and they require observation of the activity across occasions rather than inspection of an artefact ([research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md](/research/08-assessment-epas/ten-cate-epa-framework.md)). **Today assesses a packet and a defence, at a higher complexity class, and generates no entrustment evidence.** Those decisions are made much later in this programme, and saying so now is part of not overclaiming what an assessment sees.

## The workflow

**Run every platform claim in your packet through grounded documentation, and record the ones that come back empty.**

You will attach the Microsoft Learn MCP Server to your agent and use it as a citation instrument rather than as an answering one. **The server exposes documentation search, documentation fetch and code-sample search; it is remote and requires no local model.** Its exact configuration surface changes — verify against current documentation and record the date, which is the habit this whole step exists to build.

**Step 1 — extract every platform claim in your packet into a list.** Anything of the form *the platform can*, *the service supports*, *this is available*, *this is controlled by*. Include the ones you believe. **Especially include the ones you believe**, because those are the ones nobody will check.

**Step 2 — attach the server and confirm its tools are switched on.** Tools contributed by an MCP server are not automatically enabled; their checkboxes arrive cleared. **A connected server is an available tool, not a used one**, and an agent that answered without calling it has answered from general knowledge.

**Step 3 — write the instructions file that forces the absence into view.**

```markdown
---
applyTo: '**/discovery/**/*.md'
---

# Platform claims in a discovery packet

- Every claim about a platform capability MUST carry a citation to a fetched
  documentation page, in the same sentence as the claim.
- If no fetched source supports a claim, write exactly:
  'not found in the documentation, checked <date>' — and do not answer from
  general knowledge, do not infer from a related product, and do not soften it.
- Record the date every page was fetched, next to the citation.
- Where the documentation states a limit, quote the limit and cite it.
- Where the documentation is silent on something the packet depends on,
  say so explicitly and route it as an uncertainty rather than closing it.
```

**Step 4 — verify the instructions actually arrived.** Send one prompt, then inspect the chat output channel and confirm the instruction text appears in the request payload. **An instructions file you have not observed in a payload is a file, not a specification** — which is the same distinction between a control and a check that this programme keeps returning to.

**Step 5 — run the list and keep the empty results.** For each claim, ask the grounded question. Three outcomes and all three are useful: **supported**, with a page and a date; **contradicted**, with a page and a date, which is the best possible outcome because it changed something; and **not found**, which is not a failure of the exercise but a finding for the packet.

**Step 6 — route every *not found* rather than resolving it.** Each becomes an uncertainty with a mechanism attached: a spike, a support request, a test deployment, a question for a named person at the vendor. **A packet that quietly dropped its unverifiable claims is smoother than the engagement**, which is exactly what the absence pass exists to prevent.

**Step 7 — attack the packet.** Give the agent your charter, your stopping rule and your packet, and ask it to argue that **the discovery was insufficient and the decision should not be taken.** Then ask it which single piece of evidence would most change its argument. Keep what is better than your own reasoning; record what you rejected and why. **An agent that endorsed your packet was not used as an instrument** — which is [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)'s claim returning at the close of the stage.

**What this makes observable that prose cannot.** That a claim you were confident about has no documentation behind it, and that you cannot tell which of your claims those are until you check. It also makes the silence physical: **you will watch the agent decline to answer a question it would happily have answered without the instructions file**, and the difference between those two outputs is the difference between a grounded packet and a plausible one.

## What good looks like

These are the assessed elements. Each is checked as present and adequate, or not.

- **A decision changed**, and the packet says which one and in what direction. A discovery that confirmed everything is submitted with that stated as its finding.
- **Observation, report and inference are separated** throughout the packet, not only in the record they came from.
- **The stopping rule was stated before the evidence that satisfied it**, and the ordering is verifiable from your submission.
- **The stopping rule is tied to the decision waiting on it**, not to an amount of effort spent.
- **An absence is recorded rather than smoothed** — a refusal, a non-advancement, a population not sampled, or a question nobody could answer.
- **At least one uncertainty is routed away from conversation** to a mechanism that could actually settle it.
- **The central claim names what would withdraw it.**

**Nothing else is assessed.** The number of informants is not marked, whether the customer committed is not marked, whether the finding is correct is not marked, the artefact's presentation is not marked, and whether your stopping rule matches anybody else's is not marked.

## How it goes wrong

- **The retrospective rule** — the stopping rule was written to fit the evidence you already had. *Warning sign:* your rule is satisfied exactly and with nothing to spare.
- **The effort rule** — the rule is about how much discovery you did rather than about what the decision needs. *Warning sign:* your rule contains a count of conversations.
- **The confirmed packet** — everything you learned agreed with what you brought. *Warning sign:* nothing in the packet surprised you, and you have not said so.
- **The collapsed separation** — observation, report and inference were separate in [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md) and have merged in the summary. *Warning sign:* your problem statement contains an inference stated as an observation.
- **The conversational answer to a technical question** — a feasibility, capability or data-quality uncertainty is supported only by what somebody told you. *Warning sign:* your evidence for something the system must do is an interview.
- **The smoothed absence** — the refusal is in the ledger and not in the packet's account of what you learned. *Warning sign:* the packet reads better than the engagement went.

## Exercises

**The seven elements above are assessed. These exercises are not**, and they are the preparation the specification assumes. Do them before the assessment, not after it.

**1 · Write the rule, then test it against three costs.** Draft your stopping rule. Then write what being wrong about the central claim would cost if the decision is reversible in a week, if it is reversible in a year, and if it is not reversible.

> **Criterion.** A passing rule **changes** across the three cases. If it does not, the rule is not tied to the cost of error and you have written a rule about effort wearing the vocabulary of a decision. **A cheap wrong answer justifies a short discovery; an expensive one does not**, and a rule indifferent to which case it is in has not made the judgement the class boundary asks for.

**2 · Route eight uncertainties.** Take eight open questions from your engagement and mark each conversational, not conversational, or mixed. For the mixed ones, split them.

> **Model answers.** *Will the operations team use this?* — mixed: willingness is conversational, and whether it works at their latency is not. *Is the data good enough?* — mixed, and the split is the exercise: whether the fields exist is a technical check, whether anybody will release them is conversational, and whether the labels are correct is neither and needs a sample. **A passing split names a different mechanism for each half.** "We will investigate" is not a mechanism.

**3 · Run the citation pass and count the empties.** Run the workflow through step 5 over every platform claim in your packet.

> **What you should find.** At least one claim you were confident about comes back **not found**, and it is usually a claim about a limit rather than about a capability — what the service will not do, at what scale, in which region. **Write down how you came to believe it.** If the answer is "somebody said so in a meeting", you have found a report recorded as an observation, which is [S017](/wiki/seminars/S017.md)'s separation failing inside your own packet.

**4 · Restore three absences.** Find three places where the packet is smoother than the engagement was: the refusal, the person you could not reach, the question nobody could answer, the contradiction you would like to have resolved. Put each one back and say what it costs the reader to know it.

> **Criterion.** A passing restoration makes the packet **worse to read and better to act on.** If restoring the absence did not weaken any conclusion, you restored a decoration. And note the pattern you are working against: the practice material behind this stage records no failed engagement and explains disappointing results by the customer's readiness, which is a shape a packet acquires without anybody intending it.

**5 · Go back to your own item 3, then break it.** Find the stopping rule you wrote before you looked at anything. **Do not rewrite it yet.** Put it beside the criterion below and write one sentence naming the difference. Then say whether the difference is a correction or a retrofit.

> **Criterion.** A passing rule names the decision, names who takes it, and names the evidence that would satisfy it — and the evidence is describable rather than counted. "We stop after twelve interviews" fails. "We stop when we can say whether the approval step is a policy or a habit, because the sponsor's decision turns on whether it can be changed" passes.
>
> **The honest part of this exercise is the second sentence.** If your rule improved because you had seen the evidence, that is a retrofit however good the new rule is, and the assessment can see the ordering. Say so.
>
> **Why the comparison step is mandatory rather than encouraged.** The research this session's structure rests on tested exactly this: showing learners a correct answer next to their own wrong one produced no benefit, and only the group *instructed to compare them* learned more ([research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md](/research/04-professional-formation/sinha-kapur-productive-failure.md)). **School, university and laboratory evidence; no adult-professional study in its reference list.**

**6 · Rehearse the third defence question.** A peer holds your charter and your rule and nothing else, and asks: what decision turns on this, what would it cost to be wrong, and **what did you stop before finding out?**

> **What to notice.** Most candidates answer the third question with *nothing* on the first attempt, and *nothing* is the answer of somebody who has claimed a complete discovery — the position this entire stage argues against. **A good answer names a specific thing, says why it was not worth the cost of finding out, and says what would change that judgement.** That is a stopping rule being defended rather than a finding being presented.

## What this does not establish

This assessment shows that you can state a sufficiency criterion, meet it, and record what you did not learn. **It does not show that your criterion was right.**

Nothing in the material behind this stage establishes when discovery is enough. The practice corpus reports no engagement in which discovery was judged insufficient, and none in which it was judged excessive. The three interviewing sources all decline to supply a threshold: one says explicitly that stopping should be governed by the decision, the segment and whether new information is still appearing, and **not by a numeric rule**; the others give conversation counts and durations as the authors' own heuristics and warn against promoting them.

**So this session assesses the existence and the timing of the rule, and not its content.** That is a real weakness and it is the honest position available: a programme that marked the rule's content would be asserting a threshold that none of its sources supports.

**The routing categories are this repository's inference and they are not clean.** The market-and-product-risk distinction is the source's; the enterprise routing categories built on it are a course-design inference recorded as such, and the source itself treats the distinction as useful rather than exhaustive. Nothing measures whether routing improves anything.

**The platform material is definitional, perishable and single-vendor.** It was read through search excerpts rather than full page fetches; it offers no adoption data, no failure-rate data and no evidence that its recommended mappings produce better outcomes; and product names and feature boundaries are actively changing. **This repository holds no dedicated note for the documentation server used in the workflow**, so its properties are carried from an earlier session rather than verified here — which is precisely the defect the workflow teaches you to record.

**Grounding bounds an answer; it does not make it true.** A grounded agent is accurate within its corpus and silent outside it, and a documentation corpus records what a vendor says rather than what a system does. **A cited claim is a claim with a provenance, not a claim with a warrant.**

**The retrieval failure taxonomy is an experience report** from three deployments, with no frequency data and no controlled comparison, and its claim that validation is only feasible during operation is a position to be examined rather than an axiom.

**No entrustment evidence is generated today.** A specification check inspects an artefact and a defence; it does not observe an activity across occasions, and it therefore cannot support a judgement about what you can be trusted to do unsupervised.
