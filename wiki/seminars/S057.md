# S057 · Dense Retrieval and the Geometry of Similarity

**Module:** [M12](wiki/modules/M12.md) · **Quarter:** [Q6](wiki/quarters/Q6.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** IS · ME
**Whitepaper:** [WP-057](wiki/whitepapers/WP-057.md)
**Retrieves from:** S051, S052, S056
**Platform anchor:** Azure AI Search vector fields, vector profiles and integrated vectorization · Foundry embedding deployments · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (Search) · Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

[S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md) established that every embedding's notion of similarity was chosen by somebody solving a different problem. Today the student is that somebody.

Training a retriever looks like an optimisation problem and is not. It is a criterion-construction act performed in an unusual notation: **the positive pairs say what must be near, the negative pairs say what must be far, and the model learns exactly the distinction those two sets ask it to make and no other.** A model that has never been shown a hard distractor has never been asked to separate one, and it will not.

The consequence students find hardest is about evaluation rather than training. If the negatives in your evaluation were drawn the way the negatives in your training were drawn, the evaluation is measuring the distinction the model was built to make on the distribution it was built for. Nothing was copied. No document appears in both sets. **It is still [S041](wiki/seminars/S041.md)'s fourth leak class**, and it is the reason so many retrievers improve on a test set and move nothing in production.

**All of it lands in one index.** Yesterday's keyword index gains vector fields today. Each of the day's four constructions becomes a vector field with its own profile, loaded through the service's own chunk-and-embed pipeline, and queried through the same endpoint the room used yesterday. Two of the day's sharpest findings are not about training at all — they are about what the pipeline did to the documents before any model saw them, and about a score the service reports that is not the number its metric is named after.

The target: **a dense retriever is [S052](wiki/seminars/S052.md)'s lookup with one head over a whole corpus, its notion of similarity is the one your negatives defined, and an evaluation that shares its negative construction with training reports the training objective back to you.**

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

A corpus, a query log and a supplied training harness are described. Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. **Where do the positive pairs come from?** Name a source.
2. **Where do the negative pairs come from?** Name a source. *Most answers to item 1 are immediate; most answers to item 2 are not.*
3. Two retrievers are described only by how their negatives were drawn — one at random from the corpus, one mined from the keyword baseline's top results. **Which scores higher on a test set whose negatives were drawn at random?**
4. Here are **the respects this cohort could not predict at [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md).** For each, predict what a retriever trained on this substrate will do.
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to add vector search to yesterday's index over this corpus, chunking and embedding the documents for you, from a description of the corpus and nothing else. Write down what it will produce — the chunk size, the overlap, the vector dimensions, the vectorizer — and **name the one thing it will get wrong.**

Item 2 is the day's opening finding and it needs no framework: **the positives are obvious and the negatives are a design decision nobody has made yet.**

Item 5's design expectation is that the room will predict a wrong *model* and that the agent's actual error will be a wrong *chunk boundary* — a quantity that is invisible in the schema, invisible in the query, and determines what every subsequent number in this module means.

**Warrant.** Pretesting constructed around an asymmetry the students can feel. Item 3 is answerable correctly by reasoning and is answered incorrectly by most, because the shared construction between training and test is invisible until named. Item 4 imports the cohort's own measured blind spots from five days earlier and is the first half of this day's rule-6 discharge to [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md). **Item 5 is sealed here and may not migrate into phase 5**, per instrumentation rule 8.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative retrieval set (20 min)

Free recall, no notes.

- [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md): *similar to what*, and what a training signal freezes.
- [S052](wiki/seminars/S052.md): the lookup, and why the map is a routing record rather than an explanation.
- [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md): the four terms in order of size — corpus, queries, criterion, method.
- [S041](wiki/seminars/S041.md): the four leak classes, and why the obvious leak is the least dangerous.
- **Bridge:** [S052](wiki/seminars/S052.md) said what counts as a good match is [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md)'s question, answered separately by every head. **A dense retriever is one such lookup, with one head, over the whole corpus. Who answers it?**

The room answers: whoever chose the training pairs. Which today is them.

**Warrant.** Expanding-interval retrieval that assembles the day's premise from four prior days without introducing anything. The bridge's value is the last clause — the students have spent two modules identifying whose definition of similarity they inherited, and the answer changes ownership here.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups have **one index, one architecture, one corpus, one positive-pair construction, and four negative constructions loaded as four vector fields on that index**, each with its own profile. Every evaluation in this phase is issued against the live service.

- **D1** — negatives drawn at random from the corpus.
- **D2** — negatives mined from the keyword baseline's top results for each query.
- **D3** — trained on the substrate's click log, with unclicked shown results as negatives.
- **D4** — an off-the-shelf general-purpose retriever, never trained on this substrate, reached through the service's own vectorizer.

Groups predict the ordering and commit. **Then the supplied evaluation is run.** Then, separately, **two further evaluations are run**: one whose negatives are lexically near-identical documents, and one on **the query class this cohort mis-predicted worst at [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md) step 4.**

The payload is **D1**. It wins the supplied evaluation by a clear margin and is last on both of the others. The supplied evaluation's negatives were drawn at random, which is how D1's training negatives were drawn, and no document is shared between the two sets.

**D4 is conditional and the condition is stated to the room.** Reaching a general-purpose retriever through the service's catalogue vectorizer requires a managed-compute deployment and the quota to hold it. **Where the programme's tenant cannot provision one, D4 is demonstrated rather than operated** — its results are supplied, the room is told they were supplied, and **no specification element depends on it.** The other three run.

Task after: **for each of the four, name a query on which it would fail and the property of its negatives that causes the failure.**

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases with the architecture, corpus and positives held fixed so that every difference is attributable to the negatives. The staged reveal is required: running all three evaluations at once lets a group notice the discrepancy without first committing to a conclusion from the one evaluation they were given, which is the conclusion the ordinary training pipeline produces. **The third evaluation's query class is selected from [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md)'s step 4 prediction data**, which is this day's discharge of the obligation S056 placed on it under instrumentation rule 6. The D4 caveat follows the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md): a capability the programme cannot operate is named as demonstrated and is not assessed.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **A dense retriever is one soft lookup over a corpus.** [S052](wiki/seminars/S052.md)'s operation with a single query representation, a single key representation per document, and the corpus in place of a context window. There is no new mechanism on this day.

2. **The training signal is the relevance definition.** [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md) point 3, with the ownership changed: the students are no longer recovering somebody's definition from behaviour, they are writing one and compiling it.

3. **Positives say what must be near; negatives say what must be far; and the negatives are where the work is.** Positives are usually available and rarely disputed. Negatives are constructed, and the construction is the model's decision boundary. **A model never shown a hard distractor has never been asked to separate one.**

4. **An evaluation whose negatives were drawn as the training's negatives were is a leak.** [S041](wiki/seminars/S041.md)'s fourth class — selection of the evaluation set — arriving in the form students find hardest, because nothing was copied and no document appears twice. What is shared is the *construction*, and construction is exactly what an evaluation is supposed to vary.

5. **Scores are uncalibrated and not comparable across models.** [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md) point 2, unchanged and now load-bearing: a similarity threshold is a per-model, per-corpus empirical quantity, and porting one across a retriever change is the most common production failure in this material.

6. **One vector must serve every question the document answers.** A document that is the right answer to three unrelated queries is represented once, and the single representation cannot be near all three regions at once. This is a capacity limit of the representation rather than of the training, and it is the argument for multi-vector and late-interaction methods, which this day names and does not build.

7. **Reranking is a compute trade, not a repair.** Cheap wide recall, then expensive narrow scoring over a small candidate set. It buys precision with latency and it cannot recover a document the first stage did not return — which makes the first stage's recall the whole system's ceiling and is [S058](wiki/seminars/S058.md)'s subject. **On this service the claim has a number attached: the semantic reranker rescores a fixed and small number of already-returned results and cannot rerun the query over the corpus.** The documentation says so in those words. A student who expects reranking to find something the first stage missed has misread a stage boundary that the vendor has stated plainly.

8. **The chunking trap. Marked perishable, and the day's most consequential platform content.** Nothing in points 1 to 7 mentions that the thing you embedded was not a document. It was a fragment produced by a splitter with a size, an overlap and a mode, all of which somebody chose. **The same corpus, split six ways with the vendor's own documented parameters, produces wildly different numbers of chunks — the documentation's own worked example ranges from tens to over thirteen thousand for one book, purely as a function of mode, page length and overlap.** Every downstream quantity in this module — what a vector represents, what recall means, what a relevance judgement attaches to — is defined against that fragmentation and not against your documents.

    Three consequences are stated and one is a security fact rather than a relevance fact.

    - **Chunk boundaries decide what a single vector must represent**, which is point 6's capacity limit arriving as a configuration value rather than as a property of the model.
    - **Characters are not tokens.** The splitter's length is measured in one unit and the embedding model's limit is stated in another, and the documentation says explicitly that the two do not align. A pipeline tuned in characters can still exceed a model's token limit.
    - **Chunking moves your document's fields onto its chunks only if you project them.** For ordinary metadata an unprojected field is an inconvenience. **For permission metadata an unprojected field is a leak**, because the parent document's access control does not travel to its chunks by itself. That is [S078](wiki/seminars/S078.md)'s content and it is named here because this is the day the chunks are created.

9. **Defaults are opinions and thresholds are policy — and three of them decided what your vectors mean.**

    - **The splitter has a default mode and a recommended size and overlap**, published as a starting point, adopted by nearly everyone as an ending point.
    - **The vectorizer must match the embedding model that produced the index**, and nothing enforces it. A mismatched pair returns results, ranked, with no error, and they are meaningless. This is [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md) point 2 with a failure mode instead of a caution.
    - **`@search.score` for a vector query with a cosine metric is not a cosine value.** It is reported in a bounded range that is not cosine's range, on the same response field that carries the unbounded keyword score. **A threshold ported from a textbook, from another service, or from the same service's keyword side is wrong in a way that produces plausible output.**

    An engineer who cannot state what was chosen for them does not understand their own system, and on this day three such choices sit between the student's relevance criterion and the number they are about to report.

10. **Scope, in the strong form.** Points 3 and 4 are stated for the dual-encoder case with a supervised objective, which is the day's construction and the substrate's. **Safe** as a standing obligation for any retriever you train or fine-tune: name your negatives, and evaluate against negatives drawn differently. **Unsafe** as a general theory of retrieval training — it omits objectives with no explicit negatives, distillation from a stronger scorer, and unsupervised pretraining, all of which are in use and none of which this day equips a student to reason about. **A student who concludes that every retrieval training regime is characterised by its negative sampling will mis-read the majority of published training descriptions**, and the honest position is that they will need IS203 and the literature for the rest. Points 8 and 9 carry their own boundary: they are **safe** for reasoning about your own pipeline on the version in front of you, **unsafe** for any other service by analogy, and **unsafe as an account of what a good chunk size is** — the documentation supplies a starting point and states that the optimum depends on your content, which means the only defensible chunk size is one you measured.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Points 3 and 4 are the retained pair and point 4 is the hardest idea in the module. Point 5 is inherited verbatim and is repeated because it is the error with the worst production consequences. Point 6 is examined in [WP-057](wiki/whitepapers/WP-057.md) §8, which finds it asserted rather than demonstrated. **Point 8 is IS203's named anchor delivered at the moment it becomes true of the students' own index**, and its third consequence is the module's only security content, placed here rather than at [S078](wiki/seminars/S078.md) because the chunks that would leak are created today. Point 9 is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required naming of defaults.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 4. The relevance definition, the negatives and the probe set are the student's. This phase produces a build.**

Each student ships **a working vector index over the substrate corpus** — chunked, embedded and queryable — alongside yesterday's keyword fields, using Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server.

1. **State the relevance definition your retriever is being asked to learn** — your own [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md) criterion, unchanged, or an amendment with the reason for it.
2. **Construct your probe set.** It must cover **the respects this cohort could not predict at [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md)**, which are supplied as required dimensions, **plus at least two respects of your own** drawn from your step 1 definition.
3. **Define your positives and negatives explicitly, and state how each was drawn.**
4. **Predict** what your retriever will do on every probe dimension and on which query class it will lose to the baseline. Commit in writing.
5. **Train, index and evaluate.** Load the vectors into the index against your [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md) relevance judgements, and report by query class. **State your chunk size, your overlap and your splitter mode as decisions, with what you expect each to cost you** — an accepted default is a decision and is recorded as one.
6. **Evaluate a second time against negatives drawn differently from your training negatives, and report both numbers together.**
7. **State the query class on which your retriever loses to the [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md) baseline**, and whether the answer is a better retriever or a hybrid.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave the agent, in order, including the ones you abandoned.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that your own pipeline would have caught an intervention-worthy error — an assertion that the vectorizer's model matches the model that produced the index is the obvious example and is accepted, as is a chunk-length check expressed in the embedding model's own unit rather than in characters.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch. Both specific, both from this build.

**Fading:** step 3 is worked with the room, since negative construction is the day's new technical decision and is where students stall. The skillset and index schema are produced by the agent and read by the student. **Step 4 is unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 4 in which the student writes the similarity definition they spent [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md) learning to recover from someone else, **and ships the index that embodies it.** **Step 2 is the artefact element [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md) named under instrumentation rule 6**: the respects the cohort could not predict there are required dimensions here, so a blind spot measured five days ago determines what this day's probe set must contain. Step 5's chunking clause makes point 8 an assessed decision rather than an interesting fact. Step 6 is the day's single hard requirement and step 7 is its discriminator — a student reporting that their retriever loses nowhere has evaluated against one negative construction.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate retriever fine-tuned on the substrate's own data, which improved on the team's held-out evaluation by a margin large enough to justify the deployment, and moved no user-facing measure at all for a quarter. The evaluation's distractors were sampled at random from the corpus; the distractors users actually face are near-duplicate documents differing in version, product line or date. Attention is directed at the fact that **the team's evaluation set was built before the training pipeline and never revisited**, so nobody made the decision that caused the failure.

Then the agent case, and it is from this material. A student asked Copilot to add vector search to their index, chunking and embedding the corpus. The agent produced a complete pipeline: a splitter, an embedding step, a vector field with the right number of dimensions, a profile, a vectorizer, and a working query. It ran. It returned results. **It had set the splitter's overlap to zero** — a legal value, a documented value, and the value that guarantees that any answer spanning a boundary is in neither chunk. The student reviewed the pipeline by running three queries they knew the answers to, and all three answers were short and sat inside a chunk. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the failure is invisible on any query whose answer is short, and every query a student uses to check a new index has a short answer, because those are the queries they can check.**

Then students, in threes: a time you tested something against inputs that shared an assumption with the thing you were testing, or a parameter today whose value you accepted and cannot justify.

**Warrant.** Error management targeting point 4. The instructor case has no decision in it — the shared construction arose from ordinary sequencing rather than from a choice — which is what makes it a lesson about pipelines rather than about judgement. The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen because **the review a student would actually perform is structurally incapable of finding it.**

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **their step 6 pair of numbers and their step 7 loss class.**

The partner's task: **construct a negative-sampling scheme under which the student's reported improvement over the baseline would shrink to nothing or reverse**, and say what property of the substrate would have to hold for that scheme to be the realistic one.

Both parts are required. A scheme that erases the gain but that no user population would produce is not a defeat.

Per rule 3, the outcome is recorded in the artefact. Per rule 5, this reuses [S029](wiki/seminars/S029.md)'s dual-defeat structure — **a validated remedy**, having run at S029, S047, S048 and S053.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with the dual demand that makes the phase a test of the evaluation rather than of the model. The second clause is what stops the phase producing adversarial constructions with no referent, which is the failure mode of every "construct a defeating case" structure this design uses.

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- Sealed items opened. **Item 2's negative sources, tallied — including how many students had no answer.**
- Item 3's predicted ordering against phase 3's three evaluations.
- **Item 4's predictions on the [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md) unpredicted respects against step 5's probe results.** Whether the cohort's blind spots survived being named is the day's most quoted result.
- **Item 5 opened.** Sealed Copilot predictions against what each agent in fact produced, tallied for whether the named error was a model choice, a dimension, or a chunk boundary. The cohort's frontier hit rate is recorded.
- Step 4's predictions against steps 5 and 6, split by probe dimension and by query class.
- The cohort's step 6 gaps between the two evaluations, shown as a distribution.
- Each student names **one evaluation they have relied on whose negatives they cannot account for**, and **one pipeline parameter they accepted today without measuring.**
- Retrieval schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 6.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in IS203. Per [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the relevance definition with any amendment justified; the probe set covering the supplied required respects plus at least two of the student's own; the positives and negatives with their draw stated; the pre-committed predictions; **the shipped vector index with its chunk size, overlap and splitter mode recorded as decisions**; per-class results against the student's own judgements; **both evaluation numbers reported together**; the loss class with the hybrid decision; the phase 7 outcome; **the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

**A retriever evaluated only against negatives drawn as its training negatives were drawn fails.** This is the day's single hard requirement and it is step 6.

**A probe set omitting a supplied required respect fails.** The required respects are the cohort's own measured blind spots and are not optional.

**A chunking configuration reported as a value rather than as a decision fails.** Point 8 is checkable in the artefact, not only stated in the instruction.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the pipeline's own checks could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**D4 is not assessed** where the tenant could not provision it, per the phase 3 condition.

No entrustment judgement. The quarter's is at [S060](wiki/seminars/S060.md).

**Rule-6 discharges.** Two obligations were placed on this day and both are built.

- [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md) named this day's **step 2** as the response occasion for its step 3 prediction accuracy by respect. **The element exists and is assessed: the respects the cohort could not predict at S051 are supplied as required probe dimensions at step 2, and a probe set omitting one fails the specification.** The obligation is discharged.
- [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md) named this day's **phase 3** as the response occasion for its step 4 prediction accuracy by query class. **The element exists: the third of phase 3's evaluations is run on the query class the cohort mis-predicted worst at S056, and it is one of the two evaluations that reverse the payload's apparent ordering.** The obligation is discharged.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions:

- **The step 5 margin over the [S056](wiki/seminars/S056.md) baseline, by query class**, identifies where the dense method's advantage is thinnest and therefore where an approximation loss would erase it. **Response occasion: [S058](wiki/seminars/S058.md), phase 3, whose four index configurations are evaluated on the class where this cohort's margin was smallest.**
- **The step 2 probe results on [S051](wiki/seminars/S051.md)'s unpredicted respects** measure whether a named blind spot closes. **Response occasion: [S058](wiki/seminars/S058.md), phase 1, whose pretest asks students to predict what quantisation and pruning do to those same respects.**

**Frontier instrumentation.** Item 5's sealed prediction accuracy, coded for whether the named error concerned a model, a dimension or a chunk boundary, is carried to the [Durable and Perishable Register](wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md) per instrumentation rule 9. Per rule 3 the sample is stated: one cohort, one corpus, one service, one agent version, on a task where a silent failure was known to be available.

## Perishable content in this day

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| The retriever is a corpus-scale lookup | Vector fields and profiles on one index |
| The training signal is the relevance definition | Which embedding deployments the tenant holds |
| Negatives are the decision boundary | Every margin in phase 3 |
| Shared construction is a leak | The composition of the supplied evaluation |
| Scores are not comparable across models | The reported range of a vector score, and its distance from the metric it is named after |
