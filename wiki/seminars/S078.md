## What this day is for

[S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 6 said the students owned three of the four terms and would hear about it. [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5 said something harder and left it open, in these words:

> **Every retrieval system selects for something nobody asked for.** Your relevance criterion selected for a kind of answer; your corpus selected for **whose documents are findable at all**. The second is the one with ethical weight, it is the one your users cannot see, and **it is not answered today** — it returns at [S078](/wiki/seminars/S078.md), where a relevant document may be one this user may not have, and at [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md), where the person whose documents were never indexed is the person not in the room.

**Today is the first of those two days and it takes the first half only.** A relevant document may be one this user may not have. The other half — the person whose documents were never indexed — is [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md)'s, it is the half [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) called the one with ethical weight, and this day does not answer it. That is stated to the room at phase 4 point 7 rather than left in a whitepaper.

The technical shape of the half this day does take is simple to state and hard to place. **Relevance and authorisation are two different questions about the same document, and only one of them is in the index.** [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)'s geometry knows nothing about who is asking. [S073](/wiki/seminars/S073.md) established that a tool call crosses a boundary of trust and that the question is whose authority the call carries; a retrieval is a tool call, and on most systems the answer is *the service's, over everything, with a filter applied afterwards.*

That last word is the day. **A check applied after the content has entered the context is not an access control**, for the reason [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) point 6 gave one week ago, and the three places a defence can sit turn out to be four places a check can run, of which one is not a check at all.

And the check has a cost that falls unevenly. **Filtering degrades recall, and it degrades it most for whoever has the least access** — so the person with the fewest permissions gets the worst answers, is told nothing, and has no way to find out.

**This is the day the platform makes exact, and it is exact in an uncomfortable direction.** Document-level authorisation at retrieval time is not an architectural preference; on this stack it is a documented four-way choice with published trade-offs, and three facts about it are the day's spine.

- **The generally available option is the one the documentation itself calls a workaround.** The three that move the trust boundary from your application code into the service are in preview.
- **Enforcement runs against permission metadata already stored in the index, not against live source permissions.** A revocation does not bite until a synchronisation happens. The documentation states that a timing lag occurs and **publishes no figure for it**, which makes the latency something you measure or something you do not know.
- **Chunking silently discards permissions unless they are projected onto every chunk.** This is a live failure a student can produce, watch, and then close in one afternoon, and it is the single most instructive thing in the topic.

Everything on this day is executed against a real index. **Nothing runs against real users' documents**: the corpus is the substrate's own disposable copy with the cohort's synthetic principals, and the reason is in the brief.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

The substrate's retrieval system is supplied with its corpus, its permission model, and **four queries from four named principals** with different access. Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. **For each query, list the documents the system should return.** Work from the permission model on the page.
2. **Where in the pipeline would you enforce that?** Name the stage.
3. **A document's permissions changed yesterday. When does the answer change?** Give a time, not a mechanism.
4. **Predict: when you add the check, whose answers get worse, and by how much?**
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to *add document-level access control to this retrieval path.* Write down what it will produce and **name the one thing it will get wrong.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 4 is the day's payload administered before the day names it, and it is administered here rather than at phase 5 deliberately. **The design has recorded twice that it places its phase 5 instruments downstream of its own instruction** — opened at [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 and recorded as a second instance at [WP-066](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-066.md) §8 — and an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall. Item 4 is measured against step 3's measurement, and both readings exist.

Item 5 is the day's second payload. The design's expectation is a specific and testable one: **the agent will produce the string-filter approach**, because it is the generally available one with the most documentation and the most sample code, will implement it competently, and **will not say that the documentation calls it a workaround** or that the trust boundary now sits in the code it just wrote. The second expectation is sharper still — that whatever it produces will not project permission metadata onto chunks, because the chunking skillset and the access control are documented on different pages.

**Warrant.** Pretesting against a real corpus and a real permission model with the correct answer derivable from the page, so that item 1 produces a graded result rather than an opinion. Item 3 asks for a duration because the students will supply a mechanism, and the substitution is the finding: staleness is a latency and the room does not have it as one. **Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 per instrumentation rule 8**, and it is the day's contribution to the frontier map. It is also the only Copilot prediction in the module with a documented right answer available before the day runs, which makes it the cohort's cleanest calibration datum — and the reason phase 8 reports the *named-the-failure* rate separately from the *predicted-a-failure* rate.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**Executed, not read.** Groups have **a disposable copy of the substrate's index** — the same corpus, the cohort's synthetic principals, no real users' documents — stood up with Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server. They configure **four placements of one authorisation decision over one corpus and one query set**, run the query set through each, and compare their results against the retrieval records of the periods those placements ran on the substrate: what was returned, to whom, with what latency, and what the users did next.

- **P1 — index time.** A separate index per permission group. Correct results, no leakage, and an index count that grows with the permission structure rather than with the corpus. Staleness is a re-index.
- **P2 — query time.** The permission predicate is pushed into the approximate index as a filter over metadata. Fast, and — this is [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md)'s material arriving where it was flagged to arrive — **its recall depends on the selectivity of the filter.** The more restricted the principal, the worse the search.
- **P3 — retrieval time.** Post-filter the top *k*. Simplest, most widely deployed, and it **silently truncates**: a principal permitted four of the top ten receives four results and no indication that six were removed.
- **P4 — answer time.** Redact the generated answer. **The payload.**

Groups rank the four by *how much does this leak*, commit in writing, and only then run them and receive the records.

**P4 wins the room's ranking and loses on the record.** Nothing unauthorised appears in the output, which is what the ranking measures. What the records show is that the answers were *conditioned* on documents the principal could not see — the summaries carry their content, the ordering carries their existence, and two of the substrate's own evaluations caught the model paraphrasing material it had been instructed to redact. **A control applied after the content has entered the context is not an access control**; it is [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) point 6's first place wearing the second place's clothes, and the room proved it one week ago in a different register.

**One further configuration is run and it is not a placement.** Groups take their P2 index, add a chunking step of the kind integrated vectorisation uses, and query it again with the same restricted principal. **The permission metadata stays on the parent document and the chunks come back unfiltered**, because the fields moved from indexer field mappings to index projections and nobody moved them. Nothing was misconfigured in the sense a review would catch: the permission filter is present, the token is attached, the enforcement is correct, and it is enforcing over rows that no longer carry the data. **This is the day's most important five minutes** and it is placed inside phase 3 rather than in the consolidation because a room that has watched it happen will not need to be told twice.

Task after: **for each placement, name the principal who gets the worst answers, and say how that principal would find out.**

In three of the four, they would not.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across placements of one decision, matched on corpus, query set and relevance criterion, and varied only on where the check runs — which is the target dimension — **executed against a real index rather than supplied as records, with the substrate's own retrieval records as the historical adjudicator.** P4 defeats the expectation that later is safer, and it defeats it on the criterion the room chose to rank by, which is what makes the defeat instructive rather than merely surprising. The task-after is the phase's real product and it produces point 4 before phase 4 states it.

**The chunking run is a deliberate second payload and it does a different job from P4.** P4 shows a control that is correct and irrelevant; the chunk projection shows a control that is correct, relevant, and applied to the wrong rows. The two together are the day's whole argument about what "the check is in place" is worth as a statement, and neither is reachable by exposition.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **Relevance and authorisation are two questions about the same document, and only one of them is in the index.** [S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md)'s geometry has no coordinate for *who is asking*. Every architecture in phase 3 is an answer to where the second question gets asked, and none of them puts it in the same place as the first.

2. **Four placements, four failures.** Index time trades index count and re-index latency; query time trades recall against filter selectivity; retrieval time trades silent truncation; answer time trades nothing and does not work. **This list omits two things and both are load-bearing.** It omits models where the predicate is computed rather than stored — attribute- and relationship-based authorisation, where the answer depends on data the retriever would have to join at query time. And it omits **inference from aggregates**: a set of documents a principal may each see can jointly reveal a fact they may not, and no placement on this list addresses it. **Safe as a design frame for document-level permissions that are stored, enumerable and per-principal; unsafe wherever authorisation is computed or wherever the sensitive object is a fact rather than a document** — and the second is not answered anywhere in this programme.

3. **A check that is not on the retrieval path is not a check.** SG301's claim, applied. The retrieval path is where the document is selected; anything after that is operating on content that has already entered the context, and [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) point 6 established what that is worth. P4 is the demonstration and it is the one the room ranked safest.

4. **Filtering degrades recall, and it degrades it most for whoever has the least access.** [S058](/wiki/seminars/S058.md)'s recall-latency trade with a predicate attached: an approximate index searched under a selective filter visits fewer viable candidates, so the more restricted the principal, the worse the search — **and the degradation is invisible to every evaluation the cohort has built, because those were run as the service.** This is the day's technical payload and it has a distributive consequence: your system is worst for the person with the least access and reports nothing.

5. **Staleness is a latency and has the same shape as detection latency.** The time from a permission change to its effect on an answer. State it in [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s four-part form — what condition, within what time, observed by whom, at what false-alarm rate — for the condition *a revoked permission is still effective.* On most systems nobody owns it, nobody measures it, and it is bounded by a cache expiry somebody chose for performance.

6. **What you say about what you withheld is a disclosure decision, and it is not yours.** Three options and each leaks something different: **silent omission** (the user cannot distinguish absence from non-existence and will act on the wrong one, which is phase 6's case); **acknowledged withholding** — *there are results you cannot see* — which is honest and reveals that a document exists; and **refusal**, which reveals that the query was of a kind that touches restricted material. There is no option that leaks nothing. The choice has an owner, per [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 2, and the owner is whoever bears the cost of the wrong disclosure — which is not the engineer.

7. **The authorisation half of [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) point 5, discharged.** That day said your corpus selects for whose documents are findable at all, that this is the selection with ethical weight, that your users cannot see it, and that it returns here and at [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md). **This day answers the half it was assigned:** a relevant document may be one this user may not have, the decision about that has four possible homes and one correct one, and what the user is told about it is a decision with an owner. **This day does not answer the other half.** The person whose documents were never indexed is not affected by any placement on this list, is not visible in any per-class recall measurement, and is not in the permission model at all — because the permission model only describes documents that exist in the corpus. **That is [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md)'s and the instrument is named in this day's specification**, per the deferral rule in the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md), which requires that a deferral name the instrument by which the receiving day shows it was discharged.

8. **What one vendor built, what it decided for you, and what it will not tell you. Marked perishable; the reasoning is not.** [Azure AI Search](/research/06-microsoft-platform/azure-ai-search-and-acl-patterns.md) documents four approaches to enforcing per-document permissions, and reading them against points 1 to 5 is the most efficient hour available on this subject.

   - **The four approaches are a choice about where the trust boundary sits, not about how the filter is written.** A **security filter** carries an identity into the query as a string; a **permission filter** compares the security principal behind the caller's own token against permission metadata stored on the document; **sensitivity labels** delegate to an enterprise classification system; **SharePoint ACLs** delegate to the source system's own model. The stated selection rule is the durable content: **reuse the authoritative permission system rather than reimplement it.**
   - **The generally available option is the one the documentation calls a workaround.** In its own words the string approach is "simple string comparison" and the token approach is "recognized as Microsoft Entra ID authentication". A string filter is exactly as trustworthy as the code that supplies the string, and **nothing in the service checks that the caller is who the filter says they are.** The other three are in preview. **This is the day's default the platform chose for you** and it is chosen in the direction of availability: the option with the weakest trust boundary is the one that works everywhere and has the most sample code, which is why item 5 predicts the agent will produce it.
   - **Two checks, not one.** With permission filters, the service checks that your *application* holds the index-read role, and then, given a user token attached in a separate request header, checks that the *user* is permitted each document returned. **Application authorisation and user authorisation are separate concerns evaluated separately**, and a design that conflates them has one check where the model has two.
   - **Granularity is a property of the source, not of your intent.** Document-level scope is preserved for one storage service and only container-level scope for another; SharePoint refreshes permissions on items with unique permissions on each successful indexer run and requires an explicit resynchronisation for changes inherited from a parent scope. **The inherited case is both the more common one and the one that does not self-heal**, which is point 5's staleness with a mechanism attached and an asymmetry nobody would guess.
   - **Enforcement is against indexed metadata and the lag is unquantified.** The documentation states repeatedly that claims are compared against permission metadata already in the index, that source-side changes take effect only after synchronisation, and that a timing lag occurs. **It gives no latency figure, and no accuracy figure appears anywhere on the page.** So the revocation window is not a number you look up; it is a number you measure, which is what step 5 now requires.
   - **The chunking trap, which you have just produced.** When a skillset chunks documents, the permission fields move from indexer field mappings to index projections, and without that projection chunk-level references are not filtered. The durable statement is more general than the product: **any transformation that creates new rows creates rows with no permissions unless somebody carries them across**, and the transformation is standard practice.
   - **The governance layer is sampled and slow, and says so.** Purview's data risk assessment for oversharing runs by default on a weekly cadence over a bounded set of the estate's most-used sites, with multi-day delays before first results. Its argument is the one worth keeping: **a capable retrieval system converts every dormant permissions error in an organisation into a live one**, because oversharing was survivable only while nobody was reading. **No detection rate or false-positive rate is published for it either.** Where the institution's tenant does not permit the assessment to be run, it is **demonstrated rather than operated and is not assessed.**

   **Safe** as a way of choosing where a check runs, of predicting what each choice costs and to whom, and of knowing what to measure. **Unsafe** as a claim that any of them is correct: the documentation compares the four on fit and **not on security strength**, so a student who writes "we use the native approach, therefore we are authorised" has quoted a product page rather than made a statement about their system. It is also unsafe as a completeness claim about the *approaches* — point 2's two omissions, computed authorisation and inference from aggregates, are omissions of this list too, and the second is not answered anywhere in this programme.

9. **Class 6: what you are handing over is a statement of who can see what, to somebody who will be asked by a user.** The warranty is what you will stand behind when the answer is wrong in the direction of disclosure — which is the direction that cannot be undone, unlike the other one. A statement with a placement, a measured cost, a measured revocation latency and a disclosure position is a handover. A statement with a placement is a configuration note.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3, with point 4 produced by the task-after rather than asserted and point 3 imported from [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) one week old. Points 2 and 6 carry the enumeration scope statements and point 2 carries the strong form. **Point 7 is the discharge of a deferral made four weeks ago and it is written so that the discharge does not become a completeness claim** — the half not taken is named, its home is named, and the instrument is in the specification rather than in this warrant. **Point 8 is the day's platform-specific content**, is marked perishable, names the default and carries its own strong-form scope statement, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md); its sixth bullet consolidates what the room produced at phase 3 rather than introducing it, which is the arrangement the pending rule proposed at [WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 would require.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 6. The handover is an authorisation position and the receiver is the substrate's data owner. This phase produces a build.**

Students use **their own retrieval path**, shipped at [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md) and operated since, **a disposable copy of its index carrying synthetic principals**, and **Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server**.

1. **State the principal model:** who asks, on whose authority the retrieval runs, and — where those differ — which of the two the index was built for.
2. **State the placement you have chosen and the three you have not**, and for your choice state **the failure you are accepting**, in the terms phase 3's records supplied. **Name which of the platform's four approaches your placement corresponds to, and where its trust boundary sits** — in your code or in the service.
3. **Ship the check to the copy and measure the recall degradation it imposes, per principal class**, against the same relevance judgements you used at [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md). **Name the class it is worst for**, and state how many principals are in that class. If the class is small, say how small — a measurement over three people is a measurement over three people.
4. **State the capability bound for requests whose context carries content from an untrusted path:** which actions are unavailable, and which retrieved sources count as untrusted for this purpose. **This is [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) step 7 continued**, and a student who removed no action there has nothing to state here, which is itself the result.
5. **Measure the revocation latency rather than estimating it.** On the copy, revoke a synthetic principal's access to a document that is currently retrievable for them, and time how long it remains retrievable. **Report the measurement in [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s four-part form**, for the condition *a revoked permission is still effective*, with the provenance of the figure and your confidence in it in the same field. **Where the copy's synchronisation mechanism differs from production's, say so and say what that makes the measurement worth** — an inherited-scope change and a unique-permission change do not behave the same way and the artefact must name which one it measured.
6. **Chunk one document in the copy and check the projection.** Confirm whether your permission metadata reaches the chunk rows, then fix it if it does not, and record both results. **A student whose pipeline does not chunk records that, and says what would have to change before this check applied.** This is a checked element and it is checked for the record rather than for the outcome.
7. **Decide and record the disclosure position** from point 6's three options. Name **the person entitled to make it** — a person, not a function — and where you cannot obtain that person's decision, **record the position as unowned**, per [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 8.
8. **Take the position to the substrate's data owner**, who is present for the last thirty minutes and is not obliged to agree. Present it and ask **one question**: *when this is wrong, who do I tell, and what do I tell them?* Record the answer verbatim, including a non-answer.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md): the **prompt record**, the **intervention record** — **empty fails**, unless the artefact shows the design could have caught an intervention-worthy error, and the accepted example here is exact and cheap: **a test in the pipeline that queries as the least-privileged synthetic principal and fails the build if a document outside their entitlement is returned** — and the **frontier statement**.

**Fading:** step 1 is worked with the room and step 3's per-class measurement form is supplied. **Step 7 is unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 6 on the student's own production retrieval path, producing a handover to a named receiver in the room, **and a working check on a disposable copy rather than a described one.** **Step 3 is the day's discriminator and step 7 is its judgement** — the measurement can be done by anybody once the form is supplied, and deciding what a user is told about what they did not get cannot.

**Step 5 is the change the platform forces and it is a real improvement on describing a latency.** The documentation states that a timing lag occurs and publishes no figure; a student who measures one has produced the only number available on the subject for their own system, and a student who reports it without naming which synchronisation path they exercised has produced a number that does not transfer. **Step 6 is the chunking trap converted from a demonstration into an obligation**, and it is checked for the record rather than for the outcome because a student whose projection was already correct should not be worse off than one who broke it.

**Step 8 uses an external observer per instrumentation rule 1** and reuses [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) phase 7's one-question structure, on the ground that a position that survives only in the room where it was made is not a position. Per rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8 the reuse is **contingent** on [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s reported outcome. **Step 4 is the artefact element [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) named under instrumentation rule 6.** Step 3's class-size clause is a correction under [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) specification rule 2 and under [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md)'s discipline: a per-class result whose class size is not stated in the same field will be read as a rate.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate case in which **a permission was correctly enforced and a user was harmed anyway.** The retrieval returned nothing, the interface said there were no results, the user concluded the document did not exist, and acted on that conclusion. Every component behaved as specified. The permission was correct, the filter was correct, the message was the one the interface had always shown, and nobody had ever decided that *no results* and *no results you may see* should look the same.

Then the agent case: a Copilot-produced access-control change that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. Asked to add document-level access control to a retrieval path, it added a permissions field to the index, populated it from the source system, generated a filter expression, wired the caller's group identity into the query, wrote a test with two personas that passed, and cited the documentation at every step. **It was the string-filter approach, it was implemented correctly, and it was described in the pull request as "document-level access control enforced at query time."** Two things were absent and neither is visible in a diff: the documentation's own word for that approach, and any projection of the permissions field onto the chunk rows the pipeline had been producing since [S060](/wiki/seminars/S060.md). Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the reviewer checked the code against the cited page and the cited page was correct**, and the failure was in which page had been cited.

Then students, in threes: **a time a system's silence told you something false.**

**Warrant.** Error management targeting point 6, with an instructor case containing no defect at any layer. It is chosen because the alternative case — a leak — would let the room locate the failure in a component, and this failure is located in an absent decision. The student prompt is deliberately not about permissions, so that the disclosure is cheap and the transfer is the student's own. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition**, and it is the exact failure item 5 asked the room to predict at nine in the morning — so phase 8 can report how many students named it in advance, watched it, or did it.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **their step 2 placement and its accepted failure, and their step 7 disclosure position**, withholding the step 8 answer.

The partner takes the role of **the least-privileged principal in the student's own step 3 measurement** — the class the student has just named as worst-served — and asks **one question**: *what did I not get, and how would I know?*

The pair then decides whether the position answers it, and records the answer. **A position that cannot answer is a result about the position**, not an absence of one, and it is recorded as such.

Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) specification rule 3, the outcome is recorded in the artefact.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with an absent-party test, reusing [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) phase 7's structure and its one-question limit under rule 5, contingent. Using the student's *own measured* worst-served class rather than a generic user is the change from [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md): the partner is not playing a hypothetical stakeholder but a class the student identified twenty minutes earlier with a number attached, which removes the escape that the affected party is speculative.

## Between-day protocol

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** Relevance and authorisation are two questions about the same document, and only one of them is in the index. **A check applied after the content has entered the context is not an access control** — and the check you do add degrades recall most for whoever has the least access.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** The approximate-search notes here — [research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/malkov-yashunin-hnsw.md) and [research/02-technical-foundations/subramanya-diskann.md](/research/02-technical-foundations/subramanya-diskann.md) — carry **direction and mechanism only**: an index searched under a selective predicate visits fewer viable candidates. **No magnitude is asserted.** The four approaches sit at [research/06-microsoft-platform/azure-ai-search-and-acl-patterns.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/azure-ai-search-and-acl-patterns.md); that documentation compares them on fit and **not on security strength**, says a synchronisation lag occurs without publishing a figure, and publishes no detection or false-positive rate.

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Measure the revocation window; do not estimate it**, and name which synchronisation path you exercised.
- **Report recall per principal class and name the worst-served class with its size.** A mean has discarded the finding.
- **Check that permissions reached the chunk rows.** Any transformation that creates new rows creates rows with no permissions unless somebody carries them across.
- **A check's authority is a property of the tier it runs in, not of the check.** Per the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md), a filter your own code supplies is exactly as trustworthy as that code.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Ranking the latest check as the safest. Redaction at answer time leaks nothing into the output and still conditions the answer on documents the principal may not have.
