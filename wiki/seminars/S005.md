# S005 · Induction and the Machine That Runs Forever

**Module:** [M01](/wiki/modules/M01.md) · **Quarter:** [Q1](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md) · **Complexity class:** 1
**Strands:** MS · CF · SY
**Platform anchor:** GitHub Copilot agent mode; custom instructions as executable specification · Copilot as a proof-checking adversary · **MCP servers:** Microsoft Learn MCP Server
**Whitepaper:** [WP-005](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-005.md)
**Depends on:** S004
**Re-tests:** S001, S003

---

## What this day is for

S002 established that a machine's behaviour is determined. That establishes nothing useful on its own, because determined behaviour over an unbounded number of steps cannot be checked by tracing. The gap between *determined* and *known* is closed by one technique, and induction is that technique.

The day teaches induction as **the only available method for making a finite argument about an unbounded process**, and it teaches it on loops rather than on natural numbers, because loops are where students will use it and natural numbers are where students learn to distrust it.

The module closes here. Everything in it — trace, representation, argument — is required to say something true about a program that has not finished running.

**It is also the day the module's most uncomfortable finding about the instrument arrives, and it arrives by accident of construction.** Phase 3's loop 4 was designed, long before this day had a platform anchor, to be the case that defeats the room. It is also the case that defeats the agent. Four days of evidence have shown students a tool that fails in places they can catch; today it fails in the one place they cannot, and the lesson is the one the jagged frontier is actually about. **A tool whose errors are correlated with yours is not a second opinion.** Running one is not a check.

---

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Four items.

1. A loop is given. State what is true after every iteration. (Most students state what is true *at the end*, which is a different and weaker claim.)
2. An inductive argument is given with a subtly wrong base case. Is the conclusion true?
3. A property is stated that holds for the first thousand inputs. Does it hold for all?
4. Predict: will your answer to item 1 be the property the instructor has in mind?

**The Copilot prediction, sealed.** A fifth item:

5. The agent will be shown four loops with four candidate properties and asked, for each, whether the property is an invariant. **How many will it get right — and will its errors be in the same places as yours?**

The second clause is the item, and it is the last Copilot prediction of the module because it is the one the module has been building toward. Four days of predictions have asked *where will the tool be wrong*. This one asks **whether being right yourself will tell you.**

**Warrant.** Item 1 is the day's core skill, pretested before instruction; the characteristic error — confusing the postcondition with the invariant — appears immediately and is diagnostic. Item 2 separates a true conclusion from a warranted one, retrieving S004 phase 1's argument C. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 per instrumentation rule 8, and is the module's terminal frontier item.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative retrieval set (20 min)

Free recall, no notes, interleaved.

- What are the four argument-failure classes from S004?
- State the S003 common-structure sentence.
- What was your S001 P2 prediction about? (Not its value — its subject.)
- Which of the S002 error classes and which of the S004 failure classes are the same failure under different names?

The final item is a discrimination demand across two days and typically produces genuine disagreement, which is left unresolved for ninety seconds before the answer is discussed.

**Warrant.** Expanding-interval retrieval with cross-day discrimination. The S001 item at a nine-day gap is the first genuinely spaced retrieval in the programme.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

*First, 25 minutes, unchanged.* Groups receive **four loops** and, for each, a candidate property.

- Loop 1: the property is a true invariant.
- Loop 2: the property is true at termination but not during iteration.
- Loop 3: the property is true during iteration but says nothing useful at termination.
- Loop 4: the property is *almost* an invariant — it fails on exactly one iteration and re-establishes itself.

Task: for each, decide whether the property can be used to prove anything about the loop, and if so what. Then — the generation demand — **for loop 3, construct a property that would be useful.**

Groups commit in writing.

Loop 4 is the case that defeats groups, and it is the case that teaches what "invariant" actually requires.

*Then, 20 minutes: four prompts, run.* Each group puts one of the four loops and its candidate property to the agent, with the question *is this property an invariant of this loop, and how do you know?* Groups commit their own verdict first, in writing, and only then run.

**The payload is loop 4, and it is the only payload in this module that is a case the room also fails.** The agent affirms the near-invariant. It produces a short argument for establishment, a short argument for preservation, and the preservation argument does not examine the iteration on which the property fails. The argument is well formed, correctly structured, uses the right vocabulary, and skips the case.

Three things are then true at once and they are put on the board together:

1. The room got loop 4 wrong.
2. The tool got loop 4 wrong.
3. **Nobody in the room could have discovered 1 by consulting the tool.**

Loops 1 to 3 are the control and are answered correctly, which is what makes point 3 bite: four days of the tool being wrong in catchable places have built a habit of catching it, and that habit is worth nothing here. This is the jagged frontier's actual content, met on a case the design chose for its difficulty rather than for its ability to embarrass a tool — and the coincidence is stated to the room as a coincidence, because a designed embarrassment would prove nothing.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases: four surfaces, one underlying question about what an invariant must satisfy. The four cases are constructed so that the definition is recoverable from the contrast alone, which is the condition under which a definition is understood rather than memorised. The executions hold the question constant and vary only the loop, and the groups' prior written commitment is what makes the correlation between their errors and the tool's observable rather than asserted.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **The problem.** Unbounded process, finite argument. Tracing cannot close this gap and nothing else can.
2. **Induction over the naturals**, briefly, as the familiar case.
3. **Induction over structure** — lists, trees — as the same argument with a different well-founded ordering.
4. **The loop invariant** as induction over iteration count. Establishment, preservation, and what it gives you at termination. Indexed directly to loops 1 to 4.
5. **Termination is a separate obligation.** An invariant tells you what is true *if* the loop ends. A variant tells you that it does. Conflating the two is the day's most consequential error.
6. **Where inductive arguments fail:** base case that does not establish, step that assumes what it proves, an ordering that is not well-founded.
7. **A correlated reviewer is not a reviewer.** Indexed to phase 3's loop 4, and stated once, plainly, because it is the module's closing claim about its own instrument.

   The rule the room has been operating for four days is: *predict where it will be wrong, then check.* The rule is sound and it has a precondition nobody stated, which is that **you can tell.** Loop 4 is the case where you cannot, and it is not rare. A near-invariant that fails on one iteration and re-establishes is one of the most common shapes of real defect in production code, and it is common precisely because it survives review — human review and machine review alike, for the same reason. **Independence is a property of the pair, not of either member**, and a check whose failures coincide with yours has told you nothing on exactly the occasions you needed telling.

   What follows is not *do not use the tool*. It is that a second opinion must be **differently constructed**, and the only differently-constructed check available today is one that executes: phase 5's property check does not reason about the loop and therefore cannot make the loop's argument badly.

   *Perishable, marked as such:* that this model on this day affirms this near-invariant. A later model may not, and if it does not, **the claim in this point is unchanged** — correlation between a reasoner's blind spots and yours is not a fact about one release. *Durable:* that a check must be independent of the thing it checks, and that reasoning-shaped checks correlate.

8. **A default the platform chose for you: how long the reasoning lives.** Where planning is enabled, the agent maintains a human-readable plan and a machine-readable one; the documentation states that plans are stored temporarily and deleted when the session ends unless saved manually. The reasoning that produced today's verdict on loop 4 is, by default, gone. **Your evidence about how a conclusion was reached has a retention policy, and it was not you who set it.** This is why the prompt record and the intervention record are written by the student and not exported from the tool. *Perishable: the storage location, the file names, the enablement setting. Durable: that a record you did not create is a record you do not control.*

9. **The professional transposition.** A monitoring invariant, a rate limit that must hold under all traffic, a safety property that must survive every state — all of these are this, and all of them also require the termination question asked separately. And every one of them will, in three years' time, be reviewed first by something that reasons the way you do.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Point 5 is the day's key distinction and is given a full segment because it is the one students collapse. Point 7 is placed after the failure classes because it is a claim about review and needs them; it is the module's terminal claim about the instrument and is stated once rather than distributed. Scope rule, strong form: point 7 is safe as a statement about any reviewer that reasons and unsafe as a statement about executable checks, which fail differently and are the subject of phase 5.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**The build: a check that is not a reasoner.**

Students take a loop from the S002 machine program — the running-maximum computation they traced by hand in week two — and:

1. **State its invariant.** Commit in writing before proving anything.
2. **Prove establishment and preservation.**
3. **State and prove the variant** that guarantees termination.
4. **Write the proof for a named reader**, using the S004 protocol, and have it rejected once.
5. **Break it.** Modify the loop so that the invariant fails, and state precisely which of establishment, preservation or termination broke.
6. **Build a runnable check**, directed through the agent: a program that executes the loop and asserts the invariant *at every iteration*, reporting the first iteration at which it does not hold. Run it against the sound loop and against the broken one from step 5.
7. **Report what the check caught and what it missed**, in those words. Then run it against phase 3's loop 4 — the near-invariant the room and the tool both affirmed — and report the result.

Step 5 is the day's payload. Constructing the failure is harder than constructing the proof and demonstrates whether the proof was understood or performed.

**Step 7 is the module's closing argument.** The check catches loop 4. It catches it because it does not reason about the loop; it runs it and looks. The single iteration on which the near-invariant fails is exactly the kind of thing a reasoner skips and an executor cannot. **That is what an independent check means**, stated in one sentence and demonstrated in one run, and it is why phase 4 point 7's claim ends in a construction rather than in a warning.

Step 7 also has a second half, and it is the honest one: **the check misses termination entirely.** A check that asserts at every iteration says nothing about a loop that never reaches another iteration, and a student who reports the check as validating the loop has collapsed exactly the distinction point 5 spent a full segment separating. The invariant obligation and the termination obligation need different instruments, and the day ends with only one of them built.

**Faded guidance:** step 2 is worked for a simpler loop with the room; step 3 is a completion problem with the variant's form supplied; steps 4 to 7 are independent.

The three additional deliverables:

- **The prompt record.**
- **The intervention record.** **This is the module's first day on which an empty record may legitimately pass**, and it is designed to be. The check at step 6 is small, the agent usually writes it correctly, and there may be nothing to correct. The [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s exception applies: an empty record passes where the artefact demonstrates the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error, and step 7's run against the broken loop is that demonstration. A student whose check does not fail on the broken loop has not met the exception and an empty record fails.
- **The frontier statement.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 1. Variability of practice across the natural, structural and iteration forms of the same argument. Step 5 is a generation demand of the productive kind — it has a right answer and students reach it by construction. Step 6 is the module's only build whose value is that it is *stupider* than the thing it checks, which is the property the module wants remembered.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Opened by the instructor with an invariant from the substrate's monitoring configuration that was believed to hold, did not, and was not noticed for some time — with attention to *why the belief survived*.

Then students: a property you assumed held across a whole computation and did not check.

Collected by the phase 4 point 6 failure classes.

**The agent case, and it is the module's last.** The instructor puts up the agent's phase 3 loop 4 argument in full: establishment, preservation, conclusion, correctly structured, using the day's vocabulary before the day taught it, and skipping the iteration that matters. Alongside it goes a group's own written verdict on loop 4 from twenty-five minutes earlier, which reached the same conclusion by the same omission.

The question is not why the agent was wrong. It is **why the group, reading the agent's argument, found it convincing** — and the answer is that it agreed with them and was better written. A reviewer who confirms you and does it fluently is the hardest reviewer to reject, and the room has now experienced that specifically, at week five, on a case where being wrong cost nothing.

The instructor states plainly what this does and does not license. It does not license distrusting the tool; the tool was right about loops 1, 2 and 3 and about most of what it did this week. It licenses one operational rule: **where you cannot construct the failure case yourself, an agreeing review is not evidence.** That rule is the module's contribution to the three-year frontier map and is written down as such.

**Warrant.** Error management, with the instructor's disclosure chosen so that the failure class is the one the day just taught, in a production setting, at real cost. The agent case is placed second so that the substrate failure has already established the class, and the room is asked about their own reception of the argument rather than about the argument, because the reception is the correctable part.

---

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. One student presents their step 5 broken loop **without saying what they broke**. The partner must identify whether establishment, preservation or termination failed, and construct an input that exhibits the failure.

Roles swap.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with a diagnosis demand. Identifying which obligation failed from the behaviour alone is the same move as incident diagnosis at S079, and is planted here for retrieval two years out.

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- Item 4 resolved. The invariant-versus-postcondition confusion rate is shown to the room.
- **Item 5 resolved, and the module's frontier map closes.** The cohort's predictions about the agent's four verdicts are compared against the runs, and the second clause — *will its errors be in the same places as yours* — is answered from the room's own committed verdicts. The five days' frontier statements are collated into one page. It is a sample: one cohort, five hand-checkable problems, one model, one week. **The page says so on it**, and its reader is next year's cohort by way of the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md). Each student's own page is **carried to [S010](/wiki/seminars/S010.md) and returned to them alongside that day's assisted-versus-unassisted comparison**, so that the comparison is read against a baseline the student wrote themselves rather than against nothing.
- **The module closes.** Students write one paragraph: what they now believe about programs that they did not believe five days ago. These are kept and returned at S090.
- The S004 undefended-claim commitment is checked: defended, withdrawn, or still outstanding.
- Retrieval schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 6, and a module-level cumulative set in week 8.

## Assessment

One specification item, jointly specified in MS101 and CF101 — the only such item in Q1, deliberately, so that the argument stance is enforced in the programming course as well as the mathematics one. Binary. Resubmittable. No entrustment evidence.

Checked:

1. The committed invariant, written before anything was proved.
2. Establishment and preservation proofs.
3. The variant and the termination argument.
4. The reader-directed version, with the rejection recorded.
5. The broken loop, with the failing obligation correctly identified.
6. The runnable check: it runs, it asserts at every iteration, it reports the first failing iteration.
7. The three runs — sound loop, broken loop, loop 4 — and the statement of what the check caught and what it missed. **The termination omission must be named**; a report claiming the check validates the loop fails.
8. **The prompt record.**
9. **The intervention record.** May be empty, and passes empty only where item 7's broken-loop run shows the check failing as designed.
10. **The frontier statement**, both halves, specific to this build.

**Nothing else is assessed.** The check's code quality is not assessed. Whether the agent wrote the check well is not assessed. Whether the student's frontier statement agrees with anyone else's is not assessed.

*Annotation at the point of change.* Items 1 to 5 are the original specification, unchanged, and the [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md)'s S001–S040 non-retroactivity boundary holds over them. Items 6 to 10 are new element classes and are stated in the closed form the rule requires. **Item 9 is the first exercise in the programme of the intervention record's exception clause**, and the day was chosen for it because a small, correct, executable artefact is the one case where an honest empty record is the expected outcome rather than a suspicious one.

## Perishable content in this day

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| A reasoning-shaped check correlates with a reasoning human and is not a second opinion | This version's loop 4 argument: establishment, preservation, and the iteration it skipped |
| Independence is a property of the pair, not of either member | The coincidence, this year, between the room's committed loop 4 verdict and the tool's |
| A check that executes fails differently from one that reasons | The runnable property check built through the agent-mode surface at step 6 |
| A check that asserts during iteration says nothing about a loop that stops iterating | The three step 7 runs — sound loop, broken loop, loop 4 |
| A record you did not create is a record you do not control | The human-readable and machine-readable plans, stored temporarily and deleted at session end unless saved manually |

**Durable — assessed.** That induction is the only method for making a finite argument about an unbounded process. That establishment, preservation and termination are three obligations and the third is separate. That a check must be independent of what it checks, and that reasoning-shaped reviewers correlate with reasoning humans. That a check which asserts during iteration says nothing about a loop that stops iterating.

**Platform instance — not assessed.** That this model on this day affirms this particular near-invariant, and the wording of the argument it produced. The planning-artefact retention behaviour and its storage location. The agent-mode surface through which the check was built. **If a later model resolves loop 4 correctly the day is unchanged**, because the day's claim is about correlated review and not about one release's competence; the phase 3 case is then re-selected and the register records that it was.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **A check must be independent of the thing it checks, and independence is a property of the pair rather than of either member.** A reviewer that reasons the way you reason will skip the case you skipped. That is not a defect in one release; it is what correlation means, and why a check that merely executes is worth more here than one that argues.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** The observability apparatus behind phase 6's disclosure is verified here, in [research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md](/research/05-fde-craft/microsoft-ise-engineering-playbook.md). The jagged-frontier position underlying phase 4 is grounded in [research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md](/research/04-professional-formation/dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier.md), **a note written from the official abstract rather than a full text**; the shape is usable, no number from it is, and none is asserted. The monitoring-invariant failure class is this design's own construction, described by no note in [research/05-fde-craft/](/research/05-fde-craft/collected-materials.md).

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Build the check that runs rather than the one that reasons.** It is stupider than the thing it checks, and that is the property you want.
- **Ask termination separately.** Nothing you assert during iteration says anything about a loop that stops iterating.
- **Where you cannot construct the failure case yourself, an agreeing review is not evidence.**
- **Say what your check licenses.** An executable check establishes only that non-conformance *of the shape it tests* can be detected — the second of the five classes on the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md) — which is why yours catches the near-invariant and misses termination.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Reading agreement as confirmation. The room and the tool reached the same verdict on loop 4 by the same omission, and the better-written argument was the one nobody was equipped to reject.
