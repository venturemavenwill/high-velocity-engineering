## The situation

Four sessions ago you wrote a position on an engagement you knew almost nothing about. Since then you have located your own boundary, stripped the technology out of the request, and read the room you were invited into.

Today you decide, and somebody argues with you.

Answer these in writing before the session opens. They are the last set you will answer cold in this stage.

1. What do you now believe the problem is, and what changed that belief since [S001](/wiki/seminars/S001.md)?
2. Which of your beliefs about this engagement rests on something you observed, and which on something you were told?
3. Is the disqualifying condition you wrote at S001 still the right one? If you amend it, what fired the amendment?
4. What is the decision, and what would reverse it?
5. Who has to agree with you for the decision to have any effect, and have they?

**Item 2 is where most positions come apart.** Almost everything a learner believes at this point was told to them by somebody with an interest in the answer.

## What you must produce

A **qualification decision** — proceed, proceed on a named condition, or decline — with the problem stated, the evidence separated from the assumption, the condition that would reverse it, and the authority it depends on. Then you defend it against challenge.

## What good looks like

- The problem is stated as a difficulty a person experiences, with no technology in the sentence.
- Known and assumed are separated on the page, **and the assumed column is not empty.**
- Each condition names who would observe it and when, so that somebody other than you could tell whether it had fired.
- The decision is consistent with the boundary you wrote at [S002](/wiki/seminars/S002.md): you do not claim a decision you already told us you may only propose.
- Under challenge you can name evidence that would reverse you, and you do not reach for it defensively.

## How it goes wrong

Four failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **The confident inventory** — everything is stated as known, because the assumption column looks like weakness. *Warning sign:* your evidence column contains things nobody in the room has seen.
- **The condition that cannot fire** — the disqualifying condition survives contact with the brief because it was never capable of contacting it. *Warning sign:* you cannot describe a Tuesday on which it would occur.
- **Authority drift** — the decision is stated as though it were yours, four sessions after you wrote down that it was not. *Warning sign:* your decision and your boundary statement do not mention the same people.
- **Defence by repetition** — challenged, you restate the decision more slowly. *Warning sign:* your answer to the objection contains no new information.

## Do it

Work from [Brief A](/wiki/quarters/Q1.md). Assemble the decision from the four artefacts you already hold; do not start again.

You have one challenger. Their instructions are to take the sponsor's side, concede nothing, and make one reasonable offer that does not address your condition.

## Show it

The defence is the assessment. It is short, it is live, and it is not a presentation.

**You are not being marked on the decision.** A defended decline and a defended proceed pass identically. An undefended either does not.

## Assessment

Checked elements:

1. The problem is stated without naming a technology.
2. Beliefs are separated into observed and reported, and each reported belief names its source.
3. At least one condition is stated in a form that could be observed to occur, with a named observer and a time at which they would observe it.
4. The decision is consistent with the role boundary written at [S002](/wiki/seminars/S002.md).
5. The decision names the person or function whose agreement it requires.
6. Under challenge, the learner names at least one piece of evidence that would reverse the decision.

**Nothing else is assessed.** The decision itself is not marked, the challenger's satisfaction is not marked, and the quality of the underlying engagement idea is not marked.

## What this does not establish

That this decision is right, or that decisions made this way are better than decisions made some other way. Nothing behind this programme measures qualification against any outcome, and the practice corpus it rests on records no engagement anybody declined.

**What is being assessed is a form of reasoning, not a result.** A learner who passes here has shown that they can hold a position under pressure and say what would change it. Whether that capacity improves an engagement is untested, and the argument for it is made in the paper rather than demonstrated anywhere.

## Perishable content in this session

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| A belief you were told and a belief you observed are different objects and belong in different columns | The tooling used to hold the four artefacts, and whatever the cohort standardises on this term |
| A condition nobody else could adjudicate will not fire | The sector-specific detail in the supplied brief, which will read as dated within a few years |
| Defending a decision is not the same act as making one | The challenger script, which is calibrated to this cohort's confidence and will need recalibration |
