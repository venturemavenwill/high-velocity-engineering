# S077 · Poisoning, Extraction and Exhaustion

**Module:** [M16](/wiki/modules/M16.md) · **Quarter:** [Q8](/wiki/quarters/Q8.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** SG · IS · ME
**Whitepaper:** [WP-077](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-077.md)
**Depends on:** S043, S064, S076
**Platform anchor:** Microsoft Defender for Cloud AI threat protection and its published alert catalogue · Azure API Management token-limit policy · the Foundry AI Red Teaming Agent · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server · Foundry MCP Server · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

## What this day is for

Yesterday's adversary had a write path into the context. Today's has a write path into the corpus, a query budget, or a credit card.

The three attacks are not variants of each other. **They attack three different quantities, they arrive at three different times, and a defence against one is not a defence against another.**

- **Poisoning** changes the **function**. The adversary writes to something the system learns from or indexes, and the system's behaviour changes for inputs the adversary chose. It happens before the request that exploits it, sometimes months before, and the aggregate evaluation shows nothing because the change is conditional on a trigger nobody sampled.
- **Extraction** reads out the **answer channel**. The adversary asks questions and assembles what came back. Nothing is written, nothing is broken, and every individual request is one a legitimate user might send. The leak is the answer, and a system that answers well leaks well.
- **Exhaustion** consumes the **capacity**. No model behaviour changes at all. The adversary moves your operating point — the one [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) to [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) had the student choose and pay for — and the failure that follows is a queueing failure, a memory cliff, or a bill.

**[S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) is the apparatus for the third and the room already has it.** Capacity, quota and budget are three numbers with three owners, and an exhaustion attack is aimed at whichever of the three is weakest, which is a property of your commitments and not of your architecture.

The day's second target is the harder one and it is the module's thesis arriving on day two. **All three attacks, and the ordinary accidents that produce identical records, are indistinguishable in the evidence most systems keep.** What separates an attack from an accident is not in the trace. It is provenance and population, and both are recording decisions someone had to make before anything happened.

**One of the three has a name in a vendor's alert catalogue and the other two mostly do not**, which is itself worth an hour. Defender for Cloud publishes named detections for AI resources, and two of them are for the **wallet attack** — driving cost rather than downtime, which is exhaustion under a name usage-based billing invented. The catalogue also carries indirect injection, jailbreak attempts blocked and jailbreak attempts detected-and-not-blocked, anomalous tool invocation and reconnaissance. **It publishes a name and a severity for each and a detection rate for none**, which is the fact point 3 turns into an obligation.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Four incidents are supplied from the substrate's own history and its neighbours', **stated only as observed symptoms** — what the operators saw, in the order they saw it, with no attribution. Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. **For each incident, say what the adversary did.**
2. **For each, name the signal that would have shown it, and say whether your own component emits that signal.**
3. **Which of the four would your [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) detection design have caught?** Answer per incident.
4. **Predict: which of the four is cheapest for the adversary?**
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to *tell me whether my component is being attacked, from the evidence it has.* Write down what it will produce and **name the one thing it will get wrong.** Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 1 is phrased as it is on purpose, and phase 8 reads it back: **it assumes an adversary, and one of the four incidents does not have one.** Not a single student is expected to challenge the framing of the item, and the framing is the day's opening finding about the room rather than about the incidents.

Item 2 is administered before instruction because the same question is asked again at step 3 after phase 4 has supplied the answer, and the design needs both readings rather than the second alone.

**Item 5 is item 1's smuggled premise handed to a second respondent.** The prompt contains the same assumption the item does, and the design's expectation is that the agent will answer it — confidently, with a structure, and without objecting that the evidence cannot decide. A student who predicts that is predicting the day's payload from the agent's behaviour rather than from their own.

**Warrant.** Pretesting on symptom records with the attribution withheld, so that the day's central discrimination is attempted cold. Item 3 joins the module to [M14](/wiki/modules/M14.md)'s detection work at the moment the student still believes their design covers the case, and its answer is the entry point for point 6. Item 1's smuggled premise is the pretest's payload and is the only item in the programme whose finding is that nobody objected to the question. **Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 per instrumentation rule 8** and is the day's contribution to the frontier map; it is constructed so that the agent's failure and the room's failure are the same failure, which is the cheapest available demonstration that a jagged frontier is not a property of the tool alone.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

Groups receive **four observation records from one substrate component**, each covering a different period. Each record contains what the component actually keeps: request logs at the resolution they are stored, latency and cost series, evaluation scores over time, and the deploy log.

**The four are selected against whichever write-path class this cohort most systematically failed to mark at [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) phase 1 item 1** — if the room missed tool outputs, the poisoning trace runs through a tool-populated index; if the room missed the orchestration summary, the extraction trace runs through it.

- **T1** — the index is poisoned through a user-contributed document. Evaluation scores are flat. One query class, not in the evaluation set, returns a document that was not previously retrievable.
- **T2** — extraction by querying. Several thousand individually ordinary requests, from a small number of principals, whose *joint* coverage of the embedding space is not ordinary at all.
- **T3** — exhaustion. A request shape chosen to maximise cost per unit of the adversary's own spend, arriving below every rate threshold the component has.
- **T4** — **the payload.** A record that looks exactly like T2 at the resolution supplied. It is a retry loop in an upstream client, released three days before the period began, written by a competent engineer, and there is no adversary anywhere in it.

Groups classify all four and commit in writing. **Then the attributions are supplied, T4's last.**

**Two of the four are then produced rather than read**, on the group's own disposable copy from [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) — its own index, its own deployment, no real users, inside an announced window.

- **T1 is executed.** The group writes one document into the copy's index, reruns the evaluation set the student has maintained since [S049](/wiki/seminars/S049.md), and reads the scores. **They do not move.** Then they issue the trigger query and read what comes back. This is the day's cheapest and most important demonstration: point 2 is not that poisoning is possible but that **the evidence the room trusts most stays flat while it happens**, and no amount of stating that produces the effect of watching it.
- **T3 is executed.** The group issues the T3 request shape against the copy behind an API Management token-limit policy of their own setting, and reads two things: what the gateway counted, and what the bill would have been. The gateway's own documentation is explicit that concurrent requests can temporarily exceed a configured token limit and that a streamed request's prompt tokens are always estimated — **so the meter that enforces the budget is itself approximate, and the size of the approximation is not published.**

**T2 is not executed and the day says so.** An extraction campaign is the attack; there is no version of it that is a demonstration. It is **demonstrated rather than operated** — the group is shown the query population that produced the T2 record and the aggregate it implies — **and it is not assessed.**

Two tasks after, both required:

1. **For each record, state what additional evidence would separate an attack from an accident** — not what would confirm your classification, but what would decide between the two.
2. **For each piece of evidence you named, say whether your own component records it today.**

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across attack mechanisms with the substrate's own retained evidence as adjudicator, with the case selection instrumented to the previous day's measured blind spot — this day's discharge of [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md)'s rule-6 obligation — and **with two of the four moved from reading to execution.** The T1 execution is the change that matters: [WP-077](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-077.md) §8 objection 1 conceded that two of the three attacks could not be exercised and declined the poisoning demonstration on the ground that it would take phase 5's slot. **Moving it into phase 3 costs the phase eight minutes and takes nothing from phase 5**, and the concession accordingly narrows rather than disappears: it now bites on extraction alone.

**T4 is the module's thesis and it is placed on day two deliberately.** It defeats the room's expectation that the three attacks are the categories, and it defeats it by supplying a record that is a correct match for one of them and a wrong answer. The finding is not that students are gullible. It is that the record does not contain the distinguishing quantity, so the classification was never available — and task 2 makes each group say, about their own system, whether it would be available tomorrow.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **Three attacks, three quantities.** Poisoning attacks the **function**; extraction attacks the **answer channel**; exhaustion attacks the **capacity**. Naming the quantity is the move, because the quantity determines where a defence can sit and when it has to be there. **This list omits three things and the omissions matter.** It omits evasion at inference, which is [S044](/wiki/seminars/S044.md)'s first family and [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md)'s whole subject. It omits compromise of the supply chain below the model — weights, dependencies, credentials — which is SG302's and which is the more common cause of real incidents. And it omits attacks on the people, which no day in this programme covers and which [S082](/wiki/seminars/S082.md) touches only from the organisational side. **Safe as a decomposition of what an adversary can do to a learned component you operate; unsafe as a threat model for a deployment, which must cover all three omissions and of which this is one part.**

2. **Poisoning.** Mechanism: a write path into the training set or the index, plus capacity. [S043](/wiki/seminars/S043.md)'s account applies unchanged — a model with capacity to spare will fit a small conditional behaviour at negligible aggregate cost, which is why the evaluation cannot see it. Timing: before the exploiting request, and for a continuously updated index, continuously. **Defence sits on the write path and in the data lifecycle — admission control, provenance, and the ability to say what a document was doing in your corpus — and not at inference time**, because at inference time the poisoned artefact is behaving correctly.

3. **Extraction.** Mechanism: the answer channel. Two shapes: reconstructing the *behaviour* (what the system does) and reconstructing the *content* (what it was trained on or what it retrieved). **The defence is a property of a population of queries, not of a request.** Budgets per principal, output granularity, and refusing to answer at the resolution that leaks. A per-request filter cannot see extraction, because no individual request in T2 is anomalous — which is the finding phase 3 supplied and which is why item 2's answers will not have named the right signal.

4. **Exhaustion.** Mechanism: nothing about the model changes; the operating point moves. [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) to [S063](/wiki/seminars/S063.md) supply the physics and [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) supplies the targets. **The attack lands on whichever of capacity, quota and budget is weakest, and which one that is, is a property of your commitments rather than of your system.** A student on a reserved rate is attacked in latency; a student on pay-per-use is attacked in money; a student behind a shared quota is attacked in somebody else's availability. Defence is admission control, per-principal limits and a degradation path — the substrate's own hard ceiling and cheaper serving path are the worked example and the students have known about them since Q1.

5. **The three defences do not compose into one control.** A gateway that inspects and filters inputs stops none of the three: poisoning entered through an authorised write months earlier, extraction is a population property, and exhaustion's requests are individually valid. **Safe to say that each attack has an available defence; unsafe to say that a system with a gateway, a rate limit and a filter is defended, because those three controls cover one and a half of the three quantities and the half is the part of exhaustion that is naive.**

6. **Only exhaustion is visible in the signals you already have.** Poisoning and extraction are invisible to every technique in [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s repertoire, because both look like the system working — which is the answer to item 3 and it is *no* for two of the four. This is [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) point 7's user-visible-only class widening: there is now a second class of failure that produces no anomaly, and unlike the first it has nobody to report it, because the affected party is the one causing it.

7. **The adversary and the accident produce the same record.** Phase 3's T4. **What separates them is provenance and population**, and both are recording decisions made before the incident: where did this content come from and who put it there; and what does this principal's request distribution look like against the rest. Neither has operational value on the day it is implemented, which is why neither is implemented. **A system that cannot distinguish an attack from an accident is not badly instrumented; it is instrumented for operations, which is what it was asked for.**

8. **Cost asymmetry, and it is yours to state.** For each of the three, the ratio between what the attack costs the adversary and what it costs you is a number you can estimate for your own system, and it is the number that decides what to defend first. **It is a property of your system and not a general fact**, and a student who repeats a general ranking of these three attacks has said nothing about their component. Step 5 makes the exhaustion ratio arithmetic, using the student's own cost model, because that is the one of the three where the student has been billed.

9. **What one vendor detects, what it names it, and what it will not tell you. Marked perishable; the obligation is not.** Defender for Cloud's AI threat protection publishes a catalogue of named alerts for AI resources. Four properties of that catalogue are the day's platform content and only the last is durable.

   - **Exhaustion has a name and it is the wallet attack** — the catalogue carries two, one keyed on high volumes of *identical* requests and one on volumes *inconsistent with historical usage*. That is point 4 in a product: the second detector is tuned on your own past traffic, which is a distribution the adversary is not obliged to draw from, and [S079](/wiki/seminars/S079.md) is where that becomes the module's operational problem.
   - **The catalogue's severities are a decision somebody made for you.** Indirect injection through hidden characters is High. **Anomalous tool invocation — an agent calling a tool in a way that deviates from expected behaviour — is Low.** Whether that ranking is right for a component that can spend money is not the vendor's question to answer, and it is exactly the acceptance decision step 6 asks for.
   - **Poisoning and extraction are thin in it, and the thinness is informative rather than accidental.** The catalogue detects things visible at the resource: prompts, requests, responses, identities, volumes. Poisoning happened months earlier in a data lifecycle the resource cannot see, and extraction is a property of a set the resource does not assemble. **Point 6 predicted this before the catalogue was opened**, and the catalogue is its confirmation from an independent party.
   - **No detection rate and no false-positive rate is published for any alert in it.** Nor for the content filters underneath, nor for the posture assessments beside it. **This is the day's most important platform fact and it is a silence.** The consequence is exact and is the obligation: *an alert that did not fire licenses no conclusion*, because you cannot state a miss rate you were never given. A student who writes "no alerts fired, therefore no attack" has made a claim their evidence cannot support, and step 3 is where the artefact has to say so.

   **Safe** as a reason to know what your detection stack can see and where it is looking. **Unsafe** as coverage: a named alert is a named alert, and the three quantities of point 1 remain the frame, because a vendor's catalogue is an enumeration of what one product observes and not an enumeration of what can be done to you.

   **And the honest alternative is stated rather than implied.** This day does not prefer the silence. **If a detection rate were published, it would be taught here, students would be required to check it against their own traffic, and the day would be better for it.** What is being taught is not that unpublished is good; it is that unpublished is what you have, and an engineer who cannot say so will write next week's incident report concluding that nothing happened because nothing fired.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3. Point 1 carries the enumeration scope statement and points 1 and 5 carry it in the strong form. Point 6 answers item 3 directly and is the point that connects the module to [M14](/wiki/modules/M14.md). **Point 7 is the day's payload and it is generated by phase 3 rather than asserted here**, which is the arrangement the pending instrumentation rule proposed at [WP-064](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-064.md) §8 would require and which this design applies voluntarily while the rule remains pending. **Point 9 is the day's platform-specific content**, is marked perishable, names both a default chosen for the student and a documented silence, and carries its own strong-form scope statement, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md). It is placed last because its third bullet only lands once point 6 has been stated and believed.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 6. The handover is an exposure statement and the receiver is the staff member accountable for the substrate's serving path. This phase produces a build.**

Students use **their own component**, their own [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) cost model and [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) commitment, **their disposable copy**, and **Copilot in agent mode through the Azure and Foundry MCP Servers**.

1. **For your component, name the write path to its function, the answer channel, and the finite resource.** If one of the three does not exist for your component, say so and say why — a component with no learned or indexed state has no poisoning surface and saying so is a result, not an omission.
2. **For each of the three, state the cheapest attack and what it would cost the adversary**, with the provenance of the figure and your confidence in it **in the same field**, per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) specification rule 2. Where the honest provenance is *estimated from no data*, write that.
3. **For each of the three, state what your system records today that would distinguish that attack from an accident with the same signature. Where nothing does, say so.** This list is an output, not an omission. **Where your answer rests on a detection you did not build — a platform alert, a content filter, a posture assessment — state what its published accuracy is.** For every such control in this stack the answer is that none is published, and the entry reads *this would be distinguished by a control whose miss rate I cannot state.*
4. **Choose the quantity you judge most exposed and ship one control to your copy.** State which of the three quantities it protects and — required — **state explicitly which of the other two it does not.** Measure the control doing something: the requests it refused, the tokens it counted, the documents it declined to index. **A control that was configured and never observed acting is not a shipped control.**
5. **Compute the exhaustion position.** Using your own [S061](/wiki/seminars/S061.md) cost model and your [S064](/wiki/seminars/S064.md) commitment: what could one adversary spending a stated amount cost the substrate in one hour, and **which of capacity, quota and budget does it reach first?** Carry the forecast's confidence in the same field as the forecast.
6. **State the acceptance:** which attacks you are not defending, with the expected cost of each and **an owner, or an explicit statement that there is none**, in [S069](/wiki/seminars/S069.md) point 8's form.
7. **Present the exposure statement to the staff member accountable for the serving path**, who is present for the last thirty minutes and is not obliged to accept it. **Do not ask whether it is acceptable. Ask them to narrate what they would do on receiving it, and to say where they would stop. Record the narration.**

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md): the **prompt record**, the **intervention record** — **empty fails**, unless the artefact shows the design could have caught an intervention-worthy error, and a step 4 control with its own assertion on what it counted is the accepted example — and the **frontier statement**.

**Fading:** step 2's cost-estimation form is supplied and worked with the room, since the estimation is unfamiliar and its failure mode is systematic. **Step 3 is unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 6 producing a handover to a named receiver present in the room, **and one shipped control rather than a described one**. **Step 3 is the day's discriminator** — a student who can name a control but not the evidence that would tell them it was needed has produced a posture — and it is unsupported because the enumeration is the judgement. **Step 3's published-accuracy clause is the artefact half of point 9's silence**, and it is written so that the honest answer is available and costs one sentence, because a requirement whose honest answer is impossible to write will be answered dishonestly.

**Step 4's measurement requirement is the practical turn doing work rather than decorating.** A control that exists in a template and has never refused anything is the commonest security artefact in industry and is indistinguishable, on paper, from one that works. Requiring an observation of the control acting costs ten minutes and removes the ambiguity entirely.

**Step 7 uses an external observer per instrumentation rule 1** and uses the narrate-rather-than-approve form, which instrumentation rule 5 requires be reused wherever the same problem recurs: an accountable party asked whether a statement is acceptable will accept it, because accepting costs nothing until it matters. The form was established at [S038](/wiki/seminars/S038.md) and reused at [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md) step 5 and [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) step 8. Per rule 5 as amended at [WP-054](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-054.md) §8, **all of those reuses are contingent** on the proposing days' reported outcomes, and a single reported failure retracts every one of them including this.

Step 5's arithmetic exists because it is the only place in the module where an adversarial quantity meets a number the student has been billed against, and it is what [Q7](/wiki/quarters/Q7.md) named as this day's entry condition.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate cost anomaly that was attributed to an attack for six days. It was escalated, a rate limit was deployed, an incident channel ran, and the cause was a retry loop in the cohort's own client. Attention is directed at two facts — **the attribution was made from evidence that supported either explanation equally**, and **the six days were spent building the wrong defence, which is still deployed.**

Then the agent case: a Copilot-produced abuse-detection change that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. Asked to detect abuse of a retrieval component, it added a per-principal request-rate limit, an alert on cost, and a daily job flagging principals in the top percentile of request volume, each correctly implemented and each citing a real documented mechanism. It reads as a complete answer, and **every one of the three counts requests.** It would have caught T3, which nobody needed help with; it would not have caught T2, whose principals were individually unremarkable and whose signature was the joint coverage of a space nothing in the design computed. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the reviewer checked that each control was correct and did not ask which of the three quantities the set of them covered**, which is point 5's non-composition claim performed rather than stated.

Then students, in threes: **a time you attributed something to malice and were wrong, or attributed something to accident and were wrong.** Either direction.

**Warrant.** Error management targeting point 7, with an instructor case in which no individual judgement was unreasonable: the symptoms matched, the escalation was correct given the hypothesis, and the hypothesis was one the evidence permitted. The two-directional student prompt is deliberate — a room five days into adversarial framing will produce only the first direction unless the second is named, and the second is the more common error before this module and the first is the more common error after it. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](/wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition** and is chosen so that it fails on composition rather than on correctness, because composition is the failure a code review is structurally unable to see.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs. Each student presents **their step 4 control with its stated non-coverage, and their step 3 recording gaps.**

The partner has two tasks and both are required:

- **Construct an attack the control does not stop**, using the non-coverage the student has already written.
- **State whether the student's own records would distinguish that attack from an accident** — reading the student's step 3 list back to them.

A partner who can construct the attack and finds it already covered by the student's acceptance list at step 6 has produced a result, and it is recorded as one.

Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) specification rule 3, the outcome is recorded in the artefact, including a pair that could construct nothing, which is a result about the control rather than an absence of one.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation whose second demand attacks the record rather than the system, so that the phase exercises point 7 rather than point 1. Using the student's own stated non-coverage as the partner's starting material is deliberate: it makes the honesty of step 4 immediately consequential, which is the only reliable way to make a self-declared limitation truthful.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in SG302 with IS302. Per [Assessment Architecture](/wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the three quantities named for the student's component, with an explicit absence statement where one does not exist; the cheapest attack for each with its cost to the adversary **carrying provenance and confidence in the same field** per specification rule 2; the recording-gap list from step 3, **with the published accuracy of any control it relies on, or an explicit statement that none is published**; one shipped control with the quantity it protects, the quantities it explicitly does not, and **the observation of it acting**; the exhaustion computation against the student's own cost model and commitment, with the reached constraint named and the forecast's confidence in the same field; the acceptance list with an owner or an explicit none for each entry; the recorded accountable-owner narration; **the statement of which SG302 material was used for the supply-chain omission named at point 1**; the phase 7 outcome; **the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed** — not whether the control is good, not whether the cost estimates are right, and not the extraction material, which is demonstrated rather than operated.

**A control stated without its non-coverage fails.** This is the day's single hard requirement, and it fails because a control whose limits are unstated will be read as covering the quantity it was placed near.

**An empty recording-gap list fails.** A student who claims their system can distinguish every attack from every accident with the same signature has either not enumerated or not checked, and both are the same defect. This is [S068](/wiki/seminars/S068.md)'s empty-not-detected-list rule applied to evidence rather than to signals, and it is applied here because instrumentation rule 5 requires a remedy to be reused wherever the same problem recurs.

**A recording-gap entry that credits a platform detection without stating that its accuracy is unpublished fails.** The failure is not scepticism about the product; it is that a distinction attributed to a control with an unknown miss rate is not a distinction the student can stand behind, and this is a class-6 artefact.

**A cost figure without provenance and confidence in the same field fails.**

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**The cross-course join is a checked element.** Point 1 defers supply-chain compromise to SG302 and the artefact requires the student to name the material they used, per the [Whitepaper Standard](/wiki/program/05-Whitepaper-Standard.md)'s cross-course deferral rule.

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S080](/wiki/seminars/S080.md).

**Rule-6 discharge.** One obligation lands on this day and **the element exists.** [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) named this day's phase 3 as the response occasion for its item 1 exposure-estimate error, specifying that the four traces be selected against whichever write-path class the cohort most systematically failed to mark. **Phase 3's case selection is a function of the [S076](/wiki/seminars/S076.md) result**: the poisoning and extraction traces are routed through the missed path class, so that the day's attack material is aimed at the measured blind spot rather than at the path the room already watches. It is not a mention; it changes which four records are printed. The obligation is discharged.

## Perishable content in this day

**Security content is the most perishable material in this programme.** The three quantities are durable; every specific attack against them is not, and every vendor's catalogue of them is less durable still. The day is built so that the specification checks the first and never the second.

The durable column is written as reasoning and obligation rather than as facts about named attacks, because a claim of the form *this attack exists* expires with the attack and a claim of the form *this is what you must be able to say about it* does not.

| Durable claim — reasoning and obligation | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| Three quantities — function, answer channel, capacity — and a defence for one is not a defence for another | Which of the three the substrate's current gateway and filters touch |
| Poisoning is a capacity-plus-write-path property and is invisible to aggregate evaluation | The group's own T1 write, and the evaluation set it did not move |
| Extraction is a property of a population and is invisible to per-request inspection | The absence of an extraction detector in any product on this stack |
| Exhaustion lands on whichever of capacity, quota and budget is weakest, which is a property of your commitments | The two published wallet-attack alerts, one keyed on identical requests and one on historical usage |
| Three defences do not compose into one control | A gateway policy, a content filter and a posture assessment, side by side |
| The attack record and the accident record are the same record; what separates them is a recording decision taken in advance | The resolution of the substrate's current request log |
| Cost asymmetry is a property of your own system | The prices behind step 5's arithmetic |
| **Obligation:** state what a control's silence licenses, and if its accuracy is unpublished, say that | The Defender alert catalogue: names, severities, no rate for any of them |
| **Obligation:** a meter that enforces a budget is itself approximate; say by how much or say you cannot | Token-limit estimation under streaming and concurrency |

- **Perishable:** every figure in the four traces, the substrate's current rate limits and quota structure, the cost per request, the prices behind step 5's arithmetic, every alert name and severity in the vendor catalogue, and any specific poisoning or extraction technique named in the room. All are properties of one system and one vendor at one delivery, and the [Durable and Perishable Register](/wiki/program/09-Durable-and-Perishable-Register.md)'s entry for adversarial technique catalogues covers the last two explicitly.
- **Local:** the four observation records, the four phase 1 incidents, the six-day misattributed cost anomaly, the substrate's serving path and its ceiling, the disposable copies used at phase 3 and step 4, and the accountable staff member present at step 7.

## What to take from this day

**The claim to hold.** **Three attacks, three quantities.** Poisoning changes the function, extraction reads out the answer channel, exhaustion consumes the capacity — and a defence against one is not a defence against another. **The three do not compose into a single control.** An attack and an accident produce the same record: what separates them is provenance and population, both recording decisions somebody had to take before anything happened.

**What the sources license, and what they do not.** The aggregate-hides-conditional-behaviour argument behind poisoning's invisibility follows [research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md](/research/03-measurement-evaluation/husain-your-ai-product-needs-evals.md), cited for **direction and mechanism only**, and **no magnitude is asserted for any of the three attacks.** The posture material at [research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md](/research/06-microsoft-platform/purview-dspm-for-ai-and-defender-ai-spm-docs.md) records that its Defender half was read through search excerpts rather than a full page fetch, and **nothing is published about how often any alert in that catalogue is right or wrong.**

**Guidance for your own work.**

- **Name the three quantities for your own component**, and where one does not exist, say so and why.
- **State a control's non-coverage beside it.** A control is otherwise read as covering the quantity it happens to sit near.
- **Observe your control acting** — requests refused, tokens counted, documents declined. One that has never acted is indistinguishable on paper from one that works.
- **A configured control is not evidence**, per the [Agent Configuration Standard](/wiki/program/13-Agent-Configuration-Standard.md). An alert that did not fire licenses no conclusion while its miss rate is unpublished.

**Where this is easy to get wrong.** Writing *no alerts fired, therefore no attack.* The mirror error usually arrives first: days spent building the wrong defence from evidence that supported either explanation equally.
