# S081 · Maturity as an Evidenced Level

**Module:** [M17](/wiki/modules/M17.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-081](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-081.md)
**Retrieves from:** S055, S062, S068, S070
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

A level has been recorded for the engagement you just finished. It sits across several dimensions, it is ordinal, it looks like a measurement, and it was entered from memory by somebody on your own side on the day the work closed.

Nobody has re-derived it. The instrument does not ask anybody to.

Answer these in writing before the session opens.

1. For each dimension of the recorded level: what artefact was it scored against, and who wrote that artefact?
2. Which dimensions were scored against something the delivery team produced, and which against something the customer's estate contains?
3. Take one dimension where the level is high. What would you have had to do to score it that high without changing anything about the system?
4. If the person who entered the level left tomorrow, who could reproduce it, and from what?
5. What is the level *for* — who reads it, and what do they do differently because of it?

**Item 3 is the one the session is built on.** A rubric whose checks can be satisfied by naming a technology nobody deployed is a rubric that measures a document. That is not a hypothetical: the closest thing to a worked assessment instrument in the practice corpus behind this programme scores a system by counting properties in a table that an earlier stage of the same process filled in, and its own evaluator's definition of a specific mitigation is that it names a concrete technology.

## What you must produce

A **re-derivation procedure** for the recorded level: for each dimension, the artefact a stranger would inspect, the observation they would make, and the rule that turns the observation into a position on the scale.

Plus the part that matters more: **the dimensions your own procedure cannot reach**, each with the reason it cannot — no artefact exists, the artefact is one your team wrote, the evidence lives somewhere the procedure cannot see, or the dimension is not about the system at all.

## What good looks like

- Every dimension's evidence is something a person outside the delivery organisation could reach without asking your team a question.
- **The procedure distinguishes what the estate contains from what the plan says it contains.** A control named in a document and deployed nowhere scores nothing.
- At least one dimension is marked unreachable, with the reason, rather than approximated. **A procedure that reaches every dimension has stopped being a procedure and started being an argument.**
- Where your re-derivation produces a lower position than the recorded one, that is stated plainly and early, not as a caveat at the end.
- The scale's own construction is treated as a claim: you say where the boundaries between positions came from, and if the answer is that nobody knows, you say that.
- The procedure names who would run it and when — because a re-derivation nobody is scheduled to perform is a description of one.

## How it goes wrong

Five failure modes, each with the warning sign that precedes it.

- **The document graded as the system** — the dimension is scored from a plan, an assessment or a design record, and the estate is never inspected. *Warning sign:* your evidence for a dimension is a file your own team authored.
- **The self-filled table** — an earlier step in your own process supplied the value, and a later step counted it as evidence. *Warning sign:* you cannot say who first asserted the value you are now scoring.
- **The rubric learned** — you know what the instrument rewards, and the level rises without the system changing. *Warning sign:* you found yourself editing prose to move a dimension.
- **The precise composite** — dimensions are combined into a single position, and the arithmetic is exact while every input is a judgement. *Warning sign:* your output has more resolution than any of its inputs.
- **The level with no reader** — a position is recorded, filed, and used by nobody for anything. *Warning sign:* you cannot name a decision that would go differently at a different level.

## Do it

Work from the supplied situation in [Q9](/wiki/quarters/Q9.md). Take the recorded level and the artefacts listed there, and go dimension by dimension.

Two dimensions were scored against documents the delivery team wrote. One was scored against a control that is named in a plan and deployed nowhere. **Find them before you read this sentence again**, and then decide what your procedure does about them — because deleting a dimension is a decision and downgrading it is a different decision.

Use an agent as a hostile re-deriver rather than as an assistant. Give it the artefacts and the scale and nothing else, ask it to reach a position on each dimension, and record where it reached a different one and why. **An agent that reproduces your level from your summary has re-derived your summary.**

## Show it

State one dimension and one sentence: *this is the level, and this is what somebody who does not trust me would inspect to reach it.*

Then state the dimension you could not reach, and what you did instead. The room is listening for whether the second statement is as specific as the first. **A vague account of a limitation is how a claim survives contact with a procedure that should have killed it.**

## What this does not establish

**That any maturity instrument measures anything.** No calibration of any such instrument against human judgement exists anywhere in the material behind this programme — no gold standard, no held-out sample, no inter-rater statistic, no record of two assessors ever disagreeing, and no statement that any produced position was ever compared with an expert's reading of the same system. Where two assessing components exist in the same process, they produce positions on different scales and never reference each other.

**And the scales themselves are asserted.** Weights, category allocations, band boundaries and partial-credit rules appear as constants with no derivation, in outputs that present them with the visual language of measurement. A position with a name and a colour is read as an observation and is an assertion.

So this session teaches a procedure and claims nothing for the instrument the procedure is applied to. **A re-derivable level is a level whose provenance you can inspect. It is not a level that is true.** No source behind this programme reports that a system at one position behaved differently from a system at another, because no source behind this programme reports how any system behaved at all.
