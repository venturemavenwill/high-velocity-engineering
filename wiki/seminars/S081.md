# S081 · The Organisation Is Part of the System

**Module:** [M17](wiki/modules/M17.md) · **Quarter:** [Q9](wiki/quarters/Q9.md) · **Complexity class:** 6
**Strands:** PE · all
**Whitepaper:** [WP-081](wiki/whitepapers/WP-081.md)
**Retrieves from:** S036, S037, S069
**Platform anchor:** the substrate's own estate as the platform describes it — Azure resource inventory through the Azure MCP Server, and Purview's default data risk assessment over the SharePoint estate · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (read) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

Two years ago, at [S036](wiki/seminars/S036.md) and [S037](wiki/seminars/S037.md), the cohort learned that a request is the output of somebody's analysis and that what people say they do diverges from what they do. Both lessons were about a person.

Since then the cohort has built a service, shipped it, been billed for it, gated it, monitored it, governed it and defended it after an incident. It is used by a department. **Nobody in this room can name the people the service depends on.**

That is not a gap in the documentation. It is a consequence of the boundary the students have been drawing since [S007](wiki/seminars/S007.md), and the boundary is correct engineering. Draw it at the interface, and the system becomes tractable, testable, ownable and shippable. Draw it at the interface, and one entire class of failure becomes invisible: **the class in which the deployment succeeds on every measure its team defined, is used exactly as designed, and the organisation's cost goes up.**

The outcome the organisation is buying is produced jointly by the software and by work the software does not do. Somebody keeps a record. Somebody decides which cases reach the service at all. Somebody decides whether its output is acted on. All three have names, none of them is in the repository, and each of them can be on leave.

The target: **the organisation is not a constraint on your deployment. It is a component of it, it has failure modes, and where you draw the boundary is a decision you make that determines which of those failures are yours.**

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items, **sealed and opened at phase 8.**

1. The substrate's deployed capability is named, with the outcome the institution is buying. **List the components of the system that produces that outcome.**
2. **Name one person — a person, not a role — who does something every week without which that outcome would not occur.**
3. A deployment is described: it met every target its team set, was used as designed, and the organisation's total cost of the process rose. **How?**
4. **Predict: of the components you listed at item 1, how many are people?**
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly give the agent the same instruction as item 1, with read access to the substrate's Azure estate through the Azure MCP Server and to its Purview assessment results. **Write down what it will produce, and name the one thing it will not be able to tell you.**

Item 1 is constructed so that a complete, competent, entirely software answer is available and is the expected one. Item 4 makes the omission numeric, so that phase 8 can report a gap rather than an impression. **Item 5 is this day's contribution to the running frontier map, and the design's expectation is unusual: that the agent will answer item 1 better than any student in the room and will be no help at all with the day.** Predicting that the agent will produce a good inventory is easy; naming what a resource inventory structurally cannot hold is the measure.

**Warrant.** Pretesting the day's central omission before any instruction, with a plausible-and-wrong answer space that is not merely plausible but professionally correct under the boundary the cohort has been taught. Item 3 has no available answer at this point in the day and is scored only for whether an answer was attempted; it is [S026](wiki/seminars/S026.md)'s construct question at organisational scale and it is the item phase 3 exists to make answerable. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 and may not migrate into phase 5, per instrumentation rule 8: an instrument administered after its answer has been taught measures recall.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative retrieval set (20 min)

Free recall, no notes.

- [S036](wiki/seminars/S036.md): the four things that shape a request before it reaches you, and what the request is evidence *of*.
- [S037](wiki/seminars/S037.md): the three mechanisms of say-do divergence, and the three columns of the record.
- [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md): the four parts of a release decision, and the three escalation conditions.
- **Bridge:** [S037](wiki/seminars/S037.md) established that what a person says they do and what they do diverge, and that the divergence is not deception. **Your service is used by a department. What is the department's *said*, and what is its *done*?**

The room answers, and does so in one exchange. The *said* is the process document, the org chart and the service description. The *done* is the rota, the workaround, the spreadsheet somebody maintains, and who actually gets asked. **And the room reaches the second half without prompting: the divergence is larger at department scale, not smaller, because no single person holds the whole *done* and there is therefore nobody who could report it accurately even if asked.**

**Warrant.** Expanding-interval retrieval at a two-year gap, with a bridge that promotes an individual-scale finding to organisational scale and thereby produces the day's premise before the day states it. The bridge is answered by the room; the instructor's only contribution is to write both columns' headings.

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (45 min)

**The machine's answer first, executed (5 min).** Before anything is supplied, every group issues one prompt through the Azure MCP Server: *list the components of the system that produces this outcome.* The agent returns the substrate's resource inventory — deployments, indexes, gateway APIs, storage, the identities that hold them — and, where the group asks for it, the sites Purview's default assessment covered this week. It is fast, it is accurate, it is more complete than anything the room wrote at item 1, **and it is the fifth object on the table for the rest of the phase.** Groups keep it in front of them.

Then groups receive **four deployments of the same component into four organisations**, with the code identical in all four, the evaluation identical in all four, and the outcome different in all four. Each comes with a one-page account of how the work was done in that organisation before and after.

**These four are demonstrated rather than operated**, per the [Seminar Day Design Pattern](wiki/program/04-Seminar-Day-Design-Pattern.md): they are historical records of deployments into other organisations and there is no service against which they could be run. **They are not assessed**, and the specification checks only the artefacts produced from the substrate.

**Sampling is disclosed**, per instrumentation rule 3: the four are drawn from the substrate's own deployment history and from two partner institutions, selected because their outcomes differ while their technical artefacts do not, and the selection was made by staff who knew the outcomes. Students are told this before they begin, in the same terms the programme would demand of them.

- **O1** — adopted, used as designed, outcome as predicted. The control, and it is genuinely a control: nothing was hidden and nothing went wrong.
- **O2** — adopted, and the benefit came from somewhere else. The users kept a parallel manual record they had always kept, and the improvement was in the manual record's accuracy, not in the model's output. Withdraw the component and most of the benefit remains.
- **O3** — rejected. Technically the strongest deployment of the four. The team that would have used it did not own the problem it solved; the team that owned the problem was never asked, because it reported to a different director.
- **O4** — **the payload.** Adopted, used exactly as designed, produced the predicted improvement on the metric the deploying team defined — **and the organisation's total cost of the process rose.** The time saved in one team reappeared as new work in another team, doing reconciliation that the previous manual step had made unnecessary. Every claim in the deployment report is true.

Groups rank the four by success and commit. **Then the organisational accounts and the cost outcomes are supplied.**

The payload is **O4**, and it defeats the room's expectation directly. The room expects a deployment to fail by being rejected (O3) or misused (O2). O4 is a clean success by every criterion the cohort has been taught to apply, and it is a net loss — because **the unit of account is the organisation and the deploying team's unit was the component.**

Task after, in this order and all three recorded:

1. **For each deployment, name the parts of the delivered outcome that are not software, and say who maintains each.** No categories are supplied. Groups produce their own.
2. Then: for O4, name the measurement that would have detected it, and say who would have had to take it.
3. Then: **look at the machine inventory again. Mark every row on it that is a person.** Groups report the count, which is nearly always zero, and say whether that is a defect in the tool.

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases holding the software and the evaluation identical and varying the organisation, which is the only construction that makes the organisation the discriminating dimension rather than a confound. O4 is the payload because it is the case the cohort's entire training makes invisible, and O3 is placed third because its lesson is about routing rather than about accounting and would otherwise dominate. **Task-after item 1 is deliberately unprompted and is taken before phase 4 supplies any vocabulary**; [WP-081](wiki/whitepapers/WP-081.md) §8 explains why the reading that feeds [S084](wiki/seminars/S084.md) is this one and not phase 5's. **Task-after item 3 is the executed element's payload and is deliberately a question the design believes has the answer *no*:** a resource graph is a graph of resources, the tool is not failing, and a student who calls it a defect has not yet located the boundary the day is about.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (50 min)

1. **The deployed system is the software plus the work around it.** Name the non-software parts explicitly: the record somebody keeps, the check somebody performs, the decision somebody makes about what to send it, the escalation somebody raises, the tolerance somebody has for being told bad news. Groups produced most of this list at phase 3; this point names it.

2. **Every one of those parts has a maintainer, and none of them is documented.** The org chart is the organisation's *said*. The set of people who actually keep the outcome happening is its *done*, and [S037](wiki/seminars/S037.md)'s finding applies without modification: nobody is concealing it, and nobody can report it, because no single person holds it.

3. **Three ways an organisation carries a system.** *Absorbing* — people do work the system does not do, including the work of correcting it. *Routing* — people decide what reaches the system and what does not. *Licensing* — people decide whether its output is acted on. Each has a maintainer, each fails differently, and each is invisible from inside the software. **This enumeration omits two carrying modes this day does not handle: sustaining, where the organisation supplies the training, hiring and succession that keeps the other three staffed, and shielding, where somebody absorbs the political cost of the system's existence. Both are real, both are commoner in larger organisations, and both are [S082](wiki/seminars/S082.md)'s and [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)'s. Safe as a way of finding the people your deployment depends on this quarter; unsafe as a model of how a system survives across years, where sustaining is the mode that fails first and the one this frame will not show you.**

4. **The organisation is a component with failure modes**, not a constraint on the deployment. A constraint is a thing you design around. A component is a thing whose failure is your incident, and phase 3's O2, O3 and O4 are three of them: an absorbing component doing the real work, a routing component that never routed, and an absorbing component created by the deployment itself.

5. **Work that moves is not work that is removed.** O4. The deploying team measured the component's unit; the organisation pays in its own. This is [S026](wiki/seminars/S026.md)'s construct-instrument gap with a department as the construct, and the correction is arithmetic rather than subtle: state the outcome in the organisation's unit or you have not stated it.

6. **Whose account did you get.** [S037](wiki/seminars/S037.md) sampled a person; a deployment samples a department, and the sample is whoever was available. That is [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md) phase 1's finding one level up — the decision was made by the person who was available rather than the person with the evidence or the person with the authority — and it is the mechanism behind O3. **It is also this day's own sampling problem, and the disclosure at phase 3 exists because the day would otherwise be running the selection effect it teaches you to detect.**

7. **The boundary is a decision, not a fact, and it determines which failures are yours.** Drawing it at the interface is defensible, is what your organisation will expect, and makes O4 structurally invisible. Redrawing it to include the three carrying modes makes O4 visible and makes you responsible for things you cannot change. **Both positions are defensible and neither is free; what is not defensible is holding one without knowing you chose it.**

8. **What this day does not answer.** Who decides where the boundary goes when two parties disagree about it, and what happens when the party who would bear the redrawn responsibility is not the party who drew it. That is a question about authority rather than about systems, and it is [S082](wiki/seminars/S082.md)'s.

**Points 9 and 10 come after the day's open question deliberately.** They are the platform instance and not the claim, and this module puts the vehicle after the argument because the argument does not depend on it.

9. **The machine's inventory, and what its schema commits it to.** The tool you ran at phase 3 returned resources. The Azure MCP Server's quota namespace returns regions where a resource type is available and current usage against limits; its pricing namespace returns retail prices for a SKU; its monitor namespace returns logs and metrics. Purview's assessment returns sites, items, labels and sharing links. **Every one of those is a thing with an identifier, and nothing in the estate has a field for the person who does the work the estate does not do.** That is not an omission anybody made and it is not a defect: an inventory's schema is a claim about what kinds of thing exist, and this one is correct about its kinds. **The engineering consequence is exact — a handover assembled from a resource graph is complete and is missing the three components you named at phase 5**, which is [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md)'s problem four weeks early. **Perishable:** the namespaces, the tool names, the fact that quota is exposed read-only at all. **Durable:** an inventory tells you what its author thought a component was.

10. **A default the platform chose for you, with a person on the end of it.** Purview's default data risk assessment runs **weekly over the top 100 SharePoint sites by usage**. Nobody in your institution chose 100 and nobody chose *by usage*; both are defaults, and together they are a sampling frame. Item-level scanning is capped at **ten sites**, is **not supported on OneDrive**, and is bounded at **200,000 items per location** with the documentation stating that the count reported **may not be accurate above 100,000 files per location**. The first default assessment shows nothing for **four days**; a custom one settles after **48 hours** and expires after **30**. **So the instrument that tells your institution what its AI can reach has a documented blind spot, and the blind spot is the places nobody uses much** — which is exactly where the person nobody asks keeps their records. **Safe** as a way of finding your largest exposures this week, on the sites that are in use, within the caps. **Unsafe** as an inventory of what the system can reach, unsafe as evidence about any location that was never scanned, and unsafe as a basis for saying that a party's material is absent rather than unscanned. And note what the documentation does not say: it states the cadence, the caps and the latencies precisely and **states no detection rate and no false-positive rate anywhere**. That silence is the one to carry; the numbers will change and the silence has not.

**Warrant.** Consolidation indexed to phase 3's four cases. Points 1 and 3 name what groups produced unprompted at the task-after; point 5 and point 7 are the day's two claims with engineering consequences and both are reachable from O4, which is why O4 is the payload rather than an illustration. Point 3 carries the strong-form scope statement. Point 6 discharges the organisational half of [S036](wiki/seminars/S036.md) point 2's scope statement, which named this day. **Point 9 is reached from the phase 3 task-after's third item rather than asserted, and point 10 is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required naming of a default the platform chose** — both marked perishable in the same breath, and point 10 carries its own strong-form scope statement because a governance report is precisely the artefact a student will carry into work this day does not supervise.

## Phase 5 · Whole-task learning task (90 min)

**Class 6. The boundary is the student's to draw, and the consequence of drawing it is theirs.**

Students work on the substrate's own deployed capability — the service this cohort operates.

1. **Name the capability and state the outcome the institution is buying**, in the institution's words rather than the service's.
2. **Draw the boundary you have been using.** List what is inside it. This is a description of current practice, not a proposal.
3. **Identify three non-software components of the delivered outcome — one absorbing, one routing, one licensing — each with a named person who maintains it.** The name must come from observation or from asking, not from the org chart, and you must record which.
4. **For each of the three, state what happens to the outcome if that person is unavailable for two weeks.** Where you do not know, mark do-not-know.
5. **State the outcome in the organisation's unit rather than the component's, and identify one place where work moved rather than being removed.** Where you cannot establish one, mark do-not-know and say what evidence would settle it.
6. **Redraw the boundary to include the three components. State what the redrawing makes you responsible for that you were not responsible for before**, and whether you are willing to be.
7. **Name the PE301 material you applied at steps 3 and 6**, specifically, and say what it told you to do.
8. **Ship the inventory.** Thirty minutes, with Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server in read configuration. Produce a component inventory for the substrate's deployed capability that a receiving organisation could actually read: every resource with its owner tag, every consumer with its metric dimension, every index with its permission model — **and one row for each of your three named maintainers, carrying the carrying mode, the two-week consequence and the provenance of the name.** The machine writes the first part in minutes. **The three rows it cannot write are the ones the inventory exists for**, and an inventory delivered without them fails.

**Two of the named maintainers are present for the last thirty minutes** and may be asked what they actually do. They are not obliged to agree with the student's description.

**The build is bounded and the encounter is not.** Step 8 is timeboxed to thirty of the ninety minutes and runs alongside steps 1 and 2. **The maintainers' thirty minutes are timetabled first and the build is what compresses**, because the build can be finished afterwards and the person cannot be re-run. A group that arrives at the maintainers with an unfinished inventory has made the correct trade and the specification says so.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** The instructions you gave, in order, including the ones you abandoned, each with why. Not a transcript.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected the agent, and what the correction was. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error — an inventory that checks its own row count against the subscription's resource list, or that refuses to emit a maintainer row whose provenance field is empty, is the obvious example and is accepted.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch. Both specific, both from this build. **On this day the second half is frequently not a mistake but a silence**, and a statement that names the silence precisely — what the agent could not have known rather than what it got wrong — is the stronger answer.

**Fading:** step 2's boundary notation is supplied; step 5's unit conversion is worked once with the room on a supplied example. **Steps 3 and 6 are unsupported.**

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 6 on the cohort's own service, with the maintainers present, which satisfies instrumentation rule 1 — the people best placed to say whether the description of their work is accurate are in the room for their own reasons, and the observation is theirs rather than the student's or a peer's. Step 4 is the phase's discriminator: a student who has named a maintainer they cannot answer step 4 about has named a role wearing a name. Step 6 is unsupported because accepting responsibility you cannot discharge is the day's whole judgement and the point at which class 6 is actually earned. **Step 7 is the prior-course join form [WP-069](wiki/whitepapers/WP-069.md) §8 named and declined**; it is recorded as a proposed remedy in [WP-081](wiki/whitepapers/WP-081.md) §7 and its reuse at [S084](wiki/seminars/S084.md) is contingent on this day's reported outcome. **Step 8 is the day's build and it is deliberately an artefact whose value is the part the tool cannot supply**; [WP-081](wiki/whitepapers/WP-081.md) §8 faces the objection that a build on a day about people is a displacement and argues the case.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (40 min)

Instructor first: a substrate capability withdrawn — correctly — because a person left. Nothing about the software changed. The person had been performing a routing step nobody had written down, described in the handover as "answers queries about the service", and for eleven days after their departure the service produced output that was technically correct and about the wrong cases. Attention is directed at the handover document, which was complete, accurate, and did not contain the sentence that mattered because the person did not know it was a sentence.

Then the agent case: a Copilot-generated inventory that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. It enumerated every resource in the subscription through the Azure MCP Server, resolved each one's owner from the resource tags, reported one hundred per cent owner coverage, and read as thorough work — and the owner tags were group mailboxes, three of which resolved to a distribution list whose single remaining member had left the institution in March. The inventory was accurate about every field it populated. Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the reviewer checked that every resource had an owner, and the artefact demonstrated that every resource had an owner.** An owner field that is populated is not an owner that exists, and no check in the pipeline distinguished the two.

Then students, in threes: **something you do that keeps something working, which nobody has written down.** What happens if you stop?

**Warrant.** Error management targeting points 2 and 4. The case contains no individual error at all — the departing person wrote an honest handover, the receiving team read it, and the omission was invisible from both sides — which is what makes the failure about documentation of the *done* rather than about diligence. **The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen so that it is the same failure in the machine register**: a complete record of a thing that is not the thing, passing a review that checked completeness. It is also the day's own step 8 fail condition demonstrated before the specification states it. The student disclosure is deliberately about themselves rather than about somebody else's omission, because a room that has just spent ninety minutes cataloguing other people's undocumented work needs to find itself in the category.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (30 min)

Pairs, each holding the other's step 3 record.

Each student in turn takes the role of **one maintainer the partner named and did not observe**, and asks: *what do you think I do?*

The partner answers from memory. The student then reads back the partner's own step 3 entry for that person and states **which parts of it are the org chart and which parts are the observation** — pointing at the words.

Both roles are performed by both students. Per specification rule 3, the outcome is recorded in the artefact, including a record in which the pair could not tell the two apart, which is a result about the record rather than an absence of one.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with bilateral production, applying [S037](wiki/seminars/S037.md)'s said/done/inferred discipline to an organisational record at a two-year gap. The structure is deliberately not the absent-party one-question form used at [S039](wiki/seminars/S039.md) and [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md): that protocol has now run twice and a third administration would begin measuring familiarity with it rather than the quality being probed, per instrumentation rule 2.

## Assessment

**One specification item**, in PE390 with CAP390. Per [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) rule 1, the checked elements are: the capability with the outcome in the institution's words; the current boundary with its contents; three non-software components, one per carrying mode, each with a named person and a record of whether the name came from observation or from the org chart; a two-week unavailability consequence or an explicit do-not-know for each; the outcome in the organisation's unit with one moved-work instance or a do-not-know and the evidence that would settle it; the redrawn boundary with the new responsibilities and the student's statement of whether they accept them; the named PE301 material with what it told the student to do; the phase 7 outcome; **the shipped inventory with its three maintainer rows and their provenance fields; the prompt record; the intervention record; and the frontier statement.** **Nothing else is assessed.**

**A maintainer named as a function fails.** "The service desk", "the data team", "whoever is on rota" — a function cannot be unavailable for two weeks and cannot be asked what it does. This is [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s owner rule applied to a component rather than to a decision, and it is this day's only hard requirement.

**An inventory with no maintainer rows fails**, and so does one whose maintainer rows carry an empty provenance field. This is the same rule applied to the build: an inventory that contains only what the tool returned is the tool's output and not the student's artefact.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the design could have detected an intervention-worthy error.

**A record with no do-not-know marks at step 4 or step 5 is examined and may fail.** Three maintainers whose two-week absence a student can confidently describe, and a moved-work instance established without evidence, is the pattern of a student who has asked nobody. The assessors check the provenance record at step 3 before concluding.

**The four phase 3 deployments are not assessed**, because they are demonstrated rather than operated and the specification does not assess what the day could not run.

**The prompt record, intervention record and frontier statement are checked exactly as the [Assessment Architecture](wiki/program/03-Assessment-Architecture.md) specifies and not more.** There is no rubric for a good prompt, and whether the frontier judgement is correct is not assessed and cannot be, because the programme holds no ground truth for the frontier.

No entrustment judgement. The module's is at [S085](wiki/seminars/S085.md).

**No inbound rule-6 obligation lands on this day.** [S069](wiki/seminars/S069.md)'s two instruments both respond at [S070](wiki/seminars/S070.md); its week-4 between-day link to this day is a retrieval commitment and is discharged at phase 2 and at phase 4 point 6.

**Two scope-statement deferrals are received and one is discharged.** [S036](wiki/seminars/S036.md) point 2's omitted material — how requests are produced in organisations — is named for this day and [S082](wiki/seminars/S082.md); the organisational-production half is discharged at phase 4 point 6 and at phase 5 step 3, and the incentive and multi-party halves go to S082. **[WP-037](wiki/whitepapers/WP-037.md) §8's observation-sampling gap is not discharged here and this day does not claim to.** That paper stated the programme does not address who chooses which sessions are observed; this day discloses its own case sampling at phase 3, which is a different and smaller act, and the gap remains open.

**Instrumentation under rule 6.** Two instruments with named response occasions:

- **The phase 3 task-after category coverage** — which of the three carrying modes the cohort produced unprompted, before any vocabulary was supplied. **Response occasion: [S084](wiki/seminars/S084.md), phase 3, whose four adoption cases are selected so that their differences fall on whichever carrying mode this cohort did not produce.** If the cohort omitted *licensing*, S084's cases are chosen so that the decisive resistance is a manager declining to act on correct output; if it omitted *routing*, so that the decisive resistance is in what reaches the system at all. The reading that feeds this branch is phase 3's and **not** phase 5 step 3's, for the reason [WP-081](wiki/whitepapers/WP-081.md) §8 gives.
- **The step 5 moved-work do-not-know rate** — how many students could not establish where work moved, and what evidence they said would settle it. **Response occasion: [S086](wiki/seminars/S086.md), whose transfer inventory must carry one moved-work item per student, or an explicit statement that the evidence named here was sought and not found.** [WP-086](wiki/whitepapers/WP-086.md) §7 must report whether that element exists.

| The boundary is a decision that determines which failures are yours | Where the subscription ends and the institution begins |

- **Durable:** the deployed system as software plus the work around it; the undocumented maintainer; the three carrying modes with their scope statement; the organisation as a component with failure modes; work that moves rather than being removed; whose account you got; the boundary as a decision that determines which failures are yours; and the observation at point 9 that an inventory is bounded by its schema.
- **Perishable:** every figure in the four deployments' cost outcomes; the substrate's current staffing and rota; **and every number, cadence, cap and tool name at points 9 and 10.** All are properties of particular organisations, or of one vendor's product at one date, and none appears in an assessment item. **The documentation's silence about detection rates is not perishable and is taught as the durable half of point 10.**
- **Local:** the four deployments, the substrate's own capability and its institution, the two maintainers present at phase 5, and the withdrawn-capability case at phase 6.

**What this module's durability implies, stated here because it is unusual.** M17 carries the most durable content in the programme and the thinnest platform layer. Re-anchoring this day to a different vendor's inventory tool and a different governance product would change points 9 and 10 and nothing else. **The day is therefore cheap to maintain and expensive to staff**, and the risk register should read the other way round from every earlier module's: the thing to protect is not the currency of the citations, it is the two maintainers' willingness to sit in the room.
