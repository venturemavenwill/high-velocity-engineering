# S058 · Impact Assessment as a Living Document

**Module:** [M12](/wiki/modules/M12.md) · **Quarter:** [Q6](/wiki/quarters/Q6.md) · **Complexity class:** 4
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-058](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-058.md)
**Retrieves from:** S022, S024, S057
**Entry state:** this-programme-only

## The situation

[S057](/wiki/seminars/S057.md) established that the regime applies. Now you write the assessment, and the regime tells you — repeatedly, in several documents, in different words — that it is a **living document**.

Refresh at least annually. Refresh on every new intended use. Refresh before every release stage. Refresh at every milestone. Re-screen every sprint, in the team's own cadence.

**Now look at the form.** The signature block has one row shape and no way to sign again. The revision history has no version number and no change description. The submission is one-shot with no expiry. The completed assessment is filed in a document repository rather than in the system where the work happens.

**The best-designed control in the whole regime is the per-sprint re-screen, and it is the least observable thing in it — no artefact, no checkbox, nowhere at all to record that it happened.**

Answer these before instruction.

1. Look at any assessment your organisation holds. When was it last true?
2. What in your process would cause somebody to reopen it?
3. Take one claim from an assessment. What measurement would show it false?
4. Your review fires. What does it cost, in whose time?
5. If nobody reopens it for a year, what breaks — and who finds out?

**Item 1 usually cannot be answered from the document itself**, which is the finding. **Item 4 is the one people skip**, and it decides whether anything you build here will ever run.

## What you must produce

**An impact assessment for your own system, with the instrumentation the regime omits, built and priced.**

- **The assessment**, with at least one claim rewritten into falsifiable form: **a named measurement, a stated threshold, a verification method, an accountable owner.**
- **A version field and a change description**, so that a reader can tell what moved.
- **A re-sign-off row**, so that agreeing again is recordable.
- **An expiry**, after which the document is stale rather than silent.
- **A trigger bound to a state change**, not to a person remembering.
- **A home in the system of record where the work lives**, not in a document repository.
- **The price of one review**, in whose time, and your judgement about whether it will be paid.

## What good looks like

- **The falsifiable claim has all four fields.** *The system will be monitored for bias* has none of them and is not a claim.
- **The trigger is a state change somebody else makes.** A release, a scope change, a new intended use entering a register. **Not a calendar reminder and not an intention.**
- **The version field is used**, not present. There is more than one entry by the time you submit.
- **The document lives where the work lives.** If your team never opens the place it is stored, it is filed rather than held.
- **The review's price is stated in other people's time**, because that is what actually determines whether it happens.
- **You have said whether it will be paid**, honestly, including if the answer is no.

## How it goes wrong

- **The one-shot assessment** — written to clear a gate, never reopened, describing a system that stopped existing. *Warning sign:* it was completed in one sitting.
- **The calendar trigger** — refresh annually, with nobody owning the calendar. *Warning sign:* your trigger has a date in it and no actor.
- **The unversioned document** — it changed, and nothing records what. *Warning sign:* the revision history has one row.
- **The unfalsifiable claim** — a commitment nothing could disturb. *Warning sign:* you cannot say what observation would embarrass it.
- **The unpriced ritual** — you designed a review nobody can afford, so it will not run. *Warning sign:* your review requires a meeting.
- **The wrong home** — correctly filed, permanently unread. *Warning sign:* you had to look up where it goes.

## Do it

**First, make one claim falsifiable.** Take the vaguest commitment in your assessment and give it the four fields the template supplies: a named measurement, a stated threshold, a verification method, an accountable owner. **These four fields are the reusable part of the whole regime** and the rest of the template is form-filling. Doing this once will show you how many of your other claims are decoration.

**Second, bind the trigger to a state change rather than to a memory.** The practice material behind this programme gets this exactly right somewhere else and never connects the two: **its environment withdrawal is triggered by a state change in an authoritative register, not by somebody remembering** — marking a project closed is what starts the decommissioning. **Borrow that shape here.** A release entering a state. A new intended use recorded. A scope field changing. **A trigger that depends on a person remembering is not a trigger, and a trigger with a date and no actor is a calendar entry.**

**Third, build the missing instrumentation.** A version field. A change description. A row for signing again. An expiry after which the document reads as stale rather than as current. **None of this is clever and all of it is absent**, and its absence is why an assessment written to clear a commercial gate goes on describing a system that no longer exists.

**Fourth, move it to where the work lives.** A governance artefact in a document repository is filed. The same artefact in the system of record your team opens daily is held. **This is the cheapest change available and the one most likely to be refused**, because the document repository is where the compliance function looks.

**Fifth, price one review and be honest about it.** How long, whose time, what it displaces. Then say whether it will be paid. **A review that costs more than anyone will pay is a review that does not exist**, and the practice material behind this stage demonstrates the general case: its wellness guidance is advisory inside a system of checklists, and **advisory guidance inside a system of checklists is guidance that loses.**

## Show it

Give your assessment to somebody who has not read it and ask one question.

**When was this last true?**

If they cannot answer from the document, your instrumentation failed, whatever you built. The answer must be derivable from the artefact — not from you, not from a wiki page, not from somebody's memory of a meeting.

Then ask the second: **what would have to happen for you to open this again?** If the answer is *somebody would ask me to*, your trigger is a person.

## What this does not establish

**Nothing here shows that reviewing an assessment changes a system.** The regime this session is derived from asserts the living-document requirement in several places and **publishes no evidence that any assessment was ever revised, or that any revision altered anything.**

**The instrumentation you build is a design argument, not a fix with a warrant.** A version field does not cause anybody to look. An expiry does not cause anybody to act. **They make staleness visible, which is a precondition for acting and not a substitute for it**, and the honest expectation is that most assessments will still be written once.

**The per-sprint re-screen deserves a specific note**, because it is the strongest idea in the regime and the weakest in practice. Screening in the working cadence is where a governance question can still change a build, which is exactly where a governance question should be. **It is also the one thing in the entire collection with no artefact, no checkbox and nowhere to record that it happened.** This session asks you to give it a place to be recorded. Whether anyone will use it is untested and this programme should not pretend otherwise.

## Perishable content in this session

| Durable claim | Perishable instance |
|---|---|
| A living-document requirement with nowhere to record the review is not a requirement | Whatever assessment template your organisation issues this year |
| A trigger bound to a state change survives; one bound to memory does not | The particular register and states available in your tooling |
| A review nobody can afford does not happen | Current review-capacity expectations and team size |
| A document filed where the work is not, is not held | Present conventions about where governance artefacts live |
