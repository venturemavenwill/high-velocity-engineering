# S023 · Non-Functional Requirements That Can Be Verified

**Module:** [M05](/wiki/modules/M05.md) · **Quarter:** [Q3](/wiki/quarters/Q3.md) · **Complexity class:** 2
**Strands:** PE · ME
**Platform anchor:** none
**Whitepaper:** [WP-023](/wiki/whitepapers/WP-023.md)
**Retrieves from:** S006, S021, S022
**Entry state:** either

---

## The situation

Everything in your requirement set so far was volunteered. Somebody wanted it and said so.

**The requirements that end engagements are not volunteered.** Nobody asks for availability until it is absent, for portability until they are moving, for data-handling discipline until somebody has looked at production data, or for maintainability until the person who wrote it has left.

Answer these in writing before anything is taught.

1. Name three properties this system must have that nobody has asked for.
2. Which of them is the customer's policy rather than your engineering choice?
3. Which one, violated once, ends the engagement?
4. Who owns each of them inside the customer's organisation — by role, not by name?
5. **What is your evidence that the number you just wrote down is the right number?**

**Item 5 is the session and most people have no answer to it.** The number came from a document, or from the last engagement, or from a catalogue of examples somebody circulated. That is a real origin and it is an acceptable one, provided it is written down as the origin rather than presented as a specification.

## What you must produce

A **non-functional requirement set**, in the same eight-field capture form as [S022](/wiki/seminars/S022.md), with the same two fields marked — the measurement and the verification method — and one addition that this session imposes: **every threshold carries its origin.**

The set is produced by walking the published enumeration of non-functional categories one at a time, and for each category writing either the requirement or the sentence saying why it does not apply here. Pages read 2026-07-30.

And one more thing, which is the closing exercise: **the category the enumeration does not have**, added by you and marked as added.

## What good looks like

- **Every category is either present or explicitly declared out of scope, with a reason.** Silence is not a declaration. An engagement that decided portability was irrelevant and wrote that down has made a decision; one that never mentioned it has made the same decision invisibly.
- **Every threshold has a stated origin.** Acceptable origins include: the customer's existing policy, a contractual obligation, an observed baseline, a regulator's published requirement, and *borrowed from a catalogue that carries no source*. **That last one is acceptable exactly once per number, in writing, and it is the origin most learners actually have.**
- **Each requirement names an owner inside the customer's organisation**, by role. A non-functional requirement with no owner on the customer's side has nobody to defend it when it becomes inconvenient, which is the moment it matters.
- **Where the customer's policy makes a property absolute, it is written as absolute** rather than as a target. Some data-handling constraints are of this kind — production data is not touched, and there is no threshold version of that sentence.
- **The property whose single violation ends the engagement is identified as such**, and its verification runs before the thing that could violate it, not after.
- **One category is added that the published enumeration does not cover**, with a note saying it was added. The enumeration is a list somebody wrote, not a taxonomy with warrant.

## How it goes wrong

- **The borrowed catalogue** — the numbers are all from the example bank, none carries an origin, and the set now looks like a standard. *Warning sign:* your thresholds are round, and several of them are the same round numbers as the last project's.
- **The un-owned property** — the requirement is well written and nobody on the customer's side is accountable for it. *Warning sign:* the owner field contains your own team.
- **The enumeration read as complete** — you worked through the categories the navigation showed you. The published section's own index page does not exist, some of its category pages are two bullets long, and most but not all of them follow the section's own structure. **A menu that implies completeness is not a completeness claim.** *Warning sign:* your set has exactly the categories the list had, in the list's order.
- **The aspirational availability figure** — a number chosen because it sounds serious, with no instrument that could observe it and no consequence attached to missing it. *Warning sign:* nobody asked what it would cost.
- **The constraint discovered at assurance** — a property that was always mandatory arrives during a security or compliance review, after the design is fixed. *Warning sign:* nobody from the customer's risk, security or data functions has read this set.

## Do it

Walk the enumeration. For every category, write the requirement or write the refusal. **Expect the refusals to be the harder writing**, because a refusal has to say why.

Then go back through every number you wrote and put its origin next to it. Where the honest answer is that you copied it, write that. **A borrowed number with its provenance attached is a usable engineering artefact; the same number with the provenance stripped is a fabricated specification, and the only difference is a clause.**

Then find the category the enumeration does not have. Candidates that recur in this kind of work: what happens to the system when the engagement ends; what the customer must be able to observe without you; what evidence the system must produce about its own behaviour; and what it costs to run at the volume actually expected. **Pick one that is real for your engagement, write it in the same form, and mark it as added.**

## Show it

Hand your set to somebody whose only instruction is to ask, for every number, **where did that come from?**

Answers of "it's standard", "that's what we normally use" and "the client expects it" are struck through on the spot. What survives is your non-functional set. What was struck goes back to you with the number intact and the origin missing, which is the state most of these documents are in when they are signed.

## What this does not establish

The category enumeration is a list, not a validated taxonomy. It was read in full on 2026-07-30; most but not all of its category pages follow the structure the section itself proposes, several are a few lines long, and **the section has no index page at all** — the one address in the whole publication that does not resolve. Nothing establishes that the categories are exhaustive, correctly cut, or the right ones for any particular system.

**The threshold catalogue carries no citation of any kind.** Its numbers are illustrations by the authors' own framing, they sit in the same flat declarative register as the publication's best-sourced pages, and nothing on the page distinguishes them. This session uses them as an example bank and **forbids them as benchmarks**, which is the strongest defensible use.

The claim that an unstated non-functional requirement surfaces later at higher cost is **mechanically plausible and unmeasured here.** Direction only.
