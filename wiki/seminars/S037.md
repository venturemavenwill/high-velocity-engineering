# S037 · Contextual Inquiry with a Real User

**Module:** [M08](wiki/modules/M08.md) · **Quarter:** [Q4](wiki/quarters/Q4.md) · **Complexity class:** 3
**Strands:** PE
**Whitepaper:** [WP-037](wiki/whitepapers/WP-037.md)
**Depends on:** S036
**Platform anchor:** Azure API Management `llm-emit-token-metric` policy and Azure Monitor metrics for the Foundry deployment · **MCP servers:** Azure MCP Server (write, for the telemetry policy; monitor namespace for queries) · Microsoft Learn MCP Server

---

## What this day is for

S036 exhausted the request as evidence, and built it. What remains cannot be obtained from a document or from a deployment: **what actually happens when this person does their work.**

Students have interviewed people. At S015 they questioned a caller and derived an operation mix, and that was a good day whose method was extractive — they went in with a question and came out with an answer. This day inverts it. The method is **observation with minimal intervention**, and its central discipline is that you do not steer.

The reason is that what people say and what people do diverge, systematically and without deception. A user describing their work describes the version they believe they perform, cleaned of the workarounds, the abandoned attempts and the small accommodations they have stopped noticing. Those are the interesting parts, and they are only available by watching.

**And there is a fourth record available today that was not available at S015.** The gateway the user is working through can be made to write down what it did, request by request, while you watch them. That record is not the truth either. It is another instrument, with its own resolution, its own blind spots, and a vendor who documents both — and the most useful hour of the day is the one where the observer's record and the machine's record disagree about the same ninety minutes.

## Phase 1 · Pretest and prediction (25 min)

Five items, before the user arrives.

1. Ask your step 9 question from S036. Write the answer you expect.
2. What will they do during their work that they will not mention?
3. How many times do you expect to speak in the next ninety minutes?
4. Predict: will your expected answer to item 1 be close?
5. **Copilot prediction, sealed.** You will shortly ask the agent to instrument this session — to emit a token metric per request, with the dimensions you would want, and to give you the queries that show what happened. Write down what it will produce, and **name the one thing about this user's session it will silently fail to record**. Sealed with items 1 to 4 and opened at phase 8.

Item 3 is the day's discipline made numeric. Most students predict fifteen to twenty; the target is under five.

**Warrant.** Pretesting the expected answer so that it can be compared against the actual, and pretesting the student's own intervention rate, which is the behaviour the day is training. Item 5 is sealed at phase 1 per instrumentation rule 8, and it is the only one of the five items whose answer is checkable against a vendor's documentation rather than against the room — which is deliberate, because it is the day's first evidence that an instrument's blind spots can be looked up rather than discovered.

## Phase 2 · Cumulative retrieval set (20 min)

Free recall, no notes.

- The four behavioural questions from S036.
- The four shaping forces on a request.
- The S027 four error sources.
- Which S027 error source does asking someone to describe their work introduce?

The final item is the day's premise: self-report is a systematically biased instrument, and the bias is not dishonesty.

**Warrant.** Expanding-interval retrieval with a bridge making self-report an error-source problem rather than a trust problem, which is what allows students to discount it without disrespecting the person.

---

## Phase 3 · Contrasting cases and generation (40 min)

Groups receive **four records of the same twenty minutes**: a substrate user performing a task through the gateway, recorded four ways.

- **Transcript A** — what the user said afterwards when asked to describe the task.
- **Transcript B** — what the user said during the task, thinking aloud.
- **Record C** — an observer's record of what the user did.
- **Record D** — **the platform's own record of that episode**, retained and queryable.

A, B and C are read, because they are records of a past episode and cannot be re-run. **D is operated.** Groups query it themselves through the Azure MCP Server's monitor tooling, choosing the metric, the aggregation and the interval, and they find out what it holds by asking it rather than by being handed a printout.

The four disagree. They disagree in specific and characteristic ways, and D disagrees with C for reasons nobody in the room predicts:

- **The finest interval available is one minute.** Every platform metric on this resource is sampled at `PT1M`. The user's four rapid retries inside eight seconds are one point.
- **The metric named availability did not move.** `AzureOpenAIAvailabilityRate` is computed as total calls minus server errors over total calls, where server errors are HTTP responses of 500 and above. **The refusals the user experienced were 429s, and 429s do not reduce it.** The episode in which the user was blocked repeatedly reports availability at 100%.
- **The refusals are visible somewhere else.** The request-count metric carries a status-code dimension, so a group that splits by it sees them — and a group that does not, does not.
- **The vendor says its latency figure is not the user's latency.** The response-time metric is documented as an approximation that does not account for client-side latency between the client and the endpoint, with the recommendation that you refer to your own logging instead.

Task: **identify the three places where A and C diverge, and say what caused each.** Then: **identify one place where C and D disagree, and say which you would believe.** Then: which single record would you keep if you could keep only one?

**Warrant.** Contrasting cases across evidence types with a fixed episode. The A-versus-C divergence is the phase's original payload and its causes — omission of the automatic, omission of the embarrassing, and reconstruction of the sequence — are the day's taxonomy. **D is the phase's new payload and it does something the human records cannot: it establishes that a machine record is an instrument with a construct-instrument gap of its own, and that the gap is documented rather than discovered.** The availability result is the sharpest single fact in the module — a metric whose name is the user's word for what they wanted, computing something that excludes the thing that happened to them.

## Phase 4 · Consolidating instruction (35 min)

Deliberately short. The day's substance is phase 5.

1. **Say and do diverge,** and the divergence is not deception. Three mechanisms, from phase 3.
2. **The master-apprentice stance.** You are there to learn how the work is done, from the person who does it, in the place where it happens.
3. **The four rules:** go where the work is, watch it happen, ask about what you see rather than about what they think, and do not steer.
4. **What steering looks like** — a leading question, a suggestion offered as a question, a silence you filled, and agreeing too quickly. **This list omits the steering that occurs by selecting which session to observe and which task to ask for, which is real, is invisible from inside the session, and is not addressed anywhere in this programme.**
5. **Separating said, done and inferred** in the record. The single most important discipline and the day's assessed artefact. **The machine record is a fourth column and it is not a fifth kind of truth** — it is *done*, at the resolution the platform samples at, for the subset of behaviour the platform is positioned to see.
6. **Interruption as a cost with a benefit.** You may interrupt; each interruption changes what you will subsequently observe.
7. **What to do when they ask you what they should do.** They will.
8. **A machine record has a construct, an instrument and a gap, exactly like any other.** S026's apparatus applies without modification. The construct is *what happened to this user*; the instrument is a metric with a name, a computation, a sampling interval and a set of dimensions somebody chose. **Reading the metric's definition is not optional diligence; it is the only way to know what you are looking at**, and today the definition is one Learn MCP lookup away.
9. **Defaults are opinions, and today three were chosen for you — all of them silently.** The metric namespace defaults to **"API Management"**, so a team that emits without naming one finds their custom metrics in a namespace they did not pick and cannot easily tell apart from anyone else's. **Certain models, especially when streaming, do not include token counts in the response at all unless `include_usage` is set to true** — which means the instrument you deployed can be correct, running, and empty, and nothing will tell you. And when a stream is interrupted or terminated unexpectedly, **the captured token counts are documented as inaccurate**, without any statement of by how much or in which direction. **A silently empty instrument is worse than no instrument**, because it produces a record that looks like evidence.
10. **Perishable and durable, separated.** The metric names, the definitions, the sampling interval, the dimension limits and the default namespace are **perishable**, and the ten legacy metrics on this resource that the documentation instructs you not to use for this service will not carry those instructions forever. What is durable is that every record is an instrument, that a record's silence is a property of the instrument rather than of the world, and that the way to find out is to read the definition. **Safe** for reasoning about what today's records do and do not contain. **Unsafe** as a general claim about telemetry, and unsafe as a claim about what the platform will report next year.

**Warrant.** Consolidation kept short so that phase 5 can run at length. Point 4 carries the scope statement including the unaddressed selection-steering gap. Point 5 is the assessed discipline. Points 8 and 9 are the day's platform-specific content; point 9 satisfies the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s default requirement and its three items are chosen because all three fail *quietly*, which is the property the day is about.

## Phase 5 · The inquiry, and its instrument (100 min)

**Class 3. The inquiry is the student's. This phase produces a build, and the build is an instrument rather than a remedy.**

A **real substrate user** performs their actual work, in their actual environment, for ninety minutes. Students observe in pairs — one observing, one recording — and swap at the midpoint.

Structure:

0. **Before the user arrives, ship the instrument.** Using Copilot in agent mode through the Azure MCP Server, deploy an `llm-emit-token-metric` policy scoped to this user's traffic, with the dimensions you have decided you need. **You may configure at most five custom dimensions**, and the number of active time series the policy generates is the product of each dimension's distinct values — so this is a design decision with a cost, not a checklist. The policy observes; it changes nothing the user experiences. Per the module's build rule, an instrument that alters the service fails.
1. **Ask the S036 step 9 question** at the start. Once. Record the answer.
2. **Observe.** Record continuously, in three columns: said, done, inferred.
3. **Ask about what you see,** not about what they think. **Your partner counts your interventions**, in a fourth column, while you count your own. Two counts, independently produced.
4. **At the end**, ask the same question from step 1 again, and record the answer.
5. **Immediately afterwards**, and before discussing with anyone, write the three things you observed that the user did not mention.
6. **Then, and only then, query your instrument** for the same ninety minutes. Reconcile it against your three-column record: what it confirms, what it contradicts, and **what it could not have seen**.

Step 4's repetition is the day's instrument on the user. Step 3's paired count is the day's instrument on the observer. Step 6's reconciliation is the day's instrument on the instrument.

**The artefact carries three further elements**, per the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md).

- **Prompt record.** What you asked the agent for at step 0, in order, including what you abandoned.
- **Intervention record.** Every point at which you corrected it. **An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that your own design would have caught an intervention-worthy error — a dimension count checked against the five-dimension limit, or a pre-session smoke test confirming that token counts are actually arriving, are both accepted and are both the right check to have built.
- **Frontier statement.** One thing the agent did faster than you could have, and one thing it did badly that you had to catch.

**Warrant.** Whole-task at class 3 with a real user in a real setting. The three-column record is the discipline; step 5's isolation prevents the observation from being reshaped by discussion; step 6's ordering is deliberate and load-bearing — a student who reads the machine record first will write the human record to agree with it. **Step 3's paired count is the repair [WP-037](wiki/whitepapers/WP-037.md) §8 identified and did not make**, and it is now made: instrumentation rule 1 forbids self-report where an external observer is already present, the partner was always present, and the fourth column costs nothing.

## Phase 6 · Error clinic (35 min)

Instructor first: an inquiry they conducted that produced a confident and wrong conclusion, with attention to **the moment they started steering** and why it felt like clarifying.

Then the agent case: a Copilot-built session dashboard that was **confidently wrong and passed review**. Asked to show whether the service had been available to this user during the session, the agent selected the metric named availability, wrote a correct query, produced a correct chart, and reported 100%. The user had been refused throughout. The metric excludes 429s by definition and the definition is published.

Attention goes to why review did not catch it: **the reviewer checked that the query was right, and it was.** The chart matched the metric, the metric matched its own definition, and nobody read the definition, because the metric's name was the same word the requester had used. This is S026's construct-instrument gap arriving from the direction nobody watches — not a badly built instrument, but a correctly built one measuring a different construct.

Then students: something you asked today that you should not have, or a number you accepted today without reading what it counted.

**Warrant.** Error management on the day's specific discipline. The steering moment is almost never experienced as steering, which is what makes the first disclosure useful. The agent case is the [Copilot Practice Standard](wiki/program/12-Copilot-Practice-Standard.md)'s required addition and is chosen because the agent did nothing wrong: it answered the question it was asked, with a real metric, correctly — which is precisely the failure the module exists to teach.

## Phase 7 · Peer articulation (25 min)

Pairs — the same pairs who observed together. Each states **their three unmentioned observations** from step 5, independently written.

Where they differ, the pair determines whether one observed something the other missed, or whether one inferred something and recorded it as observed.

They then compare **their two intervention counts of each other** against each other's self-counts, and record all four numbers.

**Warrant.** Interactive-tier articulation with an independent-record comparison. Two people watching the same ninety minutes producing different records is the day's most direct evidence that observation is itself an instrument. The four-number comparison is what makes the day's principal measure external rather than self-reported.

---

## Phase 8 · Calibration and commitment (20 min)

- Items 1 to 4 resolved. **Expected answers against actual, and predicted intervention counts against both the self-counted and the partner-counted values.**
- The room's step 4 answer-changes collected: how many users answered differently at the end.
- The cohort's said/done/inferred column ratios shown; a record with an empty inferred column is discussed.
- **Item 5 opened.** The sealed predictions about what the instrument would silently fail to record are read against the step 6 reconciliations, and the cohort's hit rate is recorded. Both halves count: predicting that a record has blind spots is easy, and naming one before deploying it is the measure.
- Each student names one thing they now believe about the work that no document and no metric would have told them.
- Retrieval schedule: next morning, day 3, week 2, week 6.

## Assessment

Specification item in PE201. Checked elements: both answers to the S036 question; the three-column record with all three columns non-empty; **both intervention counts, self and partner**; the three unmentioned observations; one identified misclassification of inference as observation; **the deployed telemetry policy and its dimension choices**; the step 6 reconciliation including what the instrument could not have seen; **the prompt record; the intervention record; the frontier statement.**

**A record with an empty inferred column fails the specification.** Everything an observer records is partly inference; an empty column means the distinction was not being made.

**An artefact whose instrument changed what the user experienced fails the specification.** The module's build rule.

**An intervention record with no entries fails**, unless the artefact demonstrates that the student's own design would have caught an intervention-worthy error.

Nothing else is assessed — not the record's length, not the quality of the observations, not the intervention count's value, and not the instrument's completeness.

## Perishable content in this day

| Durable claim | Platform instance, perishable |
|---|---|
| Say and do diverge, without deception | This user's account of their own retries |
| Every record is an instrument with a construct it does not fully cover | `AzureOpenAIAvailabilityRate` excluding 429s |
| An instrument has a resolution, and behaviour below it is not absent but invisible | The `PT1M` time grain on every metric of this resource |
| An instrument can be correct, running and silently empty | Token counts omitted unless `include_usage` is set |
| Instrument design is a decision with a cost | The five-dimension limit and the active-time-series product |
| A vendor will tell you what its instrument does not measure, if you read it | The response-time metric's documented exclusion of client-side latency |

The user's specific work, the day's records, and every metric name, definition, default and limit above are **perishable**. Say-do divergence and its three mechanisms, the master-apprentice stance, the four rules, the said/done/inferred separation, interruption's cost, and the treatment of a machine record as an instrument are durable and are what the specification assesses.
